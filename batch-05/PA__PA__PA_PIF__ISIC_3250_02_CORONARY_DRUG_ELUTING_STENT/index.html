Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A purely virtual office is not permissible for the statutory registered address of a Panamanian Private Interest Foundation: Law 25 of 1995 imposes a mandatory resident agent (Agente Residente) maintaining a physical office in Panama with custody of the foundation's records and seal. For the specific scope of Class III coronary drug-eluting stent manufacturing (ISIC 3250), the OECD BEPS Action 5 and Panama's economic substance reporting framework require real operational substance in the jurisdiction where value is generated; a virtual-only structure will impair MEDSENUMAS/MINSA regulatory licensing, cause probable denials by correspondent banks, and raise UBO transparency red flags. A nominal virtual mailbox is acceptable only as an ancillary administrative facility, not as the registered office.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); online portal e-Tax (https://etax.mef.gob.pa). Customs export clearance is administered by the Dirección General de Aduanas through the SIGA platform. Private Interest Foundation formation is registered with the Registro Público de Panamá through a licensed registered agent.

E-government filing portal

For foreign entities and non-resident PIF founders, the DGI RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) is obtained through e-Tax by: (1) appointing a resident tax/legal representative and lodging apostilled constitutional documents (PIF charter, foundation council resolutions, good-standing certificates) with a licensed registered agent; (2) obtaining a digital-signature certificate (Firma Electrónica) for portal authentication; (3) filing the registration form and sworn statement of operations; (4) receiving the formal RUC resolution and, where taxable Panama activities exist, ITBMS 'Responsable' status with a monthly filing calendar. A PIF with no Panama-source commercial activity is not required to hold an ITBMS RUC but must maintain its foundation registration number and pay the annual state fee.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — For foreign entities and non-resident PIF founders, the DGI RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) is obtained through e-Tax by: (1) appointing a resident tax/legal representative and lodging apostilled constitutional documents (PIF charter, foundation council resolutions, good-standing certificates) with a licensed registered agent; (2) obtaining a digital-signature certificate (Firma Electrónica) for portal authentication; (3) filing the registration form and sworn statement of operations; (4) receiving the formal RUC resolution and, where taxable Panama activities exist, ITBMS 'Responsable' status with a monthly filing calendar. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not offer a U.S.-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections under Law 25 of 1995 and the Código Fiscal include: (i) election to operate solely as a private wealth-holding vehicle outside the Panama income-tax (ISR) and ITBMS net, or election to register as an ITBMS 'Responsable' when undertaking Panamanian taxable commerce; (ii) election to be recognized as a separate juridical person from the founder and beneficiaries for Panamanian-situs assets; (iii) election by the foundation council to adopt the restricted family-foundation regime; (iv) election to compute ISR on a territorial basis on Panama-source income only, with BEPS/MLI (Law 42 of 2017) substance requirements and hybrid-mismatch neutralization applied per DGI guidance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 250,000 of annual taxable turnover (threshold of the simplified small-taxpayer regime; all commercial enterprises otherwise register as ITBMS 'Responsables' upon the first Panama-source taxable transaction, with no general de minimis exemption).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical exportation of tangible goods (including Class III coronary drug-eluting stents) qualifies for ITBMS 0% where: (i) the goods leave Panamanian customs territory under a SIGA export declaration; (ii) the exporter holds current registration in the national exporters' registry (Ministerio de Comercio e Industrias); (iii) title and risk pass to a non-resident purchaser as documented by commercial invoice and transport documents; and (iv) sales proceeds are received in a Panama bank account through an international payment rail (SWIFT wire or letter of credit). Supplies to the Colon Free Zone and Panama Pacifico Special Economic Area, and services consumed abroad, also qualify for zero-rating.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit certificate evidencing foreign exchange receipt)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Cargo Manifest (Manifiesto de Carga)

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica) under DGI Resolution No. 201-4118 (2021) and amending rules, phased by taxpayer segment through 2024. ITBMS-registered 'Responsables' must transmit invoices in real time to the DGI SIF platform via accredited SOAP/REST API endpoints for 'validación previa' (pre-clearance). Upon validation, the DGI returns an authorization code and a digital stamp (hash/QR token) that must be embedded in the invoice XML/PDF. Face-to-face retail may use certified fiscal printers (398-A) during the transition; large exporters are expected to use accredited ERP/EDI integrators. Non-pre-cleared invoices are fiscally null for ITBMS input-credit purposes.

Export zero-rating is subject to DGI audit with a prescription period of 5 years (extended to 7 years for omitted declarations or fraud). If documentation is deficient or the foreign payment is not evidenced, the sale is re-characterized as taxable at 7% ITBMS, with a 10% recargo surcharge, monthly late-payment interest, and fines of one to five times the evaded tax. Under Law 8 of 2010, aggravated tax fraud may attract 2 to 5 years' imprisonment (up to 7 years for continued fraud), deregistration of the exporter, and joint and several liability of the foundation council and its resident agent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized; the Panamanian balboa is a nominal unit at parity with USD and does not materially circulate. Capital account and current account convertibility are unrestricted; there are no exchange-control laws, no central bank conversion authorization, and no profit-remittance licensing. Remittance hurdles are tax and compliance related rather than FX: dividends paid out of Panamanian-source profits are subject to Panama's 10% dividend tax, and banks require clean source-of-funds, tax compliance evidence, and OFAC/AML screening for any outbound distribution. Export proceeds may be retained freely in USD or other currencies both onshore and offshore.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panamanian law does not impose mandatory surrender, conversion ratios, or statutory timelines for export receipts. Exporters may retain USD or foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts; only general commercial invoicing, accounting, and Law 23 of 2015 AML record-keeping obligations apply.

Central bank supervision: Banco Nacional de Panamá (fiscal agent) and Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as banking and financial intelligence regulator; Panama has no conventional central bank and no foreign-exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium and acceptable only as an ancillary settlement rail. Offshore digital EMIs lack a Panamanian banking license from SBP and cannot legally hold statutory foundation capital, issue documentary letters of credit, or provide the level of transactional evidence required for a high-risk MedTech exporter. They may support small-value cross-border collections from distributors or e-commerce channels but must be capped at a low operational float and cannot replace a licensed Panamanian bank for treasury, escrow, or LC functions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Founder or investor capital should be routed in USD via SWIFT MT103 from the original source bank to the designated PIF operational USD account. Payment details must include the exact foundation name and the reference 'Contribución del Fundador' or 'Capital Contribution' to align with the foundation ledger and avoid AML mismatches. Panama imposes no prior approval and no exchange-control form, but correspondent banks will perform OFAC screening and the receiving Panamanian bank will apply Law 23 of 2015 due diligence. Verification dossier: certified MT103 or MT202 credit advice, founder contribution resolution, source-of-funds documents including audited financial statements or asset sale contracts, founder ID and proof of address, and bank credit ticket matched to the foundation accounting entry. No administrative strike-off is prescribed for foundations under Law 25 of 1995.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is required when aggregate controlled transactions exceed B/.3,000,000 (USD 3,000,000) per fiscal year. Master File is required when the consolidated group revenue exceeds B/.50,000,000 (USD 50,000,000) or when total controlled transactions exceed B/.10,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to Panama under international tax transparency frameworks. A PIF deriving manufacturing must demonstrate substantive business presence through physical premises, qualified employees, and board decisions in Panama. Letterbox structures will be recharacterised and may lose territorial tax benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion or Fraud

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty equal to 100% of tax due plus an additional 100% to 300% surcharge on the deficiency; cancellation of RUC; disqualification of the foundation's accountant; DGI is authorised to rescind transactions and re-assess tax at the statutory 25% rate.

Director personal liability: Directors and council members are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency when they knowingly signed false declarations; criminal imprisonment of 3 to 5 years under Title IX of the Panama Criminal Code; travel bans and judicial orders restricting departure apply until restitution is made.
Shell Company Abuse or Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: General anti-abuse rule recharacterisation: 25% tax on deemed Panamanian-source income; a 15% surcharge on late assessments; administrative fine of B/.50,000 for failure to meet Economic Substance Regulations; potential compulsory striking-off from the Public Registry.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally ordered to pay the assessed tax if the foundation becomes insolvent; a ban from holding corporate or foundation office in Panama for up to 10 years; judicial disentitlement from accessing Panamanian banking channels.
AML and Financial Crimes in Cross-Border Flows

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015 (AML/CFT), fines from B/.10,000 up to 5% of the transaction value; freezing of bank accounts and assets; annulment of the PIF's legal registration; referral to the Financial Analysis Unit for criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members and beneficial owners face criminal imprisonment of up to 10 years for knowingly facilitating money laundering; assets may be subject to civil forfeiture; international travel bans and personal financial sanctions are imposed by the Superintendency of Banks.
