Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Panama – Sede Electrónica de la DGI, available at https://www.dgi.gob.pa. Customs export control is administered by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA).

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign entity establishing a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima must first file the articles of incorporation with the Registro Público de Panamá, then apply through the DGI's online portal for a Registro Único Contribuyente (RUC). Required submissions include the juridical entity certificate (Ficha Registral), the corporate charter and registered agent confirmation, the resolution appointing a legal representative resident in Panama, certified passport copies of direct and beneficial owners, proof of a Panamanian registered address, and a completed DGI registration form. After issuance of the RUC, the entity must activate the ITBMS tax account and obtain a digital signature/e-signature certificate accepted by the DGI electronic filing system. There is no minimum capital requirement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Territorial_Scope – the Sociedad Anónima is a juridical person taxable at entity level on Panama-source income only. Foreign-source income, including cross-border tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory fees derived from non-resident clients with no Panamanian nexus, is outside the Panamanian income tax base. Panama-source business income is subject to corporate income tax at the standard 25% rate, and exported services are treated at 0% under ITBMS.

        
Available election forms
Panama has no check-the-box election equivalent to the U.S. regime; entity classification follows legal form. Available elections include: (i) the Multinational Enterprises Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007, under which qualifying service centers and administrative hubs may elect 100% exemption on qualifying cross-border service income and dividends; (ii) the simplified small-taxpayer regime under Ley 48 de 2019, permitting quarterly income tax filing for eligible small enterprises; and (iii) the territorial-source accounting election confirming that only Panamanian-source revenue is declared, consistent with Article 694 of the Panamanian Tax Code and regulatory decrees. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
7%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No economic threshold (zero) – any natural or juridical person performing taxable transfers of goods or services in Panama, including a non-resident with Panamanian-source taxable transactions, must obtain an RUC and register as an ITBMS taxpayer before commencing operations. A simplified registration procedure exists for small taxpayers with annual taxable turnover below USD 30,000, but registration remains mandatory.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating is available for: (a) goods physically exported from Panama under an accepted export customs declaration and delivered to a non-resident buyer abroad; and (b) services provided to a non-resident client when the service is consumed, used, or otherwise economically exploited entirely outside Panama, including cross-border tax structuring, management, and transfer pricing advisory services. The Panamanian exporter must demonstrate that the benefit of the service is not obtained within Panamanian territory and that no local taxable event occurs.

Panama has a mandatory phased electronic invoicing system administered through the DGI Sede Electrónica. Taxpayers exceeding the currently applicable turnover threshold (USD 300,000 annual invoiced sales) must issue approved electronic invoices (e-Factura) in real time, incorporating DGI-validated sequential numbering, a QR fiscal stamp, and the vendor RUC, synchronizing with DGI systems before delivery. Below the threshold, paper invoices are still admissible but voluntary e-invoicing is encouraged. Taxpayers in special incentive regimes, including SEM, must comply from inception. Authorized invoicing software must integrate with DGI’s application programming interface and submit a daily operations log for fiscal clearance.

      
DGI maintains intensive audit scrutiny over export zero-rating claims. If documentation is incomplete or the claimed export cannot be substantiated by physical customs exit or verifiable offshore service performance, the zero-rating is clawed back, and the taxpayer is assessed 7% ITBMS on the gross amount, plus statutory late-payment interest, surcharges, and fines of up to USD 5,000 per infringement under the Panamanian Tax Code. Fraudulent claims involving forged documents, false remittance certificates, or artificial circular transactions constitute criminal tax evasion and may lead to retrospective reassessments for up to five tax years, cancellation of tax registration or incentive-holder status, and criminal prosecution under Decree-Law 2 of 2015 and related anticorruption statutes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Full and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account. Panama has no exchange-control statute, no surrender requirement, no approval gate for inward or outward remittances, and no monetary-authority balance-of-payments justification. Dividends, royalties, fees, and capital repayments may be remitted abroad freely; because the territorial tax regime taxes only Panamanian-source income, distributions of foreign-source profits earned by an offshore-active S.A. attract zero Panamanian withholding. Domestic-source dividends are subject to a 10% withholding regime unless the distributing corporation is covered by the alternative tax-paid credit mechanism.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None. There is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no offshore-receivables repatriation duty applicable to export receipts, given the absence of a national FX position to protect. A Panamanian exporter or service provider may retain export proceeds in any foreign bank account indefinitely; if repatriated, such funds are credited to the local account in USD without formality, customs declaration, or central-bank settlement.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Panama operates without a conventional central bank. Monetary sovereignty is delegated to the U.S. Federal Reserve because the U.S. dollar is legal tender under Decree Law No. 5 of 1904; the Panamanian balboa (PAB) circulates only as token coinage at 1:1 parity. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the consolidated prudential supervisor of banks, trust companies, and fintech licensees, while Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent of the Republic and settlement agent of the national clearing house. No institution performs central-bank foreign-exchange or monetary-policy functions.

              Foreign-source income excluded under territoriality, special-regime income, and non-taxable passive items.
              Documentary proof of foreign origin is mandatory: contracts, invoices, evidence of services performed outside Panama, and bank flows.

              Box 5 - Deductible Costs and Expenses
              Ordinary and necessary expenses supported by invoices, payroll records, and transfer pricing documentation.
              Payments to non-resident related parties are deductible only if arm's length and substantiated; withholding certificates must be attached.
              Late filing: surcharge of 10% of the tax due plus default interest of 1% per month; false or incomplete returns: fines up to B/.10,000 and criminal sanctions where fraud is established; failure to file a zero return: administrative fine of B/.500 to B/.5,000.
            

            
            

              Box 6 - Taxable Net Income
              Net income after application of the 5-year loss carryforward.
              Non-residents without a permanent establishment do not file Form 100 for Panamanian-source fees; such fees are settled by final withholding.
              Late filing: surcharge of 10% of the tax due plus default interest of 1% per month; false or incomplete returns: fines up to B/.10,000 and criminal sanctions where fraud is established; failure to file a zero return: administrative fine of B/.500 to B/.5,000.

              Statutory tax on taxable net income (Box 6 multiplied by 25%).
              A foreign-source-only S.A. computes zero tax; a branch computes tax on Panamanian-source net income only.

              Net balance due to the DGIT or refund claimed.
              Payment is executed through the SIT portal; a zero-balance return must still be filed by every active S.A.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Aggregate gross income of B/.3,000,000 (USD 3,000,000) in the fiscal year triggers the mandatory transfer pricing information return and supporting Local File documentation (Articles 762-A to 762-G Fiscal Code, Law 47 of 2013); taxpayers below the threshold must still maintain contemporaneous arm's-length evidence when transacting with related parties or low-tax jurisdictions.

      
Economic substance scope: Panama has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to the UAE/BVI model; however, substance is indirectly enforced through (i) the territorial-source rules, which exclude foreign-source income only where real activity is evidenced; (ii) the beneficial-ownership registry under Law 70 of 2019; (iii) denial of treaty benefits for letterbox holding companies; and (iv) anti-abuse recharacterization where a Sociedad Anonima lacks staff, premises, and business purpose.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns (Defraudacion Tributaria)
        
Statutory sanction: Additional tax assessment at 25%, surcharge of 10% to 25% on the deficiency, monthly interest of 1%, and administrative fines up to B/.50,000; criminal conviction for tax fraud carries 2 to 5 years of imprisonment and forfeiture of the tax evaded.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false returns or order concealment of income are jointly and severally liable for the civil tax debt and may be imprisoned; the court may also prohibit exit from Panama (travel ban) while the criminal case is pending.

      

      
      

        Shell Company and Registered Agent Abuse (Alter Ego / Off-the-Shelf S.A.)
        
Statutory sanction: DGIT recharacterization of the interposed entity, reattribution of income to the ultimate shareholder, cancellation of the beneficial-ownership registration, and fines of B/.1,000 to B/.100,000 under Law 70 of 2019 for undisclosed ultimate beneficial owners.

        
Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the veil where the company is a mere instrumentality, imposing civil restitution on directors and/or the resident agent; concealment of assets in a criminal proceeding attracts personal imprisonment of up to 12 years and seizure of personal property.

      

      
      

        AML/CFT Breach (Money Laundering Predicate)
        
Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties of B/.10,000 to B/.5,000,000 and suspension or revocation of the statutory license; criminal asset laundering is punishable by 5 to 12 years of imprisonment with forfeiture of the laundered assets and auxiliary disqualification from commercial activity.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are personally indictable where they knowingly or recklessly facilitate laundering; precautionary measures include travel bans, freezing orders on personal bank accounts, and full civil restitution of laundered funds.
