Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); electronic portal: SICE (Sistema Informático de Cobro Electrónico) — includes the RUC registry, electronic invoicing module (E-Factura / Facturación Electrónica), and online filing (declaraciones); customs interface coordinated with the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) and its SICE-SADEX export declaration module.

E-government filing portal

A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) established under Law 25 of 1995 must first complete its registration at the Registro Público de Panamá (public deeds registry) with a duly appointed Registered Agent and Council (Consejo de la Fundación). If the PIF engages in any activity with Panamanian tax nexus, it must obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) by submitting Form RUC (Formulario de Inscripción), certified copy of the foundation charter and its public registry inscription, identity documentation of the Registered Agent and Councillors, proof of fiscal domicile in Panama, and appointment of a local CPA (Contador Público Autorizado) where accounting records are kept in Panama. Filing is performed online through the SICE portal or physically at DGI kolektoría offices; DGI issues the RUC certificate and activates the taxpayer's digital certificate (Firma Electrónica) enabling e-invoicing and electronic filing. A PIF conducting purely offshore dropshipping transactions (no Panama-source sales, no Panamanian employees, no local registered establishment) is not legally obliged to hold a RUC, but obtaining a voluntary RUC is the established practice for bank due diligence, platform merchant verification (Stripe/PayPal), and documentary proof of the territorial tax exclusion.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — Panama applies a strict territorial income tax system (Código Fiscal Art. 694): income, assets, and transfers sourced outside Panama are excluded from Panamanian taxation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — Panama applies a strict territorial income tax system (Código Fiscal Art. 694): income, assets, and transfers sourced outside Panama are excluded from Panamanian taxation. A PIF is a separate legal person, not a pass-through, but its foreign-source dropshipping commissions and gains are exempt from Panamanian income tax under Law 25 of 1995; the PIF is not a resident taxpayer for income tax purposes unless it develops a permanent establishment or derives Panama-source income (e.g., sales to Panamanian consumers, local fulfillment in Panama, local storage, or invoicing through a Panamanian branch). In that case it becomes an ordinary territorial taxpayer subject to 25% corporate income tax on Panama-source net income. The PIF is not eligible for 'tax-exempt zone' treatment (Colón Free Zone / Ciudad del Saber regime) unless physically established inside such zones.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists in Panama's domestic law. Available elections include: (i) voluntary registration with the RUC to document the territorial exclusion; (ii) election by the PIF's Council to conduct business through a Panamanian fiscal representative/agent for DGI compliance; (iii) election to qualify as a small taxpayer (Régimen Simplificado del Pequeño Contribuyente) where annual gross turnover does not exceed PAB 36,000; (iv) election to apply the export incentive regime for physical exports from Panama (e.g., re-export schemes using Zona Libre de Colón or the SEM regime) with the DGI registration of the exporter; (v) such electoral choices in the foundation bylaws as govern the distribution of taxable vs. non-taxable patrimony. Panama has not adopted OECD hybrid mismatch rules; Law 254 of 2021 (Economic Substance and BEPS-aligned transparency) merely imposes economic substance documentation and reporting obligations, not substitution of the PIF's legal identity or a check-the-box classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PAB 0.00 — no general de minimis turnover threshold: any person, including a non-resident foreign entity or PIF, that habitually carries out taxable transfers of goods or renders services within Panamanian territory is required to register for ITBMS and file monthly returns, regardless of volume. An optional simplified small-taxpayer regime (Régimen Simplificado del Pequeño Contribuyente) applies to taxable persons with annual gross income up to PAB 36,000 and provides a reduced compliance burden; however, this regime does not exempt a foreign e-commerce seller from the obligation to register where a Panamanian nexus (local customer base, Panamanian warehouse, or local platform account) exists. A purely automated offshore dropshipping operation with no Panamanian nexus falls outside the scope of ITBMS altogether. The standard registration threshold is therefore PAB 0.00; the operational threshold is PAB 36,000 only for the optional simplified regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Panama are zero-rated for ITBMS provided the exporter is a registered ITBMS taxpayer, the goods leave Panama under a customs export declaration processed by ANA, and the foreign currency proceeds are received by the exporter; the zero-rated treatment is evidenced on the e-invoice with the ITBMS 0% rate. Cross-border services rendered from Panama to non-resident beneficiaries (including digital distribution, software, and logistics services) are zero-rated where the services are effectively used or exploited outside Panama. For pure automated dropshipping in which goods flow directly from an offshore supplier to an offshore end-customer and the PIF only collects margins through Stripe/PayPal, no ITBMS is triggered because the transfer does not occur in Panamanian territory; zero-rating is only relevant (and available) when the goods physically transit through Panama, in which case the exporter must duly register, file the SICE-SADEX export declaration, and retain proof of export and payment. The PIF cannot reclaim ITBMS credits on purchases unrelated to a Panamanian taxable activity; refund claims are limited to excess input credits arising from Panama-source zero-rated exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación via SICE-SADEX / ANA VED system)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical departure from Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) or bank/PSP settlement statement proving receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice / Packing List issued by the PIF (or its agent) and matching the customs value
  • E-Invoice (Factura Electrónica) with ITBMS 0% rate and DGI authorization code

Mandatory electronic invoicing applies progressively to all RUC holders: taxpayers issue e-invoices through DGI-certified software or the free SICE web portal; each fiscal document requires real-time electronic authorization (Código de Autorización) generated by the DGI central validation gateway before the document is legally effective, with full JSON/XML schema validation, digital signature stamping, and archiving in the DGI's SICE repository. DGI Resolution 201-1665 of 2019, Resolution DGI 201-2880 of 2020, and successive resolutions govern the API integration requirements, the card-not-present payment certification, and the mandatory acceptance of e-invoices for ITBMS credit, export zero-rating, and customs formalities. Non-Ecuadorian foreign dropshipping merchants without a Panamanian establishment are not subject to the e-invoicing mandate; if, however, the PIF holds a voluntary RUC or conducts any Panama-source transfer, e-invoicing and real-time clearance are unconditionally mandatory even for the zero-rated export transactions.

DGI and ANA jointly verify export transactions, cross-checking customs declarations, e-invoices, freight documents, and bank remittances. If the exporter fails to repatriate the invoiced foreign currency through the national banking system within 180 calendar days from the date of shipment (or obtains a timely extension from DGI), the transaction is retrospectively reclassified as a domestic taxable transfer: the exporter is assessed the 7% ITBMS, plus monthly surcharge of 10% on arrears (maximum 100% of the tax), adjusted interest, and fines provided under Articles 712, 719, 729 and related provisions of the Código Fiscal. Fraudulent export substantiation (false customs declarations, fabricated bills of lading, fictitious inward remittance certificates, or artificially staged re-export arrangements) triggers immediate revocation of the exporter status, clawback of the ITBMS exemption for up to the full prescription period (generally five fiscal years), monetary penalties from PAB 500 to PAB 5,000, and criminal tax fraud liability carrying imprisonment of up to five years. Where the DGI proves that the PIF existed solely to obtain the export zero-rating benefit, the territorial protection of Law 25 of 1995 is disregarded under the general anti-abuse powers introduced by Law 254 of 2021, subjecting the underlying economic activity to standard territorial ITBMS and income tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates under full de jure and de facto capital and current account convertibility. The US dollar circulates as the exclusive paper legal tender, while the balboa exists solely as a fractional coin at a fixed 1:1 parity. There are no exchange-control laws, no repatriation restrictions, no currency surrender obligations, and no prior Central Bank authorization for cross-border fund movements. Profit remittances by a Private Interest Foundation (PIF) to non-resident council members or beneficiaries are freely permitted; Panama's territorial tax system imposes tax only on Panama-source income, and outbound remittances of previously untaxed offshore earnings are not subject to withholding or exchange approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rules exist in Panama. Exporters and digital-commerce intermediaries are not required to convert export receipts into local currency (there is no managed exchange rate), nor to remit proceeds to a nationalized account within a fixed timeline. All proceeds derived by a PIF from automated dropshipping networks may be retained in USD domiciled abroad or in Panama with no repatriation obligation. Compliance focuses instead on documentary support of the underlying commercial transactions under SBP AML/CFT Regulation and FATF-aligned beneficial ownership standards.

Central bank supervision: Republic of Panama does not maintain a traditional central bank; full dollarization (USD legal tender) provides monetary stability. Banking examination, AML/CFT oversight, licensing, and compliance enforcement are vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). Fiscal agency and treasury-clearing functions are performed by Banco Nacional de Panamá (National Bank of Panama). No separate foreign-exchange administration exists; the Ministry of Economy and Finance sets no exchange-control policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM with conservative legal engineering. Panama does not maintain a standalone Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime comparable to UK/Pillar EBA; non-bank payment services generally require SBP authorization under the Panama Payment System Law (Ley 99 de 2020) and adherence to SBP AML/CFT Rule 2028. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-authorised, Lithuania-licensed) may service a Panama PIF but must not establish a physical branch in Panama without a banking or payment-service license; the foundation should structure treasury flows directly to a correspondent USD account in Panama and avoid having an unlicensed EMI act as a UI-collection gateway for Stripe/PayPal settlement if those flows would amount to a regulated payment business onshore. Legal constraint: PIF bank account title must reflect the foundation's full name (Foundation Name, Private Interest Foundation); use of an unlicensed intermediary for payment routing materially elevates AML risk and may trigger SBP Article 27 reporting obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection to the PIF should be executed by SWIFT MT103 from the settlor's corporate or high-net-worth bank account to the PIF's Panamanian USD operational account at a Tier-1 bank. Recommended purpose code/remittance reference: 'CONTB / CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO PRIVATE INTEREST FOUNDATION PA_PIF' — Panama does not impose mandatory central bank purpose codes; however, include the standard ancillary code 'TRAK' for tracking, and avoid the code 'GIFT'. The initiating bank must complete robust verification protocols: match the sending legal name against the foundation charter, validate the beneficial ownership declaration, confirm the absence of sanctions and PEP hits, and in the event of an inbound of USD 100,000 or more, the receiving bank will apply SBP Rule 2028 enhanced EDD procedures. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM with conservative legal engineering. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is required when annual related-party transactions exceed USD 250,000; Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds USD 50 million. A transfer pricing informative return must also be filed where related-party transactions meet the applicable threshold.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not operate a standalone schedule of Economic Substance Regulations for PIFs in the offshore-holding context, but substance is tested through Panama's beneficial ownership registry under Law 254 of 2021, DGI anti-abuse examinations, and foreign DTA limitation-of-benefits clauses. A dropshipping PIF must demonstrate local registered agency, decision-making records, a Panama bank account, and real economic activity to defend territorial tax treatment and treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misdeclared Source of Income

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of unpaid tax plus 100% surcharge, monthly recalculated interest, monetary fines, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud with imprisonment of up to 5 years or more for aggravated evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and foundation council members who sign false returns may be jointly and severally liable for civil restitution; criminal conviction can trigger personal fines, travel restrictions during proceedings, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: DGI may disregard the PIF's separate legal personality, recharacterize worldwide income as Panamanian-source, deny treaty benefits, impose 100% penalty on the omitted tax, and invalidate transactions with retroactive effect.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil for fraud or abuse of the PIF's legal form, imposing direct restitution obligations on foundation council members and authorizing asset freezes and exit bans during criminal investigation.
AML / Payment Rails Abuse

Statutory sanction: Penalties under Panama's AML Law 23 of 2015 range from USD 10,000 to USD 1,000,000 per violation, plus license suspension for payment intermediaries and criminal liability for money-laundering offenses with imprisonment from 5 to 12 years.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members and authorized signatories can face personal criminal liability, civil confiscation of proceeds, travel bans, and imprisonment for knowingly structuring payments to avoid transparency thresholds.
Transfer Pricing / Related-Party Mispricing

Statutory sanction: DGI may adjust the transaction to the arm's length price, assess the 25% corporate tax on the adjustment, add monthly surcharges and interest, and impose TP documentation penalties of up to 40% of the adjusted amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or fail to challenge non-arm's length related-party contracts may be held liable for restitution of the resulting tax deficiency and may be subject to criminal charges if the pricing scheme is found to be fraudulent.
