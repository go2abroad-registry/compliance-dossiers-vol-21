Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Law No. 25 of 1995 prescribes no minimum statutory paid-up capital; the Foundation Charter must declare a real and quantified initial patrimony, commonly between USD 10.00 and USD 10,000.00. No mandatory escrow is imposed by statute, but incorporating counsel customarily holds the initial patrimony in a segregated trust/escrow account of the Foundation Council and delivers a written discharge receipt as registration evidence.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministry of Economy and Finance, at the taxpayer portal 'Sede Electrónica del Contribuyente' (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must be validly incorporated and registered in the Panamanian Public Registry and maintain a resident agent. For tax registration, the PIF files an application with the DGI using Form 600 (Registro Único de Contribuyente), attaching the foundation charter, Public Registry filing certificate, resident agent acceptance, registered commercial domicile, and resolution by the Foundation Council identifying the founder and beneficiaries. The DGI issues a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) for income tax and ITBMS purposes, and the foundation must register for e-invoicing/fiscal receipt obligations before commencing commercial activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide US-style check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch elections under domestic law. The PIF is a distinct legal entity taxed on its Panamanian-source income. A PIF may elect to be administered under simplified reporting rules for foreign-source assets and is not required or permitted to elect pass-through treatment for income tax; any founder/beneficiary attribution rules apply only to Panamanian-source distributions under the Tax Code. Group consolidation and accelerated depreciation elections exist for domestic corporate groups, but no elective transparency classification is available for PIFs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general exemption threshold for ITBMS registration; any person engaging in taxable transfers of goods or taxable services in Panama, or importing goods/rendering services subject to the tax, must register. A simplified small-taxpayer regime applies to natural persons whose annual gross turnover does not exceed B/.36,000, allowing reduced compliance obligations rather than exemption from ITBMS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS is zero-rated for goods physically exported from Panamanian customs territory, provided the export is directed to a foreign destination and is supported by a valid customs export declaration. Services are zero-rated when rendered in Panama but effectively utilized outside Panama, performed for a non-resident recipient who derives no economic benefit from Panamanian consumption, or expressly classified as an exported service under DGI criteria. For a general re-invoicing intermediary, income from goods that never enter Panamanian territory falls outside the territorial scope of income tax; ITBMS also does not attach to foreign-to-foreign transactions when the service is economically consumed abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Panama has adopted a mandatory electronic invoicing and fiscal clearance regime administered by the DGI under the 'Factura Electrónica de Panamá' (FEP) framework, developed through Executive Decree No. 119 of 13 July 2020 and subsequent DGI resolutions. Affected taxpayers must issue invoices in XML through an authorized e-invoicing system integrated with the DGI platform, transmit invoice data in near real time via the SIFE (Sistema de Facturación Electrónica), and obtain a digital authorization/stamp from the DGI before issuance. The mandate is phased by taxpayer category, and exporters or large taxpayers must comply with API/web-service integration, digital signature, and DGI clearance requirements for every invoice.

Export zero-rating is subject to documentary audit by DGI and the National Customs Authority. False documentation, simulated exports, or indirect reuse of zero-rated goods in the domestic market triggers retroactive ITBMS liability, plus monthly interest, surcharges of up to 50% of the tax evaded, and administrative fines of up to B/.50,000. Directors and resident agents may be jointly liable where the exporter acts with intent to evade. Criminal sanctions under the Panamanian Tax Criminal Law (Law 50 of 2017) include imprisonment for fiscal fraud; the clawback period is generally extended to six years from the date the false declaration was filed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama has full convertibility on both current and capital account, with no exchange control law and no distinction between resident and non-resident accounts. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, and capital repatriation are freely permitted, subject only to applicable Panamanian taxes (e.g., territorial income tax) and anti-money laundering reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirements exist. Exporters and trading intermediaries may freely retain, use, or remit export proceeds in US dollars without mandatory conversion or repatriation timelines, though commercial banks apply standard reportable transaction thresholds to the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF) for AML/CFT purposes.

Central bank supervision: No central bank; Panama operates a dollarized currency system under the US dollar as legal tender. Banking supervision is vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). The Ministry of Economy and Finance oversees fiscal policy but does not administer exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Panama is not a licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) regime, and offshore EMI accounts (e.g., UK/EU e-money platforms) generally restrict onboarding of Private Interest Foundations due to the lack of public beneficial ownership and the inherent use of re-invoicing trading structures. Such accounts are viable only as ancillary payment wallets for low-volume receivables, not as primary treasury rails, and may require a maintained link to a domestic bank account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when cross-border related-party transactions exceed US$3 million in the fiscal year; Master File is required when the worldwide group revenue exceeds US$50 million. A transfer pricing disclosure (Form 820-TF) must be filed if aggregate related-party transactions exceed US$250,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama endorses the OECD Inclusive Framework and Law 120 of 2018 codifies substance-over-form and transfer pricing rules with automatic CFC-style adjustments for no-substance shell entities. Although Panama has no BVI-style ESR, the Tax Administration may disregard a PIF's separate juridical personality if it lacks physical premises, payroll, bank accounts, and board minutes evidencing core re-invoicing functions in Panama. A re-invoicing hub with no operational substance is recharacterized as a sham and assessed on gross revenue rather than net margin.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% to 200% of the understated tax (Article 605-A Tax Code); criminal conviction under Article 169 of the Penal Code carries 3 to 7 years imprisonment and a fine of USD 10,000 to USD 100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax deficiency; the DGI may request preventive travel bans, freeze personal accounts, and confiscate personal property under the statutory clawback procedure.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment adds back up to 25% tax on the adjusted profit; secondary penalties of 10% to 20% of the transaction amount are imposed under Law 120/2018, plus interest at 10% per annum. Habitual non-compliance triggers loss of all tax incentives and a mandatory audit for 5 years.

Director personal liability: Board members who approved transfer pricing policies without financial analysis can be assessed personally for the secondary penalty; serious disgorgement orders may be entered, and directors can be disqualified from serving on any Panamanian company for 5 years.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the PIF as a transparent entity; the full invoice value is treated as taxable income, subject to 25% corporate income tax plus 10% dividend withholding on the deemed distribution; registration may be cancelled after three consecutive defaults of the annual substance declaration. Fines of up to USD 100,000 under Law 120/2018 for failure to maintain economic substance records.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for taxes recharacterized on the shell entity and may face civil restitution claims from creditors or foreign revenue authorities; the Panamanian registry may impose a travel ban and place the director's name on the 'Compliance Risk List' for 5 years.
Anti-Money Laundering / Trade Based Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under Decree 196/2019 and Law 41/2000 range from USD 10,000 to USD 1,000,000 per violation; criminal liability under Law 201/2017 for trade-based money laundering applies, with imprisonment of 5 to 12 years and confiscation of the instrumentalities and proceeds up to triple the laundered value.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to implement robust KYC, invoice inspection, and source-of-funds verification are criminally liable under the corporate criminal veil-Piercing doctrine; they may receive travel bans, asset freezes, and 5 to 10 year prison sentences. Civil damages are recoverable from their estates in favor of victims or the Panamanian Financial Unit.
