Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá – Sección Mercantil (Mercantile Section of the Public Registry of Panama), including the Panamá Pacifico Special Economic Zone office for registered-user expedited filings.

Governing statute

Ley General de Sociedades (Law No. 32 of 1927), as amended by Law 5 of 2017; Articles 1 to 20 (incorporation, capital, and administration of Sociedad Anónima), Articles 21-29 (registered agent requirements), Article 58 (foreign shareholders' rights), complemented by Law 52 of 2016 on due diligence of corporate service providers.

Formation / constitutional filings

Escritura Pública de Constitución (Public Deed of Incorporation) containing the Pacto Social y Estatutos, submitted via the 'Testimonio de la Escritura Pública' to the Registro Público for inscription.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 100 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital required under Law 32 of 1927; the Company may declare a nominal authorized capital (commonly USD 10,000 divided into 10,000 shares of USD 1.00 par value each) with only the subscribed shares reflected. No escrow or deposit account is mandated by Panamanian law; the resident agent maintains the share ledger and records actual paid-in contributions. The first annual 'Tasa Única' franchise tax of USD 300 becomes due upon registration.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain the 'Dictamen de Seguridad, Protección Civil y Prevención de Incendio' from the Sistema Nacional de Protección Civil (SINAPROC), issued after an on-site inspection applying NFPA 101 life-safety codes, fire-extinguisher placement, emergency-exit marking, and evacuation-plan testing. Secure structural-load clearance from the municipal 'Dirección de Obras' or a certified structural engineer for any laboratory or storage fit-out. Occupational health and safety clearance is required from the Ministry of Labor (MITRADEL) under the Resolution No. 44 of 1986 and Law 11 of 1975, evidenced by an occupational risk assessment (e.g., chemical hazard mapping, respiratory protection program, annual workplace medical surveillance), a safety-trainer certificate, and the workers' compensation registration with the Caja de Seguro Social (CSS).

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance of Panama; online portal 'Sede Electrónica de la DGI' (https://etax.gob.pa) for registration and fiscal compliance.

E-government filing portal

The foreign entity forms a Sociedad Anónima in Panama and appoints a resident agent. The notarized public deed is filed with the Public Registry of Panama to obtain corporate identification. The resident agent or local counsel then applies for the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) via the DGI Sede Electrónica using the public deed, corporate aviso, identification card of the resident agent, and proof of registered address. A provisional or definitive RUC is issued; the company must also register for ITBMS (VAT) if engaged in taxable activities, obtain an electronic signature and digital certificate for e-filing, and, if employing staff, register with the Caja de Seguro Social (CSS). No mandatory paid-in capital is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope. Panama S.A. is a domestic-tax-resident juridical person taxable only on income derived from Panamanian-source; foreign-source income is exempt.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists under Panamanian law. Domestic affiliated companies may jointly elect consolidated income tax reporting to the extent qualifying under the Tax Code; eligible groups may elect the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime or Tax-Free Zone regime; small taxpayers may elect the simplified ITBMS (Régimen Simplificado) below annual turnover thresholds.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.07%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold for registration — every natural or juridical person performing taxable transfers of goods or services within the national territory, including implicitly via digital platforms, must register for ITBMS. A simplified small-taxpayer regime is available to businesses with gross annual receipts not exceeding USD 300,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of tangible goods and to services rendered to natural or juridical persons not domiciled in Panama where the benefit or use of the service occurs entirely abroad. Clinical trial management (CRO) services performed for a non-resident sponsor will qualify when the protocol, data analysis, and results are delivered outside Panama and no link to a Panamanian local beneficiary exists. Services physically provided inside Panama but 'consumed abroad' are zero-rated only if the supplier maintains evidence of non-residency of the purchaser and offshore beneficial use; otherwise they remain subject to 7% ITBMS.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / DUA through SICE system) for any export of physical goods, including biological samples or trial kits
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing shipment from Panama to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or transfer advice from a Panamanian licensed bank showing the foreign-currency payment received from the foreign principal for the zero-rated service

Panama is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing ('Facturación Electrónica'): authorized business taxpayers must issue standardized e-invoices through DGI-approved software and digitally transmit each invoice to the DGI's Sede Electrónica for real-time fiscal validation; the DGI assigns a unique authentication stamp ('número de validación/seguridad') before the invoice is deemed compliant. The mandate is phased, initially covering large taxpayers; smaller and newly registered taxpayers may use the DGI's free web filing tool. API integration with certified fiscal printers or software is required under current technical specifications, and non-compliant invoices are inadmissible as tax credits.

The DGI treats unwarranted zero-rating as tax evasion: the taxpayer is liable for the omitted 7% ITBMS plus monthly surcharge interest (approximately 1.5% per month), fines equal to between 50% and 200% of the omitted tax depending on culpability, and potential clawback of refunds for up to six fiscal years (extended to ten years where fraud is proven). Criminal prosecution under Panama's Tax Procedure Code may apply for false documentation, with penalties including monetary fines and potential imprisonment for executives. The statute of limitations is suspended during administrative or judicial inspection.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama uses the United States dollar as legal tender (balboa coins at parity, non-issuing). Both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible with no exchange-control restrictions. Capital and profit remittances are unrestricted; dividend distributions are not subject to withholding tax under the territorial tax system. The only practical hurdles are standard AML/KYC verification and banking compliance for fund-origin documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion requirement. Export and service proceeds, including clinical trial milestone payments, may be freely retained, held, and disposed in USD or any other currency without mandatory central bank reporting or conversion timelines.

Central bank supervision: Banco Nacional de Panamá (fiscal agent) and Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as banking regulator; Panama does not maintain a monetary policy central bank or exchange control authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low direct viability. Panama does not recognize the EU-style digital EMI licence; electronic money operations require a banking or finance-company license from the SBP. An offshore EMI may act only as a payment intermediary and would require a local trust or corporate-services arrangement with a licensed bank. For the CRO, prudent treasury practice is to use SWIFT-connected licensed Panamanian banks rather than offshore digital entities.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (Impuesto sobre la Renta, ISR)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% withholding on dividends paid from profits subject to ISR; 10% withholding on dividends paid from profits exempt or not taxed at the entity level. Distributions of foreign-source income are outside Panamanian taxation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File are required if the taxpayer's Panamanian gross revenue exceeds USD 3,000,000 or related-party transactions exceed USD 1,000,000 during the fiscal year. A shorter informative transfer pricing return is required for related-party transactions exceeding USD 250,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations under Law 254 of 2021 apply to entities that are tax-resident in Panama and carry on relevant activities, including distribution and service centers. A clinical trial CRO operating a Panama S.A. must maintain a physical office, qualified local staff, and actual core income-generating activities (project management, monitoring, data control) in Panama; failure to do so triggers submission of an annual ESR return, fines for false declarations, and the potential recharacterization of income under the DGI.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalty equal to 50% to 100% of the evaded tax, monthly interest of 1.5%, and criminal penalties under Panama's tax and criminal codes, with imprisonment of 3 to 6 years for intentional evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and signing officers who approve or file false returns lose limited liability protection; they are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and fines, may be subjected to travel bans during criminal investigation, and face imprisonment upon conviction.
Shell Company With No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR legislation imposes fines from USD 10,000 for failure to file an annual substance return, increasing to USD 1,000,000 for false substance certification; the DGI may also discard the entity's separate existence for tax purposes and assess deemed dividends.

Director personal liability: Directors and resident agents are held personally liable for substance-related fines, the corporate veil is pierced where the entity is a mere alter ego, and officers face de facto director liability, travel restrictions, and civil restitution to creditors.
AML/CFT Violations in Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: Under Panama's AML Law (Ley 23 de 2015), entities face fines up to USD 10,000,000 for serious institutional violations; individuals convicted of money laundering face 5 to 12 years' imprisonment, asset forfeiture, and legal disqualification.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct such transactions are individually punishable; courts can issue travel bans and freezing orders against personal assets, and civil liability extends to full restitution of laundered or fraudulent proceeds.
Unlicensed Clinical Trial Operations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to USD 100,000 per violation, suspension and potential cancellation of the commercial license, seizure of trial records, and criminal penalties for aggravated illegal practice of health-related professions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly operate without the proper license are personally liable for damages to clinical trial subjects; under Panama law, they may receive criminal sentences of up to 5 years and are subject to civil claims without the protection of the corporate veil.
