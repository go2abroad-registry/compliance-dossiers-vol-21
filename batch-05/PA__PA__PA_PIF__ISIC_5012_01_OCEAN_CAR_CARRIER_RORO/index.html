Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) of the Republic of Panama, operating through the Sede Electrónica e-tax portal at etax.mef.gob.pa. Panama is a unitary state; no sub-national income tax agency exists for central tax collection purposes.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Ensure the Private Interest Foundation (PIF) is validly constituted and recorded: the Pacto Fundacional must be inscribed with the Public Registry of Panama (Registro Público), together with the notarized appointment of officers and the registered resident foundation agent. Step 2: Obtain a Panama digital signature certificate (Firma Electrónica / token) issued by the National Authority for Government Innovation (AIG) for the resident agent or an authorized representative. Step 3: Register the PIF in the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) through the DGI Sede Electrónica, filing the foundation charter, officers resolution, resident agent acceptance, AML/KYC data of founders and beneficiaries, and a physical address or mailing address in Panama. Step 4: If the RoRo vessel is Panamanian-flagged, complete the vessel registration and obtain the Navigation Patent (Patente de Navegación) from the Panama Maritime Authority (AMP); the flat annual navigation tax is paid through AMP, which in turn coordinates with the DGI, so no separate income tax registration is triggered for international shipping profits. Step 5: Activate the ITBMS ledger within the taxpayer account and request DGI authorization of the electronic invoicing series before any Panamanian taxable supply is made. A municipal Aviso de Operación is required only if the PIF maintains a physical commercial establishment in a Panamanian municipality; a purely offshore PIF holding a vessel does not require it.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — A Panama Private Interest Foundation (Law 25 of 1995) is a separate legal person, not a pass-through vehicle, but the Republic taxes only income sourced within Panamanian territory (Código Fiscal, Artículo 694). Income derived from international ocean RoRo cross-trade, including freight, charter hire, demurrage, and ancillary shipping revenues earned outside Panama, is outside the Panamanian income tax base. A Panamanian-flagged vessel engaged in international voyages is additionally subject to the flat annual navigation tax (Law 8 of 2011), which substitutes for any income tax on the vessel's international operations. The PIF is not located in a statutory tax-exempt zone; its favorable position flows from territorial sourcing, the flat navigation tax regime, and the absence of any Panamanian-source revenue. The PIF remains subject to non-tax registry compliance fees, including the quarterly Tasa Única de Empresas introduced by Law 129 of 2020, to the extent applicable to foundations.

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; the tax treatment follows the juridical form of the PIF and the source of income. Material available mechanisms: (1) Flat navigation tax election: a vessel owner may apply the annual per-net-tonnage navigation tax under Law 8 of 2011 in lieu of any territorial income tax attributable to international shipping profits; the election is exercised at AMP registration and evidenced in the DGI annual filing. (2) DGI Tax Residence Certificate: the PIF may apply for an official tax residence certificate through the Sede Electrónica to evidence Panamanian residence for banking, reinsurance, and international administrative purposes. (3) Small-taxpayer simplified ITBMS regime: election available for taxpayers with annual gross receipts at or below the approximate USD 36,000 threshold, subject to current DGI indexation. (4) Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007 if the foundation later establishes qualifying Panamanian management, which is not activated in this file. (5) There is no domestic hybrid-mismatch election; Panama's territorial system generally eliminates hybrid mismatches with respect to shipping income, and the PIF is not subject to CFC or controlled-foreign-entity rules for its foreign-flag or Panamanian-flag international fleet income.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully dollarized economy: the U.S. dollar circulates as legal tender alongside the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) pegged at 1:1; capital and current account transactions are fully convertible with no exchange-control law, no approval or declaration requirement for profit remittance, and no restriction on outflow of dividends, interest, royalties, or foundation capital. Balboa paper currency is not issued; only 1:1 coinage is minted, and no central-bank reserve regime constrains cross-border transfers. Treasury exposures in non-USD currencies (CNY, JPY, EUR, KRW) must be cleared offshore or through international banking correspondent rails.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panama imposes no statutory timeline for export-receipt repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio. RoRo ocean-freight proceeds may remain offshore or be freely cleared into the PIF's Panamanian account without conversion into Balboa and without any FX filing to the SBP. The only binding obligations are the UAF cash-transaction report for amounts above USD 10,000 and general AML/CFT surveillance derived from Law 23 of 2015.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) — Panamanian banking regulator and national foreign-exchange/financial-information supervisor; Panama has no central bank issuing local fiat. Banco Nacional de Panamá serves as fiscal agent for the State, and Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) operates as the Financial Intelligence Unit for AML/CFT FX-reporting functions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Moderate as a supplementary clearing rail; materially lower as a primary repository. Panama lacks a dedicated EMI license comparable to the EU EMI Directive; SBP regulates fintech activity under Decree Law 2 of 2008 and Law 23 of 2015. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Brex, Wise Business) are not recognized depository institutions in Panama and generally require a Panamanian licensed bank partner or a foreign banking group license to hold funds; they are therefore unsuitable as the sole corporate account for ISIC 5012 RoRo charter-hire settlements that require full SWIFT MT103 and correspondent-bank capture. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on Panama-source taxable income; 0% on non-Panama-source income under Panama's territorial income tax system and the Private Interest Foundation (PIF) exemption.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on distributions made from profits that have been subject to Panamanian corporate income tax; 15.5% on distributions from profits that have not been taxed in Panama, such as foreign-source shipping income of the PIF.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within three calendar months after fiscal year-end; for a calendar-year taxpayer (31 December), the deadline is 31 March of the following year.

Late filing fines range from USD 50 to USD 5,000; late payment triggers a 10% surcharge plus monthly interest (approximately 1.25% per month or fraction thereof); persistent non-filing may lead to presumptive assessment and loss of good-standing.
Box 4 - Net Taxable Income Panama-source gross revenue minus allowed deductions. For a purely offshore PIF, net taxable income is zero; for any Panama-source shipping services, apply the normal territorial calculation. Late filing fines range from USD 50 to USD 5,000; late payment triggers a 10% surcharge plus monthly interest (approximately 1.25% per month or fraction thereof); persistent non-filing may lead to presumptive assessment and loss of good-standing.
Box 5 - Tax Payable Apply the 25% corporate rate to net taxable income, less prepayments and withholding credits. If net taxable income is zero, enter zero; if the PIF uses the tonnage-tax election, report the tonnage tax amount in the appropriate supplementary schedule instead of the 25% rate. Late filing fines range from USD 50 to USD 5,000; late payment triggers a 10% surcharge plus monthly interest (approximately 1.25% per month or fraction thereof); persistent non-filing may lead to presumptive assessment and loss of good-standing.
Box 6 - Balance Due / Credit Final amount of tax due or overpayment to be credited. If all income is foreign-source, the final balance is zero; any prior monthly prepayments for Panama-source tax may be claimed as credits. Late filing fines range from USD 50 to USD 5,000; late payment triggers a 10% surcharge plus monthly interest (approximately 1.25% per month or fraction thereof); persistent non-filing may lead to presumptive assessment and loss of good-standing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 5,000,000 (Panamanian balboas / USD equivalent) in gross annual revenue, combined with related-party transactions exceeding B/. 1,000,000, triggers mandatory Local File and Master File documentation.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Law 254 of 2019 and implementing decrees apply Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) to entities engaged in Relevant Activities, including shipping, pure equity holding, IP holding, and distribution and service centers. A PIF operating an international RoRo vessel must maintain a real and adequate presence in Panama by demonstrating local management, employees, physical premises, and core income-generating activities conducted in or from Panama, and must file the annual economic substance declaration with the DGI.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud penalties under Panama's fiscal and penal laws: fines of USD 50,000 to USD 500,000 and imprisonment from 2 to 5 years; civil liability for the undeclared tax plus a 10% surcharge and monthly interest.

Director personal liability: Directors, foundation council members, and the registered agent may be criminally prosecuted, subjected to travel bans and asset freezes, and held jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of evaded taxes.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DGI transfer pricing adjustments resulting in additional 25% corporate income tax plus a 10% surcharge; fines for missing documentation range from USD 10,000 to USD 50,000; intentional false documentation may attract criminal penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign or approve false TP returns can be held personally liable for underpaid taxes and may face criminal sanctions where the documentary falsehood constitutes tax fraud.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic substance fines of USD 10,000 to USD 100,000 per fiscal year for non-compliance or false substance declarations; possible deregistration of the foundation and loss of the Panamanian flag/vessel registry privileges.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members and the resident agent may be disqualified, fined, and barred from serving as registered agents; directors willfully filing false substance declarations are exposed to criminal liability for false documentation.
Money Laundering and AML/CFT Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Panama's AML regime (Law 23 of 2015 and Penal Code provisions), penalties include imprisonment from 5 to 12 years, fines up to USD 5 million, asset confiscation, and administrative fines up to double the value of the illicit transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors, trustees, and ultimate beneficial owners face personal criminal indictment, asset freezing, international travel bans, extradition, and civil confiscation; the corporate veil of the PIF is pierced when it is used as an instrument of illicit conduct.
Non-Payment of Annual PIF Fee and Registry Abuses

Statutory sanction: Suspension and eventual cancellation of the foundation's legal personality; financial penalties up to USD 10,000 plus the accumulated annual fees; loan and banking facilities are immediately jeopardized.

Director personal liability: The resident agent is answerable to the DGI and Superintendency of Banks; foundation council members can be held liable for civil restitution to third parties for injuries caused by the false certification.
