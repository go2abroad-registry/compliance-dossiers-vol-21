Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The US dollar is legal tender in Panama; the Panamanian balboa circulates only as a unit equivalent at 1:1 with the US dollar but has no independent paper supply. Full convertibility exists on both current account and capital account transactions. Profit remittance, capital repatriation and dividend distributions are free from prior exchange approvals, reserve requirements, or statutory conversion restrictions. Banks apply only ordinary AML/KYC due diligence and may report wire transfers meeting statutory thresholds to the Financial Analysis Unit (Unidad de Análisis Financiero).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement for export proceeds in Panama. Exporters are not required to repatriate or convert foreign currency within a specified timeline. Cross-dock and third-party logistics revenue in USD or other foreign currencies may be retained in overseas settlement accounts or credited freely to Panamanian dollar-bearing accounts.

Central bank supervision: Panama does not operate a conventional central bank. Monetary and banking oversight is allocated to the Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá), with Banco Nacional de Panamá acting as fiscal agent and clearing channel for public deposits and USD-denominated liquidity. Exchange policy and coordination are facilitated through the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Superintendency of Banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-to-High for payment collection, virtual account issuance, and supplier settlement through cross-border electronic money institutions such as Airwallex, provided settlement is ultimately routed through a licensed Panamanian bank. Legal constraints include the absence of an independent electronic-money institution license regime in Panama; digital payment and wallet services are typically structured through supervised bank partnerships. An EMI is appropriate as an operational clearing rail, not as a substitute for the primary Panamanian bank account holding capital escrow and operational treasury.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Offshore PIF with undisclosed beneficial owners or nominee council members

Root cause: Offshore PIF with undisclosed beneficial owners or nominee council members

Defense evidence: Complete foundation deed and charter; updated registered beneficial ownership declaration; registered agent sworn certificate identifying founder and council members; apostilled passport pages of all economically exposed individuals; Public Registry certificate of foundation existence; corporate organizational chart demonstrating non-resident ownership legality.
Lack of demonstrable economic substance in cross-dock and overseas 3PL operations

Root cause: Lack of demonstrable economic substance in cross-dock and overseas 3PL operations

Defense evidence: Signed Panama warehouse lease or sublease; cross-dock equipment inventory; third-party logistics agreements with e-commerce merchants; ISIC 5210 and NAICS 493110 business definition; projected shipment volumes; customs broker engagement letter; payroll or local contractor records if staff are present in Panama.
Unusual expected wire pattern with high cross-border velocity and repetitive USD flows

Root cause: Unusual expected wire pattern with high cross-border velocity and repetitive USD flows

Defense evidence: Treasury policy memo describing stage-4 clearing rails; platform settlement statements from e-commerce marketplaces; customer and supplier lists; written board resolution naming authorized signatories; bank reference letter from existing financiers; transaction volume forecast with monthly reconciliation methodology; AML compliance manual and transaction monitoring responsibility matrix.
Trade and sanctions exposure from international shipping lanes and multimodal logistics

Root cause: Trade and sanctions exposure from international shipping lanes and multimodal logistics

Defense evidence: OFAC/sanctions screening policy; denied-party screening reports; bill of lading and cargo manifests for representative shipments; commercial invoices and packing lists; shipping carrier compliance terms; trade transaction review log; sworn certification that no transacting counterparty is a sanctioned person or entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection should be executed by SWIFT MT103 in USD from the shareholder or affiliate treasury to the Panamanian PIF's capital escrow account at a Tier-1 bank. The wire settlement details must state 'Capital contribution – Private Interest Foundation' and reference the approved board resolution. No central bank authorization is required, but the receiving bank will conduct source-of-funds verification; supporting documents include the capital contribution subscription, foundation deed, Public Registry certificate, and identity documents of the remitter. Wires above USD 25,000 may require a sworn source-of-funds statement. Remitter and beneficiary legal names must match bank KYC records exactly; use the same legal name in the ordering bank and beneficiary bank data fields to avoid exception processing.Outbound disbursement rails are structured as follows: supplier and freight payments settle via SWIFT MT103 with clearly populated beneficiary financial institution, account number and SWIFT/BIC codes; larger purchase order transactions may use commercial letters of credit under UCP600 issued by a Tier-1 Panamanian bank or a corresponding international bank; local operational costs such as warehousing labor and trucking are paid through Panama's local electronic clearing system aligned with Local_ACH rails; Airwallex virtual accounts are used for segregated merchant receivable collection and vendor payout in supported jurisdictions. Late filing: fine of USD 50 to USD 5,000 per declaration; late payment: statutory 10% recargo on the unpaid tax plus 1% monthly interest; omitted revenue triggered by inspection: fine of 10% of the omitted tax; repeated non-filing permits DGI ex officio assessment on presumed income and closure of the establishment; wilful evasion is referred to the criminal courts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB/USD 1,000,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions triggers the mandatory Form 190 TP information return and Local File documentation; when related-party transactions exceed PAB/USD 3,000,000, a full TP study should be contemporaneously prepared and retained; Master File documentation is recommended for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above OECD thresholds though Panama has not separately codified the Master File.

Economic substance scope: Panama's territorial system is reinforced by the Economic Substance Legislation (Law 254 of 2021) requiring taxpayers earning Panamanian-source income from relevant activities - including distribution and logistics services - to maintain local premises, adequate employees, operational direction, and actual activity in Panama proportionate to revenue. A PIF operating the ISIC_5210 cross-dock and overseas fulfilment hub in Panama must show warehouse premises, local staff, payroll, and management decisions in Panama; a letterbox PIF with no local substance risks recharacterization of revenue, denial of deductions, fines up to PAB/USD 100,000, and reassessment of up to five years. The USD 300 annual foundation fee does not by itself constitute economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Tributaria)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of one to three times the evaded tax plus imprisonment of up to five years; civil reassessment with the 10% recargo, 1% monthly interest, and disallowance of deductions for the fiscal period; DGI closure of the warehouse facility for repeated defaults.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members, the founder-beneficiary, and the fiscal representative are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of evaded taxes and surcharges; criminal conviction carries travel bans, asset freezes, and surrender of passports; the foundation veil is pierced as a sham.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure (Law 254 of 2021)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to PAB/USD 100,000; loss of all deductions and incentive regimes; re-assessment under presumed-income rules for up to five years; cancellation of the foundation registration for persistent default.

Director personal liability: Veil piercing permits collection from the founder's personal assets; registered agents and resident directors face professional sanctions and fines for certifying false substance declarations; the DGI may refer the case to the Public Ministry for false-tax-return offences.
Money Laundering / AML Breach (Law 23 of 2015, as amended)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 5 to 10 years (up to 15 in aggravated cases), confiscation of the laundered assets and ancillary profits, fines of up to double the value of the funds, plus administrative sanctions imposed by the UAF and the Superintendency of Banks.

Director personal liability: Real beneficial owners and foundation council members face arrest warrants, travel bans, and extradition exposure; the foundation's separate personality is disregarded for laundering instruments; directors executing suspicious transactions with knowledge are prosecuted as accomplices and ordered to pay civil restitution.
