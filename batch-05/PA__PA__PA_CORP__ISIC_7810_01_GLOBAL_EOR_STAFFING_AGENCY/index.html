Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); electronic portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Panama Sociedad Anónima must first appoint a resident agent and register the corporation with the Public Registry. The company must then obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) from DGI, file an Aviso de Operación (business commencement notice), and register as employer with the Caja de Seguridad Social (CSS). For indirect tax purposes, the same RUC is used for ITBMS returns; application is made through authorized public accountants or via the DGI e-platform, requiring certified incorporation documents, resident agent appointment, bylaws, and identity documents of officers/shareholders.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Panama applies territoriality as the default statutory rule; companies may elect the Simplified Tax Regime for small taxpayers if annual gross income does not exceed the legal threshold, and may separately elect the Panama Fiscal Unit (PFU) settlement method for consolidation purposes. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; transfer pricing documentation and advance pricing agreements apply for cross-border related-party transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic turnover exemption threshold in B/.; any person habitually engaged in taxable transfers of goods or services, imports, or construction contracts must register for ITBMS. A simplified small-taxpayer regime is available to businesses with annual gross income up to B/.300,000, allowing reduced compliance obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of tangible goods qualifies for 0% ITBMS when the goods physically leave Panamanian customs territory and are evidenced by a valid customs export declaration. Services qualify for zero-rating only when the service is effectively used, exploited, or consumed outside Panama by a non-resident recipient, and the supplier maintains contracts, invoices, and documentary evidence of the offshore use and payment from abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera - DUA)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Commercial invoice issued under electronic fiscal invoicing rules
  • Inward Remittance Certificate proving payment from a non-resident client
  • Export contract or service agreement
  • Proof of actual offshore consumption for exported services

Panama requires mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) for all VAT-registered taxpayers. Invoices must be issued through a DGI-authorized electronic invoicing system, transmitted in real time or near-real time to the tax authority via the Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (SFE) API, and validated with a secure digital fiscal stamp (código de impresión). Fiscal credit and export zero-rating claims require electronically accepted invoices and XML file retention.

DGI may conduct post-export audits to verify physical exit, offshore use, and actual foreign currency repatriation within 180 days. Failure to repatriate, false invoicing, or claimed zero-rating without substantive export documentation triggers reversal of the 0% rate, re-instatement of ITBMS at 7% plus late-payment interest, fines from 50% to 200% of the omitted tax, and publication as a tax defaulter. Criminal prosecution under Panama's Tax Penal Code applies for fraudulent export claims, including prison terms of 2 to 5 years and disqualification from doing business with the State.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy with the U.S. dollar as legal tender and the Panamanian balboa pegged nominally at 1:1. There are no exchange control laws; both current and capital account transactions are freely convertible. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and capital repatriations are unrestricted and do not require prior central bank authorization. Non-resident investors may hold and transfer funds in USD without conversion hurdles. Corporate escrow accounts and operational accounts may be held in USD or other freely traded currencies, subject only to AML/KYC documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate or convert export proceeds. Earnings from global EOR staffing and payroll services may be retained in USD abroad or transferred into Panama freely and immediately. There is no foreign-exchange surrender requirement for receipts invoiced in USD or any other currency.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) — Panama has no central bank; U.S. dollar circulation and monetary policy are effectively managed via dollarization, with the SBP as the principal banking and foreign-exchange intermediary regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility with legal restrictions. Panama does not have a dedicated 'electronic money institution' (EMI) statute comparable to the EU EMI directive. A foreign digital EMI (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business) may only operate through an approved Panamanian banking partner or a locally licensed finance company, and must obtain a brokerage or money-transmission license from the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá if it establishes a physical presence or targets Panamanian residents. For a pure offshore payouts engine, a regulated foreign EMI may be used for cross-border contractor disbursements without a Panama license, provided the funds never trigger regulated money-services activity inside Panama. This approach carries legal uncertainty; a tier-1 Panama correspondent bank remains the preferred compliant clearing channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital may be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's corporate bank account directly to the Panama S.A. corporate operational account. No Panamanian exchange-control approval is required because Panama is dollarized. Recommended purpose code for the wire is 'Capital Contribution' (services sector code) or 'Equity Injection' under ISO 20022 equivalent codes. The bank may require a board resolution, a certified copy of the share register, and source-of-funds statements. To maintain clean clearing, route injections from a regulated bank in the investor's home country, avoid third-party intermediary accounts, and refrain from cash couriered deposits. For multi-currency injections in EUR or GBP, the receiving Panama bank will automatically convert to USD at prevailing market rate; maintain a documented prior approach with the treasury desk to obtain transparent pricing.Outbound trade disbursements for global EOR payroll and staffing settlements should be executed via SWIFT MT103 corporate-to-beneficiary payments for supplier invoices and contractor payroll, with purpose codes such as 'Business Services' or 'Staff Hire'. For high-value outbound remittances exceeding USD 50,000, implement dual-authorization under a positive-pay system. For payments to jurisdictions with exchange restrictions, commercial letters of credit (LCs) may be used only for physical goods or where the EOR client requires trade-finance instruments; for pure service settlements, the standard clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 with settlement through the correspondent bank (e.g., JPMorgan, Citi, BNY Mellon, or Bank of America). Include the S.A. treasury invoice reference in field 70 (remittance information) to ensure audit trails. Do not use cash sweeps or unhosted wallets.
