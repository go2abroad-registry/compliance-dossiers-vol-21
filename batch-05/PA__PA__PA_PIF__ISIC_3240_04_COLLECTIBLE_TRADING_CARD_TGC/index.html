Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; e-filing portal 'Sede Electrónica' (Sistema e-Tax 2.0), https://dgi.mef.gob.pa. Customs and export administration: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA), with foreign-trade filings through the Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior (VUCE).

E-government filing portal

Foreign founders may procure the Panamanian tax identifier (RUC) entirely through a licensed Resident Agent (Agente Residente). Step 1: Create the Private Interest Foundation by private instrument and inscribe the acta constitutiva with the Registro Público de Panamá via the Resident Agent. Step 2: File the Solicitud de Registro Único de Contribuyente (Form 1) with the DGI on the e-Tax portal, attaching the certified foundation charter, notarized identity documents of the foundation council/Protector, proof of registered office, and the Resident Agent appointment. Step 3: Obtain the RUC certificate and, where digital operations are contemplated, obtain the DGI electronic-invoicing certification and the Firma Electrónica (digital signature) for authorized signatories. Step 4: If physical premises in Panama are used, obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación. Non-resident ownership is permitted and the entire registration can be completed remotely; typical processing is 5–15 business days after inscription of the foundation charter.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope. The PIF under Law 25 of 1995 is a separate juridical person and a taxable 'territorial' entity for Panamanian tax purposes: corporate income tax (ISR, 25%) and ITBMS apply only to Panama-source income and Panama-based supplies. Income derived from genuine foreign sources — including export sales of collectible trading cards to non-Panamanian distributors where effective management and economic substance are maintained outside Panama — is exempt under the territoriality principle of Código Fiscal Art. 694, subject to the Law 254 of 2021 substance/anti-avoidance rules. The PIF has no general pass-through recognition inside Panama; however, it is a hybrid for cross-border tax purposes and may be classified as a corporation, trust, or transparent entity by foreign CFC, FATCA, CRS, and US federal tax regimes.

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a domestic 'check-the-box' classification regime equivalent to US Treas. Reg. §301.7701-3; entity classification follows the constitutive charter, the Código Fiscal, and Law 25 of 1995. Statutory elections and regimes available to the PIF include: (i) election of the Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente (simplified income-tax regime) for annual gross receipts not exceeding B/.50,000; (ii) election of the Régimen Especial Simplificado del ITBMS (forfeiture of input-VAT credit entitlement) for taxable turnover not exceeding B/.50,000; (iii) voluntary registration as an Agente de Retención (withholding agent); (iv) FATCA/CRS status declaration and foreign-hybrid alignment, including the possibility of re-characterization by US owners under the US check-the-box rules to the extent the PIF is treated as a 'business entity'; and (v) conversion/continuation elections under Law 25 of 1995, including transformation of a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima into a PIF without liquidation. All elections must be supported by DGI economic-substance documentation in the post-Law 254 environment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold triggers ITBMS registration: any person habitually making taxable supplies of movable goods or services in Panama, including a PIF with Panamanian sales activity, must register for ITBMS through the RUC. The economically significant turnover threshold is B/.50,000 (approximately USD 50,000) of annual gross taxable turnover: below this level the taxpayer may elect the simplified ITBMS regime, forfeiting input-credit recovery; at or above B/.50,000 full standard registration, e-invoicing, and input-credit rules apply. Genuine export turnover is excluded from the simplified-election turnover test.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% (tarifa cero) ITBMS rate applies to: (i) exports of tangible goods — goods must physically depart Panamanian customs territory, be sold to a non-Panamanian buyer, and be evidenced by an ANA export declaration; and (ii) offshore services — services supplied to non-residents for consumption entirely outside Panama. The PIF publishing collectible trading cards may zero-rate only goods printed/assembled in Panama and actually exported; cards manufactured abroad and drop-shipped to global customers never enter Panamanian territory and fall wholly outside the ITBMS nexus. Panamanian printing and foil-stamping services acquired by the PIF carry 7% ITBMS and become recoverable input credits against zero-rated export sales or taxable domestic sales. The exporter must evidence the concluded sale, customs exit, and receipt of the price from abroad, and must retain all supporting records for at least five years. Because Panama is fully dollarized (USD is legal tender), no separate FX conversion or statutory repatriation deadline applies; documented receipt of proceeds in the PIF's bank account is the operative evidence. Zero-rating does not independently protect the PIF from Panamanian corporate income tax: the PIF must still satisfy Law 254 of 2021 economic-substance and effective-management requirements to maintain territorial exemption on foreign-source income.

  • ANA electronic export declaration (Póliza de Exportación) filed via VUCE/SUMATRÓN, indicating the applicable HS classification (e.g., 9504.40 or 4911.10) and the SFE export invoice reference
  • DGI-validated electronic commercial invoice (Factura Comercial) issued with the corresponding SFE fiscal QR code
  • Bill of Lading or International Air Waybill showing departure from Panamanian port or airport
  • Signed purchase order or international sales contract with the non-Panamanian buyer
  • Bank proof of payment from abroad (e.g., SWIFT MT103 credit advice) evidencing receipt of proceeds in the PIF's account
  • Packing list and, where applicable, certificate of origin supporting tariff treatment

Panama mandates electronic invoicing through the DGI's Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (SFE): all commercial invoices, including export invoices, must be generated by DGI-certified ERP/billing software and transmitted in near real time to the DGI for prior validation ('validación previa') through the e-Tax platform and its API/web-service interfaces. Validated invoices receive a DGI fiscal identification code and a QR-style digital seal; a non-validated invoice is legally ineffective and cannot support input-VAT credits. The SFE mandate is being phased by DGI resolutions, beginning with Gran Contribuyentes (large taxpayers) and extending to all commercial taxpayers; the PIF must obtain SFE certification before issuing its first taxable supply. Export e-invoices carry the DGI 'Exportación' classification and must be cross-referenced to the ANA export póliza number.

The DGI cross-validates SFE export invoices against ANA customs exit records, banking data, and shipper manifests using automated analytics. Where a 0% export claim is denied — goods never departed Panama, invoices are fictitious or reused, or goods are reimported abnormally — the DGI re-assesses output ITBMS at 7%, disallows the associated input credits, and adds statutory monthly surcharges, interest, and civil fines generally ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax evaded. Adverse determinations may reach back up to seven fiscal years where fraud is alleged, compared with the ordinary five-year assessment window. Fraudulent export zero-rating may also trigger criminal tax-evasion prosecution against the PIF and its legal representatives, with possible imprisonment and fines under the Código Penal and Código Fiscal, suspension of the PIF's SFE digital certification (disabling lawful invoicing), and attachment of foundation assets; the Protector and council members may be personally liable to indemnify the foundation for resulting tax liabilities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama has no foreign-exchange control regime. Capital and current accounts are freely convertible; there are no exchange-rate bands, surrender requirements, or prior approval duties for profit remittance, dividends, royalties, interest, or intercompany transfers. The use of the USD eliminates local convertibility risk. The only applicable hurdles are banking AML/KYC documentation, FATCA/CRS registration and reporting, and the Argentine? no, Panamanian Beneficial Ownership registry compliance. Profit remittance is permitted via routine banking channels, subject to the Panamanian tax code's territorial taxation and the requirement that any Panamanian-source income is declared.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama does not mandate a statutory timeline for the collection, repatriation, or surrender of export proceeds. Receipts from the sale of collectible trading cards may be retained in USD or any convertible currency, deposited with any licensed bank in the International Banking Center, and freely used for trade disbursements. Exporters must still keep complete commercial documents for customs, tax, and AML audits.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). Panama does not operate a central bank; the U.S. dollar is legal tender and the National Monetary Council and SBP provide regulatory oversight of the banking system. Foreign-exchange matters are not administrated by any Panamanian exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate-to-low viability for unlicensed offshore digital EMIs. Panama lacks a dedicated e-money institution or EMI licensing statute; digital financial activities generally require an SBP license or must operate as a 'payment initiator' within the Fintech Innovation Hub sandbox. Foreign EMIs can offer payment processing for e-commerce and card receivables through BIN sponsorship or local fintech partnerships, but they cannot legally open demand deposit accounts without an SBP banking license. The PIF should rely on licensed Panamanian banks for treasury settlement and use EMIs strictly for collection-side merchant services with robust AML/KYC integration.

Supporting documents must include the foundation council resolution authorizing the contribution, a notarized contribution/loan agreement, and the ultimate funding entity's KYC pack.
