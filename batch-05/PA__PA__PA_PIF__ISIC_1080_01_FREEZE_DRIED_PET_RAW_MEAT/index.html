Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope - Panama taxes only Panama-source income (Art. 694 Código Fiscal). The Private Interest Foundation is a separate juridical person and an entity-level taxpayer (no pass-through). Panama-source manufacturing profits from freeze-dried pet food are taxable at the standard 25% corporate income tax rate (subject to small-taxpayer relief schedules under the Law 361 of 2023 reform); non-Panama-source income, foreign bank interest and foreign dividends of the PIF are exempt. There is no CFC reattribution of foreign passive income; the Panama tax-exempt zone regimes (e.g., Panama-Pacifico special economic area) do not apply unless a specific zone license is obtained.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election or domestic hybrid-mismatch election is available. Statutory elections and regimes relevant to the PIF-manufacturer: (i) elective Simplified Regime (RUS) for businesses with annual gross receipts up to B/. 36,000, combining Renta and ITBMS in fixed monthly payments; (ii) election of a DGI-approved fiscal year other than the calendar year; (iii) election, with DGI approval, between direct accounting on net taxable income and the estimation/coefficient method for industrial taxpayers; (iv) irrevocable monthly election to offset or refund ITBMS export credit balances; (v) civil-law election by the founder to make the foundation irrevocable under Law 25 of 1995 (structuring election). OECD BEPS Pillar Two top-up rules apply to in-scope MNE groups only if and when Panamanian implementing legislation is in force.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold triggers ITBMS registration: any person habitually selling movable goods or providing services in Panamanian territory, including the PIF's manufacturing plant, must register before commencing taxable activities; voluntary registration is available. As a micro-taxpayer alternative, businesses with annual gross receipts up to B/. 36,000 (Balboa at parity with USD) may elect RUS in lieu of standard ITBMS and income tax. Differentiated ITBMS rates (e.g., 10% and 15%) apply to specified categories of services and tobacco/alcohol products.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% ITBMS with full recovery of input credits applies to: (a) tangible goods physically exported from Panamanian customs territory, including freeze-dried pet raw meat and kibble classified under ISIC 1080; (b) services provided to non-residents with effective use and benefit abroad; (c) supplies to Panamanian duty-free zone or Panama-Pacifico licensed operators. The exporter must prove the physical departure of the goods, payment from abroad, and allocation of input credits (raw meat imports, freeze-drying electricity, packaging, cold-chain logistics) to exported lots. Raw meat imported for processing incurs 7% ITBMS at customs which is creditable against output tax or recovered by refund once export zero-rating is substantiated; domestic sales of the same manufactured goods remain taxable at 7%.

  • Declaración Única de Exportación (single export customs declaration) with Aduana dispatch and exit stamp
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) proving physical departure from Panamanian customs territory
  • Commercial invoice with ISIC 1080 product description, INCOTERMS, unit values and total export price
  • Packing list and weight/lot tally cross-referenced to the customs export declaration and production records
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in a Panamanian bank account (FX substantiation)
  • AUPSA/MIDA sanitary certificate and country-of-origin certificate for animal-derived pet food, as required by the destination country

Mandatory electronic invoicing under the Facturación Electrónica de Panamá (FEP) program, phased by DGI resolutions from 2019 through 2024; industrial and ITBMS-registered taxpayers are required to issue electronically validated invoices. Invoices are transmitted as XML in near-real-time to the DGI through certified e-invoicing service providers or the Sistema T@x web service/API; the DGI performs fiscal validation and returns an electronic security code/QR (código de seguridad) that must be embedded in every invoice, export invoice and credit note. Settlement of export invoices through platform rails (e.g., PayPal/Stripe) must be traceable to the exporter's Panamanian bank account in the same e-invoicing documentation chain. Non-compliance triggers per-infraction fines under the Código Fiscal enforcement provisions (typically B/. 50 to B/. 10,000).

DGI audit priorities are fictitious exports, inflated input credits, broken production/export traceability, and unrepatriated proceeds. If zero-rating is disallowed, the DGI retroactively assesses 7% ITBMS on the invoice value plus statutory recargos and monthly interest, orders restitution of any refunded credit balance, and bars the exporter from 0% treatment for a defined remedial period. Statutory per-infraction fines (typically B/. 50 to B/. 10,000) may be applied cumulatively; forged export declarations or bills of lading trigger criminal tax fraud (defraudación fiscal) prosecution, suspension of the PIF's RUC and e-invoicing privileges, and placement on the DGI high-risk exporter list. The assessment prescription period is generally five years, extended in fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama uses the United States Dollar as legal tender and the Balboa only as subsidiary coinage at strict 1:1 parity. Full convertibility exists for both current account and capital account transactions. Profit remittance is unrestricted; Panama applies territorial taxation and does not impose exchange controls, surrender requirements, or restrictions on dividend distributions to non-residents. Foreign investors may freely repatriate capital, dividends, interest, and sale proceeds through the domestic banking system without prior central bank approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory repatriation timeline under Panamanian law. Export receipts may lawfully remain offshore or be credited to USD-denominated Panamanian bank accounts. However, SBP AML/CFT regulations require the underlying commercial documentation, shipping records, and receivable aging schedules to be available for bank compliance review on demand.

Central bank supervision: Superintendency of Banks of Panama (SBP) and Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF); Panama has no central bank and Banco Nacional de Panama acts only as fiscal agent for the Republic

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low-Medium for offshore digital banking and Electronic Money Institutions. Panama does not maintain a domestic EU-style EMI license regime; a foreign EMI cannot act as the principal clearing rail or hold the Private Interest Foundation's primary escrowed capital in lieu of a licensed Panamanian bank. Offshore EMIs may be used only as supplementary disbursement access, provided the foundation also maintains a compliance-complete account with a SBP-licensed bank. Panamanian banks generally require the account relationship to sit on domestic subsidiary ledgers with clear correspondent records.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on net taxable income derived from Panama-source activities

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends paid from Panama-source profits derived by the PIF. Dividends paid out of genuinely foreign-source profits are not subject to Panama dividend withholding under the territorial tax system; distributions recharacterized as Panama-source income are subject to the 25% corporate tax plus the 10% dividend withholding to the extent paid to a beneficiary.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 3,000,000 in aggregate related-party cross-border transactions during the fiscal year for mandatory Local File documentation; Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue above USD 850,000,000, consistent with OECD-aligned Panama rules.

Economic substance scope: Panama's New Law 254 of 2021 establishes economic substance requirements for resident legal entities, including Private Interest Foundations. A PIF engaged in freeze-dried pet raw-meat manufacturing must demonstrate real presence in Panama through physical premises, qualified local staff, board or council meetings, substantive decision-making, and adequate operating expenditure. A PIF operating as a shell with no manufacturing activity, no local employees, no bank records, and no governance documentation is subject to economic substance enforcement and may lose tax and treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Sourcing

Statutory sanction: DGI reassessment of the omitted Panama-source revenue, application of 25% corporate income tax, monthly surcharges, interest, and penalties; criminal tax-fraud prosecution carrying 2 to 5 years imprisonment and fines calculated as a multiple of the evaded tax; asset embargo and suspension of the foundation's RUC.

Director personal liability: Directors, council members, and legal representatives who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax debt and penalties; criminal proceedings can trigger preventive detention, travel bans, asset freezing, and civil restitution orders piercing the foundation's separate legal personality.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Fines and administrative penalties under Panama's economic substance regime, including up to USD 50,000 per enforcement finding; dissolution or forced liquidation of the foundation; denial of deductions; cancellation of tax residency certificates; referral to the Public Ministry for fraud and money-laundering investigations.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members, protectors, and beneficial owners face personal liability for restitution and fines when substance documents are false; courts may issue travel bans, appoint liquidators, and order personal seizure of assets in Panama and abroad through cooperation channels.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties under Panama's AML/CFT framework of up to USD 1,000,000 per violation, suspension of the foundation's activities, license revocation, and forfeiture of connected assets; criminal money-laundering conviction carries imprisonment from 5 to 12 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, resident agent, and beneficial owners may be prosecuted individually; corporate veil does not protect intentional AML facilitation; travel restrictions, exit permits, preventive custody, and extradition requests are available to Panamanian authorities.
Transfer Pricing and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: DGI may make a transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income, deny deductions, impose monthly penalties on the understated tax up to statutory maximums, and levy fines under the Panamanian transfer pricing law; deliberate concealment can elevate the matter to criminal tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Officers and council members who knowingly certify false transfer pricing documentation are jointly liable for the deficiency and fines; Panamanian courts may order asset seizure, travel bans, and criminal penalties for document falsification and evasion.
