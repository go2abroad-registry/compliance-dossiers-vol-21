Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), República de Panamá; primary portal: SITIO — Sistema Integrado de Trámites en Línea (https://sitio.dgi.gob.pa); export customs interface: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) at https://www.ana.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

A Panama S.A. obtains its RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) as follows: (1) incorporate through a licensed registered agent, recording the sociedad anónima in the Registro Público de Panamá; (2) file the Aviso de Operación with DGI prior to commencing business; (3) submit notarized corporate charter, appointment of the resident agent and legal representative, the representative's digital certificate (firma electrónica/token), and the Panamanian registered office address via the SITIO portal to request the RUC; (4) complete ITBMS registration and elect the electronic invoicing modality. Non-resident persons without a local establishment obtain a non-resident RUC for withholding-reporting purposes only; no active-business registration is triggered solely by passive foreign ownership of shares.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No pass-through or check-the-box election exists under Panamanian domestic law; a Sociedad Anónima is irrevocably an entity-level taxpayer and is a per-se corporation for U.S. check-the-box classification purposes. Available statutory elections: (a) consolidated income-tax filing for affiliated Panamanian entities under Article 704 of the Código Fiscal with DGI pre-authorization; (b) small-taxpayer simplified ITBMS regime for annual gross revenue at or below B/.36,000; (c) special Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime for qualifying regional service-export income upon favorable resolution by the competent commission; (d) declarations and regime-alignment filings under Law 254 of 2021 for international tax transparency, which do not create elective entity classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de-minimis threshold: any person habitually transferring movable goods or providing services in Panama must register for ITBMS. Optional small-taxpayer (pequeño contribuyente) simplified regime applies to annual gross revenue not exceeding B/.36,000 (Balboa, at parity with USD 36,000), substituting full ITBMS with a reduced flat-rate contribution.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% ITBMS) applies to: (a) exports of goods physically leaving Panama, evidenced by customs DUA exit; (b) exports of services, including digital and programmatic AdTech services, where the service is rendered to and the benefit is exclusively realized by a non-resident party outside Panama and the service is not exploited in Panama. The programmatic DSP/SSP platform qualifies for zero-rating on services provided to non-resident advertisers, agencies, or exchanges; services to Panamanian resident advertisers or campaigns targeting Panamanian consumers are taxable at 7% and cannot be zero-rated. The exporter may claim refund or credit of embedded ITBMS input tax (crédito fiscal) on costs attributable to the zero-rated exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA — Declaración Única de Aduanas) with ANA exit validation
  • Commercial invoice and, for service exports, signed contract (MSA/insertion order) with the non-resident counterparty
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill for goods; for services, delivery report, service logs, and geo-targeting/usage evidence proving offshore benefit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/MT103 bank confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in a Panamanian bank account (Panama has no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is documentary best practice for DGI/ANA audit review)
  • ITBMS export register and input-VAT (crédito fiscal) reconciliation for the relevant monthly period

Mandatory electronic invoicing under DGI Resolution No. 201-5874 of 2019: all active ITBMS taxpayers must issue invoices through DGI-authorized billing software integrated to SITIO via SOAP/REST APIs using a qualified digital certificate. The online modality requires real-time DGI fiscal clearance, with a validation response, authorization code, and fiscal QR (eCódigo/digital stamp) that must appear on every invoice before delivery to the client. Paper and fiscal-printer documents are no longer accepted for non-micro taxpayers; monthly electronic transmission of purchase and sales ledgers to DGI is mandatory.

DGI audit limitation period is generally 5 years from the statutory filing deadline, extendable to 6 years for omissions or errors. Zero-rating claims are scrutinized on residency, place-of-performance, and place-of-benefit; documentary deficiencies trigger retroactive assessment of ITBMS at 7%, monthly moratory interest at the statutory rate, and fines from 10% up to 100% of the omitted tax for grave infractions. Fraudulent zero-rating or refund claims constitute tax evasion under the Código Fiscal and Law 254 of 2021, exposing the company and its legal representative to criminal penalties including imprisonment up to 5 years, joint liability of the resident agent, and retrospective clawback of refunded ITBMS credits with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully dollarized jurisdiction with de jure and de facto full convertibility across both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no prior approval requirements, and no surrender or conversion obligations applicable to dividend remittances, management fees, royalties, or equity repatriation. Profit remittance is unrestricted and subject only to ordinary AML/KYC documentation and Panama's territorial tax reporting rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Panama's legal framework imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender requirement for export proceeds or any inbound foreign receipts. Revenues derived from programmatic advertising, SSP, and DSP services may be retained freely in US Dollars or any other convertible currency in Panamanian or offshore bank accounts.

Central bank supervision: Panama does not maintain a central bank or a dedicated foreign-exchange administration. The competent banking and AML/CFT supervisory authority is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); fiscal agency for the Republic is exercised by Banco Nacional de Panamá. The US Dollar circulates as legal tender and the Panamanian Balboa exists solely as a unit of account pegged at par.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, restricted to secondary disbursement rails. Panama's removal from the FATF grey list in October 2023 materially improves acceptance, yet EU/UK/Asia-Pacific EMIs frequently apply enhanced due diligence to Panama-domiciled S.A. entities because of residual offshore-vehicular perception. Legal constraints include the absence of a Panamanian EMI passporting regime and the requirement that the primary treasury relationship remain with a licensed Panamanian bank. EMIs are acceptable for low-value publisher payouts and multi-currency float segregation but should not substitute for local commercial banking in capital escrow or major settlement flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital contributions are routed via SWIFT MT202 or MT103 wire transfers from the contributing entity through its US correspondent bank to the beneficiary Panamanian bank. Owing to full dollarization, no central-bank approval, exchange-control registration, or investment permit is required. The MT103 field 70 should state 'Capital Contribution / Equity Funding' and field 72 should reference the shareholder resolution; the receiving bank will independently request the corporate resolution, updated incumbency certificate, and source-of-funds evidence before clearing the credit.US-dollar settlements to publishers, SSP partners, measurement vendors, and operating counterparties are cleared through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for cross-border payees and through Fedwire/CHIPS for US-based receiving institutions. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% on dividends distributed from earnings that have borne 25% CIT; 10% when the distributing company's earnings were taxed at a reduced or exempt rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Final return must be filed within 3 months after each fiscal year-end; for calendar-year taxpayers, the deadline is 31 March. Quarterly estimated payments are due on the 15th day of the 3rd month after each quarter.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 100 / Line 1a Gross receipts from Panama-source operations and commercial transactions. Report only revenue from Panamanian sources; foreign DSP/SSP mediation fees booked abroad should be excluded unless sourced to Panama under the nexus rules. Late filing penalty is 10% of the tax due; a 10% monthly surcharge on outstanding tax accrues for non-payment. Failure to file may lead to DGI official assessment, fines up to USD 25,000, and cancellation of taxpayer registration.

Failure to file may lead to DGI official assessment, fines up to USD 25,000, and cancellation of taxpayer registration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 2,000,000 (annual gross related-party transactions) triggers the Local File and Master File documentation duty; Country-by-Country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue equal to or above USD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Law 70 of 2019 (Economic Substance Regulations, ESR) broadly applies to Panama-incorporated entities deriving 'relevant income' from services, financing, leasing, and holding activities. An AdTech SSP/DSP that book-keeps finance and central management in Panama must maintain physical premises, full-time employees, and substantive decision-making in the Republic; pure 'shell' holding of IP or ad contracts is treated as non-compliant and a red flag for DGI and AML authorities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fiscal Code Articles 711-712: fine of one to three times the evaded tax plus interest and surcharges; criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years; seizure and forfeiture of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are jointly civilly liable to the unpaid amount, with a discretionary 50% penalty; travel bans are common for outstanding fiscal debt exceeding USD 10,000, and a conviction triggers disqualification from serving as director.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR violations attract fines up to USD 1,000,000, suspension of the tax-resident certificate, and the Commercial Registry may strike the entity off without judicial process.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute false substance declarations may incur personal fines, be removed from the registry of suitable directors, and face civil claims from creditors if the veil is pierced.
Withholding Tax Default on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The distributing company is jointly liable for the unwithheld tax, monthly surcharge of 10%, a 25% fixed fine, and interest; DGI may order encumbrances on bank accounts.

Director personal liability: A director who authorizes the transfer of funds without WHT becomes personally liable for the unpaid tax and can be placed on the DGI 'debtor watch list', triggering travel restrictions and personal enforcement.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income at 25%, plus a 3% monthly compounding penalty for late assessment, a documentation penalty of up to 1% of the cross-border transaction, and in fraud cases a 100% penalty.

Director personal liability: Deliberately falsifying intercompany pricing or destroying transfer pricing records can result in personal criminal liability, disqualification before DGI, and confiscation of personal assets in the extent of the underpayment.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: AML fines for legal entities range up to USD 1,000,000 per infraction for non-filing or inadequate systems; serious cases lead to forced liquidation and regulatory license withdrawal.

Director personal liability: Directors can be sentenced to up to 12 years for money laundering, receive individual fines up to USD 100,000, and be barred from acting as fiduciaries; the public registry can impose personal travel bans pending investigations.
