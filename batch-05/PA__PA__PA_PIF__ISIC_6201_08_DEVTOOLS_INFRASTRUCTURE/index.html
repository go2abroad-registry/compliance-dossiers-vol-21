Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá — Mercantile Section (Fundaciones de Interés Privado / Private Interest Foundations Unit)

Governing statute

Law No. 25 of 12 June 1995 (Private Interest Foundations Law), as amended, particularly Arts. 1, 3, 6, 12, and 22; Law No. 254 of 2021 establishing the Beneficial Ownership Registry (RUB); provisions of the Public Registry of Panama for registration of private interest foundations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Foundation Charter (Acta de Fundación) executed as a public deed (Escritura Pública) before a Panamanian Notary Public

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum foundation patrimony of USD 10,000 (or equivalent in assets or any currency); no statutory paid-up capital deposit, no escrow account obligation at formation, and no proof of contribution is filed with the Public Registry. The Foundation Council must maintain the foundation patrimony separately from the founder's personal assets.

Funding & unlock path: Not required for a pure remote DevOps infrastructure service with no brick-and-mortar plant. If a Panama administrative office is later opened, the company must obtain the municipal occupancy permit (permiso de ocupación), a fire-safety inspection certificate from the Benemérito Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá, and general occupational health clearance under Panama's workplace safety regulations.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum foundation patrimony of USD 10,000 (or equivalent in assets or any currency); no statutory paid-up capital deposit, no escrow account obligation at formation, and no proof of contribution is filed with the Public Registry. The Foundation Council must maintain the foundation patrimony separately from the founder's personal assets.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; e-administration portal 'SAT en Línea' at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa (e-invoicing platform: Sistema de Facturación Electrónica de Panamá / SFEP); customs clearance administered separately through the Panamanian customs administration and its SIGA/VUCEA single-window systems.

E-government filing portal

SOP for a foreign-owned PA_PIF: (1) Execute and inscribe the foundation charter at the Registro Público de Panamá under Ley 25 de 1995, appointing a resident agent; the registered PIF acquires separate legal personality. (2) File the beneficial-ownership declaration (Registro de Beneficiarios Finales) through the resident agent. (3) Obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) with the DGI, filing the foundation certificate, Public Registry inscription, by-laws and identity of the legal representative; non-resident founders are permitted and no local share capital deposit is required beyond the statutory minimum. (4) File the national Aviso de Operación (DGI/MICI) only when Panamanian commercial activity is carried out; municipal patent applies to physical premises only. (5) Register as ITBMS taxpayer (general regime) and obtain SFEP e-invoicing credentials to issue zero-rated export invoices. (6) Non-resident digital suppliers may alternatively use the simplified ITBMS registration of Executive Decree 420 of 2017 with a Panamanian fiscal representative. Standard handling: 3-10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope. The PIF is a separate taxable person under Código Fiscal Articles 693-694 and 699, taxed only on Panama-source income; foreign-source dividends, interest, royalties, capital gains and distributions to non-resident beneficiaries fall outside the Panamanian income tax net. ITBMS applies only to supplies used, consumed or exploited in Panama. The PIF is not treated as a tax-transparent pass-through entity for Panamanian purposes, but its non-Panama activities generate no entity-level tax; no Tax-Exempt-Zone status applies.

Available election forms

Panama has no OECD-style check-the-box election. Statutory and administrative elections available to the PA_PIF: (i) voluntary registration as an ITBMS taxpayer to claim input credits and lawfully issue 0% export invoices; (ii) election of the Régimen Especial de Pequeños Contribuyentes where annual gross receipts do not exceed B/.36,000; (iii) simplified ITBMS registration for non-resident digital service suppliers under Executive Decree 420 of 2017, including the appointment of a fiscal representative; (iv) by-law elections under Ley 25 de 1995 (reserved powers, irrevocable distributions to foreign beneficiaries) which are neutral for Panamanian tax because of territoriality; (v) no hybrid-mismatch or CFC election exists—foreign-only structures may be reorganized without Panamanian tax recognition. Fiscal domicile must be maintained through a resident attorney/agent.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General domestic regime: no statutory minimum—registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply made or rendered in Panama. Small-taxpayer special regime (Régimen Especial de Pequeños Contribuyentes): annual gross receipts up to B/.36,000 (US$36,000). Non-resident digital-service suppliers under Executive Decree 420 of 2017: no de minimis threshold—simplified ITBMS registration is mandatory from the first supply to Panamanian recipients, generally collected by withholding of local payment intermediaries. An export-oriented PIF should register voluntarily to issue 0% export invoices and preserve ITBMS credit positions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated when title passes to a non-resident and the goods are physically exported outside Panama under an accepted export declaration to the customs authority. Services (including DevOps SaaS, database and API-monitoring cloud services, ISIC 6201): zero-rated where the recipient of the service is non-resident and the service is actually used or exploited entirely outside Panama, with the contractual and economic place of benefit abroad. B2B cloud sales to foreign customers qualify provided no Panamanian-resident customer, local establishment or Panama-based use occurs; payment received from abroad (Stripe Billing settlement, Mercury ACH) corroborates the offshore nexus, and the PIF must issue the electronic invoice with the legend 'Exportación de Servicios — 0% ITBMS'.

  • E-invoice in SFEP format carrying the DGI validation token and zero-rating/export legend
  • Panamanian export customs declaration (SIGA/VUCEA) for goods, or DGI-accepted service-export affidavit
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or carrier certificate evidencing physical departure
  • Export service contract identifying the non-resident recipient, offshore use and place of benefit
  • Inward remittance evidence: SWIFT/MT103, Stripe settlement reports, Mercury ACH account statements
  • Foreign recipient's tax-residence certificate or CRS self-certification equivalent
  • PIF e-invoice XML and issuance log retained for 5 years

Mandatory e-invoicing (Facturación Electrónica de Panamá / SFEP) phased in since 2020 and compulsory for all taxpayers as of fiscal year 2023 (DGI Resolution 201-6024 of 2020, as amended). Model: online issuance with near-real-time transmission to and validation by the DGI; each invoice is sealed with a DGI authorization token/folio and QR code before delivery to the customer; contingency/offline invoices must be transmitted immediately upon reconnection. Certified billing platforms and ERPs integrate via DGI-approved APIs (SOAP/REST web services). E-credit notes and e-debit notes are synchronized with the same clearance mechanism; export invoices must include the statutory zero-rating legend and supporting export/customs reference.

DGI cross-checks zero-rated invoices against SFEP data and SIGA customs exit records; failure to prove export triggers ITBMS at 7% plus monthly interest (1% per month) and surcharges of 50%-100% of the evaded tax, with possible closure or suspension of the Aviso de Operación. Fraudulent export zero-rating or forged documentation falls within the criminal tax-fraud regime of Ley 70 de 2019 (imprisonment 2-5 years and fines for evasion exceeding B/.100,000), retrospective reassessments for up to 5 years (longer for fraud), revocation of e-invoicing authorization and removal from exporter registries. No statutory FX repatriation deadline applies in Panama's dollarized, exchange-control-free regime; the 180-day window is the internal substantiation benchmark requiring export proceeds to appear in the PIF's bank statements (Stripe/Mercury ACH) within that period to preserve the zero-rating evidence chain.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible dollarized regime. The Balboa is a token currency at fixed parity with the USD; no exchange controls apply to current account or capital account transactions; profit remittances, dividend distributions, interest payments, and foundation benefit transfers face no prior approval, repatriation, or reinvestment requirements. Withholding taxes attach only to Panama-source income under the territorial tax model.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender regime exists. Export receipts, foreign SaaS revenue, and software subscription collections may be retained indefinitely in USD or other convertible currencies, with no statutorily mandated repatriation timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or documentation filing beyond ordinary banking KYC references.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no issuing central bank; the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) acts as banking regulator and prudential authority, Banco Nacional de Panamá performs fiscal agency functions, and the US Dollar is legal tender alongside the pegged Balboa unit (1:1).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility with clear legal boundaries. Panama has no standalone electronic money institution (EMI) licensing category; any local deposit-taking or financial intermediation requires SBP authorization. An offshore digital EMI is therefore not permitted to solicit deposits or hold Panamanian client funds locally, but a Panama-domiciled PIF may lawfully use US-chartered neobanks (e.g., Mercury for ACH) and global payment aggregators (e.g., Stripe Billing) as collection and settlement rails, subject to each provider's internal country-risk policy, US tax identification requirements, and enhanced UBO disclosure. Crypto-centric EMIs are operationally disfavored under SBP's risk guidance.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% final withholding on dividends distributed from Panama-source taxable profits of a Panamanian resi dent paying entity. Dividends paid wholly out of foreign-source or already-taxed Panama-source profits are not within the Panama dividend withholding tax under the territorial regime; a PIF beneficiaries' distribution from foreign-source reserves is exempt.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return must be filed no later than 31 March of the year following the fiscal year-end where the fiscal year corresponds to the calendar year; for an approved non-calendar fiscal year, filing is due within three months after the close of that fiscal year. The annual PIF annuity fee must be paid by the date determined by the Public Registry, normally during the first quarter of the calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB 2,500,000 (USD, at parity) of annual Panama-source gross income generally triggers full transfer pricing documentation and reporting obligations; a Local File and Master File are required if annual related-party transactions exceed PAB 250,000 with any single related party. A lower formal reporting threshold may apply to intra-group services and financing arrangements where the PIF acts as a principal.

Economic substance scope: Panama has not adopted a full OECD-style Economic Substance Regulation applicable to all Private Interest Foundations. Nevertheless, the DGI and AML authorities apply substance-over-form principles: a PIF must maintain board minutes, a fixed or identifiable seat, registered agent records, updated beneficial ownership registers, and a real economic rationale for holding assets and receiving income. A PIF that is a party to cross-border related-party transactions must apply the arm's length principle and maintain contemporaneous evidence. Pure letter-box structures are treated as shell vehicles and may be disregarded for tax, treaty, and AML purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: A fine of between 100% and 500% of the evaded tax, statutory interest, surcharge of 1.5% per month, and criminal imprisonment for tax fraud under Panama's Penal Code and Fiscal Code. Additionally, the DGI may nullify the return and reassess with presumptive methods.

Director personal liability: The Foundation Council members, registered agent, and signatories can be jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax where the veil is pierced; Panamanian courts may issue travel bans, freeze personal assets, and impose imprisonment for up to five years.
Shell Company / Abuse of Foundation Form

Statutory sanction: The Foundation may be struck off the Public Registry, its assets may be provisionally seized, DTA benefits are denied, and transactions may be recharacterized as direct earnings of the beneficial owner; administrative fines of up to USD 1,000,000 may apply under anti-money-laundering legislation if the conduct is linked to concealment.

Director personal liability: Courts may disregard the Foundation and hold the founder, council members, and ultimate beneficial owners personally liable; the registered agent may be sanctioned and barred; travel bans, asset freezes, and extradition procedures are available for foundations used for organized fraud.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: A transfer pricing adjustment with statutory interest, an administrative penalty of up to 40% of the adjustment, and a formal fine for the failure to keep contemporaneous documentation; the tax return may be amended retroactively if the DGI identifies a sham transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors and finance officers who sign inaccurate cost-pricing declarations may be personally liable for the additional tax and penalties; professional disqualification, travel restriction, and civil claims by creditors may follow where distributions were made to beneficiaries while tax remained unpaid.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under Panama Law 23 of 2015 and supplementary AML decrees, which may reach USD 1,000,000 per violation; the Foundation's licence and registration may be revoked, and proceeds linked to money laundering are confiscated.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members, registered agents, and compliance officers may be prosecuted as principals or accomplices; penalties include imprisonment of up to six years for money laundering, travel bans, corporate veil piercing, civil restitution to plaintiffs, and extraterritorial cooperation through INTERPOL.
