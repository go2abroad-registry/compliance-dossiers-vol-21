Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate an IRS-style check-the-box election; the S.A. is by law an entity-level taxpayer subject to the territorial source doctrine (only Panamanian-source income is taxed). Available statutory elections: (i) the SEM (Sede de Empresa Multinacional) regime under Law 41 of 2007, which permits a single 5% income tax on qualifying international services and zero-rated inbound services for licensed multinational centres; (ii) the small taxpayer (pequeño contribuyente) simplified ITBMS regime for gross annual income up to B/.36,000; (iii) the Panama Pacifico special economic zone regime; and (iv) the exempt-return filing position taken when the S.A. derives no Panamanian-source income. Hybrid-mismatch elections are not recognised; the DGI applies the statutory sourcing rules of the Código Fiscal and does not give effect to unilateral hybrid-payment deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de-minimis turnover threshold: registration for ITBMS is mandatory from the commencement of habitual taxable activity (transfer of goods or rendering of services) in Panamanian territory. A simplified presumptive regime exists only for small taxpayers whose gross annual revenue does not exceed B/.36,000; such taxpayers may register in the pequeño contribuyente category instead of the standard ITBMS return cycle.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of services qualify for the 0% ITBMS rate when the service is rendered in or outside Panama to a non-resident beneficiary for use or benefit outside Panama, under Article 1059 and related provisions of the Código Fiscal. For ISIC 6619 cross-border remittance rails, the Panamanian S.A. must evidence that the remittance fee is contractually charged to, and actually settled by, the non-resident money transmitter or aggregator abroad, that the economic exploitation of the administrative, matching, clearing and payout-management services occurs outside Panama, and that the proceeds are credited from abroad. Domestic cash-pickup fees deducted separately from the local beneficiary settlement are domestic services and remain subject to ITBMS at the operative rate. Goods exported in the business (if any) require a customs DUA and movement evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA) for any goods component
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or SWIFT MT103 evidencing the cross-border payment rail
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice showing receipt of the foreign fee
  • Executed service agreement with the non-resident principal documenting the ISIC 6619 scope
  • Tax residence certificate of the foreign counterparty and DGI export-service registration records

The FEPA electronic invoicing mandate is fully phased in for all ITBMS taxpayers. Invoices must be issued through DGI-authorised issuers, transmitted in real time to the DGI via the FEPA API (SOAP/REST web services), and completed with a DGI-issued authorisation code and QR code. The recipient must accept or reject the electronic invoice in the T@ Pagás portal; no physical fiscal stamp or pre-printed authorisation form remains valid. The DGI performs central validation, and any correction or credit note must also be transmitted and authorised in the FEPA environment.

The DGI may audit zero-rating claims for up to five fiscal years and may claw back the full underpaid ITBMS at the operative rate together with statutory moratory interest and monthly surcharges where the documentation bundle is incomplete. Habitual or fabricated export claims attract fines from B/.500 to B/.50,000, a penalty of 100% to 200% of the tax evaded, and, where the amount exceeds the statutory criminal threshold, prosecution for tax fraud with imprisonment of two to five years; the UAF and Superintendencia de Bancos may concurrently suspend or revoke the remittance licence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates under full dollarization: the U.S. dollar is legal tender, while the Balboa exists solely as a fractional coin unit. There are no foreign exchange controls, no dual-exchange-rate regimes, and no restrictions on capital or current account transactions. Profit remittance, dividend payments, and capital repatriation are permitted without FX authorization; however, they remain subject to AML/KYC documentation, territorial tax compliance, and the applicable 5% or 10% dividend withholding tax where earnings are taxable in Panama. The only practical hurdles arise from financial crime due diligence and bank compliance, not from exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama maintains no mandatory export proceeds surrender regime, no conversion quotas, and no statutory time limit for repatriation of export receipts. Exporters may retain funds in any freely convertible currency or in U.S. dollar deposit accounts at Panamanian banks.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) - de facto banking regulator in a fully dollarized system; there is no traditional emission central bank, with monetary policy superseded by use of the U.S. dollar as legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Moderate. Panama lacks a dedicated electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime. Offshore digital EMIs cannot directly access national clearing rails or SBP-supervised settlement and must operate through a licensed Panamanian bank or a fintech company admitted under the SBP financial technology development framework. Legal constraints include a de facto physical-presence expectation, strict AML/CFT registration with the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF), and the need to execute a formal correspondent or agent agreement with a local bank. For the high-risk remittance business segment, offshore EMI viability is low unless a full local license or a substantial banking partnership is secured.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions are structured as SWIFT MT103 customer transfers or MT202 bank-to-bank transfers in U.S. dollars, routed through Fedwire, CHIPS, or the correspondent banking network. The payment reason must clearly designate 'Capital Contribution' or 'Investment' to classify the entry as a capital-account item. No prior SBP or FX authorization is required. Standard verification includes: bank name screen, OFAC/sanctions filtering on both originator and beneficiary, UBO alignment with the capital escrow account records, and matching the wire advice against the executed investment agreement.Outbound trade settlements are executed via SWIFT MT103 for direct irrevocable payments, SWIFT MT700/MT707 for documentary letters of credit and amendments, and MT202/MT202COV for bank-to-bank liquidity transfers. For domestic vendor payments and cash pick-up disbursements, the Panamanian ACH (Cámara de Compensación Automatizada) and local check-clearing rails are used, with net settlement in U.S. dollars. Box 19 (Deductible Expenses) Deductible operating expenses, bank and SWIFT clearing charges, agent commissions, salaries of Panamanian employees, rent, and amortisation. Expenses attributable to foreign-source revenue are not deductible against Panamanian taxable income; apply a consistent pro-rata allocation based on revenue source. Late filing: administrative fine from B/.50 to B/.5,000 plus a 10% surcharge on the unpaid tax and 1% monthly interest (Fiscal Code Art. 706-A); late payment interest accrues from the statutory due date. If management and board decisions occur outside Panama, document the absence of a Panamanian permanent establishment for the foreign corridor activities to avoid recharacterisation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.2,000,000 (Balboas, pegged to USD 1:1): mandatory Local File and transactional documentation for taxpayers with related-party transactions above the threshold in the fiscal year; Master File is additionally required where the multinational group consolidated revenue exceeds B/.50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The licensed remittance S.A. is tax-resident and subject to Panama's Economic Substance Regulations: it must maintain a physical workplace in Panama, qualified employees including a compliance officer, board meetings and accounting records in Panama, and capital commensurate with remittance volume. ESR failure permits the DGI to treat the entity as a shell, recharacterise foreign-source fees as Panamanian-source, deny WHT treaty relief, and apply a 10% surcharge on the recalculated tax base.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraudulent income tax return

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of ten to fifty times the evaded tax and imprisonment from two to five years (Criminal Code Articles 280–281); administrative surcharge of 10% plus monthly interest; disqualification from DGI tax-cleanance certificates.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct the false return are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and civil restitution; criminal conviction may trigger travel bans, asset freezing, and attachment of personal assets under fiscal criminal procedure.
Shell company / absence of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Administrative nullity of the income tax return, retroactive assessment of 25% on deemed Panamanian-source revenue, 10% surcharge, fines up to B/.50,000, and revocation of the SBP money-transmission license.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face DGI veil-piercing proceedings with court-ordered restitution of avoided tax, travel restrictions while debts are outstanding, and disqualification from managing regulated financial entities.
AML / unauthorised money transmission activity

Statutory sanction: Fines under Law 23 of 2015 from B/.100,000 to B/.500,000, immediate revocation of the special license, and criminal money-laundering penalties of up to five years' imprisonment and fines up to B/.5,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners knowingly concerned may be prosecuted alongside the corporate entity; exposure includes personal financial restitution, international travel bans, and extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Violation of profit-repatriation withholding obligations

Statutory sanction: Withheld tax treated as unpaid; 10% surcharge and 1% monthly interest; mandatory inclusion in the DGI delinquent taxpayers registry and automatic loss of low-risk taxpayer status.

Director personal liability: Directors who resolved the dividend are personally liable as 'contribuyente sustituto' for the unpaid WHT; criminal complaint may be filed for misappropriation of retained tax, with travel bans imposed until the debt is fully settled.
