Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministry of Economy and Finance (Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas); online portal: SIF (Sistema de Facturación Integrado) via https://dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation: (1) Engage a Panamanian resident Foundation Agent (Agente Residente) as required by Law No. 25 of 1995; (2) File foundation charter with the Registro Público and obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC) from the DGI; (3) Complete RUC pre-registration through the DGI's SIF online portal or in person at an Atención al Contribuyente office, submitting the public deed, the agent's identification, and a power of attorney; (4) The DGI issues the RUC (11-digit tax ID) and simultaneously registers the entity for ITBMS where applicable; (5) If the foundation engages in local commercial activities, also obtain a municipal Aviso de Operación and police/general license; ongoing annual franchise tax must be paid to the Registro Público.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope; Private Interest Foundation is subject to a flat annual franchise tax (USD 300 for assets up to USD 10 million; USD 350–400 above that threshold) and is exempt from Panamanian income tax on foreign-source income; Panamanian-source income would be taxed at corporate rates (15% on first USD 1.5 million; 25% above), but the overseas restaurant's operating revenue is sourced abroad and thus outside Panama's territorial net.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' or Panamanian entity classification election exists; the foundation's charter defines whether it is passive (standard, asset-holding only) or commercially active. A PIF may voluntarily elect to register for ITBMS and income tax as an 'empresa' if it derives Panama-source income. Panama has not implemented BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch elections; no elective flow-through treatment, but a foundation legally may be restructured to a sociedad anónima (corporation) via charter amendment, subject to DGI registration updates.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general exemption threshold: every person habitually carrying out taxable transfers of goods or services must register for ITBMS from the first operation. A simplified 'pequeño contribuyente' fixed-tax regime is available for taxpayers with annual gross turnover not exceeding USD 36,000, but a full-service restaurant chain and export activity will not qualify for that simplified regime and must file standard periodic ITBMS returns on a monthly basis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (0% ITBMS) applies to: (i) tangible goods physically exported from Panamanian customs territory, evidenced by customs exit; and (ii) services rendered to non-residents where the service is effectively used or exploited exclusively outside Panama. For this hotpot chain, local dine-in and retail sales in Panama are taxable at 7%; sales of food products (condiments, packaged hotpot sauces, prepared ingredient kits) shipped to the overseas restaurant operation qualify for zero rating when good title passes outside Panama or the goods are physically shipped with customs exit documentation. A formal 'export services' declaration must be filed with DGI where applicable.

  • DUA (Declaración Única Aduanera) presented via Panama's SIGA customs system evidencing physical exit of the goods
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (e-Waybill) evidencing transport to the overseas destination
  • Commercial invoice (export invoice) issued through the SIF e-invoicing platform with zero-rate indicator
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Comprobante de Remesa / bank credit advice) or equivalent SWIFT statement evidencing receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds in a Panama bank account
  • Contract or purchase order from the overseas affiliate/buyer substantiating the foreign commercial transaction
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (optional but recommended for food exports)

Mandatory fully electronic invoicing through the DGI's SIF (Sistema de Facturación Integrado). All ITBMS-registered businesses must issue e-invoices using a DGI-authorized facturador electrónico (cloud API web service, authorized billing software, or DGI's own web invoicing module) with near-real-time transmission of invoice data to the DGI fiscal platform; no per-invoice digital stamp fee applies; QR code certification on invoices is mandatory for consumer-facing operations such as restaurant POS receipts; all credit notes, debit notes and export invoices must also flow through the SIF clearance API.

Panama's territorial system and export zero-rating are scrutinized via DGI documentary audits; failure to produce the customs and FX-repatriation bundle within the prescribed period results in reversal of the 0% rate and assessment of the 7% ITBMS plus statutory interest (1% per month on the unpaid tax) and fines that may reach 100%–200% of the tax due under the Código Fiscal; deliberately falsified export documentation or sham offshore structures trigger criminal tax fraud and money-laundering charges, retrospective clawback for the 5-year statute-of-limitations window, blacklisting from the Registro Único de Contribuyentes, and disqualification of the Foundation Agent in serious cases.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a complete dollarization regime with full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. The Panamanian balboa is a nominal accounting unit equal to U.S. dollar at a fixed 1:1 parity and circulates only as domestically minted coinage; all paper currency is U.S. Federal Reserve notes. There are no legal exchange controls, no surrender or conversion requirements, no prior licensing for foreign exchange operations, and no restrictions on profit remittance, capital repatriation, dividend distribution, or cross-border wire settlement for licensed banks. Non-resident investors and Private Interest Foundations (PIFs) may hold, deposit, transfer, and repatriate USD without central bank approvals. The only prudential filters are the SBP's customer due diligence and currency-quality standards for physical cash handling. For taxation, Panama applies a territorial system: a PIF with only foreign-source income (overseas hotpot restaurant operations) is not subject to Panamanian income tax on those revenues, and distributions to non-resident beneficiaries are generally excluded from Panamanian withholding where the underlying wealth is foreign-source; Panamanian-source income, if any, would be assessed at the standard corporate rate of 25%, with 10% withholding on dividends paid to non-residents from Panamanian-source profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules in Panama. Exporters, including the group's condiment supply branch that ships hotpot sauces and spices to the overseas restaurant, are not required to convert, repatriate, or surrender hard currency to any government entity. All export receipts may be retained in USD in any SBP-licensed bank, freely transferred abroad, or used to fund intercompany settlement. The only administrative obligations are commercial and customs compliance: an exporter must file the Declaración Única de Importación (DUI) or its export equivalent through the National Customs Authority (Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas), maintain commercial invoices and bills of lading, and report export values for the national statistics regime (SIEX). AML obligations apply to the banking channel: cash exports or wire receipts above USD 10,000 must be traceable to a declared commercial transaction, and the paying bank will require the underlying sales contract, pro-forma invoice, and customs document reference to prevent money-laundering red flags.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama has no traditional central bank or issuer of national paper currency. The United States Federal Reserve is the de facto monetary authority because Panama is a fully dollarized economy and the U.S. dollar is unrestricted legal tender. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the prudential regulator for all licensed banks, trust companies, and financial service providers. The Banco Nacional de Panamá, a state-owned bank, acts as fiscal agent of the Republic and participates in official clearing operations, while the Caja de Ahorros operates as the second state bank. The Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) supervises the public banks and coordinates fiscal policy. For FX and treasury governance, the operative rules derive from SBP agreements and circulars (including AML/CFT Ordinances such as Acuerdo 5-2020), banking practice codified under the Ley Bancaria (Legislative Decree 2 of 2008), and the Commercial and Tax Codes, given the absence of an exchange-control decree.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility for a PIF Group. Panama does not recognize the European EMI passport and does not issue e-money institution licenses to pure offshore digital wallets. A foreign fintech or digital EMI cannot hold deposits or provide merchant acquiring in Panama without partnering with an SBP-licensed bank or obtaining a niche financial services license from the SBP. Consequently, the Primary Treasury Rail must be a Tier-1 licensed commercial bank; offshore EMIs (e.g., EU-licensed or UK-licensed e-money firms) may be used only as auxiliary aggregation tools for the overseas operating subsidiary's petty cash and local-currency soft currency payments, subject to the group's cash concentration policy and the operating jurisdiction's own EMI authorization. Any EMI holding group client money above USD 10,000 per day must be contractually backed by an SBP-compliant correspondent bank, and the PIF treasury must obtain an annual independent audit of the EMI provider.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections into the PIF are executed by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for nostro-to-nostro) in USD, leveraging Panama's dollarized clearing architecture; no local conversion step is required. The wire must transmit (i) the PIF's registered foundation number or the beneficiary company's tax ID, (ii) a reference to the governing board resolution, (iii) the economic purpose code aligned with IMF BPM6/ECB payment classification: use 'P600' for equity capital investment, 'G100' for a capital contribution to an enterprise, 'P340' for intercompany service transfers, and 'P500' for royalties or license fees where applicable. For sums above USD 10,000, the receiving bank will require a sworn source-of-funds statement, the founder's 12-month bank records, and the beneficiary's tax or incorporation document; for sums above USD 100,000, a Panamanian legal opinion and full UBO declaration are typically demanded. The foundation must also provide the bank's compliance questionnaire (Cuestionario General de Cumplimiento) and undergo the SBP's verification protocol. To avoid frozen funds, the first wire should be preceded by a pre-approval file containing the board resolution, the notarized signatory mandate, the SOF pack, and the deal-specific purpose statement.Outbound disbursements from the PIF and its overseas restaurant chain are settled through three preferred rails. First, supplier payments for condiment raw materials, packaging, and equipment are executed by SWIFT MT103 using the 'P340' or 'P500' purpose codes with full invoice, bill of lading, and pro-forma references. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium feasibility for a PIF Group. Consequently, the Primary Treasury Rail must be a Tier-1 licensed commercial bank; offshore EMIs (e.g., EU-licensed or UK-licensed e-money firms) may be used only as auxiliary aggregation tools for the overseas operating subsidiary's petty cash and local-currency soft currency payments, subject to the group's cash concentration policy and the operating jurisdiction's own EMI authorization. Any EMI holding group client money above USD 10,000 per day must be contractually backed by an SBP-compliant correspondent bank, and the PIF treasury must obtain an annual independent audit of the EMI provider.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% general corporate income tax rate on annual net taxable income exceeding USD 1,500,000; a reduced 15% rate applies to taxpayers whose annual net taxable income is USD 1,500,000 or less (Articles 699 and 700, Panama Fiscal Code, as amended by Law 52 of 2016 and Cabinet Decree No. 16 of 2016).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% for dividends and distributions paid out of profits already subject to Panamanian corporate income tax; 10% for dividends and distributions paid out of income that was not subject to Panamanian corporate income tax, including foreign-source income exempt under the territoriality principle (Articles 733-735, Fiscal Code, as amended). Beneficiaries of a Private Interest Foundation receiving distributions are subject to the same rates; foundation distributions of the original subscribed capital, however, retain the character of non-taxable capital restitution where documented.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File documentation applies where the aggregate value of controlled transactions between the PIF and related parties, including royalties or management fees charged to an overseas hotpot operating affiliate, exceeds USD 1,000,000 (B/. 1,000,000) per fiscal period; an annual related-party transaction information return is required when aggregate controlled transactions exceed USD 250,000, pursuant to Executive Decree No. 235 of 2018 and the OECD-aligned transfer pricing chapter of the Panama Fiscal Code.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not maintain a separate statutory Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to UAE ESR; substance requirements operate through (i) general anti-avoidance and anti-shell provisions permitting the DGI to recharacterize transactions lacking economic purpose; (ii) the tax-haven and preferential-regime list of Cabinet Decree No. 56 of 1971, which disables the territorial exemption where the substance test is failed; (iii) the beneficial-ownership statutory framework of Law 70 of 2019, requiring filing of ultimate beneficial owners with the Registry; and (iv) limitation-of-benefits clauses in Panama's DTAs. The overseas hotpot PIF must maintain real asset ownership or real active management of the brand and condiment-supply IP, hold board meetings, keep complete accounting records in Panama or accessible in Panama, and demonstrate that the foundation is not merely a mail-dropping shell in order to preserve territorial exemption and treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 100% to 300% of the evaded tax (Articles 106 and 713, Fiscal Code), default interest at 1% per month, late-filing fines from USD 50 to USD 500, closure of establishments, and criminal prosecution for defraudación fiscal under the Fiscal Code and the criminal procedure applicable to fiscal crimes.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members are not liable for the PIF's debts in the ordinary course, but Panama courts and the DGI pierce the foundation veil where the entity is used for fraud, evasion, or money laundering; signatories may incur personal civil restitution, asset attachments, travel bans, disqualification from serving as directors of Panamanian regulated entities, and imprisonment if fraudulent intent is proven.
Shell Entity and Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: The DGI may disregard the foundation as a sham, reallocate foreign-source income as Panamanian-source on the basis of management location, reassess tax at 25% with additional penalties of 100% to 300% and monthly 1% interest; Panama Law 70 of 2019 imposes fines on entities filing false or incomplete beneficial-ownership declarations, and a landmark non-compliance case can trigger registry cancellation, adverse FATF/Gafilat references, and revocation of the PIF's license to operate.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors are individually exposed to administrative penalties and disqualification from holding office in Panama-regulated entities; where the shell structure is used to conceal assets or launder proceeds, piercing the veil results in personal liability for civil restitution, seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and criminal imprisonment.
AML/CFT Deficiencies in Restaurant Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Violation of Panama's AML/CFT obligations under Law 23 of 2015 and related DNFBP regulations exposes the foundation to administrative fines of up to USD 1,000,000 per infraction, freezing and confiscation of funds, cancellation of the financial or commercial license, and criminal money-laundering prosecution carrying imprisonment of 5 to 12 years.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face personal criminal liability where willful blindness is proven; the criminal court may impose travel bans, provisional detention, personal fines, asset-confiscation orders, and permanent bans from holding AML-regulated positions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Cross-Border Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: The DGI may make transfer pricing adjustments recharacterizing the profit in the host jurisdiction, assess the resulting Panamanian tax at 25% (or the applicable reduced rate on reassessed Panamanian-source income), impose penalties of 100% to 300% of the adjustment amount, add 1% monthly default interest, and deny the foundation beneficial ownership of the hotpot brand or condiment IP licensed abroad.

Director personal liability: Directors and the foundation council who sign and approve artificial pricing documentation are jointly liable for the unpaid difference after veil-piercing; in aggravated cases of egregious mispricing, the same conduct is prosecuted as tax evasion, subjecting signatories to imprisonment, travel bans, civil restitution, and disqualification from serving on boards of Panamanian commercial entities.
