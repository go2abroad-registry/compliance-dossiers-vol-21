Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Article 13 of Law 25 of 1995 requires an initial endowment of not less than US$10,000 (equivalent of B/.10,000). No escrow or blocked bank account is mandatory: the endowment obligation may be satisfied by the Founder's written declaration of contribution of cash, assets, receivables, or inventory, which the first Foundation Council must accept and record in the incorporation minutes.

Funding & unlock path: Visto Bueno de Bomberos (Fire Safety Certificate) issued by the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá before opening, covering fire extinguishers, emergency exits, signage, and evacuation plans; structural load inspection of racks and mezzanines by the Municipal Engineering Directorate where storage height exceeds 1.8 m; registration of the workplace with the Ministerio de Trabajo y Desarrollo Laboral (MITRADEL) and enrollment of personnel in the Caja de Seguro Social (CSS); compliance with the industrial health and safety rules of Decree 40 de 1968, including first aid equipment, fire hydrants, ventilation, and a written health and safety plan.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Panama; digital portal: E-TAX 3.0 (Sistema Integrado de Tributación) — etax.mef.gob.pa, with SIME web services for filings and fiscal clearance.

E-government filing portal

The Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must first be duly registered at the Registro Público under Law 25 of 1995. Before commencing commercial activity, the foundation, through its resident legal representative and a licensed public accountant, must apply to the DGI for a RUC (Registro Unico de Contribuyentes) via the E-TAX 3.0 portal or DGI window, attaching the certified foundation charter, public registration certificate, appointment record of the foundation council, and notarised identification of the resident agent. The DGI assigns the RUC and, where commercial activity is declared, concurrently registers the foundation for ITBMS and relevant withholding registrations; the foundation must separately register with the Caja de Seguro Social (CSS) if it has Panamanian employees.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Panamanian law. A PIF is a distinct juridical person taxed at entity level exclusively on Panama-source income under the territorial regime (Código Fiscal); foreign-source income of the foundation remains outside Panamanian tax. Available elections include: (i) irrevocable election to be treated as an ordinary commercial taxpayer when the foundation engages in habitual business; (ii) election into the small-taxpayer simplified ITBMS regime for gross income at or below PAB 36,000 per annum; (iii) elective registration in the official exporter registry to access 0% export supplies; and (iv) election into the SEM multinational headquarters regime under Law 41 of 2019. Hybrid mismatch and transparent entity elections are not available; Law 254 of 2021 controlled foreign corporation rules apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No minimum ITBMS registration threshold for exporters or persons performing taxable transfers in Panama, but an optional simplified small-taxpayer regime applies where annual gross income does not exceed PAB 36,000; registration becomes mandatory at PAB 36,000 or immediately upon commencement of taxable activities, whichever is earlier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% ITBMS applies where (i) tangible goods are definitively exported and depart Panamanian customs territory under a valid DUA; or (ii) the services qualify as an export of services — rendered to a non-resident recipient, with legal and economic benefit occurring abroad, under Law 18 of 2019 and DGI Resolution ITBMS-2014. A PIF supplying fulfillment, kitting, and light assembly may zero-rate only the service element that is contractually and documentarily tied to goods that are exported to the foreign principal, evidenced by cross-border contracts, foreign purchase orders, title-transfer documents, and completed customs entries; logistics and warehousing consumed physically inside Panama for domestic goods remain subject to 7% ITBMS.

  • Declaración Unica Aduanera (DUA) — Customs Export Declaration, definitively accepted by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas
  • Bill of Lading (House or Master B/L, AWB, or truck manifest) evidencing physical exit of goods
  • Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas — inward foreign-currency remittance certificate issued by a Panamanian licensed bank and reported to SIMEX
  • Export commercial invoice issued in E-TAX 3.0 bearing the DGI fiscal QR and marked as export invoice
  • Supporting contract documentation: foreign purchase order, packing list, insurance certificate, and proof of transfer of risk and title

Panama mandates real-time electronic invoicing for all ITBMS registrants through E-TAX 3.0 (phased implementation concluded for all taxpayers). Every invoice, including zero-rated export invoices, must be transmitted before issuance to the DGI fiscal control system via certified API/web service, must receive a unique acceptance folio and QR code, and must be archived digitally for the statutory six-year limitation period; export invoicing additionally requires the export invoice to be linked to the corresponding DUA customs declaration.

DGI audit of export zero-rating claims may recharacterise unsupported exports as taxable domestic supplies, applying 7% ITBMS to the gross value on a retrospective basis, monthly surcharge interest of 1.5% (or fraction thereof), and fines of 10% up to 100% of the omitted tax under Código Fiscal Articles 758-762; fraudulent export zero-rating or false documentation constitutes tax evasion under Código Fiscal Article 775, punishable by 2 to 5 years of imprisonment, exclusion from the exporter registry, forfeiture of goods, and retroactive cancellation of export certificates with full tax clawback and criminal fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility and unrestricted transfer of funds are guaranteed for both current and capital account transactions. Panama is a fully dollarized economy; the US Dollar is legal tender and the Balboa is a nominal unit at 1:1 parity. Profit remittance and capital repatriation are unlimited, but outbound dividends and branch remittances sourced from Panamanian-source income attract withholding tax under the territorial tax regime. Foreign currency accounts are permitted and no surrender or conversion requirements apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement exists to surrender export proceeds to any monetary authority, nor is any compulsory conversion into local currency mandated. Exporters and service providers may hold export receipts in US Dollars or any freely convertible foreign currency in accounts at licensed Panamanian banks or foreign banks. However, cash transactions exceeding USD 10,000 must be reported under SBP AML/CFT rules, and disciplinary tax non-compliance may require furnishing of bank records to the DGI.

Central bank supervision: No formal central bank exists for Panama. Monetary policy is effectively determined through official dollarization (US Federal Reserve), while the Banco Nacional de Panamá serves as fiscal agent and clearing conduit for government transactions. Prudential supervision of banks is vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), and the Dirección Nacional de Recaudación (DGI) enforces tax and FATCA/CRS reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility is Low as a primary settlement rail and Moderate as an auxiliary receivables/payables channel. Panamanian law requires any payment service provider conducting deposit-taking or clearing activities for residents to be licensed by the SBP. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Airwallex, Revolut Business, or Payoneer can support invoicing and card payables, but they cannot replace a SBP-regulated local clearing account for ACH/Direct wire settlements. The Virtual Asset Service Provider Act (Law 62 of 2023, updated by Law 80 of 2024) further restricts unlicensed crypto/fintech overlay activities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound injections into Panama for a PIF must be routed through an SBP-licensed correspondent or domestic clearing bank using SWIFT MT103 wire with a clear beneficiary account designation. A best-practice purpose code should use generic UBO (Unstated Beneficial Owner) or more explicit narrative in field 70, e.g., 'Contribution to Foundation Capital — ISIC 5210 Fulfilment/Kitting' for capital, and 'Trade Payment for Warehousing Services' for operational flows. For capital injections exceeding USD 50,000, the receiving bank will require a board or council resolution authorizing the contribution, a source-of-funds statement certified by the remitter's home bank, and an updated foundation registry certificate. No exchange control approval is required, but corresponding anti-money-laundering declarations must identify the ultimate originating entity.Permissible outbound clearing rails include: (a) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier settlements and to pay intra-group subcontractors; (b) Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or usance) issued via a Panamanian confirming bank for large kitting raw material imports; (c) ACH/Direct (Panama ACH is USD-denominated via the Panama Automated Clearing House; Nacha-style network only for interbank local credits); (d) documentary collections (cash against documents) for low-risk repeated payments; (e) Airwallex or similar proprietary wallet-to-bank transfers only for non-resident-apocalyptic flows, with parallel reconciliation through a local operational bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends declared or distributed out of Panamanian-source profits. Dividends paid from foreign-source accumulated profits are outside Panama's territorial tax and are not subject to the 10% dividend withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three calendar months after fiscal year-end. For a December 31 fiscal year-end the filing deadline is March 31 of the following year. Advance income tax payments may be required monthly or quarterly during the tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Balboa (PAB/USD). A PIF engaged in related-party transactions with an aggregate annual value above USD 1,000,000 must prepare and file transfer pricing documentation (Local File). Master File and CbCR must accompany fiscal periods where the multinational group consolidated revenue exceeds USD 50,000,000. Related-party service contracts below the threshold must still follow the arm's length principle and retain contemporaneous six-factor analysis.

Economic substance scope: Panama's ESR framework (Law 70 of 2019 and related Decrees) applies to entities, including Private Interest Foundations, engaged in geographically mobile activities and claiming tax benefits tied to fiscal domicile. For ISIC 5210 kitting and fulfillment, the PIF must demonstrate real physical presence in Panama or in the treaty jurisdiction: leased warehouse premises, operational staff, inventory management systems, and supervision of core logistics functions. Substance is evaluated proportionately to the value of the fulfillment services. A PIF that merely holds a warehouse address and routes payments through Panama is presumed to be a shell entity and loses access to treaty and territorial-source advantages.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Tributaria)

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 50% of the evaded tax plus statutory interest and full reassessment. Criminal conviction carries fines of PAB 10,000 to PAB 100,000 and imprisonment from 5 to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Courts are authorized to pierce the PIF veil when the foundation is used to evade tax. Directors, officers, and beneficial owners are personally liable for restitution of unpaid taxes, face travel bans and exit restrictions, and may be criminally imprisoned.
Shell Company / ESR Deficiency

Statutory sanction: DGI and the Public Registry may cancel the foundation's registration, freeze its assets, declare the relevant transactions void for tax purposes, and impose fines up to PAB 50,000. The shell classification also triggers automatic exchange of information to the ultimate beneficial owner's tax residence.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell PIF are jointly and severally liable for taxes, penalties, and interest. Personal asset forfeiture, disqualification from acting as foundations' directors, and five-year entry bans to Panama are enforceable against responsible officers.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DGI may recharacterize the related-party transaction, add deemed taxable income under the approved arm's length methodologies, deny expense deductions, and levy a 30% penalty on the transfer pricing adjustment plus interest from the underpayment date.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors who sign false transfer pricing reports are personally liable for up to 100% of the assessed deficiency when fraud is proven. The same individuals are subject to criminal penalties for fiscal fraud and should expect civil restitution proceedings.
AML and Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Money laundering conviction under Panamanian Criminal Code Article 254 carries imprisonment of 5 to 8 years, asset forfeiture of up to twice the laundered value, and a criminal fine against the PIF of up to PAB 1,000,000. The foundation may also be debarred from international banking counterparties.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers of the PIF are personally named in criminal prosecutions if they processed tainted funds knowingly or with willful blindness. Personal assets are subject to freezing, international arrest warrants may be issued, and individuals are barred from future Panamanian fiduciary roles.
