Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; official taxpayer portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI (sedeelectronica.dgi.gob.pa) for RUC issuance, ITBMS filing, and export zero-rating refunds; complementary trade window: Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior (VUCE) administered by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF) obtains Panamanian tax identity as follows: (1) appoint a licensed Panamanian Resident Agent and execute the Acto Constitutivo (Foundation Charter) and Reglamento before a notary in accordance with Law 25 of 1995; (2) record the charter in the Registro Público de Panamá and settle the registry fee to obtain the foundation registration folio; (3) file the RUC application with the DGI in person or via Sede Electrónica using the Resident Agent's digital signature, attaching the certified charter, public registry certificate, Resident Agent identification, and a Panamanian correspondence address; (4) upon issuance of the RUC, elect the configuration for a private interest foundation and enroll as an ITBMS contributor/exporter where export trading in physical sugar is contemplated; (5) register with ANA for the exporter code and obtain VUCE credentials for electronic customs export declarations. No operating license is required for wholesale offshore commodity trading provided no taxable supplies are realized for consumption inside Panama.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — Panama levies income tax exclusively on Panamanian-source income. The PIF is a separate juridical person; foreign-source income from wholesale physical-vessel trading of VHP raw cane sugar, including polarization differentials, is outside the Panamanian tax net. Panamanian-source income is taxable at entity level; distributions to non-resident beneficiaries are not Panamanian taxable events. The PIF bears only the flat annual franchise fee (currently USD 300) unless it establishes local economic nexus.

Available election forms

Panama does not employ a US-style check-the-box election. The PIF's Reglamento functions as the determinative election instrument: the founder may elect reserved powers, revocability or irrevocability, beneficiary contingent rights, and administration rules, which in turn establish the foundation's transparency or opaqueness for foreign reporting. Under domestic law there are no hybrid-mismatch elections because the territorial rule is dispositive: foreign-source sugar-trading gains remain outside Panamanian taxation while Panamanian-source activities are taxable to the PIF as a separate entity. Panama's OECD-aligned tax reform (including CFC and deduction-denial rules) fragments abuse of the territorial boundary by non-resident intermediate vehicles.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory de minimis threshold: a PIF carrying on taxable transfers of goods or services in Panama must register for ITBMS from the first habitual operation under the Tax Code, irrespective of turnover. Small and micro taxpayers may elect a simplified installment regime (annual gross receipts ceiling per DGI implementing rules, currently approximated at USD 150,000–200,000) which substitutes standard compliance with presumptive monthly payments but does not remove the zero-rating obligation for exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to export sales of VHP raw cane centrifugal sugar where: (a) the seller is a registered ITBMS exporter with valid RUC and ANA exporter code; (b) the sugar is physically dispatched from Panamanian customs territory to a non-resident purchaser; (c) the export e-invoice is issued at 0% with the statutory export clause and cleared by the DGI; (d) the shipment is covered by a customs export declaration itemizing the sugar under HS 1701.13/1701.14 with polarization certificate; (e) title and risk pass outside Panama, establishing foreign business risk; and (f) export proceeds are received in Panama through banking channels or credited to the PIF's account in USD/Balboa, evidencing an actual flow of funds. Ancillary offshore services (freight coordination, commodity arbitrage advisory) rendered to non-residents and consumed abroad are likewise zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration — electronic declaration submitted via VUCE and accepted by ANA showing HS 1701.14, shipment weight, and polarization degree
  • Commercial Export Invoice — DGI-cleared electronic invoice issued at 0% ITBMS with export clause and PIF RUC/ITBMS identification
  • Bill of Lading — on-board ocean bill of lading evidencing physical vessel shipment to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank certificate, SWIFT MT103, or revolving LC realization evidencing receipt of export proceeds in Panama within the 180-day evidentiary window
  • Packing List and Certificate of Origin — where preferential tariff treatment is claimed
  • Polarization/SGS or equivalent quality certificate matching contractual polarization settlement terms
  • Foundation Council Resolution — authorizing the sale, the bank account instructions, and the ultimate beneficiary designation for AML/CFT audit purposes

Mandatory DGI electronic invoicing replacing fiscal printers for all ITBMS registrants, including PIF exporters of raw cane sugar. The Facturación Electrónica regime operates through the Sede Electrónica and requires pre-issuance clearance of each electronic invoice: generation of XML/JSON document bearing the issuer's digital certificate, DGI sequential control ticket, and printable QR/digital stamp. Transmission and acceptance occur in near-real-time for large and exporting taxpayers; the cleared export invoice becomes the operative instrument for the 0% rate and refund credits. ANA/VUCE reconciles the customs export declaration against the cleared invoice, making electronic clearance a de facto precondition for port dispatch.

DGI and ANA conduct cross-validated audits of export zero-rating. If physical export is unsubstantiated or proceeds are not received within the 180-day practice window (absent an authorized extension), the supply is recharacterized as domestic and taxable: the DGI assesses the full 7% ITBMS on the gross invoice plus retroactive interest, monthly default surcharges to the statutory cap, and fines typically ranging from USD 500 to USD 50,000 depending on aggravating circumstances. False declarations, fabricated bills of lading, or circular LC arrangements constitute tax fraud (defraudación tributaria) punishable by imprisonment of two to five years and criminal fines, together with cancellation of exporter status, exclusion from VUCE/ANA facilitation programs, and extension of the clawback limitation period. Anti-avoidance scrutiny reaches the PIF's foundational instrument where the foundation is interposed as a sham: the founder and beneficiaries may be jointly and severally assessed, and transactions never leaving customs territory also trigger AML/CFT referrals.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account. There are no exchange controls, no convertibility permits, no approval thresholds for cross-border transfers, and no profit-repatriation hurdles. Dividends paid from Panamanian-source income attract a 10% withholding tax, but genuinely extraterritorial wholesale sugar-trade margins booked through the PIF fall outside Panama's territorial tax base and are fully remittable by wire without restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline. USD-denominated export proceeds from physical VHP raw cane sugar shipments may be retained freely in domestic or offshore accounts.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) acts as the banking and financial regulator; Banco Nacional de Panamá serves as the State's fiscal agent and government clearing agent. Panama has no central bank issuing domestic currency: the economy is fully dollarized, the Balboa circulates at a fixed 1:1 with the US Dollar, and US Federal Reserve Notes are legal tender; monetary conditions and high-value USD clearing are de facto anchored by the US Federal Reserve system and US correspondent banks (Fedwire/CHIPS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium with material legal constraints. Panama has no EU/UK-style EMI licence; the SBP regulates payment companies, and Law 697 of 2023 (crypto-asset framework) covers virtual-asset service providers. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-authorised or EU-licensed) may onboard a Panama PIF but generally apply enhanced due diligence and often reject foundation structures as trust-equivalent. The legally robust alternative is a Panama-licensed payment company (Sociedad de Pago) integrated with a Tier-1 bank clearing USD via Fedwire/CHIPS correspondents; pure unregulated crypto corridors are not recommended for ISIC 4631 physical sugar trade.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound injections execute by SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire from the contributor's bank to the PIF's USD operational account at a Panamanian Tier-1 bank. The remittance information field must state 'capital contribution / dotación de capital', referencing the foundation Ficha number and the council resolution date. The receiving bank retains the credit advice as an AML/EDD record; no central-bank declaration, FX permit, or exchange-control filing is required. For contributions above USD 1,000,000, provide the council resolution plus a documented source-of-funds file to pre-empt reporting triggers under Law 23 of 2015.Supplier disbursements use SWIFT MT103 from the PIF's Panama account; for cargo-size tranches, the PIF's bank issues an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (UCP 600, MT700) or a revolving LC against presentation of a clean on-board bill of lading, certificate of origin, VHP raw cane sugar polarization certificate, phytosanitary certificate, and warehouse/vessel receipts. 10% statutory complementary dividend tax withheld by the entity on distributions paid out of Panamanian-source taxable profits; distributions funded exclusively with foreign-source non-taxable trading profits bear a 0% withholding rate, provided the underlying profits were not derived from a Panamanian permanent establishment.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months after the fiscal year-end; for calendar-year taxpayers the deadline is March 15 of the following year, with a single one-month extension available upon justified application to the DGI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 1,000,000 (USD 1,000,000 at 1:1 Balboa peg) of gross revenue in the immediately preceding fiscal year triggers the obligation to prepare transfer-pricing documentation (Estudio de Precios de Transferencia / Local File); the mandatory TP report and related-party annex must be filed with the DGI where related-party flows exceed B/. 1,000,000; MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of at least USD 750,000,000 must also file the Country-by-Country notification in the DGI electronic module.

Economic substance scope: Panama has not enacted a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code, but the DGI applies a substantive anti-abuse doctrine through three channels: (i) the territorial-source rule excludes foreign merchanting profit only where the PIF's council actually contracts, bills, collects and assumes voyage risk outside Panama; (ii) the Beneficial Ownership Registry under Law 23 of 2015 requires identifying the ultimate beneficiary and maintaining local records with a Panamanian registered agent; and (iii) Law 25 of 1995 conditions the PIF's legal existence on a resident registered agent, real minute books and authentic accounting. A sugar-trading PIF that merely stores documents without operational control over the cargo risks re-attribution of its entire margin to the supplier or beneficiary, triggering 25% CIT, 10% dividend WHT, recargos, and criminal exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing in Sugar Arbitrage

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud under Fiscal Code Articles 719-A et seq. (Law 8 of 2010, as amended): 2 to 5 years' imprisonment for basic fraud and 5 to 8 years for aggravated fraud where the amount defrauded exceeds B/. 50,000, plus a fine of 100% to 500% of the evaded tax, statutory recargos, and moratory interest; recidivist taxpayers face permanent cancellation of DGI registration and disqualification from the state supplier registry.

Director personal liability: The PIF council (órgano de gobierno), its legal representative and any director signing the false return are jointly and severally liable (responsables solidarios) for the tax deficiency; individual criminal prosecution is routine, with preventive detention, travel bans (veda de salida), passport surrender and freezing of personal bank accounts; Panamanian appellate courts have sustained 5-year sentences for directors who signed forged sugar-arbitrage invoice declarations.
Shell Company and PIF Abuse

Statutory sanction: The DGI may re-characterize the PIF under the territorial-source and anti-abuse doctrines, imposing 25% CIT on the entire related-party margin and a 10% dividend tax on deemed distributions, plus an administrative penalty of 20% of the tax due; the Registro Público may dissolve the foundation for non-compliance with Law 25 of 1995 registry obligations and levy monetary fines for the registered agent's failure to maintain current records.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: council members and the registered agent become jointly liable for dissolution costs, unpaid taxes and interest; participating individuals face a 10-year disqualification from holding Panamanian directorships or registered-agent licenses, and the beneficial owner's foreign assets become reachable through Panamanian international judicial cooperation and mutual legal assistance orders.
AML, LC/Wire Structuring and Sanctions Evasion

Statutory sanction: AML penalties under Law 23 of 2015 and FATF-aligned regulations: fines of USD 100,000 to USD 1,000,000 against the entity and up to USD 500,000 against the compliance officer; trade-based money laundering (Blanqueo de Capitales, Criminal Code Article 254) is punishable by 5 to 12 years' imprisonment; the proceeds of the revolving LC and the cargo itself are subject to freezing and in rem forfeiture under Law 24 of 2011; the PIF's access to Swift wire and correspondent banking may be terminated, with secondary sanctions exposure from OFAC and the EU.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or knowingly disregard the structuring are treated as principals in the money-laundering offence and face preventive arrest, travel bans, and asset forfeiture; the bank's compliance department must file Suspicious Activity Reports naming the PIF council members; the Superintendencia de Bancos may bar the directors from the Panamanian financial system for up to 15 years and disallow them from serving on any regulated entity.
