Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Impuestos (DGI) now integrated into the Autoridad Nacional de Ingresos Públicos (ANIP), Ministry of Economy and Finance; taxpayer portal: Sede Electrónica de ANIP (anip.gob.pa).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must apply to the ANIP (formerly DGI) for a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) by submitting, in Spanish, the foundation charter, apostilled or notarized translations, proof of the foundation's beneficial ownership and registered agent in Panama, and an address for service of process. Once the RUC is assigned, the foundation receives a taxpayer credential and must complete electronic registration for ITBMS and any other applicable taxes. For a Private Interest Foundation (PIF), the annual franchise tax of USD 300 must be filed through the ANIP portal, and direct income tax filings are required only in respect of Panama-source income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope; Entity_Level_Taxation for Panama-source income; Pass_Through_Eligible is not available under Panamanian domestic tax law.

Available election forms

No check-the-box mechanism exists for foundations. A PIF may elect to be treated as a corporation if it is organized as such, and a taxpayer may elect the simplified 'pequeño contribuyente' ITBMS regime if annual taxable turnover is below the statutory threshold. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized as a domestic Panamanian election; foreign-country classification may nevertheless differ, and the PIF may seek an advanced ruling from ANIP with respect to treaty or territorial allocation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general numeric registration threshold; registration is mandatory upon any taxable transfer of goods or services in Panama. Taxpayers whose annual taxable turnover does not exceed USD 250,000 may opt for the simplified small-taxpayer regime for ITBMS purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the physical export of goods if the goods are legally exported from Panamanian customs territory and delivered to a consignee abroad within the timeframe indicated in the export declaration. For services, zero-rating applies when the service is performed outside Panama or directly benefits a non-resident recipient, and no local input is used in Panama. The seller must possess a completed customs export declaration, evidence of the goods' departure from Panama, and proof that payment proceeds have been received in Panama in accordance with central bank rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA / Formulario de Exportación)
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List corresponding to the exported goods

Panama mandates electronic invoicing through the ANIP 'Factura Electrónica' system, with XML schema and digital signature certificate. Invoices must be transmitted to and validated by ANIP in near-real-time before issuance to the purchaser, and each validated invoice receives a unique authorization code. Exporters and manufacturers must integrate their ERP systems with ANIP's web services or API; physically stamped or pre-printed invoices no longer apply to general-regime taxpayers.

ANIP may deny zero-rating and claw back any credited VAT or refund if the export documentation is incomplete, forged, or does not establish that the goods left Panama within the statutory period. Civil penalties range from 50% to 100% of the omitted tax, plus monthly surcharges and interest; repeated offenses or fraudulent documentation trigger criminal prosecution under Panamanian tax law, carrying imprisonment of up to five years, and disqualification from future export refund claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility with zero exchange controls on both current and capital accounts. Panama has observed IMF Article VIII obligations since 1946 and maintains 'No Separate Legal Tender' status. Capital injections, trade settlements, profit remittances, and PIF beneficiary distributions cross the border freely and are not subject to central bank approval. A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) is taxed territorially under Law 25 of 1995, as amended; foreign-source manufacturing and cross-border trading income falls outside the Panamanian tax base, and distributions to non-resident beneficiaries carry no Panamanian withholding tax, subject to the economic-substance and RBF transparency regime (Executive Decree 254/2018, as amended).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory time limit, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation requirement for export proceeds. Because the U.S. Dollar is the domestic legal tender, export collections in USD may be held indefinitely in local bank accounts, and foreign-currency balances (e.g., CNY, JPY, EUR) may be maintained freely at SBP-licensed banks with no surrender obligation and no pre-export financing mandate.

Central bank supervision: None. The Republic of Panama has no central bank and is fully dollarized. Banking licensure, prudential regulation, and AML/CFT supervision are vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Superintendency of Banks of Panama, SBP). Fiscal administration, tax identification (RUC), and the beneficial-owner registry (RBF) are administered by the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) under the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas. The U.S. Dollar is legal tender (Law 84 of 1904), with Balboa coins at 1:1 parity.

Low-Medium. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on Panama-source taxable profits; 0% on foreign-source income under the territorial tax system applicable to Private Interest Foundations (PIFs).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding on dividends paid out of Panama-source taxable profits; 0% withholding on PIF distributions to non-resident beneficiaries when the underlying assets and income are foreign-source.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 2,000,000 (Panamanian Balboa, at parity with USD) gross income in the preceding tax year, provided cross-border related-party transactions exceed B/. 1,000,000; Master File and Local File are mandatory above this threshold. Country-by-Country Report filing applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue exceeding B/. 75,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Although Panama does not impose a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime identical to certain Caribbean jurisdictions, DGI and OECD-driven substance reviews require a PIF relying on territorial exemption to demonstrate substantive activity: local management decisions, physical premises adequate for the declared operations, original accounting records, and, for manufacturing, a genuine plant or controlled third-party production chain. A shell PIF with no real assets or employees will be attacked as a sham arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion penalties of 3 to 6 years of imprisonment; administrative restitution of the full tax evaded plus a monthly late-payment surcharge of 5%, capped at 50% of the tax; cancellation of the RUC and registration penalties of up to B/. 10,000.

Director personal liability: Directors, protector, and resident agent are jointly and severally liable for restitution; Panamanian courts may pierce the PIF veil, imposing personal asset seizure, travel bans, and disqualification from managing any Panamanian legal entity for up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DGI recharacterization under Panamanian general anti-abuse principles: reclassified Panama-source income taxed at 25%, penalty of up to 50% of the understated tax, corrected returns for a 5-year limitation period, and suspension of the foundation’s legal personality for fiscal purposes.

Director personal liability: Founders and managing board members may be personally liable for assessed taxes; Panamanian courts may issue freezing orders over beneficiary accounts and impose travel bans pending resolution of the sham determination.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Financing of Terrorism

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015 and Financial Analysis Unit rules, administrative fines may reach B/. 5,000,000 for serious violations; criminal sanctions include imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, seizure of money flows, and revocation of the foundation’s authorizations.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent who knowingly allow AML breaches face criminal conviction, personal fines, travel bans, and prohibition from holding financial or corporate service licenses in Panama.
Commercial Piracy / IP Counterfeiting

Statutory sanction: Panamanian copyright and industrial-property provisions impose imprisonment of 2 to 4 years, seizure and destruction of molds and finished goods, civil damages for lost profits plus up to 50% of gross infringing revenue, and DGI reassessment of royalty-bearing income that was hidden as foreign-source profit.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who order, authorize, or benefit from counterfeit production are criminally liable and subject to personal fine, extradition-eligible arrest, travel bans, and permanent disqualification from managing companies engaged in manufacturing.
