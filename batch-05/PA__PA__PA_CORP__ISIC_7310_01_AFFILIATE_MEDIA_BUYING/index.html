Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; official taxpayer self-service portal: Sede Electrónica e-Tax at https://etax.mef.gob.pa; institutional website: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign legal entity deriving Panamanian-source income or performing taxable services in Panama must obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) before issuing invoices or filing returns. Procedure: (1) file apostilled corporate existence documents with the Registro Público de Panamá, appoint a resident agent, and, if operating through a local branch, register the branch (Matrícula de Agencia); (2) file the municipal Aviso de Operación in the relevant corregimiento; (3) complete DGI RUC registration through the e-Tax portal using an accredited accountant or legal representative, including fiscal domicile declaration and foreign address details; (4) receive the RUC certificate and e-Tax user credentials. A foreign entity without a permanent establishment may register as a non-resident taxpayer (Contribuyente No Domiciliado) with a designated local fiscal representative. Estimated processing time: 2–4 weeks; the same RUC serves as the ITBMS VAT registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: Panamanian-source income is subject to corporate income tax (ISR), while foreign-source income is exempt. A Sociedad Anónima is a full entity-level taxpayer for ISR and ITBMS and is not eligible for pass-through treatment; no check-the-box mechanism exists. Dividends paid from foreign-source income remain outside Panamanian dividend withholding taxation.

Available election forms

Panama provides no US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election. Available statutory elections: (a) simplified small taxpayer regime under Law 41 of 2017 for annual gross sales below USD 10 million, with an optional final tax of 2.5% of gross sales; (b) Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007 for qualifying headquarters services; (c) election to treat cross-border services to a non-resident recipient as zero-rated ITBMS export services where the use/benefit test is met; (d) election to distribute dividends out of declared legal reserves to apply the 5% dividend withholding rate instead of 10%; (e) application to the DGI for advance rulings/agreements confirming export zero-rating treatment for digital advertising services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de-minimis economic threshold: every Panamanian S.A. is legally required to register as an ITBMS taxpayer in the RUC before conducting its first taxable transaction. An elective simplified regime (RSPC/small taxpayer aggregation) is available where annual gross sales or imports remain below USD 10 million; above that threshold, full monthly ITBMS filing applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Gabinete Decree No. 11 of 1985 and DGI interpretations, a 0% ITBMS rate applies to: (a) physical exports of goods where title passes to a non-resident buyer and the goods leave Panamanian customs territory; (b) services rendered to a beneficial user who is a non-resident without a Panamanian permanent establishment, provided the service is effectively used, exploited, or has its economic effect outside Panama; and (c) qualifying services of exportation, including performance-marketing and digital advertising media buying, where the campaigns target non-Panama audiences under contract with a foreign advertiser and are paid for from foreign bank accounts. Eligibility is substantive rather than formal: the exporter must prove the foreign beneficiary's residency, the export contract, the invoicing chain, and cross-border settlement; where the advertisement benefit is deemed to accrue in Panama, 7% ITBMS applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Exportación via SIGA/ANA) or DGI-recognized export certificate
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (Conocimiento de Embarque) evidencing the foreign consignee and actual departure from Panamanian territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificación de Remesa del Banco / SWIFT MT103) proving cross-border payment from the non-resident client
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order, service agreement, and performance metrics (ad server/geolocation reports) identifying the foreign beneficiary
  • Signed affidavit or certificate of non-use in Panama confirming that the service benefit accrued outside Panamanian territory

Panama's electronic invoicing system (Facturación Electrónica) is mandatory in phased tranches under DGI Resolution No. 201-5723 of 2019 and implementing regulations. Large taxpayers (generally those with gross annual income above USD 10 million) must issue electronic invoices through DGI-authorized software or the DGI e-Tax portal, incorporating a DGI digital stamp/QR code, and transmit the XML invoice data to the DGI within 24 hours of issuance. Smaller taxpayers may apply for voluntary electronic issuance or use authorized fiscal printing systems until their mandatory phase begins. Monthly ITBMS reconciliation is filed on Form 291; failure to issue or transmit electronic invoices can trigger RUC suspension and administrative fines.

Panama has no foreign-exchange controls or statutory FX repatriation deadline; the Balboa is at par with the USD and the economy is dollarized. However, DGI audits export zero-rating claims through exporter-focused fiscalization programs. Where export zero-rating is unjustified or fraudulently claimed: (a) the underlying ITBMS is retroactively assessed at 7% on the full consideration; (b) a late-payment recargo of 50% of the tax due may be imposed; (c) default interest accrues from the original due date; (d) fines range from 100% to 300% of the omitted tax for inaccurate or false returns; (e) the RUC may be suspended and the exporter authorization revoked; (f) criminal tax fraud (defraudación tributaria) under Law 76 of 2019 may apply, carrying imprisonment from 2 to 5 years, extended to 5–8 years where the evasion exceeds USD 1 million or involves organized criminal structures; (g) the case may be referred to the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) under Law 23 of 2015 for anti-money-laundering investigation, with asset freezing and secondary penalties for assisting professionals.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama uses the United States Dollar as legal tender alongside the Panamanian Balboa at a 1:1 par relationship. There are no exchange control laws, no restrictions on current account or capital account transactions, and no government approval requirement for inbound or outbound fund transfers. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, loan repayments, and capital repatriation are fully convertible and free from FX-specific barriers; however, all flows remain subject to standard AML/CFT cancellation, beneficial ownership verification, and documentary source-of-funds requirements imposed by licensed banks and the SBP. Panamanian withholding or dividend tax obligations apply independently of FX remittance rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

N/A — Panama imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement. Exporters may retain proceeds in USD or any freely convertible currency in any authorised bank account. Receipts from affiliate media buying and digital advertising services are treated as operational inflows and must be supported by underlying contracts, invoices, and traffic/performance reports for banking compliance purposes.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama has no central bank. Bank supervision and prudential regulation are exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). The Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent and payment agent for the State, but does not perform traditional central bank monetary functions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Moderate feasibility as an ancillary clearing layer only. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Mercury, and Payoneer do not hold Panamanian banking licences and cannot lawfully take deposits from, or provide principal clearing account services to, a Panamanian corporation without an SBP authorisation. They may be used for supplementary receivable collection or international vendor disbursements where their operating legislation permits inbound and outbound wires, but funds must ultimately be settled into an account at a Panamanian licensed bank for legal substance and AML credibility. Legal constraints under Law 23 of 2015 require full traceability, robust verification of beneficial owners, and no unnamed payee or account-number-only flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital transfers must be executed by SWIFT MT103 from the beneficial owner's own bank account to the Panama-domiciled corporate operational or escrow account at a licensed bank. The remitee must use a clear purpose identifier in SWIFT field 70, e.g., 'Capital Contribution' or 'Aporte de Capital', and the wire amount must match the bank-approved account mandate. The Panama corporation must provide the shareholder resolution approving the contribution, a copy of the share registry entry (Registro de Accionistas), and a source-of-funds declaration if the transfer exceeds the bank's AML threshold. There is no central bank conversion approval or foreign investment registration required in Panama, but the receiving bank will conduct a documentary compliance review and may request evidence of the underlying commercial purpose.For cross-border trade settlements related to digital advertising and affiliate commissions, the standard clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for single supplier wire transfers and Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) where the vendor requires bank-to-bank settlement security. Payments to major ad platforms and publishers are ordinarily sent via SWIFT using the corporate account's correspondent banking network in the United States, with local Panamanian US-dollar clearing also available through the national ACH and RTGS systems for domestic payments. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (flat corporate rate under Law 8 of 2010; a 15% preferential rate applies to the first B/. 50,000 of taxable income for small taxpayers under the 2019 progressive scale, provided total taxable income does not exceed B/. 50,000).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid, credited or distributed by a Panamanian S.A. out of Panamanian-source taxed profits; the rate increases to 20% for distributions to bearer-share holders where beneficial ownership is not disclosed. Dividends paid out of exempt foreign-source profits can be repatriated without dividend WHT only if the exemption is properly reflected in Form 200 and audited books.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For calendar-year entities, Form 200 is due by 15 April of the following fiscal year, subject to the annual DGI filing calendar assigned by the last digit of the RUC; for non-calendar fiscal years, the return is due within three months and fifteen days after the fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 2,500,000 (Balboa, pegged 1:1 to USD) in annual gross operating revenue triggers mandatory Local File documentation; Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue above USD 10,000,000; controlled transactions exceeding B/. 250,000 require transactional filing schedules.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR, Law 254 of 2023 and DGI implementing guidance) require a Panama-resident S.A. engaged in a relevant activity, including digital advertising and affiliate media buying as a service business, to maintain prescribed economic substance in Panama: physical office premises, locally employed qualified staff proportionate to the activity, real operating expenditure, local board/management minutes, and an annual substance declaration filed with the DGI. A pure mailbox affiliate buyer with no local substance and decision-making conducted abroad may be classified as a shell company, lose the territorial exemption for foreign-source income, be subject to recharacterization of income as Panamanian-source, and face deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Fiscal Code administrative surcharge of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax plus 1.5% monthly default interest; criminal tax fraud under the Penal Code carries imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and monetary fines where the evaded amount exceeds B/. 50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or authorize false returns are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt; courts may impose preventive asset embargoes, travel bans, disqualification from holding office in Panamanian entities, and imprisonment upon conviction.
Shell Company / ESR Violation

Statutory sanction: ESR fines of up to B/. 50,000 per fiscal period; administrative recharacterization of formerly exempt foreign-source income as Panamanian-source, exposing the full profit to 25% corporate tax plus interest; cancellation of the operating license and strike-off from the Registro Público.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent are personally liable for knowingly submitting false substance declarations; sanctions include debarment from acting as resident agents, monetary fines, and criminal liability for perjury or false affidavit where the declaration is used to deceive the DGI or foreign tax authorities.
AML / CFT Breach (Law 23 of 2015)

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under the Penal Code is punishable by imprisonment of 5 to 12 years and fines; UAF (Unidad de Análisis Financiero) administrative penalties may reach B/. 2,000,000; funds are subject to freezing and forfeiture, and affected bank accounts may be closed.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and the designated Compliance Officer (OFC) face personal criminal prosecution for failures in filing Suspicious Transaction Reports (ROS), implementing the AML manual, or identifying ultimate beneficial owners; travel restrictions and disqualification from financial or corporate roles apply.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DGI may make a transfer pricing adjustment increasing Panamanian-source taxable income, impose an additional 40% surcharge on the deficiency, charge 1.5% monthly default interest, and levy fines up to B/. 10,000 for missing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve unsubstantiated intercompany pricing and leave the Panamanian entity undercapitalized may be personally assessed for the transfer pricing deficiency and associated interest if the S.A. is liquidated, insolvent, or found to have disregarded corporate formalities.
