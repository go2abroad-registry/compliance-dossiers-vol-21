Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); operational e-filing and e-invoicing portal: SITe (Sistema Integrado de Tributación Electrónica) at https://sitio.dgi.gob.pa. RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) issuance and all tax returns are processed through SITe or authorized bank collection agents.

E-government filing portal

For a Private Interest Foundation (PIF) incorporated under Ley 25 de 12 de junio de 1995: (i) register the foundation deed with the Registro Público de Panamá (Public Registry) through a licensed attorney/registered agent; (ii) pay the one-time foundation registration fee (USD 350) and the annual flat tax (USD 300, due on or before June 30 each year) using the DGI collection forms (Formulario 100/DGI bank forms); (iii) submit the Aviso de Operación (Notice of Operation) to DGI only if the foundation will conduct Panama-source commercial activity; (iv) apply for an RUC through SITe (Formulario de Inscripción de Contribuyente) if the foundation engages in taxable domestic transactions or wishes to claim ITBMS export zero-rating/credits. For a pure offshore film-licensing PIF (foreign licensees, royalties remitted from abroad, no Panama establishment), RUC registration is not statutorily mandatory; however, voluntary RUC registration facilitates the issuance of export e-invoices and substantiation of the 0% rate. Non-resident founders and beneficiaries are not subject to Panamanian RUC requirements for personal capacity. The local registered agent must confirm identity documents, foundation council appointments, and beneficial ownership data (Ley 254 de 2021) during registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: The PIF is a distinct separate patrimony (patrimonio autónomo) with legal personality. It is neither an income-tax pass-through nor an ordinary corporation; it is treated as a taxable entity only with respect to Panama-source income. Income derived from the licensing, sub-licensing, or distribution of motion picture copyrights to non-residents, with economic exploitation occurring entirely outside Panama, is outside the scope of Panama's Impuesto Sobre la Renta (ISR). If the PIF engages in Panama-source commercial activity, it is taxed at the standard corporate ISR rate of 25% on net Panama-source profit (and dividend distribution rules apply to distributable profits). The annual flat USD 300 foundation tax is a fiscal obligation independent of income and is not an entity-level income tax.

Available election forms

Panama does not maintain a US-style 'check-the-box' or hybrid-mismatch election regime for entity classification. The PIF's hybrid nature is established by its constitutive deed under Ley 25, which may elect to apply trust-like principles (fiduciary duties, beneficiary rights) without converting the foundation into a corporation. Statutory elections available for tax purposes include: (i) election to remain a non-registered offshore foundation (no RUC) versus voluntary RUC registration for export credit purposes; (ii) election by taxpayers with annual gross revenue below USD 36,000 to use the Régimen Simplificado de Pequeño Contribuyente with reduced ITBMS filing obligations; (iii) election to apply the 0% ITBMS export rate by submitting Form 727 with the export documentation bundle; and (iv) election under the SITe platform to use the 'Factura Electrónica de Exportación' modality for each zero-rated transaction. No separate legal personality election is permitted after incorporation; the deed of foundation fixes the hybrid classification at inception.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 36,000 (B/. 36,000; Balboa at par with USD) of annual gross sales — economic activities below this threshold may register under the Régimen Simplificado (simplified small-taxpayer regime) with quarterly payments; activities at or above USD 36,000 require full ITBMS registration and monthly Form 727 filings. Pure export transactions and foreign-source service revenues are not considered taxable events and therefore do not count toward the threshold; an exporter whose gross turnover includes only zero-rated foreign transactions is not required to register solely on that volume.

Panama operates a mandatory electronic invoicing system through SITe. Large and medium taxpayers must issue Factura Electrónica (XML) with a DGI-issued digital authorization code and QR stamp; transmission is performed in real time via machine-to-machine API (WSDL/REST) with the DGI validation platform. For exporters, the 'Factura Electrónica de Exportación' is validated online before dispatch, and the invoice must reference the supporting export evidence (contract, remittance certificate, and, for goods, the customs declaration). The clearance mandate includes digital stamping of each authorized document, generation of the 'Código de Autorización de Impresión' for the QR, and archiving of all XML/PDF records for the statutory 5-year period (7 years for large taxpayers). Micro and small taxpayers may use the DGI free web portal; however, credit/refund of input ITBMS is granted only to formal electronic invoicers. Failure to obtain prior electronic authorization invalidates the document and attracts per-invoice fines under Articles 758 and 760 of the Código Fiscal (up to USD 10,000), plus surcharges.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full free convertibility. The US Dollar is legal tender; the Balboa (PAB) is only a notional unit at a fixed 1:1 parity and no national paper currency is issued. Both capital-account and current-account transactions are liberalized; there are no exchange controls, no prior authorizations, and no profit-remittance or distribution restrictions for foundations. Foreign-currency accounts, including EUR and GBP, are freely held; film-royalty and copyright-licensing receipts may be repatriated and converted without SBP approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or export-receipt surrender requirement. Proceeds from film-licensing exports (ISIC 5911) may be retained indefinitely in USD or any freely convertible currency, and treasury mobilisation of such proceeds requires no central-bank notification.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no traditional central bank. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the prudential supervisor of the banking system; Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent of the State; and the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) directs monetary-fiscal coordination in the fully dollarized economy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Panama has no dedicated Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing statute; fintech payment-service providers must be licensed or sandbox-licensed by the SBP and must operate through an SBP-licensed bank. Offshore digital EMIs cannot directly access Panama's USD clearing rails, are not authorised by the SBP, and are routinely declined as treasury counterparties for Private Interest Foundation accounts. Practical use is limited to ancillary local collection wallets (e.g., licensed fintech partnerships) with the master treasury account remaining at a traditional SBP-regulated bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound funds are routed by SWIFT MT103 to the PIF's SBP-licensed USD account, with payment reference clearly stating 'Film distribution royalty / copyright license fee under ISIC 5911'. The bank performs OFAC and PEP screening, and the PIF must pre-submit the underlying licensing agreement, a beneficial-ownership attestation, and an invoice or billing letter referencing the PA_PIF client identifier. Founder capital injections are documented as gifts or expendable capital under the foundation deed, with full source-of-funds evidence from the founder's bank. No central-bank approval, FX permit, or declarations are required; however, transactions are subject to SBP transaction-reporting rules for wire activity above USD 10,000 and must be traceable to a documented receivable or contribution.Outbound settlement is executed in USD via the PIF's local Panamanian account, with onward SWIFT MT103/MT202 transfers cleared through the bank's New York correspondent (e.g., BNY Mellon, Citibank N.A.). Domestic payments use checks and ACH. For film-print delivery, archival services, dubbing/subtitling, and promotional campaigns, the PIF may also use Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 (SWIFT MT700) and Standby Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT760) as performance guarantees for foreign distributor counterparties. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% standard dividend withholding tax on distributions paid out of profits already subject to Panamanian corporate income tax. A 20% withholding rate applies to distributions made from profits that have not borne Panama tax, such as foreign-source income or tax-exempt income, unless a specific exemption applies.

Box 1 Taxpayer identification: RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), foundation name, fiscal year and registered address. Enter the Panamanian RUC of the PIF. Foreign founders, sponsors and beneficiaries need not be listed on the return, but their identifying data must be available in the foundation AML registry. Late filing imposes an administrative fine of B/. 250–1,000 and a monthly late-filing surcharge of 5% of the tax due, capped at 40% of the tax. Late payment accrues monthly interest at 1.5%, and a 10% enforcement penalty applies if collection proceedings commence. Failure to file after DGI notice can lead to an official assessment with an additional 20–40% overcharge.
Box 2 Panama-source gross income: revenue derived from activities performed, exploited, or situated in Panama, including film licensing or copyright distribution to Panama-based users. Foreign-source royalties from non-Panamanian territories or from non-resident licensees must be excluded entirely. Include only receipts sourced to Panama. Late filing imposes an administrative fine of B/. 250–1,000 and a monthly late-filing surcharge of 5% of the tax due, capped at 40% of the tax. Late payment accrues monthly interest at 1.5%, and a 10% enforcement penalty applies if collection proceedings commence. Failure to file after DGI notice can lead to an official assessment with an additional 20–40% overcharge.
Box 3 Deductions and costs: expenses directly allocable to production of Panamanian-source income, such as amortization of acquired film rights related to Panamanian exploitation, professional fees, and sub-license fees. Attach contracts and invoices. Costs allocable to foreign-source income are not deductible against Panamanian income and should be omitted from the calculation. Late filing imposes an administrative fine of B/. 250–1,000 and a monthly late-filing surcharge of 5% of the tax due, capped at 40% of the tax. Late payment accrues monthly interest at 1.5%, and a 10% enforcement penalty applies if collection proceedings commence. Failure to file after DGI notice can lead to an official assessment with an additional 20–40% overcharge.
Box 4 Net taxable income: Panama-source gross income reduced by allocable allowable deductions. If net taxable income is nil, enter zero. Do not offset foreign losses or expenses of the foundation. Late filing imposes an administrative fine of B/. 250–1,000 and a monthly late-filing surcharge of 5% of the tax due, capped at 40% of the tax. Late payment accrues monthly interest at 1.5%, and a 10% enforcement penalty applies if collection proceedings commence. Failure to file after DGI notice can lead to an official assessment with an additional 20–40% overcharge.
Box 5 Corporate income tax at statutory rate: 25% of net taxable income. Apply 25% only to Panamanian-source taxable income. There is no branch remittance tax on distributions to non-resident beneficiaries. Late filing imposes an administrative fine of B/. 250–1,000 and a monthly late-filing surcharge of 5% of the tax due, capped at 40% of the tax. Late payment accrues monthly interest at 1.5%, and a 10% enforcement penalty applies if collection proceedings commence. Failure to file after DGI notice can lead to an official assessment with an additional 20–40% overcharge.
Box 6 Credits: advance payments made during the year, withholding tax credits suffered on Panamanian-source income, and tax payments already made. Only Panamanian withholdings and DGI deposit receipts are creditable. Foreign withholding tax credits are not taken because foreign-source income is outside the territorial tax base. Late filing imposes an administrative fine of B/. 250–1,000 and a monthly late-filing surcharge of 5% of the tax due, capped at 40% of the tax. Late payment accrues monthly interest at 1.5%, and a 10% enforcement penalty applies if collection proceedings commence. Failure to file after DGI notice can lead to an official assessment with an additional 20–40% overcharge.
Box 7 Balance due or overpayment: final tax payable or amount to be credited/refunded. Any balance due must be paid electronically through DGI by the filing deadline. Overpayments may be applied to future liabilities rather than refunded in most cases. Late filing imposes an administrative fine of B/. 250–1,000 and a monthly late-filing surcharge of 5% of the tax due, capped at 40% of the tax. Late payment accrues monthly interest at 1.5%, and a 10% enforcement penalty applies if collection proceedings commence. Failure to file after DGI notice can lead to an official assessment with an additional 20–40% overcharge.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 2,000,000 (Panamanian Balboa, 1:1 with the US Dollar) in aggregate annual related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File and Master File documentation. A separate annual related-party disclosure (Formulario 21) must be filed with DGI when the aggregate threshold is exceeded or when the taxpayer transacts with related parties in no-tax or low-tax jurisdictions.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (introduced by Law 254 of 2021 and implementing Decrees) apply to any resident legal entity, including a Private Interest Foundation, that carries on a relevant activity. Film copyright licensing and distribution is a relevant activity for ESR purposes. Such a PIF must maintain a physical office and local staff in Panama, perform core income-generating activity in Panama, and keep records demonstrating board oversight. A PIF that merely holds film royalties abroad without local presence is exposed to ESR penalties and possible loss of tax residence certification.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud penalty of one to three times the evaded tax and imprisonment of two to ten years. Administering assessment adds a 25–50% surcharge on understated tax plus monthly interest.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the foundation's veil where directors knowingly sign false returns. Directors face civil restitution, travel bans, asset freezes and imprisonment for the underlying tax crime.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR assessment: annual penalties determined by the DGI, loss of tax residence certificate, real risk of mandatory exchange of information under Panama's tax transparency commitments, and possible cancellation of the foundation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for the foundation's substance obligations. Travel bans, disqualification from serving as resident agent, and personal fines may be imposed for willful ESR non-compliance.
AML / Foundations Law Breach (Law 25 of 1995 and Law 23 of 2015)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to USD 1,000,000 per violation under Law 23 of 2015, cancellation of the PIF registration, and criminal money-laundering penalties of five to ten years imprisonment. The PIF's assets may be frozen and forfeited.

Director personal liability: Directors and resident agents have personal exposure to civil restitution, travel bans and imprisonment when they knowingly enable concealed ownership or money-laundering through the foundation.
Transfer Pricing and Related-Party Noncompliance

Statutory sanction: DGI may adjust the taxpayer's income to reflect arm's length pricing, disallow deductions, impose a 10–20% understatement penalty on the additional tax, and accrue interest from the original filing due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false TP documentation or approve artificially structured royalty flows may be jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax deficiency and may face tax fraud charges for willful evasion.
