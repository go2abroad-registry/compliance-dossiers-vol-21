Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá (Public Registry of Panama), Dirección General, Sección de Personas Jurídicas / Registro Mercantil.

Governing statute

Ley No. 32 de 26 de febrero de 1927 (General Law on Corporations / Sociedades Anónimas), as amended by Ley 23 de 2015 and related decrees; applicable provisions include Articles 8–71 governing the formation, capital, governance, and resident agent requirements of the Panamanian S.A., supplemented by the Commercial Code and Public Registry regulations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Escritura Pública de Constitución / Pacto Social (public deed of incorporation and articles of association) accompanied by the resident agent acceptance and first directors' acceptance, filed for inscription in the Public Registry.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 39 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Panama imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow deposit requirement; the articles must declare an authorized capital and share classes, and issued shares may be subscribed with payment in cash or in kind at the board's determination. The first board resolution records the authorized and issued capital as declared subscribed and fully paid.

Funding & unlock path: Annual fire safety certification from the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá (fire extinguishers, exits, and inspection), municipal structural and occupancy load clearance for the registered office, and workplace health and safety clearance from the Ministerio de Trabajo y Desarrollo Laboral (MITRADEL) for office employees.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); electronic registration and filing portal: Sede Electrónica at https://sede.dgi.gob.pa with informational portal at https://www.dgi.gob.pa. Customs and Authorized Economic Operator (AEO) matters: Dirección General de Aduanas (DGA) through the Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior (VUCEN) and SISCOM.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) must obtain its RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), which serves as the TIN for income tax, ITBMS, customs and AEO filings. Procedure: (1) incorporate by public deed (escritura pública) and register it in the Registro Público de Panamá; (2) appoint and confirm a resident agent (agente residente); (3) obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación (business license) from the district municipality of the registered office; (4) submit the RUC application through Sede Electrónica, or at a DGI service window, attaching the certified deed, resident-agent confirmation, legal representative sworn declaration, registered-office proof and the entity's statutory activity scope (ISIC 7020 management consultancy / supply-chain compliance audit); (5) upon RUC issuance, register as an ITBMS taxpayer when annual taxable turnover exceeds B/.36,000 and register as an employer with the Caja de Seguro Social (CSS) if personnel are hired; (6) use the same RUC for customs declarations through SISCOM/VUCEN and for AEO certification filings with the DGA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a check-the-box regime; the S.A. is automatically an entity-level taxpayer under the territorial system (Panama-source income taxed at 25%; foreign-source income exempt). Elections available: (1) Pequeño Contribuyente simplified regime for annual gross revenue up to B/.200,000 (Ley 74 de 2019); (2) RAD (Régimen Anticipado Definitivo), an elective definitive advance tax on gross receipts creditable against ISR; (3) Advance Pricing Agreements (APA) for transfer-pricing certainty with DGI; (4) election of monthly or quarterly ITBMS filing; (5) exporter credit/refund election for ITBMS saldo a favor; (6) no hybrid mismatch or consolidated filing elections recognized under Panama law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 (Panamanian balboas, 1:1 with the US dollar) of aggregate annual taxable turnover from goods and services; any person exceeding this threshold during a fiscal year must register as an ITBMS taxpayer. Registration is also mandatory for any RUC-holder habitually performing taxable activities; taxpayers below the threshold may register voluntarily.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for the 0% ITBMS rate when the goods physically leave Panama, title transfers to a non-resident buyer, and the exporter files a customs export declaration through SISCOM/VUCEN and repatriates the foreign-currency proceeds through the Panamanian banking system. Services, including AEO certification and supply-chain compliance audit services under ISIC 7020, are zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident legal entity, the service is performed for use or exploitation wholly outside Panama, and the consideration is not attributable to Panama-source activities under the territorial test. The exporter must document contracts, work papers, performance-location memoranda, client due-diligence records and bank credit evidence. ITBMS input credits attributable to zero-rated exports are creditable against output ITBMS and any excess (saldo a favor) may be carried forward or refunded by the DGI. Panama does not codify a strict statutory repatriation date; the 180-day benchmark is applied in DGI and AEO supply-chain audit practice for foreign-exchange repatriation and profit-realization substance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación DUA) filed electronically through SISCOM/VUCEN
  • Bill of Lading (Conocimiento de Embarque), Airway Bill or equivalent transport document evidencing shipment from Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit statement (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) evidencing collection of proceeds from abroad
  • Export commercial invoice (Factura Comercial) and financial settlement evidence
  • Packing List for goods; for services: original contract, foreign client master data, service performance report and place-of-consumption evidence
  • AEO certificate/customs authorization applicable to the export operation and security audit records

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) for all active taxpayers under DGI regulations. Invoices must be issued through the DGI's free e-invoicing platform or an authorized private service provider, transmitted to the DGI in XML format, and carry a unique DGI authorization code (código de autorización) and fiscal QR; the DGI performs real-time digital validation at issuance and pre-authorized numbering ranges are used only in approved offline scenarios. Invoices without DGI authorization are void for ITBMS credit purposes, non-deductible for ISR, and expose the issuer to fines. ITBMS returns and sworn declarations are filed exclusively through Sede Electrónica.

The DGI and DGA jointly audit zero-rating claims and export documentation. Unsupported or fraudulent claims trigger: retrospective reassessment of the 7% ITBMS for the open prescription period (five years) plus monthly default interest, monetary fines up to 100% of the omitted tax under the Código Fiscal, criminal tax-fraud liability of two to five years' imprisonment for knowingly false declarations, cancellation of ITBMS refund/offset rights for all related periods, denial or revocation of AEO certification, and suspension of customs exporting privileges and of the S.A.'s paz y salvo (tax clearance) status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The US Dollar is legal tender alongside the Panamanian Balboa (PAB), which is a purely nominal unit at a 1:1 parity. There are no exchange controls or restrictions on current or capital account transactions. Profit remittances are freely permitted for properly constituted corporations; the only requirement is compliance with FATCA/CRS and anti-money laundering source-of-funds documentation. No separate capital controls exist.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirements exist for export receipts. Exporters are free to retain, transfer, or convert proceeds in any currency. Panama's territory-based tax system imposes no repatriation requirement.

Central bank supervision: No formal central bank exists; Panama operates a dollarized monetary system. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) regulates all banking activity, and Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the fiscal agent for the government.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low regulatory feasibility for offshore digital EMIs. Panama does not issue specialized Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licenses. Foreign EMIs such as Wise or Payoneer cannot maintain prohibited deposit-taking operations without a Panamanian banking license; however, their payment routing services can be used to disburse funds to Panamanian bank accounts via ACH or SWIFT. Such services are legally treated as payment processors, not local deposit-taking institutions, and may expose the company to non-bank counterparty risk. Recommended use is limited to minor operational outflows, not as a primary liquidity rail.

10% final dividend withholding tax on any dividend or effective distribution paid by the S.A., including constructive dividends; 5% reduced WHT applies where dividends are paid from foreign-source income that was subject to effective Panamanian taxation (e.g., income remitted to Panama and taxed under the territorial regime)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 3,000,000: mandatory Local File for taxpayers whose annual gross revenue reaches this threshold; Master File and CbC Report required for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue ≥ USD 750,000,000; related-party transactions with entities in zero-tax or non-cooperative jurisdictions must be documented regardless of revenue threshold

Economic substance scope: Panama adopted Economic Substance Notification (Law 70 of 2019, amended) requiring entities in an MNE group that perform 'relevant activities' (distribution and service center, headquarters, financing, leasing, shipping, IP holding) to file an annual economic-substance statement with the DGI and maintain local management, staff, premises and adequate expenditure. A pure AEO certification and audit company serving third-party clients may fall outside 'relevant activity'; however, if the S.A. acts as an intra-group back-office or regional compliance center, ESR filing and substance compliance are mandatory

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud (defraudación fiscal) carries 2–5 years of imprisonment plus fines equal to the tax evaded and 40%–100% surcharge on omitted tax; DGI can also re-assess up to 5 years and seize funds from local accounts

Director personal liability: Directors and authorized signatories who approved false returns are criminally liable as co-principals; courts may impose a travel ban (prohibition of leaving Panama), civil restitution of evaded tax plus damages, and, upon conviction, disqualification from managing companies in Panama
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: DGI may disregard the S.A.'s separate existence, re-characterize inter-company payments as dividends, assess the 10% WHT plus 25% ISR and surcharges; the Commercial Registry may dissolve and cancel the company after repeated default; ESR certificate penalties up to B/.1,000,000 apply in regulated cases

Director personal liability: Under Panamanian corporate law, a court may pierce the veil when the S.A. is the alter ego of the shareholder or assets are commingled, making shareholders and directors personally liable for the company's tax debts and third-party claims; travel restrictions and denial of the annual municipal operating license may follow
AML / Economic Sanctions Exposure

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under Law 23 of 2015 and the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF): fines up to USD 5,000,000 for non-licensed professionals participating in suspicion-free funds; money laundering is criminally punishable with 5–12 years' imprisonment and forfeiture of the underlying funds

Director personal liability: Directors who ignore red flags become criminally liable (complicity in money laundering); preventive travel bans, asset freezes and bank-account locking by the Superintendency of Banks; a formal OFAC or UNSC designation in the AEO/customs audit chain would trigger cross-border arrest and extradition exposure
Transfer-Pricing and Deduction Abuse

Statutory sanction: The DGI may adjust income, disallow deductions, and impose a penalty of 40% of the tax omitted on transfer-pricing differences; late-payment interest at the statutory monthly rate and escalation to criminal tax fraud for deliberate manipulation

Director personal liability: Signing officers who certified false TP analyses face personal administrative fines, claw-back of the adjusted tax from the corporate estate, and criminal exposure if the TP scheme was used to evade anti-money-laundering controls; travel bans are possible during tax-crimes prosecution
