Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Ley No. 25 de 12 de junio de 1995 (Private Interest Foundations Law), as amended by Ley No. 13 de 2017; Articles 1-39 (creation, endowment, Foundation Council, protector, registration and certification).

Formation / constitutional filings

Acta Constitutiva de Fundación de Interés Privado (Foundation Charter / Founding Act) submitted for inscription in the Registro de Fundaciones

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum endowment of US$10,000 (or its equivalent in lawful assets, cash, or obligations convertible to cash) required by Article 18 of Law No. 25 of 1995. No escrow account is mandated by statute; the endowment may be represented by a subscription credited to the foundation's corporate bank account in Panama, with the registered agent attesting to the contribution.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Certificado de Seguridad y Protección Contra Incendios) from the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá based on inspection of enameling ovens, chemical storage, and evacuation routes; structural load and electrical installation inspection certificate issued by a licensed Panamanian engineer and accepted by the Municipality of Panama; occupational health and workplace safety clearance from the Ministerio de Salud (MINSA) under Decreto Ejecutivo No. 24 de 1977 and the General Occupational Health and Safety Regulations, including ventilation, personal protective equipment, and chemical hazard labeling.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum endowment of US$10,000 (or its equivalent in lawful assets, cash, or obligations convertible to cash) required by Article 18 of Law No. 25 of 1995. No escrow account is mandated by statute; the endowment may be represented by a subscription credited to the foundation's corporate bank account in Panama, with the registered agent attesting to the contribution.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF); unified digital portal: SITIO (Sistema Integrado de Información Tributaria), https://sitio.dgi.gob.pa; customs export control: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA), https://www.ana.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-established Private Interest Foundation (PIF, Ley 25 de 12 June 1995) must obtain a RUC/NIT taxpayer identifier before executing any Panamanian-source supply. Procedure: (1) register the foundation charter (acta constitutiva) in the Public Registry and maintain a resident agent; (2) file the RUC registration via SITIO with certified charter, registry enrollment certificate, and legal representative credentials; (3) update the expediente with CIIU/ISIC activity code 3240, enroll in ITBMS, and request exporter status; (4) obtain the Registro de Exportadores enrollment (MICI) and register in the SADA customs system for outward clearance; (5) secure a DGI digital certificate for electronic invoicing (SFE). Non-resident ownership is unrestricted; no physical presence in Panama is required beyond the resident agent.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope – the PIF is a distinct legal person taxed at entity level (25% ISR) only on income sourced in Panama (Código Fiscal Art. 712 et seq.); foreign-source income, foreign assets, and offshore accounts fall outside the Panamanian tax net. The PIF is statutorily exempt from Panamanian taxes on offshore patrimonial income; Panamanian-source commercial income (e.g., local sales of hard enamel lapel pins) is taxed at 25% with no pass-through to founders or beneficiaries. — A foreign-established Private Interest Foundation (PIF, Ley 25 de 12 June 1995) must obtain a RUC/NIT taxpayer identifier before executing any Panamanian-source supply. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope – the PIF is a distinct legal person taxed at entity level (25% ISR) only on income sourced in Panama (Código Fiscal Art. 712 et seq.); foreign-source income, foreign assets, and offshore accounts fall outside the Panamanian tax net. The PIF is statutorily exempt from Panamanian taxes on offshore patrimonial income; Panamanian-source commercial income (e.g., local sales of hard enamel lapel pins) is taxed at 25% with no pass-through to founders or beneficiaries.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or disregarded-entity election exists in Panamanian law; classification follows civil-law form. Available elections: (i) simplified small-taxpayer presumptive regime (Ley 66 de 2022) for merchants with annual gross income up to approximately B/. 36,000, generally unavailable to entity-form taxpayers; (ii) Multinational Headquarters (SEM) preferential regime under Ley 41 de 2007 (5% ISR) for qualifying regional operations; (iii) manufacturing/export incentive regimes administered by MICI applicable to pin production and export; (iv) economic substance compliance under Ley 70 de 2019 for PIFs conducting relevant activities (distribution/service centers), requiring annual substance filings where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold: any person performing taxable transfers of goods or services in Panama must register for ITBMS prior to the first taxable event (Código Fiscal Art. 1132-A). A simplified presumptive regime covers small natural-person merchants with annual gross income up to approximately B/. 36,000 (Ley 66 de 2022); PIFs with commercial turnover remain in the general regime. Export supplies are 0% rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (including hard enamel lapel pins) are zero-rated under Código Fiscal Art. 1057-T. Requirements: (i) physical departure of goods from Panamanian customs territory evidenced by an accepted SADA/DUA export declaration; (ii) the PIF is the registered exporter with MICI; (iii) sale is to a foreign buyer, with payment sourced from non-resident payers (Etsy/Direct Pay or Stripe payout records acceptable as supplementary evidence); (iv) purchase, transport, and customs documentation are retained; (v) for services, performance and effective use occur outside Panama. Input ITBMS is creditable and excess balances may be refunded quarterly (devolución de saldo a favor) via SITIO.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA) with ANA SADA acceptance and exit stamp
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier consignment note evidencing foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT confirmation of export proceeds; Etsy/Stripe payout reconciliation reports where banking certificates are unavailable
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (zero-rated line)
  • Registro de Exportadores (MICI) enrollment certificate
  • Foundation records evidencing buyer, Incoterms, and cost of goods sold

Mandatory electronic invoicing under Decreto Ejecutivo 59 de 26 July 2019: all ITBMS registrants must issue e-invoices via the DGI SFE (Sistema de Facturación Electrónica) through DGI-certified software, the free SITIO web issuer, or API-integrated systems; each invoice is transmitted to DGI before delivery and receives a unique Código de Operación plus a DGI-validated QR fiscal seal; serial ranges for issuance are pre-authorized by DGI; alcoholic beverages and cigarettes require additional physical fiscal stamps (precintos). DGI cross-references e-invoice data with SADA export declarations and SITIO returns.

DGI and ANA cross-match export declarations, e-invoices, and foreign-exchange records. Unsupported or fictitious zero-rating claims trigger: (i) reassessment of ITBMS at 7% plus monthly surcharge and inflation adjustment; (ii) fines of 100%–200% of the omitted tax under Código Fiscal Arts. 1067 and 1139-B; (iii) retrospective clawback of refunded input credits with statutory interest; (iv) suspension or removal from the Registro de Exportadores and cancellation of e-invoice authorizations; (v) criminal tax fraud liability for directors/founders under Código Penal Arts. 583–586, with imprisonment; the DGI limitation period is 5 years from the filing date, extended in cases of fraud or concealment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The balboa circulates only as non-existent paper money; USD banknotes are legal tender. Both current-account and capital-account conversion are unrestricted. There is no exchange-control regime, no foreign-investment registration requirement for PIF contributions, and no hurdle to profit, dividend, or endowment remittance. Territorial taxation further eliminates attributable taxation on offshore-source receipts from a Panama Private Interest Foundation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion obligation exists under Panamanian law. Export proceeds in any currency may be retained in domestically chartered or foreign bank accounts without mandatory conversion to USD. AML reporting thresholds apply only through SBP-regulated banks under the Beneficial Ownership Law 23 of 2015.

Central bank supervision: None - fully dollarized economy; bank supervision and AML/CFT oversight is vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); Banco Nacional de Panamá functions as the fiscal agent and USD clearing intermediary.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility. Panama has no dedicated EMI licensing regime at the same operational ease as EU PSD2; SBP-regulated fintechs exist, but offshore digital EMIs, particularly US neobanks and UK e-money houses, routinely refuse a Panama PIF as a customer class due to enhanced due-diligence opacity regarding protectors and beneficiaries. Open banking is not yet ubiquitous, and most practical onboarding still requires a correspondent bank relationship with a SBP-licensed physical institution.
