Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama. Official e-portal: Sede Electrónica at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa covering RUC registration, e-filing, e-invoicing, and refunds. Customs administration: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) through the SIGA platform. Municipalities (Municipio) issue the Aviso de Operación in parallel.

E-government filing portal

After notarization of the incorporation deed and inscription in the Registro Público de Panamá, the Sociedad Anónima obtains its RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) from the DGI. Procedure: (1) file the notice of commencement of operations (Aviso de Operación) with the DGI and the competent Municipio; (2) submit the RUC registration form (Formulario de Inscripción de Contribuyente) with the public deed of incorporation, corporate existence certificate, bylaws, and identification of directors, officers, and the resident agent (Agente Residente); (3) obtain the RUC certificate and, if taxable turnover exceeds the ITBMS threshold, automatic ITBMS enrollment; (4) enroll in DGI e-services using the Panamanian electronic signature (Firma Electrónica) issued by the MEF certification authority; (5) if applicable, register with ANA/SIGA for customs and with the CSS for employer obligations. A non-resident entity without a Panama permanent establishment may obtain a limited RUC through a fiscal representative for VAT credit refunds and withholding compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

The Panamanian Fiscal Code does not contain a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows commercial law (S.A. is a juridical person subject to a 25% corporate ISR on Panama-source income). Available statutory elections and regimes include: (i) small-taxpayer regime (pequeño contribuyente) for taxpayers with annual gross receipts at or below B/.150,000, permitting simplified income tax and reduced ITBMS compliance; (ii) optional flat minimum quota tax (cuota mínima) for low-activity S.A.s; (iii) election among advance income tax (anticipo fiscal) installment computation methods; (iv) dividend withholding regime election, i.e., the definitive 10% non-resident withholding versus the reduced 5% rate applicable to dividends on shares held in registered/custody form; and (v) mandatory application, without elective opt-out, of the Controlled Foreign Corporation and hybrid-mismatch rules introduced by Law 254 of 2021 for foreign passive income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.250,000 of annual gross taxable turnover (threshold under Executive Decree 84 of 2005, as applied by current DGI criteria). Registration is mandatory upon exceeding; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold and is commonly used to recover input VAT or to issue compliant invoices. Differential rates include 0% for exports and selected services, with specific 10% or 15% rates for certain hotel, tourism, and island-related activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The ITBMS 0% (tasa cero) rate applies to: (i) physical exports of goods evidenced by customs exit; and (ii) export of services under Article 1057-B of the Fiscal Code, i.e., services rendered to a non-resident client, paid for from abroad, and used or enjoyed entirely outside Panama, provided the services are not attributable to a Panama permanent establishment. For the present scope (cross-border trade policy analysis and foreign stakeholder lobbying), the advisory, representation, or public-affairs service must be delivered for the exclusive benefit of a non-resident principal and must not produce an economic benefit within Panama. The DGI reviews the place of use, the foreign origin of the payment, the absence of local economic benefit, and transfer-pricing substance under Law 254 of 2021. Lobbying, government affairs, or regulatory advisory services rendered within Panama for domestic clients remain subject to the standard 7% rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Exportación filed and validated in the SIGA customs system)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or multimodal) or Air Waybill (air export), together with packing list and weight certificate
  • Commercial invoice issued through the e-invoicing flow, marked 'EXPORTACIÓN – TASA CERO' or 'EXPORTACIÓN DE SERVICIOS', bearing the DGI-issued authorization code
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/bank credit advice evidencing payment from abroad
  • Executed service or consultancy contract with the non-resident client defining the place of performance and place of use
  • Foreign client tax identification or legal existence proof (foreign VAT ID, RUC, company registry extract, or government entity mandate)
  • Evidence of offshore use or benefit, including deliverables, acceptance certificates, correspondence with the foreign principal, and confirmation that no Panama-related party consumes the service

Mandatory electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica) is phased in under DGI Resolution 201-1167 of 2020 and implementing resolutions. Taxpayers must issue XML invoices and credit notes through an authorized technology platform (proveedor tecnológico autorizado), transmit documents to the DGI fiscal clearance system for validation, and present an invoice bearing the DGI-issued authorization code (Código de Autorización). Large and medium taxpayers are fully enrolled in real-time clearance; small taxpayers operate transitionally with preprinted talonarios or fiscal printers. Export invoices under the zero-rating flow use the same XML framework with the applicable export indicator.

Panama is fully dollarized (Balboa at 1:1 with the U.S. dollar) and imposes no statutory foreign-exchange conversion or repatriation window; the 180-day reference reflects the DGI and ANA evidentiary expectation that export proceeds be credited to Panamanian banking accounts and retained in the taxpayer's records to support zero-rating and ITBMS credit-refund claims. In post-refund audits, the DGI re-examines the foreign-use requirement; if a service is recharacterized as domestic supply, the taxpayer must repay the ITBMS credits refunded plus tax at 7% (and possible ISR recharacterization of Panamanian-source fees), statutory monthly interest and surcharges of up to 25% of the tax due, graduated Fiscal Code fines which in aggravated cases may reach 100% to 300% of the omitted tax, suspension of the RUC and e-invoicing authorization, and criminal liability for fiscal evasion (imprisonment of up to five years) where false documentation is used. Assessment and clawback actions are generally subject to the five-year statutory prescription period, extended where fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility exists under both current and capital account categories; the PAB is a non-issued unit of account fully interchangeable with USD, so no exchange-control approvals, no FX budget, and no repatriation licenses apply. There is no legal distinction between resident and foreign-held accounts for convertibility purposes, and the Panamanian territorial tax system does not condition fund remittances on tax clearance. The only fiscal frictions on outbound flows are the 10% dividend withholding tax on distributions derived from Panamanian-source income and the 4.5% withholding on certain passive interest; a Government Affairs S.A. whose retainers originate from foreign clients (territorial-source services performed abroad) can remit profits freely without exchange authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or surrender obligations on export proceeds or foreign service revenues. The S.A. may retain 100% of retainer and advisory fee receipts in USD abroad or domestically, with no obligation to convert into PAB or to repatriate within a fixed period.

Central bank supervision: Panama does not maintain a conventional central bank; the monetary framework is full dollarization. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the sovereign banking supervisor and licensing authority under Decree Law 2 of 2008, the Banco Nacional de Panamá (BNP) acts as fiscal agent of the State, and the Superintendencia del Mercado de Valores (SMV) supervises securities and fintech activity. Currency issuance is limited to the Balboa (PAB), a fractional coin pegged 1:1 to the U.S. dollar (USD), which is legal tender throughout the Republic.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW for escrow and primary treasury operations; MEDIUM for disbursement-only pass-through. Legal constraints: Law 170 of 2020 and SMV Fintech Hub regulations do not confer bank-deposit status on EMIs; only SBP-licensed banks may lawfully take deposits in Panama. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Mercury, Payoneer, multi-currency payment platforms) generally decline Panama S.A. entities in the Government Affairs sector due to enhanced due diligence sensitivity, PEP-adjacent risk, and the need for a physical-compliance bridge.
