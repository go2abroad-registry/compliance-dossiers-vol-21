Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá, Sección de Fundaciones de Interés Privado (Public Registry of Panama, Private Interest Foundations Section), with mandatory prior notarial protocol.

Governing statute

Law No. 25 of 12 June 1995 (Private Interest Foundations), Articles 1-42, as amended; complemented by Law 23 of 2015 on Beneficial Owner Registry and Executive Decrees on resident agents.

Formation / constitutional filings

Foundation Charter (Carta Fundacional) executed as a public deed before a Panamanian Notary Public and presented for registration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; the Foundation Charter must allocate an identifiable initial endowment, commonly US$10,000, to the Foundation. The endowment is not escrowed or subject to withdrawal restrictions and may be held in a foundation bank account.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification by the Benemérito Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá; structural load review of assembly racks and storage shelving; occupational health inspection by the Ministerio de Salud (MINSA) covering electrical grounding, ventilation, emergency exits, noise exposure, and ergonomic workstations.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital; the Foundation Charter must allocate an identifiable initial endowment, commonly US$10,000, to the Foundation. The endowment is not escrowed or subject to withdrawal restrictions and may be held in a foundation bank account.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The Founder and initial Council of [Name] Private Interest Foundation, acting under Law 25 of 1995, resolves: (1) To adopt the Foundation Charter and accept the initial endowment of US$10,000. (2) To establish the registered office in Panama City, Republic of Panama, at the Charter address. (3) To appoint the Resident Agent, Council Members, and Protector named in the Charter. (4) To register the Foundation with the Public Registry of Panama and authorize the Resident Agent to publish required notices and execute ancillary documents. (5) To open a US dollar account and file the RUC after registration.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Ministerio de Comercio e Industrias (MICI), Dirección General de Industrias; no statutory industrial license is required for low-risk ISIC 3240 robotic toy assembly under current industrial classification rules.

Category: None mandatory; if local zoning or customs facilitation requires a municipal operating document, the applicable title is the 'Aviso de Operación' issued by the Mayoral Office of the district of operation.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministry of Economy and Finance, Panama; portal: SIAF (Sistema Integrado de Administración de Ingresos) at https://siaf.dgi.gob.pa/

E-government filing portal

The foreign entity (or its Panama resident agent) must first form or register the Panama vehicle with the Public Registry, then obtain an RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) with DGI through the SIAF online portal. For a Private Interest Foundation (PIF), the RUC is only required once the foundation conducts taxable Panama-source activity, including exports. The RUC application requires drafting by a licensed attorney, authenticated public deed, proof of the resident agent, and email/digital signature activation for e-fiscal documents. The DGI will issue a 10-digit NIT/RUC number after verification; the municipal 'Aviso de Operación' must also be obtained before commencing local business operations. Exporters must additionally register with the DGI exporter registry and the Single Customs Window (SIGA) for submission of export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No general check-the-box election exists under Panama law; classification follows the governing Civil Code and the enabling law of the vehicle. A Private Interest Foundation is treated as a separate legal person but only taxed on Panama-source income; it may choose to distribute income to beneficiaries or to accumulate without changing this default classification. Hybrid mismatch elections are not applicable. For foreign tax purposes, a Panama PIF may, under U.S. check-the-box rules, be independently classified as a corporation or trust, but this election is not recognized in Panama and does not alter the domestic territorial tax result.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general minimum turnover threshold; any person carrying out taxable transfers of movable goods or services in Panama, or importing goods, must register for ITBMS. A simplified small-taxpayer comprehensive regime (Régimen Simplizado de Pago) applies to taxpayers with annual gross income under B/.30,000 (approx. USD 30,000), which is optional and carries a fixed monthly quota.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Panama, including robotic AI pet toys assembled in Panama and sold to a customer resident abroad with physical delivery outside the Republic of Panama, are subject to ITBMS at 0% (zero-rated), provided the goods have left the customs territory under a valid export declaration and the sale is not to a Panama-resident intermediary. Services performed entirely abroad on behalf of non-residents are also zero-rated. The zero rating applies at the sale stage, and input ITBMS paid on materials, components, and local services attributable to export sales can be credited or refunded as 'credito fiscal' against the ITBMS settlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera - DUA)
  • Bill of Lading / Airwaybill / Certificado de Embarque
  • Commercial Invoice and e-invoiced counterpart (Factura Electrónica)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Bank Transfer/Payment Voucher evidencing receipt of proceeds from abroad
  • Proof of actual physical exit (Port/Customs Colonel receipt, DHL/FedEx waybill, or container seal log)

Panama has a mandatory phased electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica) system for all ITBMS-registered taxpayers. Issuers must obtain DGI pre-authorization for each invoice series through the SIAF web services; the generated XML invoice carries a QR code, fiscal digital stamp (barcode), and a registered cryptographic digital signature. No physical stamping is required, but invoices must be submitted electronically to DGI for validation and fiscal clearance. Large and medium taxpayers are required to integrate their billing systems with DGI's API (SIAF-Web Services), with enforcement and monitoring of non-compliance.

Panama Customs and DGI jointly audit export zero-rating claims for robotic toys and similar manufacturers. The exporter must maintain the complete export documentation bundle for 5 years (unless a longer audit extension is notified) and the proceeds must be repatriated to Panama within 180 days from shipment. Failure to demonstrate physical export via the DUA, Bill of Lading and bank corroboration will cause the ITBMS to be retroactively charged at the standard rate (7%) on the gross sale. Penalties include surcharges up to 30% of the omitted tax plus 10% of the original tax per month of delay; administrative fines range from B/.1,000 to B/.10,000 or up to 10 times the amount of the fraudulent credit; criminal tax fraud may result in imprisonment of 2 to 5 years. In a clawback, the exporter must return all refunded input VAT credits together with interest, and the refund bond or fiscal guarantee, if any, will be executed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both capital and current accounts. Panama operates a fully dollarized economy with no exchange control regulations; profit remittance, dividends, and capital repatriation are unrestricted. Compliance burdens are limited to anti-money laundering (AML) and know-your-customer (KYC) documentation, tax filing under the territorial tax regime, and any sector-specific disclosure obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export receipts in Panama. Since the US dollar is legal tender, all export proceeds may be retained in USD bank accounts. Exporters must comply only with customs declaration procedures and maintain underlying bills of lading and commercial invoices for treasury and banking verification.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); Panama has no central bank, and the US dollar is the legal tender. The SBP regulates banking and official licensing, supported by the Ministry of Economy and Finance for monetary policy execution.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for fully-regulated banking activity; Medium for ancillary payment facilitation. Panama lacks a dedicated e-money institution (EMI) licensing regime; nonbank digital wallets cannot operate as full banks. Offshore digital EMIs may be used for payment collection and cross-border disbursement support, but they cannot access Panama's local clearing network (ACH Panama/SIPA RTGS) and are subject to SBP innovation-hub review. Virtual asset service providers require prior SBP authorization under the Panamanian Crypto Law framework.
