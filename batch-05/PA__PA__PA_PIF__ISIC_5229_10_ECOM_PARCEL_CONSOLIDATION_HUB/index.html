Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; official e-filing and registration portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa); public information portal: https://www.dgi.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

1) PIF must first be constituted by notarized 'Acta Constitutiva' and registered with the Registro Público de Panamá, with mandatory appointment of a Resident Agent (abogado/attorney). 2) The Resident Agent files the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) application with the DGI via the Sede Electrónica, attaching the foundation charter, Public Registry certificate, and ID of the Resident Agent and beneficiaries/founders where required. 3) Obtain the Firma Electrónica (digital certificate) issued through the Registro Público for the tax administrator/representative. 4) DGI assigns the RUC and a CIIU economic-activity code; the PIF is registered as an 'Obligado Tributario' and, if it performs taxable supplies within Panama, is simultaneously enrolled for ITBMS. 5) A foreign-operated PIF must designate a local fiscal representative or maintain the Resident Agent as contact point for tax notices; non-resident entities with no Panamanian-source activity are generally not required to obtain a RUC. 6) RUC updates for changes in officers, address, or economic activities must be filed within 15 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or hybrid-mismatch entity elections. The PIF is a separate juridical person taxed at foundation/entity level on Panama-source income only (territorial taxation), while foreign-source income is exempt under Law 25 of 1995 and Article 694 of the Código Fiscal. The foundation deed may elect between: (a) strictly private wealth-administration activities (full exemption for non-Panama income; B/.300 annual foundation tax payable to the Public Registry); or (b) engaging in commercial business within Panama, which triggers RUC/ITBMS and full taxation of Panamanian-source income at the 25% corporate rate. No other elective classification is available; source characterization follows the situs rules of the Código Fiscal.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.0.00 - no annual-turnover de minimis threshold; ITBMS registration is mandatory for any person habitually transferring goods or rendering services whose economic effect is situated in Panama. An optional simplified small-taxpayer regime (trimestral) applies to persons with annual taxable turnover not exceeding B/.36,000 (USD 36,000).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% ITBMS) applies to: (i) physical export of goods from Panama to abroad, evidenced by the customs export declaration; (ii) services rendered in Panama for use, consumption, or exploitation outside Panama, including cross-border freight forwarding, parcel consolidation, sorting, and direct-injection linehaul services performed for non-resident e-commerce merchants; (iii) supplies made within Colon Free Zone (Zona Libre de Colón), Panama Pacifico and other authorized special economic zones; and (iv) services rendered to non-residents when the direct benefit and use of the service occurs outside Panama. The exporter/renderer must separately show the 0% rate on the invoice and retain input-ITBMS credits; where input tax exceeds output tax on zero-rated exports, the taxpayer files the monthly ITBMS declaration and may claim an input VAT refund ('Devolución de crédito fiscal') through the DGI. The DGI may approve the refund only upon documentary verification of the export and receipt of foreign currency.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA - Declaración Única Aduanera) duly accepted by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) with exit confirmation (regularization of 'salida' within statutory deadlines)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing actual movement from Panamanian territory
  • International commercial invoice (export pre-factura/factura) reflecting 0% ITBMS and accurate HS codes
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank credit advice proving receipt of foreign currency
  • Packing list and, where applicable, warehouse-receipt or consolidation manifest evidencing the parcel-sorting scope

Mandatory electronic invoicing ('Facturación Electrónica / eFactura') under Law 159 of 2020 and DGI regulations, implemented through the DGI's Sede Electrónica e-invoicing module. Each invoice must be structured in the official XML schema, digitally signed with a valid Firma Electrónica, and transmitted to the DGI fiscal web service for real-time authorization ('acuse') before delivery to the buyer; the system assigns an electronic invoice number and a machine-readable QR/stamping element. The regime is compulsory for Gran Contribuyente (large taxpayers) and is being phased to all obligated taxpayers; legacy DGI-authorized bound invoices (facturas físicas) and fiscal cash-register printers are being phased out concurrently. Foreign-service providers rendering offshore services without Panamanian nexus are generally outside the e-invoicing mandate.

DGI audits zero-rated export claims by cross-matching DUA exit records, B/L manifests, and the taxpayer's banking evidence, and may reclassify non-substantiated receivables as Panama-source income taxable at 25% ISR plus 7% ITBMS, interest, and punitive multipliers. Misrepresenting domestic deliveries as exports is tax fraud under Código Fiscal Article 762, subject to fines of 100% to 200% of evaded tax, retrospective clawback of the 0% rate and input-VAT refunds, suspension of the RUC, and criminal sanctions for willful false declarations. Refund claims wrongly obtained expose the taxpayer to restitution, surcharges, and personal/legal-representative liability. Repatriation of export proceeds later than 180 days is a documented audit trigger and increases the risk of source recharacterization.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama uses the USD as legal tender and the Balboa is a 1:1 accounting unit with no physical circulation. There are no exchange-control restrictions on capital or current account transactions. Profit remittances from a Private Interest Foundation operating in Panama are not subject to exchange approval; only a 10% dividend/remittance tax applies if the underlying income was sourced in Panama, and no prior FX authorization or exit permit is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama does not require export receivables to be surrendered, converted, or repatriated within any statutory timeline. Proceeds may be held in USD or any freely convertible currency in the foundation's operational bank accounts, subject only to standard transaction reporting thresholds under SBP AML rules.

Central bank supervision: No de jure central bank. Panama is a fully dollarized economy; the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the bank and finance-sector regulator, while Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent, clearing house, and government treasury bank. The Monetary and Financial Board (Junta Monetaria) sets only advisory monetary parameters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Moderate to High as an ancillary rail only. Panama's SBP regulatory sandbox and the new fintech law allow for licensed local electronic payment institutions, but offshore digital EMIs (including Airwallex, Wise, Payoneer) may hold USD funds and route settlement for cross-border parcel disbursements. They are not acceptable as a primary treasury account for a Panamanian PIF because SBP-supervised banks require a local licensed account for capital escrow, dividend distributions, and audited balance-sheet evidence. EMIs are legally usable for supplier payouts, marketplaces remittance, and sub-ledger minorexpense clearing.

Inbound capital injections are routed via SWIFT MT103 from the parent or contributor to the PIF's USD account maintained at a tier-1 Panamanian bank (Banco General or Banco Nacional de Panamá). Payment must reference 'Capital Contribution' or 'Intra-Company Loan to Private Interest Foundation PA_PIF' and include a transaction purpose code consistent with SBP classification (e.g., corporate treasury contribution). Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends or distributions from Panama-source profits of a Panamanian legal entity or foundation constituted as a business; distributions from a PIF funded exclusively with foreign-source assets are outside Panama territorial taxation and not subject to dividend WHT.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months after the fiscal year-end (for a 31 December year-end, the statutory filing deadline is 31 March of the following year).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1: RUC (Número de Identificación Tributaria) Registered taxpayer identification number of the PIF (RUC). The PIF must be registered in Panama and possess a valid RUC; a non-resident owned PIF must still obtain Panamanian fiscal documentation before filing. Late filing penalty: 5% of the tax due for each month or fraction of a month of delay, up to a maximum of 50%; late payment interest of 1.5% per month (simple) is added. Intentional non-filing may also trigger criminal tax proceedings and personal liability for directors.
Box 2: Período Fiscal Fiscal year-end date (calendar year recommended). If the PIF uses a non-calendar fiscal year, the return must reflect the approved distinct accounting period. Late filing penalty: 5% of the tax due for each month or fraction of a month of delay, up to a maximum of 50%; late payment interest of 1.5% per month (simple) is added. Intentional non-filing may also trigger criminal tax proceedings and personal liability for directors.
Line 3: Ingresos de fuente panameña (Panama-source gross income) Gross receipts from services rendered or assets located in Panama, including parcel consolidation, sorting, linehaul and related logistics services. If no physical sorting, consolidation or linehaul activity occurs in Panama, enter zero; document this with contracts, route manifests and geolocation records. Late filing penalty: 5% of the tax due for each month or fraction of a month of delay, up to a maximum of 50%; late payment interest of 1.5% per month (simple) is added. Intentional non-filing may also trigger criminal tax proceedings and personal liability for directors.
Line 4: Ingresos de fuente extranjera (Exempt foreign-source income) Gross revenue from services performed or assets used outside Panama, excluded from territorial taxation. Provide clear attribution of cross-border e-commerce logistical revenue to foreign contracts; reconcile with Airwallex and local clearing settlement statements. Late filing penalty: 5% of the tax due for each month or fraction of a month of delay, up to a maximum of 50%; late payment interest of 1.5% per month (simple) is added. Intentional non-filing may also trigger criminal tax proceedings and personal liability for directors.
Line 5: Deducciones (allowable deductions) Costs and expenses directly connected to Panama-source gross income. Do not allocate costs or overheads to exempt foreign-source income; maintain full supporting invoices and transfer pricing contemporaneous documentation. Late filing penalty: 5% of the tax due for each month or fraction of a month of delay, up to a maximum of 50%; late payment interest of 1.5% per month (simple) is added. Intentional non-filing may also trigger criminal tax proceedings and personal liability for directors.
Line 6: Renta imponible (Net taxable income) Panama-source income minus qualifying deductions. Compute in US dollars/Balboas at the official exchange rate; a zero or negative amount results in no tax due. Late filing penalty: 5% of the tax due for each month or fraction of a month of delay, up to a maximum of 50%; late payment interest of 1.5% per month (simple) is added. Intentional non-filing may also trigger criminal tax proceedings and personal liability for directors.
Line 7: Impuesto (Tax payable at 25%) Application of the 25% statutory corporate tax rate on net taxable income. If no Panama-source income is declared, enter zero and attach a sworn statement of territorial scope. Late filing penalty: 5% of the tax due for each month or fraction of a month of delay, up to a maximum of 50%; late payment interest of 1.5% per month (simple) is added. Intentional non-filing may also trigger criminal tax proceedings and personal liability for directors.
Line 8: Créditos y pagos a cuenta (Credits and advance payments) Quarterly ISR advance payments, DTA withholding credits, and other eligible credits. Attach certificates of credits and evidence of foreign tax credits only if treaty relief applies; excess credits may be refunded after audit. Late filing penalty: 5% of the tax due for each month or fraction of a month of delay, up to a maximum of 50%; late payment interest of 1.5% per month (simple) is added. Intentional non-filing may also trigger criminal tax proceedings and personal liability for directors.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory when annual Panama-source gross income exceeds B/.3,000,000 (USD 3,000,000); Master File mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated global revenue exceeds USD 15,000,000; Country-by-Country Report mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue above USD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama Economic Substance Regulations (Law 254 of 2021 and Decree 259 of 2022) apply to PIFs conducting relevant activities, including parcel distribution/consolidation, headquarters, logistics and service functions. The PIF must demonstrate direction and management in Panama, maintain adequate fixed premises and local employees, and file an annual economic substance report with the DGI.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Non-Filing of Panama-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Surcharge of up to 200%-300% of tax evaded; civil penalty of 25% of the omitted tax; criminal fine of B/.5,000 to B/.100,000; imprisonment of 2 to 5 years for tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Director and Foundation Council members can be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid Panama-source taxes; personal passport/travel bans may be imposed; criminal convictions can result in imprisonment for directors who knowingly sign fraudulent returns.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Economic substance penalty of up to B/.250,000 for false or missing substance declarations; annulment of tax exemption benefits; possible strike-off of the foundation by the DGI.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors face civil liability for the fines and any resulting tax reassessment; the corporate veil may be pierced where the foundation is found to be an instrumentality of the beneficial owner.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Abusive Related-Party Pricing

Statutory sanction: Compensatory tax adjustment plus penalty of up to 15% of the omitted tax attributable to the TP adjustment; non-deductibility of undocumented transactions; administrative fines for non-filing of TP declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve intercompany arrangements without TP documentation become personally liable for the adjusted corporate tax and penalties; travel bans can be issued pending payment.
AML / Anonymous Beneficial Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties for AML violations up to B/.500,000; criminal penalties for money laundering from 5 to 10 years' imprisonment; forfeiture of assets.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers, directors and foundation protectors may be personally indicted; civil asset seizure and travel bans apply; corporate veil piercing is permitted under Panama's AML statutes.
