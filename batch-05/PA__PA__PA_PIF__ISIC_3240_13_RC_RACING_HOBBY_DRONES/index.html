Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministry of Economy and Finance of Panama; online portal: Sede Electrónica (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation must first engage a licensed resident agent and file an application with the DGI for the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC). The application is submitted via the DGI's Sede Electrónica, using the resident agent's digital credential, accompanied by the foundation charter, notarized and apostilled identification of the foundation, proof of domicile in Panama, evidence of minimum capital endowment, and appointment of the foundation officers. The DGI issues a provisional RUC within approximately 10-15 business days, after which the foundation is automatically registered for ITBMS purposes and pending municipal business license verification. Non-resident owners may participate in the application solely through the resident agent; no physical presence is required. The RUC number is the sole tax identifier for all Panamanian tax and customs filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not adopt US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. A PIF may elect the 'Régimen Especial de Pequeños Contribuyentes (REPC)' if annual gross receipts do not exceed B/.36,000, and may elect simplified ITBMS payment for small-scale retail operations. For foreign-source income, no election is required: Panamanian territorial source rules automatically exclude such income from the RUC tax base. A PIF may also elect to be treated as a 'fundación de interés privado' under Law 25 of 1995 for tax purposes, which limits annual tax liability to the fixed foundation fee and keeps the entity outside the income tax net unless Panama-source taxable income arises.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 annual gross turnover (approximately US$36,000); below this limit the PIF may be classified as a 'pequeño contribuyente' with simplified quarterly compliance. Above this threshold, full ITBMS registration and monthly filing with a mandatory consolidated tax return are required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods, including carbon-fiber drone frames and integrated FPV racing drone units, are zero-rated when the goods physically leave Panama and the sale is made to a non-resident purchaser. Offshore services, including carbon-fiber milling and brushless motor integration rendered to foreign clients, may be zero-rated if the service is used or consumed entirely outside Panama and the payment is channeled from a foreign source through a banking institution. The zero-rating is recognized only if the exporter maintains sufficient proof that final destination is outside Panama and that the transaction is with a non-resident party.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduanas, DUA)
  • Bill of Lading (Airway Bill or Multimodal Transport Document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Canalización de Divisas) issued by a Panamanian bank
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Certificate of Non-Payment of ITBMS (Constancia de Exoneración) for the exported goods

Electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) is mandatory for all taxpayers classified as 'grandes contribuyentes' and, on a phased roll-out from 2022, for all other registered entities. Invoices must be generated through DGI-approved electronic billing service providers (Proveedores de Servicios de Facturación Electrónica), signed with a digital certificate, and transmitted near-real-time to the DGI. Each invoice receives a unique fiscal key and a QR code; the DGI performs electronic validation and clearance before the invoice is released for delivery to the buyer. Deferred transmission of batches may be permitted for small taxpayers only if prior authorization is obtained from the DGI.

The DGI may reopen prior periods for up to five years and will reverse any export zero-rating claim if the customs declaration, bill of lading, or banking channeling certificate is absent, incomplete, or inconsistent. Consequences include: (i) reversal of zero-rating and immediate repayment of ITBMS at 7%, (ii) monthly moratory interest of up to 1% until payment, (iii) an additional 10% surcharge for understatement, (iv) monetary fines from B/.500 to B/.10,000 under the Código Fiscal, (v) suspension or cancellation of the digital invoice certification, and (vi) criminal tax fraud liability with imprisonment of three to seven years for deliberate use of forged export documentation or sham transactions intended to obtain a fiscal benefit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates under full dollarization, with the U.S. Dollar (USD) serving as legal tender and the sole circulating medium. There are no exchange control restrictions, no capital-account permits, and no current-account approvals required. Both capital contributions to a Private Interest Foundation (PIF) and profit remittances to non-resident beneficiaries are freely transferable in USD. Panama's territorial tax regime imposes no withholding on profit distributions to non-residents. The only practical constraints arise from AML/KYC documentary requirements imposed by licensed banks, not from currency-convertibility limitations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion obligation for export proceeds. Exporters and intermediary trading entities may retain receipts indefinitely in USD or any freely convertible foreign currency. No timeline exists for conversion. However, all export proceeds credited to Panamanian bank accounts must be supported by commercial invoices, packing lists, shipping documents and, where applicable, customs declarations to establish the lawful origin of funds under SBP AML regulations.

Central bank supervision: None — the Republic of Panama does not maintain a central bank; the banking and FX-policy supervisory authority is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), acting together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Legal Regime of Banks (Decree Law 9 of 1998, as amended).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium for non-bank regulated Financial Technology Entities and Electronic Money Institutions operating from offshore jurisdictions. Panama does not issue standalone EMI licenses to offshore digital wallet providers without a physical SBP-licensed banking branch or a specialized Fintech license under SBP's Innovation Hub framework. Cross-border EMIs (e.g., PayPal, Stripe) can be integrated only as payment-processing rails linked to a fully onboarded Panamanian bank account or through the PIF's own bank-issued corporate credit card and settlement account. Direct use of unlicensed EMIs for foundation treasury is not a compliant substitute for a licensed bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound transfers must be executed by international wire through the SWIFT network to the PIF's USD account at an SBP-licensed Panamanian bank. The remittance must clearly populate the payment purpose field with a business-appropriate descriptor such as 'Capital Contribution to Private Interest Foundation' or 'Working Capital Injection for Trade Operations' and must never use prohibited references such as 'gift', 'donation', 'trust distribution' or 'settlement of securities transaction'. The beneficiary account name must exactly match the registered name of the Panama Private Interest Foundation. The receiving bank will require the inbound SWIFT MT103 plus an internal credit advice confirming the originator's bank and the underlying documentation if the transaction exceeds the bank's AML threshold.Suppliers of drone components, payment processors and logistics providers are ordinarily settled via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers in USD. For higher-value, documented trade transactions (typically above USD 100,000), Panamanian banks may issue a Commercial Letter of Credit in favour of the overseas supplier under UCP600 rules. Local-currency clearing for USD-denominated cheques and electronic debits is performed by the Panamanian Automated Clearing House system administered and supervised by the SBP. Manual settlement for physical documents is available for same-day internal transfers within a single bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0% standard corporate income tax (Impuesto sobre la Renta, ISR) on net taxable profits derived from Panamanian-source income under the territorial taxation model; foreign-source income realized by a Panamanian entity through operations abroad is outside the scope of ISR when the activity is not performed in Panama.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% withholding on cash dividends paid from net Panamanian-source profits already subject to the 25% corporate ISR rate; 10% withholding on dividends distributed from profits that were not subject to ISR (e.g., exempt or preferential income). Distributions made by a PIF out of offshore assets to a non-resident beneficiary are not subject to dividend WHT under the territorial system.

The annual CIT return must be filed within three months of the fiscal year-end; for calendar-year taxpayers the statutory due date is no later than 31 March of the following year, with DGI administrative extensions staggering deadlines by RUC ending digit between 15 and 31 March.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB 2,500,000 (Panama Balboas) of annual gross income per the audited financial statements triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File and Master File; additionally, any taxpayer engaged in related-party transactions exceeding PAB 250,000 per counterparty must file the annual related-party declaration (Anexo de Operaciones Vinculadas) together with the Formulario 200 return. Penalties for failure to produce documentation range from USD 10,000 to USD 50,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (introduced by Law 254 of 2021 and its implementing decree) apply to entities and foundations that carry on relevant activities, including IP holding, distribution and service centers, headquarters, and group financing. A PIF that holds drone-related patents or engages in intercompany trademark licensing for FPV products must demonstrate the following in Panama: local management and board meetings, qualified employees, physical premises, and adequate operating expenditure proportionate to income. An annual substance declaration must be filed with the ISR return; a purely passive foundation with no covered activity in Panama may file an exemption affidavit. Failure to satisfy ESR results in fines up to USD 100,000, forced strike-off, and referral to the AML Financial Intelligence Unit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Fraud surcharge of 100% of the evaded ISR; fine between USD 5,000 and USD 100,000; imprisonment of three to eight years pursuant to Articulo 775 of the Codigo Fiscal and Articulo 274 of the Codigo Penal; publication in the DGI register of tax delinquents; seizure of bank accounts and merchant processing balances.

Director personal liability: Directors, trustees, and the foundation's protector who sign or approve a false return are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, surcharges, and interest. DGI may obtain a travel ban (impedimento de salida del pais) for the responsible representative where the debt exceeds PAB 100,000 and may freeze personal assets and disqualify the individual from sitting on Panamanian boards.
Shell PIF / Undisclosed Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Under AML Law 23 of 2015 as amended by Law 70 of 2019, fines range from USD 500,000 to USD 1,000,000 for corporate entities and licensed agents per violation; cancellation of the PIF registration in the Registro de Fundaciones de Interes Privado; criminal money-laundering penalties up to ten years' imprisonment for the controlling individuals; asset forfeiture under the extended confiscation regime.

Director personal liability: The foundation council members are personally liable for the foundation's debts and taxes where the foundation is used as an instrument of fraud; civil restitution may be ordered for all tax avoided plus 50%; a criminal conviction triggers a commercial and immigration sanction, including prohibition from re-entering Panama and disqualification from managing any Panamanian-regulated activity.
ITBMS (VAT) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 100% of the omitted ITBMS; monthly surcharge of 1%; temporary closure of the commercial premises or digital seller account; DGI may levy the unpaid amount through apremio without prior court order.

Director personal liability: The resident agent and the foundation council are jointly liable for the omitted VAT if the PIF is dissolved before payment; the responsible director may face a personal penalty equal to the VAT omitted and a ban on obtaining permits or RUC in Panama for five years.
Treaty Relief Abuse / False TRC Claim

Statutory sanction: DGI denies the treaty benefit and recaptures the differential at the statutory 12.5% royalty rate or 6% interest rate, plus a 100% penalty on the undisclosed remittance and monthly interest; the taxpayer is placed on the DGI high-risk list, triggering mandatory TP audits for six fiscal years.

Director personal liability: Directors of the withholding agent who process the exempted remittance without proper TRC evidence are jointly liable for the under-withheld amount; aggressive cases lead to criminal tax-evasion charges with imprisonment up to five years and civil liability for the avoided tax plus 30% compensatory surcharge.
