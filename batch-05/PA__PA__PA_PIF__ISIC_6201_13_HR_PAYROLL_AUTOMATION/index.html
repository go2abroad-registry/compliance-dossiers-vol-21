Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), República de Panamá. Centralized e-services portal: 'Sede Electrónica de la DGI' at https://sede.dgi.gob.pa; institutional site: https://www.dgi.gob.pa. RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) administration, ITBMS registration, e-invoicing validation, and annual declarations are all processed through this authority, with interoperability with the Registro Público de Panamá and the Servicio Nacional de Aduanas.

E-government filing portal

A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) obtains its RUC as follows: (1) register the PIF with the Registro Público de Panamá through a licensed resident agent (abogado idóneo) and file the foundation's Aviso de Operación / business notice with the DGI; (2) have the resident agent activate the DGI digital identity (Firma Digital issued by a Registro Público-authorized certifier) and open the 'Sede Electrónica' account; (3) submit the RUC application electronically, attaching the public-registry certificate, foundation charter (acta de dotación and estatutos), identification of the foundation council and founder, and the registered Panamanian address; (4) upon issuance of the RUC, opt into ITBMS and e-invoicing modules to obtain the taxpayer's e-filing credentials and invoice emission codes. Non-resident principals do not file personally; their authority is exercised through the resident agent. The RUC certificate is delivered digitally and must be kept current for the annual PIF tax return and 'Paz y Salvo' clearance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not have a check-the-box or S-corporation-style election. A PIF is conclusively treated for tax purposes as a territorial asset-holding legal person: income sourced outside Panama (e.g., the payroll SaaS subscription/licensing fees of non-resident clients) is exempt from Panamanian income tax, while Panama-sourced income is taxable at the 25% corporate rate. The PIF may elect voluntarily to register as an ITBMS taxpayer in order to zero-rate its export services; it may also elect the simplified small-taxpayer (pequeño contribuyente) ITBMS settlement where gross receipts are within the applicable threshold. No fiscal-transparency or hybrid-mismatch election is available; the PIF is itself the taxpayer. Regardless of elections, the PIF must pay the fixed annual foundation tax of B/.300.00 and file the annual declaration (declaración jurada) to maintain good standing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de minimis exemption: any person habitually making taxable supplies in Panama (including cross-border digital services used or enjoyed in Panama) must register for ITBMS from the first taxable supply. A small-taxpayer (pequeño contribuyente) presumptive/simplified régime applies under DGI rules for annual gross turnover up to approximately B/.36,000. Non-resident digital suppliers have a zero threshold and register through the simplified remote procedure if their services are effectively used in Panamanian territory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports are eligible for 0% ITBMS under Ley 55 de 2008 and DGI guidance when (a) goods physically leave Panamanian customs territory, or (b) services are performed for a non-resident client and used/exploited wholly outside Panama. The payroll automation platform qualifies where the SaaS subscription, data processing, and HR compliance services are supplied to a foreign employer/payroll beneficiary with no Panamanian permanent establishment, are paid in foreign currency from abroad, and produce no benefit or economic use within Panama. Incidental back-office activity in Panama does not defeat zero-rating if the recipient and place of effective use remain offshore. Services used in Panama, or rendered to a Panamanian-resident entity, are subject to 7% ITBMS.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) filed through Panama's Single Window / Servicio Nacional de Aduanas module, containing the DGI cross-reference and ITBMS export-regime code
  • DGI e-invoicing export invoice (Factura Electrónica de Exportación) bearing the validated acceptance code and export specification
  • Commercial invoice / sales contract and, for goods, Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB); for services, the signed service agreement, delivery/access certificates, and platform usage evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) or bank credit advice demonstrating repatriation of export proceeds into Panama within the statutory deadline
  • Supporting due-diligence file: non-resident client's foreign tax ID/registration, beneficial-ownership data, IPR license records, and GL/HR-payroll records evidencing the offshore place of performance and use

Panama has a mandatory DGI electronic-invoicing regime (Resolución DGI No. 201-5144 of 2021, as amended) phased in by taxpayer category from 2022. All ITBMS-registered businesses must issue invoices exclusively through DGI-certified software and transmit each invoice to the DGI 'Facturación Electrónica' platform for real-time validation, receiving an acceptance code and QR verification stamp before legal issuance to the customer; offline/contingency emission requires immediate post-facto upload. VAT and export invoices must quote the taxpayer's RUC and pre-registered emission point. Export e-invoices must reference the customs export declaration and are machine-cross-checked with customs data. Non-resident digital-service suppliers use the simplified remote registration and invoice-emission protocol.

DGI audits export zero-rating through automated cross-checks of the customs e-export declaration, the validated export e-invoice, and the banking FX-inflow record within the 180-day repatriation window. Missing or inconsistent documentation converts the export into an ordinary taxable supply, triggering retroactive ITBMS at 7%, default interest, surcharges, and denial/recapture of refunds. Knowingly false declarations, forged transport documents, fake invoices, or non-repatriated proceeds constitute tax fraud (defraudación fiscal): fines of 100%–300% of the evaded tax, cancellation of export registration and RUC good standing, clawback of any refund with statutory interest, and criminal liability including imprisonment for aggravated evasion for the foundation, its council members, and complicit resident agents.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized economy with the U.S. dollar as sole legal tender; there are no exchange controls, no capital account restrictions, and no distinction between current and capital account convertibility. Profit remittances and dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are freely permitted though may be subject to source-of-funds and AML documentation. Legal entity taxation follows a territorial system; corporate and foundation income is taxable only on Panamanian-source income. The absence of a central bank means no foreign exchange parity risk exists for USD-denominated treasury operations, and FX conversion to other currencies is performed by commercial banks at freely negotiated market rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama does not impose any mandatory surrender, repatriation, or conversion timeline for export proceeds. Exporters and service providers, including payroll SaaS platforms, may hold, invoice, and disburse export receipts in USD or any convertible currency without statutory holding ratios, minimum conversion obligations, or release requirements under Central Bank decrees.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) — acting as the official banking supervisory authority under the absence of a central bank; monetary policy and currency issuance are functionally delegated to the U.S. Federal Reserve System through full dollarization

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Panama does not maintain a statutory electronic money institution (EMI) licence comparable to the EU/UK EMI regime. A cross-border digital bank or EMI can only operate by obtaining a full banking licence under the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá, by partnering with a cloud-banking platform with an existing Panamanian bank licence, or by functioning through an offshore entity subject to unregulated-status review by domestic banks. The PIF may lawfully hold accounts at a Panamanian bank, but onboarding of money held through non-bank offshore EMI rails is severely constrained by AML/CFT anti-shelling rules, KYC documentation requirements, and the high risk of account closure if the EMI has no physical presence or regulatory registration in a recognized reciprocal jurisdiction.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% flat headline rate on Panamanian-source net taxable income above USD 50,000, preceded by a progressive bracket scale: 15% on the first USD 11,000 of taxable income and 20% on taxable income between USD 11,001 and USD 50,000 (Article 699-A, Código Fiscal, as amended by Law 8 of 2010 and subsequent reforms). Foreign-source income remains outside the territorial tax base for a Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) whose commercial activity is executed abroad.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding tax on dividends and other profit distributions paid by a Panama PIF out of profits that were subject to Panamanian ISR (Article 733, Código Fiscal). Distributions funded exclusively from exempt foreign-source income are not subject to the 10% dividend tax, but the burden of proof lies on the PIF to trace distributable reserves to non-Panamanian sources.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual Form 026 is due no later than three months after the fiscal year-end; for a calendar-year PIF, the deadline is 31 March of the following year. Monthly advance payments of ISR are due on the 15th day of each month. The USD 300 annual foundation tax is due by 15 January of each year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD (Panama Balboa pegged 1:1 to USD). Under Articles 762-A to 762-K of the Código Fiscal (introduced by Law 52 of 2019 and regulated by Executive Decree No. 717 of 2019), a taxpayer must file Form 078 (Declaración Informativa de Precios de Transferencia) and prepare a Local File when annual gross revenue exceeds USD 2,000,000 or when related-party transactions in the fiscal year exceed USD 250,000. A Master File is mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds USD 50,000,000. Country-by-Country reporting obligations apply only to groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000 under Law 365 of 2024; standalone PIFs are exempt unless consolidated within a large MNE group.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not apply the Caribbean/UAE-style numbered Economic Substance Regulations, but the DGI enforces a substantive 'substance-over-form' doctrine rooted in Articles 697 and 698 of the Código Fiscal and reinforced by general anti-abuse rules in Law 365 of 2024. A PIF claiming territorial-source exclusion for SaaS revenue must demonstrate real and continuous commercial substance in Panama: a dedicated office or registered operational base, employed payroll and HR personnel, board decisions documented in Panama, active contracts with foreign clients, and actual performance of software engineering, licensing, and support functions. Without this, the DGI may recharacterize the PIF as a sham conduit, attribute its income to the foreign beneficial owner under source rules, and apply transfer pricing penalties. For PIFs deemed to carry on qualifying intellectual-property income activities, an organic DEMPE (Development, Enhancement, Maintenance, Protection, Exploitation) analysis must evidence that strategic decisions and core functions occur in Panama rather than in zero-tax offshore havens.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Under-Reporting (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Administrative surcharge of 50% to 100% of the evaded tax plus monthly interest of 1%; closure of the establishment and suspension of the Aviso de Operación; criminal tax fraud under the reformed Código Fiscal carries imprisonment of 3 to 6 years and fines of up to USD 1,000,000 for amounts exceeding USD 100,000.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members and the resident agent are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency when they knowingly sign false returns or order fraudulent distributions; courts may pierce the PIF veil, order civil restitution of diverted profits, issue asset freezes and travel bans (impedimento de salida), and impose custodial sentences on de facto managers.
Shell Company / Sham Transaction Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: DGI disregard of the foundation as a sham, causing all attributed income to be reclassified as Panamanian-source in the hands of the foreign beneficial owner; assessment of 25% ISR, 10% dividend tax, 100% evasion surcharge, monthly interest, and revocation of tax-exempt treatment; possible inclusion in public registries of non-cooperative taxpayers.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors or council members who facilitate sham arrangements face personal liability for resulting taxes and penalties; under general commercial fraud doctrines they may be ordered to personally restore amounts distributed, prohibited from leaving Panama, and criminally prosecuted for conspiracy to defraud the Dirección General de Ingresos.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Registry (RBI) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of USD 10,000 to USD 1,000,000 per violation for registry and AML failures; freezing of foundation assets; cancellation of the foundation's registration; money laundering predicate conviction under Law 23 of 2015 carries imprisonment of 5 to 12 years and fines of up to three times the laundered value.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members, the resident agent, and the compliance officer are personally exposed to criminal liability for laundering and registry violations; sanctions include property confiscation, disqualification from acting as officers in Panama, travel restrictions, and extradition exposure under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: DGI adjustment of the transaction to the arm's length result, imposition of a penalty of up to 100% of the underpaid tax, monthly interest at 1%, and fixed fines for documentary non-compliance of up to USD 10,000 per infraction; repeated violations may suspend the taxpayer's Paz y Salvo status.

Director personal liability: Officers who sign intercompany agreements and materially participate in pricing determinations without substantiation can be held jointly liable for the adjusted tax; the foundation's assets and the personal assets of the controlling council members are reachable where transfer pricing manipulation is found to be fraudulent.
Withholding Tax Compliance Failure (Dividends, Royalties, Interest)

Statutory sanction: The PIF as withholding agent is personally liable for 100% of the unwithheld tax plus a fine of 50% to 100% of that amount and monthly interest of 1%; third-party beneficiaries who knew of the omission are jointly liable for restitution.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members who authorize distributions or cross-border payments without the appropriate withholding expose their personal assets to collection; deliberate and repeated non-withholding is treated as fraud and may result in criminal prosecution, travel bans, and disqualification of the resident agent.
