Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá (General Directorate of the Public Registry), Mercantile Section, Panama City

Governing statute

Panama Law 32 of 1927 (General Law of Corporations), as amended, Articles 1-5 (formation), Article 12 (registered office), Article 13 (directors/officers), and Article 3 (purposes and share issuance); complemented by the Panama Code of Commerce and Law 5 of 2002 (Electronic Notarization and Registry Standards).

Formation / constitutional filings

Pacto Social y Estatutos (Articles of Incorporation and Bylaws) executed as an Escritura Pública (public deed) before a Notary Public or filed directly via the registry's electronic legalization system

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; the company may register with an authorized capital of US$10,000 divided into shares, and no escrow or physical deposit is required. Shares may be allotted without immediate payment, subject to the board's resolution and the subscribed amount stated in the charter.

Funding & unlock path: Certificado de Seguridad y Prevención de Incendios (fire safety certificate) issued by the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá based on structural inspection, fire extinguisher presence, emergency exit lighting, and load calculations for the occupied office; plus the Certificado de Higiene y Seguridad Laboral (occupational health and occupational risk management) issued by Mi-Trabajo (Ministerio de Trabajo y Desarrollo Laboral) and a workplace structural load verification by the municipal structural engineering unit for any vault or filing system exceeding 500 kg/m².

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance, Panama | E-Tax 2.0 portal (https://etax.mef.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities apply for a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) by submitting a notarized incorporation certificate, a valid apostilled foreign registration, a resident-agent declaration, a Panama address, and a sworn statement of income through a licensed public accountant. The DGI issues the RUC/TIN and an Aviso de Operación; non-resident entities without a permanent establishment are only registered for ITBMS if they make taxable supplies in Panama.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — Foreign entities apply for a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) by submitting a notarized incorporation certificate, a valid apostilled foreign registration, a resident-agent declaration, a Panama address, and a sworn statement of income through a licensed public accountant. The DGI issues the RUC/TIN and an Aviso de Operación; non-resident entities without a permanent establishment are only registered for ITBMS if they make taxable supplies in Panama.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Not applicable for check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections. Panama does not maintain U.S.-style classification elections; the S.A. is automatically taxed as a juridical person on Panama-source income only. Income from foreign sources is exempt, provided it is documented; available administrative options include the small-taxpayer regime and the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime for qualifying service exporters.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 (US$36,000) in annual gross taxable turnover for mandatory full registration; lower-threshold operations qualify for the simplified small-taxpayer regime

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services attract a 0% ITBMS rate. The exporter must prove the supply is rendered to an overseas recipient, that legal title and/or the place of performance is outside Panama, and that payment originates from abroad. For patent-prosecution services, the agency must maintain a service contract identifying the foreign principal, the relevant foreign patent/trademark offices, and delivery of work products to the foreign client; offshore legal services are zero-rated only if the service is effectively used abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUI)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT confirmation)

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) is being phased in by DGI. Taxpayers above the turnover threshold must issue invoices through the E-Tax 2.0 system or a DGI-certified e-invoicing operator, using the official XML/JSON schema, a digital signature, and a unique invoice code. The DGI validates each invoice before issuance (real-time clearance model) and requires the inclusion of a QR/electronic stamp. Smaller taxpayers may continue using DGI-authorized pre-printed invoice forms until the mandatory rollout is completed.

The DGI may conduct a six-year retrospective audit of zero-rated export claims. If the documentation is missing, the export is recharacterized as taxable and the corresponding ITBMS is assessed with a 25% surcharge, fines from B/.1,000 to B/.10,000, and possible criminal sanctions for tax fraud (Tax Code Articles 210-220). The agency is jointly and severally liable for foreign exchange proceeds not repatriated within 180 days or not properly recorded in Panama.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full currency convertibility under a dollarized economy: the U.S. dollar is legal tender, and the Panamanian balboa (PAB) exists only as a purely nominal divisional currency at a fixed 1:1 parity. There is no legal distinction between capital and current account operations for exchange control purposes. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted and not subject to any FX approval, withholding tax, or surrender requirement; only standard corporate tax (25% territorial) and optional dividend tax at 10% for earnings derived from foreign sources remitted through Panamanian registry apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export receipts. Service revenue, including patent prosecution fees, may be retained in any freely convertible currency, and there is no requirement to repatriate funds to Panama or convert to local equivalent.

Central bank supervision: Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá); monetary and fiscal agency functions are delegated to the National Bank of Panama (Banco Nacional de Panamá) under the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF). Panama has no central bank in the traditional sense.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High viability but strictly conditional. Licensed digital payment institutions and electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise and Payoneer may operate in Panama through an approved local banking partner or possess a fintech license under Law 1 of 2023 (Regulatory Framework for Fintech and Crypto-Assets, as implemented by the Superintendency of Banks). Fully offshore digital EMIs without local licensing or a commercial partnership with a Panamanian licit bank cannot open active corporate balances in Panama, but can be used as inbound remittance feeders. For official patent office fees, mandatory use of SWIFT MT103 through a licensed bank is recommended over pure EMI channels.

Banks will request a form documenting the source of funds and the underlying PCT or trademark prosecution retainer. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% on dividends paid from Panamanian-source taxed profits; 10% on dividends paid from untaxed offshore-source profits under the territorial regime.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 1,000,000 in aggregate related-party transactions (Panamanian Balboa equivalent), triggering mandatory Local File; Master File required when consolidated group revenue exceeds USD 20,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (Law 52 of 2022): entities undertaking patent prosecution must demonstrate physical offices, local qualified staff, and core revenue-generating activities in Panama; failure results in denial of tax residency certificate and a 12.5% penalty on profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine equal to 100% to 300% of tax omitted; criminal imprisonment for 3 to 6 years; cancellation of tax license; publication of taxpayer name on Panama's non-compliant list.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt; civil restitution, seizure of personal assets, and travel bans pending criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Administrative dissolution; fine from USD 10,000 to USD 100,000; revocation of any tax incentive; 10% annual interest on retroactive assessment.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors and shareholders personally liable for corporate obligations if the entity is adjudicated a sham; 5-year travel ban and criminal liability for fraud under Panama's Corporate Law.
AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Fines up to USD 5,000,000 under Law 23 of 2015; suspension or revocation of the professional license; mandatory external audit and compliance monitor.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal criminal prosecution (2 to 10 years imprisonment), civil restitution of laundered funds, and prohibition from holding corporate office in Panama.
Unlicensed Patent Practice

Statutory sanction: Cease-and-desist order; fine of USD 5,000 per act; compensatory damages to clients; administrative disqualification for up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers personally liable for clients' economic losses; employment ban in regulated IP services; criminal conviction leads to imprisonment and travel ban.
