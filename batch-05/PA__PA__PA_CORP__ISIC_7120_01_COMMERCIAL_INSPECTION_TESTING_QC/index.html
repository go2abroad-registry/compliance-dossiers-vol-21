Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) - Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; online portal: Sistema de Gestión de Recaudo (SGR) via www.dgi.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must: (1) register its Panamanian Sociedad Anónima or foreign-entity branch with the Registro Público de Panamá through a licensed resident attorney; (2) obtain municipal and operating licenses if establishing physical premises in Panama; (3) apply for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) from DGI through the local legal representative, submitting a notarized power of attorney, certified charter/articles of incorporation, and proof of registered address; (4) activate the SGR e-credential and obtain the DGI ITBMS resolution; (5) undergo electronic invoicing enrollment with SEFA or an authorized e-invoicing provider before issuing any tax documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box election. A Sociedad Anónima is a separate legal taxable entity under Panamanian law, taxed only on Panamanian-source income. Eligible taxpayers may elect the Simplified Small-Taxpayer regime for ITBMS purposes when annual gross income does not exceed PAB 36,000, or apply for special incentive regimes such as the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007. No separate hybrid mismatch election is codified; general anti-abuse rules apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.07%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No uniform monetary turnover threshold for ITBMS registration; any person habitually selling taxable movable goods or providing taxable services in Panama must register for RUC/ITBMS before commencing activities. A simplified small-taxpayer regime applies to taxpayers with annual gross income up to PAB 36,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods from Panama qualify for the 0% ITBMS treatment when the goods are physically shipped outside Panamanian territory under a valid export customs declaration. Services supplied to non-residents for use entirely outside Panama, and services connected with international transportation or Panama Canal operations, fall outside the territorial scope of ITBMS. Exporter must hold the full documentary chain proving actual export and foreign receipt; the associated input ITBMS may be credited or refunded where properly attributable to export operations.

  • Declaración Única de Aduanas (DUA) - Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill signed by the carrier
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT credit advice evidencing payment from the foreign buyer
  • Certificate of Origin when preferential treatment is claimed
  • Evidence of the foreign buyer's identity and tax residence (corporate registry excerpt or equivalent)

Panama has a mandatory phased electronic invoicing regime (Facturación Electrónica) administered by DGI through SEFA or DGI-authorized private e-invoicing providers. Each invoice must be issued as a digitally signed XML document, transmitted in real-time to DGI, validated for fiscal authorization, and stamped with a unique electronic voucher number/QR code. API integration is required for system-to-system clearance, and export invoices must also be transmitted under the same fiscal authorization workflow before issuance.

DGI and Panama Customs jointly scrutinize export zero-rating claims. Incomplete, inaccurate, or fraudulent export documentation will reverse the 0% treatment, causing immediate assessment of ITBMS at 7% plus monthly interest and late-payment surcharges, penalties of up to 200% of the tax due under the Código Fiscal, and criminal liability for fiscal fraud. Export supporting documents must be retained for at least five years, and the exporter bears the burden of proof. Failure to evidence foreign receipt within the applicable 180-day period may recharacterize the transaction as a taxable domestic supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Balboa is merely an accounting designation at parity with the U.S. dollar. Both current and capital accounts are fully convertible with no exchange controls, no prior authorization for foreign investment, and no profit remittance hurdles; dividends and repatriation can be moved freely.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Exporters have no statutory obligation to surrender export receipts and no mandatory conversion ratios. Proceeds may be retained, freely reinvested, or remitted in U.S. dollars without SBP or executive approval.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank; the U.S. dollar is legal tender under full dollarization. Banking system regulation and AML supervision are exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), while financial intelligence matters fall under the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Panama does not maintain a separate EMI licensing passport; foreign EMIs may open settlement accounts or use local banks as payment gateways, but they need an SBP-licensed entity or a partnership with a local bank to access the clearing network. Legal constraints include the prohibition on taking deposits from the public without a banking license and UAF registration requirements for money or value transfer services.
