Ley 25 de 12 de junio de 1995 (Law 25 of June 12, 1995) on Private Interest Foundations (PIFs), as amended by Law 18 of 2011 and Law 3 of 2016 (AML/CFT amendments); Articles 7, 18-26 (endowment, charter content, foundation council, and registry requirements).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum foundation endowment of US$10,000 (B/.10,000) per Article 7 of Law 25 of 1995. The endowment may be composed of cash, securities, or other assets, must be fully subscribed by the founder in the Charter, and need not be placed in an escrow bank account; the Charter may provide for staged contribution of the endowment within a defined timeline.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate (Certificado del Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá) issued under Law 34 of 1997 after inspection of fire suppression systems, emergency exits, and hazardous material storage; structural load inspection certificate from the Municipal Engineering Department confirming building suitability for ISO Class 8 cleanroom and analyzer assembly equipment; occupational safety clearance from the Ministry of Labor and Labor Development (MITRADEL) under Law 7 of 1975, including workplace exposure assessments for isopropyl alcohol and formaldehyde-free reagents, chemical hygiene plan, and employee medical surveillance protocols.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) - Panama; electronic portal SITIO (Sistema Integrado de Tributos, https://sitio.dgi.gob.pa); registration and taxpayer update portal RUES (Registro Único de Contribuyente)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must first file its constitutive documents (e.g., foundation charter or branch registration) with the Panamanian Public Registry through a licensed resident agent/attorney. Once the public registration is effective, the resident legal representative submits the RUC application (Solicitud de Registro Único de Contribuyente) via the DGI/RUES portal or at DGI offices, including the certificate of public registry, certified articles/bylaws, resident agent acceptance, proof of registered address in Panama, and identification of the legal representative. The DGI assigns the RUC, and the taxpayer activates SITIO digital credentials. Before commencing taxable business in Panama, an Aviso de Operación must be obtained from the corresponding municipality, and where applicable, e-invoicing certification must be obtained from DGI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch entity classification election. A Private Interest Foundation is treated as an entity-level taxpayer on Panama-source income only; foreign-source income is outside the Panamanian tax base. No election exists to disregard the foundation. Available statutory choices include voluntary ITBMS registration, special export zero-rating registration, and application of preferential regimes such as free zones, Panama Pacifico, SEM (headquarters), and Ciudad del Saber if qualifying.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary turnover threshold; any person or entity habitually carrying out taxable sales of goods or services in Panama, including through a permanent establishment or registered branch, must obtain RUC and register for ITBMS (PAB/USD).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods (including microfluidic cartridges and immunoassay analyzers) and of services actually rendered outside Panama qualify for ITBMS zero-rating, provided the seller is registered for ITBMS, is recognized as an exporter, the transaction produces foreign currency proceeds, and the goods or services physically leave/are consumed outside Panama. The zero rate must be supported by documentary evidence of export within the tax period; otherwise domestic 7% ITBMS applies.

  • Panama Customs Export Declaration (DUSAR/Declaración Única de Exportación, validated through SICE)
  • Bill of Lading (original or certified copy) or airway bill evidencing dispatch from Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank confirmation (SWIFT or TT) showing export proceeds received in Panama or in the exporter's Panamanian bank account from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice in final form, clearly stating the good/service exported
  • Packing list
  • Purchase order, sales contract, and any certificates/documents required by the destination country (e.g., certificate of origin, sanitary or IVD product licensing documents)

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Panamá, FEL) for VAT-registered taxpayers. Invoices must be issued through DGI-certified software or DGI's free e-invoicing platform, generated in XML format, digitally signed, and transmitted to DGI for validation. DGI validation returns a tracking code (Código de Trazabilidad) and QR code for each invoice, enabling fiscal clearance and audit trail. Fiscal printer invoices are gradually being replaced; only DGI-validated electronic invoices are acceptable for input VAT credits and documentary substantiation.

DGI may audit export files up to the general five-year statute of limitations, verifying physical export, customs validation, and inward FX proceeds. If export documentation is missing, false, or inconsistent, DGI may claw back the zero-rating and assess the 7% ITBMS, plus monthly interest and surcharges. Fraudulent zero-rating claims may attract penalties of 100%-200% of the omitted tax, civil fines, cancellation of the exporter registration, and criminal liability for tax and customs fraud; provincial customs authorities may additionally impose penalties under Panama's customs framework.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized territory with no capital or current-account restrictions, no central-bank visa procedure, and no convertibility hurdles for inbound investment, profit remittance, or capital repayment. Foreign-source income of a Private Interest Foundation is excluded under Panama's territorial tax regime, so its remittance abroad is free of Panamanian tax. However, Panamanian-source commercial profits derived by the foundation from its IVD manufacturing and trading activities are subject to the 25% corporate income tax under the Fiscal Code, and any distribution charged to those Panamanian-source profits attracts the 5% dividend withholding tax before the funds may be released abroad. Proper DGI filings and board resolutions are therefore the only practical prerequisites to an unimpeded outward remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no surrender, repatriation, conversion, or retention requirement on export proceeds. There is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for medical-device export receipts; USD and other foreign currency may be held freely in SBP-licensed banks or offshore accounts. Exporters must still comply with the AML/CFT documentation duties set out in Law 23 of 2015 and Decree No. 52 of 2015, which require banks to evidence the commercial reality of unusual transaction flows.

Central bank supervision: No central bank exists in Panama. The banking and foreign-exchange regulator of the International Banking Center is the Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá, 'SBP'). The monetary system is governed by Decree-Law No. 8 of 1904 and the 1972 Monetary Agreement with the United States, under which the U.S. dollar is full legal tender; the Panamanian balboa exists only as a unit of account for fractional coinage and circulates at a 1:1 parity with USD. Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the fiscal agent of the Republic but performs no central-bank functions, does not issue currency, and exercises no exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Panama-domiciled private interest foundations are rarely accepted by offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Mercury) because those providers are not licensed by the SBP and their correspondent banks apply heightened scrutiny to Panamanian legal-person clients. Wise Business supports some LatAm entities, but onboarding for a Law 25 foundation usually requires full audited documentation, UBO declarations, and a live operating history; many EMIs ultimately reject the foundation account class or restrict inflow from third parties. Legally, no non-bank EMI may operate a licensed domestic payment system in Panama without SBP authorization, so any EMI balance should be used only as a supplementary operating wallet, with the SBP-regulated bank account remaining the primary clearing and treasury rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial and follow-on capital contributions from shareholders or founders should be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 USD credit to the foundation's USD account at its SBP-licensed Panamanian bank. Panama applies no central-bank filing or exchange-control purpose code; however, the remitter must state a clear payment reason (e.g., 'Capital contribution to Private Interest Foundation under Law 25 of 1995') and the foundation's Public Registry Folio and RUC, since the receiving bank's Know-Your-Transaction screen requires an economic-purpose description. For contributions above USD 1 million, the compliance package must include the endowment deed, the remitting entity's board resolution, a source-of-funds statement, and a group ownership/UBO chart. The foundation must maintain its registered agent, registered office, and beneficial-ownership registry filing; obtaining a RUC at the DGI is required before engaging in Panamanian commercial activity.Operational supplier payments, distributor refunds, and service fees are cleared via SWIFT MT103 transfers containing the UETR (field 111) and the underlying invoice reference. Large-ticket procurement of microfluidic cartridges, analyzers, and clinical reagents is routed through commercial documentary letters of credit issued under UCP 600 by the Panamanian bank through its U.S. correspondent (SWIFT MT700/MT707). Domestic USD obligations are settled via the Panamanian automated clearing house and interbank same-day transfers operated by Banco Nacional de Panamá, while intra-group treasury concentration may use zero-balance sweep structures at the SBP-regulated bank; cash volumes should remain consistent with the stated medical-device trade flows to minimize enhanced-outlier monitoring.Outright deliverable FX forwards (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, CHF/USD) executed with an SBP-licensed bank or its international branchCross-currency swaps (XCS) covering EUR or CNY trade exposure, governed by an ISDA Master Agreement and Credit Support AnnexNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or illiquid Asian/LatAm currencies (e.g., CNY, MXN, ARS) booked through an offshore licensed counterparty because the Panamanian market lacks those deliverable pairsPlain-vanilla OTC currency options on deliverable pairs to cap depreciation risk on distributor invoicing currenciesInterest-rate swap or cross-currency basis swap combinations linked to the commercial LC and trade-loan facilities to neutralize floating-rate exposure

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on net taxable income derived from Panamanian sources under Panama's territorial income tax system.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Standard dividend withholding tax is 5% on distributions made out of net profits that have already been subject to Panamanian corporate income tax. Distributions from non-taxed or exempt profits are generally subject to a 10% withholding tax; distributions of invested capital are not taxable.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB 1,000,000 (Panamanian balboas, pegged 1:1 with the US dollar). Taxpayers with aggregate related-party transactions at or above PAB 1,000,000 are required to prepare and maintain a Local File and, where applicable, a Master File. In addition, local operational rules and DGI guidance require arm's-length recordation and annual transfer pricing reporting for controlled transactions with non-resident affiliates in the IVD distribution and IP licensing chain.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), introduced by Law 70 of 2019 and extended by Cabinet Decree and DGI resolutions, apply to tax-resident juridical persons, including Private Interest Foundations that carry on relevant activities. Manufacturing, distribution, financing, leasing, headquarters, shipping, investment holding, and intellectual property exploitation are relevant activities. A PIF earning IVD-related royalties or engaging in distribution must demonstrate adequate physical presence, qualified employees, active management in Panama, and proportionate expenditures in Panama. An annual economic substance declaration is mandatory, and failure to satisfy substance requirements may result in fines, reassessment of foreign-source income as Panamanian-source, and possible strike-off of the entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Income Tax Declaration

Statutory sanction: Administrative reassessment of the evaded tax plus a surcharge of up to 100% of the unpaid tax and monthly interest; criminal prosecution under Panama's Fiscal Code and Penal Code may trigger imprisonment and criminal fines. DGI may attach bank accounts, freeze credits, and embargo movable and immovable assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and Foundation Council members who sign false declarations, participate in the fraud, or knowingly benefit from the evasion can be personally liable for civil restitution, subject to travel bans and disqualification, and exposed to imprisonment where criminal intent is proven.
Shell Company / Insufficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Panama ESR Law 70 of 2019 and accompanying regulations, non-compliance attracts monetary penalties, restrictions and potential nullification of the foundation's public registration, and reassessment of otherwise foreign-source profits as Panama-source taxable income at the 25% ISR rate.

Director personal liability: The founder, council members, and officers who permit a shell structure without substance may be subject to personal fines and disqualification from holding Panamanian fiduciary offices; civil liability can attach for taxes, penalties, and interest if they knowingly caused the non-compliance.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The DGI may disregard improper transfer prices, reallocate income, impose a hidden-distribution treatment on amounts repatriated to the non-resident parent/foundation, and apply penalties of up to PAB 10,000 plus interest and late-payment surcharges for failure to maintain and file TP documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Foundation Council who knowingly approve artificial related-party margins may be jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax deficiency and penalties, and may face personal attachment and travel limitations in serious non-compliance cases.
Dividend and Cross-Border Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is personally deemed the taxpayer for the unwithheld amounts, with liability for the full tax, a 10% surcharge, monthly interest, and penalties. Criminal sanctions may apply if the non-withholding was carried out as part of a scheme to deceive the DGI.

Director personal liability: Officers who direct the payment of dividends or royalties without applying the required withholding may be civilly liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; repeated violations can support criminal prosecution and the restriction of travel pending payment of the tax debt.
Anti-Money Laundering / UBO Concealment

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015, regulated service providers and the foundation council can be fined severely for AML deficiencies; criminal money-laundering prosecution can lead to asset forfeiture and imprisonment. The AML risk tier for this cross-border medical device commercial model is Medium-Risk Enhanced, requiring CDD, wire monitoring, and beneficial ownership records.

Director personal liability: Directors, Foundation Council members, and compliance officers can be personally prosecuted, with penalties including imprisonment, civil asset confiscation, travel bans, and prohibition from serving as directors of any Panamanian juridical entity.
