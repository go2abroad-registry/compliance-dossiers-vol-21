Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas, República de Panamá; taxpayer portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI at https://sede.dgi.gob.pa; electronic invoicing, RUC issuance, and tax filings are administered through this portal.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Panama Sociedad Anónima: (1) incorporate with the Registro Público de Panamá, file articles of incorporation, and appoint a resident agent (agente residente); (2) file the Aviso de Operación (Notice of Operation) electronically with the DGI through the Sede Electrónica, which constitutes the principal tax registration act and generates the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente); (3) obtain a DGI-accepted digital signature certificate (firma electrónica) for the legal representative; (4) obtain the municipal business license (Aviso de Operación Municipal) from the relevant Municipio; (5) register with the Caja de Seguro Social (CSS) and register as an employer upon hiring any Panama-based employees; (6) if the foreign entity performs no taxable activity in Panama and derives no Panama-source income, no RUC is required, and any Panama client's withholding agent files and remits taxes on a non-resident basis; a foreign entity merely invoicing Panama clients from abroad and performing all services abroad should not register as an ITBMS taxpayer.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box election nor a hybrid-mismatch entity election. Relevant statutory elections for a Panama S.A.: (i) small taxpayer regime (Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente) under Law 135 of 2020, available where annual gross income does not exceed B/.150,000, substituting standard income tax and ITBMS obligations with a single 2% gross-receipts tax; (ii) Multinational Headquarters (SEM) license under Law 41 of 2007, as amended, for qualifying regional headquarters, granting defined income tax, ITBMS, and dividend exemptions; (iii) DGI authorization election for electronic invoicing models, either through the DGI's free web-based issuer or an accredited technology provider; (iv) election of the standard territorial-source taxation regime as reflected on the corporate income tax return (Form 201), under which only Panama-source income is subject to income tax and foreign-source income is excluded by statute. No consolidated tax filing, tax-transparent entity election, or hybrid-mismatch election is available for S.A. entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No fixed numeric threshold is codified as an ITBMS registration trigger; any natural or legal person habitually selling movable goods or rendering taxable services in Panama must register for ITBMS with the DGI. A separate simplified-regime ceiling of B/.150,000 (Panamanian balboas at 1:1 parity with USD) of annual gross income applies to the small taxpayer election, but this ceiling is not a general exemption from ITBMS registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Panama's territorial ITBMS, a 0% zero-rated export treatment is available for qualifying exports of goods and exports of services. For goods: the taxpayer must be a registered Panamanian seller, the goods must physically depart Panama, and the export must be validated through the unified customs declaration. For services: the service must be rendered by a Panamanian supplier to a non-resident recipient, performed and benefited wholly outside Panama, with no local consumption within Panama, and the supplier must retain the service contract, an export invoice, and evidence that payment was routed from abroad. For the analyzed scope (international student placement and immigration/golden visa advisory), Panamanian visa applications and residency filings are consummated in Panama and the benefit is enjoyed in Panama, so the standard 7% ITBMS applies to local immigration work; only clearly offshore placement services, where the beneficiary is domiciled abroad, no Panamanian admissions step is performed, and the benefit is realized wholly abroad, may be documented as zero-rated exports. Input ITBMS attributable to genuine exports is creditable or refundable through the monthly ITBMS return. Panama imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark stated below is the customary documentary window applied by Panamanian banks and the DGI for export proceeds realization and zero-rating substantiation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Exportación / DUA) stamped and verified by the Servicio Nacional de Aduanas
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the goods from Panama
  • Commercial invoice and packing list in Spanish as required by customs regulations
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 credit evidence from a Panama-regulated bank showing payment received from the foreign buyer
  • Export service contract and certificate of use and benefit abroad for zero-rated service exports

Panama mandates a phased electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica) regime. Taxpayers must obtain an express habilitación (authorization) resolution from the DGI before issuing fiscal documents, either through the DGI's web-based issuer on the Sede Electrónica portal or through an accredited technology provider. Each electronic invoice, credit note, and debit note must be digitally signed, carry a DGI security code or validation stamp, and be transmitted to the DGI's fiscal repository within the approved transmission cycle; the DGI may impose real-time or near-real-time clearance requirements depending on the taxpayer's category and risk profile. Mandatory fields include the issuer's RUC, the customer's RUC (or passport for non-residents), the applicable ITBMS rate (7%, 0%, or exempt), and amounts expressed in balboas. Documents issued without the DGI security code or without timely transmission are treated as non-fiscal, exposing the issuer to fines and denial of input ITBMS credits; buyers may lawfully reject non-fiscal invoices.

The DGI and the Servicio Nacional de Aduanas jointly enforce export zero-rating. A false export declaration, forged customs or transport documentation, or a sham offshore-services structure (e.g., services actually consumed in Panama, artificial pass-through of local services, or circular invoicing with related parties) triggers: immediate revocation of the zero-rating; retroactive assessment of the omitted 7% ITBMS plus statutory interest from the date the tax was due; monetary fines and surcharges under the Panamanian Tax Code, calculated on the tax omitted; denial of input ITBMS credits associated with the fraudulent transactions; and criminal referral for the offense of defraudación tributaria (tax fraud), which is punishable by imprisonment under Panamanian law. Retrospective clawback reaches the open statutory limitation periods, and where the fraud was directed or approved by the S.A.'s directors, officers, or legal representative, such persons may be held jointly and severally liable for the assessed tax, interest, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy with no foreign exchange controls and full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. Profit remittance and dividend distribution are unrestricted from an exchange-control perspective, but payment channels are subject to bank-level KYC, AML/CFT reviews, and FATF-aligned due diligence. Practical hurdles arise only from documentary verification, beneficial ownership disclosure, and source-of-funds substantiation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios apply to export proceeds. Exporters may freely retain, disburse, or reinvest US-dollar receipts. The only obligations are accounting, tax, and AML/CFT record-keeping duties, including traceability of cross-border wire flows and suspicious transaction reporting.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as the banking and AML/CFT supervisor; Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent and clearing agent. Panama has no currency-issuing central bank; the US dollar is legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium feasibility. Panama does not have a dedicated EU-style electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime. Offshore digital EMIs are generally not permitted to provide payment accounts to Panamanian corporate entities without a locally licensed fintech or banking partner. Any cross-border EMI arrangement must rely on a licensed Panamanian bank for clearing and settlement; otherwise the unlicensed collection of client funds may constitute a banking or AML violation under the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá framework.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be routed through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers to a Panamanian bank account using the corporate legal name and standardized narrative 'Capital Contribution for Corporate Treasury Purposes – Non-Refundable'. The sending institution must include the full corporate registration number, registered address, and ultimate beneficial owner information. Verification is performed by the receiving bank against the entity registry and KYC file. Where required, a notarized corporate resolution and board authorization confirming the capital injection must be attached. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends distributed by a non-listed Panama S.A.; 5% on dividends from shares listed on the Panama Stock Exchange or recognized public securities markets. Dividends paid out of income already taxed at the full corporate rate remain subject to the shareholder-level withholding, unless reduced under an applicable income tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three calendar months after the fiscal year-end. For a calendar-year S.A., the statutory deadline is 31 March of the following year.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Panama-Source Gross Income All gross revenue recognized from Panamanian-source activities, including advisory fees, placement commissions, and visa-processing charges earned through services performed or consummated in Panama. A non-resident-owned S.A. must include only Panamanian-source income. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File are triggered when annual gross revenue in the preceding fiscal year exceeds B/.3,000,000 (Panamanian Balboa, pegged 1:1 to the U.S. dollar), or whenever the S.A. has cross-border transactions with related parties exceeding B/.1,000,000 and applies a preferential tax regime. Country-by-Country Reporting filing applies where a multinational group meets the OECD consolidated revenue threshold as domesticated under Panamanian law.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations apply to entities carrying on relevant activities, which include professional services such as advisory, consultancy, and immigration-visa services. The S.A. must maintain a real physical office in Panama, employ qualified staff working in Panama, incur minimum local operating expenditure proportional to income, and hold board meetings and strategic decisions in Panama. Failure to satisfy substance requirements will cause the tax authority to disregard claimed territorial treatment, reclassify revenue as Panamanian-source, and deny treaty or incentive benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — False or Omitted Panama-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Omitted tax is immediately assessable plus monthly interest and an additional surcharge of up to 100% of the omitted amount. Criminal tax fraud prosecutions carry fines and imprisonment under the Panamanian Fiscal Code; the S.A.'s RUC may be suspended and its commercial registry operations blocked.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false or fraudulent tax declarations become personally liable for restitution where the corporation is used as a mere instrumentality. Courts may pierce the corporate veil and impose travel bans, asset seizures, professional disqualification, and imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of territorial exemptions, re-characterization of all income as Panama-source taxable at 25%, penalties of up to 50% of the corrected tax liability, and possible administrative cancellation of the corporate license by the Superintendency of Non-Financial Entities.

Director personal liability: Registered agents and directors may be sanctioned for facilitating an economic-substance sham; they face monetary fines, suspension of professional practice, and personal responsibility for tax arrears if they knowingly participated in misrepresentation.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Mandatory TP adjustment to arm's-length result, plus interest and penalty of 10% to 30% of the adjustment; administrative fines for non-filing of documentation can reach B/.50,000, and the DGI may terminate audit proceedings in favor of the taxpayer only after full compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute related-party contracts at artificial prices may be jointly and severally liable for the resulting TP adjustment and penalties, particularly where distribution to foreign owners is disguised as deductible fees to strip Panama earnings.
AML — Escrow and Tuition-Fund Handling Violations

Statutory sanction: Under Panama's Law 23 of 2015 and AML regulations, fines range from thousands to millions of balboas depending on severity and the value of unlawful transactions, with additional confiscation of criminal proceeds. Repeat violations cause forced administrative dissolution, prohibition from the regulated immigration-advisory sector, and criminal prosecution for money laundering with imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for AML compliance may face incarceration, personal fines, travel bans, and extradition cooperation in cross-border cases. Panama courts will pierce the S.A. when the corporate form is used as a laundering conduit, making shareholders and beneficial owners directly liable, not merely the entity.
