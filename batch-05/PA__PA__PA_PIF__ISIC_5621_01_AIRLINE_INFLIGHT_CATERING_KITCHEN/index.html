Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) – Ministry of Economy and Finance, Republic of Panama; electronic taxpayer portal at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa (SITIO e-filing system).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must be duly registered in the Public Registry of Panama, appoint a resident agent, obtain a Notary-certified extract of the foundation charter, apply for a digital signature token, and then register online or in person with the DGI Single Registry of Taxpayers (RUC). The application must include the foundation deed, registered address, description of the taxable activity (airline inflight catering and ramp delivery), and the resident agent's identification. The DGI will inscribe the PIF for income tax and ITBMS (VAT) and issue the statutory RUC number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available under Panamanian law. The Private Interest Foundation is treated as a separate juridical person subject to entity-level taxation on Panama-source income only. Foreign-source income is outside the territorial tax base; qualifying export revenues are zero-rated for ITBMS. A small-taxpayer simplified regime may be elected only for persons with annual gross receipts not exceeding USD 36,000.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 36,000 annual taxable turnover for compulsory regular ITBMS registration in the general regime; persons at or below this threshold may be inscribed under the small-taxpayer regime subject to a reduced rate of 2% on taxable sales with no input VAT credit. Once engaged in commercial operations in Panama, obtaining RUC and ITBMS registration is mandatory even if actual turnover has not yet reached the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Meals, beverages, and inflight supplies produced in a bonded catering kitchen and physically delivered to an aircraft departing from Panamanian territory qualify as exports of goods for ITBMS purposes when the goods leave the customs territory under an authorized customs procedure. Ancillary ramp-handling and logistics services directly linked to the offshore flight are zero-rated when they are performed for the benefit of a non-resident carrier and substantiated as consumed outside Panama. Eligibility requires that the supplier holds an export license from the National Customs Authority (ANA), maintains separate accounting for export sales, and submits periodic ITBMS declarations containing a zero-rated schedule. Direct proof of departure and receipt outside Panama must be maintained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera) electronically validated by the National Customs Authority (ANA)
  • Master and house air waybills evidencing the aircraft dispatch from Panama
  • Signed proof of delivery / ramp handover receipt from the receiving airline or authorized ground handler
  • Commercial invoice and packing list explicitly identifying the zero-rated ITBMS treatment
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing payment of the export invoice by the non-resident airline
  • Flight schedule or departure report confirming the aircraft's registration and destination outside Panama

Panama mandates phased electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica) through the DGI's SITIO system. Taxpayers with annual gross turnover above USD 5 million must transmit invoices in real time or within 24 hours for DGI digital validation and assignment of a unique financial fiscal stamp; other taxpayers must adopt e-invoicing according to the DGI schedule. Export invoices must carry the zero-rated ITBMS feeder code and reference the export customs document. Fiscal printing equipment remains acceptable for non-mandated taxpayers, but electronic issuance is the long-term statutory standard.

DGI and ANA jointly scrutinize zero-rating claims, with a general tax audit statute of limitations of five years. If export evidence is found false, incomplete, or inconsistent, the zero rating is annulled retroactively, resulting in immediate assessment of the 7% ITBMS, monthly late surcharges of 1.5%, and fines ranging from 100% to 200% of the omitted tax, together with criminal penalties for customs fraud. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days may trigger recharacterization of the unrepatriated amount as a Panama-source taxable gain subject to 25% corporate income tax and additional non-compliance surcharges for late filing and payment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates under full de facto dollarization, so the current account and capital account are completely convertible in U.S. dollars. There are no central-bank exchange controls, no prior approval for cross-border payment instructions, and no profit-repatriation license requirements. The Panamanian balboa exists only as coins pegged 1:1 to the USD and does not create convertibility friction. Profit remittances and foundation beneficiary distributions can be made freely through SBP-licensed banks. The principal obstacle is not FX but the territorial tax framework: Panamanian-source commercial profits are subject to corporate income tax and dividend equalization rules, whereas income sourced outside Panama is outside the territorial tax net if the PIF does not operate a Panamanian-source commercial establishment. A passive PIF can remit distributions to beneficiaries without an FX conversion or exchange-control filing; an onshore catering kitchen would need to account for Panamanian-source revenue as taxable income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory deadline for the repatriation of export or trade revenue. Receipts from airline in-flight catering contracts, including wire payments from non-resident airlines, may be held indefinitely in USD in Panamanian bank accounts or foreign accounts. The only compliance requirements are the SBP and Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) anti-money-laundering reporting rules, including currency transaction reporting for cash above USD 10,000 and suspicious-transaction monitoring for electronic wires; these do not amount to exchange controls.

Central bank supervision: Not applicable - Panama does not have a central bank and does not maintain a national foreign-exchange administration. The monetary framework is full dollarization and the U.S. dollar is legal tender. The principal banking regulatory authority is the Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá, SBP). The state-owned Banco Nacional de Panama acts solely as a commercial and fiscal agent, not as a monetary authority or exchange-control body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for use as the primary treasury account; Medium as an auxiliary low-value collection and disbursement channel. Offshore digital EMIs and non-bank financial service providers such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex are not licensed as banks by the SBP and are not treated as Panamanian banks. They cannot issue commercial letters of credit, hold regulatory deposit status, or serve as the principal escrow account for PIF capital. Some EMIs can process inbound/outbound USD transfers through local correspondent banks, but account opening for a PIF by an offshore EMI will still require the full beneficial-ownership file, apostilled foundation charter, notarized Foundation Council resolution, and passport verification. Balances held with an unlicensed EMI are not protected by Panama's deposit framework and should be limited to low-value disbursement rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial foundation capital must be wired as a serial SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident settlor to the PIF's designated Capital Escrow Account at the Panamanian bank. The payment narrative should state Endowment to Private Interest Foundation under Panama Law 25, with no commercial invoice reference. The beneficiary account field must match the foundation name exactly as shown in the foundation charter and bank account opening documents. For commercial receivables from airline catering clients, the remitting airline or its treasury vendor should send USD by SWIFT MT103 with payment reason Flight catering invoice [number], catering contract [reference], and the airline's IATA billing code. Panama has no central-bank purpose-code taxonomy, so the receiving bank relies on the SWIFT narrative for AML classification. Verification procedures include OFAC screening, PEP and sanctions checks, account-title matching, and comparison of the payment reason against the PIF's declared business purpose. There is no exchange-control filing or FX conversion because the funds remain in USD. The Capital Escrow Account must be segregated from the Operational Account until the Foundation Council passes a written resolution authorising use of the endowment.Cross-border supplier payments should be executed by SWIFT MT103 serial or cover payment from the PIF's Operational Account. Payment references must include the supplier invoice number, purchase order, and catering-kitchen import document reference. For high-value imported meal components and galley equipment, the preferred clearing rail is a commercial documentary letter of credit issued by the Panamanian bank through SWIFT MT700/MT710, payable at sight or on a 30-to-60-day tenor against presentation of commercial invoice, airway bill, cold-chain certificate, packing list, and sanitary certificate. Local recurring payments for airport concession fees, rent, utilities, and monthly wages should be settled through Panama's local ACH clearing mechanism or same-day bank transfer; because Panama has no central-bank RTGS, large same-day USD values are finally settled through the banks' correspondent accounts in the U.S. clearing system. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory dividend withholding tax when distributions are made from profits that have borne Panamanian corporate income tax; 20% withholding tax applies to dividends or beneficiary distributions made from profits that have not been subject to Panamanian corporate income tax. For a Private Interest Foundation, distributions of Panamanian-source catering profits to beneficiaries are treated as dividend-equivalent distributions for withholding purposes.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where gross income exceeds PAB 3,000,000 and controlled transactions with related enterprises exceed PAB 1,000,000; Master File is mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds PAB 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations, implemented through Law 70 of 2019 and Executive Decree 167 of 2021, impose substance requirements principally on entities deriving mobile or cross-border income under preferential regimes. In-flight catering physically performed from a Panamanian kitchen is not a relevant activity; nonetheless, the DGI may disregard the separate personality of a Private Interest Foundation if it operates as a shell without personnel, premises, or equipment. The PIF must maintain actual staff, kitchen facilities, delivery vehicles, and operational control over ramp logistics to defend its territorial tax treatment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Declaring

Statutory sanction: Panama's Criminal Code penalizes tax evasion and fraud with imprisonment of two to five years; the tax authority may additionally assess the evaded tax, monthly default interest, and surcharges of up to 50% of the deficiency, followed by seizure and judicial auction of kitchen assets and delivery vehicles.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members, founder, and registered agent may be held personally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax. Courts may pierce the Private Interest Foundation's veil where it was used as a sham to evade tax; travel bans, restriction of movement, disqualification from serving as foundation agents, and imprisonment can follow.
Shell Foundation / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The DGI may disregard the foundation's separate legal status, re-characterize all catering revenue as Panamanian-source, disallow deductions, assess the full 25% corporate tax plus default interest and penalties, and impose fines for failure to maintain economic substance and registered beneficial ownership records.

Director personal liability: Directors and foundation council members who knowingly operate the foundation without real economic activity face personal liability for assessed tax deficiencies. Travel bans may be imposed pending criminal investigation, and convictions for fraudulent use of legal persons can lead to imprisonment.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Panama's AML regime authorizes fines of up to USD 1,000,000 per violation, suspension of the foundation's commercial license, confiscation of the funds involved, and criminal prosecution under money laundering statutes with sentences of five to twelve years of imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members and compliance officers face direct criminal responsibility, personal fines, travel bans, and extradition exposure when the foundation's financial infrastructure is deliberately used to conceal illicit proceeds.
Transfer Pricing and Related-Party Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The DGI may impose penalties equal to 1% of the adjusted related-party transaction value, recalculate the taxpayer's taxable income using the most appropriate arm's-length method, assess the resulting tax at 25%, and add default interest and late-filing surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve inaccurate transfer pricing documentation may be jointly liable for the additional tax, interest, and penalties. In aggravated cases involving coordinated evasion through the foundation, personal civil restitution, travel bans, and criminal sanctions apply.
