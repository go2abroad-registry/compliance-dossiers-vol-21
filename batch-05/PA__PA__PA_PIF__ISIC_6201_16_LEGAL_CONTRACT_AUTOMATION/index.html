Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) of Panama; official tax portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa; electronic taxpayer services portal: https://sede.dgi.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

A Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must first be duly constituted and registered in the Registro Público de Panamá through a foundation deed (escritura constitutiva) or private instrument authenticated by a licensed attorney and a Panama Resident Agent. After registration, the Resident Agent files the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) application with the DGI through the Sede Electrónica, attaching the registered foundation deed, certified copy of the foundation charter, resolution appointing the foundation council, identification of foundation council members and the Resident Agent, and proof of registered address. No physical presence or power-of-attorney travel by foreign founders or foreign beneficiaries is required. The DGI issues a single RUC that is used simultaneously for income tax, ITBMS, and other fiscal identifiers. If the PIF engages in Panama-source taxable activities, it must also obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación before issuing invoices or receiving taxable income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama has no 'check-the-box' election. A PIF is treated as a separate juridical person with territorial tax liability: only Panama-source income is subject to Panamanian income tax; foreign-source income remains outside Panamanian taxation if it is not derived from activities carried out in Panama. The PIF may elect the simplified small taxpayer ITBMS regime (pequeño contribuyente) if annual Panamanian-source gross turnover does not exceed B/.36,000, using a fixed deemed-credit rate instead of the standard accrual ITBMS. For offshore LegalTech/SaaS services, the PIF may document its ITBMS records as zero-rated export services when all statutory requirements are met. No hybrid mismatch check-the-box elections or similar statutory classifications are available under Panamanian law; foreign entity classifications are governed by the foundation instrument and the residence of the founder for tax avoidance purposes, subject to general anti-abuse rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 annual Panamanian-source gross turnover (Balboa at par with USD) is the statutory threshold for the simplified small taxpayer ITBMS regime; regular ITBMS registration is mandatory above this threshold. In practice, any entity that imports goods or performs taxable commercial transactions in Panama must obtain an RUC regardless of turnover. Non-resident suppliers of digital services are also subject to ITBMS on supplies effectively used or enjoyed in Panama and must comply with DGI registration or withholding obligations without the benefit of the small-taxpayer local threshold where Panamanian law requires tax collection by payment facilitators or electronic platforms.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under ITBMS applies to: (1) tangible goods physically exported from Panama through a definitive customs export process; and (2) services supplied to non-resident customers for use, exploitation, or benefit wholly outside Panama, including cloud document automation, e-signature authentication, and LegalTech SaaS services, provided the service is not performed in Panama and no Panamanian taxable nexus is created. The PIF must prove that the customer is non-resident, the digital platform or service is delivered outside Panama, and all revenue originates from abroad. The zero-rating must be reflected in the ITBMS monthly declaration and supported by permanent documentation retained for at least the statutory limitation period.

  • Definitive Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación Definitiva) filed with the Dirección General de Aduanas (DGA) through the Panama Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical movement of goods from Panama; for SaaS/digital services, a signed digital service certificate and user-delivery report in lieu of transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of payment in foreign currency from the non-resident customer in a Panamanian bank account
  • Commercial invoice or export invoice expressly stating 'Gravado 0% ITBMS - Exportación'
  • Signed foundation customer contract / LegalTech SaaS agreement with payment, access, and IP-location evidence demonstrating use outside Panama
  • Copy of the PIF's RUC registration and the ITBMS declaration pages showing the zero-rated export line items

Mandatory electronic invoicing under Panama's DGI 'Facturación Electrónica' (SFE) system is in force. All ITBMS-registered taxpayers must issue e-invoices by transmitting the invoice data to the DGI SFE platform in real time at or before issuance and receive a unique authorization code and a digital QR/digital stamp from the DGI. The exchange uses DGI-approved software, an API, or the DGI free web portal with a structured XML/JSON payload defined by DGI technical guidance. The DGI does not recognize as valid any invoice lacking the online authorization; late transmission, unauthorized invoices, or erroneous zero-rating codes are rejected and subject to penalties and surcharges. The timeline of mandatory implementation is phased by taxpayer category, with large, middle, and small taxpayers progressively integrated through DGI resolutions issued since 2022.

The DGI may reclassify zero-rated export transactions as taxable Panama-source supplies if the documentation, user-location evidence, or 180-day receipt/repatriation proof is inadequate. In such cases the DGI will assess the undeclared 7% ITBMS, default interest at the legal tax rate, monthly surcharges up to the statutory maximum (frequently up to 50% of the tax), and statutory fines for inaccurate filings. Fraudulent export zero-rating, forged customs declarations, false bills of lading, or sham service contracts constitute tax and customs offences under the Código Fiscal and Panamanian customs legislation, exposing the PIF, its officers, and its Resident Agent to criminal sanctions, retrospective clawback for the full period not yet prescribed, cancellation of e-invoice authorization privileges, and removal from exporter registries.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized economy with no central-bank foreign exchange control decree, no surrender requirement for export proceeds, and no convertibility bottleneck for current or capital account flows. Capital account convertibility is effectively free for foundational contributions, PIF asset transfers, and foreign-held beneficiary disbursements, subject to bank-level AML/Source-of-Wealth due diligence. Profit and dividend remittances from Panamanian business activities are routed under the territorial tax model; the 10% dividend tax (Law 52 of 2017) applies to distributions sourced from Panama- sourced taxable profits, while amounts attributable to foreign-source income and passive non-Panama activities can be distributed without additional tax withholding. A PIF is not a commercial operating company and its foundation council distributions are generally private patrimony transfers rather than dividends, so remittance barriers are limited to bank proof-of-foundation-validity and internal compliance classification rather than government FX authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation to surrender, repatriate, or convert export receipts. The Panama legal framework contains no mandatory conversion ratio, no 90/180-day export settlement calendar, and no central bank ledger requirement for foreign currency invoices. The relevant compliance burden is bank-side: invoices and customs documentation supporting services under ISIC 6201 must be preserved in the client dossier for AML transaction-scope testing, but the PIF can retain all USD and non-USD funds in local or offshore accounts without forced conversion. The same regime applies to cloud automation and e-signature licensing revenue, which is treated as foreign-source service income if the customer, execution, and contractual nexus sit outside Panama's territory.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) — Banking and financial licensing supervisor; the Panama Balboa serves as the onshore exchange-rate anchor to the US Dollar under full dollarization. The Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) exercises no foreign exchange allocation authority and the Panamanian Tax Administration (DGI) does not administer exchange control approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) accounts have marginal viability for a Panama PIF. The SBP maintains a closed licensing perimeter for deposit-taking and e-money services, so a foreign EMI cannot hold the PIF treasury without either a Panama branch license or a local license as a 'Sociedad de Emisión y Pago Electrónico'. A low-risk LegalTech SaaS PIF may use a licensed Panamanian EMI as a payment-acceptance gateway, but settlement of STIRPE credit-card receivables must be collected through a Panamanian bank account that enjoys USD clearing capability. Viability rating: LOW as a primary treasury conduit and MEDIUM only as an auxiliary pay-in gateway subject to SBP-regulated third-party clearing and full PCI-DSS controls.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions to the PA_PIF must be wired in USD through SWIFT using MT103 with a precise OBI/remittance field: 'Capital Contribution to Private Interest Foundation PA_PIF – Foundation Charter Date – Council Resolution'. Panama does not require exchange-control approval; however, the receiving bank will apply a beneficiary-profile screening and OFAC/UESAN sanctions filter. The PIF treasury should request a pre-account-opening wire test with a nominal USD amount, maintain proof of the founder's bank, and document the transaction as a pure donation/patrimony contribution rather than a sales invoice payment. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends distributed by Panamanian companies from Panama-source profits to resident or non-resident shareholders, unless the PIF is a foundation that does not distribute 'dividends' but may distribute (when structured as corporation). If a PIF is organized as a corporation (SA) under Law 32 of 1927, the 10% dividend withholding applies. If pure foundation, distributions to beneficiaries are not subject to dividend WHT if not classified as Panamanian-source corporate profits but may be taxable to foreign beneficiary in resident country.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panamanian TP documentation thresholds under Decree No. 356 of 2016 and Resolution No. 58-2018: entities with gross yearly revenue above USD 3,000,000 or related party transactions equal or above USD 1,000,000 are required to prepare Local File. Master File required for groups with consolidated annual revenue of USD 21,000,000 or more. Full OECD compliant documentation required if part of group with global turnover equal or above USD 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not have legislative ESR mirroring UAE, but since Law 254 of 2021, legal entities (including PIF) must report economic substance on their beneficial ownership declaration. DGI may require justification of business purpose activity with sufficient human/asset presence if the company seeks Panama tax residency or treaty benefits. Lack of substance for a PIF generating SaaS revenue can lead to income reclassification as Panama-source if management and control exercised in Panama or if contracts/funds flow through Panama bank accounts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Territorial Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Fine equal to 25% to 200% evaded tax; criminal imprisonment of 3 to 5 years under Article 282-E Código Penal; administrative closure of the entity by DGI.

Director personal liability: Directors and foundation council may be jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts if they knowingly signed false returns or approved fraudulent distributions; travel bans and asset freezes under Panama ATP/GAFILAT enforcement.
Shell Company Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DGI may pierce through and apply full 25% tax on all worldwide revenues; registration cancellation under Law 254 reporting requirements; AML fines up to USD 50,000 – 100,000; criminal sanctions for abuse of legal personality under Law 23 of 2015.

Director personal liability: Directors or registered agent may incur personal penalties, revocation of professional license, travel bans and potential seizure of personal assets if found facilitating a sham corporate structure.
AML / KYC Violations (Banking Secrecy)

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties from USD 10,000 to USD 1,000,000; suspension of PIF rights by Registry; criminal money-laundering penalties up to 12 years imprisonment under Law 23 of 2015.

Director personal liability: Council members can be personally fined and imprisoned for laundering if they knew or should have known origin of funds, with confiscation of benefits received.
Failure to Maintain and Retain Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: DGI can adjust taxable basis and impose penalties between 50% and 100% of transfer pricing adjustment; dedicated penalty under Tax Code Art. 761; if intentional, criminal surcharge may apply.

Director personal liability: No criminal veil pierce for mere TP failure, but directors who neglect documentation can be liable for the penalty by law if shown gross negligence; authorities may move against third-party professional advisers jointly.
Improper Profit Repatriation / Dividend Withholding Non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Unpaid 10% WHT tax with interest; penalties up to 200%; criminal withholding evasion if deliberate; DGI refusal to issue clean tax solvency certificate (Certificado de Paz y Salvo).

Director personal liability: Directors and PIF council can be personally liable for withheld taxes under joint liability provisions; preventing bank transfers/sale of assets in Panama to enforce lien.
