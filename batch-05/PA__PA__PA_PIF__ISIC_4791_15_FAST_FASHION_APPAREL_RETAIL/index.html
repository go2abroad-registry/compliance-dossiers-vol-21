Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Panama law does not impose a fixed statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Private Interest Foundation; standard registry practice requires a fully subscribed initial endowment of US$10,000 stated in the foundation deed. The endowment may be in cash or assets, recorded in the notarial instrument, and held in any licensed Panamanian bank; no escrow account is statutorily mandated.

Funding & unlock path: Fire prevention certification issued by the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá for any leased office or warehouse, including fire extinguishers, emergency exits, signage, occupancy load compliance, and emergency lighting; structural load inspection required only for warehousing racking; no hazardous-materials clearance applies to apparel storage; workplace safety inspection falls under the Occupational Health Regulations of the Ministry of Labor (MITRADEL).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); electronic portal: SITIO (Sistema Integrado de Información Tributaria en Línea) at https://sitio.dgi.gob.pa. Ancillary authorities: Registro Público de Panamá (foundation registration) and Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA)/SISEA II (export clearance).

E-government filing portal

(1) Appoint a resident agent (licensed Panamanian attorney) under Law 25 of 1995; (2) execute and notarize the foundation charter (acta constitutiva) reflecting the e-commerce fast-fashion retail purpose; (3) register the charter with the Registro Público to perfect the PIF's legal existence; (4) file for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) via SITIO through the resident agent, submitting the registered charter, Panamanian registered address, ISIC 4791/NAICS 458110 activity code, and projected turnover; (5) activate in the same process the ITBMS registration and, for zero-rated exports, the DGI exporter registration and ANA exporter code; (6) obtain the corporate digital signature (firma electrónica), enroll in DGI's e-invoicing workspace, and open the operating bank account in Panama.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — a Panama Private Interest Foundation is a separate 'persona jurídica' subject to entity-level income tax (ISR) exclusively on Panama-source income; foreign-source income is excluded by the territoriality principle without an affirmative election; no pass-through or check-the-box treatment exists for the PIF itself.

Available election forms

Charter election (cláusula de reserva) under Law 25 of 1995 allowing trust-like administration and flexible beneficiary designation to allocate local-source income; elective 'Régimen Optativo' under Law 193 of 2021 blending Panama-source and foreign-source income with foreign-cost deductibility and foreign-tax credits; fiscal-unity (grupo fiscal) election for qualifying related Panamanian entities; tax-stability covenant with the MEF for qualifying investments. The territorial exclusion of foreign income is automatic and requires no election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 36,000 (US$ 36,000, balboa pegged 1:1 to the USD) of annual gross taxable turnover: at or below this threshold the e-commerce seller may remain in the simplified small-taxpayer regime (Régimen Especial Simplificado) without full ITBMS registration or monthly ITBMS returns; exceeding B/. 36,000 makes full ITBMS registration, monthly filing, and DGI e-invoicing mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% ITBMS applies when apparel/footwear is physically exported from Panama to non-resident buyers (proof: approved SISEA II export declaration, delivery on board or international courier, foreign-currency proceeds). Services: 0% only for services exhaustively listed in Executive Decree 84 of 2006 (offshore/digital services for foreign recipients) rendered to non-residents and consumed abroad. Domestic sales and digital services delivered into Panama remain taxable at 7%. DGI exporter registration and a valid ANA exporter code are preconditions for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (declaración de exportación) processed and authorized via SISEA II of the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill/international courier despatch ticket) showing physical exit from Panamanian customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign-currency proceeds received through the Panamanian banking system)
  • Commercial invoice in DGI e-invoicing format annotated 'Exportación' with the DGI authorization code
  • SITIO zero-rating working file: sales orders, packing lists, warehouse traceability records, and the ITBMS export refund/workings report

Mandatory DGI 'facturación electrónica' for all ITBMS-registered sellers: each invoice must be transmitted to the SITIO clearance engine in DGI-approved XML/JSON at the moment of issuance; the DGI engine returns an authorization status ('aceptada'/'rechazada') and a security code, and the invoice must carry that code and a QR-style digital stamp. Automated e-commerce platforms (Shopify Payments, Klarna BNPL) must integrate via DGI-sanctioned API/SFTP interfaces; offline emission is limited to DGI-approved outage windows with batch reconciliation.

Unsupported zero-rating claims are recharacterized by DGI as taxable domestic sales, with full 7% ITBMS clawback on the invoice value plus compensatory interest and graded monetary fines under the Tax Procedure Code (Law 76 of 2019). Forged or fictive export documents trigger criminal tax-fraud proceedings (defraudación fiscal), criminal fines and prison for responsible officers, cancellation of DGI/ANA exporter registrations, and retrospective clawback of all zero-rated transactions in the audited period. DGI routinely cross-validates SITIO e-invoice export stamps against ANA SISEA II cargo-clearance data to flag fictitious exports.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama Law 72 of 1946 (Cabinet Decree) and the Constitutional framework establish the U.S. Dollar as legal tender. There are no foreign exchange controls of any kind: both capital account and current account transactions are freely convertible and transferable without prior approval, surrender requirements, or licensing. Profit remittances, dividends, interest, and capital repatriations from a Private Interest Foundation are not subject to exchange control, withholding taxes (at source on a territorial basis), or regulatory hurdles. Trade settlement in USD is seamless; cross-currency balances (EUR, GBP) are freely converted through the banking system or non-bank clearing platforms without SBP or any government oversight for convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement exists in Panama. Exporters and service suppliers—including e-commerce fast-fashion retailers—are not obligated to repatriate foreign currency or convert export receipts within any timeline. Balances may be held indefinitely in USD or foreign currency accounts within the Panamanian banking system or abroad, subject only to bank-level AML/CFT record-keeping obligations under SBP Agreement (Acuerdo) 01-2014 and Decree 168-2017 implementing FATF standards.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as the prudential banking regulator; Panama maintains no conventional central bank and does not issue a domestic currency. Full legal tender is the U.S. Dollar, with the U.S. Federal Reserve effectively serving as the monetary authority, resulting in dollarization-based monetary stability.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High with restrictions. Panama enacted Ley 81 de 2019 (FinTech Innovation Hub and Regulatory Sandbox) under SBP direction, permitting digital banking and electronic payment providers. However, foreign offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) are not licensed as banks in Panama and cannot maintain SBP-prudential accounts. They may provide auxiliary payment/clearing services (merchant collection, FX conversion) via the Panamanian payment ecosystem provided they operate under an SBP-authorized ACH/Card processor license or serve only as unregulated foreign-utility providers. The PIF may use offshore EMIs for international settlement, but core treasury concentration should remain at an SBP-supervised commercial bank to preserve legal certainty, access to correspondent banking, and acceptable audit trail for the foundation's protector and beneficiaries.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the PIF's initial endowment paid up by the appointer/subscriber: wire via SWIFT MT103 from the appointer's bank (Panama, Colombia, or other) to the foundation's Capital Escrow Account at Multibank or another Tier-1 SBP bank. Use purpose code 'G000' (general/unspecified), but include in the remittance field: 'PIF capital endowment — contribution to foundation' plus the foundation name and Panama Public Registry charter number. Thereafter, for operational capital from the e-commerce platform's parent or Ecuadorian vendor structure, each inbound wire must reference a commercial invoice or cash-pooling agreement to pass SBP-required transactional due diligence.
