Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Autoridad Nacional de Ingresos Públicos (ANIP); created by Law 315 of 2023, this unified national revenue agency absorbed the former Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the customs authority as of 2024. Official portal: https://www.anip.gob.pa; RUC registration and income-tax returns are processed through ANIP's RUC and e-declaration modules; electronic invoicing is administered through ANIP's Sistema de Facturación Electrónica; customs export declarations are processed via Panama's single window (VUCE/customs system).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Constitute the Private Interest Foundation (PIF) under Law 25 of 1995 through a licensed resident agent and inscribe it in the Registro Público de Panamá to obtain juridical personality and the foundation's registration ficha. Step 2: Appoint a natural or juridical person as resident agent and any required resident legal representative; the registered office in Panama is mandatory for the PIF, and the PIF pays the compulsory annual state fee of USD 400 through the resident agent (this fee is not an income tax). Step 3: For tax purposes, apply to ANIP for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) using the registration form, attaching notarized and apostilled constitutive documents, the Public Registry issuance certificate, the resident agent's acceptance, and a Panama street address; for a foreign-operated PIF, the non-resident owner must be identified under Law 23 of 2015 AML/CDD rules but non-resident ownership is permissible. Step 4: If the PIF operates a physical office or commercial establishment in Panama, obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación (business license) from the relevant Alcaldía, and register as an employer with the Social Security Fund (CSS) only if it has Panamanian payroll. Step 5: Obtain ANIP-issued electronic signature certificate for e-invoicing and online declarations. Step 6: For the fertilizer wholesale activity, register for ITBMS at the DGI/ANIP office and apply for exporter-of-record status if exports will be declared; the PIF that limits activity to foreign-source wholesale and logistics outside Panama may remain classified as a non-active taxpayer but must still file RUC renewals and respond to annual declarations as required by ANIP, since territorial source rules govern Panama-tax exposure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope; the PIF is a separate juridical entity taxed at entity level only on Panama-source income (corporate income tax 25%), while all foreign-source income, capital gains, and distributions of foreign income to non-resident beneficiaries are outside the Panamanian income-tax net; the PIF is not a pass-through entity in Panama, but its foreign income is statutorily exempt, which creates a de facto exemption for cross-border wholesale trading (exempt from income tax on profits from offshore sales of fertilizers such as prilled urea under HS 3102 and potassium chloride under HS 3104; any domestic Panama sale is taxable locally).

Available election forms

Panama has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections and special treatments include: (1) simplified small-taxpayer regime for ITBMS and income tax where annual gross income does not exceed USD 36,000 in local market operations; (2) election of monthly or quarterly ITBMS return filing periods based on ANIP's turnover-band tables; (3) consolidated income-tax administration for groups of companies held directly or indirectly at a 100% ownership level; (4) voluntary designation as exporter of record to substantiate 0% ITBMS treatment on outbound physical deliveries; (5) for operations routed through the Colon Free Zone or other free-zone regimes, the relevant free-zone license election that keeps the merchandise outside the Panamanian tax territory; and (6) hybrid mismatch-related treatment: Panama has not enacted domestic hybrid-mismatch rules under BEPS Action 2, so the PIF's hybrid nature may create deductible/non-inclusive outcomes only in the counterparty jurisdiction, which must be assessed under the counterparty's rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary de minimis threshold for ITBMS registration: any person habitually performing taxable transfers of goods or services within Panama, or obtaining taxable imports, must register and obtain a RUC regardless of turnover; a simplified small-taxpayer regime is available only below annual gross income of USD 36,000, and exporters of goods (including wholesale fertilizer traders) must register as regular taxpayers even if all supplies are at 0% so that input credits can be claimed; domestic wholesale sales of urea and potassium chloride within Panama trigger 7% ITBMS at the time of invoicing/delivery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% ITBMS) is available for: (1) goods physically exported from Panama to foreign territory, including FOB/CIF sales of bulk fertilizer where the seller is the exporter of record and goods exit Panamanian customs; (2) services rendered to non-resident persons and effectively used or exploited wholly outside Panama; (3) supplies to free zones (including Colon Free Zone) and to ships or aircraft in international traffic. The PIF may claim 0% on wholesale physical charter sales of prilled urea and potassium chloride only when the sales contract, INCOTERM, Letter of Credit, bill of lading, and customs declaration all evidence removal from Panama to the foreign buyer; a sale where title or risk passes within Panama is a taxable domestic transfer at 7%. Exporters may file refund applications (devolución de saldos a favor) for accrued input ITBMS; refunds are subject to ANIP documentary review and may be retained as credit against future taxes until cleared.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) lodged through Panama's customs single window (VUCE/customs system) with ANIP endorsement and proof of physical exit from Panamanian territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the export declaration
  • Clean on-board Bill of Lading or Airway Bill issued to the foreign buyer or shorter line; for surface/land export, the corresponding international waybill or exit certificate
  • Sales contract and Purchase Order, plus documentary credit (SWIFT MT700) or wire-transfer order evidencing the foreign-domiciled counterparty
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice or SWIFT MT103 evidencing collection of proceeds to the PIF's account
  • For offshore services: written contract, foreign-resident certificate of the recipient, and evidence that the service was consumed outside Panama

ANIP requires mandatory electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica) in phased rollout fully operative for all registered taxpayers as of 2025. Issuers must generate XML invoices using ANIP-certified software or the ANIP web issuer, transmit each invoice for validation, and receive an ANIP-issued authorization code (Código Único de Facturación) with a QR/digital seal before the buyer accepts the document. Large and medium-risk taxpayers are subject to real-time API clearance using web services (REST/SOAP with PKI e-signature); smaller taxpayers may use web-based batch submission. Non-authorized documents are legally invalid, support no input ITBMS credit, and trigger rejection of expense deductibility.

ANIP may retroactively re-characterize a zero-rated export as a domestic taxable supply if the customs declaration, shipping evidence, contract chain, or incoming payment record is missing, inconsistent, or circular. Consequences include: (i) reopening of closed fiscal years under the 3-year statute of limitations (extendable on fraud grounds), (ii) assessment of 7% ITBMS on the gross invoice amount plus moratory interest, (iii) fines up to 100% to 200% of the omitted tax in cases of willful misstatement, (iv) criminal tax-fraud liability of the PIF's legal representative and resident agent under the Código Fiscal and Código Penal, including imprisonment sanctions, (v) cancellation of exporter status and denial of future e-invoicing authorization, and (vi) if proceeds are not received within 180 days from shipment, a rebuttable presumption of Panama-source sale unless the PIF produces a formal amendment or extension approved by ANIP and bank/credit documentation showing genuine renegotiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates on full hard-dollarization with the United States dollar as legal tender. The Panamanian Balboa exists only as fractional coinage pegged at exactly 1:1 to the USD. Capital account and current account transactions are fully convertible; there are no differential exchange rates, no surrender requirements, and no exchange-rate approval gates. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, capital repatriation, and intra-group funding may be executed freely in USD without central bank filing or conversion hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to sell export receipts into local currency. Fertilizer exporters and PIF treasury desks may retain export collections in USD, maintain offshore settlement accounts, or liquidate through any licensed commercial bank without administrative authorization.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as banking and financial system regulator, with Banco Nacional de Panamá acting as fiscal agent, clearing bank, and depositary of public funds; there is no issuing central bank because Panama is a formally dollarized economy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMI firms without a Panama banking license or SBP payment-system authorization cannot directly access local USD clearing or domestic deposit infrastructure. A Panama PIF engaged in fertilizer/physical commodities should use EMIs only for pre-funded ancillary disbursements, never for primary escrow, LC, or high-value trade settlement. Any EMI claiming to clear through Panama must evidence a licensed local sponsor bank and valid SBP authorization.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be routed as high-value SWIFT MT103/MT202 USD payments from financiers, shareholders, or parent trading companies directly into the PIF's escrow or operating account at a fully licensed Panamanian bank. Since Panama has no central-bank exchange registration, the controlling evidentiary burden lies with the receiving bank: the remitter must use a clear reference such as 'INVERSION DE CAPITAL - TRADE EQUITY' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN FOR FERTILIZER PREPAYMENT', and the PIF must provide a source-of-funds certificate plus the underlying charter party or sale-purchase contract. Beneficiary bank verification may include KYC calls, callback confirmation, a copy of the MT103 confirmation, and a notarized council resolution authorizing receipt of funds. In the absence of central bank purpose codes, Panamanian banks treat inbound wire descriptions strictly under their internal transaction classification, so treasury instructions should never use opaque or mixed descriptions that combine personal gifts with commodity prepayments.Trade disbursements for physical fertilizer cargo should use SWIFT MT103 for direct payments to suppliers, freight forwarders, port agents, and surveyors. For high-value or open-account de-risking scenarios, commercial documentary collections and irrevocable confirmed at-sight letters of credit (LC) via MT700 are the preferred clearing rails, especially when a Panamanian PIF acts as intermediary between an Oceanic seller and a Latin American buyer. Panamanian banks may issue or confirm LC's backed by vessel and warehouse collaterals; payment against presentation of full-set bills of lading, commercial invoices, certificate of origin, quality certificates for prilled urea or potassium chloride, and insurance certificates. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB 2,000,000

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), introduced by Law 70 of 2019 and the Executive Decrees on Substance for Panamanian entities, require businesses that engage in 'relevant activities' to maintain adequate staff, expenditure and premises in Panama. A PIF acting as a wholesale commodity trading company must satisfy the economic substance test in proportion to its activities; otherwise, the Panama DGI may recharacterize its existence as a shell and impose sanctions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 100% of the evaded tax plus monthly surcharges and interest; criminal prosecution under Panama Criminal Code Article 278 (tax evasion) with imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and debarment from future government contracts.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil is pierced; directors or foundation council members who signed false declarations are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, subject to travel bans (solicitud de impedimento de salida), property embargoes, and personal arrest.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Penalties under Law 70 of 2019: fines of up to USD 50,000 for each taxable year of non-compliance; the entity may be struck off the foundation registry and forced into liquidation.

Director personal liability: Members of the foundation council are personally liable for the reinstatement costs, unpaid ESR penalties; the Panama Superintendent of Non-Financial Businesses may petition to block travel and freeze assets pending investigation.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: ML/TF Law of 2023 (Law 105) fines: up to USD 1,000,000 for aggravated non-compliance; the PIF's registry entry may be suspended and the financial license of associated trustee (resident agent) revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors and foundation protectors face criminal liability, confiscation of assets, a 10-year travel bar and imprisonment of 5 to 12 years for knowingly executing or permitting the offending acts.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Penalty

Statutory sanction: Economic adjustment by DGI of up to 100% of the price differential; late filing penalty of 0.5% of the transaction amount per month or a fixed fine of USD 2,500–10,000; failure to present supporting local file triggers a presumption of irregularity and a fine of up to 2% of the controlled transaction value.

Director personal liability: Where the transfer pricing director knew of the non-arm's length arrangement, the director is personally jointly liable for the tax adjustment plus interest; in severe cases a criminal referral for prefabricated evidence may be made.
