Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas – SITIO electronic portal (https://sitio.dgi.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (including a Private Interest Foundation) must obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) by submitting a sworn registration form through SITIO, attaching certified articles of incorporation, certificate of registry, power of attorney granted to a Panama-licensed attorney, and identification documents of foundation council members and beneficial owner. The application is processed online, after which an RUC certificate is issued and the entity is registered for income tax and ITBMS purposes. Failure to register triggers fines and bars legal invoice issuance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available under Panamanian law. A PIF may, however, elect to remain within the territorial regime by filing a sworn affidavit under Article 100 of the Foundation Law, and may elect the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007 if it establishes qualifying headquarters activities, thereby accessing specific tax stabilization incentives. No pass-through election is recognized for PIFs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 36,000 (annual gross turnover) for the simplified small taxpayer regime; otherwise, no general de minimis threshold, and any entity habitually selling goods or rendering services in Panama must register for ITBMS with the DGI.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The sale of tangible goods exported from Panama is subject to ITBMS at 0%, provided the goods must leave the country within 30 days after invoicing or as contractually agreed, and the exporter maintains full documentary evidence flowing from the Panama Customs export entry. Services are zero-rated when rendered to a foreign recipient who is not domiciled in Panama for use and economic enjoyment entirely outside Panama, subject to the service recipient being taxable or engaging in commercial activity in its home jurisdiction. Food products classified under ISIC 1079, including self-heating instant meals, are eligible for zero-rating upon export only if the exporter is an ITBMS-registered taxpayer and the goods are physically delivered to a foreign buyer.

  • Customs Export Declaration – Declaración Única Aduanera (DUA) duly accepted by the Panama Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing goods exited Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer credit statement proving payment from abroad for the exported goods

Mandatory electronic invoicing is phased in for large taxpayers and all registered taxpayers; invoices must be structured in XML and transmitted through the SITIO portal for DGI validation and digital stamping. The system requires the use of digital certificates issued by a DGI-accredited certification provider; the electronic invoice must embed a QR code and unique invoice number. Validation by DGI is near real-time for large taxpayers, with referential deadlines for smaller taxpayers as set forth in DGI Resolution 253-2021 and subsequent amendments.

Export zero-rating is strictly scrutinized by DGI and Customs. Each exporter must maintain independent records of each shipment and corresponding foreign remittance for at least 5 years. If documentation is incomplete or remittance is not received within 180 days from shipment, the ITBMS at 7% (or the applicable higher rate for certain goods) becomes due immediately, with interest (currently 10% per annum) and a surcharge of 10% per month up to a maximum of 50% of the tax due. Deliberate falsification of export documents exposes the taxpayer to criminal penalties under Panama Criminal Code, including imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine equal to 200% of the tax evaded. DGI may also revoke the taxpayer's RUC and zero-rating authorization for up to 2 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama has no independent domestic currency; the U.S. dollar is legal tender, and the Panamanian balboa is a non-circulating unit of account pegged at 1:1 to the U.S. dollar. Both current and capital accounts are fully convertible in practice; there are no exchange controls, no currency restrictions, and no prior Central Bank authorization is required for dividend remittances, repatriation of investment capital, or trade payments. Profit remittances are tax-deductible for foreign corporations subject to the territorial tax system, and only a 10% dividend tax is withheld on distributions sourced from Panama when paid to non-residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Exporters are free to maintain U.S. dollar balances in any foreign or domestic bank, settle in any currency freely negotiable, and there is no statutory conversion ratio enforced. Export documentation requirements are for customs control only and have no exchange-control purpose.

Central bank supervision: No central bank exists in Panama. Banco Nacional de Panamá functions solely as a fiscal agent for the Republic of Panama and engages in limited reserve management, while the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the regulatory authority for commercial banking activity and the Ministry of Economy and Finance governs financial and exchange policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for fully offshore digital EMIs without any physical or legal presence in Panama. While the SBP has issued fintech-related regulations (notably the authorisation of short-term microfinance and crowdfunding; a broader sandbox and payment provider licensing are evolving), a non-bank EMI incorporated abroad will not generally be permitted to hold Panamanian corporate operating accounts or to process ACH/wire clearing in Panama unless registered with the SBP under a banking or payment-services license. An established cross-border EMI may clear USD via its own U.S. banking partner but cannot rely on Panamanian domestic rails. Legal constraints also include the obligation to comply with Panamanian KYC/AML Law 23 of 2015 and the availability of physical-regulated intermediaries.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder capital is routed via SWIFT wire transfer (MT103) from the investor’s bank to the PIF’s operational or capital escrow account in a Panamanian Tier-1 bank. As Panama uses the U.S. dollar, incoming funds do not require conversion or Central Bank approval; however, the receiving bank will request a purpose statement. A compliant purpose code in the message should be 'TRF' (Transfer) with narrative 'Contribution to capital/working capital of Private Interest Foundation – ISIC 1079 food manufacturing'. If the PIF has a direct U.S. correspondent, the wire may also clear via Fedwire. The receiving bank will verify that the sending institution is not on OFAC, EU sanctioned, or SBP watchlist lists, and will match the sender name against the foundation’s registered UBO declarations.Outbound payments for raw materials, packaging, and logistics are executed by SWIFT MT103 or via local telegraphic transfer (TT) through the Panamanian bank’s own correspondent network. For commercial settlements exceeding USD 100,000, a standard documentary letter of credit (UCP600) may be used with the bank as advising or confirming institution. Because Panama is a dollarized jurisdiction, no FX conversion is needed when the beneficiary is in a USD-denominated jurisdiction; for non-USD beneficiary accounts, the payment is routed via a general correspondence bank and converted at a market rate plus standard margin. All outbound instructions must include the beneficiary bank’s BIC/SWIFT and an accurate purpose code; a commercial invoice or purchase order must be on file to satisfy the bank’s trade-transaction audit requirements.Outright forward FX contracts with a Panamanian commercial bank or a U.S. correspondent bank for currencies such as EUR, GBP, or JPY when trade disbursements are denominated in those currencies.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) with international banks for exotic currencies where convertibility is constrained in the local market (e.g., certain LATAM currencies); these are executed offshore as no Panama FX restrictive permit exists.Cross-currency swaps with an investment-grade international counterparty to convert long-term non-USD debt or receivable flows into USD, thereby eliminating currency risk on the PIF’s balance sheet.Vanilla interest-rate swaps (basis swaps) are not classified as FX hedging but may be permitted by SBP-registered banks to hedge interest-rate exposure on USD-denominated facilities; these are allowed for commercial purposes.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File when annual related-party transactions exceed B/.2,000,000; Master File required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds B/.10,000,000; a simplified related-party return (Form 930) must be filed by all taxpayers with intercompany transactions regardless of threshold

Economic substance scope: Energy Substance Regulations (Law 24/2019 and Executive Decree 95/2020) apply to PIFs engaged in relevant activities such as IP holding, headquarter services, distribution and service centers, financing, or shipping. Manufacturing of self-heating meals (ISIC 1079) is not itself a relevant activity, but if the PIF owns the brand IP and licences it to a Panama manufacturer, the licensing activity will trigger ESR substance requirements: physical local office, qualified staff, and core income-generating activities are required. Pure equity-holding PIFs must meet a lighter substance test (registered office, local director, bookkeeping). Non-compliance can lead to forced dissolution and rejection of tax residency certificates.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Panamanian Fiscal Code and Law 70/2019: imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, fines of 100% to 300% of the evaded tax, monthly surcharge interest of 1.2%, and automatic revocation of any tax incentives or preferential rates

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, and legal representatives are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and fines; criminal liability attaches personally; courts may impose travel bans, emergency property embargoes, and international extradition requests
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR administrative penalties: B/.10,000 per violation for clear substance failure (up to B/.100,000 for repeated violations); DGI may reject the PIF's CRF, strike the foundation from the Registro Público, and freeze its bank accounts

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined from B/.5,000 to B/.50,000 and disqualified from serving as officers for up to five years; they cannot block account freezes; travel bans are likely for breach of the Economic Substance and AML laws
Dividend Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Panama's DGI will hold the withholding agent jointly liable for the unwithheld 10% dividend tax, plus monthly interest of 0.95% and a penalty of 100% of the omitted withholding if correction occurs after 18 months

Director personal liability: Directors as responsible withholding agents become personally liable for the unremitted tax; criminal tax fraud charges may result in personal monetary judgments and prohibition from leaving Panamanian territory
AML / UBO Disclosure Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Panama's Law 23/2015 and the General Banking Act, fines up to B/.250,000 and suspension of operations for non-compliance; knowingly false UBO declarations carry criminal liability punishable by imprisonment of up to three years

Director personal liability: Directors and members of the Foundation Council are subject to criminal prosecution if they knowingly failed to identify the UBO; sanctions include travel bans, asset forfeiture, and prohibition from serving as directors of any Panamanian legal entity
