Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Under Law 25 of 1995, there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital; the Foundation Charter must state a definitive initial endowment (patrimonio fundacional) sufficient for the declared purpose. Any undelivered endowment must be identified and, if cash, held in a designated bank account of the Foundation pending registration; no general statutory escrow account is required for private interest foundations, but customs bonded-warehouse approval will require a surety bond or customs guarantee.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification from the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá; structural load inspection of the warehouse by a licensed engineer and municipal works approval; occupational health and safety registration with MITRADEL; worker's risk insurance with CSS; civil-defense and building-code clearance for warehouse occupancy.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama applies a civil-law classification: no US-style check-the-box election exists. A Private Interest Foundation is a separate juridical person under Law 25 of 1995 and may elect the annual flat tax of PAB 300 (Article 24, Law 25/1995) rather than entity-level income taxation, provided it performs no Panamanian-source commercial activities. Where bonded trading is conducted in Panama, the territorial corporate income tax applies only to Panamanian-source revenue; the foundation may elect the SISA simplified regime if gross receipts are at or below the statutory threshold (currently PAB 200,000), and affiliated resident entities may elect fiscal-group consolidation. No hybrid-mismatch election is recognized; Panama applies internal source rules, transfer-pricing rules, and anti-deferral provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ITBMS registration is mandatory from the first habitual taxable supply in Panama; no general de minimis turnover threshold applies. Exporters and bonded-warehouse operators must hold full ITBMS registration to issue zero-rated export invoices and claim input credits/refunds. Small taxpayers with Panamanian-source gross receipts at or below PAB 200,000 may elect the SISA simplified regime, which consolidates income tax and ITBMS; bonded wine/spirit operators remain required to maintain full registration and electronic-invoice compliance in all cases.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports—including removals of duty-suspended aged Scotch and Bordeaux wines from an ANA bonded warehouse to a foreign destination—are zero-rated (tax at 0% with input credit/refund entitlement) provided: (i) the PIF is a DGI- and ANA-registered exporter; (ii) goods physically depart Panama under a validated DUA and transport document; (iii) the purchaser is a non-resident and the sale is documented as offshore; (iv) the invoice is an electronically stamped export invoice; (v) settlement is in USD or a freely convertible currency with bank documentation. Panama imposes no statutory FX repatriation term because the US dollar is legal tender; the 180-day window is applied as the banking and audit benchmark for documenting export proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduana – DUA) validated by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) with physical exit confirmation
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing shipment from Panama to the foreign destination
  • DGI-stamped electronic commercial invoice bearing the export legend and DUA cross-reference
  • Packing list and lot/weight schedule identifying each wine and spirit lot, bottle sizes, and precise alcohol strength
  • Certificate of origin or supplier's origin statement (e.g., EUR.1 / Annex declaration) where preferential duty treatment is sought
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit advice evidencing USD settlement of the export proceeds

Mandatory fiscal e-invoicing is enforced through the DGI's SICD platform. All invoices must be issued through an authorized certification service provider, electronically signed, and covered by a DGI digital stamp ('sellado digital') carrying a QR verification code before delivery to the purchaser. Export invoices must quote the ANA export declaration (DUA) number and be transmitted under the DGI technical protocol in near-real time; bonded-warehouse removals require an electronic dispatch notice linked to the ANA single window. Incomplete or late transmission is sanctioned as non-issuance of the invoice.

Misdeclared export zero-rating triggers re-characterization of the removal as a domestic taxable supply: ITBMS at 7% (plus applicable ISC on alcoholic beverages) together with monthly interest, surcharges of 25%–100% of the omitted tax, and fines under the Código Fiscal and DGI sanction schedules; retroactive clawback assessments are permitted within the five-year statutory term, extended where concealment or forgery is found. Criminal defraudación fiscal liability, including custodial exposure, may attach to directors, the protector, and the resident agent for simulated exports or forged DUA/B/L documentation, and the ANA may suspend or cancel the bonded-warehouse license and delist the operator from the exporter registry. Panama's absence of exchange controls means 'repatriation' compliance is assessed on documentary consistency, bank credit advisements, and AML indicators rather than a statutory day count.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized jurisdiction with no exchange controls on either current-account or capital-account transactions. The balboa circulates only as coins pegged 1:1 to the U.S. dollar. Profit remittances and capital transfers are freely permitted without FX authorization. However, the territorial corporate income tax (25%) and withholding rules on dividends distributed to non-resident individuals (potential 10% surcharge) require the foundation to document exempt foreign-source income separately from Panamanian-source income; no FX licensing or conversion approval is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline on export receipts. Proceeds from bonded wine and spirit exports may be retained and disbursed freely in USD. Nevertheless, exporters must comply with tax filing obligations, SBP AML/CFT reporting under Law 23/2015 for transactions above USD 10,000, and customs exit documentation through the SIEC system including bonded-warehouse withdrawal documents ('Libranza' or 'Solicitud de Salida') and destination declarations.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent. Panama has no conventional central bank; the regime is anchored on full dollarization with the U.S. dollar as unrestricted legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by the SBP and cannot serve as the primary treasury, escrow, or settlement account for a Panamanian PIF. Reliance on unsupervised EMIs creates unlicensed money-services exposure under Law 23/2015 and will typically cause SBP-regulated banks to reject incoming transfers as an AML red flag. A non-local EMI may be acceptable only for low-value ancillary disbursements if it is duly licensed in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction, is linked to verified trade documentation, and is never used for capital contributions or bonded-trade settlements.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (flat rate on net taxable income from Panamanian-source activities, Article 699 of the Código Fiscal; a compliant Private Interest Foundation under Law 25 of 1995 is exempt from corporate income tax on foreign-source bonded trade income)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory dividend withholding tax (Article 733-A of the Código Fiscal) applies to distributions from Panamanian-source profits of corporations; distributions made by a Private Interest Foundation to its beneficiaries are legally characterized as foundation distributions (not dividends) and are exempt from the 10% WHT under Law 25 of 1995, subject to proof that the distributed funds originated from foreign-source or otherwise exempt income

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File arise where associated transactions in a fiscal year exceed B/.2,000,000 (USD 2,000,000) or where gross revenue exceeds B/.2,000,000; a Country-by-Country report is required for a PIF that is part of a multinational group with consolidated revenue of at least B/.750,000,000; an Informative Return of transactions with related parties (Form ICT) is required when cross-border related-party transactions exceed B/.250,000

Economic substance scope: A Private Interest Foundation is under an economic-substance presumption under Law 25 of 1995, Law 23 of 2015 and OECD BEPS Pillar Two: the PIF must maintain a Panamanian registered agent, an internal register of council members and beneficiaries (escriturada), meeting minutes, and real external contracts with the UK/French bonded warehouses; if DGI determines the PIF carries on economic activity wholly or partly within Panama, the territorial exemption lapses and 25% ISR plus 10% annual interest is levied on the recapture base; no separate ESR notification is filed in Panama, but the DGI, the Superintendencia de Bancos and the beneficial-ownership registry may request substance evidence in any AML/CTF review

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Payment of the evaded tax plus default interest at 1.5% monthly; administrative fine of 1 to 3 times the evaded tax; criminal penalty of 2 to 5 years imprisonment; placement on the DGI public defaulter register; disqualification from customs bonded operations

Director personal liability: Directors and foundation council members are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and fines; the corporate veil is pierced, allowing seizure of personal assets; courts may issue travel bans (impedimentos de salida) and extradition requests under mutual legal assistance treaties
Sham Foundation / Shell Company Abusement

Statutory sanction: Retroactive loss of the Law 25 tax exemption; 25% ISR recapture of all prior exempt income with 10% annual interest; fines of B/.10,000 to B/.100,000 under Law 23 of 2015; risk of being listed as a non-cooperative entity by foreign tax authorities and being excluded from correspondent banking lines

Director personal liability: Civil restitution to creditors and beneficiaries; directors criminally prosecuted for fraudulent insolvency or fraud (Código Penal Articles 265 and 266) with 3 to 8 years imprisonment; disqualification from serving as company officers in Panama for 5 years
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Fines per violation of B/.10,000 to B/.1,000,000 against the foundation and its registered agent; suspension or cancellation of the registered agent license; freezing of the PIF's bank accounts; potential international financial blacklisting of the entity

Director personal liability: Council members personally liable for AML fines; imprisonment up to 3 years for money laundering where proceeds are knowingly derived from illicit distilled-spirits trafficking; travel bans and international arrest warrants may be issued
Excise / Customs Bonded Regime Breach

Statutory sanction: Immediate seizure of the bonded stock; fine equal to 1 to 3 times the evaded excise duty; suspension or cancellation of the customs bonded-warehouse operating permit; publication in the customs sanctions registry; reference to the DGI for criminal tax evasion proceedings if ITBMS is also evaded

Director personal liability: Joint and several responsibility of the licensee directors for the full unpaid excise and ITBMS amount; closure of the bond with consequential civil damages to the storage owners; directors face imprisonment of 1 to 4 years for customs smuggling (Ley de Delitos Aduaneros)
