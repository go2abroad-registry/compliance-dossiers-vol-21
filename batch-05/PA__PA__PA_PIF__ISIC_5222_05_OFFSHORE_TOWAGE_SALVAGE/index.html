Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum foundation capital of USD 10,000.00 under Law 25 of 1995, Article 9, which may be satisfied in cash or assets of equivalent value; no statutory escrow deposit is required, but the contributed assets must be identified in the Charter or Regulations and recorded in the foundation accounts, and the stated capital must be reflected in the registered Charter.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas), Republic of Panama; official e-filing and taxpayer portal: Sede Electrónica DGI (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A foreign Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must first register its foundation charter with the Registro Público de Panamá and appoint a resident agent with a Panamanian registered address. The resident agent then files the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) application through the Sede Electrónica DGI, submitting the notarized foundation deed, public registry certificate, resolution appointing foundation officers, notarized passport copies of the founder and council members, and the Aviso de Operación obtained from the Ministry of Commerce and Industries (MICI) when the PIF directly engages in commercial maritime activities. The DGI issues a single RUC number that serves simultaneously as the income tax TIN and the ITBMS (VAT) registration identifier. Non-resident entities without a fixed establishment in Panama obtain a non-resident RUC (RUC-NE) and must designate a local fiscal representative for service of process and tax notifications; maritime towage and salvage operations additionally require registration with the Autoridad Marítima de Panamá (AMP) and, for customs purposes, enrollment in the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) exporter registry.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election in the style of the U.S. or the OECD Pillar Two rules. However, under Law 25 of 1995 the founder may elect in the foundation charter whether the PIF's beneficiary rights are irrevocable or revocable and whether the foundation is governed by trust-like asset-partitioning provisions, which informs its civil and tax characterization. For Panamanian tax purposes, an electing PIF providing eligible offshore towage and salvage services may apply the Special Maritime Services Regime established under Law 63 of 2007, administered by AMP, and may also elect the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007 for qualifying headquarters services. Pursuant to Law 254 of 2021, small enterprises with annual gross revenue not exceeding B/. 200,000 may elect the Simplified Regime (Régimen Simplificado) on the Sede Electrónica before the start of the fiscal year, substituting ordinary income tax and full ITBMS compliance with a single-rate turnover tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 36,000 (Balboas/U.S. dollars) in annual gross turnover is the historical small-taxpayer exemption threshold; under Law 254 of 2021, economic operators with annual gross revenue of up to B/. 200,000 are eligible for the Simplified Regime with reduced ITBMS compliance, while operators with annual gross revenue exceeding B/. 200,000 are mandatorily subject to full regular ITBMS registration as a registered taxable person (sujeto pasivo) regardless of legal form.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% ITBMS is available for exports of tangible goods physically removed from Panama and for export services under Article 1058-A of the Código Fiscal as developed by Law 30 of 2012. Deep-sea towage and emergency salvage services supplied to foreign-flag or internationally trading vessels are treated as zero-rated service exports when the service is deemed performed outside Panamanian territorial waters or where the beneficial use and economic consumption occurs outside Panama, and where the vessel is engaged in international navigation. The service provider must substantiate the cross-border nature via a DGI-approved service-export classification (acuse de exportación de servicios) obtained through the Sede Electrónica before invoicing at 0%; towage in Panamanian internal waters or in connection with port or Canal assistance may instead be outside the zero-rating scope.

  • Declaración de Exportación (Formulario Único de Exportación) filed with the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) through the VUCE single-window for foreign trade
  • Conocimiento de Embarque (Bill of Lading) or ocean/sea waybill evidencing carriage to a foreign port or an international voyage
  • Certificado de Remesa de Divisas (Inward Remittance Certificate) issued by the receiving Panamanian bank evidencing repatriation of export proceeds into Panama's financial system
  • Contrato de Remolque/Salvamento (towage or salvage contract), including the Lloyds Open Form (LOF) for wreck-removal and marine salvage engagements
  • Certificado de Registro de Buque (Vessel Registration Certificate) confirming the flag and international trading status of the vessel serviced
  • Factura Comercial de Exportación (commercial export invoice) with the corresponding packing list and the DGI export-stamped electronic invoice

Electronic invoicing is mandatory for registered ITBMS taxpayers pursuant to Law 254 of 2021 and the implementing DGI regulations in force (Resolution DGI-201-0246 of 2020 and subsequent amendments). Invoices must be issued through a DGI-certified electronic invoicing system using a valid AIG-issued digital certificate (X.509) and transmitted in real time to the DGI's Facturación Electrónica clearance platform via a documented web-service API (SOAP/REST) with XML schema validation. Each invoice receives a unique electronic acceptance code (código de aceptación) and a digital stamp, and must display a QR code for the ANA and DGI audit interoperability; legacy fiscal printers (impresoras fiscales) are tolerated only for certain small taxpayers during the approved transition windows.

Export zero-rating is subject to a statutory audit window of five years from the filing date. Where the DGI determines that zero-rating was improperly claimed, it issues a retroactive assessment for the omitted ITBMS plus statutory interest at the legal rate of 10% per annum from the original due date and applies fines of two to five times the tax evaded under Article 1232 of the Código Fiscal, as amended by Law 254 of 2021, with a maximum surcharge of up to 200% of the assessed tax. Fraudulent export zero-rating or VAT-refund claims exceeding B/. 30,000 constitute the criminal offense of tax fraud under Law 70 of 2019, punishable by four to ten years of imprisonment, disqualification from the exporter and e-invoicing registries, and the revocation of ANA customs clearance privileges and maritime operating licenses issued by AMP.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized: the U.S. dollar is legal tender. There are no exchange-control restrictions on current or capital account transactions. Capital and profits may be freely repatriated. Panama applies a territorial tax system, taxing only Panamanian-source income; foreign-source towage and salvage income earned by a Private Interest Foundation is generally outside Panamanian tax unless it involves Panama-source activities. Remittances of profits require documentary support but do not require exchange-control approval or forced conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules or mandatory conversion ratios apply to export proceeds. Because Panama is dollarized and has no central exchange-control bureaucracy, salvage and towage fee receivables may be retained in any currency at any licensed financial institution. Only the UAF cash/negotiable-instrument disclosure threshold (USD 10,000 or equivalent when physically imported or exported) must be observed.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no traditional central bank. Financial supervision is exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). The Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) is the AML/CFT financial intelligence unit, and Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent for the Republic.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. Foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) are not passportable into Panama and cannot act as licensed deposit-taking institutions. An offshore EMI may only serve as an auxiliary payment channel through a Panamanian intermediary bank account, but it cannot provide a standalone Panamanian treasury-clearing rail. For a Private Interest Foundation engaged in offshore towage and salvage, a fully licensed SBP-regulated commercial bank is the only robust and legally defensible clearing route.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on taxable Panama-source net income for ordinary corporate taxpayers. A Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) is not subject to corporate income tax on non-Panama-source income; it instead pays a flat annual tax of USD 350.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on distributions characterized as dividends paid out of Panama-source retained earnings of an ordinary corporation. For a Private Interest Foundation, distributions to beneficiaries are generally foundation allocations or bequests and are not characterized as dividends where the distributed assets derive from non-Panama-source income. If a PIF engages in Panama-source commercial activity and distributes those profits, the 10% dividend withholding tax may apply.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

No later than three months after the fiscal year-end. For calendar-year taxpayers, the filing deadline is 31 March of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 250,000 – Mandatory Local File and contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Study required when related-party transactions exceed USD 250,000 in a fiscal year. Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds USD 50,000,000. Country-by-Country reporting is triggered at consolidated group turnover above USD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but economic substance and effective management are required to establish Panamanian tax residence, obtain treaty benefits, and avoid substance-over-form recharacterization. A PIF deriving offshore towage and salvage income must maintain a registered office, minutes of the Foundation Council, vessel charter or salvage contracts, voyage records, bank accounts, and an actual administrative presence in Panama where treaty residence is claimed. A paper-only foundation engaged in invoice routing is vulnerable to GAAR, tax evasion classification, and AML enforcement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Omission in CIT Return

Statutory sanction: Under the Panama Tax Code and Criminal Code, evasion is punishable by imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and fines up to three times the evaded tax; the DGI may re-determine income using indirect methods and apply the 25% CIT with monthly interest surcharges.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members who sign false returns or benefit from the diverted profit are jointly and severally liable for evaded taxes and penalties; travel bans, asset seizures, civil restitution, and disqualification from serving as directors in Panama may be imposed.
AML / Money Laundering Through PIF

Statutory sanction: Law 23 of 2015 and Criminal Code Articles 254–256 impose imprisonment of 5 to 15 years, administrative fines by the Unidad de Análisis Financiero of up to USD 1,000,000 per violation, and mandatory asset freezing and confiscation.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members who knowingly accept or conceal tainted funds face criminal liability, joint civil restitution, travel restrictions, provisional embargoes, and revocation of professional licenses.
Transfer Pricing Non-Disclosure / Inadequate Documentation

Statutory sanction: The DGI may adjust Panama-source income to arm's length, apply the 25% CIT on the adjustment, and impose penalties of up to 1% of the adjusted transaction value or USD 50,000, whichever is higher; NOL benefits may be recalculated or denied.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve deficient transfer pricing documentation may be assessed personally for resulting penalties under negligent management duties; denial or revocation of the tax residence certificate may trigger cross-border collection and travel bans.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance in PIF

Statutory sanction: The DGI can disregard the PIF under substance-over-form principles, attribute the underlying towage/salvage profit to the beneficial owners, impose Panama CIT at 25%, and revoke the foundation's registration for non-payment or administrative non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the foundation veil subjects Foundation Council members and beneficial owners to civil restitution, joint liability for unpaid CIT and penalties, travel bans during fraud investigations, and imprisonment for wilful use of a sham legal vehicle.
