Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Charter capital must be at least USD 10,000 (or equivalent in balboas). Legal practice requires 25% of the capital to be paid up or definitively contributed at execution; no escrow account is mandated — capital may be satisfied by declaration of monetary or non-monetary assets of the founder, evidenced in the notarial act.

Funding & unlock path: Per SINAPROC (National Civil Protection System) Decree No. 761 of 2011, a fire-safety certification is required only for physical warehouses exceeding 1,000 m² or storing substantial combustible materials; for leased 3PL premises the operator holds the fire-certification duty. MITRADEL (Ministry of Labor and Labor Development) issues occupational hygiene and safety clearance for any local staff; hand-packing and administrative operations are subject to basic OSH standards (Executive Decree No. 314/2019). Pure drop-shipping operations with no local premises are exempt from physical inspections but remain subject to MITRADEL general worker protections.

Paid-in capital rule: Charter capital must be at least USD 10,000 (or equivalent in balboas). Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Autoridad Nacional de Ingresos Públicos (ANIP) — successor to the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) since October 2023; official portal: https://anip.gob.pa; electronic filing platform: E-TAX 4.0 (e-tax.anip.gob.pa); customs matters are administered by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA)

E-government filing portal

A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) is not automatically registered for tax purposes. It must obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) only if it engages in taxable economic activities generating Panamanian-source income or performs taxable supplies in Panama. The foundation's Resident Agent prepares a certified copy of the foundation deed (public deed registered with the Registro Público de Panamá), the nomination documents of the foundation council members, and the ITBMS/income-tax registration application, and files them through the ANIP E-TAX portal or at ANIP service windows. ANIP issues a single RUC consolidating income-tax, ITBMS, and customs-obligation status. A purely passive or foreign-source PIF (selling auto parts abroad) may lawfully remain unregistered; it should obtain a written 'non-taxpayer status' confirmation from ANIP where required by foreign counterparties or payment processors (Stripe/PayPal).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama applies a mandatory territorial-source classification; no US-style check-the-box election exists. A PIF may elect to become a regular registered taxpayer by applying for a RUC and may opt into the simplified small-taxpayer ITBMS regime if eligible by turnover. No hybrid-mismatch elections are available; Panama does not adopt OECD hybrid rules, but CFC and transfer-pricing rules (Law 254 of 2021; Law 52 of 2016) apply to offshore-controlled entities. Advanced pricing agreements and administrative rulings are available from ANIP for cross-border transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PTY 0 — no de minimis monetary threshold; any person, including a non-resident PIF, performing taxable supplies of movable goods or services within Panamanian territory must register for ITBMS as of the first taxable operation. A PIF whose auto-parts sales are entirely destined for foreign buyers and physically exported from Panama is not required to register solely for those export operations. Physical imports of goods into Panama are subject to ITBMS at the 7% rate at customs, irrespective of importer registration status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% ITBMS rate applies to: (i) tangible goods physically exported from Panama and released by ANA under an accepted export declaration, provided the PIF is registered as exporter and title and risk pass to a non-resident buyer; (ii) services rendered in Panama to non-resident recipients whose benefit is wholly realized and exploited abroad; (iii) supplies to Colon Free Zone operators, international transportation, and diplomatic missions. The zero-rating claim requires proof that the goods left Panamanian customs territory within the statutory period and that export proceeds were collected within 180 days.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduana, DUA) accepted and validated by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical departure from Panamanian customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and purchase order or written contract with the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement proving collection of export proceeds in USD (USD is legal tender in Panama) within the 180-day deadline
  • Exporter registration certificate with ANA and ANIP, plus a schedule of zero-rated export invoices supporting any ITBMS credit/refund claim

Mandatory electronic invoicing (factura electrónica) through ANIP-certified billing software or the free ANIP E-TAX portal. Every invoice must be transmitted to ANIP in near-real time for validation and issuance of a digital signature, unique security code, and QR code. In-store point-of-sale receipts may be produced via ANIP-authorized fiscal printers (impresoras fiscales) with daily settlement upload. Export operations are separately reported through the Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior (VUCE) and the ANA customs clearance system; no additional tax-stamping applies to exported goods.

ANIP and ANA may audit zero-rated export transactions for up to five years (extendable in cases of fraud). Failure to substantiate physical export or foreign-source benefit causes the transaction to be recharacterized as a domestic taxable sale, giving rise to ITBMS at 7%, monthly statutory interest, and fines of up to 100% of the omitted tax. Any ITBMS input-credit refund improperly claimed from zero-rated exports is retrospectively clawed back with a surcharge. Knowingly submitting fictitious export declarations, false DUA filings, or non-existent foreign buyers constitutes tax fraud and customs fraud, punishable under Panamanian criminal and customs statutes, with imprisonment of 2–5 years, criminal fines, and forfeiture of the goods or their value, in addition to loss of the right to export zero-rating for a compliance term.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy; the US dollar is legal tender alongside the Balboa (pegged 1:1). Both current account and capital account transactions are freely convertible. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repatriation are unrestricted, subject to documentary compliance with anti-money laundering rules, OFAC screening, and Panama's territorial income tax regime. No prior Central Bank approval is required for cross-border transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Panama operates a dollarized monetary system without exchange controls. Exporters are not subject to any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; receipts may be retained in USD or any other currency at the exporter's discretion.

Central bank supervision: No standalone central bank exists in Panama. The Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá) is the primary banking and foreign exchange regulatory authority. The National Bank of Panama (Banco Nacional de Panamá) acts as fiscal agent for the Republic.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low to Medium feasibility. Panama Law No. 42 of 2021 introduced an authorization regime for innovation service providers, including digital payment facilitators; an offshore digital EMI without a Panama license cannot lawfully conduct banking or payment services. A Private Interest Foundation may, however, utilize licensed local banks as primary settlement rails and integrate global payment gateways (Stripe, PayPal) via commercial merchant accounts. Account opening with unregulated foreign EMIs is generally discouraged and may trigger stringent KYC scrutiny.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - RUC Unique taxpayer registration number (Registro Único del Contribuyente) of the PIF. The PIF must hold an active RUC before filing; a non-resident-owned PIF uses its Panama RUC and the domicile of its resident agent. Late filing is sanctioned with fines of B/.50 to B/.5,000 under Tax Code Art. 706, plus monthly mora surcharges and compensatory interest of 1% per month on unpaid tax; failure to pay the determined tax triggers enforced collection, definitive assessment ex officio, and temporary suspension of the RUC and import/export authorizations.
Box 2 - Panamanian-source gross income Gross revenue from sales, services, or any economic activity performed or consummated within Panamanian territory. Include online retail proceeds only where goods are delivered into Panama or the sale is negotiated and consummated in Panama; Stripe/PayPal receipts from foreign customers with foreign delivery addresses are foreign-source and excluded. Late filing is sanctioned with fines of B/.50 to B/.5,000 under Tax Code Art. 706, plus monthly mora surcharges and compensatory interest of 1% per month on unpaid tax; failure to pay the determined tax triggers enforced collection, definitive assessment ex officio, and temporary suspension of the RUC and import/export authorizations.
Box 3 - Foreign-source gross income Income derived from sources outside Panama; exempt under the territorial system. Report foreign e-commerce turnover here as informational where the PIF holds Panamanian-source income; retain country-level records, logistics proofs, and payment statements of Stripe/PayPal to support the extraterritorial character. Late filing is sanctioned with fines of B/.50 to B/.5,000 under Tax Code Art. 706, plus monthly mora surcharges and compensatory interest of 1% per month on unpaid tax; failure to pay the determined tax triggers enforced collection, definitive assessment ex officio, and temporary suspension of the RUC and import/export authorizations.
Box 4 - Deductible costs and expenses Costs, expenses, and charges economically linked to Panamanian-source income, supported by invoices and accounting records. Allocate the cost of goods, freight, payment-gateway fees, and fulfillment costs against Panamanian-source revenue; foreign-source cost allocation must be clearly documented to avoid the DGI's proportional re-attribution. Late filing is sanctioned with fines of B/.50 to B/.5,000 under Tax Code Art. 706, plus monthly mora surcharges and compensatory interest of 1% per month on unpaid tax; failure to pay the determined tax triggers enforced collection, definitive assessment ex officio, and temporary suspension of the RUC and import/export authorizations.
Box 5 - Net taxable income Panamanian-source gross income minus allowable deductions and applicable loss carryforwards (maximum five years). Apply the Law 13 of 2019 progressive brackets; losses from foreign-source activities cannot be used against Panamanian-source income. Late filing is sanctioned with fines of B/.50 to B/.5,000 under Tax Code Art. 706, plus monthly mora surcharges and compensatory interest of 1% per month on unpaid tax; failure to pay the determined tax triggers enforced collection, definitive assessment ex officio, and temporary suspension of the RUC and import/export authorizations.
Box 6 - Income tax determined CIT computed at the applicable 15%-25% progressive rate. No tax arises where Box 2 is nil; a nil Form 157 is mandatory even when the PIF is fully foreign-source. Late filing is sanctioned with fines of B/.50 to B/.5,000 under Tax Code Art. 706, plus monthly mora surcharges and compensatory interest of 1% per month on unpaid tax; failure to pay the determined tax triggers enforced collection, definitive assessment ex officio, and temporary suspension of the RUC and import/export authorizations.
Box 7 - Advance payments and withholdings Monthly advance payments (Formulario 156) and any prior withholding credits. Recoverable credits require proof of remittance through the DGI system; foreign-source-only PIFs may demonstrate zero prepayment obligation. Late filing is sanctioned with fines of B/.50 to B/.5,000 under Tax Code Art. 706, plus monthly mora surcharges and compensatory interest of 1% per month on unpaid tax; failure to pay the determined tax triggers enforced collection, definitive assessment ex officio, and temporary suspension of the RUC and import/export authorizations.
Box 8 - Tax payable / credit balance Balance of CIT due after advance payments, or refundable credit balance. Payment is executed in Panamanian balboas at the collection desk of the DGI portal; non-resident PIFs must maintain a local payment channel or instruct the resident agent. Late filing is sanctioned with fines of B/.50 to B/.5,000 under Tax Code Art. 706, plus monthly mora surcharges and compensatory interest of 1% per month on unpaid tax; failure to pay the determined tax triggers enforced collection, definitive assessment ex officio, and temporary suspension of the RUC and import/export authorizations.
Line A - RUC Taxpayer identification of the PIF. Same RUC as the annual return; no monthly payment is due when the PIF has no Panamanian-source revenue. Failure to file and pay the monthly advance results in a fine of B/.50 to B/.5,000, monthly mora interest at 1%, and potential denial of the annual return's advance-payment credit; continued default can trigger DGI provisional assessment based on bank and payment-gateway records.
Line B - Gross Panamanian-source receipts of the period Gross cash and electronic payment receipts subject to the advance payment base. Exclude foreign-source card settlement amounts; maintain Stripe/PayPal merchant summaries showing card-holder country, shipping destination, and gateway fees. Failure to file and pay the monthly advance results in a fine of B/.50 to B/.5,000, monthly mora interest at 1%, and potential denial of the annual return's advance-payment credit; continued default can trigger DGI provisional assessment based on bank and payment-gateway records.
Line C - Advance payment computed Higher of the 2% statutory coefficient on gross Panamanian receipts or the coefficient determined from the preceding annual return. Where the PIF's e-commerce activity is entirely foreign-source, the computed amount is zero and no monthly form is required. Failure to file and pay the monthly advance results in a fine of B/.50 to B/.5,000, monthly mora interest at 1%, and potential denial of the annual return's advance-payment credit; continued default can trigger DGI provisional assessment based on bank and payment-gateway records.
Box 185-A - Related-party transactions threshold Aggregate value of transactions carried out during the year with related parties. Where aggregate related-party dealings exceed B/.1,000,000, the annex is mandatory; a foreign-only e-commerce operation dealing with an overseas supplier under common control must analyze the supply contract, warehouse fees, and payment-gateway charges. Failure to file Form 185 or to produce the Local File on demand triggers penalties of B/.10,000 to B/.100,000 plus the recharacterization of the transaction at market value with the 25% CIT and mora surcharges; deliberate omission is treated as tax evasion under Art. 706-A.
Box 185-B - Method selection Arm's-length method applied per transaction category. CUP is preferred for online retail of automotive parts and tuning accessories; TNMM is accepted where comparables exist; the analysis must be documented even if no Form 185 is due. Failure to file Form 185 or to produce the Local File on demand triggers penalties of B/.10,000 to B/.100,000 plus the recharacterization of the transaction at market value with the 25% CIT and mora surcharges; deliberate omission is treated as tax evasion under Art. 706-A.
Box 185-C - Intercompany agreements Reference to contracts, invoices, and comparability adjustments. The PIF must retain supplier agreements, pricing lists, freight documents, and gateway fee schedules to sustain the transaction reality of each related-party flow. Failure to file Form 185 or to produce the Local File on demand triggers penalties of B/.10,000 to B/.100,000 plus the recharacterization of the transaction at market value with the 25% CIT and mora surcharges; deliberate omission is treated as tax evasion under Art. 706-A.
