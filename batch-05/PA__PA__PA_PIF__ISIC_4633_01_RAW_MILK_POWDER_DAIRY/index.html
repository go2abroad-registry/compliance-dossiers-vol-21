Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; operational portal: Sede Electrónica DGI (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa). Customs export controls are administered by Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA).

E-government filing portal

Phase 1: Incorporate the Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) at the Panamanian Public Registry and obtain the foundation charter, certificate of registration, and legal personality. Phase 2: Obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) from DGI through the Sede Electrónica portal, submitting the foundation charter, registered agent acceptance, local registered address, and identification of the foundation council or officers. Phase 3: If any Panamanian taxable commercial activity is commenced, complete the ITBMS taxpayer registration and, when applicable, register for electronic invoicing. Non-resident foreign entities without a permanent establishment in Panama are not generally required to hold a RUC unless engaged in Panama-source activities; all customs export operations must nevertheless be processed through a licensed customs broker with his/her own RUC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: the PIF is taxed only on Panama-source income under the Panamanian territorial system. Foreign-source income from cross-border dairy commodity trading, including whole and skimmed milk powder sales to non-resident buyers, is exempt from Panamanian income tax. The PIF remains a separate legal person and, absent Panama-source income, may file an annual nil income tax return with DGI.

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime and does not classify PIFs as pass-through entities by default. Available elections under Panamanian law include: (i) voluntary ITBMS registration for exporters not otherwise required to register; (ii) election to access the simplified small taxpayer regime where annual gross income does not exceed B/.36,000; (iii) election of accounting books in US dollars/Balboas at par; and (iv) election to treat certain foreign-source investments as non-taxable remittances under the territorial income tax rules. Hybrid mismatch or economic substance elections are limited by general transfer pricing and substance requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.07%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold exempts habitual suppliers or exporters from ITBMS registration in Panama. Any person or entity making taxable transfers of movable goods or services in Panama, or exporting goods, must register for ITBMS; under the simplified small taxpayer regime an annual gross income threshold of B/.36,000 may reduce compliance burdens, but export transactions otherwise remain zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% ITBMS rate applies to exported movable goods and to offshore services supplied for use outside Panama. For raw milk powder dairy products, the goods must physically leave Panamanian customs territory, be destined for the account of a non-resident buyer, and be evidenced by the customs export declaration and transport documents. Sales to the Colon Free Zone may also be zero-rated if the goods are eventually re-exported, subject to the current ANA protocol. The seller must retain complete transactional documentation showing export delivery, economic substance, and foreign consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaracion de Mercancias de Exportacion)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificacion de Ingreso de Divisas)
  • Commercial Invoice bearing the 0% ITBMS indicator
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin for dairy commodity shipment
  • Warehouse/container receipt or SISA/port exit proof

Taxpayers issuing ITBMS invoices must use the Panamanian electronic invoicing system (Facturacion Electronica de Panama) through DGI-approved software or the DGI free portal. Invoices must be transmitted in near-real-time as structured XML/JSON files and receive a DGI authorization number; physical invoices must carry a QR code and digital stamp. Export declarations are processed through the ANA customs single-window environment, and the zero-rated export invoice must be electronically matched with the customs export declaration for fiscal clearance. Authorized electronic fiscal printers or online invoicing APIs may be required for high-volume traders, and every invoice must contain the RUC, DGI-validated series, zero-rating indicator, and product HS code for dairy goods.

DGI and ANA may jointly audit export zero-rating claims. If the required physical export documentation, transport certificates, or FX inward remittance evidence are not completed within the 180-day deadline, DGI may reverse the 0% rate and assess ITBMS at the standard 7% rate, together with statutory default interest and surcharges. Fraudulent or repeated export zero-rating claims, or the use of false export documents, also expose the taxpayer to penalties under the Codigo Fiscal and the Panamanian Customs Law, including fines up to 200% of the omited tax (B/.5,000 to B/.50,000 or more per infraction), temporary or permanent loss of the RUC, and criminal liability for trade-based money laundering, tax fraud, and smuggling.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Panama has no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, no prior approval for capital inflows or outflows, and no differential treatment between residents and non-residents. Profit remittance and foundation distributions to foreign beneficiaries are unrestricted and require no central bank permit; banks apply only standard KYC narrative purpose codes and FATF-compliant due diligence. The only practical friction is the absence of an own currency: all liquidity, correspondent settlement, and pricing risk is expressed in USD, so cash management is driven entirely by USD correspondent banking rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export receipts. All proceeds from the export of whole and skimmed milk powder may be held offshore or onshore in USD; in practice, SBP-regulated banks require the underlying trade documents (sales contract, commercial invoice, bill of lading, customs exit certificate) to classify proceeds as legitimate trade income and to comply with Law 23 of 2015, but the funds themselves are freely usable.

Central bank supervision: N/A – Panama maintains no central bank of issuance. The Republic is fully dollarized; the US Dollar (USD) is legal tender and the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) exists only as a subsidiary coin unit at a 1:1 fixed parity. Monetary and financial supervision is exercised by the Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá, SBP) under Decree Law 2 of 2008 and Law 23 of 2015 (AML/CFT). The Banco Nacional de Panamá (state-owned) operates the national clearing house and acts as fiscal agent. The Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) performs residual financial policy functions. There is no FX control authority because no exchange control regime exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium – Limited to ancillary operations. Panama's Law 3 of 2023 (Financial Innovation Law) and SBP AML guidelines permit licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) to serve commercial clients; however, offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, WISE-type platforms, UK/European EMI passporting) do not hold a Panamanian banking license and cannot lawfully provide principal account services to a Panama PIF without SBP authorization or a locally licensed partner bank. Legal constraint: an unlicensed offshore EMI soliciting deposits from a Panamanian foundation may violate SBP Circular SBP-G-2017-002 on advertising and the practice of banking. Recommended structure: use a Tier-1 local bank as the treasury anchor and a single licensed local EMI (or a regulated international gateway via a licensed correspondent) only for high-frequency, low-value expense flows. Feasibility rating: Medium, subject to a local agent and a written legal opinion on the EMI's licensing status.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque UBO structure – foundation beneficiaries not disclosed in public registry, frequent red flag under Law 23 of 2015 CDD rules.

Root cause: Opaque UBO structure – foundation beneficiaries not disclosed in public registry, frequent red flag under Law 23 of 2015 CDD rules.

Defense evidence: Notarized extract of the Foundation Charter (Acta Fundacional) and Regulations (Reglamento) naming the foundation council and benefit holders; sworn declaration of source of funds and source of wealth for the initial endowment; Panamanian attorney's legal opinion mapping the UBO chain; certificate of good standing from the Public Registry (Registro Público); current passports of founder, council members, and any protector; apostilled background reference letters from home-country banks.
Rapid cross-border trading with no physical trace – high invoice counts for dairy commodities routed through multiple jurisdictions without title documents.

Root cause: Rapid cross-border trading with no physical trace – high invoice counts for dairy commodities routed through multiple jurisdictions without title documents.

Defense evidence: Complete trade dossier: signed sales/purchase contracts, commercial invoices, bills of lading, packing lists, certificates of origin (e.g., EU/New Zealand/USDA export certificates), SPS health certificates, warehouse receipts, freight invoices, and customs exit/entry declarations matching invoice value and shipping dates.
Large round-number inbound transfers or structured deposits inconsistent with commodity trade volumes.

Root cause: Large round-number inbound transfers or structured deposits inconsistent with commodity trade volumes.

Defense evidence: Audited or reviewed financial statements of the PIF; payment schedule matched to contract delivery milestones; board/council resolutions authorizing each treasury transaction; bank reference letters from the previous correspondent bank covering 12 months; narrative explanation of the commercial cycle from purchase order to final customer payment.
Correspondent bank exposure to high-risk or sanctioned corridors (e.g., payments routed via OFAC/UNSC-listed jurisdictions).

Root cause: Correspondent bank exposure to high-risk or sanctioned corridors (e.g., payments routed via OFAC/UNSC-listed jurisdictions).

Defense evidence: Country-by-country transaction matrix with commercial justification for each corridor; end-beneficiary declarations (KYC certificates from customers); shipping company verification of no transshipment through sanctioned ports; compliance undertaking signed by the foundation council to refrain from restricted corridors; periodic sanctions screening reports from a reputable screening provider.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for the initial foundation endowment and subsequent capital injections: (1) Priority is a direct SWIFT MT103 from the founder's home-country bank to the PIF's USD account at the Panamanian Tier-1 bank; field 70 (Remittance Information) must state 'CAPITAL APORTADO – PRIVATE INTEREST FOUNDATION – ISIC 4633 DAIRY TRADING'; field 50K must show the founder's name, and field 57A must identify the Panamanian beneficiary bank. (2) If routed through a US correspondent intermediary (e.g., Citibank NA, BNY Mellon), the MT202 COV instruction must match the MT103 mirror message. (3) Verification bundle: notarized resolution of the foundation council approving the capital contribution; source-of-funds declaration; signed subscription/donation agreement; bank remittance certificate from the sending bank; PIF Non-Profit or Private Interest Foundation registration certificate; SBP has no capital import authorization to grant, so no government permit is required. (4) For trade-related pre-finance, a commercial purpose code in field 70 is added, e.g., 'ADVANCE PAYMENT AGAINST PROFORMA INVOICE'.Two rails: (A) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments below USD 250,000 or for stop-gap settlements; standard execution via Banco General, Banistmo, or Global Bank with same-day USD clearing through US correspondents; narrative field must carry the invoice number and ISIC 4633 dairy commodity description. (B) Commercial documentary Letters of Credit (L/C) under UCP 600 for bulk whole and skimmed milk powder purchases exceeding USD 250,000 – issuance via MT700, amendments via MT707, and negotiation under MT740/MT752; the Panamanian bank is the issuing or confirming bank as required; the L/C must be irrevocable and payable in USD at sight or 90-day tenor. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on Panama-source net taxable income of legal entities and private interest foundations engaged in local commercial activity; territorial tax system applies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding on dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders; 5% on dividends paid to resident natural persons from profits already subject to corporate income tax; 10% on distributions from profits that were not subject to Panama corporate income tax. PIF capital distributions to beneficiaries are generally outside the corporate dividend regime unless classified as economic benefits from Panama-source commercial profits.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File are required when aggregate Panama-source gross revenues exceed USD 3,000,000 in a fiscal year. A contemporaneous transfer pricing study is expected for all related-party cross-border transactions involving the purchase or sale of whole and skimmed milk powder over USD 1,000,000 per annum.

Economic substance scope: Panama Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to PIFs and corporations conducting 'relevant activities', which include distribution, agricultural commodity trading, shipping, holding, and financial services. A PIF engaged in milk powder trading must demonstrate: (a) its management and board meetings are held physically in Panama; (b) it maintains a tangible office in Panama with exclusive use; (c) it employs an adequate number of qualified full-time employees, at least one senior trader and two support staff; (d) its annual operating expenditure in Panama is proportional to the gross trading volume; and (e) it files the annual Economic Substance Declaration with the DGI. Failure to satisfy these requirements will annul the territorial tax exemption and create economic employer status in Panama.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to the evaded tax plus 10% surcharge and monthly default interest at 1%; criminal prosecution under Article 853 of the Fiscal Code and Law 70 of 2019 may impose imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, a fine of up to twice the evaded tax, and confiscation of the proceeds of under-reported sales.

Director personal liability: Directors and foundation council members who knowingly sign false declarations become jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax. Courts may issue travel bans, asset freezes, and dissolution orders; a criminal conviction results in imprisonment and a permanent director disqualification.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Non-filing of the Economic Substance Declaration carries fines up to USD 75,000 or 1% of gross turnover, whichever is higher; repeated non-compliance results in the foundation's registration being suspended and eventually struck off the Public Registry, causing automatic loss of the territorial tax exemption.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members cease to benefit from the limited liability shield and become personally responsible for the entire unpaid corporate tax liability, third-party debts, and employee social security obligations if the foundation continues operations without substance.
AML / Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Money laundering penalties range from 5 to 12 years' imprisonment for responsible individuals, plus confiscation of the full transaction amount and monetary fines of not less than USD 1 million for covered intermediaries. The PIF's banking license can be cancelled and the proceeds repatriated to the affected jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Directors and the compliance officer (CUAT) may be criminally prosecuted even if the entity itself is a foundation; personal assets can be seized under civil asset forfeiture, and professional bans extend to all regulated financial activities.
Dividend Distribution Abuse

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation as a constructive dividend: additional corporate income tax at 25% on the full distribution, a 10% dividend withholding tax payable by the PIF as withholding agent, interest from the original filing date, and a 50% penalty for deliberate concealment of taxable profit distributions.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members act as withholding agents under Article 781 of the Fiscal Code; on failure to withhold and remit the dividend tax, they become personally liable for the full unpaid withholding amount, subject to civil restitution orders and a travel ban pending payment.
