Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá – Sección de Fundaciones de Interés Privado (Public Registry of Panama, Private Interest Foundations Section).

Governing statute

Ley No. 25 de 12 de junio de 1995 (Ley sobre Fundaciones de Interés Privado), as amended by Ley No. 54 de 2004; principal provisions on constitution and purpose (Articles 1–7), minimum initial capital (Article 8), Foundation Council and Protector (Articles 13–18), and registration at the Public Registry (Articles 19–26).

Formation / constitutional filings

Acta Constitutiva (Foundation Charter) executed as a private instrument authorized by a licensed Panamanian attorney or as a notarized public deed, submitted together with the foundation registration form (Formulario de Inscripción) and the registered agent's acceptance letter.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum stated initial capital of US$10,000.00 (B/.10,000.00) under Article 8 of Law 25 of 1995, satisfiable with cash, assets, credits, or intellectual property rights; no escrow account or on-shore bank deposit is required at formation, and the capital is evidenced by a capital inventory (Inventario de Bienes) retained at the registered office.

Funding & unlock path: Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá – fire prevention certificate covering extinguishers, emergency exits, alarm systems, evacuation plan, and annual inspection; Municipio de Panamá – building occupancy permit confirming seismic and structural load compliance and approved office use; MINSA – Aviso Sanitario de Funcionamiento for non-health commercial premises; MITRADEL – registration under the national occupational health and safety regulations (COPANIT), including workplace risk assessment, industrial hygiene, and formation of a joint health and safety committee where employee thresholds are met.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum stated initial capital of US$10,000.00 (B/.10,000.00) under Article 8 of Law 25 of 1995, satisfiable with cash, assets, credits, or intellectual property rights; no escrow account or on-shore bank deposit is required at formation, and the capital is evidenced by a capital inventory (Inventario de Bienes) retained at the registered office.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas, Republic of Panama. Official self-service portal: https://www.dgi.gob.pa (DGI 'Portal de Trámites y Servicios').

E-government filing portal

RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) procurement for a foreign-owned PIF: (1) Register the foundation charter with the Registro Público de Panamá and maintain the mandatory Panamanian resident agent; (2) File the RUC application (Formulario de Solicitud de RUC) with DGI, attaching certified copies of the registered charter, resident-agent appointment, foundation-council minutes identifying officers, notarized identification of founders/beneficiaries or their designation mechanism, and evidence of the local address of the resident representative; (3) Obtain the RUC number and activate the DGI e-portal account, which is the precondition for ITBMS registration, electronic-invoice authorization, and service-export contract registration. Non-resident founders and beneficiaries are permissible; all official tax notices are served on the resident agent. A PIF deriving only foreign-source income must still maintain an active RUC for banking, AML, and e-invoicing compliance.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope - under the Código Fiscal, the PIF is a separate entity-level taxpayer, but only Panamanian-source income is taxable (standard 25% corporate ISR on domestic profits); foreign-source income from the EMR SaaS licence fees paid by non-resident medical clinics is outside the scope of Panamanian income tax. Law 25 of 1995 additionally exempts the foundation's organization, registration, and asset-holding operation from national taxes, provided it does not carry on active commercial operations in Panama; any domestic Panama sales of the SaaS would be subject to ISR and ITBMS. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope - under the Código Fiscal, the PIF is a separate entity-level taxpayer, but only Panamanian-source income is taxable (standard 25% corporate ISR on domestic profits); foreign-source income from the EMR SaaS licence fees paid by non-resident medical clinics is outside the scope of Panamanian income tax. Law 25 of 1995 additionally exempts the foundation's organization, registration, and asset-holding operation from national taxes, provided it does not carry on active commercial operations in Panama; any domestic Panama sales of the SaaS would be subject to ISR and ITBMS. The PIF is not a statutory pass-through entity, but is fiscally neutral in practice because distributions of foreign-source funds are not taxed in Panama.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; the functional entity-classification election occurs at formation by choosing the PIF, corporation, or trust form at the Public Registry. Available elections and filings: (i) voluntary early ITBMS registration to perfect exporter status and preserve input-credit refund rights; (ii) registration/certification of each service-export contract with the DGI before issuing 0% invoices; (iii) optional election of the taxed Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime where the PIF creates the required local substance (5% ISR instead of 25%, on qualifying Panama-source service income); (iv) small-taxpayer integral quota regime (Régimen de Cuota Integral) for domestic activities with annual gross income not exceeding B/. 200,000; and (v) informational return on hybrid arrangements under Law 254 of 2021 (BEPS Action 2), applicable where cross-border royalty or financing flows with clinic clients create hybrid mismatches. None of these elections alter the PIF's territorial tax base.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 0 - no de-minimis turnover threshold. Every person performing taxable transactions in or from Panama must register for ITBMS; an exporter of services must register to issue valid 0% invoices and to claim refunds of input ITBMS. A distinct small-taxpayer integral quota regime is available for taxpayers with annual gross income not exceeding B/. 200,000, but full-registrant status remains the operative structure for exporters seeking input-credit refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% export rate applies when cumulative conditions are met: (i) the buyer is a non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in Panama (e.g., foreign licensed medical clinics); (ii) the EMR SaaS platform is effectively used and consumed entirely outside Panama (offshore hosting, licensing, cloud support, and maintenance qualify); (iii) the service-export contract is registered with the DGI before the first invoice; (iv) the invoice is issued through the cleared e-invoicing channel at 0%; and (v) the proceeds are received through a Panamanian bank as an inward remittance. The same export character supports territorial exclusion of the corresponding service fees from Panamanian ISR.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) - filed via the DGI/VUPE single window for physical goods; for SaaS, the DGI-registered service-export contract file serves as the operative export declaration
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill for goods, or equivalent electronic delivery and usage evidence for SaaS (hosting registry, access logs, uptime and audit reports demonstrating offshore consumption)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificación de Ingreso de Divisas) issued by the receiving Panamanian bank showing payment from abroad
  • Zero-rated commercial invoice cleared through the DGI e-invoicing platform with the registered export-contract reference
  • Certificate of fiscal residence / non-resident status of the foreign client, including attestation that the client maintains no Panamanian permanent establishment

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) under the DGI's phased programme: each invoice series requires DGI authorization; invoices must be generated in the DGI XML schema with XMLDSIG digital signature and transmitted to the DGI clearance platform for synchronous validation before issuance, with QR/digital stamping on the delivered PDF or printout. Export invoices must display the 0% ITBMS rate and the registered export-contract reference. Foreign-currency billing is permitted, with ITBMS computed in Balboas/USD at the transaction-date exchange rate. Monthly ITBMS returns and export-credit refund petitions are filed electronically through the DGI portal.

Panama imposes no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline; the 180-day window is the DGI's customary documentary benchmark for export-proceeds review and refund substantiation. Unwarranted zero-rating or fictitious export contracts trigger: (i) disallowance of the 0% rate with retroactive ITBMS assessment; (ii) denial and clawback of input-credit refunds already paid; (iii) surcharges (recargos) and default interest computed from the original return due date; (iv) administrative fines of approximately 100% to 200% of the tax evaded under the Código Fiscal; (v) suspension of the RUC and electronic-invoicing authorization; and (vi) criminal prosecution for tax fraud (defraudación tributaria) against the foundation's officers for intentional falsification of export documentation. Export refunds remain subject to documentary and bank-ledger audit for up to the full five-year statute of limitations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility under Panama's dollarized regime. The U.S. dollar circulates as legal tender alongside the nationally minted balboa (PAB), which is a subsidiary unit at par 1:1. There are no exchange controls, no capital transfer restrictions, and no prior authorization requirements for inbound or outbound funds. Profit remittances by Private Interest Foundations to non-resident beneficiaries are unrestricted once the foundation satisfies its legal tax and AML obligations under Law 25 of 1995 (as amended) and Executive Decree 163 of 1996. The only practical hurdles arise from banking due diligence and the SBP's AML / CFT supervision under Law 42 of 2000, which requires banks to report unusual transactions and verify beneficial ownership.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export proceeds. Under the constitutional monetary system, all receipts—including service export revenue from the EMR SaaS platform—may be freely retained in USD, held in domestic bank accounts, or transferred abroad without central bank authorization. The lack of a consolidated exchange control regime means the 15-day rule and conversion percentages found in some Latin American jurisdictions do not exist. Exporters are nonetheless subject to commercial invoicing standards and the SBP's oversight on transaction value reporting for statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as the primary banking regulator; the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) oversees monetary policy with no autonomous central bank issuance function. The National Bank of Panama (Banco Nacional de Panamá) acts as fiscal agent of the Republic and provides banking services to the public sector but does not exercise independent monetary authority. The Decreto de Gabinete No. 98 of 1941, as amended, codifies the U.S. dollar as legal tender and establishes the Banking Law frame.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Panama does not currently maintain a statutory cross-border electronic money institution (EMI) license; fintech banking functions are restricted to licensed banks or non-bank money remitters supervised by the SBP under the Remitters' Registry (Registro de Empresas Remisoras de Fondos). Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Mercury, Wise Business, Payoneer) generally accept Panamanian entities with transparent UBO disclosure, but they may impose enhanced due diligence on Private Interest Foundations due to the inherent nominee layer. A sociedad de fundación can satisfy these platforms if it presents a full long-form foundation deed, registered nominee declarations, audited financials, and a demonstrable operational tie to the HealthTech sector.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The preferred route is a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the foundation's operational account using a transparent purpose code such as '38' (non-trade services) or '91' (business services). The sending entity must include the foundation's full legal name, foundation number assigned by the Public Registry of Panama, and the reference to the shareholder loan or capital contribution agreement. For capital injections above USD 10,000, the Panamanian bank will perform extended due diligence on the sending entity, request the underlying transaction documentation, and file a suspicious activity report if any information mismatch appears. To facilitate rapid clearing, the foundation should prepare the following bundle: certificate of good standing, latest financial statements, board resolution authorizing the capital injection, and a clear narrative of the intended use of funds in the EMR SaaS operation.For routine SaaS vendor payments and cloud infrastructure charges, the most efficient rail is the U.S. ACH and Federal Reserve system when the beneficiary is in USD; for non-USD destinations, SWIFT MT103 is the standard clearlink. Larger disbursements, such as procurement of medical-device integrations or licensing of proprietary algorithms, may require a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) issued by a Panamanian bank through a correspondent bank in the United States or EU. The LC must be tied to a commercial invoice and the Foundation Council's purchase resolution. International disbursements above USD 300,000 per day require SBP supervision and will likely trigger enhanced due diligence on the beneficiary, so the foundation should pre-clear such transactions and maintain auditable evidence of the artificial intelligence and data processing services rendered.US Dollar forward contracts against the Panamanian balboa (USD/PAB with 1:1 peg) ; though not needed for parity risk, such instruments are permissible and used to lock in cross-border settlement rates when the foundation invoices in other Latin American currencies.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on the USD/PAB peer group, mainly USD/BRL, USD/MXN and USD/COP, to hedge vendor payments and royalty flows in those markets.Cross-currency swaps in the USD/CLP or USD/PEN corridor when the foundation holds a structured debt facility or a commercial contract requiring fixed-price local-currency flows.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on net taxable income exceeding USD 200,000; 15% on the first USD 200,000

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding on dividends paid out of Panamanian-source taxable profits; a 4% Advance Dividend Tax (ADT) is prepaid quarterly and credited against the final 10% WHT.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 – Gross Revenue Total gross revenue from activities that generate Panamanian-source income, including landing fees, monthly subscriptions, and per-seat EMR software licensing derived from customers located in Panama. Only include income sourced in Panama. Foreign SaaS subscriptions (client servers or use outside Panama) should be excluded from Box 1 and reported in an explanatory statement under Box 21; do not inject foreign-source revenue into the Panamanian tax base. Late filing fine: 10% of the tax due per month or fraction of delay, capped at 50%; late payment surcharge: 1% per month plus interest (Banco Nacional interest rate); failure to file may lead to default assessment, asset seizure, and criminal liability in cases of fraudulent intent.
Box 2 – Deductions and Costs Allowable deductions: direct operating costs (cloud hosting local to Panama, employee salaries, medical-clinic licensing fees, software development costs, and administrative expenses), depreciation, and NOL carryforward claimed within 5-year limit. Deduct only expenses that are economically connected to Panamanian-source revenue. Expense allocations must be supported by contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation if affiliated-party transactions exist; foreign-source costs are not allocable to Panama-source income. Late filing fine: 10% of the tax due per month or fraction of delay, capped at 50%; late payment surcharge: 1% per month plus interest (Banco Nacional interest rate); failure to file may lead to default assessment, asset seizure, and criminal liability in cases of fraudulent intent.
Box 3 – Net Taxable Income Taxable base after deductions and loss carryforwards, applying the territorial-source limitation. Non-resident entities operating a branch in Panama must allocate taxable income under arm's-length principles and file a branch tax return; the 4% Advance Dividend Tax (ADT) is prepaid on the taxable base regardless of actual dividend distribution. Late filing fine: 10% of the tax due per month or fraction of delay, capped at 50%; late payment surcharge: 1% per month plus interest (Banco Nacional interest rate); failure to file may lead to default assessment, asset seizure, and criminal liability in cases of fraudulent intent.
Box 4 – Tax Liability Corporate income tax at progressive rates: 15% on first USD 200,000; 25% on the excess. Non-resident companies do not incur Panamanian income tax on foreign-source income; ensure that the tax computation excludes non-territorial revenue and that transfer pricing adjustments are reflected in the Panamanian-source taxable base. Late filing fine: 10% of the tax due per month or fraction of delay, capped at 50%; late payment surcharge: 1% per month plus interest (Banco Nacional interest rate); failure to file may lead to default assessment, asset seizure, and criminal liability in cases of fraudulent intent.
Box 5 – Advance Payments, Withholdings and Credits Credits for quarterly advance payments (Form 103), the 4% ADT already paid, and any withholding tax on payments credited to the company (e.g., DGI withholding on professional fees). Non-resident entities are eligible to claim creditable withholding taxes from Panamanian clients only if they comply with registration and retention requirements under the territorial law; refunds for excess ADT are possible through formal petition to DGI. Late filing fine: 10% of the tax due per month or fraction of delay, capped at 50%; late payment surcharge: 1% per month plus interest (Banco Nacional interest rate); failure to file may lead to default assessment, asset seizure, and criminal liability in cases of fraudulent intent.
Line 1 – Income Tax Instalment Base Computed as a percentage of the prior year's net income (typically 30% in April and October, 20% in July and December) or a self-assessed estimated current-year taxable income. Non-resident entities with Panamanian-source profits must compute the instalments using the prior year's Panamanian-source taxable income; foreign-source income is excluded from the base. Underpayment of quarterly instalments triggers interest and a 10% surcharge on the missed amount, plus a fine of 10% of the underpaid amount; failure to file Form 103 three consecutive times disqualifies the taxpayer from the segmented compliance program and can lead to DGI deregistration.
Line 2 – Advance Dividend Tax (ADT) 4% of the estimated net taxable income for the period, paid as an advance of the 10% dividend withholding tax; credit is applied against final dividend tax when distributions are declared. Non-resident shareholders cannot claim a refund of the ADT directly; the Panamanian company may apply the ADT credit on the final Form 101 and corporate distribution of dividends to non-residents is the moment the 10% WHT is settled. Underpayment of quarterly instalments triggers interest and a 10% surcharge on the missed amount, plus a fine of 10% of the underpaid amount; failure to file Form 103 three consecutive times disqualifies the taxpayer from the segmented compliance program and can lead to DGI deregistration.
Line 3 – Quarterly Amount Due Instalment Tax (Year 1 or prior-year base) + ADT (4% of estimated taxable income) minus any permitted credits; payment is made directly through DGI portal or enabled banks. Ensure the payment is made by the statutory date without fail; if the prior year ended with a PIF company that had zero or negative income, the estimated base is the amount derived in the current period, and declaration of zero or minimal amount must be factually supported. Underpayment of quarterly instalments triggers interest and a 10% surcharge on the missed amount, plus a fine of 10% of the underpaid amount; failure to file Form 103 three consecutive times disqualifies the taxpayer from the segmented compliance program and can lead to DGI deregistration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 3,000,000 – annual aggregate transactions with related parties exceed this threshold, triggering mandatory Local File and Master File under Law 78 of 2017 (Executive Decree 284/2018); entities below the threshold must still maintain contemporaneous evidence if controlled transactions are undertaken.

Economic substance scope: Panama Economic Substance Regulations (Law 22 of 2018, Implemented by Executive Decree 352/2019) apply to all entities (including PIFs) engaged in 'relevant activities' in Panama and benefiting from preferential or territorial tax regimes. An EMR SaaS PIF must demonstrate physical presence in Panama — an office, local staff, server/IT assets — and active management decisions executed from Panama to substantiate Panamanian-source revenue. If the PIF operates purely as a passive holding company (fiduciary clause structure) without local substance, it will be exempt from the annual sworn substance filing; but if it argues that foreign SaaS income is outside territorial scope, DGI may challenge the lack of substantive management in the year of loss or low profit and reclassify the entity as a shell, subject to 25% tax and penalties. ESR yearly declaration is mandatory for non-exempt entities; failure to file triggers a USD 10,000 fine and potential strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Stating of Panama-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Panamanian Fiscal Code (articles 800-802) criminalizes omission of taxes: imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, a fine of up to 100% of the evaded tax, plus 10% monthly surcharge to 50% maximum; DGI may impose a management liability notice and automatic sanction of 10% of the unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Any officer signing a false return or directing the under-declaration is jointly and severally liable; civil restitution of evaded taxes, travel ban (impedimento de salida), loss of visa status for non-resident directors, and in aggravated cases, pre-trial detention and disqualification from acting as director in Panama for up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Abuse of PIF Legal Personality as an Economic Substance Avoidance Vehicle

Statutory sanction: DGI and the Financial Superintendence (SUDEB) under Law 22/2018 and Decreto 352/2019 may disregard the PIF (lacking substance), recharacterize its activity as a Panamanian commercial business, and levy the full 25% corporate tax on worldwide-derived EMR SaaS revenue; additional fines up to 200% of omitted tax, strike-off of the PIF from the Registry, and recapture of the 4% ADT as ineffective.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil applies: the president, secretary, and fiduciaries are jointly liable for unpaid taxes and penalties, and can be subjected to civil exclusion, travel bans, and up to 4 years' imprisonment for unlawful underdeclaration; a conviction under Law 23/2015 AML predicates can trigger confiscation of shares and foreign-located assets.
Money Laundering and AML Non-Compliance in Capital Inflows

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under Law 23/2015 are up to USD 5 million per violation for regulated entities, and illegal concealment of funds amounts to a criminal predicate carrying a sentence of 5 to 12 years in a national penitentiary; the PIF may be dissolved for recidivous violations.

Director personal liability: Directors are presumed criminally responsible if they fail to observe KYC, registration, and record-keeping obligations; the corporate veil will not shield them from asset freezing, provisional imprisonment, or extradition from if convicted — professional licensure and international travel restrictions also result.
Illegal Dividend Distribution / Inadequate 10% Dividend Withholding

Statutory sanction: The corporation is liable to pay the full 10% WHT owed, plus a 50% surcharge for failure to withhold, and a fine of 25% of the distribution amount; DGI may also require immediate closure of bank accounts and could issue a criminal referral for tax fraud under Art. 800.

Director personal liability: A director who votes in favor of an unlawful distribution without confirming DGI payment becomes personally liable for the distribution amount and any resulting criminal fines; criminal prosecution in an aggravated case can lead to 2 to 5 years' imprisonment and a travel ban during the ongoing investigation.
