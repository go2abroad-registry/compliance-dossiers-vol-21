Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), an organ of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) of Panama; digital filing via Sede Electrónica de la DGI and the RUC/ITBMS portal (dgi.mef.gob.pa; sede.mef.gob.pa). Customs matters are administered by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) through the SIGA customs system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or a Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) engaged in taxable business must obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) before commencing activities. The applicant files the constitutional document (Foundation Charter registered in the Registro Público, or certified foreign incorporation documents with translation/legalization), designates a resident legal representative or fiscal agent, registers the commercial and business address, and obtains Municipal Aviso de Operación where applicable. Non-resident digital service suppliers that derive ITBMS-liable revenue from Panama customers must complete remote RUC registration through a Panamanian fiscal agent; no minimum capital or local equity is required for foreign-owned PIFs.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign entity or a Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) engaged in taxable business must obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) before commencing activities. The applicant files the constitutional document (Foundation Charter registered in the Registro Público, or certified foreign incorporation documents with translation/legalization), designates a resident legal representative or fiscal agent, registers the commercial and business address, and obtains Municipal Aviso de Operación where applicable. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections in the US/CFC sense. A PIF may, by its charter or founder's instructions, elect to act either as a non-commercial patrimonial foundation (ordinarily not engaged in taxable commercial transactions) or as a commercial foundation, becoming a corporate taxpayer on Panama-source business income only. The territorial system is mandatory and does not permit a worldwide-income election or pass-through/transparent treatment for income tax purposes; branch remittance and dividend withholding rules apply only when profits are distributed from Panama-source funds.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general numeric de minimis for ITBMS registration: RUC registration is mandatory for any person performing taxable transfers of goods or services, including software subscriptions and e-commerce, unless covered by the small-taxpayer simplified regime for businesses with annual gross income up to PAB 36,000 (US$ 36,000). Non-resident digital service providers must register and collect ITBMS when they supply services to consumers resident or located in Panama, regardless of total global turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (0% ITBMS) applies to the export of goods and to services provided to non-resident customers for use or consumption entirely outside Panama, including online language-learning subscriptions and automated tutoring software supplied abroad by a Panamanian PIF, provided the service is not effectively connected to Panama, payment is received from abroad in freely convertible currency, and the subscription contract, terms of use, user geolocation data, and server/delivery records evidence foreign destination.

  • SIGA electronic customs export declaration (Declaración Aduanera de Exportación)
  • Commercial invoice and signed subscription/licence agreement with the foreign customer
  • Transport document: Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for goods; for SaaS and digital services, the certified digital delivery log, IP/geolocation record, and platform in-app purchase reconciliation report
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank settlement statements evidencing foreign-source cash inflow (including Apple IAP and Google Play proceeds-net-of-commission statements)
  • DGI export registration and, where applicable, the export licence or free-sale certificate required by the destination market

Panama has phased in mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) administered by DGI. Commercial software or DGI's free electronic invoice tool must render invoices in XML/DGI format, obtain digital validation from the DGI authorization queue before the sale may be fiscally delivered, assign a fiscally valid sequential stamp/correlation, and transmit the invoice and credit/debit notes in real time. The DGI may block billing numbers and apply fines for pre-validation billing; paper invoices are permissible only in the authorized imprenta-virtual transitional regime for certain small taxpayers.

DGI audit review compares invoice flows, platform settlement reports, and customs records to verify the reality of foreign consumption; false zero-rating is recharacterized as taxable domestic service revenue, attracting 7% ITBMS, default interest, and fines ranging from 100% to 200% of the omitted tax under Código Fiscal sanctions, plus possible criminal prosecution for tax fraud and money laundering where the alleged sham export is used to launder funds; export privileges and RUC validity may be suspended pending resolution.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized: the U.S. Dollar is legal tender, and the Balboa is a purely nominal accounting unit pegged 1:1 to the USD. There is no national currency and therefore no capital-account vs current-account convertibility distinction; all transactions may be freely settled in USD, EUR, or any convertible currency. Profit remittance is unrestricted: dividends, royalties, and service fees require no exchange approval. Withholding taxes apply only on Panamanian-source income (notably the 5% dividend withholding on distributions), and beneficial-ownership transparency obligations apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no export-proceeds surrender requirement. EdTech subscription revenue and export receipts may be retained or disbursed freely in USD without central-bank clearance.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama operates without a central bank and without any monetary or exchange-control authority. Banking supervision is conducted by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) under Decree Law 2 of 1971 and Decree Law 9 of 1998; monetary statistics and balance-of-payments coordination are handled by the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-to-Low. Panama does not maintain an EU-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime. Electronic-money issuance and payment processing are confined to SBP-licensed banks and supervised payment providers under the SBP regulatory perimeter, including Acuerdo 5-2018 on payment systems and the Ley 697 de 2023 framework for financial innovation. Offshore digital EMIs can only operate as white-label payment facilitators and require a Panamanian correspondent bank or a local licensed bank account to deliver final USD settlement for a PIF; they are suitable as treasury plumbing, not as a primary custodian.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends or distributions paid out of Panama-source taxable profits; distributions of already-taxed profits or foreign-source income may be exempt if documentary evidence is maintained.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual return must be filed within three months after fiscal year-end, i.e., by March 31 for a December 31 fiscal year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Line 1: Gross Income Total gross income derived from all sources attributable to Panama (territorial source). A non-resident PIF must report only Panama-source income; foreign-source EdTech subscription revenue is excluded when sourced abroad. Late filing penalty of 10% of the tax due, plus monthly surcharges of 1.5% per month (capped at 30 months); failure to file a nil return can trigger administrative fines up to USD 1,000. Interest accrues on unpaid tax.
Line 2: Exempt and Foreign-Source Income Income specifically exempt under Panamanian law or income sourced outside Panama under territorial rules. Documentation supporting foreign-source classification must be retained, including subscriber location records and IP hosting evidence. Late filing penalty of 10% of the tax due, plus monthly surcharges of 1.5% per month (capped at 30 months); failure to file a nil return can trigger administrative fines up to USD 1,000. Interest accrues on unpaid tax.
Line 3: Deductible Costs and Expenses Expenses deductible against Panama-source gross income, subject to arm's length and territorial apportionment rules. Expenses connected to foreign-source income are not deductible against Panama-source income; proper allocation is required. Late filing penalty of 10% of the tax due, plus monthly surcharges of 1.5% per month (capped at 30 months); failure to file a nil return can trigger administrative fines up to USD 1,000. Interest accrues on unpaid tax.
Line 4: Net Taxable Income Gross taxable Panama-source income minus allowable deductible expenses. If no Panama-source activity exists, net taxable income is zero; a loss may be declared and carried forward for five years. Late filing penalty of 10% of the tax due, plus monthly surcharges of 1.5% per month (capped at 30 months); failure to file a nil return can trigger administrative fines up to USD 1,000. Interest accrues on unpaid tax.
Line 5: Tax at 25% Corporate income tax computed at the statutory 25% rate on net taxable income. Enter zero if no Panama-source taxable income; no minimum tax is imposed for inactive EdTech foundations. Late filing penalty of 10% of the tax due, plus monthly surcharges of 1.5% per month (capped at 30 months); failure to file a nil return can trigger administrative fines up to USD 1,000. Interest accrues on unpaid tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File documentation is required for multinational groups with Panama operations when annual transactions with related parties exceed USD 10 million, or when tangible/intangible transactions exceed USD 250,000 per related party. Panamanian entities with annual turnover above USD 10 million must also prepare transfer pricing documentation.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to entities, including Private Interest Foundations, that earn relevant income from activities such as distribution, service, financing, or intellectual property. An EdTech PIF must demonstrate substantial economic activity in Panama for core income-generating activities (employees, premises, management) proportionate to its income; failure to comply triggers annual penalties and potential strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to five times the evaded tax and imprisonment of the responsible parties; civil penalties include accrued interest, 50% surcharge on unpaid tax, and administrative sanctions from the DGI.

Director personal liability: Directors and foundation council members who sign false returns can be jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes, penalties, and surcharges; criminal conviction can lead to travel bans, civil restitution, and imprisonment from 2 to 5 years.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of income as Panama-source, retroactive taxation at 25% plus penalties, Economic Substance Regulation fines up to USD 250,000, possible foundation dissolution, and cancellation of the registered agent license.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the foundation's tax liabilities under look-through principles; they face travel bans, director disqualification, and civil restitution for amounts unjustly avoided.
