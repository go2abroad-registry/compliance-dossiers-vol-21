Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) — official portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa (electronic services platform 'TieneDGI' for TIN/VAT ID issuance and compliance filings)

        
E-government filing portal
Foreign entities without a permanent establishment in Panama must engage a local registered agent (agente residente) to obtain a Panama Tax Identification Number (RUC — Registro Único de Contribuyente). The agent files the pre-registration form via the DGI/TieneDGI portal, submitting: (i) certificate of incorporation or equivalent foreign registry extract (apostilled); (ii) notarized power of attorney in favor of the resident agent; (iii) proof of registered address in Panama; (iv) identification of directors/officers; and (v) a sworn declaration stating the absence of a Panama-sourced taxable base. Upon approval, the DGI issues the RUC, which simultaneously serves as the VAT ID (RUC for IVA purposes) and the withholding agent registration number if the entity will import or procure goods/services in Panama. The RUC must be renewed or validated according to DGI fiscal calendar rules and kept current for any customs operations at Panama's Colon Free Zone or Pacífico Free Zone.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — Panama operates a pure territorial tax system (source-based taxation). Income is subject to Panamanian corporate income tax (ISR) only when derived from Panama-source activities, as defined under the Territorial Tax Regime (Ley 76 de 2019, effective 1 January 2020). The PA_PIF hybrid structure (Private Interest Foundation) does not automatically generate Panama-source taxable income solely by virtue of its legal existence. Foreign-source income from the cross-border sale or procurement of battery-grade lithium carbonate (ISIC 4662) purchased from non-Panamanian suppliers and sold to non-Panamanian buyers via offshore banking channels is excluded from gross taxable income if the underlying transaction and economic activity occur outside the Republic of Panama. The PIF's passive income, capital gains, and foundation assets held outside Panama are generally outside the Panamanian tax net. Any incidental Panama-source items (e.g., income from activities carried out in Panama, sales completed within Panama, or services physically performed in Panama) are separately assessable. The PIF must also evaluate the source rule for non-resident seller procurement versus resale with final delivery outside Panama to conclusively fall outside the territorial reach. — Foreign entities without a permanent establishment in Panama must engage a local registered agent (agente residente) to obtain a Panama Tax Identification Number (RUC — Registro Único de Contribuyente). Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Territorial_Scope — Panama operates a pure territorial tax system (source-based taxation). Income is subject to Panamanian corporate income tax (ISR) only when derived from Panama-source activities, as defined under the Territorial Tax Regime (Ley 76 de 2019, effective 1 January 2020). The PA_PIF hybrid structure (Private Interest Foundation) does not automatically generate Panama-source taxable income solely by virtue of its legal existence. Foreign-source income from the cross-border sale or procurement of battery-grade lithium carbonate (ISIC 4662) purchased from non-Panamanian suppliers and sold to non-Panamanian buyers via offshore banking channels is excluded from gross taxable income if the underlying transaction and economic activity occur outside the Republic of Panama. The PIF's passive income, capital gains, and foundation assets held outside Panama are generally outside the Panamanian tax net. Any incidental Panama-source items (e.g., income from activities carried out in Panama, sales completed within Panama, or services physically performed in Panama) are separately assessable. The PIF must also evaluate the source rule for non-resident seller procurement versus resale with final delivery outside Panama to conclusively fall outside the territorial reach.

        
Available election forms
Panama does not maintain a US-style 'check-the-box' pass-through election regime. However, the following statutory elections and classifications apply to a Private Interest Foundation: (i) the PIF's organic law (Ley 25 de 1995, as amended by Ley 13 de 2017) permits the foundation council (Consejo de la Fundación) to elect a 'reserved beneficiary' structure with confidentiality, but this has no tax classification effect; (ii) under Article 15 of Ley 76 de 2019, taxpayers with territorial-source income may elect the option of crediting foreign-source losses only to the extent of net foreign-source income, subject to DGI authorization — a hybrid mismatch election is not available because Panama does not recognize hybrid entity classifications for foreign-law entities; (iii) a PIF may elect to register as a 'small taxpayer' (pequeño contribuyente) under the simplified IVA regime (Régimen Simplificado de IVA) if annual gross receipts from Panama-source activities ≤ USD 300,000, or, alternatively, elect full IVA-registered status; (iv) for customs-secured purchases, the operator may elect the optional deferred IVA payment mechanism under the customs system for imports of raw materials (including lithium carbonate) entering for re-export or transformation, subject to prior authorization by the DGI and Aduanas de Panamá; (v) the entity may elect to have a permanent establishment recognized for tax treaty purposes (none) or elect to remain a mere offshore vehicle without permanent establishment, provided no fixed place of business exists in Panama. Anti-hybrid and CFC rules are not applicable to Panamanian domestic law for PIF beneficiaries, except that beneficiaries who are resident in other jurisdictions may be subject to their home-country CFC/anti-avoidance rules.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
7%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
USD 36,000 per annum of taxable gross receipts (sales, services, and import of goods) sourced within Panama. Any importer, even below this threshold, must register for IVA if engaging in regular importation of goods into Panamanian customs territory, including temporary shipments entering under drawback or inward-processing regimes. Actual IVA registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, and voluntary registration is allowed. Free-zone operators within the Colon Free Zone, Pacífico Free Zone, or Panama-Pacific Special Economic Area are treated as exporters and are exempt from standard IVA collection on their sales to non-Panamanian buyers; such operators are subject to a special single-rate regime and the annual free-zone operating license fee, not the ordinary IVA registration.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating (0% IVA) applies to: (i) the export of tangible goods from Panamanian customs territory to a foreign destination, evidenced by exit from Panamanian jurisdiction; (ii) supplies of goods to free-zone enterprises, particularly Colon Free Zone users, that subsequently export; (iii) international freight and logistics services directly linked to exported cargo; (iv) services physically rendered abroad and used abroad. For the cross-border procurement-and-resale arbitrage structure of battery-grade lithium carbonate, the following conditions must be cumulatively satisfied: (a) the goods never enter Panama (merely routed from a third-country supplier directly to an overseas buyer, via e.g. Singapore/China to Europe or North America), in which case Panama IVA is never incurred and no zero-rating claim is required; or (b) the goods transit through Panama's free zones (e.g., Colon Free Zone or Panama-Pacifico) with the PIF acting as a free-zone dealer, which automatically confers 0% status on resale to non-Panamanian buyers, provided the operator is a licensed free-zone user; or (c) the goods are temporarily imported into Panama and subsequently re-exported under the 'Tránsito Aduanero' or 'Reexportación' regimen, supported by a customs re-export declaration. Offshore intermediaries that merely negotiate contracts and do not take title or possession in Panama are not considered exporters for IVA purposes, and their offshore commission income is outside IVA.

Panama does not currently mandate continuous real-time transaction clearance through a central fiscal server for standard domestic operations. The DGI requires issuance of physical or electronic invoices (Facturas Electrónicas) under the e-invoicing framework phased in via Resolución DGI-202-2019 and later DGI regulations, with a mandatory 'Factura Electrónica de Importación' for imported goods. The system requires digital signature certificates (Firma Electrónica) issued by the Panama Tax Authority or registered certification authority. E-invoices must be transmitted to the DGI's 'Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (SFE)' within 5 business days, and QR/code stamping is not yet a permanent national mandate for all taxpayers (piloted only for large taxpayers). For exports (including lithium carbonate outbound from Panama), a 'Soporte de Exportación' electronic document must be filed with the integrated customs system SISAM-Aduanas, referencing the bill of lading and the e-invoice. Imports of lithium carbonate into Panama for re-export or for supply to bonded warehouses require a customs entry via SISAM-Aduanas and payment of import duties (variable) and IVA (7%) at the point of import, unless covered by a suspension/drawback authorization. Exporters claim zero-rating by filing the 'Exportación Electronica' record in SFE and linking it to the DGA export declaration (Declaración de Exportación).

      
The DGI and Aduanas de Panamá apply joint audit scrutiny to zero-rated export claims. Fraudulent zero-rating or circular free-zone transit schemes (including simulated export sales, false bills of lading, 'round-tripping' of goods through Panama to artificially claim refunds, or undervaluation of lithium carbonate cargo) trigger: (i) immediate revocation of the zero-rating and reassessment of the 7% IVA on the full export invoice value, plus any import IVA forgone; (ii) a surcharge of 50% of the evaded tax if the underpayment results from intentional misrepresentation; (iii) fines ranging from USD 5,000 to USD 50,000 per false declaration, plus criminal prosecution under the Customs Criminal Law (Ley 11 de 2018) for smuggling/false documentation, with penalties of up to 5 years' imprisonment and dissolution of the commercial license; (iv) the DGI may retroactively claw back IVA refunds granted up to the statutory limitation period of three years (extended to six years for non-filed or fraudulent declarations, per Ley 76 de 2019), plus default interest of 1.5% per month on the understated tax. Free-zone users who violate the 'exports only' condition lose their special regime retroactively and are reclassified as ordinary taxpayers.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Panamanian balboa (PAB) exists only as fractional coinage at a 1:1 parity with the US dollar and is not independently convertible. The economy is fully dollarized. There are no exchange controls, no capital controls, no prior authorization requirements for foreign-currency transfers, and no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, capital, or loan repayment proceeds. Current-account and capital-account transactions are free. A Private Interest Foundation may hold USD, EUR, CNY, or other foreign currency in onshore Panamanian banks or offshore banks without limitation. The only practical requirements are documentary support of the underlying trade or investment and compliance with the SBP's anti-money-laundering rules for wire transfers above USD 10,000.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No statutory surrender or conversion obligation applies to export proceeds in Panama. Panamanian law does not require exporters to repatriate foreign-exchange earnings within a specified timeframe, nor to convert them into local currency. Export receipts may be retained in US-dollar denominated accounts at domestic banks, non-resident accounts, or foreign banks indefinitely. The lithium carbonate procurement structure may choose to keep all proceeds in USD or any freely usable currency without central-bank intervention; only general tax and corporate recordkeeping rules apply.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) — the supreme banking supervisor and de facto monetary regulator; Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the state-owned fiscal agent and central clearing bank. Panama has no traditional central bank issuing fiat currency; the legal tender framework is defined under Decree-Law No. 1 of 1967 and the Monetary Law of 1904, with the US dollar as unlimited legal tender.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Medium-High as a supplementary rail only. Panama's SBP authorized fintech entities through a regulatory innovation framework under Law No. 44 of 2021 and SBP Resolution No. 0021-2022, but digital-only EMIs are not licensed banks. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, and similar) cannot open domestic clearing accounts within Banco Nacional de Panamá and may not accept deposits from Panamanian residents absent an SBP license. The PIF, as a corporate body, may lawfully use such EMIs as a client for pre-funded outward disbursements to suppliers, but all principal operating liquidity and capital injections must pass through a tier-1 regulated Panamanian bank. Any fintech EMI used must comply with FATF Recommendation 16 for travel-rule data and should be subject to an OFAC screening protocol.

The PIF, as a corporate body, may lawfully use such EMIs as a client for pre-funded outward disbursements to suppliers, but all principal operating liquidity and capital injections must pass through a tier-1 regulated Panamanian bank.
