Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá (Panama Public Registry), Dirección General, Sección de Fundaciones de Interés Privado (Private Interest Foundations Section), Panama City.

Governing statute

Law No. 25 of June 12, 1995, as amended, on Private Interest Foundations, regulated by Executive Decree No. 1003 of December 13, 1996. Applicable sections: Articles 1-8 (formation and charter contents), Articles 9-14 (organization of the Foundation Council), and Articles 20-24 (beneficiaries and foundation rules).

Formation / constitutional filings

Acta de Fundación de Fundación de Interés Privado (Private Interest Foundation Charter), executed as a notarized private instrument before a Panamanian notary and registered with the Public Registry in certified copy.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital is required under Law No. 25 of 1995; the Foundation must have an identifiable initial patrimony or endowment contributed by the Founder (market practice: at least USD 10,000). Panama does not mandate a paid-up capital escrow, deposit certificate, or bank verification at formation.

Funding & unlock path: SINAPROC (Sistema Nacional de Protección Civil) fire-safety technical inspection and certification covering extinguishers, emergency exits, alarms, and signage; Municipality of Panama structural and occupancy clearance for the commercial unit; MITRADEL (Ministerio de Trabajo y Desarrollo Laboral) workplace risk assessment and occupational hygiene compliance; CSS (Caja de Seguro Social) employer registration for all staff.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital is required under Law No. 25 of 1995; the Foundation must have an identifiable initial patrimony or endowment contributed by the Founder (market practice: at least USD 10,000). Panama does not mandate a paid-up capital escrow, deposit certificate, or bank verification at formation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); electronic filing and registration portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa); e-invoicing authorizations issued pursuant to DGI Resolution No. 201-5360 and subsequent amendments.

E-government filing portal

A Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) already inscribed in the Registro Público must obtain a taxpayer registration number (RUC, Registro Único de Contribuyente) prior to commencing business operations. Procedure: (1) appoint a resident legal agent/abogado and register the foundation's economic-beneficial-owner declaration (Declaración Jurada de Beneficiario Final) with the DGI; (2) file the RUC application online via the Sede Electrónica using Form RUC-15, attaching the foundation charter (Escritura Pública), padrón certificate, local business lease, and board resolution authorizing the tea salon activity; (3) select the applicable tax regime: standard corporate ISR (25%) or the Pequeño Contribuyente simplified regime if expected annual gross income is between B/.36,000 and B/.200,000; (4) register for ITBMS liability and, if staff are hired, as an employer with DGI-Secretaría CSS; (5) obtain the municipal aviso de operación (business license) and integrate the DGI e-invoicing system by requesting an authorized facturador electrónico or the DGI Web-Invoice module, plus an X.509 digital signature for invoice clearance. Ordinary turnaround is 5–10 business days after presentation of the municipal license.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope / Entity_Level_Taxation. The PIF is a distinct taxable person under Art. 692 of the Código Fiscal: only Panamanian-situs income is subject to corporate ISR at 25% (or the 5%–15% progressive small-taxpayer scale if the applicable gross-receipts election is made). Foreign-source foundation assets and offshore income remain outside Panamanian tax jurisdiction. Ordinary turnaround is 5–10 business days after presentation of the municipal license.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope / Entity_Level_Taxation. The PIF is a distinct taxable person under Art. 692 of the Código Fiscal: only Panamanian-situs income is subject to corporate ISR at 25% (or the 5%–15% progressive small-taxpayer scale if the applicable gross-receipts election is made). Foreign-source foundation assets and offshore income remain outside Panamanian tax jurisdiction. The PIF is not eligible for pass-through treatment in Panama; the local tea salon turnover is attributed to and taxed at the foundation level.

Available election forms

(1) Régimen de Pequeños Contribuyentes (Law 66 of 2016 and Art. 714-A of the Fiscal Code): election for taxpayers with annual gross income not exceeding B/.200,000, replacing ordinary ISR with a simplified fixed quarterly contribution measured on gross receipts (2% on commercial sales) while ITBMS continues at 7%; (2) standard regime election with full ITBMS input-credit deduction where annual gross income exceeds B/.200,000; (3) cash-basis accounting election under Decree 170 of 1993 for qualifying small businesses; (4) depreciation and exempt-zone elections under Law 41 of 2007 for investments in less-developed provinces; (5) no 'check-the-box' equivalent exists; territorial sourcing applies mandatorily.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No universal financial exemption threshold—any person performing taxable acts on Panamanian territory (including rental of salon premises, sale of prepared tea beverages, and retail ceramic goods) must register for ITBMS. Small-taxpayer simplification available up to B/.36,000 annual gross income; mandatory e-invoicing phase-in thresholds: taxpayers with annual gross income ≥ B/.500,000 were required to issue electronic invoices from 2021, and all registered taxpayers (including small merchants) must be fully integrated into the DGI electronic invoicing framework with fiscal ticketeras or facturador electrónico modules as of December 2023.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% ITBMS applies to (a) physical exports of loose-leaf tea, tea wares, and specialty ceramic products sold to non-resident consignees provided the goods physically depart Panamanian customs territory within 180 days of the sale and are covered by an accepted DUI export declaration; and (b) services wholly rendered and economically consumed outside Panama (e.g., digital tea-culture instruction or offshore consulting activities of the foundation). Domestic in-salon prepared tea, take-away beverages, and local retail sales to residents and tourists remain subject to 7% ITBMS and may not be zero-rated.

  • DUI (Declaración Única de Aduanas) export declaration duly accepted by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas with exit stamp and proper HS tariff lines (HS 0902 for tea; HS 6912 for ceramic ware)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill indicating the foundation as shipper and a non-resident consignee, with port of discharge and precise cargo description matching the DUI
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 issued by the receiving Panamanian bank evidencing transfer of the export proceeds from the foreign buyer, and
  • Commercial invoice and packing list cross-referenced to the DUI, plus (if available) the international freight contract and marine insurance certificate proving cross-border movement

Panama imposes mandatory near-real-time electronic invoicing and fiscal clearance. The PIF must transmit XML-formatted invoices (schema v2.0, DGI annexes) to the DGI through an authorized facturador electrónico using a bidirectional HTTPS API and an X.509 digital signature. The DGI validates each invoice, assigns a unique 'expediente de comprobante' number (digital stamping), and returns a validation code; B2C point-of-sale transactions are transmitted in daily batches using an approved fiscal ticketera, with night-time synchronization. Failed schema validation, duplicate invoices, or missing QR codes trigger automatic rejection and per-invoice penalties (from USD 100 to USD 500 under DGI Resolution No. 201-3605 as amended). All export and zero-rated transactions must still be electronically reported with fiscal code 'E', including the associated DUI number.

The DGI runs biannual cross-matching audits comparing DUI export records, freight manifests, shipping-line databases, and bank inflows. Zero-rating claims that are unsupported by physical exit or international remittance within the 180-day window are per se reclassified as local taxable sales: ITBMS is assessed at 7% retroactively, plus 25% ISR understatement interest accrue at 1.5% monthly (Art. 1054, Código Fiscal). Civil penalties range from 100% to 200% of the net tax or refund claimed (Art. 741, Código Fiscal); criminal liability attaches under Art. 346-A of the Código Penal where false declaratory documents exceed USD 10,000, carrying imprisonment of 4 to 8 years and mandatory cancellation of the export registration for three consecutive fiscal years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized monetary system with no central bank issuing discretionary domestic currency and no statutory exchange controls. Both capital account and current account transactions are freely convertible, with no general restrictions on profit remittances, dividends, or capital repatriation for domestic and foreign investors. The balboa exists only as a unit of account and as coinage; all banking liabilities are U.S. dollar-denominated. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to standard corporate governance formalities and withholding tax obligations under the Territorial Taxation regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation requiring the surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion of export receipts in Panama. Exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in any offshore or domestic account, and all receipts are freely negotiable in the U.S. dollar financial system. Mandatory conversion ratios are not applicable.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF). Panama does not maintain a central bank; the U.S. dollar serves as legal tender alongside the Panamanian balboa at a fixed 1:1 parity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Panama permits digital banks and electronic payment institutions under the SBP licensing framework on a sandbox or full-branch basis, but pure offshore digital EMIs without a local physical office have limited viability. Foreign fintechs generally require incorporation, licensing, and a representative office in Panama. Existing special fintech banking licenses allow multi-service electronic money issuance, yet compliance burdens and capital requirements remain material. A fully regulated, physically present Panamanian bank account is strongly preferable for establishing liquidity rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be executed via SWIFT MT103 or equivalent U.S. dollar wire to the PIF's Panamanian bank account. Panama does not impose exchange controls; however, the remitter should clearly state the purpose code corresponding to 'Financial Assistance / Capital Contribution' or 'Foreign Direct Investment – Equity Investment' for banks' AML profiling. The Panamanian receiving bank may require a copy of the foundation resolution, UBO information, and source-of-funds documentation. Funds are cleared in Fedwire or via correspondent U.S. banking network with normal one to two business day settlement.Outbound trade settlements for specialty tea importation and cultural ceramic purchases are processed using SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments and Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) for deferred or documentary trade finance. Panamanian banks provide U.S. dollar-cleared disbursements through their correspondent global network. Where the counterparty is in a non-dollar jurisdiction, the disbursement leg may involve a correspondent conversion into local currency at prevailing market rates. Trade payments should be linked to commercial invoices, packing lists, and shipping documents to satisfy SBP anti-money-laundering and sanctions screening.Cross-currency forward contracts (USD/EUR, USD/GBP, USD/JPY)Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market tea-origin currencies such as CNY, INR, VND and IDRCross-currency swaps for tenor-matched funding in EUR, CHF, or RMBOTC options on USD/EUR and other G10 crosses, executed via a licensed international counterparty

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% on dividend distributions paid by a Panamanian PIF from profits that have borne the 25% corporate income tax. If the distributed profits originated from untaxed or exempt Panama-source earnings, the applicable withholding rate is 10%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panama's transfer pricing rules require taxpayers with consolidated Panamanian-source gross revenue exceeding B/. 3,000,000 in the prior fiscal year to prepare and maintain Local File and Master File documentation and file Form 1050-TP. In addition, aggregate related-party transactions exceeding B/. 1,500,000 trigger a materiality-based disclosure obligation. A specialty tea salon below these thresholds is not automatically required to file the TP report, but must retain a contemporaneous memorandum explaining any cross-border tea leaf procurement and management service pricing.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not yet apply a codified Economic Substance Regulation mirroring the UAE or Bermuda regimes; however, the DGI applies a substance-based anti-abuse principle under the territorial tax system and general anti-avoidance provisions. A PIF operating a specialty tea salon lounge must prove economic substance through a lease or ownership of the salon premises, licensed food-service staff, utility bills in the foundation's name, comprehensive business insurance, and daily electronic sales records. Lack of physical presence, pitch-dark decision-making outside Panama, and absent payroll or inventory evidence will cause the DGI to disregard the foundation and recharacterise its income as belonging to the foreign beneficial owner, with interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Under Panama's Código Fiscal Article 761-A and the Criminal Code, the entity is liable for a fine equal to two to five times the evaded tax, plus monthly default interest, suspension of the food-service license, and, upon conviction, imprisonment of two to five years for the natural persons responsible. The DGI may also impose an administrative penalty of 100% of the tax amount omitted from the return.

Director personal liability: Directors, the resident agent, and the treasurer who sign a false or fraudulent return are personally and solidarily liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax and penalties. Criminal prosecution attaches to the individuals, not the PIF, and includes attachment of personal assets, travel bans, and imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced upon proof of fraud.
Shell Company and Artificial Foundation Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Panama Law 42 of 2020 and registry jurisprudence, a foundation found to be a sham may be dissolved for lack of lawful purpose or false representation. The DGI may assess tax on the worldwide income of the foundation's beneficial owner if management and control are in fact exercised from Panama, and impose administrative fines up to B/. 50,000 for failing to comply with substance notification requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered resident agent are personally liable for knowingly facilitating a sham foundation. Civil orders may be issued requiring restitution of dividends, the resident agent may be suspended from practice, and foreign non-resident directors may face extradition or, if asset tracing reveals fraud within Panama, personal seizure and denial of re-entry.
AML/CFT Violations

Statutory sanction: Panama Law 23 of 2015 imposes fines on legal entities from B/. 10,000 to B/. 1,000,000 per violation, mandatory license suspension of the food service establishment, freezing of bank accounts, and criminal sanctions of imprisonment for five to twelve years for money laundering under the Criminal Code. Named reporting officers also face monetary fines and permanent disqualification.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated compliance officer are personally liable if they knowingly fail to perform KYC, fail to report suspicious transactions, or approve transfers from an unexplained cash pool. The law enables asset seizure, travel interference to prevent departure, and extradition agreements with foreign jurisdictions for laundering proceeds.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Intra-Group Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: The DGI will recharacterise the related-party transactions under the arm's-length principle, increase taxable income by the overstated expense, impose the additional 25% corporate tax on the adjustment, apply monthly interest, and add a penalty equal to 25% of the tax deficiency. Intentional manipulation may additionally expose the entity to tax evasion criminal liability.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the inflated procurement or management service contracts are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency, interest, and penalties. Under fiduciary duty principles, the foundation may file a civil claim against them; DGI can issue distraint orders against their personal bank accounts, impose travel bans during the assessment, and report them to criminal authorities for knowing participation in the false pricing.
