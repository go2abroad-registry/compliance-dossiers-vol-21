Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Under Ley 25 de 1995, the Foundation Charter must state an initial patrimony (endowment) of no less than USD 10,000 (or the equivalent in balboas). The endowment may be recorded as a contribution promise and must be traceable in the foundation's accounting records. There is no statutory escrow account requirement in Panama; however, licensed Panamanian banks may require deposit of the stated endowment as part of AML/KYC due diligence.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate (Certificado de Inspección de Seguridad Contra Incendios) issued by the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá; structural load and mechanical safety inspection by the Dirección de Ingeniería Municipal of the Municipality of Panama, including floor-load rating of refrigeration decks; occupational health clearance (Registro de Salud Ocupacional) from the Ministerio de Salud and MITRADEL covering thermal stress, entry protocols for refrigerated atmospheres, personal protective equipment, and emergency evacuation drills.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; portal: DGI Sede Electrónica (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must be duly established through the Public Registry with a registered foundation deed. For any taxable commercial activity, the PIF must then obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC) from DGI by filing the prescribed application, submitting the foundation charter, resident agent acceptance, and appointment of the foundation council. Thereafter, registration for ITBMS (indirect tax) and, if applicable, employer registration with the Social Security Fund (CSS) must be completed before operations commence. A passive PIF with no Panama-source income may hold assets without obtaining a RUC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a check-the-box or US-style pass-through election. PIFs are not eligible for transparent tax treatment; only Panama-source income is subject to corporate income tax. A PIF may elect to conduct commercial activity through a separate taxable entity if permitted by its charter, but no hybrid mismatch deduction election is available under Panamanian tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Legal entities: mandatory registration upon commencement of taxable activities with no monetary threshold. Individuals: optional small taxpayer regime for annual gross turnover up to B/. 36,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods that physically leave Panama under a valid customs export procedure provided the exporter is registered, title passes to a foreign buyer, and the export sale is supported by a legally issued commercial invoice. Services are zero-rated when performed, delivered, or effectively used outside Panama for a non-resident recipient. Exporters must present the prescribed documentation to the DGI and the National Customs Authority (ANA) to evidence the exit of goods and receipt of foreign proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduana - DUA)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Wire Confirmation (SWIFT MT103)
  • Container Seal Number and Cold-Chain Monitoring Report (for temperature-controlled goods)

All registered taxpayers are required to issue DGI-authorized electronic invoices using the Panamanian Facturación Electrónica platform. Each invoice must bear a unique Código Único de Identificación (CUI), a digital signature, and be transmitted to the DGI tax clearance server in near real-time; failure to obtain fiscal clearance invalidates the invoice for ITBMS input credit purposes.

DGI and ANA jointly audit export zero-rating claims. Missing, inconsistent, or fraudulent documentation triggers immediate ITBMS clawback at 7%, plus monthly interest and surcharges. Penalties include fines of 300% to 500% of the omitted tax, suspension or cancellation of the RUC and exporter registry status, and criminal liability for tax evasion and customs fraud. Retrospective adjustments may be levied for up to the full statute-of-limitations period under Panamanian law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama maintains full de jure and de facto convertibility on both current and capital accounts. There are no foreign exchange surrender requirements, no exchange-rate controls, and no approval gates for profit, dividend, interest, or capital repatriation. The SBP imposes mandatory reporting under AML/CFT Law 23 of 2015, requiring beneficiary disclosure and documentary substantiation of the underlying economic transaction for any remittance above bank-specific thresholds, and financial institutions exercise enhanced due diligence on high-risk income streams from offshore jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no statutory surrender, conversion, or mandatory warehousing of export proceeds. Exporters are free to retain, deploy, or remit USD funds abroad; no percentage conversion to local currency is required. Service providers and cold-storage operators must file statistical export declarations with the Panama Customs Authority for tracking purposes, and banking counterparties will expect export contracts, commercial invoices, and customs concurrent declarations when receiving large trade settlements.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) — the sole banking and financial regulatory authority; no standalone central bank exists, and the US dollar is legal tender with the Balboa as a purely notional 1:1 fractional unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Panamanian law permits fintech payment service providers only if they hold a SBP-issued license; non-licensed offshore digital EMIs cannot execute onshore clearing or act as treasury accounts. An unlicensed EMI may be used as a supplementary disbursement channel, but all settlement must be bridged through a licensed Panamanian bank or a correspondent account, and the SBP will regard offshore EMI flows as high-money-laundering-risk triggers unless full KYC, source-of-funds, and transaction-chain documentation is produced contemporaneously.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Pass-through entity with unclear beneficial ownership or a foundation council lacking local substance

Root cause: Pass-through entity with unclear beneficial ownership or a foundation council lacking local substance

Defense evidence: The original foundation deed, internal regulations, certificate of existence from the Public Registry, a legal opinion from Panamanian counsel identifying the underlying beneficial owner, and a duly completed SBP beneficial-ownership form with precise percentage attribution; the resident agent must sign a representation letter.
High-velocity funds or abnormal transaction volumes disproportionate to the cold-storage business's declared revenue

Root cause: High-velocity funds or abnormal transaction volumes disproportionate to the cold-storage business's declared revenue

Defense evidence: Audited financial statements for the prior two years, a rolling 12-month bank statement from the originator, commercial contracts with storage clients, cargo manifests, customs declarations, and a cash-flow forecast for the coming fiscal year.
Cross-border remittances to tax-haven or hard-to-trace jurisdictions (e.g., shell suppliers, third-party settlement)

Root cause: Cross-border remittances to tax-haven or hard-to-trace jurisdictions (e.g., shell suppliers, third-party settlement)

Defense evidence: Final supplier invoices, bills of lading, purchase orders, inspection certificates, and a transactional rationale narrative; the account must also have a prior pattern of legitimate trade, matched against the declared ISIC 5210 refrigerated-logistics activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be routed through an MT103 SWIFT with a clearly stated purpose code reflecting 'Capital Contribution to Private Interest Foundation' or 'Equity Injection for Refrigerated Logistics Assets'; include the foundation's name, account number, beneficiary bank, SBP institution code, and the resident agent's reference. The sending bank must retain funds-transfer records and the Panamanian bank requires the underlying capital contribution agreement in English or Spanish, the foundation deed, proof of source of funds, and a signed compliance declaration. For amounts exceeding $10,000, the bank is obligated to report under the SBP's financial-intelligence regime; larger amounts should be split into documented, manifestly genuine capital tranches to avoid AML structuring alerts.Trade disbursements for refrigeration equipment, energy equipment, and perishable-pharmaceutical supplies should be executed by SWIFT MT103 directly to the beneficiary supplier's account, or through an irrevocable confirmed documentary letter of credit (UCP600) where transport and invoice certainty is required. USD clearing should be directed via the bank's New York correspondent using CHIPS or Fedwire, using the beneficiary bank's ABA/RIB and SWIFT BIC. For recurring payroll and domestic logistics payments, use ACH direct debits via the Panamanian Automated Clearing House.
