Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá (Public Registry of Panama), General Directorate, Private Interest Foundations Registration Section (Sección de Fundaciones de Interés Privado).

Governing statute

Law No. 25 of 12 June 1995 on Private Interest Foundations (as amended), principally Articles 6-12 (creation, endowment, registration and legal personality), 18-23 (Foundation Council, officers and beneficiaries) and 30 (Protector); supplemented by the General Rules of the Public Registry and the resident-agent AML framework of Law No. 23 of 2015 and its Executive Decrees.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acta Constitutiva (Foundation Charter), executed as a notarized public deed (Escritura Pública) before a Panamanian Notary Public and inscribed with the Public Registry.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 12 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum endowment is fixed in Law No. 25 of 1995; the market-standard declared initial endowment (dotación inicial) is US$10,000. No escrow deposit or immediate paid-in proof is required at filing; the founder signs an endowment declaration and the contribution may be completed by supplementary notarial deed.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; primary e-portals: TribuNet (https://tribunet.mef.gob.pa) and Sede Electrónica de la DGI (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa); e-invoicing platform administered by DGI.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF), including the PA_PIF fleet-dispatch logistics SaaS structure, must procure a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) from the DGI. Procedure: (1) Appoint a Panamanian resident agent and, where taxable activity will occur, a fiscal representative; (2) file and register the foundation charter (notarized deed and, if used abroad, apostilled/certified translation) with the Public Registry of Panama; (3) obtain the registered official corporate documents and the resolution identifying the foundation council; (4) apply for RUC online via TribuNet or at a DGI service window, submitting foundation documents, identity of council members, stated business activity (ISIC 6201 / logistics technology), local address, and fiscal agent letter; (5) register for ITBMS in the same RUC dossier and select the monthly/quarterly filing period; (6) obtain the digital tax certificate / electronic signature for Sede Electrónica and e-invoicing. The RUC is the unified TIN/VAT ID; no separate NIT is issued to legal entities. A purely passive offshore PIF with no Panama-source transactions may remain unregistered only if it performs no Panamanian taxable activities, but commercial SaaS operations with Panamanian customers require active RUC registration before invoicing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama has no domestic 'check-the-box' or pass-through election equivalent to US Treasury Regulations §301.7701-3; the PIF is a juridical person subject to entity-level taxation on Panama-source income only and is not transparent. There is no statutory hybrid-match election under Panama fiscal law; classification effects are determined by each foreign jurisdiction. On the domestic side, a small taxpayer may opt for the simplified/accelerated regime under DGI rules, and an enterprise may elect to register under the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime or Panama Pacífico special economic-zone regime if it satisfies the applicable substance conditions, but these elections are not entity classification elections and are not available to a pure PIF absent qualifying activity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PAB 0 (no minimum turnover threshold for registration). Under the Panamanian Fiscal Code, any person habitually selling movable goods or rendering taxable services in Panama must register as an ITBMS taxpayer before engaging in the activity; the first taxable supply or importation triggers the obligation. A special simplified regime for small taxpayers may reduce compliance mechanics but does not exempt registration from the DGI/RUC system.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% ITBMS) is claimable when tangible goods are physically exported from Panama under a customs exit declaration and title/risk passes to a non-resident buyer. For offshore services (including fleet-route optimization and real-time telematics SaaS), zero-rating / tax exclusion is available when the service is rendered for the benefit of a non-resident counterparty and is effectively used, consumed, or exploited outside Panama; mere billing to a foreign entity is insufficient. The provider must be able to prove the non-resident nature of the recipient, the offshore use/benefit, and actual receipt of payment from abroad. Creditable input ITBMS attributable to zero-rated supplies may be refunded or carried forward, but export refund claims are specifically audited for substance and documentary support.

  • Validated customs export declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera or equivalent DGI/ANAM electronic filing) showing the PA_PIF/RUC as exporter and the foreign destination
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, CIM/SMGS rail or equivalent international transport document evidencing physical departure from Panamanian customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and, where applicable, packing list describing the goods/software and the non-resident buyer
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank statement showing foreign-source payment received within the repatriation window
  • Signed service agreement or SaaS master agreement demonstrating the non-resident beneficiary, offshore use/consumption, and transfer of risk for logistics/telematics services
  • DGI zero-rating declaration/export service form or certification, if and when requested by the DGI for processing an export refund or input-credit claim

Mandatory electronic invoicing applies, on a phased basis, to DGI-registered taxpayers, including SaaS and logistics-telematics operators. Invoices must be generated in the DGI-approved XML format, signed with a digital certificate issued by an authorized Panamanian certification authority, and transmitted to the DGI's Sistema de Facturación Electrónica in near real time for validation and assignment of a fiscal authorization (electronic stamping/QR code). Offline issuance is permitted only for limited pre-authorized periods, with transmission within 24 hours. The printed representation must contain the QR code, DGI authorization, RUC, and series number; paper invoices without electronic authorization are invalid for ITBMS credit and customer deductibility. The DGI may require data-exchange APIs or certified billing providers for high-volume filers; the logistics/dispatch business must integrate its Stripe Billing and invoice flows with the Panamanian e-invoicing gateway for all Panama-taxable transactions.

Export zero-rating is strictly construed. If the DGI determines that no valid export occurred, that payment was not repatriated within 180 days, or that an offshore-service classification was artificial or unsupported, it will retroactively reassess ITBMS at the standard 7% rate on the gross transaction, add monthly late-payment interest and late-filing surcharges, deny input credits and export refunds, and impose statutory fines tied to the omitted tax (which for fraudulent statements may reach or approximate 100% of the tax). False claims of export or foreign-source revenue can also trigger criminal tax-evasion exposure under Panamanian law, debarment from DGI authorized-issuer/e-invoicing status, and, where banking AML reporting is concerned, STR/FIU referral. The taxpayer bears the burden of proof and must retain the complete customs, contract, log, telematics-use, and banking record set for at least the Panamanian limitation period (extended to the applicable statute of limitations when fraud is alleged).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Panamanian balboa is legal tender only as token coinage and exists as a unit of account; the United States dollar is legal tender for all transactions and freely circulates. Panama imposes no foreign-exchange control regime, no prior approval requirement, and no capital or current account convertibility restriction. Profit remittance is therefore free. Dividends from Panama-source income may attract the 5% dividend tax unless taxed under the alternate calculation of payable taxes, while foreign-source income is outside the Panamanian territorial income tax base. No overseas remittance tax or exchange surrender requirement applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds to a central authority. Exporters may retain all foreign-currency receipts in Panamanian banks, in US dollars or other convertible currencies, and no mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline exists. Export documentation must nevertheless be maintained for customs and for validation of the territorial-source income exemption.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama maintains a fully dollarized monetary system. The Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent of the National Government and banker for public revenues; the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the prudential regulator of all banking activities. There is no national central bank issuing monetary policy or administering foreign-exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to moderate. An offshore digital EMI cannot be used as the principal treasury banking relationship because it lacks a Panama SBP banking or e-money license and does not provide locally regulated clearing or depositor protection. Panama requires any entity holding customer funds or providing payments to be authorized as a bank, finance company, or registered payment processor. Relevant EU or UK EMIs may be used as supplementary disbursement channels for small-value SaaS subscriptions, but all statutory and core liquidity flows should reside in SBP-licensed Panamanian banks.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding on dividends or distributions made from net tax-paid Panama-source profits (Fiscal Code Art. 733). Distributions of foreign-source income by a PIF to beneficiaries are outside Panama's territorial tax scope and bear 0% withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 3 months after fiscal year-end; for calendar-year PIFs, no later than March 31 of the following year. The exact operative date is assigned by the DGI 'Calendario de Vencimientos' (Z-schedule) based on the last digit of the RUC taxpayer identification number.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same statutory window as Form 1210: within 3 months after fiscal year-end (by March 31 for calendar-year taxpayers, adjusted per the DGI Z-calendar by RUC last digit).
