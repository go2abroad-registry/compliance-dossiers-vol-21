Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama maintains complete and unrestricted currency convertibility. There are no exchange control laws, no capital controls, and no restrictions on current or capital account transactions. The absence of a domestic issuance currency eliminates convertibility friction. Profit remittances, intercompany dividends, and capital contributions may be freely transferred in USD, provided that underlying banking and AML/KYC procedures are satisfied. The territorial tax system applies to operations with Panamanian-source revenue; for a Private Interest Foundation (PIF) undertaking foreign e-commerce sales, the exemption from Panamanian tax on foreign-source income is available but requires documentary proof that profits derived from the international sale of costumes and wigs are not sourced in Panama.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters and private interest foundations may retain full proceeds in USD or any other currency. There is no deadline for repatriation, no compulsory sale to a designated bank, and no obligation to convert export receipts into local legal tender. The only compliance duties relate to anti-money laundering rules: transaction records, due diligence on counterparties, and reporting of suspicious cross-border movements to the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) under SBP Rule 4-2019.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as the sole prudential banking and financial services regulator; Panama has no central bank of issue. The US dollar circulates as full legal tender, and the Panamanian balboa exists only as coins pegged 1:1 to the USD. Monetary policy is de facto determined by the US Federal Reserve. The Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as a state-owned fiscal agent but does not exercise central banking powers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury or clearing bank; Medium viability as a merchant-payment processor. Under Panamanian banking law (Decree-Law 9 of 1998 and SBP Rule 4-2019), only SBP-licensed banks and financial entities may lawfully receive deposits or provide clearing and settlement services to persons residing in Panama. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, and Payoneer do not possess SBP licenses for deposit-taking or local settlement. Using such EMIs for a Panamanian PIF's main operating account carries elevated legal and de-risking risk, including account freezes, compliance suspensions, and regulatory censure. However, online payment aggregators such as Stripe and Shopify Payments may be used as non-deposit, payment processing gateways for customer credit card transactions, provided that final treasury balances are periodically swept into an SBP-supervised commercial bank account. The PIF should ensure the processor's merchant agreement permits the underlying business model and supports cross-border settlement into a Panamanian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The recommended compliant routing for capital contributions is a SWIFT MT103 in USD from the founder's bank to the PIF's USD-denominated account at an SBP-licensed bank. The MT103 must identify the beneficiary as the exact Private Interest Foundation name, the beneficiary account number, the foundation's registered agent, and the remittance information field (field 70) must state 'Foundation Capital Contribution – [Foundation Name]'. Because Panama has no exchange control restrictions, no panamanian central bank prior authorization is required; however, the receiving bank must screen the transaction for OFAC/UN/EU sanctions and verify the originator's details. The bank's AML unit may request a Board Resolution confirming the contribution, a notarized foundation certificate of good standing issued within one month, and a sworn UBO declaration before crediting the funds. For transfers originating from a jurisdiction that is a non-cooperative tax jurisdiction or grey-listed, the bank will likely request enhanced due diligence including source-of-funds bank reference and legal certificate from the paying bank. Correspondent routing is through major US clearing institutions (JP Morgan, Bank of America) or directly via Cascadia/Credit Suisse networks, with typical USD clearing settlement within one to two business days.For trade disbursements to foreign suppliers of synthetic fabrics, thread, cosmetics-grade polypropylene for wigs, and packaging materials, the primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 direct transfers and documentary commercial letters of credit (MT700). A sight documentary credit is appropriate where the supplier requires signed commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, insurance certificate, and a third-party inspection certificate. For regular raw material purchases below USD 100,000 per lot, direct MT103 wires are faster and cheaper; but the bank's trade finance desk should be consulted because Panama is a full US-dollar clearing corridor and there is no domestic cross-border bulk ACH system. All outgoing transfer instructions must include the beneficiary's IBAN, CLABE (Mexico) or SWIFT/BIC, the expected cost-center and purchase-order reference, and explicit statement in field 70 such as 'INV[invoice number] – [PIF name] – Ref: Cosplay Supplies'. Where Chinese suppliers are paid in CNY, disbursements may be executed through an offshore CNY clearing arrangement or by converting USD in the Hong Kong offshore renminbi (CNH) market; the bank will require the underlying purchase contract to support the currency conversion and to manage anti-money laundering exposure. The PIF should maintain a consolidated payment matrix and use SWIFT GPI tracking for end-to-end transparency and settlement acceleration.Plain vanilla deliverable foreign exchange forward (FX forward) on USD/EUR and USD/CNH, with a tenor up to 12 months and underlying trade payables/receivables documentation.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) on onshore CNY (USD/CNY) executed offshore through an international bank, used in connection with Chinese supplier invoices that require USD settling on a non-CNH basis.Cross-currency swap (CCS) between USD and EUR or USD and CNH for notional hedging of long-term recurring trade obligations; the swap must be documented by an ISDA Master Agreement under English law and supported by the foundation's annual procurement forecasts.
