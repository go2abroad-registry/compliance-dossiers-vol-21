Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); online portal: SISTEMA E-TAX 2.0 (https://etax.mef.gob.pa). The DGI administers the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC), the ITBMS registry, the mandatory electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica) platform, and the taxpayer fiscal mailbox (Buzón Fiscal).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Panamanian Private Interest Foundation (PIF) engaged in online retail, the statutory procedure is: (1) incorporate the PIF before the Public Registry and appoint a resident agent (agente residente) and, where taxable local activity exists, a Panamanian-domiciled fiscal representative; (2) register with the DGI through the E-TAX 2.0 portal by submitting the foundation deed, the resident agent acceptance, a local service address, and a certified copy of the Ficha de Fundación inscribed in the Registro Público; (3) obtain the RUC and the ITBMS registration; (4) obtain a Panamanian digital signature certificate (firma digital) from the accredited certification authority to sign XML invoices; (5) request authorization to issue electronic invoices through the Factura Electrónica module; and (6) where goods are imported into Panama for delivery to Panamanian customers, register as importer of record with the Dirección Nacional de Aduanas (SIGA system). A PIF whose activities are strictly foreign-source is not required to obtain an RUC, but the e-commerce sale of oversized furniture to customers in Panama creates a mandatory registration obligation for the online seller irrespective of local physical presence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: Panama applies a territorial income tax system; a Private Interest Foundation (Law 25 of 1995, as amended) is a hybrid entity. Income derived from sources outside Panama is exempt, and assets held abroad are not subject to Panamanian income tax. However, Panama-source income realized through online retail (sales delivered to customers in Panama) is taxable at the standard corporate rate of 25% on net taxable income, and the PIF remains subject to the annual flat foundation tax of USD 300. The PIF is not automatically tax-exempt when it engages in commercial activity within the Republic of Panama.

Available election forms

Panama does not maintain a US-style check-the-box classification regime. Law 25 of 1995 permits a PIF, through a formal foundation council resolution, to elect to be governed by the tax provisions applicable to public or private corporations (sociedades anónimas) for the administration of its estate and business. A taxpayer with annual gross turnover not exceeding USD 36,000 may elect the Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente (small taxpayer regime) and adopt simplified ITBMS reporting on a quarterly basis. No hybrid-mismatch election exists under Panamanian law; cross-border planning relies on the objective territorial-source nexus rules, which the DGI applies on a transaction-by-transaction basis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de minimis turnover threshold is prescribed: any person, including a foreign online seller or a Panamanian PIF, that performs taxable supplies of goods or services which are realized, utilized, or delivered within Panamanian territory must register for ITBMS from the first taxable transaction. The optional Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente applies to annual gross taxable turnover of no more than USD 36,000, permitting quarterly payment and simplified invoicing; non-resident digital and e-commerce sellers of tangible goods to Panama consumers must register and appoint a fiscal representative regardless of turnover under DGI rules governing foreign suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% ITBMS applies to (a) the definitive sale and physical export of movable goods shipped out of Panamanian territory by the seller; (b) services rendered abroad or exclusively to non-resident persons, including international freight, logistics, and fulfilment services that are ancillary to the export of oversized furniture, provided the recipient is located outside Panama and the benefit of the service occurs abroad; and (c) goods introduced into and re-exported from free zones (zonas francas) under customs control. For the Panamanian PIF selling online to foreign customers, the 0% rate is available only where legal title transfers outside Panama and the goods physically leave Panamanian customs territory within the period stated in the export declaration; supplies delivered to customers within Panama, including delivery to Panama free-trade-zone permanent establishments, are taxable at 7%.

  • Declaración de Exportación (DEX / Formulario Aduanero Único) validated electronically through the SIGA/SISCO customs system of the Dirección Nacional de Aduanas
  • Conocimiento de Embarque (Bill of Lading) or Guía Aérea (air waybill) evidencing physical departure of the oversized goods from Panamanian territory
  • Certificado de Embarque (embarkation certificate) issued by the carrier or shipping agent
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) or bank transfer record evidencing settlement of the export proceeds in the exporter's Panamanian banking account
  • Commercial invoice and packing list cross-referenced to the zero-rated fiscal invoice (factura con tarifa 0%) issued through the E-TAX 2.0 platform
  • Certificate of origin and, if applicable, the free-zone certificate of exit for goods previously admitted to a zona franca

Mandatory nationwide e-invoicing (Factura Electrónica) is enforced through the SISTEMA E-TAX 2.0 platform. The DGI requires the electronic transmission of XML invoices for real-time validation and clearance at issuance; each invoice receives an acceptance acknowledgement (comprobante de recepción) and must display a QR security code. Taxpayers must transmit the invoice data to the DGI using the Panamanian digital signature, and the invoice number must be pre-authorized by the fiscal administration (rango de facturación). E-commerce sellers of oversized furniture must issue the electronic invoice at checkout for Panamanian customers, transmit the XML to the DGI, and retain the acceptance receipt as a condition for ITBMS credit in the purchaser's name. Imported goods accompanied by the customs declaration (DEX) are cleared through the Dirección Nacional de Aduanas SIGA platform, which is cross-referenced with the DGI fiscal database.

The DGI and the Dirección Nacional de Aduanas jointly audit export zero-rating claims through post-clearance reviews that cross-match the export declaration, the bill of lading, the carrier manifest, and the inward remittance certificate held by the exporting bank. Exporters that cannot substantiate the physical export, that overstate export values, or that fail to repatriate hard-currency proceeds within the 180-day period are liable for the ITBMS at 7% on the full invoice value, recalculated retroactively, plus annual interest at 10% (Código Fiscal Article 738-A) and a late-payment surcharge of not less than 10% of the tax due. Issuance of zero-rated invoices without genuine underlying exports constitutes tax and customs fraud, subject to fines of USD 100 to USD 5,000 per infraction, a penalty of up to three times the evaded tax, confiscation of the goods or equivalent value, and criminal prosecution under the tax-fraud provisions of the Código Penal (Article 278-B and money-laundering predicate rules in Law 23 of 2015), which may lead to imprisonment for responsible foundation council members and revocation of the PIF's operating authorization. Claimed input ITBMS credits or refunds linked to fictitious exports are clawed back with retroactive effect within the DGI's five-year limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Panama maintains a wholly dollarized, current-account and capital-account liberalized regime with no statutory exchange controls, no licensing of foreign-exchange transactions, no profit-remittance approval, and no surrender requirements. The Private Interest Foundation can receive capital injections, online-retail proceeds, BNPL settlements, and intra-group funding in USD or any freely convertible currency without prior central-bank consent. The practical hurdles are compliance-related: the foundation must establish economic substance, maintain books and records, and satisfy Law 23 of 2015 AML/KYC onboarding, including source-of-funds and source-of-wealth documentation. Dividends, beneficiary distributions, and profit remittances are not blocked by FX rules, though Panamanian-source income may be subject to territorial taxation and must be declared to the Dirección General de Ingresos.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no central-bank reporting requirement on export receipts. Export proceeds from online retail or marketplace sales in USD, EUR, CNY, or other freely convertible currencies may be retained indefinitely in domestic or offshore accounts. The sole administrative obligation is to preserve underlying trade documents, including commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading or airway bills, and customs export declarations, to satisfy Law 23 of 2015 anti-money-laundering record-keeping duties for a period of at least five years.

Central bank supervision: Panama does not operate a central bank with monetary policy or foreign-exchange issuance functions. The financial-system supervisory authority is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), established under Decree Law 9 of 1998, while the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) manages fiscal policy. The national currency, the Balboa (PAB), is token coinage at a fixed 1:1 parity with the United States dollar, which is legal tender in Panama. There is no independent central-bank exchange-rate mechanism, foreign-exchange reserve management, or central-bank control affecting a Private Interest Foundation's treasury operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (conditional). Panama has no dedicated EU-style electronic-money-institution licence; fintech payment service providers operate under the SBP financial-innovation regime or as licensed banks, and an offshore digital EMI cannot independently access Panama's domestic clearing rails without a sponsoring Panamanian bank. A compliant treasury may use an offshore EMI (for example, a global business account provider) as a payment-collection gateway for Stripe and Affirm BNPL proceeds, provided that the EMI is contractually designated as a payment processor only and that all reserve liquidity is swept daily into the foundation's tier-1 Panamanian bank account. The Panamanian bank remains the SBP-supervised anchor treasury. The PIF must submit a complete KYC packet to the EMI and the sponsoring bank, and any local payment-service arrangement must avoid commingling of platform settlements with foundation capital funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing: (1) Founder capital injections arrive by SWIFT MT103 from the founder's verified bank account to the foundation's Capital Escrow Account, with remittance information referencing the foundation deed and the character of the contribution as 'Foundation capital contribution'; Panama applies no exchange-control approval, but the receiving bank must be able to classify the transaction under Law 23 of 2015 and the narrative must not be ambiguous. (2) Retail sales proceeds from Stripe and Affirm BNPL are settled in USD directly to the foundation's Operational Account, either by ACH or SWIFT, and are matched against the PSP's payout statements and order-level transaction reports. (3) Intra-group treasury funding is supported by a written loan agreement, an interest-and-repayment schedule, and board resolutions authorising the borrowing. (4) Verification procedures require the foundation's current juridical-person certificate, council resolutions appointing signatories, and three months of historical platform settlement statements to establish expected transaction velocity. Since Panama has no central-bank statistical purpose-code regime, the SWIFT field 70 narrative must simply state 'Trade settlement' or 'Capital contribution under Panama law' in a clear, non-misleading manner.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) For high-value factory purchase orders for oversized furniture, an irrevocable documentary commercial letter of credit is issued via SWIFT MT700, usually confirmed by an international advising bank, with reimbursement through MT103 or MT202 after compliant document presentation (commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill, certificate of quality, and transport evidence). (2) For freight, insurance, port charges, and payments below the LC threshold, the foundation executes SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers directly from its USD operational account through the bank's US correspondent network (CHIPS/Fedwire), embedding the invoice number and purchase-order reference in the payment narrative. (3) Domestic outflows for customs brokerage, port disbursements, and last-mile freight clear through Panama's interbank USD settlement systems; the bank retains the supporting trade documents for the statutory five-year period. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium (conditional). Panama has no dedicated EU-style electronic-money-institution licence; fintech payment service providers operate under the SBP financial-innovation regime or as licensed banks, and an offshore digital EMI cannot independently access Panama's domestic clearing rails without a sponsoring Panamanian bank. A compliant treasury may use an offshore EMI (for example, a global business account provider) as a payment-collection gateway for Stripe and Affirm BNPL proceeds, provided that the EMI is contractually designated as a payment processor only and that all reserve liquidity is swept daily into the foundation's tier-1 Panamanian bank account. The Panamanian bank remains the SBP-supervised anchor treasury. The PIF must submit a complete KYC packet to the EMI and the sponsoring bank, and any local payment-service arrangement must avoid commingling of platform settlements with foundation capital funds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD/Balboa 2,000,000 for Local File and Formulario 113; USD/Balboa 10,000,000 for Master File

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) implemented under Law 254 of 2021 apply to Panamanian-resident legal entities, including PIFs, that carry out 'relevant activities' and earn relevant income from those activities. An online furniture retail and distribution operation managed in Panama qualifies as a Distribution and Service Centre, requiring physical business presence, qualified employees, board supervision, expenditure in Panama proportionate to the activity, and annual ESR filing. A purely passive PIF holding foreign e-commerce investments is only subject to the light reduced-substance requirement and must not hold itself out as exercising commercial activity in Panama. Failure to comply with ESR triggers administrative penalties of USD 10,000 to USD 200,000 per fiscal year and can cause the PIF's registration to be struck off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Territorial-Source Mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: DGI recharacterization of the omitted sales as Panama-source income; 25% tax on the recharacterized amount plus 100% penalty on the omitted tax, statutory recargo and interest; criminal tax evasion conviction carries fines of B/.5,000 to B/.100,000 and imprisonment of 2 to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, forbearance council members and the resident agent who sign or approve false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and penalties. Courts may order personal asset attachment, travel bans (prohibición de salida), and disqualification from acting as director of any Panamanian entity.
Shell PIF / Sham for E-commerce Operations

Statutory sanction: Registro Público may strike the foundation after repeated non-compliance; ESR penalties between B/.10,000 and B/.200,000 per year; the PIF's contracts and invoicing may be voided as a sham and the underlying economic activity taxed directly to the founder or beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate veil is pierced, the founders and council members are personally liable for the foundation's debts, including tax liabilities. Civil restitution of distributed sums and personal fines of up to B/.250,000 may be imposed; directors may face a 5-year ban from serving in Panama-registered entities.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) fines from B/.1,000 to B/.1,000,000 and suspension of the foundation's operations. Criminal money-laundering charges, if the furniture payments are linked to organized crime, carry prison terms of 5 to 12 years and confiscation of the PIF's assets.

Director personal liability: Councillors and the resident agent are personally subject to criminal indictment if they knowingly facilitate anonymous benefit transfers. Travel bans, passport surrender, pre-trial detention, and personal asset freezing are available under Panama's AML procedural rules.
Failure to Withhold on Distributions of Panama-Source Profits

Statutory sanction: DGI assesses the full withholding tax on the gross distribution as a primary liability of the PIF, plus a 50% surcharge for failure to withhold, statutory recargo and interest. The beneficiary remains jointly liable for the tax where Panama cannot collect from the foundation.

Director personal liability: The council member or administrator who authorizes the distribution is jointly and severally liable for the unwithheld amount under the responsible-third-party doctrine. Civil recovery can include bank garnishment, mortgage lien on personal property, and prohibition on leaving Panama.
Failure to Pay Annual Foundation Franchise Levy

Statutory sanction: Late payment penalties escalate from 10% of the unreported fee to a B/.300 delinquency penalty; after two uninterrupted years of non-payment the foundation's registration is cancelled and the assets may pass to a Public Registry control until regularization. Striking-off prevents the PIF from suing Stripe, payment processors or freight carriers to recover trapped funds.

Director personal liability: Directors can be deemed personally responsible for all contracts signed after the registry suspension date. They are also barred from appearing before the Registro Público, obtaining new corporate formations, and may be personally sued by creditors for fraudulent trading.
