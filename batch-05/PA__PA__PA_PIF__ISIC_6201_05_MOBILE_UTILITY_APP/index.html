Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama applies a territorial tax system for PIFs; foreign-source income derived from outside Panama is not subject to Panama income tax and no check-the-box election is offered in the U.S. sense. A PIF operating through Panama-resident activities (e.g., executing app sales through a Panama branch or receiving Panamanian-source revenue) is subject to entity-level corporate income tax at a flat 25% under the territorial regime, unless it qualifies as a small business (15%) or as a Multinational Headquarters (SEM) enterprise with alternative tax rules. A PIF may elect to be treated as a foundation with a segregated patrimony (estate) rather than a corporation, but no U.S.-style hybrid entity classification election is available. For withholding tax and FATCA/CRS purposes, a PIF is generally classified as a financial institution if it is an investment entity, or as a passive NFFE if primarily engaged in commercial digital app sales.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Taxable persons are required to register for ITBMS once they engage in taxable activities; the DGI imposes no universal numeric turnover threshold for non-resident digital suppliers other than the general obligation of any person carrying out taxable acts in Panama. To be considered a taxable person for ITBMS, a foreign supplier is required to register from the first sale when the customer is a Panama-resident consumer or when the digital platform is marketed to Panama users. For local entities, voluntary registration is permitted. There is no small-supplier exemption of general application above zero for digital B2C services after the 2016 reform.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods from Panama qualifies for ITBMS zero-rating when the seller proves the goods are physically transferred to a foreign destination. Export of services or digital products is zero-rated only when the service is effectively used or exploited in a foreign territory and the recipient is not a Panama resident; the payer must be domiciled abroad and the benefit or use must occur outside Panama. For a mobile utility app downloaded via Apple/Google app stores by foreign users, the supply is considered an export of services if the economic exploitation/performance is abroad, but the DGI may require that the Panamanian entity maintains evidence that the service was not used within Panama. Software delivered by download from a foreign platform to a Panamanian user is subject to ITBMS at 7%, and the platform remits through the VAT/simplified regime. Offshore services to multinational clients outside Panama are zero-rated if all the preconditions of Article 186(f) of the Panamanian Fiscal Code (export of services) are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación de Mercancías) via SICARGA/SIGA Panama Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (knowledge of lading covering physical export movement)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Proof of Foreign Payment / Inward Remittance Certificate from a Panama licensed bank (Certificado de Liquidación de Divisas / comprobante de ingreso de divisas)
  • Copy of the digital service contract or end-user consumption report from the app store platform
  • ITBMS Export Ledger and statutory export register (Registro de Exportaciones)

Panama has made electronic invoicing (factura electrónica) mandatory under the DGI 'Sistema de Facturación Electrónica de Panamá (SFEP)' since 2021, phased-in between 2021 and 2024. The T@X-ON platform enables the issuance of e-invoices with QR codes and digital stamps. Real-time clearance is required for generally regulated taxpayers in categories established by DGI Resolution 202-2021; the DGI requires online validation through the SFEP for most e-invoices, with the generation of a unique CUF (Código Único de Facturación) digital signature for each transaction. Payment gateways (Apple/Google) are treated as intermediaries; the Panamanian ITBMS registration must use the DGI's simplified regime for foreign digital service suppliers. Data retention of all e-invoice records is six years.

Fraudulent or unjustified export zero-rating claims by a PIF are subject to the DGI's general corrective assessment regime, with a statute of limitations of 5 years (rising to 7 years for omissions). The ITBMS under-declaration penalty is 50-100% of the evaded tax. Interest accrues at the applicable DGI rate (currently 10% per annum plus 1% monthly surcharge). The DGI may also impose fines under the Procedural Code for non-compliance with e-invoicing requirements, which range from USD 500 to USD 50,000 per invoicing irregularity, and for failure to issue validated e-invoices. Where a PIF is used to artificially claim zero-rating without substantive foreign export or with sham foreign recipients, the government may retroactively disallow the zero-rating and obtain an automatic prior lien for all unpaid taxes, and tax evasion sanctions under Panamanian Criminal Law apply where the fraudulent amount exceeds USD 10,000, carrying imprisonment and monetary penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The US Dollar (USD) is legal tender in parallel with the Balboa (PAB) at a fixed 1:1 parity, producing complete de facto dollarization. There is no capital-account or current-account exchange-control regime and no license, registration, or prior-approval requirement for foreign-currency transactions. Profit remittances, beneficiary distributions, and cross-border movement of app-store royalties are unrestricted and are subject only to transactional due diligence by the receiving bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Because USD is national legal tender, Panama imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no clearing through a central account. Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlements may remain in USD indefinitely without reporting to the SBP.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank and no foreign-exchange administration. Banking supervision is exercised exclusively by the Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá, SBP) under Legislative Decree No. 2 of 2008. AML/CFT supervision is performed by the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF) under Law No. 23 of 2015.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Panama does not issue standalone electronic money institution (EMI) licenses; payment service providers require a banking license or admission to the SBP regulatory sandbox established under the digital-economy framework (Law No. 166 of 2020). Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Payoneer cannot serve as the PIF's primary settlement account because Apple and Google require the receiving account to be titled in the exact legal name of the developer with full SWIFT metadata. The recommended architecture is a Tier-1 domestic bank account for all app-store settlements, with an optional EMI used only for small-value cross-border contractor disbursements if the EMI accepts Panama-domiciled foundations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Founder endowment is credited by SWIFT MT103 from the founder's bank to the PIF capital escrow account at Global Bank with payment reference 'CUST-PIF endowment contribution' and citation of the foundation charter; no Panamanian prior approval is required. Operating proceeds are remitted in USD by Apple (typically Apple Distribution International Ltd.) and Google (typically Google Asia Pacific Pte. Ltd. or Google Ireland Ltd.) via SWIFT MT103 or ACH to the PIF's domestic account within 45 days after the close of the App Store sales month and within approximately 15 days after the Google Play payout cycle; the settlement account must match the developer-account legal name exactly. Funds clear in USD with zero conversion because the Balboa is pegged 1:1 to the dollar; verification is achieved by reconciling developer-console payout statements with incoming bank credits.Contractor and vendor disbursements are executed via SWIFT MT103 for foreign beneficiaries and through the SBP-supervised national automated clearing house for domestic USD credits. Procurement of cloud infrastructure, advertising inventory, or hardware above USD 250,000 should be supported by an irrevocable commercial letter of credit opened at Banco General and advised through the vendor's correspondent bank.
