Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Panama PIF legislation does not require a statutory minimum paid-up capital or a bank escrow. The Foundation Charter must declare an initial endowment, customarily USD 10,000, and the founder must contribute that endowment according to the payment terms stated in the charter. A notarized declaration of receipt suffices for registry; no escrow account is mandated.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), the unitary national revenue directorate. Registration, e-filing and electronic clearance are performed via the DGI's Sede Electrónica portal (https://sedeelectronica.dgi.gob.pa; information portal: https://www.dgi.gob.pa). Ancillary authorities relevant to NVOCC exporters: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) for export declarations and customs export validation, and the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá for FX remittance certificates and bank confirmations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Execute the Foundation Charter (Acta Fundacional) before a notary and register the Private Interest Foundation at the Public Registry of Panama (Registro Público); the charter must state a minimum patrimony of B/. 10,000 (Law 25 of 1995, as amended) and appoint a Resident Agent (Agente Residente). Step 2: Pay the mandatory annual registry fee (Tasa Única de B/. 300) to the Treasury via the Registro Público to maintain juridical good standing. Step 3: Obtain the Panamanian Digital Signature (Firma Digital) from an ANCE-certified certification authority (e.g., T-Sign, Apoyo Legal) to access Sede Electrónica. Step 4: File the RUC application (Formulario Único de Inscripción) electronically via Sede Electrónica, attaching: (a) certified copy of the Foundation Certificate issued by the Registro Público; (b) resident agent acceptance and notarized power of attorney; (c) passport copies of the Foundation Council members and the beneficial owner declaration; (d) Aviso de Operación/Licencia Comercial issued by the municipal or commerce authorities if business is conducted in Panama; (e) identification of the legal representative. Step 5: DGI assigns the RUC number (legal-entity format, e.g., '1-155-XXXXXX-XXXXX') within 5–10 business days; the same registration creates the ITBMS taxpayer account. Step 6: For NVOCC operations, obtain the AMP freight forwarding/NVOCC license from the Autoridad Marítima de Panamá and the customs brokerage authorization from ANA. Step 7: Register accounting books with DGI and retain a licensed Panamanian CPA for certification of Panama-source income and annual financial statements if required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — the PIF is a distinct juridical person taxed only on Panama-source income (Fiscal Code Arts. 694–696). Foreign-source freight, chartering, brokerage and financial income are outside the Panamanian income tax base. NVOCC income from port-to-port ocean carriage originating in Panama and services used or exploited within Panama is treated as Panama-source; pure offshore brokerage between foreign shippers and consignees remains untaxed. Entity_Level_Taxation applies on Panama-source net profits at the 25% corporate ISR rate; no pass-through treatment exists. An inactive PIF with no Panama-source income is not required to file income tax returns but remains subject to the annual B/. 300 registry fee.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or standalone hybrid mismatch elections exist in Panamanian law; tax classification is statutory under the territoriality principle. Available elections and regimes: (1) Inactive/Passive Foundation election: certify no Panama-source business activity and remain dormant for ISR/ITBMS purposes; (2) Charitable-exempt election: apply to MEF for income tax exemption under Fiscal Code Art. 679 if the foundation is dedicated exclusively to charitable, educational, scientific or cultural purposes, and remove the NVOCC business scope from the charter; (3) Branch/PE profit-allocation election: operate the NVOCC activity through a registered Panamanian branch of the PIF and allocate profit under transfer pricing rules (Law 52 of 2016); (4) Dividend/profit-distribution election: capitalise vs. distribute Panama-source profits under the 10%–15% dividend withholding regime; (5) Deemed-export election: if located in a Colon Free Zone or authorised logistics park, elect ITBMS deemed-export treatment for qualifying re-invoicing transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de-minimis exemption: any entity performing taxable transfers of goods or rendering taxable services in Panama must obtain RUC/ITBMS registration. Small-taxpayer simplification: annual gross income up to B/. 36,000 qualifies for the simplified 'pequeño contribuyente' quota regime (Executive Decree 84 of 2006, as amended). Mandatory e-invoicing threshold: annual gross income above B/. 250,000 obligates full electronic issuance (DGI Resolution 202-13894 of 2020, as amended). DGI may designate a taxpayer as Gran Contribuyente based on turnover or fiscal relevance, imposing monthly filing and specialized payment obligations. An NVOCC invoicing outbound ocean freight is structurally engaged in taxable activities; recommended practice is to register for ITBMS from the date the RUC is granted regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% ITBMS) applies to: (i) physical exports of goods accompanied by an ANA export declaration; (ii) services 'used or exploited abroad' under Fiscal Code Art. 1057-V and DGI guidelines, covering international maritime freight, ocean freight forwarding and NVOCC services rendered to non-resident principals for cargo moving between Panamanian ports and foreign ports or entirely between foreign points; (iii) services to vessels operating in international traffic, including container handling invoiced as part of the international freight contract; (iv) deemed exports for services provided to Colon Free Zone operators with prior ANA authorisation. To qualify, the NVOCC must: (a) bill freight and forwarding charges in foreign currency to a non-resident counterparty; (b) evidence physical movement of containers out of Panama through an ocean Bill of Lading showing a Panamanian load port and foreign discharge port; (c) demonstrate that the beneficial use of the service occurs abroad, e.g., destination charges, foreign agency fees, or port-to-port ocean carriage. Services considered domestic and standard-rated at 7% include local drayage between the Panamanian port and the importer's warehouse, separately invoiced terminal handling, warehousing within Panama, and inbound freight collection services rendered to Panamanian importers. Mixed contracts must state an explicit cost-allocation schedule.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduana – Exportación) issued by Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA), showing port of loading in Panama and port of discharge abroad for goods covered by the freight contract
  • Bill of Lading – Ocean or Master B/L (NVOCC House B/L plus underlying Carrier Master B/L when the PIF issues the HBL) evidencing cargo movement and freight payable by a non-resident shipper, consignee, or the foreign NVOCC principal
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Remesa Bancaria) issued by a Panamanian licensed bank evidencing collection of export proceeds within the statutory period; Swift Wire or ACH confirmation must name the NVOCC as beneficiary and the foreign client as remitter
  • Commercial invoice and executed ocean freight forwarding / slot charter contract between the PIF and the non-resident principal, including Incoterms, freight split, and commission structure
  • ITBMS refund application and supporting schedules (Solicitud de Devolución de Créditos Fiscales) filed via Sede Electrónica when input VAT credits are claimed on export costs
  • Proof of valid AMP NVOCC license and ANA customs brokerage authorization for the operating entity

Mandatory. Panama's Facturación Electrónica de Panamá regime requires all Grandes Contribuyentes to issue XML/XAdES invoices through DGI Sede Electrónica or certified API and obtain the DGI 'Código de Autorización' before issuance (real-time pre-clearance). The authorisation QR code must appear on the issued invoice. Under phased DGI Resolution 202-13894 of 2020 and Resolution 201-10241 of 2023, taxpayers with annual turnover above B/. 250,000 migrated to full e-invoicing during 2023–2024; all ITBMS taxpayers, including small registrants, must adopt by the 2025 deadline. Monthly ITBMS declarations (Form 430) are filed through the same platform; export zero-rated invoices must be flagged with the export tariff code. Non-compliant issuers face per-invoice fines of approximately B/. 50–B/. 500, denial of input VAT credit claims, and elevated fiscal risk ratings.

DGI's fiscalization directorate, jointly with ANA, audits zero-rated exporters on a documentary and bank-ledger basis. Refund denial triggers include: (i) absence of FX remittance within 180 days from the bill-of-lading date; (ii) cancelled or no-show customs export declarations; (iii) invoices issued to related offshore entities without economic substance, resulting in GAAR or transfer-pricing recharacterization under Law 52 of 2016; (iv) evidence that cargo never left Panamanian waters. If zero-rating is rejected, DGI reissues the transaction as domestic 7% ITBMS and levies retroactive interest at 1.5% per month (approximately 18% annualized), late-payment surcharges, and fines of 50% to 200% of the omitted tax (Fiscal Code Art. 760). Deliberate fraud triggers criminal tax evasion prosecution, punishable by 2 to 5 years of imprisonment under the Panamanian Criminal Code as amended by Law 38 of 2013, administrative cancellation of the RUC, the AMP NVOCC licence, and AEO status, plus disqualification of resident agents. Taxpayers may appeal through the Recurso de Apelación before the DGI and the Tribunal Administrativo Tributario, but must first pay 50% of the assessed amount or post a bond.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current- and capital-account convertibility with zero exchange controls. Because Panama is fully dollarized, there are no capital-repatriation approvals, no profit-remittance hurdles, no currency fragmentation, and no restrictions on non-resident ownership or repatriation of foundation assets. Panama's territorial tax regime taxes only Panama-source income; non-resident founders and beneficiaries may freely receive PIF dividends or liquidation proceeds, although distributions from Panama-source profits attract a 10% dividend withholding tax under the general regime (reduced rates apply under special regimes). CRS/FATCA reporting does not restrict convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Panama imposes no export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, repatriation timeline, or foreign-currency retention ratio for NVOCC freight receipts or any other export/transport earnings. Foreign-currency proceeds may be retained or converted at freely quoted commercial bank rates; the only applicable constraints are AML record-keeping and retention of underlying transport documents for five years under Law 23 of 2015.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), created by Decree Law No. 2 of 2008, supervises licensed banks and acts as the de facto foreign-exchange and payments regulator; the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) manages sovereign finance. The US Dollar is legal tender per the Currency Law of 1904 (as codified), so monetary policy is effectively set by the US Federal Reserve and all bank clearing is USD-denominated; the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) performs AML/CFT surveillance under Law 23 of 2015.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium as a primary treasury rail; Medium only as a complementary collection switch. Panama's Law No. 71 of 2019 Fintech Hub (SBP sandbox) does not create a general electronic-money-institution (EMI) license; non-resident offshore EMIs cannot obtain a Panama master account without an SBP-licensed correspondent, and almost all global EMIs classify NVOCC/freight forwarding as high-risk under their acceptable-use policies due to trade-based money-laundering exposure. The PIF legal form adds further friction because foundations are registered in the Public Registry rather than the Mercantile Registry, which many foreign EMIs do not recognize. Recommended architecture: principal USD clearing at a Tier-1 SBP bank plus a niche shipping-focused remittance fintech limited to low-value agent payouts; never route high-value freight settlement through an unregulated EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the PIF's initial endowment (minimum USD 10,000 under Law 25 of 1995) and later working-capital injections is a SWIFT MT103 (with a separate MT202 cover for high-value transfers) from the founder's or investing entity's account to the PIF's Capital Escrow / Operational Account at the selected Panama bank. Field 70 should state: 'PIF ENDOWMENT CAPITAL - LAW 25 DE 1995 - PA_PIF - founder reference'; working-capital loans must reference 'NVOCC WORKING CAPITAL LOAN' and be supported by a signed promissory note. Verification documents: apostilled founding charter, council resolution opening the account, source-of-funds declaration, and originator bank reference; no exchange approval or tax-clearance certificate is required because Panama has no capital controls. Freight receivables arrive by wire or ACH Direct and are automatically reconciled to the HBL/invoice number under Law 23 of 2015 record-retention rules.Primary rail is SWIFT MT103 for carrier, port, bunker, and agent payments; SBP-licensed banks clear USD through US correspondents (JPMorgan, Citibank), and field 70 must carry the HBL/MBL and commercial invoice reference for pre-payment sanctions screening. High-value staged payments (charter hire, stevedoring contracts) are settled by Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700 issued by the Panama bank, confirmed at the beneficiary's bank). Domestic port fees, ACP canal transits, payroll, and low-value supplier payments use ACH Direct and intra-bank sweeps, with a next-day sweep between the Operational and Capital Escrow Accounts to preserve the PIF's capital encumbrance.OTC FX Forward (Deliverable) — tenor-matched USD/EUR, USD/CNY, and USD/COP hedging executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with an SBP-licensed counterpartyNon-Deliverable Forward (NDF) — for emerging-market currencies with offshore restrictions (BRL, COP, ARS) under which only USD differentials are settled at maturityCross-Currency Swap / Short-dated FX Swap — conversion of the freight dollar float into EUR/CNY for scheduled Asian and Mediterranean agent disbursements, plus rollover swaps for intra-month liquidity smoothing

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Medium as a primary treasury rail; Medium only as a complementary collection switch. Panama's Law No. 71 of 2019 Fintech Hub (SBP sandbox) does not create a general electronic-money-institution (EMI) license; non-resident offshore EMIs cannot obtain a Panama master account without an SBP-licensed correspondent, and almost all global EMIs classify NVOCC/freight forwarding as high-risk under their acceptable-use policies due to trade-based money-laundering exposure. The PIF legal form adds further friction because foundations are registered in the Public Registry rather than the Mercantile Registry, which many foreign EMIs do not recognize. Recommended architecture: principal USD clearing at a Tier-1 SBP bank plus a niche shipping-focused remittance fintech limited to low-value agent payouts; never route high-value freight settlement through an unregulated EMI.
