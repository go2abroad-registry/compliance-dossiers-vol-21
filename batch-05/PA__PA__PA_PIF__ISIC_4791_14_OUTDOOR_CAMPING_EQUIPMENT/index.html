Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Article 5 of Law 25/1995 requires an initial endowment of no less than USD 10,000 (B/.10,000.00) or its equivalent in another currency, which may be contributed in cash, securities, or in-kind assets at the date of constitution and must be formally attested to the Resident Agent; Panamanian law does not mandate an escrow or blocked account for this endowment. The annual foundation tax of USD 300 (Law 25/1995 fee schedule) is payable for each calendar year and must be settled before the Registry issues the inscription.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification by the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá for the registered office and any storage space (exits, extinguishers, smoke detection, and means of escape); occupational safety clearance by the Ministerio de Trabajo y Desarrollo Laboral (MITRADEL) covering the workplace risk-prevention program and workstation ergonomics; structural and land-use inspection ('certificado de uso de suelo y construcción') from the Alcaldía de Panamá if additional shelving or mezzanine loads exceed ordinary office loads; and emergency-plan acknowledgment from SINAPROC (Sistema Nacional de Protección Civil) for any warehouse area.

Paid-in capital rule: Article 5 of Law 25/1995 requires an initial endowment of no less than USD 10,000 (B/.10,000.00) or its equivalent in another currency, which may be contributed in cash, securities, or in-kind assets at the date of constitution and must be formally attested to the Resident Agent; Panamanian law does not mandate an escrow or blocked account for this endowment. The annual foundation tax of USD 300 (Law 25/1995 fee schedule) is payable for each calendar year and must be settled before the Registry issues the inscription.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope. The PIF is a distinct juridical person taxed only on Panama-source income; foreign-source income, including Internet retail revenue from customers abroad where title and delivery occur outside Panama, remains outside the territorial income tax net. The PIF is not pass-through eligible for DGI purposes; net Panama-source income is taxed at the entity level, while benefits distributed to non-resident beneficiaries generally do not constitute Panama-source income.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Panama. Available mechanisms include: (i) reservation of founder powers (to revoke, amend, or designate beneficiaries) expressly inserted into the PIF charter under Law 25 of 1995, determining segregation of foundation assets; (ii) voluntary registration as a small taxpayer (pequeño contribuyente) under the simplified ITBMS regime for annual gross receipts not exceeding B/. 36,000; (iii) Multinational Headquarters (SEM) election under Law 41 of 2007 for qualifying regional service income; (iv) hybrid instrument and financial arrangement documentation elections under Law 7 of 2019 implementing OECD BEPS Action 2. All elections are formal filings with the DGI or Public Registry rather than annual elective classification forms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All persons habitually carrying out taxable transfers of movable goods or services in Panama must register for ITBMS before commencing activities; there is no general numeric annual exemption from registration. The simplified small-taxpayer regime (Régimen Simplificado de Pequeño Contribuyente) applies to businesses with annual gross receipts of B/. 36,000 or less (PAB equivalent of USD 36,000), permitting quarterly simplified filing. For foreign e-commerce sellers, any sale with delivery to a Panamanian address creates ITBMS registration nexus from the first sale; no de minimis distance-selling threshold exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% ITBMS) applies to exports of tangible goods physically leaving Panamanian customs territory, to supplies into qualifying free zones (e.g., Zona Libre de Colón), and to services rendered to non-resident beneficiaries where use or exploitation occurs abroad. Eligibility requires: (i) active DGI ITBMS registration; (ii) a valid export customs declaration (DUE - Declaración Única de Exportación) accepted by the National Customs Authority; (iii) issuance of the commercial invoice through the e-invoicing system with explicit zero-rated annotation; (iv) retention of transport documents proving physical departure; (v) for Internet retail, the goods must be dispatched to a non-Panamanian delivery address and must leave Panamanian territory, otherwise the 7% rate applies; e-commerce operators must separately identify domestic and export sales in ITBMS periodic returns to support refunds or credit carry-forward.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUE - Declaración Única de Exportación) accepted and validated by the National Customs Authority (ANA)
  • Bill of Lading (Conocimiento de Embarque) or Air Waybill evidencing departure from Panamanian territory
  • Commercial invoice with zero-rated ITBMS annotation
  • Packing list
  • Proof of foreign delivery (courier tracking record or proof of delivery from the foreign carrier)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank statement evidencing the foreign payment (Comprobante de Remesa del Exterior)

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) administered by the DGI through the SAG platform. Registered ITBMS taxpayers must: (i) obtain certification from an authorized electronic invoicing service provider (PSFE) or certify an in-house system; (ii) issue invoices in DGI-compliant XML/JSON formats, incorporating the cryptographic digital seal (Firma Digital); (iii) transmit each invoice to the DGI for near-real-time fiscal validation before delivery to the customer; (iv) use electronic credit/debit notes under the same sealing and clearance rules. Integration is performed via documented SOAP/REST APIs with the PSFE or direct SAG endpoints. DGI Resolution No. 201-8690 phased in mandatory use by taxpayer category; invoices lacking DGI clearance are treated as not issued, thereby disallowing input tax credits and export zero-rating. No separate adhesive fiscal stamp is required beyond the DGI digital seal.

Exports zero-rated without valid export documentation are reclassified as domestic taxable sales; the DGI will assess the unpaid 7% ITBMS plus late-payment interest under Article 731 of the Código Fiscal and impose fines ranging from 100% to 200% of the omitted tax for fraudulent conduct under Articles 750 et seq. of the Código Fiscal. Failure in digital e-invoice clearance automatically disqualifies the export exemption for the relevant tax period. Criminal tax evasion under Law 70 of 2019 exposes founders and foundation council members to imprisonment, ineligibility, and disqualification; the DGI may suspend the foundation's tax registration and revoke its e-invoicing certification. Retrospective clawback periods extend to five years, and are indefinite in cases involving fraud or forgery.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Panamanian balboa (PAB) exists only as divisional coins fixed at 1:1 with the USD; no paper balboas are issued. The capital and current accounts are fully open: no exchange control laws, no convertibility approvals, no quota systems, and no surrender requirements apply to any currency. Profit remittances, foundation distributions, redemption of beneficiary interests, and capital repatriation by non-residents are unrestricted and, under Panama's territorial tax system, are not taxed unless the income source is Panamanian. Foreign currencies (EUR, GBP, CNY) may be freely held, converted, or exported through commercial clearing at market rates without central-bank consent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no notification duty for export proceeds, e-commerce receipts, or other trade balances. Receipts may remain indefinitely in foreign bank accounts; the SBP only applies anti-money-laundering reporting to unusual transactions under Law 23 of 27 April 2015. For the ISIC 4791 internet-retail model, Stripe/PayPal settlements may be accumulated offshore and wired to the PIF account on the foundation's own treasury calendar without conversion mandates.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama operates without a central bank. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), established under Decree Law 9 of 26 February 1998 (as amended), is the autonomous banking and financial system regulator. Banco Nacional de Panamá, a 100% state-owned commercial bank, acts as fiscal agent for the State but holds no monetary-issue, lender-of-last-resort, or FX-intervention powers. The U.S. Dollar is legal tender under Article 184 of the Political Constitution of Panama, creating a fully dollarized monetary regime.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Settlor capital injection: route via SWIFT MT103 in USD with the beneficiary field exactly matching the registered foundation name plus 'Fundación de Interés Privado' suffix; the remittance information should read 'Capital contribution - foundation initial endowment.' Panama maintains no statutory purpose-code table; the free-text remittance field is determinative. For U.S.-origin funds, credit via Fedwire or ACH to the Panamanian bank's U.S. correspondent (Citibank N.A., JPMorgan Chase Bank N.A., Bank of America N.A., or Wells Fargo Bank N.A.), then onward SWIFT credit to the PIF account. The injected capital must equal or exceed the amount stated in the Foundation Charter and Public Registry filing (commonly USD 10,000; lower amounts may trigger enhanced SBP value reporting). Verification bundle: (1) notarized source-of-funds declaration executed by the settlor within 30 days; (2) statement proving ownership of the external funding account; (3) the registered Charter; (4) foundation council minutes ratifying account opening. PSP settlements from PayPal or the intermediate Stripe gateway should be credited only to the dedicated collections sub-account, and each settlement batch must reconcile to the PSP payout report.Trade settlements for inventory purchases: use SWIFT MT103 with tags 50K (ordering customer) and 59 (supplier) referencing genuine invoices; larger or recurring orders may be supported by irrevocable documentary letters of credit confirmed by the supplier's bank. Domestic logistics, warehousing, and utility payments clear through Panama's Automated Clearing House (ACH) operated by the banking community under SBP oversight. Structural note: for the ISIC 4791 model, Stripe does not support Panama-domiciled merchants and PayPal Panama support is limited to receiving; therefore an intermediate merchant-of-record entity executes card-not-present acquiring. Payouts from that entity to the PIF must be documented under a merchant-of-record agreement, with the intermediate entity's audited settlement statements attached to each inward credit to prevent mischaracterization as unrelated third-party flows.Deliverable outright forwards on EUR/USD and GBP/USD executed via the offshore trading desks of SBP-licensed international banksUSD/CNH non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) settled in USD for Chinese supplier exposuresCross-currency swaps (EUR/USD and USD/CNH) for notional amounts above USD 1 million with maturities beyond 12 monthsVanilla European-style FX call and put options for cash-flow-contingent foreign-currency inventory purchases

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for Panama-domiciled EMIs; Panama does not issue a dedicated electronic-money-institution license, and non-bank payment operators must be registered with the SBP. Major offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Mercury, Airwallex) do not generally onboard Panamanian Private Interest Foundations. PayPal accepts Panamanian business registrations for receiving funds, but Stripe does not list Panama as a supported merchant market. The practical structure is an intermediate merchant-of-record (e.g., a U.S. LLC or licensed PSP gateway) executing card-not-present acquiring, with settlement flows wired onward to the Panamanian PIF account under documented agreements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on the basis of Panamanian-source net income (territorial taxation); no tax on foreign-source income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on distributions to non-resident shareholders or beneficiaries, reduced to 5% when the distribution is made out of profits that have already borne the 25% corporate income tax. Distributions by a PIF of foreign-source assets or foreign-source e-commerce profits are not Panamanian-source income and are outside the Panamanian dividend withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation is required where related-party transactions in a tax year exceed B/.250,000 (USD 250,000). This includes purchases of camping equipment from affiliated suppliers, brand licensing fees, management fees, or loans with related parties. Taxpayers must also file the annual transfer-pricing information affidavit with the DGI when related-party transactions exceed the same threshold; group entities meeting the consolidated revenue threshold must submit a Country-by-Country report notification.

Economic substance scope: Panama's economic substance doctrine is embodied in Law 254 of 2021 and DGI Resolution No. 2021-72. Any Panamanian entity deriving relevant income from distribution, services, intellectual property, financing, or insurance must maintain adequate premises, staff, expenditures, and core decision-making in Panama. For a PIF conducting cross-border e-commerce of outdoor camping equipment, the relevant activities are generally foreign-source and therefore not subject to Panamanian tax, but the foundation must still demonstrate real governance by holding board meetings, keeping minutes, maintaining proper books, and controlling its Stripe and PayPal merchant accounts from or through Panama. Mere use of a Panamanian registered address without substance will be disregarded.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: The DGI may assess the omitted tax plus monthly moratory interest and a 10% late-payment surcharge. Criminal tax fraud under the Codigo Fiscal and Codigo Penal carries imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and a monetary fine of at least twice the tax evaded; the foundation's RUC is suspended and the entity is reported on the public in-arrears list.

Director personal liability: Directors, signatories, and the resident agent are jointly and severally liable for the omitted tax and interest if they knew or should have known of the under-declaration. The Tax Administrative Court may issue travel bans and asset seizure orders, and the corporate veil may be pierced when the PIF is shown to be a mere instrumentality of the director or founder.
Shell Company / Sham Foundation

Statutory sanction: Under Law 254 of 2021 and DGI Resolution No. 2021-72, the DGI may recharacterize income as Panamanian-source, assess 25% corporate tax plus adjustment penalties up to the full tax omitted, reverse treaty benefits, and dissolve the foundation through the Public Registry for continued non-compliance with annual fees and economic substance declarations.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members, fiduciaries, and the resident agent may be subject to civil restitution for all taxes, interest, and penalties. The authorities may impose judicial seizures against personal assets and prohibit the directors from serving as resident agents or officers on any other Panamanian regulated entity.
AML / Payment-Rails Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Financial Analysis Unit (UAF) may impose administrative fines up to B/.1,000,000 under Law 23 of 2015, as amended by Law 70 of 2019. Predicate money-laundering offenses under the Codigo Penal carry imprisonment of 5 to 10 years plus forfeiture of all tainted assets and closure of the relevant merchant accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and any appointed compliance officer face individual criminal prosecution, separate administrative penalties, remand, and travel interdiction. Regulatory cooperation permits freezing orders against bank and PSP accounts plus asset recovery actions against directors who controlled the payment infrastructure.
Withholding Tax Breach on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The DGI may charge the unwithheld tax, monthly moratory interest, and a penalty of 100% of the amount that should have been withheld. Royalty deductions claimed by the PIF will be disallowed, and any related DTA benefit will be revoked for the tax period.

Director personal liability: The responsible director or fiduciary who ordered or authorized the distribution or royalty payment without proper withholding becomes personally liable for the full withheld amount, interest, and penalty. If the foreign recipient is a related shell, the ultimate beneficial owner is identified through the beneficial-ownership register and prosecuted jointly with the directors.
