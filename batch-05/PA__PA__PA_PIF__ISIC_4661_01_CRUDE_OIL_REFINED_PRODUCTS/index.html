Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Panama. Online portal: e-Tax (https://etax.mef.gob.pa); ancillary statistical filing via SIME.

E-government filing portal

1) Appoint a resident legal agent and, where required, a certified public accountant; 2) execute the Private Interest Foundation charter (escritura de fundación de interés privado) before a Panamanian notary and inscribe it at the Registro Público de Panamá under Ley 25 de 1995; 3) obtain the official foundation registration record (ficha) and certified copy of the charter; 4) file the RUC application with DGI using Form 3B (Solicitud de Inscripción de Persona Jurídica) through e-Tax, attaching the registered charter, ficha, identification of the Foundation Council member(s), and the resident agent's power of attorney; 5) DGI reviews declared Panamanian-source business activities and issues the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) with economic-activity codes covering wholesale trading in crude oil and refined products at ISIC 4661 / NAICS 424720; 6) after RUC issuance, file monthly ITBMS declaration Form 304 and the annual income tax return. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope - Panama applies territorial taxation: income tax is levied solely on income derived from Panamanian sources. A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) under Ley 25 de 1995 is a juridical person of a private, non-commercial nature, but may hold assets and perform contracts; income from offshore marine-fuel brokerage and any other foreign-source activity falls outside the Panamanian fiscal net. If the foundation conducts economic activities with Panamanian-source revenue, it is taxed at the standard corporate rate of 25% on taxable income, and ITBMS obligations attach to local supplies.

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections and special regimes: (1) territorial-source election - taxpayers may segregate Panamanian- and foreign-source income using the 'causa eficiente' (effective-cause) test and supporting economic-substance evidence; (2) small-taxpayer simplified regime under Law 256 of 2022 for gross annual turnover up to B/. 300,000, involving substitute monthly taxation; (3) Multinational Headquarters (SEM/EM) licence under Law 41 of 2007, as amended by Law 254 of 2022, with a reduced 5% effective income tax rate on qualifying intragroup service income; (4) Law 254 of 2022 anti-hybrid and economic-substance provisions requiring annual certification of real activity for foundations managing financial or trading operations from Panama; (5) bilateral treaty/APAs and MLI elective positions for double-tax relief. A purely patrimonial PIF may elect not to register a RUC at all, provided it generates no Panamanian-source commercial income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for commercial operators: any person or juridical entity habitually transferring movable goods or rendering services in Panamanian territory must register for ITBMS before commencing activities. A simplified 'Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente' exists for taxpayers with gross annual receipts up to B/. 300,000 under Law 256 of 2022; such taxpayers pay a substitute monthly tax and have restricted input-credit entitlements. Wholesale marine-fuel trading is normally conducted under the general ITBMS regime to preserve zero-rating credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS at 0% applies to (a) tangible goods physically exported from Panama under an accepted customs export declaration; (b) services supplied to non-residents where the effective use or exploitation occurs entirely outside Panama; and (c) the supply of marine fuel (bunkering) to vessels engaged in international navigation from Panamanian ports, evidenced by the International Bunker Delivery Note and the vessel's IMO certificate. For ISIC 4661 wholesale brokerage transactions in which title and risk pass outside Panamanian territory, the supply is outside the scope of ITBMS and no tax is due; zero-rating is claimed only when the sales invoice is issued from Panama and the export evidence bundle is maintained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduanas - DUA) with SISA system authorization stamp
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or sea waybill (mate's receipt acceptable for local-port bunkering)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT-103 / credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in a US-dollar Panamanian bank account
  • IMO International Bunker Delivery Note (BDN) for marine fuel supplies
  • Certificate of origin or ISIC activity certificate supporting the export nature of the transaction

Mandatory e-invoicing applies under DGI Resolution 266-2019 and amendments. Large taxpayers (Grandes Contribuyentes) were phased in from February 2021; the remaining registered economic operators became subject to mandatory electronic invoicing through 2022-2023. Every invoice must be transmitted in real time to the DGI Fiscal Invoicing System (Sistema de Facturación Fiscal) or issued through an authorized technological service provider; clearance is evidenced by the 52-character Código de Seguridad Fiscal (CSF) embedded in a QR code on the PDF representation. REST/JSON API integration with enterprise resource planning systems is accepted. Export and zero-rated invoices must cite the corresponding export/zero-rating code and be reconciled with the electronic customs declaration.

DGI audits zero-rated export claims through cross-verification of customs exit records, vendor/consignee identity, bill-of-lading dates, and inward remittance traceability. Failure to substantiate a zero-rated invoice triggers retrospective assessment of the 7% ITBMS with default interest, a recalculation surcharge of up to 100% of the omitted tax (Código Fiscal Article 760), and administrative fines ranging from 100% to 200% of the tax evaded. Fraudulent export zero-rating schemes constitute tax fraud (defraudación fiscal) under Código Fiscal Article 762, punishable by imprisonment from 2 to 5 years where the amount exceeds B/. 10,000, and may also trigger customs contraband penalties of up to ten times the commercial value of the goods under Ley 66 de 2014. For a Private Interest Foundation, such violations expose the foundation to RUC cancellation, personal liability of the Foundation Council members for breach of fiduciary duties, and mandatory reports by the Registro Público to the Public Prosecutor's Office.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility under a complete dollarization regime (Law 84 of 1941; Decree Law 9 of 1998). There are no capital-account or current-account exchange controls, no IMF Article VIII restrictions, no prior-approval regime for profit remittance, dividend distribution, interest payment, or liquidation proceeds. A Panama Private Interest Foundation may hold, repatriate, and distribute funds in USD without central-bank authorization; the only practical impediments are individual-bank Enhanced Due Diligence, FATCA/CRS classification, and the need to maintain registered-agent, beneficial-ownership, and source-of-funds records. Territorial taxation applies exclusively to Panamanian-source income, so a non-resident PIF engaged in foreign trade incurs zero Panama withholding, but counterparty banks may require a provisional tax-residence and economic-substance letter to prevent CRS re-rating as a Reportable Account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panama imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export or trade receipts. Proceeds from wholesale brokerage of marine fuel oil and refined distillates (ISIC 4661) may be retained freely in U.S. dollars, held onshore or offshore, and re-deployed without clearance. The receiving bank treats the inflow as commercial turnover supported by the commercial invoice, bill of lading or bunker-delivery note, and broker confirmation; standard UAF/SBP transaction reporting applies only to cash and suspicious-activity thresholds, not to wire receipts. No declaration to the SBP or MEF is required for foreign-currency retention.

Central bank supervision: Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá, SBP), the sole banking-prudential and AML/CFT supervisor under Decree Law 9 of 1998 and Law 23 of 2015; no conventional central bank and no separate foreign-exchange-administration agency exist in Panama. The Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) and the state-owned Banco Nacional de Panamá perform fiscal-agent and domestic-clearing roles; monetary policy is de facto delegated to the U.S. Federal Reserve because the U.S. dollar is full legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility. Panama does not maintain a standalone e-money-institution (EMI) license class, and Decree Law 9 of 1998 restricts deposit-taking, escrow, and payment-account services to licensed banks or SBP-authorized payment providers. U.S. or EU EMI licenses are not passportable into Panama. Cross-border digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, ConnectPay, Currencycloud) may be used only as ancillary collection gateways for small ticket invoices; they cannot issue or confirm Standby Letters of Credit, cannot support documentary trade clearing or SWIFT MT700 flows, and their inability to provide the USD correspondent backbone required for marine-fuel settlements makes them legally and operationally unsuitable as the primary treasury rail. Feasibility rating for the ISIC 4661 broker model: Low.

Root cause: CRS/FATCA re-rating as a Reportable Account due to the Foundation's hybrid legal classification and territorial tax treatment

Defense evidence: CRS self-certification form (FATCA Form W-8BEN-E for non-US beneficial owners; Form W-9 where applicable); IRS GIIN registration match if the PIF is treated as a Panamanian Financial Institution; registered-agent confirmation and provisional non-resident tax status; corporate organizational chart proving no entity residence in a jurisdiction on the EU blacklist; contemporaneous record of registered office, minutes, and economic substance in Panama.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing uses the Foundation's own USD-denominated account at a tier-1 Panamanian bank, funded through a New York correspondent (Fedwire or CHIPS) via SWIFT MT103 with a structured remittance reference such as 'CAP/PA_PIF + folio number' and an underlying notarized capital-contribution deed or intercompany loan agreement. Because Panama operates no exchange-control registration and no purpose-code (BPM) classification regime, there is no central-bank declaration requirement; the receiving bank applies Know-Your-Transaction scrutiny requiring: (i) board or council resolution authorizing the inflow; (ii) source-of-funds confirmation with the contributing entity's bank statements; (iii) identification of the ultimate funder for any amount above USD 50,000; and (iv) OFAC/UAF screen-clear. For shareholder-loan injections, provide the signed loan agreement, interest-rate rationale, and repayment schedule; no withholding applies to capital injections or loan principal since the income is foreign-source for Panamanian territorial tax purposes.Primary clearing rails for the marine-fuel wholesale model: (1) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for direct supplier, broker, surveyor, and freight payments; (2) SWIFT MT700/MT720 commercial documentary letters of credit under UCP600 for crude-oil and refined-product purchases against presentation of the commercial invoice, bill of lading, certificate of quality/quantity, and certificate of origin; (3) Standby Letters of Credit under ISP98 or URDG 758 issued by the Panamanian bank or transmitted through a confirming bank in Miami, New York, or Geneva as performance or payment collateral; (4) MT202/MT202COV for correspondent-only interbank settlement when clearing through an intermediary bank; (5) documentary collections (DP/DA) for mid-sized purchasers where LC cover is not cost-effective; and (6) internal liquidity sweeps between the foundation's capital-escrow and operational accounts. All disbursements above USD 25,000 require dual-signature (maker-checker), automated OFAC pre-screening, and a transaction file containing the sale/purchase contract, pro-forma invoice, Incoterms, and vessel or BDN evidence.Plain-vanilla FX Forwards (USD/EUR, USD/COP, USD/MXN, USD/CLP, USD/PEN) executed through the Panamanian bank or an eligible international counterparty under an ISDA 2022 Master Agreement, provided the hedging tenor matches the cargo settlement cycle and is documented as a commercial hedge.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for Latin American currencies with restricted offshore delivery (e.g., COP, CLP, PYG) where the Panamanian bank nets settlement in USD without principal exchange.Cross-currency basis swaps (USD against EUR or cargo-related currencies) permitted for aligning receivable and payable currency mixes on long-term supply contracts, subject to SBP risk-management suitability assessment.European-style vanilla FX options for committed cargo obligations only, with plain vanilla premium payment, no speculative naked positions, and full ISDA/CSA collateral documentation.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility. Panama does not maintain a standalone e-money-institution (EMI) license class, and Decree Law 9 of 1998 restricts deposit-taking, escrow, and payment-account services to licensed banks or SBP-authorized payment providers. U.S. or EU EMI licenses are not passportable into Panama. Cross-border digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, ConnectPay, Currencycloud) may be used only as ancillary collection gateways for small ticket invoices; they cannot issue or confirm Standby Letters of Credit, cannot support documentary trade clearing or SWIFT MT700 flows, and their inability to provide the USD correspondent backbone required for marine-fuel settlements makes them legally and operationally unsuitable as the primary treasury rail. Feasibility rating for the ISIC 4661 broker model: Low.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% on dividends or other profit distributions paid by a Panama-domiciled entity to a non-resident beneficiary. Distributions from PIF to non-resident beneficiaries are generally outside Panama-source taxation unless attributed to Panama-source commercial activity.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual return is due within three months of the fiscal year end (typically March 31 for calendar-year taxpayers). Quarterly advance payments are due on March, June, September, and December; final settlement with the annual return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing documentation (Local File) required when the taxpayer's gross revenue exceeds USD 10 million or related-party transactions exceed USD 2 million (thresholds in Panamanian Balboas / USD). Master File required for multinational groups meeting the revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: Panama has an economic substance framework through the Law 254 of 2019 and DGI regulations. A PIF engaged in trading or brokering must meet physical presence, employees, expenditure, and core income-generating activity tests. A PIF that is tax transparent or only holds assets must not derive unrestricted trading income without substance in Panama; otherwise, the PIF can be re-domiciled or sanctioned as a shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Penalties of 50% to 100% of the tax evaded, plus 1% monthly interest. Criminal tax fraud prosecuted under the Criminal Code: imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and fines of USD 20,000 to USD 100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors, founder/council members, and resident agent may be held jointly liable if they signed false returns or knowingly assisted in fraud. Civil restitution, asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Economic Substance Failure / Shell Company Allegation

Statutory sanction: DGI may levy administrative fines up to USD 25,000; the Superintendence of Non-Financial Entities may dissolve the PIF. The entity may be treated as a tax shell, losing the territorial tax benefit. For PIFs used in tax arbitrage, penalties of 50% of the omitted tax can be imposed.

Director personal liability: When directors and the protector (beneficial owner) direct the shell, authorities can pierce the veil: personal fines of USD 100,000, confiscation of profits, travel bans, and criminal prosecution for money laundering or tax fraud.
AML Violations in Energy Trading

Statutory sanction: Panama's AML Law (Law 23 of 2015) imposes fines of USD 100,000 to USD 1 million, criminal prosecution for money laundering (8 to 18 years' imprisonment), and mandatory reporting of suspicious transactions. Business license cancellation is a secondary sanction.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal criminal liability: imprisonment 8 to 12 years for ordinary money laundering, 12 to 18 years for aggravated laundering. Directors may be extradited; travel bans and asset freezes are common.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% to 20% of the underpaid tax attributed to TP adjustments. Late documentation fines of USD 5,000 to USD 10,000 per category of missing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to ensure TP filing can be personally fined up to USD 10,000 and jointly liable for the underpaid tax if found negligent.
Illegal Oil Wholesaling Without License

Statutory sanction: Fines of USD 10,000 to USD 100,000, plus confiscation of profit from unlicensed operations. The DGI can disallow all deductions, yielding a 25% tax on gross receipts.

Director personal liability: The resident agent and PIF council members involved in unlicensed acts are jointly liable for restitution and fines; criminal penalties up to 3 years' imprisonment in serious cases.
