Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; digital taxpayer portal: DGI Virtual and SIF Electronic Invoicing Platform (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa); ancillary export clearance authority: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The Private Interest Foundation (PIF), already recorded in the Public Registry of Panama with its resident agent and registered office, delivers its foundation charter (acta de constitución) and Registry certificate to the DGI through the resident agent. Step 2: The agent submits the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) application in the DGI taxpayer registration module, attaching the foundation charter, Registry good-standing certificate, resident-agent appointment, and, where local economic activity exists, the Aviso de Operación and registered-office proof. Step 3: The DGI issues the RUC; the PIF is simultaneously enrolled for ITBMS where it performs taxable Panamanian-source activity, including coffee trading or domestic logistics. Step 4: If the PIF only holds protected assets and derives foreign-source income, it files a non-operation declaration (no Panamanian-source income) and submits zero/nil tax returns as required; foreign entities without Panamanian taxable activity are not obligated to obtain a Panamanian RUC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — a PIF is a distinct legal person under Law 25 of 1995; Panamanian taxation reaches only income sourced within Panamanian territory. Foreign-source income, including most cross-border trading profits, falls outside the Panamanian tax base; an entity-level or pass-through layer is not superimposed unless the PIF conducts Panamanian-source commercial activity.

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election regime. Available statutory and administrative elections: (i) registration as an active taxpayer versus a declarant of non-operation with no Panamanian-source income; (ii) election of the simplified small-taxpayer ('pequeño contribuyente') ITBMS regime for qualifying low-turnover local micro-retailers; (iii) election of the Sede de Empresas Multinacionales (SEM) preferential headquarter regime by qualifying multinational groups; and (iv) the standard territorial ISR regime, under which a PIF with no Panamanian-source income is not liable to income tax, while a PIF carrying local business is taxed at the standard corporate rate on Panamanian-source profits.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Panama imposes no general de minimis turnover threshold for ITBMS registration: any person performing taxable transfers of movable goods or taxable services within Panama must register for ITBMS and obtain a RUC upon commencing activity. A reduced 'pequeño contribuyente' simplified regime is available only to qualifying local micro-retailers with monthly turnover not exceeding approximately B/. 3,000 (annualized B/. 36,000), and is inapplicable to a wholesale green-coffee container distribution operation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods, including containerized specialty green coffee beans, are subject to ITBMS at 0%. The exporter must be a registered ITBMS taxpayer, be named as the shipper/consignor on the Panama customs export declaration (Declaración Única de Aduanas — DUA) filed through the SIGA/SISCOPE customs platform, physically remove the goods from Panamanian customs territory through an authorized port (e.g., Balboa or Cristóbal), and retain the bill of lading and payment evidence showing receipt from the non-resident buyer. Green coffee imported into Panama under a customs-suspension or transit regime and re-exported without being released for local consumption is not subject to import ITBMS, and the subsequent export is zero-rated; coffee cleared for local consumption and later exported carries ITBMS on the local purchase, with that input tax being creditable. Input ITBMS on domestic cost components (warehousing, inland freight, bagging, port handling) is creditable against output ITBMS, and excess credits are refundable by the DGI after cross-verification with customs records. Zero-rating is denied where the goods are consumed or diverted locally or where the documentation is incomplete, mismatched, or sham.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduanas — DUA) filed and validated through the Panamanian SIGA/SISCOPE customs system
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the containerized green coffee from Panamanian customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / documentary letter-of-credit settlement evidence crediting the export proceeds to the exporter's Panamanian bank account
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the DUA and transport document
  • Phytosanitary certificate issued by the Panamanian Ministry of Agricultural Development (MIDA) for green coffee bean lots where legally required
  • Container seal record and export weight/grade certificate evidencing the shipped green coffee quantity and quality

Panama operates a phased mandatory electronic-invoicing framework (Sistema de Facturación Electrónica — SIF) administered by the DGI. Registered taxpayers transmit each invoice in real time to the DGI through secured REST/SOAP APIs before issuance; the DGI validates the invoice and returns a unique authorization code ('código de autorización') with digital stamping, which the taxpayer renders as a QR element on the final invoice and simultaneously reports to the treasury platform. The mandate has been phased in by taxpayer category since 2022 (large, then medium, then small taxpayers); exporters must reconcile e-invoices with the SIGA customs declaration to substantiate the 0% ITBMS treatment. Unauthorized invoices lacking DGI clearance are legally ineffective and disallow input-credit claims.

Panama does not impose a standalone statutory foreign-exchange repatriation calendar; nevertheless, DGI export-zero-rating practice expects export proceeds to appear in the exporter's bank statements within 180 days of the DUA date, as reflected in the mandatory documentation bundle. Missing, diverted, or unrepatriated proceeds entitle the DGI to recharacterize the sale as a taxable local supply at 7% ITBMS, recompute output tax, and retrospectively claw back input credits previously claimed, adding statutory recargos, default interest (interés moratorio) per month of delay, and fines ranging from 50% to 100% of the evaded tax for erroneous or false documentation. Participating in sham export documentation (false DUA, phantom bill of lading, or unrepatriated proceeds) constitutes tax fraud under the Código Fiscal, exposing responsible officers, agents, and beneficiaries to criminal fines of up to USD 100,000 and imprisonment of up to five years. The DGI and the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) conduct joint retrospective audits, and the ordinary reassessment and clawback limitation period is generally five years from the filing of the pertinent tax return.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates under full dollarization. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible: there is no exchange-control law, no central-bank FX visa, no prior-approval requirement for inbound capital, and no restriction on outward remittances. Profit and endowment distributions to non-resident PIF beneficiaries are not subject to withholding tax or remittance approval under the territorial income-tax system; the only practical hurdles are SBP-mandated AML/KYC documentation and OFAC-compliant correspondent clearing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory surrender ratio, and no compulsory conversion of export proceeds. USD-denominated receipts from specialty green coffee sales may be retained in Panama or offshore at the foundation's discretion; only SBP record-keeping (minimum 5 years) and anti-money-laundering verification obligations apply.

Central bank supervision: No issuing central bank exists in Panama. Banking and FX supervision are vested in the Superintendency of Banks of Panama (SBP – Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá); fiscal-agent and treasury functions are performed by Banco Nacional de Panamá under the permanent US-dollarization monetary regime (US Dollar legal tender; Balboa only a nominal coinage subdivision).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

LOW-MEDIUM. Under Decree-Law 9 of 1998 (as reformed by Decree-Law 2 of 2008), only SBP-licensed institutions may take deposits in Panama. An offshore digital EMI (e.g., UK FCA, Lithuanian, or Singapore-licensed) cannot open a Panamanian-domiciled USD account for the PIF, issue Panamanian clearing identifiers, or act as the principal settlement institution for coffee trade proceeds. EMIs may serve only as ancillary retail collection channels in EUR/GBP via local acquiring partners. An in-country alternative exists under Law 185 of 2021 (Financial Innovation Hub and SBP Sandbox), but the primary clearing rails must remain at an SBP-regulated Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Protocol: (a) the foreign founder or funding entity executes a SWIFT MT103 from a FATF- or US-regulated bank; (b) the USD credit is cleared via US correspondent channels (Fedwire/CHIPS) into the PIF's account at an SBP-regulated Panamanian bank; (c) SWIFT Field 70 must state 'Dotación de Capital – Ley 25 de 1995 – ISIC 4631 specialty green coffee trading' together with the foundation's registered name; (d) the foundation posts the contribution as an asiento de dotación in its asset register and updates the escrow records. No central-bank visa, exchange contract, or purpose-code declaration is required under Panama's dollarized regime; the SBP AML duty to retain source-of-funds evidence for at least 5 years applies.Clearing rails selected by counterparty trust and ticket size: (1) SWIFT MT103 open-account USD payments to exporter banks for established suppliers in Colombia, Brazil, Central America, and Kenya; (2) irrevocable documentary Letters of Credit (MT700) issued by the Panamanian bank under UCP 600 for first-time or large-value (>USD 500,000) shipments, payable against original invoice, clean-on-board bill of lading, marine cargo insurance certificate, packing list, specialty-coffee origin certificate, and phytosanitary certificate referencing HS 0901.11; (3) documentary collections (MT400/DP-DA) as an intermediate trust-building tool. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding on dividends paid out of profits that were subject to the 25% Panamanian CIT; 20% withholding on distributions funded from territorially exempt income such as foreign-source coffee trading profit or untaxed capital gains.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months after the fiscal year-end (31 March for calendar-year taxpayers); no automatic extension; DGI may grant a one-month extension upon verified written application.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1a Ingresos brutos de fuente panameña (gross income from Panamanian sources) Report all Panama-derived turnover from green coffee bean distribution, including sales, commissions, and warehousing income; the PIF must allocate each Panama sale separately using its RUC and statutory accounting ledger. Late filing attracts a penalty of up to 10% of the tax due plus monthly late-payment interest of 1% (12% annual statutory rate) on the outstanding balance; non-filing enables DGI estimated assessment and an administrative fine of up to B/.100,000, with criminal liability for tax evasion where intentional.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.3,000,000 (US$3,000,000) in gross revenues during the tax period, or B/.1,000,000 (US$1,000,000) in cross-border related-party transactions; at or above these thresholds the PIF must prepare a Local File and, at group level, a Master File.

Economic substance scope: Panama ESR (Executive Decree 212 of 2020 as amended) classifies green coffee container distribution as a 'distribution and service center' relevant activity; a PIF with this scope must maintain in Panama a physical office, full-time employees, board minutes, a bank account, and annual operating expenditures commensurate with the value of the distribution activity; the annual ESR filing is submitted simultaneously with Form 102; absence of substance triggers penalties, loss of the DGI good-standing certificate, and potential deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code Art. 280-A: imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and fines of 3 to 10 times the evaded tax; the tax administration additionally assesses unpaid tax with 12% annual interest and imposes a 10% penalty for an incorrect or late return; DGI may seal the warehouse and seize coffee inventory pending payment.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members, officers, and the certified accountant who sign or design the fraudulent return are jointly and severally liable; Panamanian criminal courts may order preventive detention, travel bans (impedimento de salida), and civil restitution of the evaded tax; the corporate veil is pierced where the PIF is proven to be a mere façade.
Shell Company / Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR non-compliance carries fines of B/.50,000 to B/.1,000,000 for the entity; DGI may nullify tax registrations, deny refunds, and request the Public Registry to cancel the foundation's legal personality; repeat offense triggers immediate deregistration and a block on opening Panamanian bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members bear individual fines of up to B/.250,000 and may be disqualified from managing Panamanian companies for up to 5 years; the resident agent who fails to submit ESR reporting is also suspended and personally liable for penalties.
Money Laundering / AML Structural Red Flag

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015 and the Beneficial Ownership Registry, omission of BOI updates is administratively fined up to B/.1,000,000 per entity and B/.250,000 per responsible person; if the conduct constitutes money laundering, the Criminal Code imposes imprisonment of 5 to 12 years and confiscation of the full transaction value.

Director personal liability: All signatories and the resident agent face criminal prosecution; courts may issue freeze orders (embargo) on personal assets and prohibit departure from Panama; conviction results in permanent disqualification from acting as director, partner, or foundation council member in Panama.
