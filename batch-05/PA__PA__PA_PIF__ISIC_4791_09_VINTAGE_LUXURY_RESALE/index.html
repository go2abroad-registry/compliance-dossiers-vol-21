Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully dollarized economy: the US dollar is legal tender and the Panamanian Balboa circulates only as subsidiary coinage at a fixed 1:1 rate. Both current and capital accounts are completely open, with no registration, licensing, approval, or exchange-control restrictions on inward or outward transfers. Profit and dividend remittances face zero FX or conversion hurdles; the only fiscal friction is the territorial-tax framework, which applies a 10% withholding or final tax to Panama-source distributions on a defined basis, while foreign-source income realized by the Private Interest Foundation (PIF), including online resale proceeds settled through Stripe and Escrow_Pay, is outside the Panamanian tax net provided the business is managed and executed abroad. No Balboa/U.S.-dollar exchange-control band exists, so convertibility risk is effectively nil.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation/surrender requirement for export or e-commerce proceeds. The PIF may retain 100% of USD receivables in Panamanian or offshore accounts and is free to switch currencies at will. Compliance obligations are confined to banking record-keeping and UAF reporting thresholds: cash and individual wire transactions of USD 10,000 or more, or structured related transfers, are reported by banks on a suspicion/limit basis, and all client records must be maintained for five years.

Central bank supervision: Panama does not operate a central bank or a dedicated foreign-exchange administration. Banking, treasury, and FX-conduct supervision is vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) under Decree Law 9 of 1998, as amended. The Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) enforces AML/CFT reporting standards, while Banco Nacional de Panamá performs clearing and agency functions without exercising monetary policy or currency-issuance authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and fintech wallet providers: LOW viability. Correspondent-bank de-risking, the absence of a Panama license, and the PIF's enhanced-due-diligence profile cause most offshore EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury-class providers) to reject or severely restrict Private Interest Foundations engaged in cross-border luxury resale. Panama-regulated fintechs licensed under Law 300 of 2022 (SBP 'Innovation Hub' and e-money/payment-license regime) offer MEDIUM-HIGH operational viability for USD digital wallets and payment aggregation, but they do not substitute for tier-1 bank clearing and are still required to apply full KYC/BO transparency. Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Private Interest Foundation with non-registered beneficial ownership — risk of bearer-type foundation rights and opaque beneficiary decks (FATF Recommendation 24 breach in correspondents' assessment).

Root cause: Private Interest Foundation with non-registered beneficial ownership — risk of bearer-type foundation rights and opaque beneficiary decks (FATF Recommendation 24 breach in correspondents' assessment).

Defense evidence: Apostilled foundation charter (Pacto Fundacional) filed with the Registro Público; certified Rights Registry (Registro de Derechos) proving issuance only of registered, non-bearer foundation rights with a complete holder ledger; sworn Beneficial Owner Declaration (Declaración Jurada de Beneficiario Final) under UAF Resolution 005-2015; legal opinion confirming abolition and immobilization of bearer instruments under Law 13 of 2017; CVs and passports of all foundation council members and the foundation guardian (if any).
ISIC 4791 vintage luxury resale — high transaction values, high frequency, counterfeit/stolen-goods and counterfeit-authentication risk; sector is a recognized money-laundering route in trade-based typologies.

Root cause: ISIC 4791 vintage luxury resale — high transaction values, high frequency, counterfeit/stolen-goods and counterfeit-authentication risk; sector is a recognized money-laundering route in trade-based typologies.

Defense evidence: Provenance dossier per inventory lot: original purchase receipts, third-party authentication certificates (Entrupy, Real Authentication, or equivalent), condition reports and chain-of-custody logs; independent valuation certificates for SKUs above USD 5,000; trademark or IP-owner consent where branding is used; platform-side records (Stripe, Escrow_Pay) evidencing genuine merchant-buyer interaction and no structured rapid flip of high-value items; quality-assurance SOP and returns/cancellation policy in Spanish and English.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) Founder or authorized contributor initiates a SWIFT MT103 from a bank in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction; field 50K must name the remitter (founder or disclosed contributor), field 59 must show the PIF's full legal name and Panama account number, and field 70/72 reference 'APORTE FUNDACIONAL — CONSTITUTION ENDOWMENT — [Registro Público foundation number]' to ensure clear classification as capital rather than commercial revenue. (2) Panama imposes no central-bank approval code, no FX declaration, and no convertibility certificate; the only mandatory layer is bank-level EDD, under which the PIF must supply the Apostilled foundation deed, the endowment resolution, and source-of-funds files before the first credit. (3) Credits of USD 10,000 or more are reported by the bank to the UAF; credits above USD 100,000 require a pre-credit funds-flow narrative from the foundation's counsel. (4) The remitting bank's own jurisdiction may apply its own outgoing-payment rules (e.g., EU/UK greater-transparency regimes), so the PIF should attach the foundation charter and a cover letter signed by counsel to prevent the transfer being rejected by the originator's compliance desk. (5) Capital escrow and operational accounts must never be intermingled: endowment funds sit in the Capital Escrow Account until the foundation council resolves to reallocate them to working capital, evidenced by a certified council minute.Inventory and supplier settlements: use SWIFT MT103 (cover or serial method) with purpose narrative 'GOODS TRADE — SETTLEMENT OF VINTAGE LUXURY GOODS PURCHASE' and the beneficiary's full trade account; USD clearing executes via Panamanian banks' New York correspondents on Fedwire/CHIPS for same-day value. High-value single acquisitions (USD 25,000 and above, particularly with EU/UK boutique consignors) should use an irrevocable at-sight documentary letter of credit issued under UCP 600 by the Panama bank's trade-finance desk, payable against commercial invoice, authentication report, and transport/custody documents. Domestic Panamanian disbursements clear through the Cámara de Compensación Automatizada (ACH machinery operated via Banco Nacional de Panamá) in USD, while US-market disbursements can be routed via the bank's US dollar clearing account. Stripe and Escrow_Pay payouts arrive as platform settlements into the designated operational account; the PIF should maintain a rolling reserve buffer of approximately 6–10% of monthly volume to absorb chargebacks and the platforms' 180-day hold risk. All outbound wires above USD 10,000, or aggregate daily outflow above USD 20,000, require standing instruction files and, where the payee was not previously bank-verified, a source-of-funds and underlying-transaction dossier attached to the payment order.OTC deliverable FX forwards (USD/EUR, USD/GBP) with licensed Panama banks or international counterparties under ISDA 2002 or a local master agreement — tenors to 24 months, no central clearing mandate.Cross-currency swaps (USD/EUR, USD/GBP) to match inventory-funding obligations where the PIF borrows or funds in USD but must discharge EUR/GBP supplier liabilities.Plain-vanilla FX options (USD/EUR, USD/GBP) — permissible for contingent liabilities such as auction wins and competitive consignment purchases.Non-deliverable forwards: not applicable for the Panamanian Balboa (fully convertible, USD-circulating, no offshore PAB NDF market); offshore USD/EUR or USD/GBP NDFs are legally permissible where a deliverable alternative is unavailable, but are economically inferior in Panama's zero-restriction environment.Natural hedge through multi-currency operational accounts (EUR/GBP) at the Panama bank or a licensed EU/UK bank, limiting forced conversions and reducing reliance on derivative instruments.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% statutory dividend withholding tax where the profits have been subject to normal corporate income tax at 25%; 10% where the profits arise from income taxed under the alternative flat-rate regime or from exempt/offshore sources. Panama-signatory DTAs commonly cap the rate between 5% and 15%, with lower caps applicable to corporate shareholders of at least 10%-25% of the voting capital.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months after the fiscal year-end; for a calendar-year accounting period, no later than 31 March of the following year. DGI may extend the general deadline through an official filing calendar based on the final RUC digit. The legal representative or resident agent of the Private Interest Foundation must file in Panama.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Instalments are due by 30 June, 30 September, 31 December, and 31 March. The March instalment coincides with the final Form 851 deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation and the Form 843 related-party schedule are mandatory when annual controlled transactions exceed PAB 1,000,000, or when the taxpayer's gross income exceeds PAB 9,000,000. The Master File is required for members of a multinational group whose consolidated group revenue exceeds PAB 250,000,000. These thresholds derive from Executive Decree 401 of 2011, as amended by Law 76 of 2019 and supplementary DGI regulations.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not maintain the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations for all entities, but the territorial tax regime de facto requires substantive local activity to generate taxable Panama-source revenue. Since Law 70 of 2019, every juridical person, including a Private Interest Foundation, must keep a compliant beneficial ownership file; the foundation must also appoint and maintain a licensed resident agent, a registered office in Panama, a full accounting ledger, and a foundation council with authority exercised from Panama. Critically, Article 25 of Law 25 of 1995 prohibits the PIF from engaging in commercial activity; operating an ISIC 4791 online resale business in the foundation itself is unlawful. The permitted structure is a Panamanian Sociedad Anonima holding the Aviso de Operacion, employees, inventory, and payment-processor contracts, with the PIF acting solely as shareholder or beneficiary holder of that operating company; if the PIF directly executes the e-commerce trade, the DGI may reclassify the foundation as a taxable commercial entity, cancel its registration, and impose penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (ISR fraud)

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to PAB 50,000 in addition to the 10% late filing surcharge and 1% monthly interest; administrative assessment by the DGI followed by coactive seizure of bank accounts and luxury inventory; criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years under the Fiscal Code anti-evasion provisions; publication of the tax lien in official registries.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members and the ultimate founder are subject to civil joint and several liability for the evaded tax; a Panamanian court may pierce the PIF's legal personality under Law 25 of 1995 Articles 8 and 9; directors face imprisonment, judicial travel bans (prohibicion de salida del pais), and asset freezing orders triggered by criminal tax proceedings.
