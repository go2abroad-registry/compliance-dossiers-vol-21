Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas de la República de Panamá; taxpayer portal: SII Panamá (https://sii.dgi.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity engaging in taxable activities in Panama must first obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC). The DGI requires appointment of a Panama-licensed resident agent, filing of the foreign entity's constitutive documents (duly notarized and apostilled), and submission through the SII portal. The RUC is used for income tax and ITBMS. A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must register for ITBMS separately if it carries on taxable commercial activities in Panama, including aircraft engine dry-leasing operations.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign entity engaging in taxable activities in Panama must first obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC). The DGI requires appointment of a Panama-licensed resident agent, filing of the foreign entity's constitutive documents (duly notarized and apostilled), and submission through the SII portal. The RUC is used for income tax and ITBMS. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not employ a U.S.-style check-the-box election. Entity tax classification follows the chosen Panamanian legal form and the territorial-source framework. A PIF may elect in its charter whether to pursue commercial activities; if it does so, Panama-source income becomes taxable. Taxpayers may elect simplified small-taxpayer treatment for ITBMS and certain income tax installment regimes where statutory thresholds are met. Panama does not currently provide standalone hybrid mismatch elections for cross-border leasing structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No fixed monetary threshold: ITBMS registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply performed in Panama. A simplified small-taxpayer regime is available to persons with annual gross revenue below USD 36,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS zero-rating applies to exports of goods and certain export services. Goods must physically leave Panamanian customs territory and be evidenced by customs documents. Services qualify if rendered to a non-resident recipient and used entirely outside Panama, with no enjoyment of the benefit in Panama. For dry-leased aircraft engines, the lessor must document movement of the engine to a foreign location, use by a foreign operator or maintainer, and receipt of payment from a non-resident.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Panama mandates electronic fiscal invoicing under DGI's Factura Electrónica program. Taxpayers must transmit invoices through DGI's SII platform or DGI-certified authorized software, obtain a DGI validation code, and issue invoices with a digital stamp. Real-time validation applies for registered exporters and newly created taxpayers; remaining taxpayers follow DGI's phased implementation calendar. DGI publishes API specifications for certified service providers.

DGI audits zero-rating claims and may reclassify unsupported or fraudulent transactions as taxable domestic supplies at 7% ITBMS. Statutory interest runs from the original tax due date, and fines may reach up to 200% of the understated tax. Criminal tax fraud liability applies to knowingly false export documentation. Supporting export records must be retained at least seven years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized, open-account monetary system with no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. There are no restrictions on the repatriation of capital, payment of foreign invoices, or conversion between the Balboa, the U.S. Dollar, or other currencies. A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) earning exclusively foreign-source dry-lease income is not subject to Panamanian income tax and faces no statutory hurdle in distributing funds to foreign contributors or beneficiaries; the only practical hurdles are banking AML/KYC procedures, OFAC sanctions screening, and the 5% advance dividend tax if any Panama-source income were ever derived, which is inapplicable to offshore aircraft engine leasing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Panama imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no conversion ratio for export receipts, because the U.S. Dollar is the official circulating currency and the Balboa has no independent monetary existence. Export lease rentals may be retained in USD or freely converted. The only compliance overlay is AML/CFT reporting under Law 23 of 2015: banks and the receiving PIF must preserve source-of-funds and lease documentation for all payments and report to the UAF any integrated transaction equal to or above USD 10,000, with no FX conversion license required.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama has no central bank; the Banco Nacional de Panama acts only as a state-owned commercial agent bank, while banking and financial supervision is vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panama (SBP) and the Unidad de Analisis Financiero (UAF) performs AML/CFT supervision; monetary policy is effectively set by the U.S. Federal Reserve because the U.S. Dollar is the sole circulating legal tender and the Balboa exists only as a nominal 1:1 coinage unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border digital EMI (e-money institution) onboarding is rated LOW to MEDIUM for a Panama Private Interest Foundation. Foreign EMIs lacking an SBP banking license cannot lawfully provide USD clearing in Panama; the SBP treats unlicensed overseas EMI-ledgers as unregulated financial intermediation and the Bank General / etc. clearing chain will reject third-party EMI sweeps. World-class EMIs may be used only as a supplementary external payment rail for small operational disbursements if they are tied to a licensed U.S. or EU bank account, but they cannot serve as the primary treasury rail because Law 1 of 2020 sandbox entities are not yet universally accepted by Panamanian compliance desks and correspondent banks may block USD clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) the founder/contributor executes a capital endowment letter addressed to the foundation council; (2) funds are transmitted via SWIFT MT103 (priority) in USD from the contributor's bank to the foundation's operational or escrow account in Panama; (3) the wire instruction must include the payment reference 'Non-commercial capital endowment - PIF [foundation name]' and a standard purpose code (e.g., transaction code G40 or its equivalent per bank instruction) indicating an equity / capital contribution rather than trade payment; (4) the Panamanian receiving bank performs OFAC and UAF screening and may require a letter of explanation and the contributor's identification; (5) the foundation must contemporaneously record the contribution in its internal minutes and ledger. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on Panama-source net taxable income; income from sources outside Panama is exempt under the territorial tax model.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends paid from Panama-source taxed profits; 0% on distributions of foreign-source or territorially exempt income, provided the Foundation can prove the exempt origin of the distributed funds.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual filing must occur within 3 months after the fiscal year-end. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the deadline is 31 March of the subsequent year; extensions do not alter the payment deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Transfer Pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) is triggered when annual Panama-source gross income exceeds PAB 2,000,000 or related-party transactions surpass PAB 500,000 in a fiscal period. The Balboa (PAB) is pegged 1:1 to the United States dollar, and these thresholds are also met using USD-denominated contracts.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations, introduced by Law 254 of 2020, apply to entities conducting specified 'relevant activities' including shipping and asset distribution/service operations. The aircraft engine dry-leasing business may be classified as a 'distribution and service center' activity if a Panamanian entity is involved, obligating the PIF to maintain adequate physical presence, employees, and decision-making functions in Panama. A passive PIF holding foreign engine leases without Panama-based management is not automatically exempt; it must demonstrate commercial substance or face sanctions, recharacterization, and potential loss of territorial tax advantages.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Underreporting Panama-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Panama's Tax Code criminalizes tax evasion with imprisonment from 2 to 5 years and fines up to 10 times the amount evaded, plus interest and surcharges. The DGI may also nullify the Foundation's tax exemption status and impose a 20% penalty on the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members and the resident agent who sign or direct false returns face civil liability for the unpaid tax, personal seizure of assets, travel restrictions, and criminal prosecution. Where the evasion is proven as a corporate sham, courts may pierce the foundation's veil, exposing the founder and beneficiaries to joint and several liability.
Shell Company – Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Panama's ESR has sanctions for non-compliance: first a warning, then monthly fines up to USD 10,000 for failure to comply with substance requirements; revocation of registration and striking off in serious cases; courts may disregard the entity for tax purposes, attributing income to the beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: Directors or foundation council members who fail to address ESR deficiencies can be held personally liable for administrative fines and for any tax liabilities re-assessed to the PIF. The DGI can issue travel bans and refer the matter to the Public Prosecutor's Office if fraud is detected.
AML / CFT Violations in Lease Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Panama's Law 23 of 2015 (AML/CFT) imposes fines of up to PAB 5,000,000 on legal persons for serious breaches; willful money laundering carries imprisonment of 5 to 12 years. Failure to maintain transaction records triggers revocation of the PIF's license to operate.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members, the Resident Agent, and the compliance officer can be criminally prosecuted if they knowingly participate in or fail to report suspicious transactions. Personal civil forfeiture, travel bans, and statutory disqualification from holding directorial positions apply on conviction.
Abusive DTA Relief / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Tax treaty abuse is treated as tax fraud; penalties include the full recovery of under-withheld taxes, a 50% negligence penalty or 100% fraud penalty, and suspension of treaty benefits. The DGI will issue a formal determination and, if applicable, trigger foreign disclosure under the Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance in Tax Matters.

Director personal liability: The directors or council members who sign treaty relief declarations are personally liable for the unpaid taxes and penalties if beneficial ownership is misrepresented. In serious cases, they face imprisonment for perjury and tax fraud, and the foundation's tax resident status may be stripped with retroactive effect.
