Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá – Sección de Fundaciones de Interés Privado, Mercantile Section (Public Registry of Panama, Private Interest Foundational Registry)

Governing statute

Panama Private Interest Foundations are governed by Law No. 25 of 12 June 1995 (Régimen de las Fundaciones de Interés Privado), as amended by Law No. 13 of 2017, and complemented by Law No. 23 of 2015 on anti-money-laundering due diligence. Relevant provisions: Articles 1-6 on creation, endowment, and purpose; Articles 7-12 on registered agent, name, and duration; Articles 13-18 on the Foundation Council and administration; Articles 19-25 on beneficiaries and the protector; Articles 26-33 on Public Registry filing, amendment, and merger provisions.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acta Constitutiva de Fundación de Interés Privado (Foundation Charter), together with the Reglamento (Regulations), executed before a Panamanian notary and inscribed in the Public Registry of Panama.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 12 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Article 6 of Law 25 of 1995 requires a minimum initial endowment of US$10,000, or its equivalent in Panamanian Balboas, declared by the founder in the Foundation Charter. The endowment must be subscribed and available at formation; the State does not require deposit into a government escrow account, but the registered agent must verify funding evidence. For active ship-bunkering operations, chartered banks will additionally require working capital or standby credit facilities above US$250,000 before opening transaction accounts.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification from the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá for all fuel storage, transfer, and lightering points; structural load inspection and municipal occupancy certificate from the competent Municipality for any shore-side offices or tank farms; occupational health and safety clearance from MITRADEL under Executive Decree 168 of 1999; and AMP verification of marine safety management systems, crew certification, and third-party civil liability insurance for all bunkering craft and shore interfaces.

Paid-in capital rule: Article 6 of Law 25 of 1995 requires a minimum initial endowment of US$10,000, or its equivalent in Panamanian Balboas, declared by the founder in the Foundation Charter. The endowment must be subscribed and available at formation; the State does not require deposit into a government escrow account, but the registered agent must verify funding evidence. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; online portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa (TU@PA and Facturación Electrónica en Línea systems)

E-government filing portal

A Private Interest Foundation must obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) by filing an online application through the DGI TU@PA portal or in person at DGI regional offices, accompanied by the registered foundation deed, the Public Registry incorporation certificate for the Private Interest Foundation, and a sworn statement designating a fiscal representative or resident agent for tax compliance. The DGI issues the RUC within 5 to 15 business days. For ITBMS purposes, the foundation must also activate its Taxpayer Registry status and obtain a digital certificate for electronic invoicing if it will conduct taxable activities in Panama.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch elections. A Private Interest Foundation may elect to register as an ITBMS taxpayer and may further elect the Simplified Tax Regime (RTS) if annual gross income does not exceed B/.36,000. A foundation engaged in bunkering may elect to be treated as a regular juridical person under the Fiscal Code, thereby limiting tax exposure to Panama-source income and subjecting itself to standard ITBMS obligations. No irrevocable single-tier pass-through election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 annual gross income threshold for the simplified small-taxpayer regime; otherwise all taxable persons performing taxable transfers or services in Panama are required to register with DGI regardless of turnover

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of marine fuel and lubricants to vessels engaged in international navigation, including deep-sea vessels on foreign routes and ships traversing the Panama Canal, qualify for the 0% ITBMS rate as export services when the bunker supplier is registered with DGI and the delivery is documented as an international supply. Services rendered to non-residents and consumed entirely outside Panama also qualify. The bunker supplier must obtain and retain evidence of the vessel's international voyage documentation and destination outside Panama.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación de Combustible) filed through Panama Customs SISPA
  • Bunkering Delivery Receipt (BDN) signed by the vessel master and bunker supplier
  • Bill of Lading or International Voyage Certificate evidencing the vessel's foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (certificado de remesa) or SWIFT confirmation evidencing foreign-currency payment from a non-Panamanian bank account
  • DGI-registered electronic invoice for the zero-rated supply

Panama mandates electronic invoicing under the Facturación Electrónica en Línea (FEL) system. Taxpayers must obtain DGI digital authorization before issuing invoices; each invoice must be transmitted to DGI in XML format through the authorized web service or commercial API, must contain a digital signature, and must display a DGI-generated QR code. Bunker fuel deliveries require the e-invoice to be transmitted within 24 hours of supply, with real-time fiscal clearance for each transaction.

Panama's Fiscal Code and Tax Administrative Act impose a 50% surcharge on omitted ITBMS for unjustified zero-rating claims, together with graduated fines ranging from B/.500 to B/.10,000 and statutory interest. Repeated fraudulent zero-rating practices trigger criminal tax fraud liability under Article 29 of the Tax Criminal Code (Section 263-A), which carries imprisonment of 1 to 5 years and disqualification from holding tax registry authorizations. The DGI may retrospectively claw back VAT credits claimed on export supplies for up to six fiscal years and penalize the taxpayer for each false invoice issued.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible regime without capital-account or current-account controls. Panama has no exchange-control law, no convertibility permit system, and no restriction on profit remittance, dividend distribution, capital repatriation, or distribution of foundation benefits to non-residents. The US dollar is legal tender and the only settlement currency for balances; accounts may also be carried in major currencies, but the effective treasury is dollar-centered. Profit and benefit distributions are subject solely to AML/KYC verification and, where applicable, statutory tax formalities, not to any FX authorization. The legal risk profile for remittance of bunkering revenue is low from an FX-access standpoint and moderate from a compliance-verification standpoint.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panamanian law imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender window, no forced conversion ratio, and no requirement to deposit dollars with any state institution. Bunkering and refueling revenues may be retained in full in the operating treasury, credited to any bank in Panama, or reinvested abroad. The only obligations are statistical balance-of-payments reporting by licensed banks on an aggregate basis and the entity's AML obligations under Law 23 of 2015.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Superintendency of Banks of Panama) is the principal banking, FX, and payment-system regulator for the Republic of Panama; Panama has no central bank and operates a fully dollarized monetary regime under Decree Law No. 16 of 1950 and Law No. 21 of 2017. The Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent and clearing bank for the State but exercises no monetary policy or exchange-control powers. The Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Autoridad Marítima de Panamá apply ancillary controls over port-related commercial flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore unlicensed e-money institutions have no legal passport into Panama; only Sociedades de Pago authorized by the Superintendency of Banks may lawfully provide payment services, and their scope is capped at payment initiation and e-money issuance without full treasury-grade account services. A marine bunkering treasury dependent on such providers would encounter correspondent-bank de-risking, OFAC screening fragility, and FATF-related reputational concern. All liquidity must reside in licensed banks, with foreign EMIs used only as a remittance front-end to a licensed Panamanian account where expressly permitted.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 3 months after the fiscal-year close. For calendar-year filers the deadline is 31 March of the following year; any other fiscal year must file within the same three-month window. Requests for extension must be made on Form DGI 122, and approved extensions are granted only when the provisional tax is paid.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1a — Gross Panamanian-source income Report gross receipts from the sale and delivery of marine fuel and lubricants to vessels in Panama’s ports, territorial waters, or the Canal watershed. Include only income sourced within Panama. A PIF that operates as an offshore bunkering intermediary, with title to fuel passing outside Panamanian waters, is not subject to tax and should report nil in this box. Late filing: fixed fine of PAB 500 to PAB 2,000 (greater for repeated breaches) plus 2% per month of the tax due, without exceeding 60% of the total tax. Late payment: a 10% surcharge, monthly 1% interest, and daily running penalty of PAB 20 per working day. Criminal evasion may lead to a fine equal to twice the tax evaded and imprisonment of 1–5 years.
Box 1b — Foreign-source income and exempt income Report income from fuel supplied to vessels on the high seas, outside Panamanian jurisdiction, and any interest or royalties received from non-residents without a Panamanian nexus. Foreign-source income is excluded from the taxable base. Non-resident managers must maintain a transfer pricing allocation memorandum demonstrating the siphon of revenue to foreign activities. Late filing: fixed fine of PAB 500 to PAB 2,000 (greater for repeated breaches) plus 2% per month of the tax due, without exceeding 60% of the total tax. Late payment: a 10% surcharge, monthly 1% interest, and daily running penalty of PAB 20 per working day. Criminal evasion may lead to a fine equal to twice the tax evaded and imprisonment of 1–5 years.
Box 6 — Net taxable income Enter the excess of Panamanian-source gross income over the direct and apportioned costs, including the cost of aviation or marine fuel purchased, port fees, and logistics overhead. Deductions are permitted only if the underlying expenditures were subject to a 15% Panamanian tax deduction rule for services paid to local suppliers. The PIF must substantiate every deduction with invoices, contracts, and bank wires. Late filing: fixed fine of PAB 500 to PAB 2,000 (greater for repeated breaches) plus 2% per month of the tax due, without exceeding 60% of the total tax. Late payment: a 10% surcharge, monthly 1% interest, and daily running penalty of PAB 20 per working day. Criminal evasion may lead to a fine equal to twice the tax evaded and imprisonment of 1–5 years.
Box 12 — Tax payable at 25% Multiply the resulting net taxable income by 25%, then apply any available tax credits, carryforwards, and the annual minimum corporate tax of PAB 1,000 for companies with gross income over PAB 100,000. If the PIF has no Panamanian-source business and holds only foreign assets, this box must show zero. In all cases, the PIF must file the annual minimum tax return if it holds a Panama bank account or a registered office. Late filing: fixed fine of PAB 500 to PAB 2,000 (greater for repeated breaches) plus 2% per month of the tax due, without exceeding 60% of the total tax. Late payment: a 10% surcharge, monthly 1% interest, and daily running penalty of PAB 20 per working day. Criminal evasion may lead to a fine equal to twice the tax evaded and imprisonment of 1–5 years.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB 1,000,000 of aggregate related-party transactions in the fiscal year triggers the obligation to prepare and file the Local File; the Master File is mandatory when related-party transactions exceed PAB 5,000,000 or if the group’s consolidated gross revenue exceeds PAB 25,000,000. A separate informative return (Form DGI 243) is required for all related-party transactions exceeding PAB 250,000, in accordance with Resolution MT-148-2017.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (Law 35 of 2021) treat a bunkering operation as a 'logistics/distribution' relevant activity where the supply of fuel to vessels constitutes physical import, storage and distribution in Panama. The PIF must demonstrate economic substance by: (1) maintaining a physical office or using an approved agent with a fixed address; (2) employing local staff or outsourcing to a licensed service provider with at least two Panamanian activities; (3) generating income and expenses that are proportional to the bunkering volume; (4) holding board meetings in Panamanian territory at least annually; and (5) filing a substance declaration with the DGI. Foreign-owned PIFs cannot be passive holding companies with subcontracted operations that merely receive income; each contract must be signed in Panama and operational decisions must be made in Panama.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine equal to 2 to 5 times the evaded tax, surcharge of 20%, monthly interest at 1%, and criminal imprisonment for 1 to 5 years (Penal Code Articles 101–105, Fiscal Code Article 971).

Director personal liability: Directors, Foundation Council members, and any restructuring expert are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency and civil restitution; a court may impose travel bans, freezing orders on personal assets, and if the evasion exceeds PAB 1,000,000, a prison sentence up to 6 years.
Shell Company (ESR Violation)

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty up to PAB 500,000; daily fine of PAB 10,000 for continued non-compliance; cancellation of the commercial license and registration; the PIF may be struck off after 30 days of non-compliance. Under Law 35 of 2021, false declarations attract a separate criminal fine of PAB 250,000.

Director personal liability: The founder and council members are personally liable for the economic substance penalty; the Public Prosecutor may apply for a travel ban and attachment of the PIF’s bank accounts; if the empty shell is used to repatriate untaxed profits, a money-laundering conviction carries a 7 to 12-year sentence.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DGI may adjust the margin to the arm’s length benchmark and impose a penalty of 5% of the adjustment plus 10% annual interest; separate fine of PAB 25,000 for not filing Form DGI 243; serious violation is handled as a tax fraud offense with criminal fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who attest to false or non-existent transfer pricing documentation face civil liability for the full deficiency, professional disqualification for local service providers, and criminal imprisonment for 2 to 4 years for false corporate books. The court may also award restitution and prohibit departure from Panama until payment.
AML (Money Laundering and Financing of Proliferation)

Statutory sanction: Law 23 of 2015 fines: PAB 100,000 up to PAB 1,000,000 per infringement; the Panama Maritime Authority may suspend or revoke the special bunkering license; the funds and assets involved are seized; if criminal laundering is established, sentence is 5 to 12 years in El Renacer Prison.

Director personal liability: The Foundation Council, beneficial owner, and any compliance officer designated by the PIF may be arrested, subject to pretrial detention, and have their assets seized. The law requires the PIF to appoint a local AML officer; if none is appointed, every council member is deemed to bear personal criminal responsibility. Travel bans and Interpol red notices are standard in these investigations.
False Treaty Relief / Beneficial Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the full statutory 10% withholding, secondary penalty of 50% of the under-withheld tax, and non-deductibility of the reproducing dividend; if the false TRC was intentional, the fiscal offence is a crime punishable by a fine equal to three times the detriment and imprisonment up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: The council and any advisor who knowingly used a false TRC become jointly and severally liable for the tax, interest and penalties; they may face professional debarment, a travel ban during the administrative proceeding, and imprisonment for tax fraud. In the case of a non-resident beneficial owner using a Panama PIF purely to obtain treaty benefits, Panama treats the structure as an 'abusive conduit' and denies all treaty benefits under the BEPS minimum standard.
