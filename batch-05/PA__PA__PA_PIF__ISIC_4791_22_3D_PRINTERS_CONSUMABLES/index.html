Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI — General Directorate of Revenue), an integrated directorate of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (Ministry of Economy and Finance) of the Republic of Panama; taxpayer digital portal SETA (Sistema de Administración de Tributos) at https://seta.mef.gob.pa and official DGI guidance portal www.dgi.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) incorporated under Law 25 of 1995 obtains its tax identity as follows: (1) ensure valid registration of the foundation and its Pacto Fundacional at the Registro Público de Panamá, with the statutorily required resident secretary/agent; (2) file the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) inscription request through the SETA online portal or at a DGI service office using the official registration forms; (3) attach the certified copy of the registered foundation charter, identification documents (cédula or passport) of the foundation council members/directors, and proof of Panamanian fiscal domicile; (4) declare the intended economic activities (online retail of 3D printers and consumables) and choose the applicable ITBMS compliance (general or pequeño contribuyente); (5) upon RUC issuance, activate SETA electronic credentials and enrol for facturación electrónica (mandatory e-invoicing); (6) obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación from the relevant alcaldía before commencing sales; (7) pay the annual PIF registry fee (approximately USD 300) to the Public Registry, which is separate from DGI tax obligations. Non-resident founders and beneficiaries are not required to obtain personal Panamanian tax IDs; DGI requires designation of a locally resident taxpayer representative to receive electronic notifications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — the PIF is a separate juridical person taxed at entity level exclusively on Panama-source income under the territoriality principle (Código Fiscal Article 693). Panama-source retail profits from online sales to Panamanian consumers are subject to corporate ISR at the standard 25% rate; foreign-source income (export sales with offshore delivery, foreign passive investment income) falls outside Panamanian tax jurisdiction. The PIF is not eligible for US-style pass-through or check-the-box election in Panama and is not a Tax_Exempt_Zone vehicle; however, a PIF that holds assets and carries on no Panamanian commercial activity is fiscally dormant — it has no ISR or ITBMS registration duty, while remaining subject to the annual foundation registry fee.

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime. Available statutory elections relevant to a commercial PIF: (i) the simplified Pequeño Contribuyente ITBMS regime for eligible small taxpayers whose annual gross income does not exceed the statutory cap (approximately USD 150,000); (ii) corporate income-tax group consolidation for affiliated Panamanian companies (Law 8 of 2010); (iii) optional accelerated depreciation of fixed assets; (iv) the Multinational Company Headquarters (SEM) incentive regime for qualifying regional logistics/management platforms (not applicable to pure online retail); (v) simplified deemed-income methods available to small taxpayers. Panama has no hybrid-mismatch election; BEPS-type hybrid outcomes are constrained by general anti-abuse principles and the criminal tax-fraud provisions of Law 76 of 2019.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 0 — no statutory minimum economic-turnover threshold exists; every person habitually selling movable goods or rendering services in Panama must register for ITBMS prior to commencing operations. The simplified Pequeño Contribuyente regime applies to eligible taxpayers with annual gross income not exceeding approximately USD 150,000, functioning as a compliance-simplification threshold rather than an exemption. All fiscal thresholds are expressed in USD because the Panamanian Balboa is pegged 1:1 to the US dollar and USD is the circulating legal tender.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS 0% applies to (i) export of tangible goods: goods sold to a non-resident buyer and physically removed from Panamanian customs territory, evidenced by the validated customs export declaration and transport document; and (ii) export of services: services rendered from Panama to a non-resident recipient whose economic benefit is received exclusively outside Panama and whose consideration is paid from abroad, under the service-export zero-rating conditions codified by Law 8 of 2010. For the PIF's online retail of 3D printers and resin filaments, domestic deliveries to Panamanian consumers are taxable at 7%; shipments to foreign customers with a non-Panamanian delivery address qualify for 0% only where the customs export declaration (DUA or express-courier export manifest) correctly identifies the PIF (or its appointed customs agent) as exporter and the foreign buyer's payment is received on the foundation's bank account. Shipping and logistics services included in an export transaction follow the zero-rating of the underlying goods; standalone services delivered to local Panamanian customers remain taxable at 7%. Panama imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline because USD is legal tender and no exchange controls exist; the 180-day parameter below is the prudential evidentiary-retention benchmark for the documents substantiating the zero-rating claim.

  • DUA (Declaración Única Aduanera) validated by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of the goods from Panamanian customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the zero-rated e-invoice issued to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 payment receipt from the non-resident buyer
  • Signed export contract or purchase order identifying the non-resident buyer and applicable Incoterms
  • Express-courier export manifest where dispatches are made through FedEx/DHL/UPS or similar carriers

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) is phased in for all RUC holders: the PIF must obtain DGI e-invoicing credentials and issue each invoice through either the free SETA billing module or a DGI-certified service provider (PSFE), obtaining near-real-time online authorization (pre-validation) that embeds the DGI-consecutive numbering, electronic signature, and QR fiscal code. No physical fiscal timbrado remains for general-regime taxpayers. Sales data transmitted through Shopify/Stripe payment rails must be reconciled on the merchant-of-record e-invoice declared in the monthly ITBMS return (Form 209); payment gateway settlements do not substitute for e-invoicing.

DGI and ANA automatically cross-verify e-invoice data, export declarations, courier manifests, and bank remittance certificates; detected mismatches trigger desk audits and can lead to retrospective clawback of the zero-rating benefit. If the zero-rating claim is disallowed, DGI assesses ITBMS at the 7% rate on the gross export value, plus moratory interest and the statutory 10% late-payment surcharge. False or manufactured export documentation exposes the PIF to fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the omitted tax and, under Law 76 of 2019, criminal liability for tax fraud (defraudación tributaria) with custodial penalties of up to approximately five years, extended where the defrauded amount exceeds statutory thresholds or an organized false-invoicing scheme is used; additional consequences include disqualification of responsible directors and publication of the sanction. The exporter of record is jointly and severally liable for clawback where the goods are diverted back into Panamanian local consumption or where the foreign buyer's remittance certificate is fabricated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Unrestricted convertibility applies to both current-account and capital-account transactions. The U.S. dollar is legal tender and the Panamanian balboa is a nominal unit pegged 1:1 with no circulating notes. Profit remittances, foreign shareholder distributions, and capital repatriation require no exchange-control authorization. Only ordinary AML/KYC documentation, tax-formality, and OFAC/sanction screening obligations apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement exists. Panamanian law allows full retention, collection, and usage of USD export receipts, including on-line retail sales, without a statutory deadline or conversion percentage. This reflects the absence of a domestic monetary authority and the full-dollarization regime.

Central bank supervision: No central bank of issuance exists for Panama. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the de facto regulator for banking, market conduct, and foreign-exchange service providers. Monetary policy and dollar-liquidity management are functionally delegated to the U.S. Federal Reserve System because Panama is fully dollarized.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Panama does not issue a standalone Electronic Money Institution (EMI) license; therefore offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, or Mercury can only serve as ancillary payment-processing channels, not as the PIF's principal Panama-domiciled banking relationship. When used, the EMI account is typically hosted in a separate jurisdiction (U.S., U.K., Ireland, or Malta) and is treated by the SBP and Panamanian revenue authorities as a foreign financial account rather than a domestic bank account. The PIF must still maintain at least one operational account with an SBP-licensed commercial bank to satisfy legal domicile, tax ID, and administrative requirements under Income Tax Law and Law 25 of 1995.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the PIF's Panamanian operational account are executed via SWIFT MT103 using the 'OUR' charge option to ensure full credit. The USD funds may originate from a foreign shareholder, a founder, or an intercompany account. The wire narrative must state the economic purpose, e.g., 'Capital Contribution / Aporte de Capital – Non-Remunerated Equity Injection', and reference the PIF's registration number. Although Panama imposes no exchange-control filing, the receiving SBP-licensed bank will perform OFAC and internal compliance screening; therefore the originator's bank should provide a covered bank reference letter and underlying security documents. To tag funds cleanly, use the UETR (Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference) and place the PEP-eligible purpose code 'NCLG' (or the respective SWIFT 'Nature of Payment Code' for capital/equity) in the narrative. In case of USC correspondent-forwarded wires, the final credit is made in USD on a same-day or next-day basis.Outbound payments to 3D printer and consumable suppliers are cleared over the existing correspondent network using SWIFT MT103 for standard wire disbursements. For larger scheduled purchases, a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) under UCP600 is opened via the PIF's local bank through SWIFT MT700, with the beneficiary bank's account credited in USD. Trade settlement may also be supported by documentary collections (MT400) or an irrevocable Standby LC (MT799/MT760) when prepayment terms require assurance. Payment instructions must contain a clear purchase order number, proforma invoice reference, and the physical goods description to satisfy both bank AML and supplier-facing treasury controls. Panama is a fully dollarized jurisdiction; therefore the bulk of settlement uses U.S. dollar correspondent clearing via Fedwire/CHIPS, with the offshore catering for EUR/CNY settlement executed through the PIF's bank's foreign-currency branch or via multi-currency account narration.USD/EUR foreign-exchange forward contracts executed through an SBP-licensed Panamanian bank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement; because the local currency is USD, no local ringgit-style NDF is required.USD/CNY offshore NDFs transacted via an authorized international bank or money-broker in Panama or the United States, with the legal contract expressly governed by New York law or English law.USD/CNY cross-currency swaps (floating-to-fixed or floating-to-floating) for suppliers invoicing in CNY, provided the counterparty is a licensed bank or an A-rating financial institution and the exposure is designated as trade hedge.Zero-premium cost-reduction structures (collar, participating forward) for EUR and CNY payable flows, permissible as they represent genuine hedging of identified commercial invoices.Currency options (European-style USD call or USD put on EUR/CNY) obtained from an SBP-regulated bank, exercised only for the contracted invoice amount, and never used as a speculative position.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Panama does not issue a standalone Electronic Money Institution (EMI) license; therefore offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, or Mercury can only serve as ancillary payment-processing channels, not as the PIF's principal Panama-domiciled banking relationship. When used, the EMI account is typically hosted in a separate jurisdiction (U.S., U.K., Ireland, or Malta) and is treated by the SBP and Panamanian revenue authorities as a foreign financial account rather than a domestic bank account. The PIF must still maintain at least one operational account with an SBP-licensed commercial bank to satisfy legal domicile, tax ID, and administrative requirements under Income Tax Law and Law 25 of 1995.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 200,000: taxpayers with related-party transactions exceeding USD 200,000 in a fiscal year must file Annex 805 (Declaración de Precios de Transferencia) and maintain a full Local File. If cross-border related-party transactions reach USD 200,000, a Master File is also required.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime equivalent to the UAE or BVI. However, the DGI and Panama courts apply a substance-over-form doctrine to distinguish legitimate PIFs from artificial arrangements. To avoid recharacterization as a sham, the PIF must satisfy real commercial substance: physical or virtual presence, a registered agent in Panama, a board of council members that convenes, maintained accounting records, and a demonstrable business rationale for holding the online retail operations. Failure to demonstrate substance may lead to the nullification of the foundation and the imposition of full income tax, interest, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under Article 771-F of the Panama Tax Code is punishable by imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and a fine of 50% to 100% of the evaded tax. Civil liability includes the duty to pay all omitted tax plus monthly interest of 1.5% and a procedural surcharge of up to USD 5,000.

Director personal liability: Directors of the PIF, including the council members and the resident agent who signs the tax return, are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax. A fraudulent intent may lead to criminal proceedings against the natural-person director, the suspension of their residency status, a travel ban, and up to 5 years of imprisonment.
Shell Company Abuse / Sham Foundation

Statutory sanction: The DGI and courts may disregard the legal personality of the foundation under the substance-over-form doctrine. All income of the foundation is recharacterized as taxable income of the beneficial owner; Panamanian-source business income is taxed at 25%, and fines of 50% may be imposed. The foundation's registration may be cancelled under Law 25 of 1995.

Director personal liability: The protector and council members face personal civil liability for fraudulent conveyance. In serious cases, directors may be charged before the Panamanian criminal courts for fraud, resulting in pecuniary sanctions, real estate embargo, travel restriction, and imprisonment of up to 6 years when the scheme is used to evade foreign tax.
AML / KYC / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015 (amended), non-financial commercial entities that engage in online payment processing are obliged to establish AML protocols. Violations attract administrative fines from USD 10,000 up to USD 1,000,000, suspension of the commercial license, and the freezing of bank and payment accounts. Criminal money laundering is punishable by 5 to 10 years of imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute or facilitate a money-laundering transaction through the PIF are subject to criminal liability as principals. They may face civil forfeiture of personal assets, a travel ban blocking departure from Panama, and imprisonment from 5 to 10 years. The foundation's assets are subject to confiscation.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: A tax correction may be imposed by the DGI, adjusting taxable income to the arm's length amount. Applicable penalties include an addition to tax of 10% to 40% of the adjusted amount, plus monthly interest of 1.5%. Failure to file the documentation concurrently with the annual tax return leads to an autonomous fine of up to USD 30,000.

Director personal liability: The DGI may attribute the adjustment to the foundation only, but directors who knowingly prepare fraudulent transfer pricing documentation face personal liability for the additional tax and fines. Repeated non-compliance may result in criminal sanction for fraud, including a travel ban and imprisonment up to 5 years.
