Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Ley 25/1995 requires the Foundation Charter to state an initial endowment forming a separate patrimony, but fixes no statutory minimum paid-up amount. Market-standard capitalization for this DG forwarding PIF is US$10,000 in cash or identified in-kind assets, fully subscribed at incorporation; no escrow account is statutorily required, but the endowment must be determinable and allocated to the Foundation's separate patrimony in the Council's books.

Civil protection: SINAPROC hazardous-materials registration and inspection, mandatory spill contingency plan, and emergency contact declaration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas - MEF), Republic of Panama; official electronic portal and Sede Electronica: https://www.dgi.gob.pa. Customs executes export controls via the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA, https://www.aduana.gob.pa), while ocean/air forwarding operators are licensed by the Autoridad Marítima de Panamá (AMP) and Autoridad Aeronáutica Civil (AAC).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Constitute the Private Interest Foundation (PIF) under Law 25 of 1995, appoint a licensed Resident Agent, and complete registration at the Registro Público de Panamá. Step 2: Determine Panamanian-source activity; a passive PIF with only foreign-source DG forwarding income needs no RUC. Step 3: If the PIF invoices a Panamanian counterparty, operates bonded/re-export premises, or employs staff in Panama, register for the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) online via the DGI Sede Electronica: submit the certified Acta Constitutiva and Reglamento interno, certificate of foundation legal existence, Resident Agent appointment, identification of founder and Consejo de la Fundación, beneficial ownership declaration under Law 254 of 2021, local operational address, and bank/branch references. Step 4: The DGI validates the file, issues the RUC, activates fiscal obligations (ITBMS, withholdings, Anticipo advance payments), and enrolls the PIF in the electronic invoicing system. Step 5: For actual customs exports, obtain the ANA exporter registration and the AMP/AAC cargo agency and DG freight forwarder permits; such permits are independent of tax registration but required for zero-rating claims.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama has no check-the-box election and does not apply US pass-through treatment. The PIF is a hybrid civil-law entity: it is tax-transparent for foreign-source income (territorial exemption), yet is treated as an entity-level taxpayer for Panamanian-source income, including ITBMS collection. Available statutory choices: (a) passive foundation election - no RUC, zero Panamanian tax exposure; (b) active economic operation election - full RUC registration, ITBMS, advance payments, transfer-pricing file under Law 59/2014 and substance requirements; (c) special economic regime election - Zona Libre de Colón, Panama Pacifico (Ley 41/2007), free-trade zone or City of Knowledge regimes where DG warehousing/transshipment may qualify; (d) tax treaty/MLI election - Panama has deposited OECD MLI positions and does not enact domestic hybrid-mismatch rules, therefore hybrid instruments must be documented to avoid DGI re-characterization under territorial source rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Panama ITBMS imposes no general registration exemption: any person realizing taxable transfers of movable goods or services in Panama must register, with no minimum turnover. A simplified 'Pequeño Contribuyente' regime applies to taxpayers with annual gross receipts up to approximately B/. 40,000 (PAB/USD 40,000, as periodically adjusted); a DG forwarding enterprise will exceed this threshold and must file full monthly ITBMS returns.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports are zero-rated at 0% ITBMS where (i) the underlying goods leave Panamanian customs territory under an approved declaración de exportación or are re-exported from a bonded warehouse or Zona Franca; or (ii) the dangerous-goods forwarding services (international DG freight booking, multimodal transit coordination, hazmat stowage and segregation planning, documentation handling) are supplied for a foreign principal for effective use entirely outside Panama. The PIF must evidence foreign domicile of the client, indicate the service destination vis-à-vis the movement of freight, and receive settlement from foreign bank corridors. There is no DGI comfort ruling: the 0% rate is self-applied and must be sustained from documentary files. Input ITBMS incurred on warehousing, fuel, drayage and terminals in Panama attributable to exports is recoverable via the credit mechanism or a filed refund (devolución de crédito fiscal por exportadores) after submission of the export documentation set.

  • Declaración Única de Aduana (DUA) with export/reexport endorsement and ANA departure validation
  • Commercial export invoice (factura comercial) for the dangerous-goods consignment
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical departure from Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign bank SWIFT confirmation evidencing payment of the invoice from outside Panama
  • Shipper's Dangerous Goods Declaration (DGD) per IATA/IMDG with UN number, hazard class, packing group and emergency schedule references
  • Packing list, certificate of origin, insurance certificate, and hazmat stowage segregation plan
  • Bonded warehouse in-and-out records where re-export/transshipment is used

Electronic invoicing is mandatory for all registered taxpayers under the DGI Facturación Electrónica programme. Invoices must be generated as XML fiscal files using a qualified digital certificate through the DGI Sede Electronica or a certified technology provider; document numbering is pre-authorized, and each invoice must be transferred for validation, carry a readable fiscal seal (QR code plus verification code), and be reported in the ITBMS period. ANA clearance of exports is executed through the VUPA (Ventanilla Única de la Aduana) and the Declaración Única de Aduana; 'Papel de Seguridad' is allowed only as a contingency. Failure to transmit fiscal invoices in the approved channel causes nondeductibility for the customer, administrative fines, and temporary suspension of export credit refunds.

The DGI may audit any zero-rating claim within the statutory reassessment period and, upon deficiency, claw back the full ITBMS (7%) with statutory interest, late-payment surcharges and administrative fines, and may exclude the exporter from electronic invoicing, the RUC, and the ANA exporter registry. Knowingly fraudulent zero-rating documents attract customs-criminal penalties (contraband assessment, confiscation and corporate debarment), the Panamanian criminal tax fraud offense (imprisonment of one to five years for responsible directors), and personal exposure of the Consejo de la Fundación for 40% to 100% of the tax clawed back. Refunds obtained on false export packages are reversed retroactively to the original claim date, and the DGI may enforce collection against assets of the foundation and, under beneficial ownership rules of Law 254/2021, against the founder and ultimate beneficial owners where distributions were tainted by fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama has full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the U.S. dollar-based currency board-like regime. There are no foreign exchange surrender requirements, no capital controls, and no prior central bank authorization for profit distribution, dividend remittances, or cross-border loan repayments. A Private Interest Foundation conducting hazardous goods logistics may repatriate profits freely, subject only to documentary compliance with bank KYC/AML procedures and any applicable tax documentation under Panama's territorial tax system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Panama. All export receipts may be maintained in U.S. dollars or any freely convertible foreign currency, either domestically or through offshore accounts, without a reconversion timeline. The Ministry of Economy and Finance and the SBP do not require eligible export invoices to be converted into local currency because no separate local currency is issued for settlement.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama operates without a traditional central bank. The banking system is supervised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), which is the statutory regulator for all commercial banks and financial institutions. Currency issuance is effectively closed as Panama is fully dollarized and uses the U.S. dollar as legal tender; the nominal Balboa (PAB) is no longer issued as paper or coins in meaningful circulation. Fiscal agency tasks are carried out by the Banco Nacional de Panamá, a state-owned commercial bank, but monetary policy and lender-of-last-resort functions belong de facto to the U.S. Federal Reserve system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for a PIF in the dangerous goods freight segment. Panama does not possess an EU-style electronic money institution licensing regime; payment service entities operate under SBP-authorized payment system regulations and require a physical presence or resident agent. Offshore digital EMIs frequently lack direct U.S. dollar correspondent access in Panama and face correspondent banking restrictions when the underlying customer is a Panamanian PIF with opaque beneficial ownership. Most service providers will classify the entity as high-risk under AML/CFT rules and reject the relationship unless a full onshore banking subsidiary is opened.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial capital injections into the PIF's Panamanian operating or escrow account must flow via SWIFT from the contributor's bank using a bank wire / credit transfer with the beneficiary description: 'Capital Contribution to Private Interest Foundation - Fundación de Interés Privado'. As Panama does not impose mandatory purpose codes, the bank will accept the reference 'Aportacion de Capital / Capital Contribution' alongside the Foundation Pact and minutes of the Foundation Council. The clearable funds must originate from an account in the name of the identified contributor; third-party financing requires a written capital injection letter and evidence of source of wealth. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (Impuesto sobre la Renta, Fiscal Code Art. 715)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends or profit distributions paid from Panamanian-source income (Fiscal Code Art. 733); distributions representing foreign-source income are exempt.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Calendar-year taxpayers: Form 2417 due 15 March of the following year; taxpayers with a different fiscal year-end: three months after fiscal year-end. Form 2416 (detailed statements of operations) due by 30 April (or 4 months after year-end). Monthly advances are due the 15th of each following month.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panama's TP framework (Fiscal Code Art. 762-D and DGI Resolution 201-0527) requires a related-party informative return and contemporaneous documentation where reportable related-party transactions exceed B/. 1,000,000 per year, or where a counterparty is located in a jurisdiction with no tax or a preferential regime; the Panamanian currency is the balboa (B/.) at parity with the US Dollar.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations apply to PIFs carrying on 'relevant activities' (including distribution and service centres and maritime/air freight support services). The DG forwarding PIF must demonstrate an appropriate physical office, at least 3 qualified local employees (with payroll and social security registration), and locally incurred management/operation expenditure, and must file the annual ESR notification with the DGI by the same statutory date as the income tax return; failure to meet substance will result in recharacterization of income and heavy financial penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine equal to 100%–250% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of 2–6 years (Criminal Code Arts. 316–318); in addition, the DGI imposes the 10% late-payment surcharge and interest from the original due date.

Director personal liability: The Fraud Chamber of the Supreme Court may pierce the PIF's veil where the Foundation is used as a mere instrument of fraud; directors and Resident Agent are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, subject to seizure of personal assets, a prophylactic travel ban, and provisional arrest.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: DGI administrative fine of B/. 10,000 to B/. 100,000 under the Economic Substance Regime; denial of the territorial-source exemption, with deemed-sourced profits taxed at 25% plus a 30% penalty; potential cancellation of the PIF's tax ID and commercial license.

Director personal liability: Directors, the founder and the Resident Agent must demonstrate the place of effective management; upon failure, joint liability may extend to personal bank accounts, revocation of travel rights, and disqualification from holding directorships in Panama.
AML / CFT Compliance Breaches

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties from B/. 10,000 to B/. 1,000,000 per serious violation; the Superintendency of Banks and the IUCAF (Financial Analysis Unit) may freeze the PIF's bank accounts and revoke the special DG freight license.

Director personal liability: Criminal prosecution for money laundering (predicate offense: dangerous-goods smuggling) with imprisonment of 5 to 12 years; the court may impose preventive seizure of property, travel bans, and designation in the financial criminal registry; corporate veil-piercing permits recovery of civil damages against the actual managers.
