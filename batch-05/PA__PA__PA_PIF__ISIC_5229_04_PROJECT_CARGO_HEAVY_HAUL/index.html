Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; primary digital portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI at https://sede.dgi.gob.pa with institutional site https://www.dgi.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or domestic vehicle (including a Private Interest Foundation under Law 25 of 1995) must obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) before conducting taxable activities. Procedure: (1) pre-registration appointment at the DGI Sede Electrónica; (2) submission of foundation charter, public deed, authentic certificate of existence issued by the Registro Público de Panamá, identity documents of the Consejo de Fundación and the appointed agente residente; (3) evidence of registered legal address in Panama; (4) RUC application and simultaneous ITBMS registration; (5) issuance of RUC certificate together with tax ID credential and digital certificate enabling e-filing and electronic invoicing. A PIF may obtain a RUC as a non-commercial holding vehicle; however, if the PIF itself carries out Panamanian-source commercial activities, full income tax and ITBMS registration obligations apply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama has no US-style check-the-box election and no OECD hybrid mismatch election. The PIF is a separate legal person taxed only on Panamanian-source income under territorial rules; foreign-source income and capital gains are outside the Panamanian tax net absent remittance of funds to Panamanian territory in a taxable manner. Available statutory elections include: (i) entry into the Régimen Especial de Pequeños Contribuyentes for gross annual income not exceeding USD/PAB 150,000, which permits simplified ITBMS and income tax compliance; (ii) registration under the Panama-Pacifico Special Economic Area or a free-zone regime for logistics operators to obtain customs and tax privileges; (iii) maintenance of separately identified Panamanian-source and foreign-source revenue accounts in statutory records to substantiate territorial exemption; (iv) no entity-level election converts a PIF into a full taxable corporation unless the foundation's charter is amended to perform systematic commercial activities in Panama, in which case the foundation becomes a normal taxpayer under the Código Fiscal.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general statutory monetary threshold for ITBMS registration; any person transferring movable goods or providing services in Panamanian territory is subject to ITBMS and must register with the DGI. A special small-taxpayer regime (Régimen Especial de Pequeños Contribuyentes) applies where gross annual income does not exceed PAB/USD 150,000, allowing simplified quarterly payment instead of full monthly ITBMS filing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS applies at 0% to exports of goods and qualifying offshore services. Eligibility requirements: (1) tangible goods, including out-of-gauge heavy-lift project cargo, depart Panama under a customs export declaration evidencing physical exit; (2) services are rendered to non-resident customers for consumption or use outside Panama, such as international freight forwarding, heavy-lift logistics, and project cargo management performed predominantly abroad; (3) the transaction is documented with contracts, commercial invoices and shipping instruments demonstrating the cross-border nature; (4) the seller retains export documentation and foreign-currency remittance evidence. Input ITBMS incurred in respect of exempt or zero-rated export operations is creditable, and any residual monthly credit balance (saldo a favor) may be carried forward or claimed as a refund from the DGI.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduanera - DUA)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or through bill covering OOG heavy-lift carriage)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank certificate evidencing receipt of foreign funds)
  • Commercial Invoice and signed project cargo / freight services contract
  • Packing list, OOG surveyor or weight certificate, and dangerous goods declaration where applicable

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) through DGI-authorized software platforms integrated with the Sede Electrónica. Taxpayers transmit invoices in near-real time using XML with a fiscally certified digital certificate; the DGI assigns a sequential authorization code to each issued invoice, serving as digital clearance. There is no physical fiscal printer stamping. The e-invoicing mandate was rolled out progressively by taxpayer category from 2021 through 2025; logistics and project-cargo operators with active ITBMS registration must issue electronic invoices to Panamanian clients and may receive e-invoices from local suppliers to substantiate input credits. Non-resident service providers without a local establishment must appoint a local fiscal representative or use an approved e-invoicing operator to comply when invoicing Panamanian counterparties.

The DGI may scrutinize export zero-rating and refund claims within the statute of limitations of up to five years, extended for fraud cases. Where zero-rating is disallowed, the DGI will claw back the credited input ITBMS and issue unsettled assessments, plus a 10% monthly surcharge (recargo) capped by law, statutory interest of 1% per month on the unpaid balance, and / or fines of up to 100% of the omitted tax. Fraudulent export claims involving falsified invoices, false export declarations or simulated foreign counterparts give rise to criminal sanctions under both the Código Fiscal and Código Penal, including customs fraud and aggravated tax evasion penalties. Liability may extend jointly and severally to the foundation council and the resident agent if they knowingly participated in the use of false export documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both capital and current account; the economy is completely dollarized, so no central exchange-control authority or reference-rate apparatus exists. There are no statutory restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, capital repatriation, or liquidation proceeds for a Private Interest Foundation or corporate treasury holding USD accounts. Payouts are subject solely to general AML reporting thresholds (UAF/Official Financial Analysis Unit) and, where applicable, territorial taxation rules; foreign-source logistics income (heavy-haul freight, charter, demurrage) is freely remittable without source tax or prior approval. Non-USD exposures (EUR, CNY, GBP, COP) clear freely through commercial banks at market rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no mandatory surrender regime, no statutory timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio for export proceeds or service receipts (ISIC 5229 / NAICS 488510 project cargo logistics). Freight, charter hire, BAF, port handling, and OOG survey revenues may be retained in USD in any licensed Panama bank, applied to working capital, or routed offshore without central-bank authorization. Prudent practice under Decree 168 of 2013 is to retain underlying invoices and bank statements for at least five years to support transaction justification before the UAF if requested.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no traditional central bank. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panama (Superintendency of Banks of Panama, SBP) exercises statutory banking supervision, prudential oversight, and AML/CFT enforcement under Cabinet Decree 238 of 1970, Legislative Decree 2 of 2008, and Decree 168 of 2013 (as amended). The Banco Nacional de Panama operates as fiscal agent of the State and local clearing agent; monetary policy is effectively that of the U.S. Federal Reserve because the U.S. Dollar is the only circulating legal tender. The Panamanian Balboa exists solely as a nominal unit for fractional coins at fixed 1:1 parity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM for treasury-grade clearing, MEDIUM as supplementary collection channel. SBP Agreement No. 008-2021 created a permissive Electronic Money Entity (Empresas de Dinero Electronico) licensing regime and the SBP Fintech Innovation Hub offers a sandbox, but these licensed EMIs do not yet operate as primary correspondent clearing nodes for project cargo logistics. Offshore digital EMIs (UK/EU electronic-money institutions such as Wise, Currencycloud, or Airwallex) can legally collect and disburse funds in Panama only through their own correspondent relationships; they cannot hold a Panama nostro or clearing membership, and their use tends to lengthen KYC review at Tier-1 banks because of the layered payment chain. Recommended posture: use EMIs only for low-value disbursements to sub-contractors or surveyors with a clear invoice trail; retain SWIFT MT103 direct rails for all high-value milestone and LC-backed transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Approved protocol: (1) Sponsor or beneficial owner initiates SWIFT MT103 from an account held in their own name to the PIF's USD operating account at the chosen Panama Tier-1 bank; (2) Field 70 of the MT103 must state expressly 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO PRIVATE INTEREST FOUNDATION' plus reference to the specific foundation deed article authorizing the initial patrimony or subsequent capital increase; (3) for items above USD 50,000, the bank applies enhanced sanctions/wire screening, and the PIF must pre-supply a capital contribution certificate signed by the council and verified by legal counsel; (4) local Panama-to-Panama transfers may be routed through the SIP (Sistema de Pagos Interbancarios) RTGS operated by SBP clearing stations to reduce correspondent fees; (5) all injections are journaled to the foundation's internal equity/capital sub-account and applied only to project working capital, never commingled with personal receipts. Use of a documented internal capital ledger is essential for later distributable earnings calculations.Clearing rail suite: (A) SWIFT MT103 for freight payments, port handling, BAF, surveyor fees, and independent subcontractor settlement, citing purpose narrative 'Trade settlement for heavy-haul project cargo logistics services'; (B) MT202 COVER for correspondent funding in back-to-back documentary credit arrangements; (C) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) issued by the client's or charterer's bank and confirmed by the Panama Tier-1 bank for large heavy-lift vessel charters or specialised equipment purchases; (D) Documentary collections (DP/DA) with drafts for contractual milestone amounts where LC terms are unavailable; (E) ISO 20022 structured payment details for subcontractor sweeps above USD 250,000, accompanied by a signed statement of trade value and, when requested, HS classification aligned with ISIC 5229 / NAICS 488510. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM for treasury-grade clearing, MEDIUM as supplementary collection channel. SBP Agreement No. 008-2021 created a permissive Electronic Money Entity (Empresas de Dinero Electronico) licensing regime and the SBP Fintech Innovation Hub offers a sandbox, but these licensed EMIs do not yet operate as primary correspondent clearing nodes for project cargo logistics. Offshore digital EMIs (UK/EU electronic-money institutions such as Wise, Currencycloud, or Airwallex) can legally collect and disburse funds in Panama only through their own correspondent relationships; they cannot hold a Panama nostro or clearing membership, and their use tends to lengthen KYC review at Tier-1 banks because of the layered payment chain. Recommended posture: use EMIs only for low-value disbursements to sub-contractors or surveyors with a clear invoice trail; retain SWIFT MT103 direct rails for all high-value milestone and LC-backed transactions.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Panama general corporate dividend withholding: 5% on dividends paid from Panama-source taxable income; 10% on distributions from non-taxable or exempt income to recipients resident abroad (Fiscal Code Arts. 302, 302-A). PIF special rule: beneficiary distributions from foreign-source endowment are exempt from all Panamanian tax, inheritance tax, and gift tax, resulting in an effective 0% Panamanian WHT where the underlying assets are outside Panama.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 12,000,000 (Panama's official currency is the Balboa at 1:1 parity with the U.S. dollar) in annual gross revenue, or related-party transactions exceeding USD 3,000,000 in a fiscal year, triggers mandatory Master File and Local File documentation under Law 58 of 2017, Executive Decree 24 of 2018, and DGI Resolution 201-2974.

Economic substance scope: Law 254 of 2021 (Regulated by Decree 163 of 2022) applies the OECD economic substance standard to all Panamanian legal vehicles, including PIFs. The project-cargo heavy-haul logistics scope (ISIC 5229 / distribution and logistics services) is a 'relevant activity' requiring: local management and board meetings in Panama, a physical office, adequate qualified staff, and core income-generating activity in Panama. A PIF that is tax-resident and compliant in a foreign jurisdiction may satisfy the doctrine through verified foreign tax residence; otherwise, the PIF must annually file substance information and pay a substance fee. Failure renders the vehicle liable to recharacterization, information exchange, and fines up to USD 1,000,000 for serious infringements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Offshore Fraud)

Statutory sanction: Law 70 of 2019 criminalizes tax evasion with imprisonment of three (3) to five (5) years for simple evasion and five (5) to seven (7) years for aggravated evasion, plus fines of five (5) to ten (10) times the evaded tax, closure of the establishment, and forfeiture of instrumentalities. Fiscal Code Arts. 705-707 additionally impose civil penalties of 100% to 300% of the omitted tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and the foundation's registered agents are jointly and severally liable for restitution of evaded taxes, may be barred from holding corporate office for up to 10 years, and face personal imprisonment under Law 70 of 2019; preventative travel bans (inhibicion de salida) and asset freezes are routinely ordered during criminal investigation.
Shell Company / ESR Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Law 254 of 2021 and Decree 163 of 2022 impose graduated administrative fines from USD 10,000 up to USD 1,000,000 for failure to prove economic substance, fraudulent substance declarations, or incomplete annual substance filings; the DGI may also strike the foundation from the register and automatically exchange its information with foreign tax authorities under the Multilateral Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance.

Director personal liability: Directors and the foundation council are personally liable for the substance filing obligations; knowing participation in a sham vehicle triggers civil liability for all unpaid taxes and penalties, suspension from acting as director in Panama, and criminal exposure for false declarations before the DGI.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Law 23 of 2015 (AML), Criminal Code Art. 254: money laundering carries imprisonment of five (5) to ten (10) years, increased to ten (10) to fifteen (15) years in aggravated circumstances, fines of up to USD 10,000,000, asset forfeiture, and administrative sanctions by the Intendencia de Supervision y Fiscalizacion; the freight logistics sector is an obliged non-financial entity required to maintain customer due diligence and suspicious-transaction reporting.

Director personal liability: Directors are directly indictable as co-authors for laundering through the PIF; personal asset freezes, five-year travel bans, extradition exposure, and civil restitution orders apply; the corporate veil is pierced as a matter of course where the foundation is found to be a conduit for illicit cargo payments.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Fiscal Code Art. 762-K: fines of USD 10,000 to USD 30,000 for non-submission of transfer-pricing documentation, plus adjusted assessments and interest; the DGI may disregard the transaction and re-characterize the PIF's profit at the 25% head-rate with a 10% to 30% accuracy surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve related-party contracts knowing they depart from arm's length are jointly liable for the resulting tax adjustment and fines; repeated non-cooperation constitutes tax fraud under Law 70 of 2019 with personal custodial exposure.
Non-Filing and Withholding Agent Defaults

Statutory sanction: Late-filing fine of USD 50 to USD 500; late-payment surcharge of 10% for the first month plus 1.5% per month (capped at 30%); moratory interest of 1% per month; withholding agents that fail to remit WHT are liable for 100% of the unwithheld amount plus penalties of 25% to 100%.

Director personal liability: Under Fiscal Code Art. 124, directors and officers are personally and severally liable for unremitted withholdings, regardless of the foundation's insolvency; DGI enforcement includes personal asset attachment, travel restrictions, and disqualification from managing Panamanian entities; criminal liability arises where the default is fraudulent.
False or Fraudulent Declarations

Statutory sanction: Fiscal Code Art. 713 and Law 70 of 2019: fines of 100% to 300% of the omitted tax; aggravated tax evasion imprisonment of five (5) to seven (7) years; confiscation of assets derived from the fraud; and automatic publication of the sanction in the Official Gazette.

Director personal liability: Signing officers and the registered agent face personal criminal prosecution, subsidiary liability for all fiscal debts, debarment from practicing as agent in Panama, and, for forged foreign TRCs, international mutual legal assistance requests resulting in extradition.
