Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direccion General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance, Panama; online filing portal: Sede Electronica DGI (https://sedeelectronica.dgi.gob.pa) and institutional website www.dgi.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation must first be validly registered under Law 25 of 2007, appoint a resident agent, and file a RUC (Registro Unico de Contribuyentes) application with DGI. The application requires the certified foundation deed, resident agent acceptance, identification and due diligence documents of all officers and initial beneficiaries, and a local fiscal address. DGI issues a single TIN (RUC number) which simultaneously registers the entity for both income tax and ITBMS (VAT) purposes. No approval quota or tax clearance certificate is required before registration, but the agent must confirm compliance with Panama's tax secrecy and AML requirements.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation must first be validly registered under Law 25 of 2007, appoint a resident agent, and file a RUC (Registro Unico de Contribuyentes) application with DGI. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not maintain a check-the-box or entity-classification election comparable to the US Treasury check-the-box regime. Taxation follows a statutory territorial source rule; domestic-source benefits, rights, and property located in Panama are taxable at entity level, while foreign-source income of the Private Interest Foundation is generally outside Panama's income tax. The foundation charter may expressly allocate beneficial rights and distribution powers; however, such provisions must not produce a tax result contrary to Panama's controlled-foreign-corporation, transfer pricing, or OECD hybrid mismatch conventions applicable to international business activity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Panama lacks a general fiscal turnover threshold for ITBMS registration; any person habitually selling movable goods or providing services within the national territory is required to register at the RUC, subject to DGI enforcement. A simplified small-taxpayer regime is available for individuals and single-owner entities with annual gross income below the threshold periodically published by DGI (currently B/.100,000 for certain service providers, with goods sellers assessed on actual operations); the threshold does not apply to exporters, who must fully register as regular ITBMS taxpayers to claim zero-rated export treatment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exports is available to registered ITBMS taxpayers selling movable goods, including ginned raw cotton bales, when the goods are physically exported from Panama under customs control within the statutory term, ownership and risk pass to a foreign buyer, and the payment is remitted from abroad in accordance with Panama's monetary requirements. An ITBMS-rated taxpayer that is not also involved in domestic taxable sales may claim a credit/refund of input ITBMS paid on purchases directly related to the exported cotton; the refund is processed by DGI upon submission of a formal refund request with the export customs declaration and proof of re-export. Offshore services, manual handling, and brokerage commissions performed abroad and benefiting foreign persons are also outside Panamanian territorial tax if effectively rendered offshore.

  • Panama Customs Export Declaration (Declaracion Unica de Aduana - DUA)
  • Commercial Invoice issued through the authorized electronic invoicing system with a 0% ITBMS export code
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing departure of the goods from Panamanian customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire evidence (including SWIFT MT103) proving payment credited to the PIF's Panamanian bank account from a non-resident purchaser
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (when required by the destination customs authority)

Panama operates a transactional electronic invoice system under DGI Resolution No. 201-884 of 2015, as amended. Taxpayers registered as ITBMS agents must issue invoices through DGI-authorized electronic billing providers using digital certificates and standardized XML formats. Invoices are transmitted to DGI in near real-time, assigned an authorization number and digital fingerprint (hash), and are subject to DGI system validation. Physical export documents must refer to the corresponding authorized electronic invoice number. The export transaction must additionally clear the customs single window (VUCPA/ANA) with a zero-rated ITBMS code; absence of valid DGI digital authorization blocks the export declaration's release.

DGI and the National Customs Authority may jointly investigate zero-rated export declarations for documentary fraud, circular payments, or artificial routing. Failure to substantiate the export within 180 days, or any misrepresentation of the foreign purchaser, export value, or quantity, triggers retrospective reversal of the zero-rate, assessment of ITBMS at 7%, interest, late-payment surcharges, and fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded. Criminal tax fraud penalties may also apply to false customs declarations and money laundering indicators involving simulated cotton-bale exports; export privileges and the PIF's tax registration may be suspended or canceled upon repeated violations.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The US dollar is fully convertible without exchange controls. Both current account and capital account transactions are free from mandatory surrender or licensing requirements. Remittance of profits, dividends, and proceeds of liquidation by a Private Interest Foundation (PIF) is unrestricted under Law 25 of 1995, subject only to standard tax transparency and Due Diligence reporting to designated authorities in accordance with the FATCA/CRS framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists. Export receipts may be retained in USD in any local or offshore banking institution recognized by the SBP. Payments in other currencies are freely convertible into USD at commercial bank rates without central bank approval.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Superintendency of Banks of Panama, SBP); no central bank exists under the Panamanian Constitution, with the US dollar serving as legal tender since 1904 (Decree Law 1 of 1904) and no domestic monetary issuance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for wholly offshore digital EMIs. Panama has enacted a formal fintech licensing framework (Ley 112 de 2019 and SBP Rule 1-2021 for Payment System Service Providers). Only onshore-licensed or SBP-recognized financial institutions may open resident corporate accounts. Offshore EMIs without a Panamanian license cannot legally provide settlement rails, and their accounts are generally rejected by SBP-regulated banks for AML/CFT red flags.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire routing is executed via SWIFT MT103/TT through Panama's global correspondent network, primarily with US Fedwire and CHIPS correspondents. The preferred purpose code is '1302 – Imports/Debits pertaining to commercial trade' for funding of cotton procurement, or '0302 – Advances on exports' when the funds originate from a foreign buyer in prepayment. Verification includes exact matching of the PIF's corporate name, tax ID (RUC or Panamanian Legal Entity ID), and a bank reference confirming the underlying contract reference number. Potential cross-border capital injections from a foreign affiliate must be documented as a shareholder loan or contributions to the Foundation's patrimony, with a definitive agreement filed to avoid misclassification as lending under SBP Law 9 of 1998.Outbound trade settlements shall route via SWIFT MT103, Documentary Collection (DP/DA), or Direct Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) issued by a Panamanian bank through its correspondent network. For cotton bales from Brazil or the US, the preferred rail is Irrevocable, Confirmed LC with payment at sight; for intra-group or invoicing purposes, MT103 with reference to the exact invoice and container number is accepted. The rails must comply with the Panama Banking Law, Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600), and SBP Anti-Money Laundering guidelines requiring the beneficiary name, address, and NIC/PIF identifier. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory dividend withholding tax on distributions characterized as dividends by a Panama corporation. A reduced 5% rate applies where profits distributed have already been subjected to Panama corporate income tax. A Private Interest Foundation's distributions to beneficiaries are not dividends under Foundation Law No. 25 of 1995 and are exempt from Panama dividend withholding tax when the distributed assets and income are foreign-source. Distributions out of Panamanian-source income realized by a PIF through a taxable activity may be recharacterized and subjected to the applicable 10% or 5% rule.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three calendar months after the fiscal-year end. For calendar-year filers, the annual return is due no later than 31 March of the following year. A non-calendar fiscal year must be pre-approved by the DGI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 3,000,000 / B/.3,000,000 aggregate annual value of controlled cross-border transactions triggers the mandatory Local File. The Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue is USD 100,000,000 / B/.100,000,000 or more. Below the thresholds, contemporaneous arm's length support is still mandatory in principle, and DGI may request benchmark and functional documentation for any related-party transaction regardless of turnover.

Economic substance scope: Panama has enacted economic substance requirements applicable to entities carrying on relevant activities such as pure holding, financing, leasing, shipping, intellectual property holding, and distribution and service centers. A Private Interest Foundation used as an export broker for raw cotton bales must demonstrate, if challenged by DGI or the Financial Intelligence Unit, that it has adequate premises, a qualified local board or officer structure, proper books and records, and real risk-management control commensurate with the value of the cotton trade. A PIF that limits its activity to passive ownership or to contracting outside Panama may still rely on its foreign-source characterization, but it cannot simultaneously claim Panamanian operational substance and Panamanian-source trading profit while failing to maintain substance records. Failure of the economic substance doctrine raises a rebuttable presumption that the PIF is a mere shell, causing profits to be reclassified as taxable Panamanian-source income and exposing the foundation to penalty assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Mischaracterization of Panamanian-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the omitted corporate income tax at 25%, plus statutory default interest at 10% per annum, plus a penalty of not less than 50% and up to 100% of the evaded tax under Panama's Fiscal Code. Filing a false declaration may additionally constitute criminal tax fraud, with fines and imprisonment from two to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the resident agent who knowingly sign or support false Panamanian-source characterization can be held civilly liable for the unpaid tax if the foundation's assets are diverted before settlement. Panama courts may apply veil-piercing where the foundation is an instrumentality of its settlor or directors, enabling attachment of personal assets, freezing orders, travel bans, and imprisonment for criminal tax fraud.
Sham Foundation, Shell Status, and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: DGI may disregard the foundation's separate existence, reallocate the export revenue to the beneficial owner, and impose full corporate tax plus penalties. Failure to satisfy economic substance requirements can lead to administrative fines, cancellation or denial of tax-resident benefits, and reporting of the foundation to the Financial Intelligence Unit as a shell company.

Director personal liability: Directors and foundation council members who approve sham transactions may be personally liable to creditors and tax authorities for losses caused by fraudulent conveyances. Civil liability includes restitution of misappropriated funds, disqualification from acting as a director in Panama, and potential criminal liability for conspiracy to commit tax fraud. Travel bans and provisional asset seizures can be imposed during the investigation.
Money Laundering and Trade-Based AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Violations of Law No. 23 of 2015, as amended, carry severe administrative fines, mandatory reporting to the Financial Intelligence Unit, and criminal prosecution of money laundering with penalties of up to ten years' imprisonment and fines scaled to the value laundered. The Superintendency of Banks and the FIU can also impose separate compliance penalties on banks and trust service providers involved.

Director personal liability: Directors, foundation council members, and beneficial owners face direct criminal prosecution for money laundering, customs fraud, and smuggling. Panama courts may order preventive detention during the investigation, travel bans, freezing of personal and corporate assets, confiscation of proceeds, and civil restitution to affected financial institutions. A director who fails to implement the foundation's risk-based AML policy, including the maintenance of beneficial ownership records, is separately liable for regulatory penalties.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: DGI may adjust the Panama-source taxable income to the arm's length result and assess the 25% corporate income tax deficiency, together with statutory interest and a penalty of 40% to 100% of the understatement for willful failure to comply with transfer pricing documentation obligations. The TP adjustment may also trigger secondary dividend withholding tax at the 10% or 5% rate on the recharacterized distribution to the foreign shareholder or beneficiary.

Director personal liability: The directors and officers who design the intercompany pricing and sign the annual declaration can be jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency if the foundation is insolvent or its assets have been stripped. Panamanian courts may authorize attachment of the directors' personal assets and a judicial travel ban, and criminal tax sanctions can be imposed where the false TP documentation is used as a deliberate instrument of evasion.
