Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas — official taxpayer portal and electronic services platform: https://www.dgi.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) obtains its RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) by filing the DGI RUC application through a resident legal representative or licensed Panamanian attorney. Required documents include the registered foundation deed, proof of registration at the Public Registry of Panama, identification of directors and guardians, and evidence of the resident agent appointment. No commercial operating license is required for passive or offshore e-commerce asset management; RUC registration is mandatory only if the PIF conducts Panamanian-source taxable activities. DGI issues the RUC certificate and, where applicable, the taxpayer's ITBMS registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box or deemed-payment election. The PIF's tax status is fixed by Law No. 25 of 1995 and the territorial source rules of the Panama Tax Code. The foundation deed may elect to limit the entity's purpose to passive asset holding and private benefit distribution, which preserves territorial non-exposure on foreign-source income. A PIF may also elect the simplified small-contributor ITBMS regime if its annual taxable turnover falls below the statutory threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general de minimis monetary threshold for RUC registration; any person engaged in taxable transfers of goods or services in Panama must register as an ITBMS taxpayer from the first taxable transaction. A small-taxpayer simplified regime is available for annual taxable turnover not exceeding PAB/USD 36,000, subject to DGI approval and conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% ITBMS applies to tangible goods physically exported from Panama and to services supplied to offshore recipients or used entirely outside Panama. The exporter must hold a valid RUC, register as an exporter with DGI and the customs authority, demonstrate that the goods left Panama before the invoice due date, and evidence the transaction's economic substance with shipping, payment, and foreign-recipient documentation. Domestic delivery in Panama or consumption of services within Panama disqualifies zero-rating and attracts the 7% ITBMS.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) accepted by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas de Panamá
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Panama
  • Commercial Export Invoice describing the pet products, quantities, and foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/foreign-exchange evidence showing collection of export proceeds from outside Panama

Panama mandates electronic invoicing through the DGI's Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (electronic invoicing system) for most business categories, including digital retail. Invoices must be generated through an authorized technology provider using XML/JSON APIs, validated and cleared by the DGI in near-real time, and carry a unique DGI approval code or digital stamp. Over-the-counter sales to final consumers may alternatively be supported by DGI-approved fiscal POS equipment with real-time transmission of transaction data.

DGI audits export zero-rating claims by cross-matching customs export declarations with transport documents, bank remittances, and e-invoices. Any unsupported, overstated, or fraudulent zero-rating claim is reversed and reassessed at 7% ITBMS, with default interest and late-payment surcharges. Panamanian law also imposes monetary penalties ranging from 25% to 200% of the omitted tax for false declarations, and criminal tax fraud charges may be brought where intentional evasion is established, carrying imprisonment and closure of the registrant's RUC.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized and imposes no exchange controls. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are effectively fully convertible; the Balboa is a unit of account at a fixed 1:1 parity with the USD and has no independent exchange rate. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repatriation require no prior administrative or exchange-control authorization. The only practical hurdles are bank AML/KYC documentation, source-of-funds verification, and any Panamanian tax withholding obligations applicable to Panama-source income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export proceeds. E-commerce export receipts may be retained in USD or any freely convertible currency. Banks may nevertheless require export documentation and a lawful source explanation for large or frequent USD deposits under AML/CFT regulations.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank. The banking supervisory authority is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) manages fiscal aspects of the dollarized economy. The US dollar is legal tender under Panama's monetary system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for use of global PSPs and sponsored cross-border treasury tools; Low for operating a standalone offshore EMI in Panama. Under Decreto Ley 9 de 1998, only SBP-licensed banks may lawfully take deposits or clear USD payments inside Panama. Offshore digital EMIs do not receive direct SBP clearing membership; they may operate only through a licensed Panamanian bank as sponsor or processor. For this PIF, Stripe and PayPal may serve as merchant-acquiring and settlement rails, with settlement payouts in USD to a Panamanian operational account, but they cannot substitute for a local bank account or provide full treasury custody.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial endowment and subsequent capital injections must be wired through SWIFT MT103, or Fedwire/CHIPS for USD-based originating banks, directly to the PIF's account in Panama. The wire beneficiary field must show the exact PIF registered name and account number; the reference must state 'Capital Contribution / Foundation Endowment'. If the bank's system requires a purpose code, use the ISO-compatible capital-transfer code, otherwise use the free-text description 'Equity contribution to a Panama Private Interest Foundation'. The receiving bank will verify that the remitter is the founder, contributor, or an authorized representative; the signatories match the Foundation Council resolution; the source-of-funds declaration is completed; and the initial funds are received in the Capital Escrow Account if so designated. After KYC activation and registered-agent confirmation, operational liquidity may be swept via internal bank transfer to the Operational Account. No Panamanian exchange-control permit is required.Supplier and trade payments should be executed from the PIF's USD operational account using SWIFT MT103 for direct wholesale payments, with MT202 cover if interbank settlement is required. USD clearing will occur through the Panamanian bank's US correspondent network via Fedwire or CHIPS.
