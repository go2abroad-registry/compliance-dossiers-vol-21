Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Law 25 of 1995 requires every foundation to declare a patrimonio fundacional (initial endowment) sufficient to achieve its stated purpose; the standard professional minimum is USD 10,000. Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; official taxpayer e-filing portal 'Sede Electrónica de la DGI' (e-Tax 2.0) at https://sede.dgi.gob.pa; export and customs controls administered by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) through the SICE single-window platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Private Interest Foundation (PIF) under Panama Law 25 of 1995 before a Panamanian notary and Registered Agent, and register the Foundation Charter in the Registro Público de Panamá. Step 2: If the PIF will conduct domestic commercial, industrial, or export manufacturing activity (ISIC 3250), obtain an 'Aviso de Operación' (business notice) from the Ministry of Commerce and Industries (MICI) describing the exact surgical consumables manufacturing activity. Step 3: File the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) application with the DGI either at local revenue offices or through the Sede Electrónica, submitting: certified copy of the Foundation Charter, public registry certificate (personería jurídica), notarized registered-agent appointment, MICI Aviso de Operación, and evidence of the local bank account. Step 4: The DGI assigns the statutory 12-digit RUC and activates the taxpayer for Income Tax, Advance Tax (pago directo), and ITBMS obligations. Step 5: Complete the electronic invoicing habilitation under DGI Resolution No. 201-5420 of 2020, including issuance of digital signature certificates and DGI invoice folio authorization. A foreign entity without a Panamanian commercial presence must still obtain a non-resident RUC before deriving any Panama-source taxable income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box election and has no hybrid-mismatch election. The statutory elections available to the PIF and its local export manufacturing operation include: (i) election of the Multinational Company Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007 for qualifying regional services, attracting preferential payroll tax treatment and a 0% income tax rate on qualifying SEM revenues; (ii) election of the Maquila Regime under Law 12 of 1995 for manufacturing, assembly, and export operations, granting exemption from ITBMS and customs import duties; (iii) election to operate as a licensed free-zone enterprise (e.g., Panama Pacifico, Ciudad del Saber, Colon Free Trade Zone) under Law 32 of 2011; (iv) election of the simplified small-taxpayer regime (Régimen de Tributación Simplificada) where annual gross revenue does not exceed B/.36,000; (v) elective advance pricing agreements (APAs) for transfer pricing certainty under Law 41 of 2007; and (vi) the elective possibility of consolidating Panamanian group income where the local operating subsidiary is wholly owned by the foundation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory ITBMS registration applies to any person habitually transferring taxable movable goods or rendering taxable services in Panama, without a general registration turnover threshold. The statutory small-taxpayer simplification ceiling is B/.36,000 (PAB, pegged 1:1 to USD) of annual gross revenue; a person with annual taxable turnover exceeding B/.36,000 must register, charge 7% ITBMS, and file monthly or quarterly declarations. Export-only producers may register voluntarily to support zero-rating of exports and recovery of input ITBMS credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods and services rendered for use outside Panama are zero-rated (0% ITBMS) under the Código Fiscal, provided the supplier is a registered ITBMS taxpayer and the goods are physically shipped out of Panamanian territory. The PIF's surgical suture assembly operation qualifies when: (i) the buyer is a non-resident; (ii) legal title and physical risk pass outside Panama; (iii) shipment is supported by an ANA customs export declaration; and (iv) export proceeds are credited to the taxpayer's Panamanian bank account within the statutory term. Manufacturing-for-export under the Maquila Regime (Law 12 of 1995) or within a licensed free zone is simultaneously exempt from ITBMS and customs import duties on inputs such as polyglycolic acid raw material, needles, packaging, and assembly consumables. Excess ITBMS input credits generated by zero-rated exports are available for offset against domestic output tax or refundable through the DGI fiscal-credit mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DUE/DUM) filed electronically with the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) through the SICE single window
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill for air cargo) evidencing export shipment from Panama to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas / SWIFT bank credit advice) proving receipt of export proceeds in Panama for ITBMS zero-rating and territorial income tax substantiation
  • Commercial export invoice and packing list fully matching the customs declaration, including ISIC 3250 product descriptions and batch numbers
  • Certificate of origin and technical/regulatory file for the surgical suture products if required by the destination medical-device regulator

Mandatory electronic invoicing under DGI Resolution No. 201-5420 of 4 December 2020, phased in by taxpayer RUC ranges. The local manufacturing entity must issue electronic invoices, electronic credit/debit notes, and transmission slips using the DGI-approved XML schema. The system requires: (i) prior DGI 'habilitación' and authorization of invoice folio numbering; (ii) use of a DGI-registered digital signature (Firma Electrónica); (iii) near-real-time transmission and automated validation by the DGI Sede Electrónica with an acknowledged receipt (acuse de recibo) before the document becomes fiscally valid; and (iv) retention of all fiscal tax records for at least 5 years. Panama does not operate a physical fiscal-stamp or prepaid clearance mechanism in the style of Mexico or Colombia, but the DGI performs immediate automated validation and may suspend invoice numbering upon detected non-compliance; customs export-related documents are separately cleared through the ANA SICE single window.

The DGI may audit export zero-rating claims within a general assessment prescription of 5 years, extended to 7 years in cases of omission, false declarations, or fraud. An invalid or fraudulent zero-rating claim triggers: (i) retrospective clawback of the uncollected 7% ITBMS; (ii) moratory interest at 10% per annum on the underpaid tax; (iii) administrative fines generally ranging between 50% and 200% of the evaded tax depending on gravity and recidivism, plus fines for irregular invoicing; (iv) customs penalties and forfeiture or suspension of maquila/free-zone tax benefits; and (v) criminal liability for customs and tax fraud, including imprisonment for directors, officers, or the foundation's authorized agent where false export declarations have been certified, in accordance with the Código Fiscal, Ley 70 de 2019, and the Código Penal. The exporting taxpayer carries the burden of proof, including physical traceability of exported goods, shipping and insurance documentation, and SWIFT receipts, across the full retention and prescription horizon.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy: the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) exists only as a nominal accounting unit pegged 1:1 to the USD, with no separate paper currency. All capital account and current account transactions are freely convertible and there are no exchange controls or restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or principal repatriation for companies or Private Interest Foundations (PIFs). Remittances may be effected in USD without SBP authorization or special documentary filing, provided the underlying transactions comply with anti-money-laundering (AML) reporting thresholds and bank internal policies. Repatriation hurdles are effectively nil for lawful funds; the only practical constraint is satisfying bank KYC due diligence to prove lawful source and traceability of funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Panama. Exporters, including manufacturers of surgical sutures under ISIC 3250, are permitted to retain 100% of export receipts in USD or any freely convertible currency in their designated Panamanian banking accounts. There is no requirement to surrender foreign currency to any central bank or public authority, and no obligation to declare settlement of export collections beyond standard SBP financial reporting executed by the commercial bank.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as bank regulator; Banco Nacional de Panamá (BNP) serves as fiscal agent of the Republic of Panama. There is no traditional issuing or monetary-policy Central Bank. The National Treasury and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) execute fiscal functions within a fully dollarized monetary framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Panamanian law does not recognize offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) licenses as a substitute for a banking license issued by the SBP. A foreign fintech or EMI may not engage in deposit-taking, payment account issuance, or USD clearing settlement without obtaining either a General Banking License or a specific license under the SBP fintech regulatory sandbox. Offshore EMIs are legally constrained to operating through a licensed Panamanian bank as their settlement agent; this limits their viability as an autonomous treasury clearing rail. Their practical utility is confined to front-end payment APIs supported by an underlying SBP-regulated bank, with increased AML/KYC friction and no guarantee of acceptance by local clearing institutions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Funds shall be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 message types (depending on intermediary usage), with the payment referencing 'Capital Contribution to Private Interest Foundation PA_PIF, Grantor-Foundation Agreement dated XXXX, Public Registry Folio No. XXX'. Since Panama imposes no exchange controls, no central bank formality is required for the receipt of USD; however, the receiving commercial bank will require: (a) the sending bank’s KYC confirmation of the remitter; (b) the foundation charter and capital resolution approving the injection; (c) a statement of intended use for the capital (purchase of suture-needle assembly machinery, raw material procurement, payroll); (d) evidence of the lawful source of funds supported by prior account statements; and (e) for payments from US banks, a compliant OFAC-filtered instruction and the applicable SWIFT purpose code. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A Private Interest Foundation is not subject to statutory audit merely by its status under Law 25 of 1995. A mandatory independent audit arises only where (i) the Foundation Charter (Reglamento) expressly requires an external audit, or (ii) the foundation conducts Panamanian-source commercial or industrial operations and its gross annual revenue or total assets exceed the DGI threshold of USD 3,000,000, requiring audited financial statements by a licensed Panamanian CPA. A surgical-suture assembly operation located outside Panama remains outside the territorial tax net and does not trigger a Panamanian statutory audit.
