Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministro de Economía y Finanzas de Panamá; electronic tax portal: Sistema Integrado de Gestión de Recaudación (SIGER) at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity, including a Private Interest Foundation (PIF) acting as a B2B cross-border marketplace, must first appoint and maintain a duly licensed resident agent or legal representative in Panama. The resident agent submits the registration application through SIGER, attaching a notarized copy of the foundation deed or certificate of incorporation, proof of the resident agent's appointment, identification documentation of the founder and final beneficial owners, and a sworn declaration of business activities. The DGI assigns a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) and, if taxable activities are carried on, registers the entity for the Impuesto a la Transferencia de Bienes Muebles y Servicios (ITBMS). Registration must be completed prior to the commencement of any commercial activity in Panama.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign entity, including a Private Interest Foundation (PIF) acting as a B2B cross-border marketplace, must first appoint and maintain a duly licensed resident agent or legal representative in Panama. The resident agent submits the registration application through SIGER, attaching a notarized copy of the foundation deed or certificate of incorporation, proof of the resident agent's appointment, identification documentation of the founder and final beneficial owners, and a sworn declaration of business activities. The DGI assigns a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) and, if taxable activities are carried on, registers the entity for the Impuesto a la Transferencia de Bienes Muebles y Servicios (ITBMS). Registration must be completed prior to the commencement of any commercial activity in Panama.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not have a check-the-box or US-style entity classification election. A PIF has limited statutory choices: (i) if the foundation engages in commercial or economic activities in Panama, it may elect to be taxable on Panama-source income at entity level; (ii) if the PIF remains a private wealth-holding vehicle without commercial activity, it is generally outside the Panamanian income tax scope, subject only to the annual foundation franchise tax; and (iii) no hybrid mismatch or transparent-entity election is recognized under Panamanian law. The election is evidenced by the foundation's internal documentation, the registered activities clause, and the actual conduct of the entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic turnover threshold triggers ITBMS registration; any person or entity that habitually transfers goods or provides services in Panama must register. A simplified ITBMS regime is available to small taxpayers with annual gross taxable turnover of up to USD 40,000 (equivalent in Balboas), but cross-border B2B platform operators will ordinarily exceed this and are required to declare under the general regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods and services outside Panama. Goods qualify when they are physically shipped from Panama and leave the national customs territory. Services, including electronic B2B marketplace services, qualify when the provider is not resident in Panama for the underlying service, the service is supplied to a non-resident client, and the use, enjoyment, or consumption of the service takes place entirely outside Panama. The platform operator must maintain contemporaneous documentary evidence of the place of supply, place of consumption, and the cross-border nature of the transaction, including contracts, IP geolocation data, and payment routing evidence.

  • Panama Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera - DUA)
  • Bill of Lading (sea, air, or land equivalent) evidencing physical export and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement from the receiving Panamanian financial institution evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency within the applicable period

Panama mandates electronic invoicing for all ITBMS-registered taxpayers through the DGI's Factura Electrónica platform operating within SIGER. Invoices must be issued through certified software or API connectors, digitally signed, and transmitted to the DGI for validation before being delivered to the counterparty. The DGI returns a unique fiscal acceptance code (Constancia de la Operación) that serves as the legal proof of clearance. For B2B cross-border marketplace operators, payment rails such as Alibaba_Pay and SWIFT wire must be referenced on the e-invoice, and the DGI may require near-real-time API-based reporting of cross-border transactions. Digital stamping is mandatory for all invoices issued by electronic invoicing taxpayers, and unauthorized printed invoices carry fines.

The DGI actively audits export zero-rating claims and may disallow the 0% rate if any required document is missing, inconsistent, or if the supporting records do not prove physical departure of goods or foreign consumption of services. Unsupported zero-rating is treated as a withheld ITBMS failure, subject to surcharges, monthly interest, and penalties of up to 200% of the omitted tax. In cases of intentional fraud or forged customs documentation, the DGI may pursue criminal tax evasion charges and the statute of limitations for reassessment is extended from the general five-year period to six to ten years. Retroactive clawback assessments may also include the denial of all export refunds and the suspension of the taxpayer's export zero-rating privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility in both capital and current accounts. The USD is legal tender and circulates freely; the PAB exists only as a nominal accounting unit. Capital inflows (foundation endowments, debt investment, equity contribution) and outflows (profit remittance, dividend payments, capital repatriation, vendor disbursements) are unrestricted and require no central-bank approval or exchange permit. Profit remittance is hindered only by the bank's own source-of-funds controls, not by currency or convertibility regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender requirements exist. Statutory timeline: none; mandatory conversion ratio: 0% (all export and platform-settlement receivables may be held, disbursed, or converted freely in USD). This is consistent with Panama's territorial tax system, under which export-derived and offshore services income is generally outside Panamanian income tax.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Panama Banking Superintendency); Panama does not maintain a domestic central bank or foreign-exchange administrative agency. Monetary functions are performed by the dollarized framework that governs the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) at a fixed 1:1 rate to the US Dollar (USD), with no indigenous currency issuance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. Panama's Fintech Sandbox regime (Acuerdo 4-2021, Superintendency of Banks) permits licensed digital banks and electronic-money institutions, but authorization is discretionary and processing cycles are long. Offshore EMIs (e.g., US or UK digital banks) generally reject Private Interest Foundations due to beneficiary opacity and correspondent-bank de-risking. Recommendation: open the primary USD account at a Tier-1 Panamanian bank and use regulated correspondent crypto/EMI rails only for ancillary operational flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route founder/investor funding via SWIFT MT103 into the Panama PIF's USD operational account; no exchange-control approval or conversion is required because the receiving account is USD-denominated. The bank requires a purpose declaration (documentación de uso de fondos) that classifies the wire as 'Capital contribution to Private Interest Foundation' (aligning with BPM6 direct investment / capital transfer categories) and supports the transaction with the endowment letter, foundation deed, and councilor resolution. Funds received in RMB via Alibaba_Pay should be converted offshore and settled into the USD account through a regulated Chinese bank correspondent to avoid non-convertible-currency complications.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for fixed and medium-value B2B vendor payments; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or usance) with confirming banks for high-value marketplace batches and platform inventory settlements; (3) Panama's local ACH / RTP (SRL or 'Cámara de Compensación Automatizada') for domestic operator and logistics disbursements within Panama. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding on dividends paid from profits already subject to Panamanian corporate income tax; 20% withholding on distributions made from non-taxed (foreign-source) profits. The 4% quarterly advance dividend tax applies to companies distributing Panama-source taxable earnings; a PIF distributing only foreign-source wealth generally has no Panamanian dividend WHT unless a Panamanian taxpaying entity is the payor or the distribution constitutes Panama-source income to the beneficiary.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before 31 March of the year following the fiscal year-end when the fiscal year is the calendar year (1 January - 31 December); a different fiscal year may be adopted with DGI authorization, in which case filing is due within 3 months after fiscal year-end.

Late filing: surcharge of 10% of the tax due plus monthly interest of 1%; continued non-filing accrues monthly fines of up to USD 100 per month and total fines ranging from USD 500 to USD 5,000; the DGI may embargo bank accounts, block the RUC and suspend the Aviso de Operación, effectively freezing Alibaba Pay and SWIFT settlement accounts.
Box 20 (Gross Receipts) Total gross revenue derived from Panamanian-source activities; for the B2B marketplace this includes platform commissions, listing fees and transaction fees earned from Panama-based merchants and for services performed in Panama. Foreign-source commissions billed to non-resident counterparties and settled through foreign payment rails must be separately scheduled and excluded; the supporting segregation policy must be documented and consistent. Late filing: surcharge of 10% of the tax due plus monthly interest of 1%; continued non-filing accrues monthly fines of up to USD 100 per month and total fines ranging from USD 500 to USD 5,000; the DGI may embargo bank accounts, block the RUC and suspend the Aviso de Operación, effectively freezing Alibaba Pay and SWIFT settlement accounts.

No tax treaty or TRC is required for the exclusion; the taxpayer must retain platform contracts, SWIFT/Alibaba Pay settlement statements, IP logs and service-rendering evidence to prove non-Panamanian source. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 250,000 (Panama is fully dollarized; there is no separate local currency). Aggregate related-party transactions exceeding USD 250,000 in a fiscal year trigger mandatory preparation of a transfer pricing report and electronic filing of Form 930 together with the annual income tax return.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but substance is enforced through four channels: (i) territorial source rules requiring functional performance in Panama for income characterization; (ii) Law 70 of 2019 setting concrete substance thresholds for the Sede headquarters regime; (iii) Law 129 of 2020 establishing the Beneficial Ownership Registry requiring disclosure of settlor, foundation council, protector and beneficiaries; and (iv) the Fiscal Code's simulation and substance-over-form doctrine allowing the DGI to recharacterize a PIF as a sham collection vehicle when it lacks accounting records, real control over platform operations, local substance and commercial rationale.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Territorial Income Mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: Civil: reassessment at 25% on concealed Panama-source income, 10% late-payment surcharge, 1% monthly interest; Criminal: Law 76 of 2019 (tax evasion) imposes 5-10 years of imprisonment where evaded tax exceeds USD 10,000, plus fines of up to three times the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members, de facto managers and the registered agent are jointly and severally liable for restitution of evaded tax, penalties and interest; the DGI may embargo personal bank accounts, impose preventive asset freezes, and criminal courts may issue international travel bans and order pre-trial detention.
Unauthorized Commercial Activity Through a Private Interest Foundation

Statutory sanction: Regulatory correction orders and fines up to USD 10,000 for violation of foundation-law obligations; the DGI may disregard the foundation's separate patrimony and recharacterize the operation as a de facto S.A., imposing 25% corporate tax, 10% surcharge and interest on the entire Panama-source income.

Director personal liability: Civil: foundation council members are personally and severally liable for obligations contracted ultra vires; the resident agent risks license revocation and sanctions by the Superintendencia de Sujetos No Financieros for enabling an unlawful shell-like trading operation.
Money Laundering and AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to USD 5,000,000 against the foundation and its service providers, plus periodic fines for registry non-compliance; freezing and forfeiture of settlement accounts; Criminal Code Article 254-A imposes 5-12 years of imprisonment for money laundering, with fines and disqualification of the entity from operating payment accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors, council members and the registered agent are criminally liable as participants where they knew or ought to have known of illicit fund flows; the Unidad de Análisis Financiero can issue travel bans, asset freezes and referrals for pre-trial detention; personal passports may be revoked during prosecution.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to USD 5,000 for failure to file Form 930; the DGI may reallocate income by discretionary assessment and add a 10% surcharge plus 1% monthly interest; in aggravated cases an additional penalty of up to 25% of the adjusted transaction value applies.

Director personal liability: The foundation council and actual administrator are jointly liable for the adjusted tax, surcharges and interest; persistent omission permits the tax tribunal to order embargoes on personal assets and to report individuals to immigration authorities for preventive exit bans.
