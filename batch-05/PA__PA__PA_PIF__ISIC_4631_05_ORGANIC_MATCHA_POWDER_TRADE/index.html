Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (General Directorate of Revenue, 'DGI') of the Panamanian Ministry of Economy and Finance (Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas); online fiscal portal: e-Tributa (https://e-tributa.dgi.gob.pa), with the DGI public site at https://www.dgi.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must obtain the unified Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC, historically also referred to as NIT) before issuing ITBMS-invoiced transactions or claiming export zero-rating. Standard operating procedure: (1) prepare a certified Spanish-language copy of the PIF's registered charter (Acta Fundacional) recorded with the Panamanian Public Registry; (2) designate a resident agent or legal representative and provide their identity document; (3) submit the unified registration form (Formulario Único de Registro) and supporting identification documents either online through e-Tributa or at a DGI service window; (4) obtain the RUC certificate and digital access credentials; (5) activate the foundation's electronic fiscal mailbox for the mandatory electronic invoicing system and annual tax filings. Temporary single-transaction RUC registration is available to non-resident sellers, but the territorial zero-rating protocol normally requires full RUC status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not adopt a US-style check-the-box election or OECD hybrid-mismatch rules. A PIF is, by default, a separate legal and taxable person (not a pass-through trust) whose assessable income is limited to Panama-source income under the territorial tax model. Statutory elections relevant to effective taxation: (i) the simplified small-taxpayer regime (Régimen Especial de Pequeños Contribuyentes) for gross annual turnover up to B/.36,000, which reduces accounting and filing obligations; (ii) the election to use DGI-accredited electronic accounting records; and (iii) the freedom to select an accounting currency and taxable-year period, with the calendar year as default. Panama is an OECD member and participant in the BEPS Inclusive Framework, but its domestic law does not provide hybrid-mismatch election mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.0 (Balboa, 1:1 with USD) — registration in the RUC is compulsory whenever a person or entity habitually carries out economic activities in Panama; a reduced-compliance simplified taxpayer regime is available where rolling 12-month gross taxable turnover does not exceed B/.36,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The Panamanian Código Fiscal zero-rates exports of goods at 0% ITBMS when the sale involves physical departure of merchandise from Panama to a non-Panamanian destination under a completed customs export declaration, with title and risk transferred to the foreign buyer. For the organic matcha powder distribution scope, zero-rating requires: (a) the supplier/foundation to be RUC-registered; (b) goods confined to the national customs territory and then definitively exported, or transshipped under customs control; (c) invoices naming the overseas purchaser and denominated in a freely convertible currency; (d) payment demonstrably entering Panama from abroad; and (e) underlying purchase, inventory, and tonnage-reconciliation records substantiating the exported quantity. Transactions executed wholly offshore where goods never enter Panamanian territory are outside the territorial scope and are therefore not taxable rather than zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / DUA) endorsed by the Panamanian Customs Authority (Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill consigned to the overseas buyer evidencing physical departure from Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT credit note evidencing settlement from the foreign buyer within 180 days of export
  • Zero-rated commercial invoice and purchase order identifying goods, quantities, unit prices, and HS 0902.10 classification for organic matcha powder
  • Supplier invoices and warehouse or import records reconciling the exported tonnage to substantiate any input ITBMS credit offset by zero-rated sales
  • Foundation board or governing-body resolution authorizing the specific cross-border transaction

Mandatory electronic fiscal invoicing applies to ITBMS-registered traders through the DGI's Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (SIE). Approved invoicing software or ERP providers transmit invoices in the DGI-defined XML schema to the e-Tributa platform, where the DGI validates the fiscal sequence, affixes a digital acceptance code, and communicates status in real time before the invoice is legally issued. B2B and B2C electronic invoices must contain the DGI digital stamp and QR code; printed invoices require a DGI-authorized cryptographic imprint. Invoices not cleared by the DGI cannot support input-credit claims, and repeated clearance failures trigger automated compliance blocks against the RUC.

The DGI may audit any zero-rated sales and associated input-credit refund claims within the statutory limitation period. A disallowed export claim triggers: (i) retroactive nullification of the 0% rate, reassessment of 7% ITBMS on the full invoice value, and reversal of input credits; (ii) monthly statutory default interest from the original due date; (iii) administrative surcharges and progressive fines under the Código Fiscal, generally from 50% to 100% of the corrected tax for negligent underpayment and up to treble the tax for fraudulent documentation; and (iv) criminal tax-fraud referral of the foundation's officers and resident agent, with custodial penalties. Consistent failure to substantiate exports can lead to RUC suspension, denial of future refund claims, and reclassification of foreign receipts as Panama-source taxable revenue with the corresponding income tax exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility across both current and capital account lines. The Balboa (PAB) exists solely as coinage at an immutably fixed 1:1 parity with the United States dollar; US banknotes are unlimited legal tender. There are no exchange-control licenses, import/export FX restrictions, profit-remittance permits, or capital-transaction approvals. Non-resident beneficiaries of a Panamanian PIF may receive distributions freely without Panamanian exchange-control clearance or withholding tax. The only operative hurdles are documentary: banks must verify lawful origin of funds and genuine commercial purpose. Direct trading activity should be carried through a Panamanian stock corporation (S.A.) owned by the PIF to respect the foundation's asset-management scope under Law 25 of 1995 and avoid the appearance of the PIF itself conducting a merchandising business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export-proceeds surrender rules do not exist in Panama. There is no statutory timeline for USD or foreign-currency repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, no requirement to sell export receipts through any designated bank, and no central-bank approval of trade settlements. Exporters and trade intermediaries may retain, borrow, or invest proceeds in any currency. Banking-AML rules nevertheless require that export receipts be traceable to genuine commercial transactions; banks apply internal scrutiny to large or fragmented wire credits under Law 23 of 2015 and SBP Anti-Money-Laundering Resolution.

Central bank supervision: No traditional central bank exists in Panama. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Panama Superintendency of Banks, SBP) is the financial regulator and de facto foreign-exchange conduct authority; the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) is the AML/CFT financial-intelligence unit; Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the State's fiscal agent and clearing-house supervisor; the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) handles monetary integration policy. Panama is a fully dollarized jurisdiction.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Panamanian law (Law 23 of 2015 and SBP Banking Rules) requires any operator receiving deposits or performing payment services for Panamanian client structures to be a licensed bank or an SBP-sanctioned fintech-sandbox institution. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Revolut Business are not licensed to hold PIF accounts in Panama and generally decline Panama-connected entity structures. Their utility is limited to ancillary, low-balance receipts in Japan or China supplier markets; they cannot serve as a PIF's primary treasury or settlement rail. The entrenched correspondent network of Banco General, Banistmo, and Multibank remains the legally robust alternative.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panama (USD/Balboa): mandatory Transfer Pricing Informative Return (Form 350) required when annual gross income exceeds USD 10 million; contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation is triggered when related-party transactions exceed USD 3 million, or when the taxpayer qualifies under the TP regime.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), introduced by Law 248 of 2021 and related rules, require all relevant legal entities conducting 'relevant activities' (including distribution and service center operations) to maintain adequate physical offices, local employees, and core income-generating activities in Panama. A PIF that merely holds matcha contract rights and invoices from a mailbox address without local substance will be exposed to mandatory substance declarations, annual reporting obligations, and risk of administrative dissolution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misrepresentation of Panama-Source Revenue

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 5 years, fines of 2 to 10 times the evaded tax, plus retroactive tax assessments, monthly interest at 2%, and an additional 10% surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors or foundation council members who sign false tax returns may have the corporate veil pierced; they are individually liable for civil restitution, may face criminal prosecution, travel bans, and disqualification from standing as company directors in Panama.
Shell Company / Complete Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Violation of Panama's ESR Law and Fiscal Code: fines from USD 10,000 to USD 1,000,000, cancellation of the Foundation's PIF registration, automatic denial of treaty or withholding relief, and re-characterization of income as Panama-source with 25% corporate tax plus penalties.

Director personal liability: Foundation directors are jointly and severally liable for ESR fines if they fail to file economic substance declarations; they can be banned from entering public procurement contracts for up to 8 years and barred from directorship of any Panamanian entity.
Money Laundering / AML Non-Compliance in Trade Channel

Statutory sanction: Under Panama's AML Code (Law 23 of 2015 and related FATF/CRS regulations): imprisonment for 2 to 4 years, corporate fines up to USD 1,000,000 per violation, asset forfeiture, and automatic listing in the national money-laundering offenders registry.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers involved in or wilfully ignoring suspicious matcha trade flows face minimum 3-year prison sentences, freezing orders, travel bans during investigation, and personal seizure of assets.
Failure to File, Pay, or Withhold Tax; Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of 5% of tax due; monthly interest of 2% on unpaid tax up to 24%; TP non-filing penalties of up to USD 10,000; DGI may levy estimated assessments plus a 15% enhancement.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for withheld dividend taxes not remitted to the DGI; they may be subject to departure restrictions (travel bans) and administrative arrest until the debt is paid, plus civil enforcement against personal assets.
