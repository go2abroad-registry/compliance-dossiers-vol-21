Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Panama; official electronic portal: 'Sede Electrónica DGI' (e-Tax) and the DGI Facturación Electrónica platform at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa/; customs counterpart for export control: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA).

E-government filing portal

(i) Execute the Private Interest Foundation (PIF) charter before a Panamanian notary and record it in the Registro Público under Law 25 of 12 June 1995; (ii) appoint a licensed resident agent in Panama and maintain a registered office; (iii) apply through the DGI Sede Electrónica for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), the statutory TIN and VAT-equivalent ID, submitting the foundation certificate, registered charter, resident agent appointment, and registered-office proof; (iv) simultaneously register the PIF as an ITBMS taxpayer in the general regime (or the simplified regime if eligible) and enrol in the mandatory electronic invoicing system; (v) open a Panamanian bank account and associate it with the RUC to receive export proceeds through SWIFT wire or PayPal remittances.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — A PIF is a separate legal person subject to entity-level taxation (not a pass-through), and Panama applies a territorial income tax model under the Código Tributario: only Panama-source income is taxable. Export income from the sale of die-cast scale automotive models (ISIC 3240) delivered to foreign buyers is generally excluded from Panamanian-source ISR; the foundation's assets held for private-interest purposes are likewise outside the ISR net unless commercial activity is carried on in Panama.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or entity-classification election exists under Panamanian law; the PIF cannot elect pass-through treatment and has no hybrid-election mechanism. Available statutory elections within the target facts: (a) election to apply the Régimen Simplificado de ITBMS for small taxpayers whose annual taxable turnover does not exceed approximately B/. 60,000, and (b) election to treat qualifying exports as zero-rated ITBMS supplies. Panama has adopted OECD hybrid-mismatch reporting and, if a mismatched tax outcome arises from another jurisdiction disregarding the PIF, DGI will neutralise it domestically by denying the deduced benefit.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general-regime exemption threshold: any person engaged in taxable industrial or commercial activity in Panama, including a PIF conducting die-casting and tampo printing, must register for ITBMS before issuing its first invoice regardless of volume. A simplified fixed-contribution regime (Régimen Simplificado) is available only to micro and small enterprises with annual gross sales at or below approximately B/. 60,000; the target manufacturing operation exceeding that volume must register in the general regime and file monthly ITBMS returns.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods — including zinc alloy die-cast 1:18 scale car replicas — are zero-rated under ITBMS when the goods are definitively removed from Panamanian territory and sold to a non-resident buyer. Eligibility requires: (i) a customs export declaration electronically filed with the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA); (ii) physical departure of the goods from Panama within the declared period; (iii) commercial transport documents evidencing the foreign destination; (iv) payment received in convertible currency through the Panamanian banking system, including SWIFT wire or PayPal credits deposited into the PIF's Panamanian bank account; and (v) retention of the inward-remittance certificate. Export services directly related to the exported goods are also zero-rated. Input ITBMS attributable to zero-rated export sales is creditable and refundable.

  • Customs export declaration (Documento Único de Aduanas, e-DUA) validated and exit-certified by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (Conocimiento de Embarque) identifying the foreign consignee and the port of discharge outside Panama
  • Electronic invoice (Factura Electrónica) containing the CUF, the zero-rated export indicator, and the 0% ITBMS line
  • Inward remittance certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) issued by a Panamanian bank confirming collection of proceeds via SWIFT wire or PayPal credit
  • Foreign sales contract or purchase order, packing list, and transport insurance documentation

Panama mandates electronic invoicing ('Factura Electrónica'). All general-regime taxpayers must issue e-invoices through DGI-authorised technological service providers; each invoice is transmitted to the DGI platform in near real time and receives a unique Código Único de Facturación (CUF). There is no physical fiscal stamp; fiscal clearance is digital. For exports, the e-invoice must carry the zero-rated export indicator and must be linked to the corresponding customs e-DUA. Failure to comply disentitles the taxpayer to ITBMS input credits and to zero-rating claims.

DGI and ANA jointly audit zero-rated export transactions on both documentary and physical verification bases; fictitious exports, inflated declared values, misdeclared destinations, or non-repatriation of proceeds are treated as fiscal fraud. Consequences under the Código Tributario, as amended by Law 76 of 2019, include reassessment and restitution of the avoided 7% ITBMS, default interest and regulatory surcharges, and substantial fines, which in aggravated cases may reach up to 100% of the omitted tax. Wilful fraudulent export claims constitute criminal tax evasion and may result in imprisonment. DGI may additionally cancel the taxpayer's e-invoicing authorisation, block refunds of input ITBMS credits, and apply extended limitation periods where export documentation is falsified.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized economy with unrestricted convertibility. No separate legal tender exists, and the U.S. dollar circulates freely. Both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible without foreign exchange approval. Profit remittances, dividends, interest, and royalties are freely transferable abroad through the banking system with no quantitative limits, withholding only applying under domestic tax law for Panama-source income. No restrictions on the repatriation of capital or dividends for non-resident equity holders in a Private Interest Foundation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no export proceeds surrender requirement and does not mandate conversion of export receipts into any domestic currency because the U.S. dollar is the legal tender. Foreign currency earned from exports may be retained in any bank account, locally or abroad, and used freely for trade or other payments without any statutory timeline or compulsory conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: No central bank exists in Panama; the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the banking and financial regulator, and the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) oversees fiscal and financial policy. The U.S. dollar is legal tender and the national currency, so foreign exchange administration functions are effectively replaced by a dollarized monetary system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low to Medium. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not automatically recognized as licensed banks or regulated payment service providers under Panamanian law. They may offer ancillary cross-border payment facilitation only through a local licensed bank or through a Panama-registered non-bank financial intermediary. Direct operation without a physical Panamanian agent or a licensed local partner is legally constrained. The SBP does not issue a pure 'EMI license' equivalent to the EU framework, and any payment intermediation into Panama's banking system requires compliance with the SBP's criminal, anti-money laundering, and consumer protection circulars.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are executed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers to the foundation's USD-denominated operational bank account in Panama. No exchange control approval or import license is required. Beneficiary bank details, foundation legal name, and the registration number from the Registro Público de Panamá must be included to satisfy the receiving bank's compliance. The intended purpose should be clearly stated as 'equity contribution to foundation' or 'operating capital injection', and supporting board resolutions must be retained for AML verification. Correspondent banks in the U.S. may request the originator's full legal name, address, and purpose code; all incoming funds are credited in U.S. dollars with no conversion requirement.Trade settlements for manufacturing inputs and finished die-cast collectibles are made primarily by SWIFT MT103 for direct wire payments, with MT202 for interbank transfers when intermediary institutions are involved. For medium and large transactions involving international suppliers, commercial letters of credit (L/C) opened by the Panamanian bank with SWIFT MT700/MT707 messages are used as the principal clearing rail. Documentary collections (CAD or D/P) via SWIFT MT400 may also be used. All disbursements must reference the commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading to support the underlying trade. Since Panama is dollar-based, no foreign exchange conversion occurs for invoicing in USD; non-USD trade settlements are executed through bank-provided FX contracts.Deliverable foreign exchange forwards against the U.S. dollar (USD/EUR, USD/GBP, USD/CNY) for non-USD trade payables and receivablesCross-currency swaps between USD and major trading currencies to manage longer-term cash flow positionsFX options purchased from licensed Panamanian banks for hedging currency exposure on non-USD sourcing or distribution contractsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) may be permitted only where the counterparty is an international bank with a registered Panama branch or a licensed offshore bank operating under the SBP, but their use is secondary as the local currency is the USD

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% (Panama-source dividend distribution). The tax is levied on all dividends paid by a Panama-registered entity, including a PIF, from Panama-source profits.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Form 61 annual return must be filed no later than 31 March of the year following the fiscal year-end. Monthly advance income tax payments (pagos directos) must be filed by the 15th of the following month on Form 415 or through TUA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File (and Master File, where applicable) is triggered where the taxpayer's aggregate related-party transactions in the fiscal year exceed B/.2,000,000 (USD 2,000,000) or where the taxpayer's gross annual Panama-source income exceeds B/.1,500,000 (USD 1,500,000). A Master File is additionally required for multinational groups with consolidated turnover of USD 50 million or more.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), introduced by Executive Decree 261 of 2018 and amended by Decree 108 of 2021, are applicable to legal entities, including PIFs, carrying on 'relevant activities' and deriving income from outside Panama. Die-cast scale-model manufacturing itself is not a statutorily listed relevant activity; however, if the foundation generates IP-holding, distribution, financing, or headquarters income, it must demonstrate (i) local management and control, (ii) adequate local employees, premises, and expenditure, and (iii) full local bookkeeping and accounting records. A PIF with physical factory operations in Panama and substantive local payroll automatically satisfies the purpose test for manufacturing and would not be treated as a purely offshore shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Statements

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 50% of the tax evaded plus monthly interest at the rate of 1% of the amount due until payment; criminal conviction for tax fraud carries a penalty of imprisonment from 2 to 6 years, disqualification from commercial activity for up to 5 years, and a monetary fine equivalent to two to ten times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Person who signs or validates a false tax return is jointly and severally liable for all unpaid taxes, fines, and interest; in the presence of fraudulent intention, the director may face civil restitution of the tax amount, a court-ordered travel ban, and imprisonment term of up to 6 years. The corporate veil is pierced when the foundation is used as a mere instrumentality to commit fraud.
Shell Foundation and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The DGI may disregard the foundation for tax purposes, assess unreported Panama-source income at 25%, impose fines of 5% per month up to 50% of the tax, and levy a 10% surcharge for late filing. Additionally, an unlicensed fiduciary front may be sanctioned by the Superintendency of Banks with fines between USD 10,000 and USD 50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally responsible for the tax liability under the doctrine of alter ego where the foundation is undercapitalized and commingles assets; they face criminal charges for fraudulent simulation and may be subject to heritage and patrimonial attachment orders and travel restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Arm's-Length Violation

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment to arm's-length prices; penalty of 40% on the entire adjustment; if the taxpayer fails to present documentation on time, a fixed penalty of 1% of the transaction value, capped where otherwise lawfully applicable; automatic anti-avoidance fines for structures with no commercial purpose.

Director personal liability: Directors and tax officers who sign the TP report and knowingly use false pricing data may be fined personally up to USD 10,000; in flagrant cases, the accountant or director may be charged with tax fraud for facilitating the mispricing, subject to imprisonment and civil damages.
AML / Counter-Terrorist Financing Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Panama AML Law 23 of 2015, unlicensed non-financial designated businesses engaged in certain commercial activities must register with the UAF and are subject to fines up to USD 1,000,000; failure to file STRs/CTRs may result in license revocation, and the underlying funds may be subject to forfeiture in rem actions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit the PIF to be utilized for money laundering (e.g., layering payments from suspect jurisdictions) face criminal liability up to 12 years' imprisonment, civil liability for laundered value, personal peace and travel bans pending trial, and consequent disqualification as directors of any Panamanian entity.
Unremitted Dividend Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: The foundation remains liable for the unpaid 10% dividend tax, plus a surcharge of 20% of the unpaid amount and late-payment interest at 1% monthly; the DGI may also issue a default judgment for the grossed-up amount and suspend the RUC, preventing imports and exporting licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve a dividend distribution knowing that the 10% withholding tax has not been paid are jointly and severally liable for the tax plus surcharge; the DGI may obtain a civil judgment against them, freeze personal assets, and the directors may be criminally prosecuted if they stripped the foundation's assets to render the foundation insolvent.
