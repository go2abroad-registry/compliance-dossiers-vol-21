Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) — Electronic portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity or a Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must appoint a Panama-licensed resident agent/attorney (mandatory under Law 25 of 1995). The resident agent inscribes the foundation charter with the Registro Público de Panamá, declaring the minimum capital (statutorily set in the charter, conventionally at least B/.10,000, not necessarily paid in). After inscription, the resident agent obtains the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) from the DGI by filing: (a) certificate of registration from the Registro Público; (b) notarised foundation charter and bylaws; (c) appointment of the legal representative and foundation council; (d) proof of the municipal Aviso de Operación issued by the Alcaldía of the domicile district; and (e) a completed RUC application with e-signature registration through the DGI Sede Electrónica. The RUC is generally issued within 5-10 business days of a complete filing. The same RUC serves as the VAT (ITBMS) identification number; no separate VAT certificate is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections relevant to a PIF: (1) election to remain a purely patrimonial holding vehicle, which leaves income from foreign sources outside Panamanian ISR (income tax) entirely; (2) election to engage in commercial operations within Panama, thereby starting Panamanian-source taxation under the Código Fiscal and ITBMS; (3) election to be taxed under the integrated dividend/ISR regime with deemed dividend distribution on non-tested profits; (4) election for small taxpayers to enter the REPECO (Régimen Especial de Pequeños Contribuyentes) simplified regime where annual gross income does not exceed B/.36,000; and (5) election to use the cash-basis ITBMS declaration instead of the accrual basis where the taxpayer is a small contributor. No hybrid mismatch or check-the-box election exists for PIFs; the PIF is a separate juridical person for all Panamanian tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.0.00 — no monetary exemption threshold; any person, including a foreign-owned PIF, that carries out taxable transfers of movable goods or taxable services in Panama must obtain the RUC and register for ITBMS before commencing operations. The only statutory simplification threshold is B/.36,000 of annual gross income for REPECO eligibility; registration itself is mandatory from the first taxable supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS is applied at 0% where: (a) goods are physically exported from Panama, evidenced by a valid Declaración de Exportación (DEX) accepted by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) and by passage of risk/title to the foreign buyer outside Panamanian territory; or (b) services are supplied to an overseas recipient and are economically used or consumed entirely outside Panama, supported by the underlying contract, delivery certificates, and payment evidence from abroad. The PIF must link zero-rated outward supplies to corresponding ITBMS input credits; excess input credits attributable solely to export sales may be carried forward or reclaimed as a refund from the DGI under the exporter refund procedure, subject to a DGI desk audit.

  • Declaración de Exportación (DEX) duly accepted and stamped by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical dispatch of the polymers
  • Commercial Invoice issued to the foreign counterparty
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit evidence (where applicable)

Compulsory Electronic Invoicing (Facturación Electrónica de Panamá) implemented under Res. DGI No. 201-1864 of 2018, as subsequently amended. All RUC-holders with commercial activity must issue invoices through DGI-authorised technological service providers (PSI - Proveedores de Servicios de Impresión/Solución Tecnológica) or through the DGI free web portal, with real-time transmission of the XML invoice to the DGI Sede Electrónica for fiscal validation, algorithmic risk scoring, and digital stamping (sello digital de la DGI). The DGI API/web-service framework mandates standard XML schemas, taxpayer digital signatures, receipt acknowledgment codes (Aceptado/Rechazado), and rejection correction workflows. As of the current stage, the mandate covers all active taxpayers, and a PIF trading bulk petrochemical polymers must integrate its ERP with an authorised PSI and must not use legacy pre-printed fiscal invoices except during officially recognised coexistence periods.

Panama is fully dollarized (Balboa at 1:1 with USD), so FX repatriation is not a currency-conversion issue; nevertheless, export proceeds must be economically repatriated/evidenced within a 180-day commercial cycle to support zero-rating substantiation. Fraudulent export zero-rating triggers: (i) immediate recapture of the 0% treatment with 7% ITBMS on the full export value plus compensatory surcharges; (ii) administrative fines under the Código Fiscal of 25%-300% of the omitted tax; (iii) clawback of all ITBMS input credits previously attributed to export sales, with interest; (iv) criminal fiscal evasion liability for wilful false declarations; and (v) suspension or cancellation of the RUC and electronic-invoicing certification, which effectively halts the PIF's trading capacity in Panama. The DGI and ANA cross-match DEX filings against banking/payment evidence to detect fictitious or inflated exports, with heightened scrutiny for petrochemical bulk commodities routed through free zones.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility exists in this fully dollarized jurisdiction. Panama imposes no exchange-control regime, no balance-of-payment restrictions on current or capital account transactions, no mandatory approval for inward capital injections, and no repatriation authorization for profits. The Private Interest Foundation may remit dividends, foundation distributions, or trading profits freely through any SBP-licensed bank; only FATCA, CRS, and RBS beneficial-ownership reporting obligations apply to nonresident beneficiaries, without any convertibility hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. There are no statutory timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation obligations for export proceeds because Panama has no central bank and no foreign-exchange administration. USD proceeds from wholesale petrochemical polymer exports may be retained offshore or credited to the PIF's Panamanian USD account with no declaration to any monetary authority.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank and no currency-issuing authority; the U.S. dollar is legal tender under the monetary agreements of Law 54 of 1904 and Decree Law 9 of 1941. Banking oversight rests with the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) under the Ley Bancaria (Decree Law 2 of 2008, as consolidated). Banco Nacional de Panamá and Caja de Ahorros function as state-owned commercial and development banks, not as monetary authorities, and Panama has no lender-of-last-resort facility and no official exchange-rate desk.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary settlement channel and Medium as a supplementary disbursement layer. The Ley Bancaria reserves deposit-taking and payment account servicing to SBP-licensed banks and supervised fintech payment gateways; an offshore digital EMI holds no legal account-seating license in Panama and cannot clear USD directly through the local banking system. Working capital and treasury balances must therefore reside in a full-service SBP-licensed bank, while a licensed international e-money provider may only facilitate specific disbursements through a local partner bank. Any unlicensed neobank holding of PIF funds would constitute unlawful deposit activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The settlor initiates an institutional SWIFT MT103 wire in USD to the PIF's SBP-licensed Panamanian account; no FX form, license, or central bank approval is required. The remittance reference must cite the foundation charter and the capital-contribution resolution, while the purpose is coded as a BPM6 equity contribution or private transfer between affiliated persons for FATCA-CRS consistency. Injections above USD 100,000 require a complete source-of-funds file; amounts above USD 500,000 trigger treasury-desk pre-approval and US correspondent bank OFAC screening. All capital movements must pass through named institutional accounts, never as bundled cash deposits.Supplier payments settle via SWIFT MT103 for open-account transactions and through MT700 irrevocable documentary letters of credit under UCP600 for polymer cargoes; a confirming bank at top-tier correspondent level is recommended for US Gulf or Middle East producers. Typical trade structures use sight or 60-day usance drafts against full bill-of-lading, certificate of analysis, and inspection documents. Open-account MT103 payments to Latin American buyers may be combined with trade credit insurance or standby LCs.
