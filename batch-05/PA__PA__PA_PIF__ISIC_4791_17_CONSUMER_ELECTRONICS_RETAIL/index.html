Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; online services through Sede Electrónica at www.dgi.gob.pa and the SIF electronic invoicing platform (Sistema de Facturación Electrónica).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned or non-resident-founded Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) from DGI. Procedure: (1) appoint a Panamanian licensed attorney or firm as resident agent; (2) register the foundation's public deed and articles of incorporation with DGI through the authorized agent; (3) declare the fiscal domicile in Panama and the primary economic activity code corresponding to ISIC 4791 (online retail); (4) request the RUC certificate and, if Panamanian territorial operations exist, simultaneous ITBMS registration; (5) obtain an electronic signature credential for SIF/e-filing. The nominal foundation endowment required by Law 25 of 1995 is satisfied by the statutory minimum of USD 10,000 declared in the foundation deed; no capital payment or deposit is required for tax registration purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: The PIF is a separate juridical person and a separate taxable person. It is not a pass-through entity. Under Panama's territorial income tax (ISR), only income whose source is located in Panama is taxable. Foreign-source e-commerce receipts (online sales of consumer electronics to non-residents, with delivery and use outside Panama) fall outside the Panamanian territorial nexus. Income derived from activities carried out in Panama, including local stock, logistics, or fulfillment operations, would be Panamanian-source and subject to ISR at the standard corporate rate; Law 25 of 1995 additionally grants PIFs exemption from national taxes within its statutory scope, subject to DGI interpretation for active commercial activity.

Available election forms

None applicable. Panama does not provide check-the-box classification elections comparable to the United States, and has not implemented OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules. The territorial source regime is mandatory and cannot be opted out of; no election is available to treat foreign-source income as taxable or to claim pass-through treatment for a PIF.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general numeric ITBMS registration threshold: any person performing taxable transfers of goods or services in Panama, or importing goods for sale, must obtain RUC and ITBMS registration. The simplified Small Taxpayer Regime (Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente) applies to taxpayers with annual gross income of B/. 300,000 or less (Balboas, at parity with USD), allowing reduced filing obligations. Pure offshore e-commerce sales with no Panamanian inventory, fulfillment, or local buyer nexus do not trigger ITBMS registration or taxation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The ITBMS 0% rate applies to: (a) goods physically exported from Panama, including distance/online retail sales of consumer electronics delivered abroad to non-resident buyers; (b) services rendered to non-residents and effectively used or consumed outside Panama; and (c) sales into Panamanian Zonas Francas. The seller must hold an active ITBMS registration if it maintains Panamanian operations, must issue a compliant SIF e-invoice with the export zero-rating code, and must prove that the goods left Panamanian territory and the buyer is a non-resident. For a PIF with no Panamanian logistics, the underlying income is foreign-source and outside the ISR territorial base; export zero-rating is only invoked where goods are physically shipped from Panama and the seller has a Panamanian ITBMS registration.

  • DUA (Declaración Única Aduanera) export declaration duly validated and sealed by the Panama Customs Authority (ANA) through the SIA/SIGA single-window system
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the e-invoice issued through SIF
  • Transport document evidencing physical departure from Panama: ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier consignment note (DHL, FedEx, UPS)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit advice showing payment settlement from the foreign buyer's account
  • Evidence of non-residence of the buyer: overseas billing address, shipping destination, and IP/geolocation records for the online transaction

Mandatory electronic invoicing through the DGI's SIF (Sistema de Facturación Electrónica). Taxpayers carrying out taxable activities in Panama must issue e-invoices using SIF or DGI-approved software integrated via web-service API; invoices are transmitted to DGI in near-real-time and must carry a unique DGI authorization number. No physical ink stamp or fiscal seal is applied to electronic invoices; the electronic XML record is the controlling evidence. Non-electronic paper invoices require prior DGI authorization and may be used only in excepted cases.

Panama has no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day period is the DGI-benchmarked substantiation standard for export transactions and foreign-source income treatment. Failure to substantiate an export within DGI's documentary requirements results in reassessment of ITBMS at 7% plus statutory interest and surcharges, alongside administrativemonetary penalties under Código Fiscal Articles 759 through 762, which may reach 100% of the omitted tax. Fraudulent or sham export documentation, including false DUAs, backdated bills of lading, or fabricated remittance certificates, triggers criminal tax fraud prosecution under Articles 802 and following of the Código Fiscal, retrospective clawback for up to five fiscal years, nullification of the zero-rating position, and potential suspension of the PIF's RUC and the resident agent's license.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility applies to both current-account and capital-account transactions. Because the USD is legal tender and the PAB is a purely nominal unit, there is no separate exchange rate to administer, no surrender requirement, and no capital-control authorization regime. E-commerce receipts from Stripe and PayPal settle in USD without an intermediary conversion. Profit remittance, founder capital injections, distributions to foundation council members or beneficiaries, and payments to foreign suppliers are unrestricted, subject only to bank-level source-of-funds verification and suspicious-transaction reporting. For the Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF), which is tax-classified under a territorial model, foreign-source income from cross-border online retail is not subject to Panamanian income tax, and capital can be freely accumulated or distributed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. There is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no export-proceeds surrender obligation in Panama. Export earnings generated through digital marketplaces settle in USD, which is the lawful circulating currency, so 100% of proceeds may be retained, reinvested, or disbursed without Central Bank-style approval. The only compliance overlay is the territorial tax rule: revenue derived from foreign-market consumer sales is generally foreign-source income exempt from Panamanian income tax, provided the PIF does not maintain a permanent establishment in Panama; the applicability should be confirmed with the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) for ITBMS treatment of physically delivered gadgets.

Central bank supervision: Panama maintains no conventional central bank and consequently no domestic monetary authority setting reserve requirements or exchange-rate policy. The Republic is fully dollarized: the US dollar is legal tender and circulates alongside the Panamanian balboa, which exists only as fractional coinage at a fixed 1:1 parity. Banking supervision and prudential regulation are vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) under Legislative Decree 2 of 2008 (amending Law 9 of 1998). Banco Nacional de Panamá performs limited fiscal-agency, government-cash-management, and clearing-house functions, while AML/CFT supervision of non-financial sectors falls under the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) and Law 23 of 2015.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Panama has no specialist electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime analogous to UK FCA e-money licenses or Lithuanian EMI authorizations; bank-account opening for a corporate foundation requires an SBP-licensed commercial bank. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Mercury, Payoneer, Airwallex) generally classify Panama-domiciled Private Interest Foundations as high-structure risk, require physical or substantive regional nexus, or reject them outright because the PIF lacks a licensed-entity status recognized by the EMI's third-party compliance stack. Legal constraints: Law 23 of 2015 and SBP general regulations require the PIF to identify all authorized signatories and beneficial owners through a DGI-registered beneficial-ownership declaration; EMIs unable to access the Panamanian Beneficial Ownership Registry typically decline onboarding. Additionally, the target payment rails (Stripe, PayPal) serve as the PSP layer, and funds ultimately must clear into a domestic banking account; therefore the optimal structure is a PAN-licensed bank as master settlement account with Stripe/PayPal as acquirer gateways, not an offshore EMI as treasury home.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the founder remits seed capital via SWIFT MT103 from the founder's own external bank account to the PIF's USD account at the selected Panamanian bank. Payment narrative must state: 'Capital endowment - Private Interest Foundation [legal name]' or 'Aportación de capital inicial - Fundación [legal name]', accompanied by a board-of-council resolution authorizing receipt of the endowment. The bank's compliance unit will require a source-of-funds letter, proof of the founder's identity, and the DGI beneficial-ownership declaration made prior to first credit. Because Panama has no exchange-control filing, no central-bank license or purpose-code approval is required; however, the wire must not be split to avoid round-sum treatment, and cash deposits above USD 10,000 trigger STR reporting under UAF rules. For a foundation whose charter sets a minimum capital, the injection should equal or exceed that stated capital, with the constitution act referencing the payment.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 serial payments for pre-paid T/T settlements to electronics suppliers in China and APAC; (2) Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) issued by the Panamanian bank under UCP 600 for container-volume procurement from verified vendors; (3) direct USD clearings through US correspondent banks (e.g., JPMorgan Chase, Citibank, or Bank of America correspondent networks used by Banco General/BAC/Banistmo) to final beneficiary accounts; (4) PSP settlement inbound rails from Stripe and PayPal, which aggregate consumer card payments and deposit net proceeds in USD into the PIF operational account; (5) for CNY-denominated supplier invoices where USD settlement is not available, a fee-based conversion through the bank's FX desk (spot USD/CNH) or a binding NDF is used, with the underlying trade contract evidenced to the FX desk. No documentary LC is required for parcel-level e-commerce supply; a stand-by LC may be used to collateralize a Chinese trading-company credit line where the PIF desires open-account terms.Outright USD/CNY non-deliverable forward (NDF) executed through an SBP-licensed Panamanian bank or an international counterparty with a recognized dealing capability (settlement in USD regardless of CNY movement)Deliverable USD/EUR outright forward for European inventory sourcing, with standard ISDA 1998 or CSA documentation where applicableUSD/CNH deliverable forward contracts via correspondent branch network for vendors able to receive CNYCross-currency swap (USD to EUR or CNY) for term-matched liquidity hedging of fixed procurement commitmentsPlain-vanilla FX options (USD calls/puts on CNY or EUR) for conditional procurement pipelines, permitted without regulatory pre-approvalZero-cost collar structures combining a put and call on USD/CNY where vendor payment timing is uncertain

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW. Panama has no specialist electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime analogous to UK FCA e-money licenses or Lithuanian EMI authorizations; bank-account opening for a corporate foundation requires an SBP-licensed commercial bank. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Mercury, Payoneer, Airwallex) generally classify Panama-domiciled Private Interest Foundations as high-structure risk, require physical or substantive regional nexus, or reject them outright because the PIF lacks a licensed-entity status recognized by the EMI's third-party compliance stack. Legal constraints: Law 23 of 2015 and SBP general regulations require the PIF to identify all authorized signatories and beneficial owners through a DGI-registered beneficial-ownership declaration; EMIs unable to access the Panamanian Beneficial Ownership Registry typically decline onboarding. Additionally, the target payment rails (Stripe, PayPal) serve as the PSP layer, and funds ultimately must clear into a domestic banking account; therefore the optimal structure is a PAN-licensed bank as master settlement account with Stripe/PayPal as acquirer gateways, not an offshore EMI as treasury home.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (progressive schedule per Law 134 of 2023: 19% on taxable income up to PAB 50,000; 24% on income from PAB 50,001 to PAB 1,500,000; 25% on income exceeding PAB 1,500,000; the Panamanian Balboa is pegged 1:1 to the US dollar, which circulates as legal tender)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% dividend withholding tax on distributions made out of Panama-source profits that have been subject to corporate tax (Article 731, Fiscal Code); distributions sourced from foreign-source exempt reserves are not subject to dividend WHT if properly evidenced; Law 134 of 2023 introduces reduced rates (5%) for certain small-taxpayer distributions within defined brackets.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 5,000,000 (US dollar/Balboa). A taxpayer whose related-party transactions in a fiscal year exceed USD 5,000,000 must file Form 430 (Declaración Informativa de Precios de Transferencia) and prepare a full transfer-pricing study. Entities belonging to multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding USD 135,000,000 must additionally prepare a Master File and Local File; Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue exceeding USD 750,000,000 under OECD-aligned rules.

Economic substance scope: The Panamanian Economic Substance Act (Law 254 of 2019; Executive Decree 345 of 2019) applies to legal entities, including PIFs, that carry on 'relevant activities' in or from Panama. Online retail conducted through a Panamanian distribution centre or with staff soliciting, negotiating, or concluding contracts in Panama will be classified as a 'distribution and service centre' and will require local substance, including a local office, qualified personnel, and direction of core income-generating activities in Panama. A purely passive PIF holding foreign e-commerce contracts and receiving book-outsourced revenue outside Panama is not subject to ESR; however, the DGI may disregard artificial arrangements that lack substance and recharacterize them as Panama-source.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (underreporting Panama-source income)

Statutory sanction: Civil surcharge of up to 50%-150% of the evaded tax, plus interest; criminal tax-fraud penalties include imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, increased to up to 12 years for aggravated offences involving multiple schemes or large values; applicable fine of USD 5,000 to USD 50,000 or more depending on tax amount involved.

Director personal liability: Directors, Foundation Council members, and the resident agent who knowingly sign false returns or consent to the omission are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, surcharges, and penalties; courts may pierce the corporate veil, impose asset embargoes and travel bans, and order personal criminal custody.
Shell Company / sham foundation

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations administrative fines of USD 15,000 to USD 25,000 per violation and per year; repeat violations can trigger deregistration of the foundation, nullification of its acts, and reassessment of taxes for up to five prior years.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members and the fiduciary who falsely certify substance reports are personally liable for fines; fraudulent simulation may expose beneficial owners to anti-money-laundering prosecution with forfeiture of assets and criminal penalties.
Transfer Pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: DGI may adjust the taxable base upward, apply a 20% surcharge on the adjusted tax, and impose fines up to USD 5,000 for failure to file informative returns; daily interest continues to accrue until settlement.

Director personal liability: Directors who disregard TP obligations after written DGI notice may be personally liable for the reassessed tax and fines; malicious cross-border transfer pricing that shifts taxable profits abroad is treated as tax fraud and can lead to criminal indictment and travel restrictions.
Dividend withholding and repatriation evasion

Statutory sanction: The PIF is liable for the withheld tax and becomes subject to a 20% surcharge plus interest; false characterization of dividends as deductible fees triggers disallowance, a double economic tax charge, and fines of up to USD 10,000; criminal sanctions apply for fraudulent repatriation schemes.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members and the resident agent who authorize unauthorized distributions or knowingly mischaracterize cross-border payments are personally liable for the deficiency; the DGI may impose personal asset embargoes, exit bans, and, in money-laundering-connected cases, up to 12 years' imprisonment.
