Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Panama; operational online portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa); electronic invoicing platform operated through the same DGI web services.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must: (1) maintain a licensed Panamanian resident agent and a registered office; (2) submit to the DGI the certified Public Registry inscription certificate (Ficha Registral) of the foundation together with the foundation charter and powers of attorney; (3) file the RUC inscription application (Formulario de Inscripción al Registro Único de Contribuyente) via the DGI portal or through a licensed Panamanian CPA; (4) obtain the RUC number and, where taxable activities are carried out, activate the ITBMS taxpayer registration; (5) obtain a DGI-recognized digital certificate (certificado digital) for mandatory electronic invoicing. The initial endowment/minimum capital requirement established in the foundation charter must be evidenced to the DGI; no separate statutory paid-in capital threshold applies to PIFs.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope. The PIF is a distinct legal person classified as a non-commercial foundation for Panamanian tax purposes. Income sourced and effectively realized outside Panama is outside the Panamanian tax net; only Panamanian-source income is subject to the 25% corporate income tax (ISR) and the 7% ITBMS. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope. The PIF is a distinct legal person classified as a non-commercial foundation for Panamanian tax purposes. Income sourced and effectively realized outside Panama is outside the Panamanian tax net; only Panamanian-source income is subject to the 25% corporate income tax (ISR) and the 7% ITBMS. The PIF is not a pass-through entity and is not subject to entity-level tax on foreign-source assets or income.

Available election forms

Panama applies a territorial/source-based tax system without a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections for a PIF include: (a) registration in the simplified Small Taxpayer Regime (Pequeño Contribuyente / Régimen Simplificado) where annual gross taxable turnover does not exceed B/.36,000; (b) the option to maintain an offshore character under the 1995 Foundation Law, with no Panamanian income tax exposure while no Panamanian-source income arises; (c) election of the accelerated ITBMS export credit refund procedure (reintegro rápido) for zero-rated exporters; and (d) election of a special tax-exempt zone regime (e.g., SEM, Zone Franca, or Panama Pacifico) when the activity qualifies. No hybrid-mismatch election or deemed dividend election exists under Panamanian domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 (Balboas, approximately USD 36,000 at the fixed 1:1 parity) of annual gross taxable turnover for the simplified Pequeño Contribuyente regime; full ITBMS registration is mandatory when taxable supplies exceed this threshold or when the taxpayer opts for regular-taxpayer status. Non-resident digital services supplied into Panama may create a registration obligation where the DGI determines a Panama-source supply or a taxable local user base.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% ITBMS applies when merchandise is physically exported from Panama under a customs export declaration evidencing departure. Services, including the email API and newsletter hosting platform: 0% ITBMS applies when the service is rendered to a non-resident recipient and the benefit is effectively received or used abroad; cross-border digital services qualify if the client is established outside Panama and the place of consumption is foreign. The PIF must demonstrate that subscribers, users, and payment rails are foreign, and the invoice issued to the foreign client must state that it covers a zero-rated export service to a non-resident.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera/DUA or DUCA transmitted through the SIGA customs system to the DGI and National Customs Authority)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure of the goods from Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) evidencing receipt of the export price through the Panamanian banking system; for services, bank statements showing settlement of Stripe/credit-card income into a Panamanian bank account
  • Commercial invoice and export service contract identifying the non-resident client, the Panamanian PIF exporter, and the place of consumption abroad
  • Supporting server/IP and user-domicile logs or customer records where relevant, plus certificate of origin if requested by the DGI

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) applies in Panama; exporters, large taxpayers, and progressively all ITBMS taxpayers must issue invoices through DGI-approved channels using DGI-assigned invoice ranges and a fiscal QR/digital stamp generated by the DGI e-invoicing API. Invoices are validated in near-real-time with a PKI-based digital certificate; XML/CII structured data are transmitted to the DGI e-invoicing web service. Traditional printed 'bollo' fiscal forms are being phased out.

The DGI audits zero-rated exporters under the territorial-source and repatriation rules. Claiming 0% ITBMS without customs documentation, valid service contracts, or payment/repatriation proof is treated as illegal invoicing and abusive zero-rating. Consequences include: denial and retrospective clawback of the ITBMS credit refund; fines of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax; statutory default interest and surcharges under the Código Fiscal; possible criminal liability for tax evasion; and, where export proceeds are not brought into the Panamanian banking system within the 180-day window, reclassification of the income as Panama-source with exposure to 25% income tax, additional surcharges, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama imposes no exchange controls and maintains full convertibility under both capital and current account classifications. There is no central-bank exchange approval, no capital repatriation permit, and no differentiation between hard and soft currencies. Profit remittances from Panama to foreign beneficiaries are unrestricted, subject only to the tax rules of Panama's territorial taxation regime and standard SBP-mandated anti-money-laundering KYC evidence. For the offshore email newsletter and SaaS platform activity of a Private Interest Foundation (PIF), all receipts may be held, cleared, and disbursed in USD with no conversion or surrender requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation exists for export or services proceeds. Panama is not a central-bank dollar-rationing regime. Entities may retain 100% of their export/service revenue in USD. The only requirements are ordinary banking documentation, source-of-funds traceability, and suspicious-transaction reporting obligations under SBP AML/CFT regulations.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama has no independent central bank. The monetary system is fully dollarized with the United States dollar as legal tender. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) regulates all banking, foreign-exchange-adjacent, and AML/CFT activities; monetary policy is conducted essentially through U.S. dollar monetary conditions, administered regionally by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) and SBP supervisory policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium only for secondary settlement channels, not as primary treasury infrastructure. Panama permits licensed fintech and payment system participants to operate within its bank-regulated framework, but offshore digital EMIs without an SBP license cannot clear into Panama's local USD settlement network. Offshore EMIs or neobanks holding European or other foreign licenses are not recognized as local banks in Panama and may create correspondent-bank derisking, prohibited payment service exposure, and card-scheme non-compliance. An SBP-regulated bank, or a Panamanian bank's licensed digital banking arm, must be used as the governed clearing endpoint for Stripe Billing, credit card, and API-related settlement flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from founders or external investors must be routed through international USD wire rails, principally SWIFT MT103 or U.S. Fedwire, with the PIF's exact legal name, Public Registry foundation certificate number, and beneficiary account number included in the payment message. The wire purpose field should unambiguously read 'Capital contribution to Panama Private Interest Foundation' or 'Subscription of foundation assets'. Panama imposes no central-bank filing or exchange-control preapproval, but the receiving SBP-regulated bank will require a concurrent submission package: foundation capital subscription instrument, foundation council resolution approving the contribution, source-of-funds evidence for each remitter, passport copies and certified corporate records of the contributing entity, and the PIF's bank account application. Where a non-resident corporate shareholder or funding company is involved, include its certificate of good standing, directors register, UBO declaration, and audited financial statements to avoid correspondent-bank return.For operational disbursements to overseas vendors and infrastructure suppliers, the clearing protocol prioritizes (i) SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers through a Panama tier-1 bank, using the full U.S.-dollar correspondent clearing footprint of CHIPS or Fedwire; (ii) local USD clearing through Panama's automated clearinghouse for domestic subscription payments and payroll; (iii) commercial letters of credit only for high-value hardware or data-center procurement when contractually required, with standard UCP600 terms; and (iv) card-scheme payouts routed through Stripe's acquiring bank into the PIF's Panama treasury account via merchant-payout MT103 or automated settlement file. All transfers must include a precise purpose narrative, standard description 'email SaaS subscription / API infrastructure disbursement', and the ultimate beneficiary's legal name. PCI DSS requirements must be preserved because card settlement data flows through Stripe and the Panama bank. For any counterparty in an OFAC-watch country or high-risk industry, an enhanced due-diligence memo and balance attestation must be attached to the treasury file.Deliverable U.S. dollar foreign-exchange forward contracts with Panama tier-1 banks or their international correspondents against USD/EUR, USD/GBP, USD/COP, USD/MXN, and USD/BRL to hedge any non-USD revenue or operating expenses, subject to an approved foundation council treasury policy.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) executed through U.S. or international counterparty desks for restricted or non-clearing currencies such as USD/CNY, USD/ARS, and other manageability-constrained emerging-market currencies, with risk mitigation limits and a prohibition on speculative trading.Cross-currency swaps for long-dated infrastructure or acquired-company liabilities, documented with a formal hedge rationale and foundation council authorization, provided no leverage or speculative output is created and all derivative transactions are recorded in the PIF's books.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard Corporate Income Tax on Panamanian-source income under the territoriality principle, Article 694 of the Tax Code)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% complementary dividend tax (impuesto complementario) on dividends paid out of Panamanian-taxed profits (Tax Code Article 733); 20% final distribution tax on dividends paid out of untaxed or exempt profits unless the PIF elects to pay the 10% catch-up complementary tax on such profits, in which case the 20% distribution tax is waived.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months after the fiscal year-end; for calendar-year taxpayers the deadline is 31 March of the following year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Each instalment equals 25% of the prior year's final income tax liability and is payable during the months of April, July, October and December of the running fiscal year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due concurrently with Form 105 (within three months after fiscal year-end, i.e., 31 March for calendar-year filers) only when the statutory transfer pricing threshold is exceeded.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panamanian balboa (B/.) threshold: taxpayers with annual consolidated gross revenue equal to or above B/.10 million must file Form 910 and prepare Local and Master Files; taxpayers falling below B/.10 million are still required to prepare contemporaneous local documentation when related-party transactions exceed B/.2.5 million per counterparty or when a counterparty is resident in a low-tax or preferential regime.

Economic substance scope: Law 254 of 2021 and Executive Decree 105 of 2022 apply the Economic Substance Regulations to PIFs engaged in relevant activities. The email-newsletter infrastructure platform, software licensing and IP holding constitute relevant activities and the PIF must demonstrate in Panama: (i) core income-generating activities performed locally (development, hosting oversight, client support, contract management); (ii) adequate physical premises and full-time employees in Panama; (iii) sufficient local operating expenditure commensurate with the income; and (iv) board or foundation council meetings held in Panama. A pure equity-holding PIF benefits from reduced substance requirements but must still file the annual Economic Substance Declaration with the DGI.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Penal Code Article 284 and Tax Code Article 761-A: imprisonment of 2-5 years, criminal fines, civil penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax, plus monthly 1% default interest and the nullity of the fraudulent return.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members and signatories are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt when false returns were signed or corporate funds were diverted; criminal arrest, DGI travel ban (inhibición de salida del país), asset embargo and disqualification from acting as directors.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to B/.1,000,000 per infraction; cancellation of the Panamanian tax residence certificate; recharacterization of income as taxable in the beneficiary jurisdiction; the PIF's separate legal personality may be disregarded as a mere facade.

Director personal liability: Members of the Foundation Council and the Resident Agent face personal fines; repeated or wilful false declarations expose them to imprisonment for false testimony before the DGI and to travel restrictions pending resolution of substance audits.
AML / Money Laundering and False Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to B/.1,000,000 imposed by the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF); freezing and confiscation of accounts; money-laundering offences carry 5-12 years of imprisonment; the PIF's foundation charter may be cancelled.

Director personal liability: Directors, ultimate beneficial owners and the Resident Agent incur joint criminal and civil liability; courts pierce the corporate veil where the PIF has no substance; travel bans, preventive detention and international asset-seizure orders are routinely granted.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: DGI adjustment of taxable income to the arm's length result; 30% surcharge on the omitted tax; 1% monthly interest; fines of B/.10,000-B/.50,000 for missing Form 910; automatic extension of the statute of limitations while documentation remains outstanding.

Director personal liability: Officers who certified a false Form 910 face fines and imprisonment (2-5 years); parent-group directors may be held personally liable for the tax differential on intercompany pricing; travel bans and embargoes apply during the assessment phase.
