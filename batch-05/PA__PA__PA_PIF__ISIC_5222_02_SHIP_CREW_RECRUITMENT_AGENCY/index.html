Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office service (including a local registered address and agent) is legally permissible for the Private Interest Foundation's registered office obligation under Law 25 of 1995 (Art. 15). However, in the specific ISIC 5222 maritime crew recruitment context, the physical presence must be adequate to meet the functional substance test of Panama's tax territoriality principle and the Maritime Sector Authority (AMP) requirements. A call center / virtual location may be accepted for pure back-office functions, but for active business licensing by the AMP (Habilitación de Agencia de Contratación de Tripulantes), an actual local office and a full-time in-country operations manager must be demonstrated. The use of an all-inclusive 'registered agent office' is acceptable at incorporation stage; the agency must transition to a physical office with a letterhead and personnel within six (6) months of commencing crewing operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas de la República de Panamá; official online portal: Sede Electrónica DGI (dgi.mef.gob.pa) and e-Tax/ITBMS filing platform.

E-government filing portal

A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) formed for crew recruitment must first register its charter with the Public Registry of Panama, appoint a licensed resident agent, and then obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) from the DGI. The foreign-parent application is submitted by a Panama licensed public accountant or the resident agent with: (i) public deed of foundation formation; (ii) DGI form 01 and providing details on directors, protector (if any), and beneficial ownership; (iii) proof of registered address of the resident agent; (iv) passport copies of the founder, director, and beneficiary; and (v) bank reference. DGI issues a provisional RUC on filing, a definitive RUC post verification, and an e-invoice authorization code for future billing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a federal check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election. A PIF may elect, on a rolling annual basis, to be treated as a non-tax-resident entity for territorial income purposes and to opt out of bank secrecy protections by filing an express waiver with the DGI. No pass-through election exists; instead, distributions made by the PIF to non-resident beneficiaries are generally outside Panamanian tax and ITBMS.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 0 – no general monetary threshold; registration is mandatory upon the first taxable act in Panama. A simplified small-taxpayer regime (monotributo or pequeña empresa) is available for gross annual revenue up to USD 36,000, but it does not suspend the duty to obtain an RUC and issue authorized invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Crew recruitment and maritime manning services qualify for ITBMS zero rating as international services where the recipient is a non-resident shipowner or operator, the services are performed outside Panama territory (including on high seas), and the benefit is entirely consumed abroad. The PIF must hold a valid RUC, provide a signed manning/crew management agreement identifying the foreign principal, maintain crew payroll records, and be able to evidence that the remuneration was invoiced to the foreign entity. For tangible exports (e.g., crew supplies or vessel stores), zero rating further requires a customs export declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

DGI requires all ITBMS taxpayers to issue invoices under strictly authorized series and folios. Large taxpayers (declared as such by DGI or with annual gross revenue above USD 1,000,000) must migrate to the electronic invoicing system (Sistema de Facturación Electrónica – SFE/FEP), transmit invoice data to DGI through a certified fiscal printer or REST/API-based billing platform, and include a machine-readable fiscal code (QR/barcode) generated by the DGI authorizing server. In practice this is an online pre-authorization with periodic transmission, not real-time single-invoice clearance; digital stamping is achieved via the DGI authorization codes.

The DGI may re-open zero-rated transactions for five fiscal years. False or forged export documents supporting ITBMS zero rating trigger: (i) disqualification of the zero rating with full 7% ITBMS assessed on the gross invoice amount; (ii) fines of 100% to 300% of the omitted tax, plus monthly surcharges; (iii) retroactive clawback of any claimed ITBMS input credits; (iv) for crew-service exports, the deemed Panama-source income tax (flat 35% for a non-compliant PIF) with the difference added to the tax rolls; and (v) criminal liability under Law 8 of 2010 (Tax Crimes) with imprisonment of two to ten years, applicable to the resident director and the accountant signing the returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility in both current and capital accounts. There are no exchange control laws, no central bank approval gates for inbound or outbound capital, no profit repatriation license, and no restriction on contracting or invoicing in foreign currencies. The territorial tax regime exempts foreign-source income from corporate income tax, and no withholding applies on distributions to foundation beneficiaries. The only practical impedance is bank-level compliance: SBP Acuerdo 26-2021 requires banks to evidence the commercial substance, source of funds, ultimate beneficial owners, and the underlying crewing contract before executing large or cross-border transfers for a Private Interest Foundation (PIF), particularly because Panama is currently under FATF enhanced monitoring (grey-listed in June 2024).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion to Balboa, no repatriation deadline, and no export-proceeds declaration for service income. Crew recruitment and manning fees may be held in USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency in Panama or abroad, for any period, without penalty or reporting to the SBP, the UAF, or the DGI, subject only to general AML record-keeping duties.

Central bank supervision: Panama operates without a conventional central bank or independent monetary authority. The US Dollar is legal tender (Law of 1904; Decree Law 9 of 1904), and the Balboa (PAB) is only peripheral coinage pegged 1:1 to USD. Banking licensing, prudential supervision, and AML/CFT oversight rest with the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP, per Decree Law 2 of 2008); Banco Nacional de Panamá functions as the state depositary fiscal agent; the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) oversees financial policy; and the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) serves as the Financial Intelligence Unit for STR filing and beneficial-ownership validation under Law 23 of 2015 and Law 254 of 2021.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High as a secondary collection or disbursement rail after an anchor Tier-1 Panamanian account exists; Low as a standalone replacement. Panama legally enables fintech via Ley 169 of 2020 (SBP Innovation Hub and regulatory sandbox) and Ley 697 of 2023 (digital assets, with SBP implementing rules), but these laws do not charter offshore EMIs to clear USD domestically. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Mercury, Currenxie) accept Panamanian PIFs only on a case-by-case basis and always impose: (i) proof of real economic substance (registered office, operative maritime manning license, financial statements); (ii) a complete and current Law 254/2021 beneficial-ownership report; (iii) a professional bank reference from a Tier-1 Panamanian bank; and (iv) enhanced due diligence triggered by Panama's FATF grey-list status. EMIs provide no SBP deposit protection, no UAF filing interface, no escrow function, and no documentary letter-of-credit capability. Legally viable only for auxiliary liquidity concentration of multi-currency payroll flows, never as the PIF's primary regulated treasury bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Panama imposes no exchange control, so no statutory purpose-code scheme exists; the SBP, correspondent banks, and the clearing network rely on SWIFT narrative descriptors. Recommended initial-injection protocol: the founder wires via MT103 from the founder's personal account, with field 70 reading 'Contribution to Initial Foundation Capital of [PIF Name]', field 50K showing the founder's legal name, and field 59 naming the PIF's Panamanian account. The USD clearing path runs SWIFT to the Panama bank's New York correspondent (e.g., Citi or JPMorgan), then inward via the SBP-supervised ACH/RTGS settlement into the PIF's operational account. No statutory capital escrow is required for a PIF under Law 25 of 1995, but banking practice demands an initial endowment commensurate with the declared activity (typically USD 10,000 to USD 100,000) and full traceability. Pre-funding verification requires: (i) the foundation charter and council resolution approving the endowment; (ii) apostilled and legalized copies; (iii) the full source-of-funds package of the founder; (iv) for amounts above USD 50,000, a contemporaneous capital statement. For commercial inflows, shipowners remit crew-service fees by MT103 referencing the invoice and the AMP manning contract; no additional purpose code is needed beyond the wire narrative.The primary clearing rail for crew payroll and trade suppliers is SWIFT MT103 from the PIF's operational account; no Panamanian regulatory approval is required, and the SBP network settles USD same-day. For bulk payroll, the client submits a batch of MT103 instructions or an MT101 multi-transfer request, each leg carrying deterministic narration such as 'Seafarer payroll [month/year] - [crew contract reference]'. The bank screen-searches each beneficiary against OFAC/UAF sanction lists at the moment of payment; therefore the full payroll register must be delivered to the bank no later than T-2 before payday. For shipowners and port agents requiring trade instruments, deferred crew-management invoices may be settled by commercial documentary credits (MT700) against presentation of signed delivery orders and crew release certificates, while performance obligations are collateralized by standby letters of credit (MT760) issued in favor of shipowners or port agents. Multi-currency disbursement (EUR invoices, Philippine Peso payroll, Indonesian Rupiah wages) is executed by the Panama bank's conversion at the daily rate or under a pre-agreed forward, with the beneficiary paid in local currency through the correspondent network. Treasury discipline demands three segregated accounts at the same Tier-1 bank: a Payroll Operating Account, a Trade Settlement Account for LCs and standbys, and a Foundation Treasury Account for capital surplus; commingling with unregulated EMIs is prohibited as a matter of policy.Outright deliverable FX forwards (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, CHF/USD and selected Asian crew currencies) with SBP-licensed banks under an ISDA Master Agreement, matched to seafarer payroll cycles and shipowner invoices.FX swaps and rolling spot forwards for intra-month USD liquidity management and conversion of mixed-currency receivables into the payroll base currency.Cross-currency swaps (fixed-to-fixed USD versus EUR or GBP) for long-dated crewing contracts exceeding approximately USD 5 million in cumulative counterparty commitment, executed only with a licensed Panama bank or its international branch.Vanilla currency options (calls and puts on EUR, GBP, PHP, IDR) for contingent hedging of wage-rate fluctuations in seafarer source jurisdictions.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) solely in offshore non-convertible crew currencies (e.g., PHP, IDR, BDT) via an international panel bank, since no NDF market exists on the USD/PAB leg where the US Dollar is legal tender at a fixed 1:1 peg.All derivatives must be intermediated by an SBP-regulated bank; direct bilateral swap arrangements with unlicensed third parties are impermissible and void for banking-law purposes.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium-High as a secondary collection or disbursement rail after an anchor Tier-1 Panamanian account exists; Low as a standalone replacement. Panama legally enables fintech via Ley 169 of 2020 (SBP Innovation Hub and regulatory sandbox) and Ley 697 of 2023 (digital assets, with SBP implementing rules), but these laws do not charter offshore EMIs to clear USD domestically. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Mercury, Currenxie) accept Panamanian PIFs only on a case-by-case basis and always impose: (i) proof of real economic substance (registered office, operative maritime manning license, financial statements); (ii) a complete and current Law 254/2021 beneficial-ownership report; (iii) a professional bank reference from a Tier-1 Panamanian bank; and (iv) enhanced due diligence triggered by Panama's FATF grey-list status. EMIs provide no SBP deposit protection, no UAF filing interface, no escrow function, and no documentary letter-of-credit capability. Legally viable only for auxiliary liquidity concentration of multi-currency payroll flows, never as the PIF's primary regulated treasury bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required when annual gross revenues exceed B/ 3,000,000 or related-party transactions exceed B/ 1,000,000. Master File required when consolidated gross revenue exceeds B/ 6,000,000. Penalties for non-compliance: up to B/ 50,000 plus a 5% surcharge on the recharacterized adjustment.

Economic substance scope: Panama has not issued standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) analogous to BVI/Cayman. However, the DGI can invoke anti-abuse provisions (Arts. 760-761 Fiscal Code) and the Beneficial Ownership Law (No. 23 of 2015) to challenge shell structures. For ship crew recruitment, the entity must hold a valid SEP/CPR license and maintain real presence: a physical office in Panama, resident agent, payroll records, and vessel crew contracts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine equal to 100% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment of 2 to 5 years under Panama's Penal Tax Law (No. 14 of 2009). The DGI may also order suspension of the PIF's beneficial ownership permits.

Director personal liability: Directors and Foundation Council members are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax. A court may issue travel bans, freeze personal accounts, and impose imprisonment against individuals who signed false declarations.
Shell Company / Inadequate Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The DGI can disregard the legal personality under Article 760, impose a 100% penalty on undeclared tax, and revoke the foundation's plumbing (permanent establishment) status. Fines can reach B/ 250,000 for repeated violations.

Director personal liability: Directors can be required to pay the foundation's unpaid corporate tax from personal assets (civil liability). Flagrant abuse may trigger criminal money-laundering prosecution with a 6-year custodial sentence.
Anti-Money-Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties under Law 23 of 2015: fines up to USD 1,000,000 per violation, plus revocation of the crew recruitment license. Criminal anti-money laundering sanctions may impose 5 to 12 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face criminal imprisonment for willful AML non-compliance. Travel bans, civil asset forfeiture, and being barred from serving as officers in Panama are automatic upon conviction.
Failure to Report Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of B/ 500 to B/ 2,500 per late month, and potential cancellation of the PIF's Certificate of Good Standing if the default persists for more than 90 days.

Director personal liability: The Resident Agent and Foundation Council members are jointly liable for the fine. Persistent non-compliance may result in an order for dissolution of the foundation and disqualification of the directors from acting in any Panamanian commercial entity.
