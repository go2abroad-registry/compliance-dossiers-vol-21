Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Panama's national tax administration; e-filing portal: Sede Electrónica / e-Tax Panama (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa). Export customs clearance is administered by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) through the Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior (VUCE).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF): (1) appoint a licensed Panamanian attorney/resident agent; (2) register the PIF instrument at the Registro Público under Law 25 of 1995; (3) if the PIF will carry on any Panamanian-source commercial activity or export sales, file the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) application with the DGI within 30 days of commencement, submitting the registered foundation instrument, resident-agent acceptance, founder/council identification and the CIIU/ISIC 3240 activity code; (4) obtain an electronic signature and activate the DGI Sede Electrónica taxpayer profile; (5) obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación and register in the ANA/VUCE exporter database; (6) a purely passive PIF holding offshore assets requires no RUC or ISR/ITBMS registration, but the annual foundation annuity to the Public Registry/Treasury must be paid to maintain legal good standing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election regime. Available elections: (a) foundation-instrument election to limit the PIF to passive offshore asset holding, keeping it outside Panamanian territorial ISR/ITBMS taxation; (b) voluntary election to register as an ITBMS general-regime taxpayer to access export zero-rating and input-credit refunds; (c) simplified small-taxpayer election (Law 88 of 2010) for local gross receipts up to B/.36,000 per year, replacing ISR and ITBMS with a single 2%-5% tributo único; and (d) mandatory territorial sourcing — no election exists to tax foreign-source income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general de-minimis turnover threshold: any person habitually transferring taxable goods or rendering taxable services in Panama must register for ITBMS from the first taxable operation. A simplified small-taxpayer regime (single tax of 2%-5% on gross receipts) is available where annual gross income does not exceed B/.36,000 (Balboa at par with USD). Exporters are required to register under the general ITBMS regime even when they have no domestic taxable turnover, so as to lawfully claim the 0% export rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: supplies of movable goods physically exported from Panama are zero-rated, provided the goods leave Panamanian customs territory through the ANA within the applicable export period and are covered by a valid DUA in the exporter's name. Services: services rendered to non-resident recipients without a Panamanian establishment are zero-rated as export services when the benefit is consumed wholly abroad, payment originates from foreign currency outside Panama, and the supplier is an ITBMS general-regime taxpayer. Input ITBMS attributable to zero-rated export activities is creditable and excess credits are refundable (devolución de saldo a favor) by the DGI; the exporter must file the periodic ITBMS return with the export schedule and maintain the complete documentary bundle. Although Panama is dollarized and imposes no statutory FX-repatriation rule, bankable practice requires the inward USD credit to appear within 180 days of export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera, DUA) electronically certified by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA), identifying the exporter's RUC, the HS/CIIU 3240 commodity code and the actual departure date from Panama
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued to the order of the Panamanian exporter, evidencing physical receipt, shipment and export of the silicone stress-relief fidget toys to the foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice from a licensed Panamanian bank evidencing settlement in US Dollars (Balboa at par) by the foreign buyer within the 180-day documentary benchmark

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) under the DGI's e-Tax program, phased in by taxpayer segment (already binding for large and medium taxpayers). General-regime taxpayers must issue invoices through a DGI-accredited PSFE and transmit the invoice XML to the DGI Sede Electrónica clearance API in real time to obtain a digital validation stamp (QR/XML reference) before the invoice is delivered to the buyer. Export e-invoices must contain the ANA export declaration (DUA) number and the zero-rated export reason code. Invoices without the DGI validation code are invalid for ITBMS credits and cannot substantiate export zero-rating.

The DGI may re-assess zero-rated exports within the general 5-year limitation period (extended to 7 years for unregistered taxpayers). If export substantiation fails, the 7% ITBMS is clawed back retroactively together with: (i) surcharges of 10% (first 2 months), 20% (2–6 months) or 30% (beyond 6 months); (ii) compensatory interest; (iii) fines of B/.250–B/.10,000 for false or defective invoicing; and (iv) an evasion penalty of up to 100% of the omitted tax. Falsified DUAs, simulated exports, forged Bills of Lading or sham inward remittances are tax and customs fraud punishable by 2–5 years' imprisonment, RUC suspension, loss of refund rights and joint liability of the registered agent and the foundation council; previously refunded credits are recoverable with interest from the refund date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized monetary system with zero exchange controls. Both the US dollar and national balboa circulate as legal tender. Capital account and current account transactions are freely convertible and reversible, with no surrender requirements, no movement restrictions, and no profit repatriation hurdles. Taxes may still apply on territorial source income, but there is no FX approval mechanism.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rules exist under Panamanian law. Exporters may retain and freely dispose of foreign currency proceeds, typically through commercial banks.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) – Panama does not maintain a distinct central bank; the SBP is the banking supervisor and FX compliance authority for licensed commercial banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI (electronic money institution) viability is Low-to-Medium. Panama does not yet have a dedicated EMI licensing regime, so foreign EMIs may only operate through locally licensed banks, trust companies, or payment processing partnerships. The SBP requires physical or risk-based compliance and does not grant standalone digital licenses to unregulated offshore EMIs.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant injections are effected by SWIFT wire from the founding shareholder or parent entity to the PIF's operational or escrow bank account, with purpose code 'A1 – Direct Investment' or 'K1 – Capital Contribution' as accepted by the receiving Panamanian bank. The bank holds the foundation charter and board resolution on file and may require a source-of-funds declaration for high-value transfers. Marketplace receipts through 'Platform_Pay' are channeled as operating revenues into the same account once the marketplace settlement processor is registered with the bank; no separate exchange control approval is necessary.Outbound supplier payments are cleared via SWIFT MT103 for open account, advance payment, and pre-shipment transactions, or by an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit for larger silicone procurement cycles. The paying bank requires underlying invoices, packing lists, transport documents, and the commercial purpose code corresponding to import of goods. Any Platform_Pay refunds or chargebacks are also executed via SWIFT MT103 using the appropriate general refund purpose code.USD-denominated accounts eliminate the need for local FX hedging; where CNY, EUR, or other trade currencies are involved, Panamanian banks offer deliverable currency forwards and cross-currency swaps to settle expected trade flows.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible for currencies subject to domestic transfer restrictions, notably the offshore CNY NDF market for Chinese silicone suppliers.Foreign currency options and structured collars are available for large procurement cycles from tier 1 banks, though they are subject to client risk classification and credit limits.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Ingresos Brutos Total gross revenue derived from Panama-source activities, including local sales of silicone fidget toys and services rendered in Panama. Enter zero if all revenue is foreign-source. A Private Interest Foundation deriving only offshore income must file a nil return and separately disclose exempt foreign-source income. Box 3 - Renta Neta Gravable Net taxable income after deductions and exemptions. This is the base for applying the statutory rate. Should be zero for a foundation with no Panama-source income or after exhaustion of loss carryforwards. Late filing triggers a surcharge of 10% of the tax due plus 1.5% monthly interest (or fraction thereof), capped at 50% of the tax. Additional fines under Tax Code Article 715 can reach USD 5,000. Criminal prosecution for willful non-filing can result in imprisonment of up to two years.
Box 4 - Impuesto a la Renta (25%) Computed corporate income tax at the 25% statutory rate on Box 3. Leave zero when Box 3 is nil. Tax credits cannot generate a refund unless provided by law. Late filing triggers a surcharge of 10% of the tax due plus 1.5% monthly interest (or fraction thereof), capped at 50% of the tax. Additional fines under Tax Code Article 715 can reach USD 5,000. Criminal prosecution for willful non-filing can result in imprisonment of up to two years.
Box 5 - Retenciones y Créditos Advance income tax payments, foreign tax credits, withholdings suffered, and statutory investment credits. Include only credits genuinely sourced from Panama operations. Non-resident foundations typically have no such credits. Late filing triggers a surcharge of 10% of the tax due plus 1.5% monthly interest (or fraction thereof), capped at 50% of the tax. Additional fines under Tax Code Article 715 can reach USD 5,000. Criminal prosecution for willful non-filing can result in imprisonment of up to two years.

Any tax due must be paid by wire transfer to the DGI identified collection account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 3,000,000 in related-party transactions per fiscal year triggers mandatory Local File / Master File documentation under Panama's transfer pricing rules; lower thresholds apply for transactions with entities in no-tax jurisdictions.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 70 of 2019) apply. A Private Interest Foundation actively manufacturing silicone fidget toys in Panama must demonstrate adequate physical presence, qualified employees, local management, and core income-generating activities inside Panama. Failure to substance results in reclassification, fines, and removal from the registry.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty equal to 100% of the evaded tax, plus 10% surcharge, 1.5% monthly interest, and criminal fines under Panama Criminal Code Article 283; imprisonment of 2 to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members and directors are jointly and severally liable for evaded taxes when they order, participate in, or knowingly benefit from the evasion. Courts may order civil restitution, freeze personal assets, impose travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Law 70 of 2019, fines up to USD 1,000,000, mandatory dissolution, and strike-off from the Public Registry. The entity is reclassified as fully taxable in Panama on worldwide income.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced if the foundation is a sham or alter ego. Directors and council members become personally liable for all unpaid taxes and fines; personal assets may be seized, and officers may be prohibited from leaving Panama pending resolution.
Undeclared Profit Repatriation / Dividend WHT Failure

Statutory sanction: The foundation is liable as withholding agent for the full 10% unpaid tax, plus a 50% penalty under Tax Code Article 769, interest at 1.5% monthly, and possible criminal prosecution for defrauding the fisc.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the dividend resolution or directed the payment are personally liable for the un-withheld amount. Courts may impose civil restitution, asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment of up to three years for willful non-compliance.
AML/CFT Breach in Commercial Payments

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015, fines up to USD 1,000,000 per violation, revocation of the foundation's banking arrangements, and dissolution for repeated offenses.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members are strictly liable for AML compliance failures. Penalties include personal fines up to USD 1,000,000, disqualification from serving as directors, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 10 years for money laundering offenses.
