Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope. The PIF is a separate juridical person and is not a pass-through entity for Panamanian income tax. It is subject to entity-level income tax (ISR) at 25% exclusively on Panama-source income under Código Fiscal Article 694 et seq.; all foreign-source income, including export-trading profits with legal title transferred and economic control exercised outside Panama, is exempt from ISR with no remittance restriction. ITBMS applies only to Panamanian taxable supplies; properly documented physical exports are zero-rated (0%) with entitlement to input VAT credit refunds. Distributions of foreign income by the PIF to beneficiaries are not treated as dividends and are not subject to Panamanian withholding tax.

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a 'check-the-box' or hybrid-mismatch election. The PIF is statutorily classified as a taxable juridical person, and no pass-through election is available for income tax. Relevant statutory elections: (i) election to be taxed under the simplified small-taxpayer ITBMS regime where annual gross turnover does not exceed B/.36,000; (ii) election under the CAIR (Cálculo Alterno de Impuesto sobre la Renta) alternative minimum tax computation where applicable; (iii) for Panamanian corporations, the election to pay the 2% complementary/accumulated tax on taxable income to reduce the dividend withholding rate from 10% to 5% — not available for PIF distributions, which are not classifiable as dividends; (iv) election under Law 70 of 2017 to use the accelerated PIF incorporation procedure. No election can override Panama's territorial source-of-income rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The US dollar is legal tender and circulates freely alongside the national balboa (parity basis). No external exchange controls exist; both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible without central-bank approval. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repatriation by a Private Interest Foundation are unrestricted from an FX perspective. Panama's territorial tax system does not impose a withholding tax or FX permit requirement on outbound profit remittances originating from foreign-source income; only Panamanian-source income may attract withholding, depending on the nature of the distribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Panama. Exporters retain free disposition over foreign currency receipts, and no timeline is imposed for repatriation or conversion. The absence of a central bank means that no enforced FX settlement regime applies to proceeds from cross-border e-commerce sales.

Central bank supervision: Panama does not operate a central bank; the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) supervises banking, financial, and trust activities, while the Ministry of Economy and Finance coordinates monetary and financial policy within a fully dollarized legal framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Airwallex, or Currenxie may accept a Panama Private Interest Foundation on a case-by-case basis, but their legal status is not a regulated Panamanian financial institution. Such accounts cannot directly clear against Panama's local ACH (Sistema Clave) and generally rely on a tier-one bank sponsor. Feasibility is rated Medium for secondary receivables consolidation; primary treasury balances and supplier disbursements should be settled through a licensed Panamanian commercial bank to maintain full local enforceability and audit continuity.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate on net Panamanian-source profits of a licensed commercial entity. Private Interest Foundations (PIFs) are not per se exempt from corporate income tax; they are taxed only on income from Panamanian-source activities, consistent with Panama's territorial tax model. A purely passive PIF deriving foreign-source income is generically outside the Panamanian income tax base.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends or profit distributions paid out of Panama-source taxable profits. Panamanian private interest foundation distributions to beneficiaries are generally treated as dispositions of foundation assets rather than taxable dividends when the underlying assets are not derived from Panamanian commercial activity. Where a PIF does distribute Panamanian business earnings, the 10% dividend withholding tax is required, payable with the corporate income tax return through the corresponding Form 198 (Declaración Mensual de Dividendos) and annual reconciliation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 2,500,000 (two million five hundred thousand Panamanian Balboas, at parity with USD) in annual gross income. Panamanian tax regulations require entities above this threshold to prepare and submit transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File contours) with their annual CIT return when they execute cross-border controlled transactions. For lower-income taxpayers, contemporaneous TP documentation should still be maintained if related-party transactions are material, but statutory Local/Master File filing obligations are triggered by this gross income threshold.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to entities licensed or registered in Panama carrying on relevant activities, including distribution and service center activity, financing, leasing, headquarters, holding, intellectual property holding, and shipping. A PIF that merely owns a custom keyboard keycap brand or IP and receives royalties without local management and physical operations may be challenged as lacking economic substance. ESR compliance generally requires: (i) full board and management control exercised in Panama, (ii) adequate local premises proportionate to the activity, (iii) qualified local staff, and (iv) operational spending in Panama. A PIF deriving only foreign-origin trading income through non-Panama supply chains should maintain an ESR file demonstrating why it is not tax resident in Panama or why the Panamanian relevant activity is not carried out. The absence of substantive business activity, combined with the use of personal liability foundations, exposes the structure to substance-based audits and exchange-of-information disclosures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Panamanian Corporate Income Tax

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion is punishable with a fine of up to ten times the evaded tax and penal imprisonment. Administrative re-assessment carries a 25% surcharge plus default interest at 10% per annum. The DGI may also suspend the RUC and revoke existing tax clearance certificates, freezing the entity's ability to receive payment funds through local PSPs or banks.

Director personal liability: Panamanian courts have upheld lifting the foundation/corporate veil where the foundation was an instrument of tax evasion. Directors, resident agents, and effective beneficial owners may be held jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt, subject to travel bans, asset seizures, and imprisonment of up to 5 years for fraudulent concealment.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Panama's economic substance enforcement framework and anti-money laundering rules, non-compliant entities may be subjected to fines from USD 10,000 to USD 1,000,000; cancellation of the registration and the foundation charter; loss of access to the tax exemption for foreign-source income; and mandatory revocation of the resident agent license where the agent knowingly assisted in the sham arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors and resident agents who sign false declarations can be fined up to USD 500,000 and disqualified from serving in Panama for 10 years. In serious cases, civil restitution claims may be brought by the DGI against the director's personal estate, and corresponding travel restrictions are issued to prevent flight.
Anti-Money Laundering / AML Red Flags on Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Panama's AML Law 23 of 2015 and subsequent reforms impose administrative fines of up to USD 5,000,000 for seriously impaired AML controls. Criminal penalties for organized crime or money laundering include 5 to 12 years imprisonment. The AML supervisor (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá) may order immediate freezing of the relevant accounts and termination of PSP agreements with Shopify Payments or Stripe processing.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members (directors) and appointed compliance officers are personally liable for failing to maintain adequate records of the beneficial owner. The penalty extends to imprisonment of 2 to 5 years for false documentation and the assessment of civil damages against the personal estate of the director for facilitating the money laundering.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with transfer pricing documentation triggers a penalty of 5% of the value of the controlled transactions, with a maximum penalty of USD 100,000. If the DGI makes a primary transfer pricing adjustment, a 20% surcharge on the additional tax due plus interest accrues. Persistent non-compliance can result in double counting of penalties and loss of territorial exemptions for the underlying transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign and approve false transfer pricing reports may be personally assessed for 50% of the penalty when they were conscious of the misstatement. In aggravated cases, they are exposed to criminal tax liability under Article 51 of the Panama Criminal Code and may suffer personal bankruptcy orders, travel bans, and restitution orders.
Franchise Tax and Statutory Filing Default

Statutory sanction: The Public Registry may impose late fines for foundation annual fee defaults, and after repeated non-payment, the foundation's legal status is suspended, rendering the PIF unable to execute contracts or maintain bank and payment processing accounts. DGI penalties for late Form 110 filing range from USD 50 to USD 5,000, plus default interest.

Director personal liability: The foundation council members are under a fiduciary duty to ensure the annual fee is Paid. If the foundation's insolvency or non-compliance damages a beneficiary, the council members and resident agent can be held personally liable for restitution under Foundation Law 25 of 1995, including claims for improper distribution and breach of fiduciary obligations, with courts able to issue personal asset freezes.
