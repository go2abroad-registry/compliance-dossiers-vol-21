Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) – Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; electronic portal: Sede Electrónica DGI (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must appoint a resident legal representative and register with the DGI to obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente). The application is filed online or through a notary/public registry, presenting foundation charter, apostilled passport/incorporation documents, proof of local address, and the appointed representative's identification. The RUC is issued after verification and is required for ITBMS (VAT) registration, customs operations, and any Panama-sourced taxable activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — Foreign entities must appoint a resident legal representative and register with the DGI to obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente). The application is filed online or through a notary/public registry, presenting foundation charter, apostilled passport/incorporation documents, proof of local address, and the appointed representative's identification. The RUC is issued after verification and is required for ITBMS (VAT) registration, customs operations, and any Panama-sourced taxable activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No federal check-the-box regime; Panama applies territorial taxation. A Private Interest Foundation may elect to be treated as transparent for administrative purposes, but ITBMS registration is compulsory for taxable supply activities. Hybrid mismatch elections under the Multilateral Instrument apply only where Panama has a relevant tax treaty; no domestic deemed-dividend or pay-through election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary threshold; any person or entity, including foreign-owned foundations, that engages in taxable supply of goods or services in Panama must register for ITBMS. For small businesses delivering occasional services, annual gross turnover under USD 12,000 may qualify for simplified collection, but customs brokerage services are deemed regular commercial activity and require full registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the final export of tangible personal property shipped from Panama to a foreign destination, and to services rendered for use wholly outside Panama or directly related to exported goods, provided the foreign customer is legally established outside Panama. Customs brokerage and bonded transit services qualify when physically performed in relation to Panama customs territory but are contractually tied to goods leaving Panama under an export declaration. The taxpayer must keep a file evidencing the actual movement of goods or, for offshore services, a contract plus documentation of performance and payment from abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Formulario Aduanero Único)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L or Air Waybill, including electronic equivalent from the Panama Customs Single Window)
  • Commercial Invoice or Service Invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Bank Proof of Payment from the Non-Resident Customer
  • DGI Electronic Factura con Código de Autorización stamped for export
  • Contract or purchase order indicating the offshore destination

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) is phased in for all VAT-registered taxpayers. Invoices must be transmitted in real time to the DGI's Sede Electrónica via the web portal or an authorized API using XML/JSON format, and each invoice obtains a unique authorization code and QR/digital stamp. Refusal of digital stamping or failure to transmit within the prescribed time (generally 24 hours) triggers fines and disallowance of input VAT credits.

The DGI and Panama Customs Authority conduct coordinated audits of export zero-rating claims. Missing customs declarations, proof of overseas payment, or timely remittance within the 180-day window results in reversal of the zero-rating, immediate collection of ITBMS at 7%, plus surcharge, revaluation, and fines of up to three times the tax amount. Fraudulent issuance of export invoices is criminal tax fraud punishable with imprisonment of two to five years, delisting from the exporter registry, and cancellation of the customs broker license.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. Panama maintains no exchange-control law, no capital-export permit regime, no foreign-investment registration requirement, and no balance-of-payments clearance. US Dollars may be held, transferred, and remitted freely. A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) may distribute profits and endowments to beneficiaries abroad without central bank authorization; the only operational constraint is SBP-regulated bank KYC/AML verification. A PIF conducting customs brokerage in Panama realizes Panamanian-source income subject to territorial taxation (corporate income tax and, where applicable, dividend or distribution withholding), but such obligations are fiscal duties, not currency-convertibility or profit-repatriation hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None apply. Panama imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no central bank deposit requirement for export receipts or service proceeds. USD brokerage fees, commissions, and duty pass-through funds may be retained in full, netted, or freely deployed for working capital. The surrender concept is legally inapplicable in a fully dollarized economy.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank and no foreign exchange administration. The Balboa (PAB) is a purely nominal monetary unit at parity with the US Dollar, which is legal tender under Monetary Regime Law Decree No. 15 of 1904. Banking regulation and supervision are vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) operates as the AML/CFT financial intelligence unit; customs and customs-broker licensing are governed by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA); and Banco Nacional de Panamá serves only as fiscal agent and depositary of the State, not as a monetary authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Medium legal viability. Panama has no standalone Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing class; deposit-taking and payment-intermediation require an SBP general or official banking license. Offshore digital EMIs and virtual-account providers (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Mercury, Currencycloud) are not supervised by the SBP or UAF and cannot serve as the primary treasury or customs-duty clearing channel; ANA duty remittances must originate from SBP-licensed banks. Foreign EMIs may be used only as ancillary receivables-collection rails for small overseas clients, subject to an internal concentration cap (not more than 10% of monthly collections), same-day reconciliation into the licensed Panamanian account, and mandatory tagging of each collection to a declared import declaration (DUI).

Defense evidence: Certified copy of the foundation deed (acta fundacional) and bylaws; Registered Agent certificate of good standing from the Registro Público de Panamá; final beneficial owner declaration consistent with Panama's centralized Final Beneficial Owner Registry (RBF) under Law 70 of 2019; a signed bank-specific waiver of foundation confidentiality limited to the institution's compliance file; organizational chart of the foundation council and all beneficiary classes.
Commercial activity without Panamanian tax and operational nexus - PIF acting as a customs broker but holding no RUC, Aviso de Operación, or ANA license.

Root cause: Commercial activity without Panamanian tax and operational nexus - PIF acting as a customs broker but holding no RUC, Aviso de Operación, or ANA license.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial endowment: SWIFT MT103 from the founder's home bank to the SBP-licensed operational account, with remittance field 70 stating Initial capital contribution - Private Interest Foundation (Ley 25 de 1995) and referencing the foundation deed registration number; sending bank must transmit complete originator data (name, account, address) under Law 23 of 2015 record-keeping and travel-rule obligations. The statutory minimum initial endowment is USD 10,000 (B/.10,000); any inbound contribution above the bank-defined threshold requires source-of-funds documentation before value-dating. Client duty prepayments: SWIFT MT103 or Panama ACH credit referencing the specific DUI number and narrative Customs brokerage fee and duty pass-through; duty monies must land in the segregated escrow sub-account, never the operational account. Panama imposes no mandatory national purpose-code taxonomy, so the MT103 field 70 narrative must fully describe each transaction. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends distributed by a Panamanian corporation out of taxable Panama-source profits; a reduced incremental 5% rate may apply under the dividend-tax-credit mechanism when the distributing corporation can prove that underlying profits were fully taxed at the 25% territorial CIR and the distribution satisfies the Fiscal Code credit-attribution rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous Local File and Master File preparation is triggered when the Panamanian taxpayer's annual gross revenue exceeds B/.2,000,000 (Balboa/USD at par) or its related-party transactions in a fiscal year exceed B/.250,000 per related party. A stricter B/.100,000 threshold applies to transactions with a related party resident in a zero-tax or non-cooperative jurisdiction. A Country-by-Country Report to the DGI is required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least USD 750,000,000; the TP annex must be filed with the annual return or by the DGI-determined supplementary date.

Economic substance scope: Panama applies Economic Substance Regulations and substance-over-form doctrine to tax residents and licensed juridical persons. A licensed customs brokerage house is not a passive PIF entity; its core income-generating activities must be performed in Panama through a licensed operational office, qualified staff, local board meetings, actual decision-making, and adequate expenses and physical assets. Holding a customs brokerage license through a foundation with nominee directors and no commercial activity is a shell arrangement and will be disregarded for tax purposes. The PIF holding structure may own the customs brokerage, but the brokerage itself must demonstrate real presence, employment, and operational substance independent of its foreign beneficiaries.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Customs Declarations

Statutory sanction: Civil tax penalties equal to a substantial percentage of the evaded tax, daily late-payment interest, revocation of Paz y Salvo, suspension of the customs broker license, confiscation of goods, and criminal tax evasion charges with imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and fines of two to three times the evaded amount under the Panama Fiscal Code and Penal Code.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced where the PIF or brokerage is found to be a mere instrumentality. Directors, the resident agent, and the licensed customs broker are jointly liable for unpaid taxes and penalties when they signed false returns, directed false invoicing, or knowingly participated in smuggle-friendly operations. Criminal conviction may trigger travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment.
Shell Company and Beneficial Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: DGI may disregard the foundation, attribute the brokerage income directly to the beneficial owner, assess the 25% CIR plus penalties, revoke the RUC and customs license, and administratively dissolve the entity. Failure to file accurate beneficial ownership information under Law 23 of 2015 and the Corporate Transparency regulations exposes the entity to fines, registration cancellation, and criminal sanctions for false declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors, foundation council members, and the registered agent who sign false beneficial ownership declarations or conceal ultimate owners are personally liable for civil restitution and fines. Courts may issue travel bans, disbar the licensed customs broker, and impose imprisonment for money laundering and fraudulent misrepresentation.
AML, Trade-Based Money Laundering, and Illegal Payment Channeling

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of B/.50,000 to B/.1,000,000 or more for non-financial designated entities, license revocation, closure of the operational office, and confiscation of funds. Criminal conviction for trade-based money laundering carries imprisonment of 5 to 12 years and fines equal to the laundered amount or its equivalent.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and customs brokers are individually liable for knowingly failing to file suspicious transaction reports, allowing wire-payment structuring, or participating in trade-based laundering. Sanctions include personal asset seizure, travel bans, director disqualification, and imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced for AML liabilities under Panamanian law when the entity is used as an instrument of crime.
