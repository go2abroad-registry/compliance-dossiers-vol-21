Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá — Sección de Fundaciones de Interés Privado (Public Registry of Panama, Private Interest Foundations Section)

Governing statute

Law No. 25 of 12 June 1995 (Private Interest Foundations), as amended by Law No. 63 of 2018; principally Articles 1, 3, 5, 6, 11, 15, 16, 22, 23, 25, 31 and 35; supplemental Civil Code provisions on juridical persons.

Formation / constitutional filings

Foundation Charter (Acta Constitutiva de Fundación de Interés Privado) formalized as a notarial Escritura Pública; optional Foundation Regulations (Reglamento); registration recorded in the Folio de Fundaciones de Interés Privado.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 19 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum initial endowment of US$10,000 (or equivalent value in assets) must be declared in the Foundation Charter under Article 11 of Law 25 of 1995. No statutory escrow account is required; the endowment is recorded in the Foundation's books and must remain available for the lawful purposes of the Foundation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety compliance certificate from the Benemérito Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá covering electrical safety, egress, and flammables storage; municipal structural and occupancy inspection of retail and fulfillment premises; workplace ventilation, equipment grounding, and personal protective measures per Panamanian occupational safety regulations applicable to garment-printing operations.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum initial endowment of US$10,000 (or equivalent value in assets) must be declared in the Foundation Charter under Article 11 of Law 25 of 1995. No statutory escrow account is required; the endowment is recorded in the Foundation's books and must remain available for the lawful purposes of the Foundation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; online portal SITIO (Sistema de Tributación en Línea) at https://sitio.dgi.gob.pa. Ancillary authorities: Registro Público de Panamá (entity and registered-agent records), Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA; SIDUNEA customs clearance), Autoridad de Innovación Gubernamental (AIG; advanced e-signature certificates).

E-government filing portal

For a Private Interest Foundation (PIF) formed under Ley 25 de 1995 by a non-resident founder: (1) register the foundation deed with the Registro Público through a licensed Panamanian registered agent; (2) execute an apostilled power of attorney appointing a Panamanian lawyer/fiscal representative; (3) file the Form 'Inscripcion RUC' in SITIO, declaring ISIC 4791 (POD custom apparel e-commerce) and the target payment rails (Stripe, PayPal); (4) upload certified copy of the foundation deed, founder and beneficiary passports, registered-agent certificate, and a local address for notices; (5) undergo DGI identity and reference validation; (6) on RUC issuance (12-digit format), optionally register for ITBMS and activate SITIO electronic invoicing with an AIG advanced e-signature. Non-resident ownership is lawful; no local capital contribution or substantive presence is required. The PIF also pays the flat annual USD 300 foundation tax through SITIO.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope with entity-level taxation limited to Panama-source income. The PIF is a distinct hybrid legal person (neither a corporation nor a trust) and is not treated as pass-through for Panamanian tax purposes. Foreign-source income arising from POD online sales to non-resident customers (design, fulfillment, and payment abroad) falls outside the Panamanian income tax (ISR) base. The 25% corporate ISR rate applies only to Panama-source profits. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope with entity-level taxation limited to Panama-source income. The PIF is a distinct hybrid legal person (neither a corporation nor a trust) and is not treated as pass-through for Panamanian tax purposes. Foreign-source income arising from POD online sales to non-resident customers (design, fulfillment, and payment abroad) falls outside the Panamanian income tax (ISR) base. The 25% corporate ISR rate applies only to Panama-source profits. Distributions to non-resident beneficiaries out of foreign-source profits incur no Panamanian withholding; no inheritance, estate, or net-worth tax applies to foundation assets.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Panamanian internal law. Elections available to a PIF: (1) simplified small-taxpayer regime (cuota fija de ITBMS) where annual gross turnover is at or below USD 36,000; (2) cash-basis accounting election for small taxpayers; (3) monthly ITBMS input-credit carryforward and refund election; (4) SEM (Multinational Headquarters) regime under Ley 41 de 2007 for qualifying regional headquarter services; (5) advance-pricing agreement with DGI for related-party export service margins; (6) voluntary early adoption of SITIO e-invoicing before the mandatory rollout date.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No minimum turnover threshold: any person habitually performing taxable transactions in Panama must register for ITBMS from the first taxable sale (RUC obligation). A special small-taxpayer regime (cuota fija) is available where average annual gross turnover is at or below B/.36,000 (USD 36,000; Balboa at par with USD). A pure exporter with no in-country taxable supplies may refrain from ITBMS registration while submitting only export/zero-rated declarations in SITIO.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a sale of tangible merchandise to a non-domiciled purchaser qualifies for the 0% ITBMS rate if the goods physically leave Panamanian customs territory (ANA/SIDUNEA export declaration) within the contractual shipping period and the vendor retains transport and risk-of-loss evidence. Services (exportación de servicios): services rendered to, and economically used by, a non-resident (including POD design and fulfillment for foreign end-consumers) are zero-rated or considered outside the territorial scope of ITBMS when payment originates abroad. For online retail, a foreign billing address, non-resident payment method, marketplace settlement reports (Stripe/PayPal), and customer geo-IP data substantiate offshore use. Export-related input ITBMS is creditable in the monthly ITBMS return; a resulting monthly saldo a favor may be refunded through SITIO after DGI verification. Panama maintains no statutory FX repatriation deadline because of full dollarization; 180 days is applied as the prudent banking and trade-settlement benchmark for consistency of export documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación submitted through ANA/SIDUNEA)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (master/house B/L where POD fulfillment is outsourced to a 3PL)
  • Commercial invoice (HS codes, incoterms, and explicit 'ITBMS 0% export' clause)
  • Customer evidence: foreign contract/purchase order, non-resident tax ID, foreign shipping address, geo-IP or marketplace settlement report
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/marketplace SWIFT settlement proof (Stripe/PayPal payout record tied to the commercial invoice)

Mandatory e-invoicing under Res. DGI 201-1863 de 2022 (as amended): every RUC holder performing economic activity must issue electronic invoices as XML in SITIO with real-time validación previa. The invoice is submitted to the DGI web-service/API before delivery; DGI returns an authorization number and timestamp, and the final print/PDF must include a QR code. Taxpayers connect through SOAP/REST APIs and X.509 advanced e-signatures issued by AIG; pre-printed fiscal-printer invoices were fully phased out from fiscal year 2024. POD export invoices must carry the 0% or 'no sujeto' code, identify the foreign customer, and comply with DGI catalogues of goods and services codes.

DGI audits cross-match SITIO e-invoices, ANA/SIDUNEA export entries, ITBMS returns, and bank/payment-facilitator settlement data under SBP rules. Unsubstantiated zero-rating triggers: (i) retroactive clawback of refunded ITBMS plus default interest at the DGI variable legal rate; (ii) administrative fines of 50% to 100% of the omitted tax under the Código Fiscal (with graduated penalties for formal inaccuracies); (iii) exclusion from export-refund procedures for up to two years where falsity is proven; (iv) criminal liability for defraudación fiscal under Ley 70 de 2019 (Código Fiscal Art. 789-A) carrying 2 to 5 years of imprisonment where the omitted tax exceeds USD 10,000 in a fiscal period; (v) forged export documents additionally prosecuted as document falsification under the Código Penal. Tax prescription runs for 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy; the Balboa is a nominal unit at 1:1 and only coins circulate. Current-account payments for trade and services and capital-account transactions such as foundation contributions, distributions, loan servicing, and asset transfers are fully convertible with no quota, tax, or prior approval requirement. Profit and distribution remittances from a Private Interest Foundation are subject only to bank KYC and the territorial tax regime; there are no legal remittance hurdles, although the bank may require source-of-funds documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirement exists. Export proceeds may be received in USD or any convertible currency, retained offshore or onshore, and freely deployed; no mandatory conversion ratio, statutory timeline, or central bank report applies to receivables arising from the PIF's POD retail activities.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as banking supervisor; Banco Nacional de Panamá as fiscal agent. There is no Panamanian central bank and the U.S. dollar is legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Offshore digital EMIs without a Panama license can process card payments on behalf of the POD business only through partner processors and cannot offer a full transaction-grade treasury account. SBP-regulated local fintechs and EMIs may provide USD-denominated accounts and API clearing, but foreign passporting is not available; therefore the PIF should contract directly with a licensed Panama bank or an SBP-approved electronic payment provider for stable and compliant liquidity rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from foreign founders or donors are routed as ordinary USD-clearable SWIFT MT103 wires to the PIF corporate account. The originator must include the PIF's full name, foundation registry number, and the reference 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / FOUNDER SUBVENTION UNDER LEY 25 DE 1995'. The receiving bank will require a corporate resolution and a source-of-funds statement. Because Panama has no foreign-exchange regime, no FX approval and no mandatory national purpose-code framework applies; the sending bank's purpose field should state 'capital transfer to private interest foundation' and reflect the payer's tax identification number where relevant for tax transparency reporting.Outbound supplier payments for POD manufacturing, apparel blanks, and logistics should be cleared via SWIFT MT103 for invoices up to USD 50,000. For larger pre-production batches, a confirmed irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit issued by a Panama bank and discounted at a U.S. correspondent bank is the most compliant rail. Because Panama operates fully on the U.S. dollar, settlement timing is typically one to three business days via U.S. correspondent clearing, with no FOREX conversion requirement and no tax clearance certificate needed. All disbursements must be matched to the PIF's approved disbursement policy and recorded with purchase orders, supplier KYC, invoice, and proof of delivery.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forwards (e.g., EUR/USD, GBP/USD, CNY/USD) executed with SBP-regulated banks or licensed international brokers, subject to ISDA documentation and PIF council authorization.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies without full USD deliverability or restricted markets, settled in USD, permissible as derivative transactions under a Panama banking relationship.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) and FX swaps for medium-term funding and working-capital cycles, permissible under Panama law and SBP banking practice; no central bank approval is required, but the bank must classify the PIF as an informed client and apply suitability requirements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard Panamanian corporate income tax rate applied to net taxable income from Panama-source activities)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% on dividends or distributions sourced from profits already taxed in Panama; 10% on distributions sourced from untaxed or foreign-source profits of a Panamanian entity; PIF distributions to beneficiaries are analogously subject to the 5%/10% withholding regime when derived from Panama-source earnings.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For calendar fiscal year ending 31 December, due on or before 31 March of the following year; for non-calendar fiscal years, within three months after the fiscal year close

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 2,000,000 aggregate related-party transactions in a fiscal year triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation and filing; OECD-aligned Country-by-Country reporting arises where the multinational group total consolidated revenue is at least USD 850,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama imposes economic substance requirements on entities claiming residence benefits and engaging in relevant activities, including retail distribution, IP exploitation, financing, and shipping. A PIF engaged in active POD retail must demonstrate in Panama adequate premises, local staff, board meetings, and core decision-making. Passive asset-holding PIFs with no Panama-source activity remain outside the substantive tax net but must be able to prove the passive nature through contemporaneous records.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Administrative re-assessment with monthly late-payment surcharges, a penalty of up to 50% of the evaded tax, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud under Panama’s tax crime legislation, carrying imprisonment of 2 to 6 years.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members and the resident agent who sign false returns may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and fines; criminal conviction can impose travel bans, asset embargos, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Abuse of Private Interest Foundation

Statutory sanction: DGI may disregard the foundation form, assess the entire Panama-source income at 25%, impose underpayment penalties and surcharges, and retroactively revoke any prior tax-exempt treatment.

Director personal liability: Panamanian courts may pierce the foundation veil, rendering directors, foundation council members, and controlling beneficiaries personally liable for the tax deficiency, civil fraud damages, and court costs.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Registry Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines under Panama’s Law 23 of 2015 (as amended) reaching up to USD 1,000,000; money-laundering conviction carries imprisonment of 5 to 10 years, forfeiture of assets, and administrative disqualification.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and resident agents face personal criminal liability, travel restrictions, and freeze orders; the Panamanian license of the resident agent may also be suspended.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: DGI adjustment of cross-border margins to arm's length, upward revision of taxable income, a fine of USD 10,000 to USD 50,000 for documentary failure, and a 5% penalty on the transfer-pricing adjustment if the taxpayer’s documentation is rejected.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length intercompany agreements can be held personally liable for the resulting tax deficiency and penalty if the PIF is treated as a sham or the conduct is found to constitute bad faith management.
