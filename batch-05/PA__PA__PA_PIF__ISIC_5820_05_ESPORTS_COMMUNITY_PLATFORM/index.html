Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama. Main online taxpayer portal and systems: 'e-Tax 2.0' (DGI web services at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa), the 'Sistema de Facturación Electrónica' (SIF), and the 'Registro Único de Contribuyente' (RUC) module.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF), the following statutory SOP applies: (1) appoint a Panamanian resident agent (agente residente) — a licensed attorney or trust company — and execute the Foundation Charter by public deed; (2) register the Foundation with the Public Registry (Registro Público de Panamá) and obtain the 'Ficha Registral' evidencing legal existence; (3) if the Foundation will realize Panama-source income or carry on an economic activity in Panama, file the DGI 'Solicitud de Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC)' through e-Tax 2.0, attaching the public deed, ficha registral, resident agent appointment, proof of appointed directors, notarized Spanish translations, and the domicile address; (4) receive the RUC certificate and update the 'Datos Generales del Contribuyente' before issuance of any invoice; (5) obtain a qualified electronic signature (Firma Electrónica) from a DGI-accredited provider for e-filing and e-invoicing; (6) register in the SIF electronic-invoicing system and obtain the ITBMS/export registration code if the activity comprises taxable supplies; (7) file a Final Beneficiary declaration under Law 254 of 2021 and complete the municipal 'Aviso de Operación' only where physically present. Non-resident PIFs with no Panamanian-source revenue, no office, and no customs activity are not under an obligation to obtain a RUC, but an elective RUC may still be requested to facilitate zero-rated export transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama applies a territorial-source income tax system and does not maintain a US-style 'check-the-box' pass-through election. A PIF is a separate legal person subject to entity-level taxation on Panama-source income only; distributions to beneficiaries are not a taxable deduction. Relevant elections: (a) election to register as a foreign-flagged exporter or ITBMS zero-rated service supplier by filing a formal application and, where appropriate, obtaining a DGI resolution confirming the 0% export treatment; (b) election, under the invoice/accounting regime, to compute taxable income on either an accrual or cash basis where permitted by the DGI for small taxpayers, but the territorial scope cannot be changed by election; (c) election in the Foundation Charter whether profits are accumulated or assigned to beneficiaries — while civil-law consequences follow the election, tax transparency is not conferred, and a Panamanian court or the DGI may re-characterize hybrid arrangements under the anti-abuse rules of Law 254 of 2021.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Panama does not prescribe a general turnover threshold for ITBMS registration. Registration is compulsory from the first taxable transfer of goods or provision of services carried out in Panama, including electronically supplied services consumed in Panama by a non-resident digital service platform. A simplified 'small taxpayer' compliance regime may exist for very low-turnover natural persons, but a PIF carrying on an esports tournament/matchmaking platform exceeding the de minimis threshold must register for ITBMS before operations commence. No monetary safe-harbor applies to foreign platform operators under Panamanian territorial rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (i) sales of movable goods exported by the seller from Panamanian customs territory to the foreign purchaser under an official export declaration, and (ii) services rendered by a Panamanian supplier to a non-resident recipient where the service is consumed entirely outside Panama or is directly related to export transactions. For services, the taxpayer must obtain from the foreign recipient a tax residence certificate, a declaration of actual use/benefit abroad, and a written service contract reflecting the cross-border supply. Esports tournament platforms may qualify only for fees attributable to offshore prize pooling, international matchmaking, and overseas streaming rights; not for services economically consumed by Panamanian participants. The exported goods must leave Panama within the statutory shipping window and be sold against payment to a foreign customer. No ITBMS is passed on at export, and input ITBMS attributable to the export transaction can be credited against domestic taxable output and/or refunded by DGI following an audit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / DUA), validated by the Dirección General de Aduanas, identifying the goods, tariff heading, destination, and exporter's RUC.
  • Original transport document — Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document — evidencing physical departure of the goods from Panama and delivery to the foreign destination.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a Panamanian bank, or a bank credit/transfer advice, demonstrating cash receipt of the export proceeds in Panama within the 180-day repatriation term.
  • Commercial invoice and the underlying sales contract/purchase order, clearly referencing the export terms, destination country, and unit prices.
  • Validated e-invoice (factura electrónica) containing the SIF traceability code, the cause code 'exportación', zero-rated VAT line, and any additional certificate of origin or packing list required by customs.

Electronic invoicing is mandatory. Taxpayers must connect to the DGI's 'Sistema de Facturación Electrónica' (SIF) and transmit invoices in XML format using the web portal or authenticated machine-to-machine/SFTP APIs. The DGI performs real-time schema and RUC validation, digitally signs each invoice, and returns a unique 'Código de Trazabilidad Fiscal' and QR code; an invoice with no validation response is treated as not issued for tax purposes. API integration requires a mutual TLS X.509 certificate, DGI-approved electronic signature, and uses encrypted JSON/REST endpoints at the SIF gateway. Invoices must contain the RUC, issuance number, site code, ITBMS rate, product/service code, and the zero-rating/export cause code where applicable. 'Digital stamping' in the strict sense does not apply to commercial invoices in Panama; the DGI validation service acts as the equivalent clearance mechanism. Certain municipal and notarial instruments remain subject to 'timbre fiscal'.

DGI cross-checks the SIF e-invoices against customs export declarations (DUA) and banking records. Where the exporter has not obtained inward remittance of the proceeds within 180 days, has made material misrepresentations about the foreign customer or the offshore use of a service, or has issued an e-invoice with an unreal export cause code, the DGI will retroactively reverse the zero-rating and assess unreported ITBMS at 7% on the transaction, together with compensatory moratory interest and a fine of up to double the tax omitted. False export documentation triggers a customs fraud penalty under Customs Law No. 19 of 2014, disqualification from future zero-rating for a period to be determined by DGI, and, in repeated or coordinated schemes, criminal tax fraud prosecution before the Criminal Courts of Panama. Tax assessments for fraudulent zero-rated export schemes can be reopened within ten years from the date of the offending return or invoice, and input VAT credits or refunds related to those exports are clawed back with the same penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility is available on both current and capital account. The Panamanian balboa is a purely nominal unit pegged at 1:1 with the US dollar, and the economy is dollarized, which removes any national exchange-control bottleneck. Profit repatriation and capital outflow require no prior authorization, no exchange licence, and no central-bank permit. The only material hurdles are bank-level AML/KYC documentation, beneficial-ownership disclosure by the Private Interest Foundation, and compliance with the US dollar clearing rules of correspondent banks in New York.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies. Exporters, including digital platform operators such as the esports tournament business, may retain 100% of their export proceeds in USD or any other currency in offshore or onshore SBP-licensed accounts. There is no mandated repatriation timeline. The only compliance output is the SBP reporting framework applicable to transfers having a value of USD 10,000 or more and the filing of suspicious transaction reports under the panamanian AML regime.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Panama Superintendency of Banks, SBP); Panama does not maintain a traditional central bank, and Banco Nacional de Panamá serves as the Republic's fiscal agent; monetary and exchange-rate functions are effectively delegated to the US dollar regime as the circulating legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a primary settlement rail, Medium as a supplemental collection rail. Panama is outside Stripe's standard merchant footprint, so the Platform cannot rely on Stripe as its regulated acquiring channel; PayPal supports Panama corporate accounts for tournament-ticket collection but imposes settlement holds and does not confer SBP deposit-taking status. Offshore digital EMIs (Payoneer, Wise Business, DLocal) may be used for disbursement convenience but cannot lawfully present themselves as Panamanian bank accounts and remain outside SBP prudential supervision. The lawful architecture is an SBP-licensed commercial bank as master settlement account, with EMIs used exclusively for low-value player payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The founder injects capital via SWIFT MT103 from the origination bank to the Foundation's receiving bank in Panama (Banco General, Banistmo or Towerbank), with field 70 stating CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - PA_PIF FOUNDATION COUNCIL RESOLUTION [date]. Because Panama has no exchange-control filing, no central-bank permit or Form A is required; however, the receiving bank applies pre-funding clearance that includes a source-of-funds questionnaire, a notarized capital contribution deed, the Foundation council minutes, and a compliance certification of the originator's bank. USD is settled through New York correspondent relationships, and incoming transfers above USD 100,000 should be pre-notified to the compliance officer to avoid 3-5 day clearing holds.Prize pools, tournament tickets, sponsor payables, and platform operating expenses are settled primarily through SWIFT MT103 customer credits from the Foundation's Panama account to beneficiaries worldwide. Intra-Panama US-dollar flows clear same-day via ACH-Panamá, the local automated clearing house operated under SBP rules. For high-value publisher or licensing agreements above USD 250,000, a sight commercial letter of credit (L/C) opened with Banistmo or Banco General provides documentary settlement with deferred-payment modality. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No independent audit is mandatory for a passive asset-holding Private Interest Foundation. A full statutory audit becomes compulsory where the foundation carries on commercial or industrial activity in Panama and is treated as a corporation (Law 25 of 1995, Art. 25), where annual gross Panamanian-source income exceeds B/.3,000,000, or where the entity is classified as a DGI large taxpayer (Gran Contribuyente). Entities with gross revenue of B/.1,000,000–B/.3,000,000 must file financial statements certified by a licensed Panamanian CPA (dictamen avalado) without a full audit.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends paid out of Panama-source net taxable profits; 0% on distributions of foreign-source earnings due to territorial taxation. For a Panama Private Interest Foundation, distributions to beneficiaries are generally not classified as dividends, provided the foundation itself does not conduct commercial operations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.200,000 (Panamanian balboas, equivalent to USD) annual aggregate related-party cross-border transactions triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation per DGI regulations.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 254 of 2021, ESR) apply to Panamanian resident entities engaged in relevant activities, including digital and e-commerce services. A Private Interest Foundation that does not operate commercial activities is outside the ESR if it limits itself to holding passive assets and administering beneficiaries. Separate Panamanian operating entity for the esports platform must demonstrate local management, physical presence, adequate staff and IT infrastructure in Panama; non-compliance can result in fines up to USD 1,000,000 and loss of tax residence privileges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 2 to 10 years, fine equal to 100% of the evaded tax, plus payment of all late surcharges and interest, and potential closure of the commercial activity.

Director personal liability: Directors and establishment commission members who sign fraudulent returns are personally liable for civil restitution of the evaded amount, may be subject to travel bans preventing departure from Panama, and may be imprisoned if the evasion exceeds the statutory threshold.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under Panama ESR Law 254 of 2021, an effective penalty range of USD 50,000 to USD 1,000,000 plus loss of tax benefits and possible cancellation of the legal entity's registration; directors and council members are jointly and severally liable.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: directors and council members face personal fines, civil seizure of personal assets, and a travel prohibition where the entity is deemed to be a shell with hidden ownership.
AML Non-Compliance and Unlicensed Payment Facilitation

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015 and the UAF regulations, fines of USD 100,000 to USD 10,000,000 for the entity, suspension of payment-processing agreements, revocation of any relevant operational license, and criminal prosecution for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally criminally liable, with imprisonment up to 12 years for aggravated money laundering, civil restitution of laundered amounts, freezing of personal accounts, and a Panamanian travel ban while investigations are pending.
