Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (General Directorate of Revenue) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); online filing and registration portal: e-Tax / SIME at https://etax.mef.gob.pa; institutional portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must engage a licensed Panamanian attorney as resident agent; register the foundation deed and obtain legal personality (Personería Jurídica) at the Registro Público de Panamá; then file RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) registration with the DGI through the e-Tax portal using the foundation's constituted documents, domicile agent appointment, and a notarized and apostilled power of attorney; after RUC issuance, the foundation must apply for digital signature (Firma Electrónica) and separately register for ITBMS and electronic invoicing before carrying on taxable activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: The PIF is not a pass-through entity; it is a separate legal person taxed at entity level only on Panama-source income. Foreign-source income, gains, and activities taking place outside Panama fall outside the territorial tax net and are fully excluded from Panamanian income tax. The PIF remains subject to fixed annual franchise/annuity fees and to ITBMS only on Panama-situs taxable supplies; active AI SaaS operations are taxable in Panama only if the underlying service is rendered, used, or exploited within Panamanian territory.

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections include: (1) registration as an ordinary ITBMS taxpayer versus a simplified small-taxpayer regime where turnover thresholds are met; (2) election to file income tax returns on a calendar-year basis with standard DGI forms; (3) election to register as an exporter for zero-rating and input-VAT refund purposes; and (4) alignment with OECD hybrid-mismatch outcomes through Panama's territorial rules and treaty tie-breaker provisions, though no standalone hybrid-election statute exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

9%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 50,000 (USD 50,000) in annual gross turnover for mandatory general ITBMS registration in Balboa (pegged 1:1 to USD); however, foreign and domestic suppliers of digital services, including AI avatar video SaaS, must register for ITBMS regardless of turnover, and non-resident digital platforms are required to collect ITBMS on supplies to Panamanian recipients.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are zero-rated at 0% ITBMS under the Panamanian ITBMS statute. AI avatar video generation and AIGC SaaS services qualify as exported services when supplied to non-resident customers, paid from abroad, and effectively used or consumed entirely outside Panama. Eligibility requires: ITBMS registration and exporter status, a written contract or license agreement with the non-resident counterparty, verifiable payment inflow through banking or licensed payment intermediaries (Stripe/Paddle), and supporting records demonstrating the foreign situs of delivery and use; related input ITBMS credits are then claimable as refunds through the DGI export-refund procedure.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) for physical goods; not applicable to pure SaaS/avatar video exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical shipments; for SaaS: signed license or service contract with the non-resident customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) or bank statements evidencing settlement of export proceeds from abroad
  • Commercial invoices issued under the DGI electronic-invoicing regime referencing the zero-rated export supply
  • Stripe/Paddle payment-rail transaction reports and payout statements evidencing non-resident counterparties and foreign-source receipts
  • Non-resident status evidence (foreign registry extract, tax residency certificate, or jurisdiction of incorporation) for each material customer
  • ITBMS export declaration and refund application submitted through the DGI e-Tax/SIME portal

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) is in force for all RUC taxpayers through the DGI e-Tax/SIME platform. Invoices must be issued through DGI-approved software, transmitted to the DGI for prior validation and authorization via web services/API, include a QR code and digital stamp, and bear the taxpayer's electronic signature. Large taxpayers are subject to stricter real-time clearance requirements; non-resident digital service providers must issue simplified compliant records and submit periodic ITBMS returns electronically.

The DGI may retrospectively disallow zero-rating and claw back ITBMS credits where export substantiation is deficient, payment is not repatriated within the substantiation period, or documentation is fraudulent; such adjustments attract recargo interest on underpaid tax, fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the omitted or overclaimed tax, and potential criminal tax-fraud prosecution for knowingly false export records or forged invoices under Panama's tax and criminal statutes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama applies a full, unregulated dollarized monetary framework: the official unit of account is the Balboa (PAB), pegged at 1:1 to the United States dollar, and U.S. dollars circulate as unrestricted legal tender. There is no exchange control board, no prior authorization regime for capital movements, and no restriction on current-account transactions such as software licensing royalties, SaaS subscription fees, or trade payments. Capital-account operations, including shareholder/founder capital injections, loans from non-residents, and distribution of endowments, are fully convertible and may be freely remitted through the domestic banking system. Profit and capital remittance hurdles for a Private Interest Foundation are effectively nil, subject only to standard AML/KYC verification and the Panamanian territorial income tax rules, under which foreign-source income and capital gains earned entirely outside Panama are not subject to Panamanian income tax (Ley 25 de 1995 and Fiscal Code Art. 694).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender, forced conversion, or minimum repatriation timelines applicable in Panama. A Panama Private Interest Foundation engaged in AI video synthesis and SaaS licensing may hold 100% of its export proceeds in USD, EUR, GBP, or any freely convertible currency in any licensed bank without mandatory conversion to PAB. Exporters and service providers must simply comply with standard commercial invoicing, tax record-keeping, and UAF reporting thresholds: cash transactions at or above USD 10,000 and all suspicious transactions must be reported to the UAF under Law 23 of 2015. No percentage of export or licensing proceeds must be surrendered to any state authority.

Central bank supervision: Panama does not operate a traditional central bank or a monetary authority with discretionary FX powers. The banking system is supervised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Superintendency of Banks of Panama, 'SBP'), which is the competent prudential and conduct authority for all licensed banks and financial institutions pursuant to Decree Law 9 of 1998. Fiscal agency functions are undertaken by the Banco Nacional de Panamá (National Bank of Panama), which acts as fiscal agent of the State, while the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) governs macroeconomic and financial policy. AML/CFT oversight is exercised by the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) under Law 23 of 2015.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-Medium viability as a full treasury rail. Panama does not yet have a dedicated E-Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime equivalent to the EU/UK EMI framework; non-bank fintechs must generally partner with an SBP-licensed bank to hold funds, and the SBP does not directly license standalone digital wallets for corporate multi-currency settlement. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, or Payoneer may accept a Panama PIF where the UBO register is transparent, apostilled foundation documents are provided, and the account is used purely for settlement of Stripe/Paddle payouts, but these providers frequently decline private interest foundations due to perceived asset-protection structures, and their funds are not covered by Panamanian deposit insurance. The foundation should therefore treat offshore EMIs as collection-only and daily-sweep balances to a Tier 1 Panamanian bank; for large AI-compute and corporate disbursements, a full SBP-licensed bank account is mandatory.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% flat corporate income tax (Impuesto sobre la Renta) on net taxable income derived from Panamanian-source activities; a Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) with purely foreign-source revenue is outside the 25% charge by virtue of Panama's territorial tax system (Art. 694, Código Fiscal).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% withholding tax on dividends distributed to shareholders or beneficiaries out of profits that were subject to the 25% corporate income tax in Panama; 10% withholding on distributions made out of untaxed Panama-source profits; distributions of foreign-source accumulated profits received by a PIF from its AI operating subsidiaries are not subject to Panamanian dividend WHT because the territorial regime treats them as foreign income.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed within three months after the close of the fiscal year (31 March for taxpayers on a calendar-year basis); no extension is granted automatically, and interest accrues from the day following the statutory due date.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Payable every calendar year within the first quarter (1 January to 31 March); a foundation that fails to pay by 31 March incurs automatic interest and suspension of its legal standing and certificate of good standing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.250,000 (USD 250,000) aggregate related-party transactions in a fiscal year: crossing this threshold triggers the mandatory preparation of the Local File (Estudio de Precios de Transferencia), the Master File, and the TP informative return (Formulario 749), which must be filed with the annual Form 700; penalties apply for failure to file or for inadequate documentation.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Requirements (ESR), implemented through Decree 36 of 2021, apply to a PIF engaged in relevant activities, including holding business and IP exploitation: the foundation must demonstrate physical premises, qualified employees, and core income-generating activities in Panama, and must file the annual substance declaration with ANIP/Registro Público. A PIF holding AI avatar video-generation IP and deriving licensing royalties is treated as an IP-based relevant activity and carries a heightened burden to prove local decision-making, adequate staff, and active management of the IP; passive or 'pure holding' foundations enjoy a lighter substance test but must still maintain registered agent, records, and a Panamanian board of directors.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent income tax returns

Statutory sanction: Panama's Criminal Code and Ley 14 of 2016 impose imprisonment of 2 to 5 years for tax fraud, fines of up to three times the evaded tax, and mandatory restitution of the tax, statutory interest, and surcharges; the tax administration may also apply a 10% non-deductible surcharge and 1% monthly interest.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members, the registered agent, and the beneficial owner who sign or cause false returns to be filed incur personal criminal liability, civil restitution of the evaded tax, asset freezes, travel bans (impedimento de salida), and disqualification from acting as directors in Panama.
Sham structure / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: The Registro Público may impose ESR administrative fines, suspend or cancel the foundation's registration, and the tax authority may disregard the foundation as a sham for tax purposes, reattributing all revenue to the beneficial owner at the 25% corporate rate plus interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and protectors may be held jointly and severally liable for the foundation's unpaid taxes and third-party claims where the veil is pierced; aggravating conduct involving concealment of assets leads to civil restitution and criminal prosecution.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) violations

Statutory sanction: Law 23 of 2015 and Panama's Criminal Code impose imprisonment of 5 to 10 years for money laundering, increased to 10 to 15 years in aggravating circumstances, with fines up to B/.1,000,000 and forfeiture of funds held in Stripe/Paddle accounts or Panamanian bank accounts.

Director personal liability: The foundation council, the registered agent, and the beneficial owner are criminally liable as principals or accomplices; remedies include preventive travel bans, extradition, and personal seizure of assets beyond the foundation's patrimony.
Failure to file returns and to pay annual taxes

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of B/.500 to B/.10,000, a 10% late-payment surcharge, monthly resarcitorio interest of 1%, and suspension or cancellation of the PIF's legal registration; interest and surcharges continue to accrue until full settlement.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent are personally responsible for ensuring filing and payment; persistent non-compliance bars the use of the foundation's separate asset ring and subjects officers to civil liability for losses suffered by beneficiaries.
Dividend withholding tax and distribution violations

Statutory sanction: The payer is liable for the full under-withheld amount, a 50% penalty on the unpaid withholding, default interest at 1% monthly, and administrative fines; repeated violations are treated as tax fraud and escalate to criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Members of the foundation council who authorize a distribution without withholding are personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; where the distribution is disguised to conceal taxable profit, directors face criminal sanctions, travel bans, and civil restitution to the tax administration.
