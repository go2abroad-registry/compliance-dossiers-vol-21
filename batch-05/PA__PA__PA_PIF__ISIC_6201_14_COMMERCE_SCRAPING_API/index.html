Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Law 25 of 1995 (Art. 12) requires the foundation to hold an initial patrimony adequate for its stated purposes; established market and registry practice requires a minimum endowment of US$10,000.00, contributed in cash or in kind and declared in the charter. No escrow account is statutorily mandated; the endowment is evidenced by a bank certificate or asset inventory and the foundation is not subject to paid-up capital filing obligations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); e-Tax portal: https://etax.mef.gob.pa; official website: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

1) Engage a licensed Panamanian resident agent (agente residente) for the PIF. 2) Execute the foundation deed (acta fundacional) and reglamento and record with the Registro Público de Panamá. 3) File via the DGI e-Tax portal the RUC application (Solicitud de Registro Único de Contribuyente) attaching the certified foundation deed, resident agent acceptance, identity documents of council members, and proof of registered address. 4) Obtain the RUC certificate and activate the e-Tax digital credentials (clave de acceso). 5) Register for ITBM as a collector when habitual taxable services are intended and enroll in the electronic invoicing system (SFE). 6) Obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación/business license for the registered commercial activity. A foreign entity may act through its local fiscal representative; the PIF functions as the operative taxpayer and foreign founders/beneficiaries need no personal Panamanian TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists; a PIF is the entity-level taxpayer for Panama-source income (general corporate ISR 25%), and all foreign-source income is expressly exempt under Panama's territorial system. Available elections and mechanisms: (i) small-taxpayer ITBM simplified regime election for annual gross income at or below USD 36,000; (ii) charter-level elections under Ley 25 de 1995 on distributions to beneficiaries and reincorporation of surplus; (iii) election of the DGI transfer-pricing documentation safe harbors under Ley 254 de 2019 for related-party cross-border service contracts; (iv) application of the Multinational Company Headquarters (SEM Law, Ley 41 de 2007) incentive regime if administrative/back-office functions are established. Panama does not provide domestic hybrid-mismatch or check-the-box elections; hybrid mismatch outcomes are managed under BEPS Action 2-compliant disclosure in the annual sworn returns.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de minimis threshold: any person habitually transferring taxable goods or rendering taxable services in Panama must register as an ITBM collector. A small-taxpayer option (quarterly settlement at reduced rate) is available for gross income up to USD 36,000 per annum.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Panama are zero-rated at 0% upon physical exit supported by the customs export declaration. Services qualify for 0% ITBM when provided to a non-resident recipient and economically utilized entirely abroad, including data processing, software development, and commercial web-scraping/API gateway services, provided the recipient is non-resident, the service is not consumed or personally used in Panama, and consideration is paid from abroad. The PIF must maintain stream-of-activities evidence showing the scraping targets, proxy/delivery infrastructure, and server-side data flows remain outside Panamanian territory to sustain the offshore characterization.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / FUE) for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or electronic transport evidence
  • Commercial invoice and service contract (identifying non-resident recipient and offshore use)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT or banking credit evidence of foreign payment
  • Certificate of service utilization abroad (testimonio de utilización en el exterior)
  • Foreign recipient tax residence certificate
  • ITBM zero-rating register reconciliation filed in the periodic ITBM return

Mandatory electronic invoicing under the DGI 'Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (SFE)' with phased rollout based on annual gross income and taxpayer category. Invoices must be issued in XML/PDF-A through certified software and transmitted to DGI at issuance; each invoice carries a DGI-authorized numeration range and a verifiable QR code. The model is advance authorization with immediate transmission (not full real-time central clearance). Digital signature (Firma Electrónica Avanzada) is mandatory for issuance; input ITBM credits are denied for non-compliant paper or unauthorized invoices. API integration with the e-Tax platform is supported for ERP/automated data-pipeline billing.

Panama imposes no exchange-control repatriation statute, but proceeds must be credited to Panama within 180 days as documentary proof of foreign-source/export status; failure to repatriate or evidence payment triggers recharacterization as Panama-source income and loss of the zero-rating. DGI audit scrutiny focuses on fictitious exports, back-to-back invoicing, and shell foreign recipients. Fraudulent zero-rating claims result in retroactive assessment of the full 7% ITBM from the date of supply, statutory surcharges and late-interest (reconocimiento) under the Código Fiscal, fines (including up to 200% of the omitted tax for reiterated infractions), and criminal defraudación fiscal prosecution where falsified export documentation or fictitious counterparties are proven. The applicable assessment limitation period is the general statute of limitations under Panamanian tax law, extendable in cases of omission or fraudulent conduct.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama uses the U.S. dollar as sole legal tender and maintains no exchange-control law. Current and capital account transactions are fully convertible without central bank approval; profit remittances and capital repatriation are unrestricted, subject only to AML/CFT record-keeping and reporting obligations under Law 23 of 2015 and SBP Agreement 002-2018.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or central bank reporting requirement for export proceeds. U.S. dollar receipts are freely retainable, transferable, and settleable in any clearing channel.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) — no standalone central bank exists; Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent and clearinghouse operator for the U.S.-dollarized financial system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-Medium. Panama has no distinct electronic-money institution (EMI) licensing regime; payment service providers require a banking license or SBP fintech sandbox authorization. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File must be filed when the taxpayer's aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed B/. 250,000 and gross revenue is above B/. 2,000,000; for multinational groups, the Master File is compulsory when consolidated group turnover exceeds B/. 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR, Law 254/2020) apply to PIFs carrying on a relevant activity (distribution and service centre operations; intellectual property holding; financing/leasing; or headquarters). The PIF must show physical premises in Panama, qualified full-time local employees, and actual decision-making in relation to those activities. A PIF holding only passive foreign assets and deriving no Panama-source income is classified as Pure Equity Holding Entity and files a simplified annual substance statement. Failing the substance test invalidates claims of treaty benefits and can trigger de-registration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Blindness

Statutory sanction: Reassessment plus interest at the statutory 10% annual rate; administrative surcharge up to 50%; and criminal tax evasion conviction carrying fines equal to 100% of evaded tax and imprisonment of 2 to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members and the resident agent who knowingly signed a false declaration are jointly and severally liable for the assessed tax; the court may impose a two-year travel ban, attachment of personal assets, and debarment as a director/trustee.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Sham characterization by the DGI; ESR fines from B/. 10,000 to B/. 1,000,000; cancellation of the foundation registration; and possible revocation of the tax-residency certificate used by your foreign banking relationship.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the veil and order directors to compensate third-party creditors for trades done through the empty shell; directors are also exposed to foreign court judgments and travel restrictions under the Hague Apostille/International Letters Rogatory.
Anti-Money Laundering / Crypto Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Panama's Law 23 of 2015: administrative fines of up to B/. 5,000,000 for deficient AML/CFT controls; conviction for money laundering may impose from 5 to 12 years' imprisonment and confiscation of all linked digital assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who 'turned a blind eye' may be prosecuted as principals for laundering, resulting in criminal imprisonment, personal asset freezing, travel bans, and prohibition from future fintech/digital-asset licenses.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing of Related-party API/Data Charges

Statutory sanction: The DGI can arm's-length adjust the fee, assess underpaid tax plus 1% monthly surcharge and statutory interest, and impose a 20% penalty on the amount of the adjustment if documentation is absent after the 30-day documentary request.

Director personal liability: Although TP penalties are generally payable by the entity, directors can be held personally liable for the equivalent of unpaid withholding tax if the mispricing is recharacterized as a constructive distribution; serious fraud referrals can expose them to the criminal penalties in red flag #1.
