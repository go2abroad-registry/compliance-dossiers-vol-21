Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministry of Economy and Finance, Panama; operational portal: Sistema de Facturación Electrónica and e-Tax 2.0 (https://etax.dgi.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (including a Private Interest Foundation) must appoint a resident legal representative and register with DGI to obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC). The application is submitted via e-Tax 2.0 or in person at DGI offices, providing the foundation's registered articles, certificate of foundation, registered agent details, a local service address, and statutory declaration of economic activity. DGI issues the RUC within 5–10 business days. The RUC is required for invoicing, VAT/ITBMS registration, and customs transactions.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign entity (including a Private Interest Foundation) must appoint a resident legal representative and register with DGI to obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC). The application is submitted via e-Tax 2.0 or in person at DGI offices, providing the foundation's registered articles, certificate of foundation, registered agent details, a local service address, and statutory declaration of economic activity. DGI issues the RUC within 5–10 business days. The RUC is required for invoicing, VAT/ITBMS registration, and customs transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a check-the-box regime; tax classification follows the statutory form. A Private Interest Foundation is a separate legal entity; if it exercises commercial activities in Panama, it elects to be taxed as a corporation under the territorial income tax rules. A PIF may also elect the Simplified Regime for Small Taxpayers if annual gross income does not exceed B/.200,000. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; taxpayers may, however, elect to apply OECD-aligned transfer pricing documentation thresholds and, under specific conditions, enter into advance pricing agreements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.07%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 annual taxable turnover (gross sales of movable goods and services; DGI Resolution No. 201-1581 of 2009, as updated by digital economy reforms)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Panama are subject to a 0% ITBMS rate. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated if the benefit is consumed outside Panama and the supplier retains evidence of the foreign recipient's country of residence. An exporter must hold a valid exporter registration with DGI, present the actual customs export evidence, and deposit export proceeds in a Panamanian banking institution to secure the 0% rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Exportación – DUE, issued by Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas)
  • Bill of Lading (original maritime carrier document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank verification of export payment repatriation)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (if applicable)

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) is phased from 2022 to 2025 for all ITBMS-registered taxpayers, including large, medium, and small taxpayers. The system requires real-time transmission of e-invoices to DGI via the 'Sistema de Facturación Electrónica' using DGI-authorized XML schemas, a digital signature certificate, and a unique successive authorization number (NIF). Invoices must carry a QR code verifying DGI clearance, and all records must be archived for six years. API integration is required for enterprise financial systems, and the framework is aligned with the Central American Digital Integration System.

Panamanian tax authorities may audit export transactions for substance and timing compliance. Failure to prove physical export or to repatriate proceeds within the statutory term triggers reversal of the zero-rating, additional ITBMS at 7% plus monthly interest from the invoice date, and penalties up to 200% of the tax omitted. False documentation or fraudulent export claims constitute tax evasion under Ley 8 de 2012, subject to criminal sanctions (imprisonment up to 5 years) and prohibition from future export privileges. A retrospective clawback period of 5 years applies for fraud, and 3 years for non-fraudulent procedural errors.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. Panama operates a dollarized, open economy with no exchange controls, no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, or capital, and no mandatory Central Bank approval for cross-border transfers. Legal tender is the U.S. dollar (USD) alongside the on-par Panamanian Balboa (PAB) which exists only as coins; bank accounts are commonly maintained in USD. For a Private Interest Foundation (PIF) engaged in offshore vessel leasing to non-residents, capital and income realized outside Panama's territorial tax jurisdiction are generally free of local tax, and remittance of charter hire and sale proceeds can be effected without FX conversion or surcharge.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Panama does not impose any surrender requirement, mandatory conversion timeline, or retention obligation for export proceeds. A Panama-domiciled entity may retain all USD earnings in local designated accounts or offshore accounts without reference to a statutory conversion regime. For marine service exports, including offshore supply vessel charters, invoicing is typically denominated in USD, and there is no requirement to exchange through local banks or to report export proceeds beyond the standard tax and statistical filings.

Central bank supervision: Republic of Panama has no de jure central bank; the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) serves as banking regulator and overseer of the national payment system. For foreign exchange and trade related transactions, the Ministerio de Comercio e Industrias and the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) have ancillary oversight. The U.S. dollar is legal tender, and the National Bank of Panama (Banco Nacional de Panamá) acts as fiscal agent but does not perform monetary control functions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate feasibility. Panama’s banking law requires formal financial infrastructure licensing for money transmission and electronic payment services; an offshore digital EMI (e.g., non-Panamanian e-money institution) cannot directly provide a local account or process local ACH/SPI transfers without partnering with a licensed Panamanian bank. Cross-border EMIs, including those from the EU or the UK, may open segregated operational accounts for the PIF only if they hold a recognized license and the PIF satisfies their global KYC rules. However, many EMIs decline Panama-domiciled foundations due to non-transparent beneficial ownership perceptions; a PIF should expect elevated due diligence requiring foundation deed, current public registry certificate (where applicable), and a certified beneficial owner affidavit.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Foundation deed and by-laws with certified Spanish translation; certificate of the Private Interest Foundation issued by the Public Registry of Panama (Registro Público); explicit name and passport copy of any council members and founder; and a sworn declaration of the final beneficial owner (with passport, residence proof, and professional reference), followed by an organizational chart identifying the natural person controlling the vessel chartering entity.
Offshore maritime charter activity regarded as high-velocity cross-border cash flows

Root cause: Offshore maritime charter activity regarded as high-velocity cross-border cash flows

Defense evidence: Copies of the underlying charter party contracts (BIMCO SUPPLYTIME or analogous) listing the foundation as lessor; invoices and corresponding payment schedule; evidence of statutory vessel registration (Panama SEGUMAR / ship registry) or a bareboat charter-in certificate; and a 12-month projected transaction ledger showing counterparty names, vessel identifiers, and expected remittance corridors.

Sanctions or PEP (politically exposed person) exposure associated with a vessel lessee in an international oil and gas jurisdiction

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the Private Interest Foundation’s initial equity or debt injection (typically USD for newbuild or second-hand offshore supply vessel acquisition), routing must use a United States dollar SWIFT transfer (MT103) from the founder's home country bank to a non-resident offshore USD account held by the PIF in Panama. The purpose code should be 'Trading proceeds - capital injection for offshore vessel chartering' under the IMF Balance of Payments classification; the wire must reference the PIF's unique identification (the 'PIF' Registry number) and the underlying vessel acquisition contract. To mitigate the Enhanced Due Diligence triggered by high-value first transfer, a pre-validation letter from the receiving Panamanian bank, a certificate of good standing from the Public Registry, and a copy of the signed charter/lease warrant is required. The receiving bank may impose a 5-7 business day verification period and freeze the funds until receipt of the compliance packet. Cleared or escrow settlement for the vessel purchase should use a maritime escrow service provider regulated by the SBP or an international maritime escrow arm of a tier-1 Panamanian bank to provide a compliant trail.Operating disbursements (crew, fuel, insurance, port dues, and maintenance) are settled via SWIFT MT103 wires or S.W.I.F.T.-based ACH equivalents (for Panamanian local beneficiary accounts) from the PIF's USD operational account. For charter hire received from leasing the offshore supply vessel to an international oil operator, receipts may be routed via (i) client bank to PIF USD account using SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'rent for offshore platform support vessel', or (ii) a commercial letter of credit (LC) issued by charterer's bank. Disbursements to suppliers typically involve MT103 or higher-tier MT202 COVER for multi-bank chains; when adapting to US banks, the PIF must ensure the account name and foundation title match the Panamanian record to avoid OFAC matching errors. For vessel disbursement hubs (e.g., Houston, Aberdeen, Singapore), the PIF can receive and disburse through a Panamanian bank's correspondent account in the US or the UK, with all payments accompanied by an invoice and voyage number.US Dollar (USD) is the functional currency; the Balboa (PAB) is fixed at 1:1 to the USD, meaning no cross-currency hedge is required domestically.For charter hire quoted in non-USD currencies (e.g., GBP or EUR), fully deliverable FX forwards or cross-currency swaps are permissible through licensed Panamanian banks or international brokers, provided the instrument is for hedging of actual vessel charter exposure and not speculative.Panama-domiciled PIFs may enter into non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only via an offshore bank with a US banking presence, and such trades are generally permitted only with proof of an underlying trade payable/receivable.Currency swaps hedged against USD for 3-12 month charters are permissible and settlement must be in USD or PAB at the same value; no Panamanian central bank authorization is required.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on net Panamanian-source income; non-resident PIF with wholly foreign-source charter revenue is exempt under territoriality principle

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends distributed from Panamanian-source net profits; no withholding on distributions from foreign-source funds held by PIF

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD: annual gross income of consolidated group exceeding USD 2,000,000 or aggregate related-party transactions exceeding USD 500,000 triggers contemporaneous Local File and Master File requirement in Panamanian pesos (US dollar equivalent)

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) applicable since 2021 to Panamanian-licensed entities engaged in relevant activities; offshore supply vessel chartering is a relevant activity requiring: physical office in Panama, presence of officers and employees, adequate expenditure from Panama, and core income-generating activities carried out locally; PIF sheltered from registration but economically active maritime operators must file annual substance declaration and maintain constitutional and operational records proving nexus

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of up to 200% of evaded tax; monetary fine USD 50,000 per violation; cancellation of operating license; criminal imprisonment up to 6 years for aggravated evasion

Director personal liability: Directors, foundation council, and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for restitution; travel bans and asset freezing; disqualification from holding directorship in Panama; criminal prosecution may pierce PIF veil where foundation is used as a sham
Shell Company / Abuse of Private Interest Foundation

Statutory sanction: Foundation struck from Registry; registration voided; fines up to USD 100,000; denial of tax-protected status retroactively making all worldwide income taxable at 25% plus penalties; potential freeze of bank accounts and assets

Director personal liability: Founders and council members can be personally taxed on undistributed foundation profits under an administrative recharacterization; criminal liability for money laundering; removal and compensation orders; inability to leave Panama until tax liability satisfied
AML / Maritime Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Panama AML penalties reach USD 1,000,000 and confiscation of the vessel and charter proceeds; refusal of registry renewal; extradition risk under international maritime law; vessel bonding and arrest; criminal sanction for economic-terrorist links up to 15 years

Director personal liability: Directors and bearer-share principals subject to arrest, extradition, and 10-year prison term; civil liability for proceeds and damages; directors' personal assets attachable where vessel is arrested; statutory disqualification from operational duties and forced resignations within 30 days
