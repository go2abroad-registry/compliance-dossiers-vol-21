Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá — Departamento de Fondaciones de Interés Privado (Public Registry of Panama, Foundations Department), with the Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC) subsequently administered by the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI).

Governing statute

Law No. 25 of 12 June 1995 (Ley No. 25 de 12 de junio de 1995) on Private Interest Foundations, as amended by Law No. 53 of 2001 and Law No. 13 of 9 April 2015; applicable sections on foundation charter execution, endowment, registered agent, and the Foundation Council (Consejo de la Fundación).

Formation / constitutional filings

Acta de Fundación de Fundación de Interés Privado (Foundation Charter), executed by private document or public deed and filed for inscription with the Public Registry.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The foundation endowment must be at least US$10,000 (or assets of equivalent market value); no full cash escrow is mandated at formation—the Foundation Council may evidence the endowment by declaration in the charter or by a subsequent contribution protocol, subject to the Public Registry's minimum capitalization verification.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification (Visto Bueno) issued by the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá after technical inspection of the warehouse egress, suppression systems, and emergency illumination, plus the Sistema Nacional de Protección Civil (SINAPROC) disaster-response alignment certificate; structural load inspection certification of racking and mezzanine systems by a licensed engineer; occupational health clearance for warehouse personnel issued by the Comisión de Salud Ocupacional (General Directorate of Occupational Health) of MINSA and a workplace-risk assessment protocol under the MITRADEL Hygiene and Safety regulations, together with the mandatory biosecurity protocol for pharmaceutical cargo handlers.

Paid-in capital rule: The foundation endowment must be at least US$10,000 (or assets of equivalent market value); no full cash escrow is mandated at formation—the Foundation Council may evidence the endowment by declaration in the charter or by a subsequent contribution protocol, subject to the Public Registry's minimum capitalization verification.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); e-portal: https://www.dgi.gob.pa; export and customs supervision by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA)

E-government filing portal

Non-resident founders must appoint a resident agent (agente residente), then register the Private Interest Foundation (Fundación de Interés Privado) charter with the Registro Público de Panamá to obtain the ficha (foundation file number). Thereafter: obtain a municipal Aviso de Operación; file the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) application with DGI via its portal or local office, presenting the certified foundation charter, ficha, resident agent credentials, registered office confirmation, and identification of the foundation council. The RUC serves as the single TIN/VAT ID. The PIF must then register as an ITBMS collector, select the invoicing modality (electronic invoicing or authorized fiscal printer), and complete the annual Aviso de Operación renewal. No separate NIT exists beyond the RUC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama has no check-the-box regime. A PIF is a separate juridical person taxed territorially: only Panama-source income is subject to income tax. The foundation may elect in its charter to adopt corporate-style governance; it may elect to register with DGI for Panama-source logistics/invoicing activities and, where average monthly gross sales do not exceed USD 3,000 (annualized USD 36,000), may elect the simplified small-taxpayer ITBMS quota regime. No pass-through or hybrid-mismatch elections exist under Panamanian law; territorial source attribution follows the Fiscal Code, principally Article 699.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold: any person performing taxable supplies in Panama must obtain RUC and register for ITBMS. A simplified small-taxpayer quota regime (Régimen Especial Simplificado) applies to businesses with average monthly gross turnover not exceeding USD 3,000 (annual maximum of USD 36,000). GDP pharma freight operators are expected to register under the general regime because of export documentation and refund mechanics.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (0% ITBMS) is available for: (i) physical export of goods to a non-resident consignee where the goods leave Panama; (ii) international transport of cargo, including freight forwarding, customs brokerage, and ancillary logistics services directly connected with the export operation; and (iii) services rendered to persons residing abroad whose benefit is realized entirely outside Panama. The GDP pharma freight operator must hold a valid RUC/exporter registration, issue zero-rated invoices, and prove that the cargo physically departed its Panama facility. Panama-to-Panama transport legs and pre-export warehousing remain subject to 7% unless directly and exclusively linked to an export transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) validated by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the goods from Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/bank transfer proof of foreign currency settlement
  • Zero-rated export invoice and underlying commercial invoice with GDP cargo description
  • GDP temperature excursion/transport protocol records evidencing cargo integrity
  • Exporter registry certificate (Registro de Exportadores), pharma license, and GDP accreditation certificate where required

Panama is progressively mandating electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica) through DGI-authorized e-invoicing platforms or the DGI online system. Invoices must be transmitted to DGI, digitally signed, and carry a unique DGI authorization code and QR/validation stamp; authorized fiscal printers remain transitional for qualifying small taxpayers. Zero-rated export freight invoices must identify the GDP shipment, the client's foreign or Panamanian tax identifier, and the applicable 0% export code, and must be issued through the approved ITBMS invoicing channel.

DGI may retroactively revoke zero-rated status where the export documentation bundle is incomplete, inconsistent, or untimely, reassessing ITBMS at 7% with statutory interest, surcharges, and administrative fines. The clawback limitation period is five years, extended to ten years in cases of fraud, concealment, or omission. Wilful false export declarations constitute tax fraud, exposing the PIF, its foundation council, resident agent, and signatories to criminal prosecution under Panama's Penal Code, fines, registry suspension, customs penalties under Panama's customs legislation, and exclusion from the exporter registry.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. The US dollar is legal tender under Decree Law 20 of 1904; there is no exchange control, no mandatory central bank approval, and no restriction on the repatriation of profits, dividends, or capital. Profit remittance is subject only to corporate tax and any applicable dividend distribution taxes under the territorial tax regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender and no mandatory conversion ratio exists. Export receipts may be retained in USD or any freely traded currency, credited to any Panamanian banking institution, and disbursed without exchange control consent.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank; the foreign exchange and banking regime is administered by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Superintendency of Banks), with Banco Nacional de Panamá acting as fiscal agent and the Ministry of Economy and Finance supervising monetary and exchange matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability until a full Panamanian banking license is obtained. Panama does not issue a dedicated EMI license; digital banks and fintech platforms must operate through a licensed Panamanian bank or hold a foreign license, which is generally not accepted for direct clearing. Offshore EMI accounts may be used for ancillary payment collection but are not viable as primary treasury rails for GDP pharma freight settlement without a Panamanian license and local compliance seat.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% uniform CIT on Panama-source income; 5% surtax on taxable income exceeding USD 1.5M yields a 30% marginal rate for large taxpayers

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% complementary withholding on dividends paid out of Panama-source taxable profits of a commercial corporation or a PIF taxed as a corporation (combined effective burden: 25% CIT + 7.5% net dividend tax ≈ 32.5%); distributions by a PIF from foreign-source foundational assets are executed as gratuitous transfers and are subject to 0% Panamanian withholding; a 5% advanced dividend tax applies to non-distributed taxable profits at year-end

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months after fiscal year-end; DGI assigns a staggered calendar by last RUC digit (typically extending to 15 April / 30 June for calendar-year taxpayers). A PIF with exclusively foreign-source income files no Form 31; the return becomes mandatory only when the PIF derives Panama-source commercial or logistics income.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Must be settled within the first semester of each fiscal year, with the DGI calendar generally setting 30 June as the final payment date; the fee is obligatory regardless of income or activity

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panama Balboas (PAB) — pegged 1:1 with USD. Threshold: taxpayers whose annual gross income exceeds USD 5,000,000 or whose cross-border related-party transactions exceed USD 5,000,000 must file Form 21 (Declaración Informativa de Precios de Transferencia) and a sworn transfer-pricing technical report (Informe Pericial) prepared by an independent expert registered with the DGI. PIFs with foreign-source-only income fall below the threshold and bear no TP documentation duty unless they elect to file Form 31 or engage in Panama-source intercompany transactions

Economic substance scope: Panama has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute applicable to all entities; substance is evaluated through (i) the territorial-source test for CIT, (ii) the SEM regime requiring a physical office and at least 3 resident employees within 12 months, and (iii) DTA/EOIR substance expectations for treaty access. A PIF acting as a passive holding vehicle for foreign GDP freight assets satisfies the minimum substance threshold if it maintains: a Registered Agent in Panama, complete accounting records lodged in Panama, an assets register, and minutes of the Foundation Council; mere brass-plate PIFs relying on DTAs to shift freight income face re-characterization as sham entities

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Código Penal Art. 288: imprisonment of 2 to 5 years; fine of 5 to 10 times the evaded tax; confiscation of assets and instruments; administrative addition of 50% surcharge plus monthly interest of 1.5% on the omitted tax; loss of deduction and credit rights

Director personal liability: Foundation Councillors, directors, and the resident agent who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and fines; during the fiscal-criminal process the court may impose a travel ban (impedimento de salida) and civil attachment on personal assets; conviction results in concurrent imprisonment for the legal representatives
Artificial Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under Law 254 of up to USD 50,000 for incomplete or inaccurate beneficial-ownership filings; cancellation or suspension of the Registered Agent's license; the foundation's legal standing is suspended, accounts are frozen, and automatic exchange of information is triggered in favor of the foreign tax authority

Director personal liability: Piercing of the foundation's statutory shield: contributions made in fraud of creditors are voidable via the acción pauliana; the Foundation Council and settlor are personally liable to creditors for the repatriated value; directors may face travel bans and civil restitution orders from Panamanian courts; criminal liability for false declarations to the DGI beneficial-ownership registry
AML/CFT Violations (Law 23 of 2015, as amended by Law 70 of 2019)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines from USD 10,000 to USD 1,000,000 or twice the benefit obtained, whichever is higher, per infraction; suspension or revocation of the GDP freight operating license; the PIF's assets may be subject to forfeiture proceedings before the Public Ministry

Director personal liability: Criminal prosecution of the compliance officer and Foundation Council members for money laundering under Código Penal Arts. 254–255: imprisonment of 3 to 10 years; freezing of personal bank accounts; travel bans and posting of a pecuniary bond; directors who execute or benefit from laundering transactions face extended terms and are barred from holding office in any Panamanian regulated entity
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed fine of USD 10,000 for each unfiled obligation (Form 21 and the technical report); disallowance of intercompany deductions; TP adjustment to taxable income plus a 50% surcharge on the adjustment; recharacterization of the transaction and extended statute of limitations for assessment

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the pricing policy are jointly liable for the adjustment, surcharge, and fines; the DGI may pierce to the parent group's assets if the Panamanian entity is undercapitalized; criminal referral is possible when the TP adjustment indicates fraudulent revenue shifting, resulting in travel bans and seizure of personal property
