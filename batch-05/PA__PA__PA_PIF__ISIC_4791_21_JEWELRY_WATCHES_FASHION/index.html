Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow account is required under Law 25 of 1995; the Foundation Charter must identify an initial endowment of stated value, which may be nominal and must be allocated to the foundation's private benefit purposes.

Funding & unlock path: No fire safety certificate, structural load inspection, or occupational clearance applies to a non-operational foundation. If the e-commerce operator later leases a local warehouse or office, the Panama Fire Department safety inspection and municipal premises permit must be obtained before occupation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) — Sede Electrónica portal: https://sede.dgi.gob.pa (also https://dgi.mef.gob.pa); electronic invoicing and real-time clearance: Sistema de Facturación Electrónica administered by the DGI; customs export control and verification: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) through the SIGA customs platform.

E-government filing portal

Panamanian TIN/VAT ID procedure for a foreign-owned entity: (1) incorporate the Private Interest Foundation under Law 25 of 1995 in the Registro Público de Panamá using a licensed Panamanian attorney; (2) execute notarized and, where required, apostilled powers of attorney appointing a resident agent (agente residente) and an enrolled Contador Público Autorizado as fiscal agent; (3) file the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) registration application via the DGI Sede Electrónica, attaching the foundation deed and by-laws, foundation council and protector identity documents, and the Aviso de Operación where any Panamanian business presence exists; (4) the DGI assigns the RUC, which serves as both the income-tax registration and ITBMS VAT identification number; (5) complete post-registration compliance: enroll in electronic invoicing, file ITBMS returns where Panamanian-source taxable activity exists, file annual income tax returns, and maintain transfer-pricing documentation where cross-border related-party transactions occur. A PIF operating exclusively in foreign commerce may register in zero-activity status; however, the RUC must be in place before zero-rated export sales, refund claims, or official export certifications are processed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election and no EU/OECD hybrid-mismatch election regime currently exists in Panamanian tax law; Panama applies a unitary territorial-source system. Available elections and structural choices: (a) the Special Small Taxpayer Regime (Régimen Especial de Pequeño Contribuyente) for annual gross income not exceeding B/. 100,000, allowing simplified quarterly ITBMS settlement; (b) the five-year net operating loss carryforward election in the annual income tax return; (c) unilateral, bilateral, or multilateral Advance Pricing Agreement (APA) requests under Panama's transfer-pricing rules; (d) foundation-charter characterization of the PIF as an autonomous patrimony pursuant to Law 25 of 1995, fixing the entity's civil-law classification and the non-application of Panamanian withholding taxes on distributions of foreign-source assets to non-resident beneficiaries.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Panama does not operate a general economic-turnover registration threshold: any person that performs taxable transfers of goods or services in Panamanian territory must obtain the RUC/ITBMS registration before commencing activity. Administrative classification thresholds in Balboas (B/., pegged 1:1 to USD): Pequeño Contribuyente (simplified quarterly regime) up to B/. 100,000 annual gross income; Mediano Contribuyente B/. 100,001 to B/. 3,000,000; Gran Contribuyente above B/. 3,000,000. Online sellers receiving orders from Panamanian consumers or conducting fulfillment in Panama trigger the same registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports qualify for the 0% zero rate under Panama's territorial ITBMS rules. Eligibility conditions: (i) the PIF holds a valid RUC and is identified as exporter on the export documentation; (ii) jewelry/watches or other goods are physically dispatched from Panama to a non-resident buyer under an accepted customs export declaration or express-courier export procedure, with departure evidenced before the statutory deadline; (iii) services and digital supplies are made for the benefit of a non-resident and are not used, consumed, or exploited in Panama; (iv) the entire export consideration is credited in USD/B. to a bank or electronic-money settlement account of the foundation (Stripe/PayPal proceeds acceptable as payment evidence); (v) no re-entry of goods into Panamanian customs territory occurs. Supplies delivered in Panama or to Panamanian-registered payment cards are domestic sales chargeable at 7%.

  • Customs export declaration (SIGA electronic customs export document) evidencing the physical export of the goods and their departure from Panamanian customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list and the DGI electronic invoice folio
  • Bill of lading (ocean) or air waybill/international courier airway bill naming the non-resident destination consignee
  • Bank inward remittance certificate or payment-platform settlement evidence (Stripe/PayPal) demonstrating receipt of export proceeds in USD
  • Foreign buyer purchase order or sales contract and identification/residence evidence of the non-resident purchaser
  • Export-support file containing the zero-rating justification and, if claimed, the ITBMS refund application submitted to the DGI

Panama has made electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica de la DGI) mandatory in phased roll-outs that cover e-commerce sellers and all registered taxpayers. Each invoice must be an XML/CSV document bearing the taxpayer's digital certificate, unique authorization folio and QR code, and must be cleared with the DGI in real time before legal issuance through the DGI Web Services (API), the DGI web portal, or a certified third-party facturador electrónico. The zero-rate export invoice and domestic 7% invoice are equally subject to this clearance. Offline contingency issuance is accepted only under DGI-authorized network failures with mandatory submission immediately upon restoration. A PIF operating an e-commerce business must therefore align its Stripe/PayPal checkout with a compliant e-invoicing engine, generating an invoice per transaction and preserving settlement records.

If the DGI or ANA disallows an export zero-rating, the supply is re-characterized as taxable at the 7% ITBMS rate, with retrospective assessment plus statutory late-payment recargo, moratory interest, and pecuniary sanctions generally equal to at least 100% of the evaded tax where fraudulent invoices or false customs declarations are found. Senior management — the foundation council, protector, resident legal representative, and the registered fiscal agent — may be jointly declared liable, and criminal tax-fraud proceedings under Panamanian law can result in imprisonment. Repeated or structured abuse leads to cancellation of the RUC, prohibition on further zero-rating, and clawback of refunds previously paid. The confidentiality features of a Private Interest Foundation do not shield books, banking or payment-platform records from lawful judicial or tax-competent authority access in fraud investigations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de facto and de jure convertibility. Neither capital controls nor exchange restrictions exist. The US dollar is the sole statutory monetary unit; profit remittance, dividend distributions, and capital repatriation are unrestricted and require only standard banking documentation and DGI tax-compliance receipts. OFAC sanctions and UAF reporting thresholds remain the only substantive barriers to convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export surrender or mandatory conversion requirements apply because exporters invoice and settle in USD. The statutory obligations are limited to maintaining underlying trade documents (commercial invoices, bills of lading, customs declaración) and reporting any single cash deposit above USD 10,000 to the UAF in accordance with Law 23 (2015) and its implementing regulations. Foreign-currency purchase or conversion is never required for USD-denominated export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Panama does not maintain a Central Bank or Foreign Exchange Administration. The USD is legal tender under official dollarization (Decree No. 221 of 1904). Banking and AML supervision is vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), while the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) administers terrorism-financing and money-laundering reporting. Fiscal and customs enforcement is handled by the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) and the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) respectively.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-Low. Panama has not yet enacted a comprehensive Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime; the SBP's proposed fintech law (Bill 207) is still pending. Offshore EMIs are not considered authorized regulated banks in Panama and cannot lawfully onboard an exempted private interest foundation as a direct custodian of foundation assets. A PIF targeting e-commerce (Stripe/PayPal) must establish a Panama-licensed commercial or operating subsidiary, or execute a 'wrapped' payments arrangement through a licensed bank's merchant acquiring services. Full EMI status is not viable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The founder's initial capital injection must be remitted via international wire in USD, using SWIFT MT103. Beneficiary bank instruction should carry purpose code 'BP' (business payment) and reference 'Capital Injection – PIF ISIC 4791'. Any wire equal to or exceeding USD 10,000 is automatically reported to the UAF, necessitating a complete source-of-funds file. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding on dividends paid out of Panama-source profits; reduced to 5% where dividends are paid on registered shares out of profits that have already borne corporate income tax. Dividends paid from foreign-source (territorially excluded) profits are not subject to Panamanian dividend withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.1,500,000 in gross annual revenues (or assets of B/.3,000,000), triggering the obligation to maintain contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation and to file the annual transfer pricing informative return (Anexo de Precios de Transferencia) in the e-Tax portal.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 254 of 2021) apply to PIFs and corporations conducting eligible activities, which include distribution, service centers, holding activities, shipping, and intellectual property holding. The annual ESR report must be filed together with the payment of the annual franchise tax on or before 31 March. Sufficiency requires a physical office in Panama, full-time employees, local board meetings, and operating expenditure proportionate to the income generated. An e-commerce jewelry PIF must therefore document that online order management, payment settlement decisions, and contract negotiation functions are performed in Panama. Non-compliance attracts fines from US$10,000 to US$1,000,000 and possible revocation of the foundation's license.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Civil fine of 50% of the omitted tax, increased to 150% where fraud is established, plus 1% monthly interest, surcharges, and tax lien registration; criminal tax evasion carries imprisonment of 2 to 6 years where the evaded amount exceeds US$100,000 (Panama Tax Code and Criminal Code).

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficiaries who participate in evasion face joint civil and criminal liability; Panamanian courts may apply 'levantamiento del velo corporativo' (piercing the corporate veil), order asset confiscation, impose civil restitution, issue travel bans, and bar directors from leaving the country.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Law 254 of 2021 ESR fines of US$10,000 to US$1,000,000 per violation, revocation of the foundation registration, and denial of the territorial exclusion, resulting in assessment of 25% CIT plus 10% dividend withholding on deemed distributions.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members are jointly and severally liable for the foundation's fiscal debts; courts may impose personal fines, disqualify directors for up to 5 years, and compel civil restitution of distributions made while the foundation was insolvent or non-compliant.
AML/CFT Deficiencies in E-Commerce Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Law 23 of 2015 as amended by Law 32 of 2017 authorizes fines of up to US$1,000,000 per violation, asset freezing, liquidation of the PIF, and prohibition from transacting with Panamanian licensed banks.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be imprisoned for 5 to 12 years for willful money laundering; they are liable in rem for the proceeds of illicit goods, and travel bans and extradition cooperation apply.
Failure to File / Withholding and Annual Franchise Tax Default

Statutory sanction: A 10% monthly surcharge up to 50% of the tax, 1% monthly interest, and a minimum B/.50 fine per unfiled form; cumulative fines can reach B/.50,000; the DGI may publish the tax debt and cancel the PIF's legal personality.

Director personal liability: Directors causing the omission may be held personally liable for the foundation's tax liabilities up to the value of assets distributed; they additionally risk 5-year disqualification from serving on Panamanian boards and suspension of corporate rights.
