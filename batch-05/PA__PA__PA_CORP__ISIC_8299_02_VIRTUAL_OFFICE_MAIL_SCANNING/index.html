Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) - portal: SIT (Sistema Integrado de Tributación) at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa/; e-invoicing and RUC management are performed exclusively through the SIT platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporation: execute the public deed of the Sociedad Anónima before a Panamanian notary, appoint a licensed resident agent and officers, and register the deed at the Registro Público de Panamá (no minimum share capital required; non-resident ownership is permitted). Step 2 - Municipal license: obtain the Aviso de Operación from the municipality of the registered fiscal address (for a CMRA/virtual-office business, the municipal commercial license category 'servicios profesionales y de correspondencia'). Step 3 - RUC application: file the DGI RUC registration through the SIT web portal or at the DGI regional office presenting the testified deed copy, the resident-agent certificate, the Aviso de Operación, the certified appointment of the legal representative, passport copies of the foreign officers, and proof of the Panamanian fiscal domicile. Step 4 - Activation: DGI issues the 11-digit RUC (the single tax identifier for income tax, ITBMS, and withholdings) and activates the taxpayer in the ITBMS registrant registry. Step 5 - E-invoicing enrollment: request registration in the facturación electrónica system through SIT, upload the legal representative's electronic signature certificate, and obtain the E-Code issuing credentials. Step 6 - Operations start only after the RUC and e-invoicing activation are confirmed; the Aviso de Operación must also be renewed/validated by the municipality for the fiscal year.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope - the Panamanian S.A. is a tax resident but is taxed only on Panama-source income at the entity level (25% corporate rate); active foreign-source income is exempt under the territorial rule if the operative situs of the activity is outside Panama. No Pass_Through_Eligible status exists for S.A. companies, and the Tax_Exempt_Zone regimes (Colón Free Zone, City of Knowledge SEDE) are not applicable to a domestic virtual-office CMRA operation.

Available election forms

Neither US-style check-the-box nor hybrid-mismatch elections exist in Panama. Available statutory elections: (i) classification as Gran Contribuyente (Large Taxpayer) under DGI criteria with large-taxpayer filing obligations; (ii) election to apply the Simplified Income Tax Regime (SIS) with optional IVA promedio (approximate ITBMS) for businesses with gross Panama-source income up to B/.300,000 per year - strategically relevant for a small CMRA S.A.; (iii) board-resolution election designating the company as an export-service provider to evidence the 0% ITBMS export treatment for cross-border mail-scanning services; (iv) election to pay dividends tax through the statutory dividend prepayment mechanism (CUM) rather than upon actual distribution; hybrid-mismatch defenses rely exclusively on evidencing the non-residence of the beneficiary rather than on any elective regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.0.00 (no de minimis exemption): any person or entity regularly transferring goods or rendering services in Panama must register for ITBMS upon the first taxable transaction. A CMRA S.A. generating Panama-source service income must register regardless of volume. Non-resident digital-service platforms were additionally captured by Law 30 of 2022, which established simplified ITBMS registration for foreign suppliers of digital services with a B/.250,000 annual digital-services turnover threshold; this threshold does not apply to a Panamanian-domiciled S.A.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (physical departure through customs) and exports of services are zero-rated at 0% ITBMS. A virtual-office/mail-scanning S.A. qualifies for zero-rating when: (a) the recurring subscription and scanning services are contracted by a non-resident client with no Panamanian establishment; (b) the digital files are delivered or made accessible outside Panama (remote platform, overseas data center, foreign e-mail domain of the client); (c) payment for the service remits from abroad; (d) the contract contains a place-of-consumption/place-of-use clause excluding Panama; (e) an export-service declaration is filed with DGI and the export-service contract is registered. Zero-rating is denied, and the service becomes taxable at 7%, where the mail-scanning activity benefits a Panamanian branch, a Panama-domiciled co-working user, or any Panamanian addressee/beneficiary. Panama operates a fully dollarized economy with no central bank and no statutory FX-repatriation obligation; therefore no 180-day inward remittance deadline exists and the structured field is set to 0 days.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación de Mercancías) evidencing physical exit of hard-copy mail or consolidated shipments when physical forwarding is used - filed through the customs electronic single window
  • Registered Export Services Certificate or notarized export-service contract identifying the non-resident client, service description, fees, and place of consumption outside Panama
  • Bill of Lading / Master Air Waybill for physical mail-outs, or the digital delivery log showing server-side transmission of scanned data files to the client's offshore platform with IP geolocation records
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT or foreign-bank transfer evidence proving receipt of foreign-currency payment from the non-resident buyer
  • E-code ITBMS export invoice (DGE-XML export document type) issued through the DGI e-invoicing system
  • Certificate of non-residency or the foreign tax identification number of the client together with the executed subscription agreement
  • Internal work-order records demonstrating that the mail-scanning activity took place in Panama but the output was delivered abroad

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance model under Law 179 of 2020 and Executive Decree No. 41 of 2022 (as amended). The taxpayer's billing software must transmit each e-invoice XML instance (DGE-XML document standard) via the SIT web-service API (SOAP/REST with mutual TLS authenticated by the PKCS#12 digital certificate of the legal representative) to DGI; DGI validates the document in real time and returns a unique electronic authorization code (E-Code) plus a validation timestamp; only the version containing the E-Code may be delivered to the client and recognized as a fiscal document. A QR code containing the E-Code is embedded in the invoice. Credit notes, debit notes, and export invoices follow the same real-time flow; export invoices must carry the dedicated export-document type in the DGE-XML schema and reference the customs export declaration. The rollout is phased by taxpayer size and became mandatory for all active RUC taxpayers in the 2024-2025 cycle. Sanctions: invoices without a valid E-Code are void for tax-credit and expense-deduction purposes, and the issuer is fined under the Código Fiscal.

DGI's fiscalization unit (Dirección de Fiscalización) audits export-credit claims with emphasis on sham non-resident clients, intra-group service arrangements, and service consumption by Panamanian beneficiaries. Where zero-rating is disallowed, DGI re-assesses 7% ITBMS on the undeclared revenue plus statutory compensatory interest (currently about 9% per annum) and fines: (i) up to 100% of omitted ITBMS for false E-code e-invoice declarations; (ii) RUC and e-invoicing authorization suspension for repeat violations; (iii) criminal liability under the tax-crime provisions of the Código Fiscal and Ley No. 8 de 2010 - three to six years imprisonment and fines of B/.10,000 to B/.100,000 for export documentation containing falsified customs data. Any ITBMS export credit refunded as 'Saldo a Favor' (offset against future taxes or paid out by DGI) is clawed back with interest, and the taxpayer is barred from generating export e-invoices for 12 months. Automatic fraud-review red flags include export invoices exceeding 120% of declared export revenue, recurrent invoice cancellations after E-code issuance, and client addresses matching Panamanian or regional mail-forwarding clusters (classic CMRA fraud indicators).

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% flat on net taxable income (unified rate introduced by Law 77 of 2019; previously progressive scale up to 30%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends paid from profits already subject to Panamanian ISR; 20% on dividends distributed out of non-taxed or exempt profits (e.g., offshore income not subject to ISR in Panama).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three (3) months after the fiscal year-end; for calendar-year companies the deadline is March 31 of the following tax period.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 – Período Fiscal Stated fiscal year or fiscal period end date (e.g., 31/12/2024). The filing entity is the Panamanian S.A.; a non-resident shareholder does not complete this field. Untimely filing: late-filing surcharge of 10% of the tax due, plus daily interest (approximately 1% per month). Administrative fines for non-filing range between USD/PAB 100 and USD/PAB 500. Fraudulent filing: criminal prosecution for tax evasion, restitution, fines and imprisonment from 2 to 5 years.

The company must register for a RUC through a Panamanian legal representative before any filing is accepted. Untimely filing: late-filing surcharge of 10% of the tax due, plus daily interest (approximately 1% per month). Administrative fines for non-filing range between USD/PAB 100 and USD/PAB 500. Fraudulent filing: criminal prosecution for tax evasion, restitution, fines and imprisonment from 2 to 5 years.
Box 4 – Ingresos No Gravables Income derived exclusively from sources outside Panama (territorial exemption) and other expressly exempt receipts. The taxpayer bears the burden of proof; documentary evidence of the foreign source must be retained and produced on DGI request. Untimely filing: late-filing surcharge of 10% of the tax due, plus daily interest (approximately 1% per month). Administrative fines for non-filing range between USD/PAB 100 and USD/PAB 500. Fraudulent filing: criminal prosecution for tax evasion, restitution, fines and imprisonment from 2 to 5 years.
Box 5 – Costos y Gastos Deducibles Ordinary and necessary operating expenses: office lease, utility bills, employee wages, equipment depreciation, software and scanning consumables. Charges from foreign related parties must be arm's-length and supported by documentation; undocumented payments are disallowed. Untimely filing: late-filing surcharge of 10% of the tax due, plus daily interest (approximately 1% per month). Administrative fines for non-filing range between USD/PAB 100 and USD/PAB 500. Fraudulent filing: criminal prosecution for tax evasion, restitution, fines and imprisonment from 2 to 5 years.
Box 6 – Renta Neta Gravable Gross taxable income minus deductible costs and expenses. Negative balances are available as losses and may be carried forward for a maximum of five (5) fiscal years. Untimely filing: late-filing surcharge of 10% of the tax due, plus daily interest (approximately 1% per month). Administrative fines for non-filing range between USD/PAB 100 and USD/PAB 500. Fraudulent filing: criminal prosecution for tax evasion, restitution, fines and imprisonment from 2 to 5 years.
Box 7 – ISR Causado Tax determined by applying the 25% statutory rate to net taxable income. If the CAIR alternative calculation produces a higher amount, the excess must be added in the appropriate supplementary field. Untimely filing: late-filing surcharge of 10% of the tax due, plus daily interest (approximately 1% per month). Administrative fines for non-filing range between USD/PAB 100 and USD/PAB 500. Fraudulent filing: criminal prosecution for tax evasion, restitution, fines and imprisonment from 2 to 5 years.
Box 8 – Anticipos y Retenciones Credit for quarterly advances prepaid during the year, plus any withholding taxes borne by the company. Advances are not refundable; any surplus credit carries forward to the following year. Untimely filing: late-filing surcharge of 10% of the tax due, plus daily interest (approximately 1% per month). Administrative fines for non-filing range between USD/PAB 100 and USD/PAB 500. Fraudulent filing: criminal prosecution for tax evasion, restitution, fines and imprisonment from 2 to 5 years.
Box 9 – Saldo a Pagar o Crédito a Favor Final tax due after credits, or the excess credit available for carry-forward. The balance must be paid in Balboas (pegged 1:1 to the US Dollar) via the DGI's electronic banking channels. Untimely filing: late-filing surcharge of 10% of the tax due, plus daily interest (approximately 1% per month). Administrative fines for non-filing range between USD/PAB 100 and USD/PAB 500. Fraudulent filing: criminal prosecution for tax evasion, restitution, fines and imprisonment from 2 to 5 years.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD/PAB 6,000,000 in aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions for mandatory preparation of the TP Local File and Master File (Law 54 of 2017, as regulated by Executive Decree 486/2019). Taxpayers below the threshold must nevertheless substantiate arm's-length pricing for all related-party dealings.

Economic substance scope: Law 70 of 2019 and Executive Decree 351/2019 impose Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) on companies tax-resident in Panama that perform 'relevant activities,' including distribution and service centers. A CMRA/virtual-office entity that receives and scans mail in Panama qualifies as a service-center activity and must demonstrate, on an annual ESR declaration filed with the DGI, adequate physical premises, qualified full-time staff, operational expenditure and management located in Panama. An S.A. operating on paper only, without actual execution of the CMRA activity in Panama, fails the substance test and may forfeit its tax-residence status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Compulsory restitution of the evaded ISR, late-payment interest at approximately 1% monthly, a late-filing/adjustment surcharge of up to 10% of the tax, and monetary fines of up to ten times the evaded tax. Criminal prosecution under the Panamanian Penal Code and Tax Code carries imprisonment of 2 to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers signing false returns may be indicted as co-perpetrators, face preventive embargoes of personal assets, travel bans, disqualification from holding corporate office, and civil restitution obligations when the corporate vehicle is used as a façade.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to USD/PAB 50,000 for ESR non-compliance, denial or withdrawal of the tax-residence certificate by the DGI, disclosure to the EU/OECD under harmful-tax-practices frameworks, and possible strike-off or forced liquidation through the Registro Público.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified from acting as officers of any Panamanian company, held civilly liable to the State for penalties, subjected to cross-border travel restrictions, and criminally charged if the shell structure was used to conceal beneficial ownership.
Money Laundering via CMRA

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under Law 23 of 2015 and Penal Code Article 254: imprisonment of 5 to 12 years, corporate fines, confiscation of proceeds and instrumentalities, and reporting to the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF). Aggravated circumstances raise penalties to 10 to 15 years.

Director personal liability: Officers who execute, conceal or benefit from the laundering scheme face extended imprisonment, asset freezes and forfeiture, travel bans, debarment from financial-sector positions, and joint and several civil liability for the illicit proceeds.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: DGI recharacterization at arm's length, additional ISR at 25% on the upward adjustment, monthly interest at approximately 1%, penalties on the omitted tax (with enhanced fines in fraud cases), and mandatory CAIR reconciliation.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign defective TP documentation may be jointly liable for the adjusted tax, interest and penalties, and face criminal tax-evasion charges if the mispricing is shown to be intentional.
