Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (DGI-MEF); official e-portal: Sede Electrónica (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa). Customs export controls are administered by the Dirección General de Aduanas (DGA) under the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA), linked to the DGI for fiscal clearance.

E-government filing portal

For a Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) engaging in shipyard operations: (1) appoint a licensed Panamanian resident agent and a fiscal representative domiciled in Panama; (2) register the foundation deed with the Public Registry and obtain the certificado de inscripción and aviso de operación; (3) submit RUC application Form RUC-01 together with the foundation charter, public registry filing certificate, resident-agent acceptance, director/beneficiary identification documents (as applicable) and proof of the Panamanian operational address to DGI-MEF; (4) obtain the AIG-issued digital signature (Firma Digital) and activate the Sede Electrónica e-tax account; (5) acquire the DGI-issued RUC and, if carrying on taxable local business or exporting goods, register under the ITBMS and the export customs regime; (6) for zero-rated exports, enroll with DGA and appoint a licensed customs broker, furnish a customs bond, and request an export registration in the customs electronic system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panamanian law does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election. A PIF is treated as a separate taxable person for Panamanian-source income and is not automatically transparent; however, treaty and international anti-abuse rules may require look-through treatment where the foundation is used for tax evasion or lacks economic substance. A PIF may elect the simplified 'pequeño contribuyente' ITBMS regime when its local gross receipts remain below the statutory threshold, may elect to register as an exporter to claim input ITBMS refunds, and may apply for a foreign-trade or export-incentive stability contract under the law of multinational company headquarters regimes if it performs qualifying headquarter services for the shipyard group. No separate hybrid-mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary exemption threshold: any person or entity habitually or occasionally transferring taxable movable goods or rendering taxable services in Panamanian territory is required to obtain a RUC and register for ITBMS. For a foreign shipyard exporter, compulsory registration arises from the first taxable act performed in Panama or pursuant to DGI export requirements. An optional small taxpayer (pequeño contribuyente) regime is available to local-source taxpayers below the annual gross receipts ceiling set by DGI regulations; exporters do not benefit from that threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under ITBMS applies to goods sold by a registered Panamanian exporter to a non-resident buyer and physically exported from Panama under a customs export declaration, including newly constructed deep-sea container vessels delivered at the shipyard and cleared for international voyage. It also applies to services physically utilized abroad, services directly connected with exported goods, and services performed for non-resident persons in respect of foreign-going vessels. The shipyard must prove transfer of title to the non-resident owner, actual movement of the vessel out of Panamanian customs territory, full consideration received in convertible foreign currency, and the absence of any local consumption or use in Panama before export. Input ITBMS incurred on materials, subcontracts and yard services is creditable/refundable only to the extent the export is duly documented and the exporter is registered at the time of the transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduanas / DUA)
  • Bill of Lading (or non-negotiable Sea Waybill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas)

Panama operates a mandatory electronic invoicing framework (Facturación Electrónica) through DGI's Sede Electrónica. Authorized taxpayers, including ITBMS-exporting shipyards, must issue electronic invoices using software connected to the DGI platform by certified application programming interfaces; each invoice is validated and assigned a DGI fiscal authentication code. Export invoices must carry the RUC, the word 'Exento/Exportación', a link to the customs export declaration, and the digital stamp generated by the DGI system. The invoice must be transmitted before customs clearance and retained in the electronic filing system. Failure to obtain DGI invoice authorization invalidates the exporter's zero-rating substantiation.

DGI and DGA may jointly conduct post-shipment audits of all export files; the exporter must retain the documentary bundle for at least the statutory fiscal prescription period. A false or unsupported export zero-rating claim is reversed retroactively, and DGI claws back uncollected ITBMS on the exported goods, disallows corresponding input credits, and assesses monthly late interest and surcharges. Material misrepresentation for the purpose of obtaining a refund constitutes tax fraud under the Fiscal Code, exposing the shipyard to fines of 100%–500% of the tax evaded, cancellation of the exporter registration and electronic-invoicing authorization, and criminal penalties for the responsible directors and fiscal representative. Intentional customs false declarations additionally attract customs penalties, forfeiture, and suspension of the customs broker license.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both current and capital account transactions. The US Dollar is legal tender and the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) is a nominal unit at parity, circulating only as fractional coinage. No prior central-bank approval, exchange-control declaration, or repatriation restriction is imposed on inbound capital, trade settlements, dividends, or profit remittances. The only operative obstacles are the AML/KYC underwriting standards of the receiving Panamanian bank and the correspondent due-diligence of US clearing banks; no legal FX restriction exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama's dollarized regime imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export receipts. Proceeds from container-vessel sales may be retained in USD in the PIF's Panamanian accounts and freely redeployed, subject only to ordinary customs export declarations to the Dirección General de Aduanas and the territorial income-tax audit trail maintained with the DGI.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the sole banking regulator, complemented by Banco Nacional de Panamá as the State's fiscal agent; Panama operates under full US-dollar legal tender with no autonomous central bank and no monetary policy discretion.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Panama grants no standalone non-resident EMI passporting license; an offshore digital money institution (EU/UK/Asian EMI) cannot clear for the PIF without local regulatory grounding. Permissible channels are (i) the SBP FinTech innovation sandbox under Law 55 of 2021, (ii) a regulated partnership with an SBP-licensed clearing bank, or (iii) use of EMIs solely as low-value receivables collectors with daily concentration into the Panamanian treasury. Primary corporate liquidity and all escrow and LC rails must remain at SBP-licensed banks.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on taxable net income exceeding USD 50,000 per annum; progressive rates of 15% on the first USD 11,000 and 20% on income between USD 11,001 and USD 50,000 apply to smaller corporate taxpayers; territorial taxation applies only to income derived from a Panamanian source.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing and final payment must occur on or before the last day of the third month following the fiscal year-end (31 March for a calendar-year shipyard); quarterly advance payments (pagos a cuenta) are due in June, September, December, and March during and after the fiscal year.
