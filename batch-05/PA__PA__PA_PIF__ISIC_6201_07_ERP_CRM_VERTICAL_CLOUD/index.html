Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá – Sección de Fundaciones de Interés Privado (Public Registry of Panama, Private Interest Foundations Section)

Governing statute

Ley No. 25 de 12 de junio de 1995 (Private Interest Foundation Law), as amended by Ley No. 49 de 12 de septiembre de 2012; key provisions: Article 1 (foundation powers), Article 4 (constitution methods), Article 7 (minimum endowment), Article 8 (Charter/Rules), Article 12 (Foundation Council), Article 18 (restriction on commercial activity) and Article 24 (Registry inscription)

Formation / constitutional filings

Fundación de Interés Privado – Escritura Pública Constitutiva y Estatutos (Foundation Charter executed before a Panamanian Notary Public and inscribed in the Foundations Registry)

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 21 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The Foundation must be endowed with a minimum initial capital of USD 10,000.00 (B/.10,000.00) or its equivalent in lawful assets, per Article 7 of Law 25 of 1995. The endowment may be satisfied in cash, movable property, securities or receivables; Panama imposes no mandatory bank escrow for PIFs, but the Notary must certify the actual transfer of the capital into the Foundation's patrimony at execution, and the Public Registry records the declared capital on the inscription certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Benemérito Cuerpo de Bomberos de la República de Panamá: mandatory fire safety inspection and Fire Code compliance certificate (Permiso de Seguridad Contra Incendios) for the registered office and any server room. Municipal permitting authority: structural load inspection and construction permit validation if rooftop antenna, cooling or UPS equipment is installed. Ministerio de Trabajo y Desarrollo Laboral (MITRADEL): occupational health and safety workplace registration and, for payroll of 25 or more employees, mandatory internal safety and hygiene committee. Caja de Seguro Social (CSS): employer registration and payroll affiliation for all Panamanian staff.

Paid-in capital rule: The Foundation must be endowed with a minimum initial capital of USD 10,000.00 (B/.10,000.00) or its equivalent in lawful assets, per Article 7 of Law 25 of 1995. The endowment may be satisfied in cash, movable property, securities or receivables; Panama imposes no mandatory bank escrow for PIFs, but the Notary must certify the actual transfer of the capital into the Foundation's patrimony at execution, and the Public Registry records the declared capital on the inscription certificate.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED: (1) That the initial endowment of USD 10,000.00 contributed by the Founder is accepted and recorded as the Foundation's minimum statutory capital under Law 25 of 1995; (2) that the Foundation's registered office be established at the Registered Agent's offices in Panama City, Republic of Panama; (3) that the persons named in the Foundation Charter are appointed as members of the Foundation Council, with full management powers over the Foundation's assets and authority to enter into software transactions; and (4) that the Council President is authorized to lodge Registry instruments, open bank and escrow accounts, and certify these resolutions.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No specialized industrial licensing authority applies to ISIC 6201 (software publishing). The operational registrations are administered by the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance for tax activation, the Alcaldía del Distrito de Panamá – Dirección de Rentas Municipales for the Aviso de Operación and Patente Municipal, and the Ministerio de Comercio e Industrias (MICI) only for commercial registry matters of the operating company.

Category: No statutory industrial license (requires_statutory_license = false). Mandatory operating registrations for the vertical ERP/CRM cloud business: (i) RUC with DGI; (ii) Aviso de Operación and annual Patente Municipal with the Alcaldía; (iii) DGI electronic invoicing authorization (facturación electrónica); and (iv) personal data processing registration before the Autoridad Nacional de Transparencia y Acceso a la Información (ANTAI) under Ley 81 de 2019 if Panamanian customer data is processed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); electronic portal: e-Tax (https://e-tax.gob.pa). Customs-side authority: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas de Panamá (ANA) via SICE (Sistema Integrado de Comercio Exterior).

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned PIF must obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente). Procedure: (1) execute the Pacto Fundacional with a licensed Panama resident agent and register the foundation at the Public Registry; (2) file the DGI RUC application (assigning the economic activity to ISIC 6201) attaching the certified foundation deed, registered agent acceptance, and municipal Aviso de Operación where commercial activity is intended; (3) obtain a Firma Electrónica Avanzada digital certificate; (4) activate, through e-Tax, the ISR (income tax) and ITBMS registers and appoint a local tax representative where the foundation board is non-resident. Processing period: approximately 5 to 10 business days; RUC issuance typically completes within 1 to 3 business days after document validation.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — Entity-level taxation confined to Panama-source income. Foreign-source dividends, interest, capital gains, and offshore software services of the PIF are outside the Panamanian tax base; Panama-source activity (local ERP/CRM sales to Panamanian customers) triggers ISR at 25% on net taxable profit and ITBMS at 7%. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — Entity-level taxation confined to Panama-source income. Foreign-source dividends, interest, capital gains, and offshore software services of the PIF are outside the Panamanian tax base; Panama-source activity (local ERP/CRM sales to Panamanian customers) triggers ISR at 25% on net taxable profit and ITBMS at 7%. A foundation confined to estate-planning assets with no Panama-source activity holds a de facto non-taxable status rather than a statutory tax-exempt certificate.

Available election forms

Panama does not administer a U.S.-style check-the-box election, nor an elective hybrid-mismatch regime. Available statutory elections: (i) election, within the foundation instrument, to operate as a purely private estate-planning vehicle vs. a commercial entity (commercial status requires the municipal Aviso de Operación and full DGI registration); (ii) election to be assessed under the simplified ITBMS quota regime where annual gross taxable receipts are B/. 200,000 or less; (iii) election to accede to the DGI large-taxpayer compliance group (Grandes Contribuyentes); and (iv) election of the taxable year and tax-consolidation parameters only as prescribed by the Código Fiscal. No free classification choice exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 0 — no de minimis turnover exemption; any person carrying out taxable transfers of goods or rendering taxable services in Panama must register for ITBMS before the DGI and file monthly returns. A reduced simplified regime (fixed quota) is accessible where annual gross receipts do not exceed B/. 200,000 (approximately USD 200,000); non-resident digital service providers must also register via e-Tax and appoint a responsible local agent for Panama-source digital services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS zero-rating is available to: (1) exporters of tangible goods whose physical departure from Panama is evidenced in SICE; and (2) providers of services used exclusively abroad, including offshore cloud ERP/CRM subscriptions where the contracting counterparty, place of effective use, and invoicing address are non-Panamanian. Digital services supplied for use in Panama are outside zero-rating and attract 7% ITBMS, including supplies by non-residents under Law 187 of 2019. To assert 0%, the PIF must evidence the foreign economic destination through the service contract, service-scope documentation, and customer non-residence certificates.

  • Panama Customs Export Declaration filed in SICE (Declaración de Exportación)
  • Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill or Air Waybill as transport evidence
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (for goods; services are instead substantiated by the foreign service contract and usage report)
  • Inward Remittance / bank transfer certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Foreign non-residence certificate or equivalent tax-ID proof of the offshore ERP/CRM customer

Mandatory electronic invoicing through the DGI 'Facturación Electrónica de Panamá' system under a phased mandate covering all registered taxpayers. Taxpayers must obtain DGI authorization for each fiscal invoice series, transmit invoice XML transactions to the DGI validation web-service/API, and incorporate a digital certificate signature plus QR/código de seguridad on each document. The model is authorization-and-validation (near-real-time), not continuous cryptographic stamping; smaller taxpayers may use the DGI free e-Tax invoicing module while large taxpayers must use certified fiscal operators.

DGI examines zero-rating and ITBMS credit claims through post-clearance audits that cross-match SICE export declarations, service contracts, bank credits, and monthly ITBMS returns. Missing or contradicted documentation, fictitious exports, or failure to substantiate effective offshore use within the administrative review trigger: (i) immediate reinstatement of 7% ITBMS plus moratory interest; (ii) administrative fines under the Código Fiscal from B/. 1,000 to B/. 100,000 and surcharges of up to 100% of the evaded tax for concealment; (iii) late-filing and under-declaration penalties assessed via e-Tax; and (iv) criminal tax-fraud proceedings where intent or forged documents are proven. The reassessment limitation period is generally 5 years, extended where fraudulent concealment or non-registration is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized monetary system. The US Dollar is legal tender and the Balboa exists solely as a nominal unit for copper coins. Capital and current account transactions are fully convertible and unrestricted: there are no exchange controls, no prior approval requirements, and no withholding or restrictions on profit repatriation, capital distributions, loan servicing, or portfolio transfers. Foreign-currency accounts—including USD, EUR, GBP, and CNY—are freely permitted at SBP-supervised banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Panama imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts or any other FX proceeds. Software export revenue and cloud subscription receipts may be retained in any currency, in any jurisdiction, and repatriated to a Panamanian bank account at any time without central-bank authorization.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as the banking and financial supervision authority; Banco Nacional de Panamá as the State's fiscal agent. Panama maintains no issuing central bank or exchange-control regulator; monetary operations are conducted under the dollarized legal-tender framework in which the US Dollar circulates as lawful currency alongside the Balboa monetary unit pegged 1:1.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium with material constraints. Panama's SBP-supervised fintech regime (including the financial innovation hub and sandbox licensing for clearing services) does not permit a purely offshore EMI to access SIPE local clearing or ACH rails without holding a banking, payment, or crypto-related license under SBP rules. Offshore EMIs such as Wise or Payoneer may be used as pass-through collection wallets for non-Panamanian SaaS revenue, but inbound credits from these EMIs into Tier-1 Panamanian accounts trigger enhanced AML review, third-party-funding flags, and frequent compliance holds. Viable strategy: use a licensed Panamanian payment service provider or a merchant-of-record arrangement (Stripe Billing) with direct settlement to a Banco General or Banistmo operational account, avoiding EMI routing as the primary treasury rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound founder capital or shareholder advances are delivered by SWIFT MT103 using the beneficiary account at the Panamanian bank. Panama requires no central-bank approval and imposes no admission tax or waiting period for capital contributions. The remittance narrative should state 'Capital contribution to Private Interest Foundation — Ley 25 de 1995' and the SBP statistical transaction type for inward direct investment (Code 61/62) or, for working-capital advances, Code 58 'Business services — ISIC 6201'. The bank will require the Foundation Charter, Registry Certificate, Beneficial-owner declaration, source-of-funds evidence, and validation that the remitter is a named founder or a shareholder of the foundation. Crediting occurs same-day through SIPE US-dollar clearing.Outbound trade and treasury settlements are executed via SWIFT MT103 and MT202 COV for infrastructure procurement, licensor fees, subcontracting and software acquisition; Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (UCP 600) are available through Panamanian banks and the Panama Chamber of Commerce for larger capital goods. For recurring inbound collections, the recommended rails are Stripe Billing with a Panamanian merchant of record or direct-debit initiation through a licensed payment processor into the foundation's bank account. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the annual return is due by 31 March of the following year; for any other fiscal year-end, the return is due within three months after the close of the taxable year. The DGI generally does not grant blanket extensions for corporate income tax returns.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: US$3,000,000 (B/.3,000,000) gross annual revenue for mandatory Local File and Master File documentation; country-by-country reporting threshold is consolidated group revenue equal to or greater than US$850,000,000. Panamanian taxpayers executing cross-border related-party transactions above the threshold must file the annual transfer pricing informative return and all OECD-compliant documentation.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 254 of 2019 and its implementing amendments) apply to tax-resident Panamanian legal entities, including Private Interest Foundations, that carry on a relevant activity (IP exploitation, holding assets, distribution and services, financing and leasing, or headquarters activity). The doctrine requires real physical presence in Panama, qualified local employees, sufficient premises, local management meetings, and substantiated local expenditure proportionate to the income. A PIF whose only activity is passive holding of foreign IP without a Panamanian commercial substance is treated as a shell and exposed to annual ESR reporting, economic substance review, deregistration, and administrative fines. A PIF that generates no Panamanian-source income and is not tax-resident is generally outside the ESR scope, but the DGI may reclassify it if management decisions are effectively taken in Panama.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Income Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud penalty: imprisonment for 5 to 8 years, restitution of evaded tax, and a fine equal to three times the tax evaded; civil tax liability includes the unpaid principal plus interest and 10% late-filing penalties. The court may also impose temporary closure of the taxpayer's Panamanian operations.

Director personal liability: Directors, foundation council members, and registered agents who sign or cause false returns to be filed are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and fines if the corporate veil is pierced for fraud. Criminal judges may impose asset embargos, travel bans, and prevent foreign transfer of PIF assets pending trial.
Shell Company / Artificial Economic Substance Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Under the ESR and anti-shell rules, the DGI can require annual ESR filings, impose non-compliance fines of up to USD 100,000 per filing violation, strike the entity from the public registry, and reclassify past foreign-source income as Panamanian taxable income on the basis of effective management in Panama. Repeat offenders face license revocation and professional disqualification of the resident agent.

Director personal liability: Any director or administrator who knowingly uses the PIF as a sham to conceal beneficial ownership is jointly liable for the reassessed tax and ESR fines. Courts may issue travel bans and passport restrictions against directors, and the Public Registry may be judicially directed to cancel the foundation registration.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Money laundering predicate offense under Panama's Penal Code and Law 23/2015 can be punished by imprisonment from 8 to 22 years for aggravated cases, plus confiscation of assets and fines of up to USD 1,000,000 for institutional failures; the registered agent can also lose its professional license.

Director personal liability: Directors who conceal beneficial owners or who execute suspicious cross-border transfers are exposed to criminal indictment, personal financial liability through asset recovery orders, travel bans, preventive detention, and disqualification from serving on Panamanian foundations or companies.
Transfer Pricing / Cross-Border Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The DGI may adjust taxable income to arm's length, disallow base-eroding payments, impose a 30% surcharge on the additional tax determined, plus monthly interest, and levy administrative penalties for failure to file transfer pricing documentation. Significant and deliberate manipulation may be charged as criminal tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing certificates or who approve fictitious intercompany royalty structures are jointly liable for the adjustment and fines. In serious cases, bankruptcy of the Panamanian entity triggers civil liability for breach of fiduciary duty and criminal sanction for fraudulent insolvency.
Unlawful Profit Distribution and Director Mismanagement

Statutory sanction: Unpaid dividend tax attracts a penalty equal to 10% of the omitted tax plus monthly interest, and the DGI may uncannily reclassify distributions as taxable dividends and assess the 10% tax on the gross amount. Violation of foundation rules may also expose directors to civil liability for unauthorized distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorize non-compliant distributions are personally liable to the PIF and its creditors for the amount unlawfully distributed. The court may pierce the foundation's veil, impose an asset embargo, prohibit leaving Panama, and convert the civil case into a criminal indictment for breach of trust or fraudulent administration.
