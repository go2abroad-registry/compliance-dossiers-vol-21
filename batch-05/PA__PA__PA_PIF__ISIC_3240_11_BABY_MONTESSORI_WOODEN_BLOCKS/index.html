Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; electronic portal: Sede Electrónica del MEF (https://etax.mef.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation deriving Panama-source taxable income must appoint a resident agent and legal representative, submit the foundation deed (Escritura Pública) registered in the Public Registry, resident agent acceptance, and identification documents to DGI to obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes). The application is filed through the DGI e-tax portal; DGI issues a provisional RUC and, after verification, a final registration certificate with ITBMS status. Non-resident entities without Panama-source income are not legally required to register, but voluntary registration is accepted for invoicing and supplier compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Panamanian law. A Private Interest Foundation is an opaque juridical person taxable on Panama-source income only; pass-through treatment is not available. Taxpayers may elect territorial-source allocation methods on income tax returns and may apply foreign tax credits under treaty or domestic provisions. No hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general ITBMS registration threshold applies; any person habitually selling taxable goods or services in Panama or exporting goods must register for RUC/ITBMS before commencing operations. An optional simplified regime (pequeño contribuyente) is available for taxpayers with annual gross income up to PAB 36,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods qualify for the 0% ITBMS rate when the goods are physically removed from Panama under an approved customs export declaration, the seller has title and risk transfer to the foreign buyer, and the transaction is evidenced by commercial documents and payment from abroad. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated when effectively rendered to and consumed outside Panama. The taxpayer must be registered as an ITBMS exporter and must separately record zero-rated transactions to support input ITBMS credits or refunds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduanas, DUA)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer/FX evidence
  • Export contract or purchase order

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) through DGI-approved software and the Sede Electrónica. Qualified taxpayers must issue invoices in real time using a digital signature certificate and obtain an electronic acceptance code (código de aceptación) from DGI before delivery of the invoice. API integration is required for ERP billing systems; each accepted invoice is stamped with the DGI validation code and QR.

DGI may claw back any improperly claimed export zero-rating or refund, assess interest at the statutory legal rate, impose surcharges, and levy fines of up to 100% of the evaded ITBMS. False or fraudulent export declarations can trigger criminal tax evasion penalties with imprisonment up to five years. The statute of limitations for tax audits is five years, and full documentation must be retained and produced upon DGI request.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates under full dollarization with unrestricted currency convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. The Panamanian balboa is a non-issued accounting unit pegged at 1:1 to the USD and exists only as fractional coinage. There are no exchange control laws, no prior approval requirements for inward or outward remittances, and no surrender obligations. Profit remittance hurdles are purely documentary and anti-money-laundering based: the PIF must maintain a valid taxpayer registry number (RUC), submit to beneficial ownership transparency requirements under Panama's Law 52 of 2016, and demonstrate that distributions to founders or beneficiaries comply with the foundation deed and territorial tax rules. A Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) deriving exclusively foreign-source income from cross-border sales of Montana Montessori wooden blocks will face no Panamanian income tax on remittances, although a 10% dividend tax may apply to Panamanian-source corporate dividends if the foundation elects to hold a local operational company.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists for export proceeds. Panama imposes no obligation to surrender, repatriate, or convert foreign exchange earnings. The PIF may retain export receipts in USD balances worldwide, may hold foreign currency accounts offshore, and may freely use proceeds for working capital, supplier payments in EUR or CNY, or reinvestment. Only general AML/CFT reporting thresholds apply, particularly cash transactions above US$10,000 and suspicious transaction reporting under the SBP regulations, and any foreign bank account must be declared in the foundation's annual compliance records if applicable.

Central bank supervision: Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá, SBP), together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) and the Banco Nacional de Panamá, which acts as the government's fiscal agent. Panama has no traditional central bank and no independent monetary policy; the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender in circulation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-to-High on a documented, constraint-adjusted basis. Offshore digital EMIs, such as Wise, Airwallex, or CurrencyCloud, can be viable supplemental treasury rails for collecting Amazon Pay and Stripe settlement proceeds and for converting EUR/CNY into USD, but they cannot act as the primary domestic bank of the PIF. Legal constraints: non-Panamanian EMIs are not licensed under the Panamanian Banking Law to operate deposit-taking institutions or to offer local clearing in Panama; they are acceptable only as payment-service counterparties holding segregated client funds under a foreign e-money license (e.g., EEA EMI, FCA authorization, or MAS license). The PIF must maintain a tier-1 Panamanian bank as the master operating account, and the EMI must be linked via the foundation's governance resolution as an auxiliary treasury tool. A certified copy of the EMI's license, evidence of segregated account treatment, and a formal legal opinion that the EMI arrangement does not constitute unlawful banking activity in Panama materially increase viability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be executed exclusively through SWIFT MT103/MT102 wire transfers from the founder's or a donor's bank into the PIF's USD-denominated account in Panama. For BPM6 classification, remittance instructions should cite purpose code 5215 (direct investment equity) or 5220 (other direct investment capital) where applicable; if funds are non-equity donations to the foundation, use code 5225 or the bank's internal transfer purpose 'contribution to private interest foundation.' The receiving bank will require a copy of the Foundation Deed, a certificate of incumbency for authorized signatories, and a board resolution specifying the capital contribution. Amounts above US$50,000 trigger a standard source-of-funds questionnaire; amounts above US$1,000,000 require a formal source-of-wealth narrative, notarized by a Panamanian notary, together with supporting bank or brokerage statements. The PIF should provide an apostilled copy of the foundation's Privilege Certificate in Spanish and English and ensure the wire's field 70 (Remittance Information) includes the PIF's full name and RUC to avoid delayed clearing.Outbound trade disbursements for beechwood, non-toxic paints, packaging, freight, and third-party logistics must be cleared through one of three compliant rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 corporate telegraphic transfers for direct supplier and service-provider payments, with beneficiary name and invoice reference quoted in field 70; (2) commercial letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700 for large or recurring import orders, incorporating presentation of bills of lading, commercial invoices, packing lists, and certificates of origin; and (3) platform-native settlement rails for Amazon Pay and Stripe refunds, platform fees, or payout sweeps. All USD-denominated payments remain within the PIF's Panama clearing network; for EUR or CNY disbursements, the PIF may either maintain a multi-currency account with a tier-1 correspondent bank or instruct the bank to perform a spot conversion during the SWIFT execution. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 2,500,000 (Panamanian Balboas, equivalent to USD) of gross revenue in the preceding fiscal year combined with related-party transactions; mandatory annual Transfer Pricing Return Formulario 325 and contemporaneous Local File. Master File is required for multinational groups whose consolidated annual revenue exceeds B/. 150,000,000. Transactions below the threshold must still apply the arm's length principle but are exempt from full documentation; retain comparability benchmarks.

Economic substance scope: Panama has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute equivalent to UAE/BCI regimes, but substantive activities are enforced through: (1) territorial-source income rules requiring business presence for Panamanian-source profits; (2) Law 254 of 2021 requiring sufficient physical presence, employees, and records for resident entities claiming tax advantages; (3) the 'beneficial owner' substance doctrine applied by DGI and the Superintendency of Banks; and (4) OECD peer-review pressure. A PIF holding only toy-brand intellectual property with no operational substance in Panama risks reclassification as a pure shell, and its 'foreign' sales may be attributed to an undisclosed Panama PE.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fraud (defraudación fiscal) punishable with 5-10 years' imprisonment; civil penalty of up to 200% of the evaded tax; surcharge and interest accrual; seizure of Panamanian bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members and the Resident Agent are jointly and severally liable for false certifications; personal assets may be attached to cover the corporate tax deficit; directors face national exit bans and 5-year imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of all income as Panama-source with tax assessed at 25%; disqualification of the PIF's tax exemption; fine of B/. 10,000 to B/. 50,000; cancellation of the legal entity registration in the Public Registry upon judicial order.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing under Law 25 of 1995 and the General Corporation Law: directors/incorporators personally liable for unpaid taxes plus civil restitution; 6-year travel ban (prohibición de salida) pending fiscal liquidation.
AML / Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015: imprisonment of 5-12 years; fine up to B/. 5,000,000; freezing of the foundation's assets; mandatory dissolution of the PIF and revocation of its Resident Agent license.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Resident Agent are criminally liable for willful AML omissions; individual penalties include up to 12 years' imprisonment, travel bans, and disqualification from serving as directors of any Panama entity for 10 years; personal civil liability for laundered amounts.
False Documentation / Compliance Fraud

Statutory sanction: Formal fine of B/. 5,000 to B/. 50,000; nullification of the tax return and full re-audit with burden of proof reversed to the taxpayer; publication of the sanction in official registry; referral to the Public Ministry for forgery (falsedad documental).

Director personal liability: Criminal prosecution of the signing director for document forgery: 4-8 years' imprisonment; civil restitution to the State equal to 50% of the evaded amount; travel ban and asset embargo.
