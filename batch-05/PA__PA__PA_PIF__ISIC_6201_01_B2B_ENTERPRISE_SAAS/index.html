Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direccion General de Ingresos (DGI) - General Directorate of Revenue, Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF), Panama; online tax portal: SITIO (Sistema Integrado de Tributacion en Linea) at https://sitio.dgi.gob.pa and national digital services portal https://www.panamadigital.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF) organized under Law 25 of 1995: (1) register the foundation charter with the Public Registry (Registro Publico) and obtain the certificate of incorporation; (2) maintain the mandatory resident agent (agente residente); (3) if conducting commercial activities generating Panamanian-source income, obtain an RUC (Registro Unico de Contribuyente) from DGI by filing Form DGI-UR-01 via SITIO with notarized and apostilled constitutive documents, proof of resident agent, and officer identification; (4) if the PIF exclusively owns foreign assets and derives foreign-source SaaS income, no RUC/ITBMS registration is mandatory, but voluntary registration is recommended to claim ITBMS export zero-rating and refunds; (5) issue the Aviso de Operacion for any physical nexus; (6) obtain a DGI-accredited digital signature certificate (Firma Electronica) to authenticate electronic filings and e-invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama applies territorial (source-based) taxation: non-resident PIFs and resident PIFs are taxable only on Panamanian-source income (Codigo Fiscal Art. 694). Under Law 25 of 1995, a PIF may elect a purely private non-commercial status with no income-tax filing duty, or elect to engage in commercial activities, becoming entity-level taxed only on Panamanian-source profits. No US-style check-the-box election exists; however, Law 254 of 2021 (fiscal modernization) incorporates BEPS hybrid-mismatch rules that may recharacterize a PIF treated as transparent in another jurisdiction. For ITBMS, a PIF with annual sales under USD 36,000 may elect the simplified small-taxpayer regime (regimen simplificado de pequenos contribuyentes).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Standard regime: no monetary threshold - registration is mandatory for any person habitually supplying taxable goods or services in Panamanian territory. Simplified small-taxpayer regime (Pevmes): optional for annual taxable turnover up to USD 36,000; mandatory registration applies upon crossing any taxable transaction that generates Panamanian-source revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS 0% applies to: (i) exports of goods evidenced by a customs export declaration; and (ii) services supplied from Panama to non-resident clients whose benefit is wholly realized abroad and whose consideration is paid from outside Panama (Codigo Fiscal Art. 1059-B; DGI interpretative resolutions). B2B Enterprise SaaS qualifies when the counterparty is a foreign enterprise, the software is delivered via cloud and used exclusively outside Panama, no Panamanian-domiciled user or benefit arises, and payment is remitted from foreign bank accounts or payment rails (e.g., Stripe, Wise, SWIFT). Services benefiting a Panamanian customer or used within Panama remain taxable at 7%. Taxpayers must hold exporter service status registered in the DGI export module to issue zero-rated invoices; monthly ITBMS returns must disclose zero-rated export transactions to generate refundable credit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA - Declaracion Unica Aduanera) for physical goods; not applicable to pure cloud SaaS
  • Bill of Lading (BL) or Airway Bill (AWB) for physical exports evidencing movement of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/bank statement proving receipt of payment from the non-resident customer
  • Signed B2B Enterprise SaaS subscription agreement identifying the Panamanian PIF as supplier and the foreign customer
  • Offshore service-delivery evidence: cloud datacenter location report, server/IP geolocation logs, or hosting certificate proving use outside Panama
  • Zero-rated electronic export invoice issued through SITIO showing the 0% ITBMS line item and DGI acceptance code

Mandatory e-invoicing (facturacion electronica) for all ITBMS-registered taxpayers: invoices must be issued through the DGI SITIO e-invoicing platform or an authorized private billing provider. Each invoice is validated by DGI with a unique digital acceptance code (Codigo de Aceptacion) and QR verification; real-time clearance uses REST/SOAP API integration and an X.509 digital signature from a DGI-accredited certification authority. Taxpayers must transmit invoice data to DGI in nearly real time (daily), submit monthly XML aggregates, and retain electronic records for 7 years.

DGI audits export zero-rating claims within the 5-year assessment period (6 years for unregistered entities). If foreign-use or foreign-payment documentation is deficient, the 0% rate is retroactively revoked: 7% ITBMS is assessed on the full invoice amount, monthly interest of 1% applies under the Codigo Fiscal, and fines range from 50% of the omitted tax (serious infraction) to 200% (fraud), with refund clawback plus interest. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days, without an approved DGI extension, rebuts the offshore-payment requirement and recharacterizes revenue as Panamanian-source, triggering the same assessment, interest, and penalties. False export documentation exposes officers to criminal tax-fraud prosecution and removal from the DGI exporter registry.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full currency convertibility exists for both current-account and capital-account transactions. The US Dollar is legal tender, the Balboa exists only as a fractional coin at 1:1 parity, and no exchange-control authorization, convertibility restriction, foreign-exchange rationing, or profit-remittance approval is required. B2B SaaS subscription invoices and foundation capital contributions can be freely transferred in and out; practical friction arises only from bank-level FATCA, CRS, and enhanced-due-diligence screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export or SaaS service proceeds. Receipts may remain indefinitely in any Panamanian or offshore account, be reinvested, or be disbursed abroad without central-bank or SBP consent because the domestic legal tender is the US Dollar.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no issuing central bank. The apex banking regulator is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); the fiscal and tax administration authority is the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) through the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI); the AML/CFT financial intelligence unit is the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF). The US Dollar circulates as exclusive legal tender under Panama's dollarized monetary system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium with legal constraints. Panama has no dedicated e-money institution (EMI) licensing statute, and SBP-regulated deposit-taking or payment processing may not be conducted by unlicensed offshore fintechs. Wise and Stripe Billing/Treasury are legally viable only as payment-facilitation and collection rails operating through a licensed sponsor bank or correspondent clearing relationship; a Private Interest Foundation should maintain its primary treasury and escrow accounts at a Tier-1 Panamanian bank and use offshore EMIs as ancillary settlement conduits.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% Panamanian corporate income tax (ISR) on Panamanian-source taxable income; the first USD 50,000 of net taxable income is taxed at 15% and the excess at 25%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends or profit distributions made by a Panamanian entity out of Panama-source net profits. Dividends sourced from exempt foreign-source income are not subject to this tax; distributions made by a Private Interest Foundation to beneficiaries under the foundation's final purpose are legally characterized as asset distributions and are not dividends subject to the 10% WHT.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual ISR return must be filed and paid within the first three (3) calendar months after the close of the taxable year; for calendar-year taxpayers the deadline is 31 March. No automatic extension is available; a discretionary deferment must be pre-approved by the Direccion General de Ingresos (DGI).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Payable and filed monthly no later than the last day of the month following the corresponding month's accrual; a final settlement is reconciled in Form 600.

Non-resident PIFs must declare Panama-source revenues only when the SaaS service is performed or utilized in Panama, and must exclude amounts attributable to a foreign permanent establishment or to contracts fully performed and consumed abroad. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD thresholds: a Local File (detailed documentation report) is mandatory when the aggregate value of related-party transactions executed by the Panamanian entity equals or exceeds USD 3,000,000 per fiscal year; a Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated gross revenue equals or exceeds USD 50,000,000; a Country-by-Country Report applies where consolidated group revenue exceeds USD 750,000,000. A Transfer Pricing Informative Declaration must be filed with Form 600 when related-party intercompany transactions exceed USD 1,000,000 in the fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: The Panamanian Economic Substance Regulations (Law 70 of 2019, Executive Decree 179 of 2019) apply to a PIF that is tax-resident in Panama and derives relevant income from specified activities. A PIF engaged in B2B SaaS that licenses internally developed software IP may be classified under the Intellectual Property activity and must demonstrate: (a) board meetings held physically in Panama with substantive management decisions; (b) qualified employees and adequate premises located in Panama; and (c) core income-generating expenditure in Panama. Failure to satisfy the tests results in financial penalties, dissolution of the foundation, and loss of PIF tax neutrality; a pure equity holding PIF is exempt but must file an ESR notification and simplified annual declaration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud (defraudacion fiscal) under Articles 761-762 of the Fiscal Code: imprisonment of one (1) to five (5) years, a fine equal to 100%-200% of the evaded tax, full repayment of tax, monthly 1% delinquency interest and the 10% late-filing surcharge; retroactive revocation of all export-services tax benefits.

Director personal liability: Officers, directors, the PIF's designated secretary, and the registered agent may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax and penalties; court orders may include asset freezing, travel bans, dissolution of the foundation, and a judicial declaration prohibiting the responsible natural persons from serving as Panamanian corporate officers for up to ten (10) years.
Shell Company / Sham Foundation

Statutory sanction: Under Law 51 of 2012 and Law 70 of 2019, a determination of artificiality leads to compulsory dissolution of the PIF by the Public Registry, annual franchise tax penalties, fines of up to USD 10,000 for failure to maintain foundation books, and ESR non-compliance penalties up to USD 1,000,000 with cancellation of the tax-residence certificate.

Director personal liability: Panamanian courts may pierce the PIF veil on proof of fraudulent conveyance or liability-shielding; founders, beneficial owners, and directors are subject to civil restitution of unpaid taxes and third-party debts, travel bans and attachment orders over personal assets, and criminal imprisonment of two (2) to four (4) years for asset concealment.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: The Panamanian Financial Intelligence Unit (UAF) and the banking supervisor can impose fines of USD 1,500 to USD 25,000 per missing record, suspend the registered agent, freeze PIF bank accounts, and confiscate assets; aggravated money-laundering cases trigger criminal fines up to USD 1,000,000 and forfeiture under Article 254 of the Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors and the PIF compliance officer are personally accountable; travel bans and imprisonment of two (2) to five (5) years may be imposed for active money laundering under Law 23/2015; voluntary remediation before detection reduces exposure to civil fines but does not eliminate the personal criminal liability of natural-person signatories.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: A TP corrective adjustment of the taxable base is imposed, plus a penalty of 10% of the adjusted amount (minimum USD 500) when documentation is absent; omission of the informative declaration triggers fines of USD 1,000 to USD 10,000 and monthly 1% interest; the foreign-source exemption claim is disallowed if the transfer price is not at arm's length.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the return are jointly liable for the full TP adjustment, penalties, and interest if they knew of artificial pricing; administrative disqualification and notification to the Public Registry may follow; grossly negligent TP reporting is recharacterized as willful tax evasion and prosecuted against the natural-person directors.
