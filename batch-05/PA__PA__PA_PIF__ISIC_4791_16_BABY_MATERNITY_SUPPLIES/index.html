Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); online portal: SIME (sistema.gob.pa) / dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) by submitting the Private Interest Foundation charter, evidence of legal existence, notarized and apostilled documents, and proof of appointment of a local resident agent/fiscal representative. The application is lodged through the DGI's SIME (Integrated Revenue Management System) portal and results in a permanent RUC for tax declaration purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — Foreign entities must obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) by submitting the Private Interest Foundation charter, evidence of legal existence, notarized and apostilled documents, and proof of appointment of a local resident agent/fiscal representative. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Not applicable (no check-the-box election). Panama's territorial system taxes only Panamanian-source income. A PIF may elect simplified small-taxpayer treatment for local-source commercial activities if annual gross receipts do not exceed USD 36,000; otherwise it is automatically under the general ITBMS regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 36,000 in annual taxable gross receipts for the simplified small-taxpayer election; mandatory RUC/ITBMS registration is required for any person or legal entity engaged in taxable activities with Panamanian nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods and qualifying exported services are taxable at 0% ITBMS where the goods are transferred and shipped abroad to a non-resident purchaser within the statutory timeframe, and where the seller holds documentary evidence of the foreign sale, physical export, and receipt of payment from a non-resident account.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Panama mandates real-time electronic invoicing through the DGI's SIME platform or DGI-approved technological providers. E-invoices must be validated in real time, carry a unique invoice code (VUC), digital stamp (hash/QR), and fiscal authorization before issuance. Non-compliance results in invoice rejection and potential RUC suspension.

Fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger retrospective clawback of the 7% ITBMS, a penalty of up to 200% of the evaded tax, default surcharges and interest, and criminal prosecution under Panama's Tax Code and antifraud statutes. Customs authorities may also impose fines and suspend the exporter's registration for repeated documentary irregularities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully dollarized economy with the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) existing only as a currency-unit pegged 1:1 to USD and issued solely as coinage. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible with no prior exchange-control approval, no repatriation permits, and no surrender requirements for trade receipts. Profit remittances and capital distributions from an operating PIF are subject only to the territorial income-tax regime (income sourced outside Panama is exempt) and to the relevant bank's own AML/fintech screening protocols, which creates no de facto conversion hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender or repatriation obligation on export proceeds or foreign-currency receivables. A PIF conducting online cross-border sales may retain USD settlement balances offshore or onshore without central-bank declaration, filing requirement, or exchange-control penalty.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); Panama has no conventional central bank and the US Dollar (USD) is full legal tender under Cabinet Decree No. 18 of 1950; Banco Nacional de Panamá operates as the State fiscal agent, while the SBP exclusively licenses and supervises banks and financial entities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-Medium under Panamanian law. Panama does not recognize the EU-style 'Electronic Money Institution' license as a distinct banking category; digital payment providers and virtual-asset service providers must register with the SBP under Law 23 of 2015 (AML/CFT), SBP Agreement 004-2021, and the SBP Fintech Innovation Hub (Framework 001-2022). An offshore digital EMI without a Panamanian SBP registration cannot lawfully hold a Panamanian-domiciled PIF's funds as a local account, although the PIF may use such EMI as a treasury-byproduct rail provided settlement ultimately passes through an SBP-regulated local bank. For the target payment rails (Stripe and PayPal), the PIF should establish the merchant account in the entity's legal name and configure settlement to a Panama Tier-1 USD account; Stripe/PayPal themselves operate as unregulated-in-Panama payment facilitators but do not require a Panamanian EMV license for the merchant of record, which is the PIF.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard ISR rate) applied to net Panama-source taxable income; alternative minimum tax of 4.67% of gross revenue must be paid when it exceeds the 25% calculation for taxpayers with annual gross income ≥ B/. 3,000,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory WHT on dividends and profit distributions paid out of Panama-source profits; distributions of foreign-source profits by a territorial-scope PIF are exempt from Panamanian WHT; a 5% advance WHT applies to dividend-like advances before formal declaration.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

No later than the last working day of the third month after fiscal year-end (calendar-year legal entities: 31 March); DGI may grant extensions under Tax Code art. 668 on justified application before the original due date.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 101 – Gross income (ingresos brutos gravables) All Panama-source gross receipts from the e-commerce business, including online sales of maternity and infant care supplies where goods are sold, delivered, or used in Panama. Exclude foreign-source turnover (dropshipping orders fulfilled abroad and settled in Stripe/PayPal outside Panama); the territorial regime requires the PIF to enter only revenues with Panamanian source. Late filing: fine of 5% of the tax due (Tax Code art. 713); late payment: 10% per month or fraction of the unpaid tax, capped at 50%, plus default interest; repeated failures can trigger suspension of the Aviso de Operación, non-issuance of fiscal clearance (paz y salvo), and travel restrictions for responsible directors.
Box 102 – Cost of sales (costo de ventas) Direct procurement, inbound freight, warehousing, and delivery costs attributable to the baby/maternity products sold in Panama. Support every cost with commercial invoices, DUA import declarations, and bank settlement records; related-party costs must be arm's-length documented for TP purposes. Late filing: fine of 5% of the tax due (Tax Code art. 713); late payment: 10% per month or fraction of the unpaid tax, capped at 50%, plus default interest; repeated failures can trigger suspension of the Aviso de Operación, non-issuance of fiscal clearance (paz y salvo), and travel restrictions for responsible directors.
Box 103 – Deductible operating expenses (gastos operativos deducibles) Marketing, platform subscription fees, Panama payroll, rent, and DGI-registered expenses directly connected with Panamanian-source operations. No deduction is allowed for distributions to foreign beneficiaries, private expenditures of the founder/protector, or expenses allocated to foreign-source profits. Late filing: fine of 5% of the tax due (Tax Code art. 713); late payment: 10% per month or fraction of the unpaid tax, capped at 50%, plus default interest; repeated failures can trigger suspension of the Aviso de Operación, non-issuance of fiscal clearance (paz y salvo), and travel restrictions for responsible directors.
Box 104 – Net taxable income (renta neta gravable) Box 101 minus Boxes 102 and 103, after applying net operating loss carryforwards (up to five years). If the PIF derives only foreign-source e-commerce profit, enter zero; retain a territorial-income reconciliation worksheet for DGI inspection. Late filing: fine of 5% of the tax due (Tax Code art. 713); late payment: 10% per month or fraction of the unpaid tax, capped at 50%, plus default interest; repeated failures can trigger suspension of the Aviso de Operación, non-issuance of fiscal clearance (paz y salvo), and travel restrictions for responsible directors.
Box 105 – Calculated ISR (impuesto calculado) 25% of Box 104; when Box 101 exceeds B/. 3,000,000, compare with 4.67% of gross revenue and remit the higher amount. A wholly offshore PIF enters zero and must be prepared to prove the absence of Panama-source sales on audit. Late filing: fine of 5% of the tax due (Tax Code art. 713); late payment: 10% per month or fraction of the unpaid tax, capped at 50%, plus default interest; repeated failures can trigger suspension of the Aviso de Operación, non-issuance of fiscal clearance (paz y salvo), and travel restrictions for responsible directors.
Box 106 – Advance payments and credits (pagos adelantados y retenciones) Quarterly anticipos, withholding taxes, and other credits recoverable against the annual ISR liability. Foreign income taxes paid abroad are not creditable against Panamanian ISR because Panama applies territorial taxation. Late filing: fine of 5% of the tax due (Tax Code art. 713); late payment: 10% per month or fraction of the unpaid tax, capped at 50%, plus default interest; repeated failures can trigger suspension of the Aviso de Operación, non-issuance of fiscal clearance (paz y salvo), and travel restrictions for responsible directors.
Box 107 – Net tax payable / refund (impuesto a pagar o saldo a favor) Box 105 minus Box 106; payment ordered through the DGI's virtual collection module. Funds must be settled from the PIF's Panamanian bank account; unpaid amounts accrue a 10% monthly surcharge capped at 50%. Late filing: fine of 5% of the tax due (Tax Code art. 713); late payment: 10% per month or fraction of the unpaid tax, capped at 50%, plus default interest; repeated failures can trigger suspension of the Aviso de Operación, non-issuance of fiscal clearance (paz y salvo), and travel restrictions for responsible directors.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 5,000,000 (five million Panamanian balboas, at parity with USD) in aggregate annual related-party transactions, above which the taxpayer must maintain and file a Local File and related Transfer Pricing Declaration under Executive Decree 171 of 2014; Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting are owed by groups with consolidated revenue ≥ USD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 254 of 2021, effective 1 January 2022) apply to PIFs and other Panamanian entities conducting 'relevant activities'—including e-commerce distribution—and deriving foreign-source income. The PIF must maintain a registered physical office in Panama, qualified personnel, adequate operating expenditure, and local senior management, and must file an annual economic substance declaration; failures are penalized with progressive administrative fines and can lead to cancellation of the foundation's registration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion: imprisonment of 2–5 years and fines of B/. 5,000–B/. 100,000 (Tax Code arts. 770 et seq.); administrative retaliation: 100% surcharge on the evaded tax plus the 10% monthly late-payment surcharge capped at 50%.

Director personal liability: Directors and foundation-council officers who sign fraudulent returns face joint and several civil restitution, DGI-ordered travel bans (impedimento de salida), seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Shell Foundation / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Law 254 of 2021 ESR: administrative fines escalating from B/. 10,000 to B/. 1,000,000; repeated or serious violations cause deregistration of the PIF; DGI may re-characterize the foreign-source income as taxable Panama-source income.

Director personal liability: Protectors, council members, and directors can be held personally liable for the fines; aggravating elements lead to director disqualification for up to 3 years and travel-blocking orders.
Money Laundering / AML Breach through Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Panama AML/CFT framework (Law 23 of 2015 and Penal Code): imprisonment of 5–12 years, fines of up to B/. 10,000,000, and asset forfeiture; the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF) may freeze the foundation's accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and protectors who participated or exhibited willful blindness are criminally liable as co-principals; they face personal asset freezing, extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing and Documentary Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines from B/. 1,000 to B/. 25,000 (Tax Code arts. 735, 743–744); DGI may re-determine the taxable income at arm's length and assess additional ISR plus the 10% monthly surcharge (cap 50%).

Director personal liability: Directors signing incomplete or fraudulent TP documentation are jointly and severally liable for the re-determined tax; false declarations can escalate to criminal penalties for fraud.
