Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Ley 25 de 1995 imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital; however, the Foundation must dedicate an initial endowment sufficient for its purpose. Registry practice requires declaring a minimum initial patrimony of US$10,000 (cash or in-kind) in the Foundation Charter. No escrow or immediate deposit rule applies; the endowment is accepted by the Foundation Council at the first meeting. Annual state tax (tasa única anual): US$150 for capital up to US$50,000; US$300 for capital from US$50,001 to US$100,000; US$400 for capital above US$100,000.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a US-style check-the-box or general hybrid-mismatch election. Available elections: (a) PIF operates as a distinct 'patrimonio autónomo' separate from founder and beneficiaries; only Panamanian-source income is within the tax net; (b) inclusion in the mandatory informative 'Régimen de Transparencia Fiscal Internacional' (Law 254 of 2021) via Formulario 940 for non-Panamanian-source income, while Panamanian-source business profits remain taxable at the standard 25% corporate income tax rate; (c) election of the ITBMS small-taxpayer simplified regime (pequeño contribuyente) where annual gross turnover does not exceed B/. 36,000; (d) election of ANA/MICI 'Autorizado Exportador' status to apply simplified export zero-rating. Any re-characterization of foreign income as Panamanian-source by DGI would require exceptional anti-abuse analysis under Law 254.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 36,000 per annum (36,000 Panamanian Balboas at 1:1 parity with the US Dollar) of gross income from taxable transfers of movable goods or provision of services; once this threshold is reached or exceeded, the PIF must be inscribed as an ITBMS registered taxpayer in its RUC and issue FEL electronic invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% ITBMS applies to turbine-driven critical-care invasive ICU ventilators (HS 9018.90/9019.20) when: (i) goods physically depart from Panamanian territory under a valid ANA export declaration in the name of the exporting PIF or its contract manufacturer; (ii) the foreign buyer is non-resident and title/risk passes abroad under EXW/FOB/CIF INCOTERMS; (iii) the sale is recorded as zero-rated in the FEL e-invoice linked to the customs number; (iv) the exporter holds an active RUC with ITBMS registration; (v) proceeds are received in the Panamanian banking system (USD/PAB) from abroad within the 180-day export proceeds benchmark and traceable to the same sale; (vi) for sales to free zones or bonded re-export, equivalent zero-rating certificates (zona franca purchaser export proof) are retained; supporting documentation must be kept for the DGI limitation periods of 5–7 tax years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Mercancías / DUA) filed electronically in the ANA SINTAD system, specifying HS 9018.90/9019.20 and the ICU ventilator particulars
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill evidencing physical exit of the goods from Panamanian territory
  • Commercial export invoice issued under FEL (electronic) with the 0% rate and DGI authorization code
  • Purchase Order / International Sales Contract with the foreign buyer stating INCOTERMS, quantity, unit price and destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/MT103 credit advice from the receiving Panamanian bank evidencing foreign-currency/USD payment collection
  • Exporter Registry certificate (MICI – Viceministerio de Comercio Exterior) and ANA Export Log enrollment
  • Certificate of Origin (where a preferential trade instrument is claimed) and, if required by ANA/DGI, the sanitary or medical-device export clearance for the ventilator units

Mandatory DGI electronic invoicing under the 'Facturación Electrónica en Línea (FEL)' framework administered in e-Tax 2.0: all registered ITBMS taxpayers must transmit invoices in the national XML schema to DGI in near-real time for validation and digital stamping (sellado electrónico); each accepted invoice obtains a unique DGI authorization code embedded in a QR code; taxpayer software and APIs must be homologated after testing in the DGI sandbox and operate under a valid Panamanian certified electronic signature (firma electrónica issued by an accredited certification authority under AIG/Registro Público rules); export invoices must cite the ANA customs declaration number and are subject to DGI exit-validation checks.

DGI audit under Código Fiscal Articles 744–749 applies formal strictness to zero-rating evidence: incomplete or inconsistent export files are recharacterized as local taxable sales, generating 7% ITBMS plus moratory interest (interés de mora) and fines ranging from 10% to 200% of omitted tax depending on negligence or intent; refunded input VAT is retroactively clawed back with indexation and the exporter is suspended or removed from the RUC/e-invoicing registry; false declarations expose the PIF and its directors to the criminal tax-evasion offense (introduced by Ley 70 de 2019 into the Código Penal), carrying imprisonment of 2–5 years; ANA customs fraud proceedings under Ley 76 de 2019 impose confiscation of shipments and fines of 100%–300% of customs value; regulatory revocation by MICI/ANA and DGI disqualification follow health-sector medical-device violations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The US dollar is legal tender and no separate national currency exists; there is no exchange control regime, no dual market, and no foreign-exchange approval requirement. Both current account (commercial trade, services, profit remittance) and capital account transactions are freely convertible. Profit remittances are unrestricted from a currency perspective, but fund outflows are subject to SBP AML/CTF reporting and Panama's territorial taxation rules; income sourced abroad is generally not taxed in Panama. Dividends and profit distributions made by Panamanian entities out of Panamanian-source income are subject to the applicable dividend tax at statutory rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; exporters may retain all receipts in USD or any foreign currency and deploy them without central bank approval. Compliance is limited to banking documentation and anti-money-laundering requirements for the underlying export transaction.

Central bank supervision: None — Panama is fully dollarized and has no central bank. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the banking and financial system regulator, and Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent of the State.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail but viable as a secondary collection channel for smaller buyer payments. Offshore digital EMI virtual accounts are not treated as Panamanian bank accounts and cannot clear Government LCs or obtain SBP licensed banking services. Any locally active fintech or electronic payment institution must hold a Panamanian license from the SBP and must ring-fence clients' funds through a local commercial bank guarantor; therefore, the PIF and its operating company should maintain core liquidity and vendor disbursement capacity in a licensed tier-1 local bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route inbound capital via SWIFT MT103 from the parent or foundation board treasury to the Panamanian operational account. Wire details must show the exact legal name of the PIF or operating entity as beneficiary, the US-based correspondent bank of the Panamanian receiving bank, the account number, and a clear payment purpose in field 70 such as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION'. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail but viable as a secondary collection channel for smaller buyer payments. Offshore digital EMI virtual accounts are not treated as Panamanian bank accounts and cannot clear Government LCs or obtain SBP licensed banking services. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 250,000 in annual related-party transactions (the Panamanian Balboa is pegged 1:1 to the USD, so the local statutory threshold is B/.250,000)

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Executive Decree 266 of 2020) apply to entities engaged in 'relevant activities', including IP holding, headquarters, distribution, financing, shipping, and insurance. Manufacturing of ICU ventilators is not a designated relevant activity; however, the PIF's commercial operations must still be evidenced by physical premises, qualified staff, operational expenditure, and board decisions made in Panama. A pure asset-holding PIF with rented filing-office substance is exposed to ESR and AML challenge.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and under-declaration of revenue

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the omitted ISR with a 10% surcharge, monthly interest of 1.5%, administrative penalties under the Fiscal Code, and criminal prosecution for evasión fiscal where the omission is intentional and material

Director personal liability: Directors and resident agents who sign false declarations may have the corporate veil pierced for civil restitution of the unpaid tax; conviction can result in arrest warrants, travel bans, and imprisonment under Panama's criminal tax law
Shell company abuse / Private Interest Foundation misuse

Statutory sanction: Administrative dissolution of the foundation, denial or revocation of the PIF certificate and statutory manufacturing license, monetary fines under AML Law 23 of 2015, and debarment from government LC procurement processes

Director personal liability: The founder, council members, and protector may each be held personally liable for wrongdoings committed through the foundation; travel bans, asset freezes, and criminal charges apply where the foundation is deemed a sham instrument for money laundering
Transfer pricing manipulation and false invoicing

Statutory sanction: DGI arm's length adjustment of Panamanian-source taxable profit, recharacterization of the intercompany transaction, 10% surcharge, 1.5% monthly interest, and fines up to 100% of the tax deficiency where fraudulent documentation is found

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the false pricing or forged invoices are jointly and severally liable for the deficiency; involvement in forged LC documentation attracts criminal money-laundering liability and personal attachment of assets
AML violations on government payment rails

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to USD 1,000,000, suspension of the entity's commercial license, mandatory closure of the bank account, and reporting to the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) as a predicate money-laundering event

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent face personal criminal liability under Law 23 of 2015 for willful blindness; sanctions extend to restraint orders over personal assets, travel restrictions, and sentences that can reach 15 years' imprisonment for aggravated laundering
