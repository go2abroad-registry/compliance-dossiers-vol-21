Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Panama Private Interest Foundation has no statutory minimum paid-up capital. Law 25/1995 requires the founder to assign an initial endowment (patrimonio inicial) sufficient to carry out the foundation's purposes; a nominal US$500.00 is recorded as fully subscribed and contributed in the charter. No escrow account is legally mandated unless the foundation will administer regulated assets or trust funds; during formation the endowment is commonly held in a Panamanian attorney trust account.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection and certification are required through the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá (Panama Fire Department) before physical occupation. The office space must also pass the Municipality's structural load inspection and behavior-of-use review. Labor workplace clearance is obtained by filing the 'Registro de Salud y Seguridad Ocupacional' with MITRADEL (Ministerio de Trabajo y Desarrollo Laboral), confirming adequate emergency exits, lighting, ventilation, and ergonomic workstations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) - General Directorate of Revenues, Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); principal portal: official DGI website and Sede Electrónica e-tax single window. Ancillary authorities: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) for customs export declarations; Registro Público de Panamá for Private Interest Foundation (PIF) charter registration, corporate records and annual registry annuities; Agencia de Gobierno Electrónico (AIG) for issuance of the advanced electronic signature (Firma Electrónica Avanzada) required for e-tax filings. The tax system is unitary at the national level; Panama has no state or municipal income tax displacing DGI jurisdiction.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF, Ley 25 de 1995) is constituted by a foundation charter (Carta Fundacional) executed before a Panamanian notary and filed with the Registro Público, with a resident agent (Agente Residente), minimum initial endowment of B/.10,000 (USD 10,000, USD legal tender in Panama), one-time formation fee and annual registry annuity due by 30 June. DGI registration is triggered only when the foundation performs Panamanian-source taxable activity (Ley 25, Art. 4 restricts local commercial activity). When required, the PIF must: (i) obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) through the DGI Sede Electrónica by submitting the registration application, certified foundation charter, identification of the founder and president, resident agent evidence and a Panamanian fiscal domicile; (ii) obtain the AIG advanced electronic signature before filing; (iii) register for ITBMS within 30 days of commencing taxable activities; and (iv) if exporting services, apply for DGI export-services status to claim zero-rating and input VAT refunds. A PIF receiving purely foreign-source helpdesk or SaaS subscription fees from non-domiciled clients, with the services performed and economically consumed abroad, is not required to obtain a RUC or file Panamanian ISR because Panama taxes only Panamanian-source income (Código Fiscal Art. 694).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: the PIF is an entity-level statutory vehicle that is neither transparent nor subject to entity-level income tax on foreign-source income; Panamanian-source income alone (services physically performed or economically exploited in Panama) falls within the 25% corporate ISR framework if the PIF conducts local commercial activities. Non-resident founders and beneficiaries are not taxable on distributions of foreign-source foundation assets, and the PIF preserves the territorial exemption only while avoiding commercial activity inside Panama.

Available election forms

Panama has no check-the-box or US-style entity classification election; classification follows statutory form. Available elections include: (i) an election in the foundation charter to pursue Panamanian commercial activity and thereby become an ISR/ITBMS taxpayer or, alternatively, to remain a purely patrimonial offshore vehicle; (ii) voluntary ITBMS registration and election for DGI export-services status, permitting zero-rating of eligible helpdesk exports and reclaim of input ITBMS credits; (iii) the simplified small-taxpayer regime under Ley 66 de 2012 for low-turnover businesses (approximately B/.36,000 annual gross receipts), which simplifies ITBMS compliance but is not advantageous for exporters claiming credits; (iv) elective treatment of service fees as foreign-source under the DGI export-services framework where the contractual beneficiary and economic use are outside Panama. No hybrid-mismatch or comprehensive double-tax-treaty elections are available for this structure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy: the United States Dollar (USD) is the only circulating paper currency and the Balboa (PAB) exists solely as 1:1 subsidiary coinage. There are no exchange controls, no separate central-bank FX market, and no convertibility restrictions on either current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittances, dividend payments, interest, royalties, and PIF endowment distributions to founders or beneficiaries are unrestricted and are not subject to any exit, conversion, or withholding hurdle when the income is not of Panamanian source under the territorial tax regime. The only practical friction is bank-level AML/CFT documentation for large or high-velocity remittances, not governmental FX authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Because USD is national legal tender, Panama imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. SaaS, helpdesk, and customer-support subscription proceeds may be retained offshore or held in any domestic USD account without central-bank repatriation, surrender, or conversion obligations. The only applicable discipline is standard SBP-regulated AML/CFT transaction monitoring by the collecting bank.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Superintendency of Banks of Panama, SBP). Panama has no issuing central bank; the SBP is the competent regulator for banking, foreign-exchange, and payment-system matters, with Banco Nacional de Panamá acting as fiscal agent and the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas coordinating monetary policy under the full US-dollar legal-tender regime (Decreto Ley 9 de 1998 and Ley 21 de 2017).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium. Panama's Law 163 of 2021 and SBP implementing rules permit locally licensed payment service companies (empresas de servicios de pago) to issue electronic money and access domestic clearing, but an offshore digital EMI (EEA/UK-licensed) is legally feasible only as a merchant collection node for Stripe Billing and PayPal Business flows; it cannot access Panama's local ACH or RTGS. The PIF should hold primary treasury liquidity in a Tier-1 Panamanian bank and sweep EMI settlement balances daily to reduce concentration risk. Material constraints: offshore EMIs are unlicensed in Panama, balances held with them are outside the Fondo de Garantía de Depósitos, and such balances must be classified as operational float rather than treasury assets.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capitalization must be initiated by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for large value) from the founder's or investor's own bank account; third-party wires are rejected under standard PIF banking policy. The beneficiary name must exactly match the registered PIF name, and the remittance reference should state 'Apport capital - incremento de patrimonio fundacional' (capital contribution to foundation endowment). No exchange-control permit or central-bank approval is required; the receiving bank performs OFAC/AML screening and reconciles the remitter against the foundation charter and the SBP beneficial-ownership file before crediting. Documentary bundle: apostilled charter, registered-agent certificate, council resolution designating the account, source-of-funds declaration, and up-to-date KYC for each signatory.Outbound disbursements for the helpdesk SaaS (cloud hosting, data-center capacity, subcontracted support, software licenses) settle via: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct credits for international counterparties; (ii) irrevocable documentary letters of credit under UCP 600 for procurement commitments above USD 50,000 or where vendors demand security; (iii) domestic USD ACH clearing through Panama's Cámara de Compensación Electrónica for onshore payments; and (iv) Stripe/PayPal payout rails for micro-disbursements. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% flat rate on Panama-source net taxable income (Código Fiscal Art. 699; Ley 8 de 2010); 0% on foreign-source income under Panama's territorial tax system.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Corporate distributions: 5% withholding where profits have borne Panama CIT; 10% where profits were exempt or untaxed (Art. 733 Código Fiscal). Private Interest Foundation distributions are not dividends and are exempt from both the 5%/10% dividend tax and from remittance withholding to non-resident beneficiaries.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Fiscal year ends 31 December unless a different date is lawfully fixed in the Foundation Charter; Form 101 is due within four months of fiscal year-end according to the RUC last-digit schedule (endings 1–5: March; endings 6–0: April–15 June). Non-resident PIFs with no Panama-source activity may file a zero return on the same schedule; there is no extension for non-residents.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Law 70 of 2019 and Executive Decree 73 of 2021: mandatory annual TP Declaration and Local File are triggered where the taxpayer's Panama-source gross income exceeds PAB 3,000,000 in the prior fiscal year, or where controlled transactions with related parties located in low-/no-tax jurisdictions exceed PAB 1,000,000; Master File documentation applies to members of MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of at least PAB 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Law 254 of 2021 and Executive Decree 235 of 2022 (Panama ESR) apply to PIFs carrying on a relevant activity — including technology and helpdesk services — performed in Panama. A PIF must evidence, via an annual Substantial Presence Statement, the presence and proportionality of a local registered office, resident employees, physical assets, and board/management autonomy, and must file the declaration within the statutory calendar. A pure offshore helpdesk arrangement with no employees, office, or management in Panama remains outside ESR only if contracts are demonstrably concluded and executed outside Panama.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion by source mischaracterisation

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the full Panama-source income; 10% monthly surcharge plus late-payment interest; criminal tax evasion under the Código Penal: imprisonment of 2 to 5 years for the responsible legal representative, plus a fine equal to 100%–300% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members and directors who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable; the civil fraud exception to patrimonial separation lifts the PIF veil, exposing the founder's and beneficiaries' personal assets, and the DGI's coercive collection regime may issue travel bans against directors for unpaid assessed tax.
Shell foundation / artificial substance

Statutory sanction: Annulled tax exemptions and recharacterisation of the PIF as a taxable Panama permanent establishment; ESR monetary penalty of PAB 10,000–100,000; strike-off of the foundation from the Registro Público; possible freezing of foundation bank accounts upon adverse FATF/AML findings.

Director personal liability: Panamanian courts disregard legal personality where the foundation is a sham or mask used for abuse of law; directors and the registered agent risk administrative penalties under Law 23 of 2015 and disqualification, with possible personal criminal exposure for aiding misrepresentation.
AML / beneficial ownership concealment

Statutory sanction: Law 23 de 2015 sanctions: fines up to PAB 1,000,000 per unverified operation; deregistration of the registered agent; money-laundering predicate offence under Criminal Code Art. 254 punishable by imprisonment of 5 to 10 years for the beneficial owner and executive officers.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability applies to council members who knowingly conceal the economic beneficiary; the corporate veil is inapplicable in money-laundering cases, and travel bans (impedimento de salida) are routinely imposed during parallel DGI/AML proceedings.
Transfer pricing / thin-cap stripping

Statutory sanction: DGI adjustment to the arm's-length price; denial of interest deductions on the excess portion; recharacterisation as a deemed dividend subject to 5% WHT; penalty of 10% of the adjusted tax plus interest at the legal rate; additional fine of up to PAB 50,000 for non-submission of the TP declaration.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for deliberate under-pricing where they authorised the related-party contracts and signed the tax declaration; the DGI may disregard the foundation separation defence under the fraud exception and execute directly on the directors' personal assets.
