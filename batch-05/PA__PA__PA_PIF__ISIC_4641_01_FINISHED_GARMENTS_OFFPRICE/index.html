Ley No. 25 de 12 de junio de 1995 (Private Interest Foundations Law), as amended by Ley No. 12 de 2016 and Ley No. 15 de 17 de mayo de 2024; Articles 4 (charter contents), 8 (registration), 19 (minimum endowment), 21-23 (Foundation Council) and 24 (Resident Agent).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) of the Republic of Panama; online portal: Sede Electrónica (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa). Complementary customs authority for export controls: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) through the VUPEA single window (https://www.ana.gob.pa).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Constitute the Private Interest Foundation (PIF) under Law 25 of 1995 (as amended) through a licensed Panamanian resident agent and record the foundation charter in the Registro Público de Panamá. Step 2 — Obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente / TIN) at the DGI through Sede Electrónica or in person: submit the foundation deed, Public Registry inscription certificate, identification of founder and members of the Foundation Council, and notarized appointment of a fiscal representative (representante legal) with a local address; the RUC is pre-assigned and the definitive taxpayer certificate is issued electronically. Step 3 — Activate the ITBMS (VAT) registration in the RUC and enroll in the SFE electronic-invoicing platform. Step 4 — Settle the flat annual foundation tax of PAB/USD 300 (due by 30 June of each year) through DGI treasury collection channels; the DGI payment receipt (paz y salvo) is a prerequisite for subsequent administrative acts. Step 5 — A municipal Aviso de Operación or patent fee is triggered only if the PIF maintains a physical commercial establishment in Panama; no sector-specific license is required for off-price apparel wholesale liquidation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — Panama exempts foreign-source income; a PIF is a separate legal person which, for domestic tax purposes, is subject to entity-level Corporate Income Tax (ISR) at the standard 25% rate solely on net Panama-source income, plus a 10% dividend or branch remittance tax where profits are distributed to non-resident beneficiaries. All income derived from sources outside Panama, including offshore off-price trading margins on finished garments, foreign dividends, bank interest, and capital gains realized abroad, falls outside the Panamanian tax jurisdiction provided the purchase order, delivery, risk of loss, and payment occur outside Panama and the merchandise never enters Panamanian customs territory. The PIF also bears the flat annual foundation tax of PAB/USD 300 under Law 25 of 1995.

Available election forms

Panama has no domestic check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch-specific election, although Panama ratified the OECD Multilateral Instrument (effective 2020). Available statutory elections and administrative options: (i) election of the small-taxpayer simplified regime ('pequeño contribuyente') where authorized by DGI for low-income taxpayers, replacing standard monthly ITBMS filings with fixed quotas; (ii) election of the fiscal-year accounting period with prior DGI notification (calendar year remains the default); (iii) exporter-status election enabling ITBMS credit refund applications; (iv) optional re-domiciliation of the PIF under Law 25 to change governing law and administrative seat; and (v) for foreign federal tax purposes only (e.g., the United States), the PIF may be eligible for a US check-the-box classification, while under Panamanian domestic law it remains an entity-level taxpayer and no domestic pass-through election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.07%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None under the standard regime — any enterprise (including a PIF) performing taxable transfers of movable goods or services in Panama must register for ITBMS from the first taxable act. A simplified small-taxpayer quota regime applies to economically small taxpayers with gross annual income at or below PAB/USD 60,000, subject to DGI qualification; wholesale off-price garment liquidation entities are normally captured by the standard monthly ITBMS regime at the 7% rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for the 0% ITBMS rate when the goods are physically removed from Panamanian customs territory, the PIF is the exporter of record on the customs declaration, and legal title and risk pass to a non-resident buyer. Services qualify for 0% when rendered to a non-resident recipient, paid from outside Panama, and effectively consumed or economically used outside Panama. For off-price apparel liquidation: pure offshore drop-ship trades with no entry into Panama generate foreign-source income outside the scope of ITBMS altogether; if goods are imported into Panama and subsequently re-exported, the re-export is zero-rated and the ITBMS paid at importation (input credit) may be offset or reclaimed from DGI as an exporter credit refund (solicitud de devolución de saldo a favor), subject to full documentary substantiation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) processed through ANA VUPEA/SISCOA single window and electronically linked to DGI
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued to the foreign importer evidencing physical exit of the finished-garment shipment
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order, or letter of credit mirroring the exact goods, quantities, prices, and zero-rated sale terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice (SWIFT/ACH statement) showing receipt of proceeds from the foreign buyer into a Panamanian or foreign bank account
  • Evidence of the input ITBMS credit underlying the refund claim (import entries, customs duty and ITBMS payment receipts, purchase invoices of the overstock inventory)
  • Certificate of origin or free-sale documentation where the destination jurisdiction requires it for apparel imports

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica de Panamá) applies to all active taxpayers through the DGI SFE platform under a phased incorporation calendar. Sales invoices, credit notes, and debit notes must be generated in the DGI-approved XML/PDF format, transmitted through the SFE free web portal or via automated API/SFTP integration for high-volume issuers, and validated by DGI real-time fiscal clearance; each accepted invoice carries a digitally stamped QR/verification code. Any invoice issued outside the SFE without DGI authorization is invalid for ITBMS input-credit and export-refund purposes.

DGI risk-based audits reconcile customs export declarations, vessel and master data, and banking inflows; exporters failing to prove physical exit and receipt of foreign exchange within the 180-day evidentiary benchmark face retroactive ITBMS at 7% on the gross export value, monthly surcharge of 1.5% on the unpaid tax, and fines up to 100% of the deficiency. Fabricated or dual-use export documentation triggers criminal tax fraud (defraudación tributaria) under Law 70 of 2019 with imprisonment of three to five years, cumulative ANA customs penalties, money-laundering reporting consequences under Panamanian AML rules in cases of false remittances, and clawback of refunds with statutory interest within the applicable DGI assessment limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The regime is one of complete, unrestricted convertibility. There are no exchange-control laws, no capital-account barriers, no prior-approval requirements for foreign investment, and no restrictions on profit or capital repatriation. The Balboa does not float independently and simply mirrors the USD. Consequently, a Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) conducting off-price apparel liquidation can move funds freely in USD to Latin America, Asia, and Europe. The only frictions are bank-commission and compliance costs at transaction level, not regulatory convertibility limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no export-proceeds surrender requirement. A PIF may retain 100% of export sale proceeds in USD in any onshore or offshore account, and may settle supplier invoices directly from an overseas USD or multi-currency account. Holding export proceeds offshore triggers no penalty; the only obligations are accurate accounting under Panama's territorial taxation rules and full bank disclosure sufficient for SWIFT and OFAC name-screening protocols.

Central bank supervision: Panama does not operate a classical central bank. Banking and payment-system supervision is exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). The Banco Nacional de Panamá (state-owned) serves as fiscal agent of the Republic and clearing agent for public-sector funds, while the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) oversees fiscal and financial policy. The United States dollar is de facto legal tender; the Balboa is a nominal coin-only unit at a fixed 1:1 parity with the USD.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Medium for USD settlement through SBP-licensed digital banks; Low for pure offshore non-licensed EMIs. Panama's FinTech Hub regulatory sandbox, administered by SBP under the Panamanian FinTech law framework, permits supervised digital banks and electronic-money institutions to be licensed. An overseas EMI (e.g., an FCA-licensed UK EMI or a US MSB) cannot independently open a domestic clearing account at a Panamanian bank without SBP licensing; it may, however, function as a front-end payment-instruction platform routing into a Panamanian bank-owned IBAN. Legal constraint: operating an unlicensed payment service or holding client funds in USD within Panama territory is prohibited; a PIF may use licensed foreign EMIs only for non-resident treasury operations, not for domestic payroll or clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: (1) The originator shareholder or capital contributor sends USD via SWIFT MT103 to the PIF's account at Banco General, Banistmo, or Multibank, with the correspondent leg settled through the bank's US partner via Fedwire or CHIPS. (2) Recommended wire narrative: 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — CAPITALIZATION RESOLUTION NO. [X]' together with the Foundation's RUC (tax ID) number. The bank requires the capitalization minutes and confirmation that the beneficiary account passes OFAC name-screening; receipt of sanctioned-related funds is prohibited and mandates immediate freeze. (3) For amounts above USD 100,000, banks pre-clear the transaction with the internal anti-money-laundering officer, obtaining the equity-injection resolution, the Foundation Charter, and a source-of-funds declaration. (4) Panama operates no separate FDI declaration system; foreign direct investment is registered for statistical purposes only, and the wire itself is the recognized capital-injection record for territorial-tax exemption purposes.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 open-account wire transfers for routine supplier payments below USD 500,000 per invoice, used for recurring purchases of overstock garments from Southeast Asia, Latin America, and Turkey; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary sight or usance LCs) issued by a tier-1 Panamanian bank for high-value branded-liquidation contracts, with transferable and confirmed LC structures allowing 90–120 day tenors and payment upon presentation of compliant shipping documents; (iii) domestic USD clearing through Panama's ACH/Cámara de Compensación for local commission and logistics payables; and (iv) ACH Direct merchant settlement rails for e-commerce liquidation receivables. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding on dividends paid from Panamanian-source profits. The rate is reduced to 0% on distributions of foreign-source profits (which are exempt at the corporate level) and may be effectively reduced to 5% when the distributing PIF has already paid corporate income tax at 25% on the underlying profits, because the corporate tax is creditable against the dividend withholding. Where the PIF is in the reduced 15% bracket for its first USD 1.5 million of taxable income, the dividend withholding rate on that portion is 10% with no corporate tax credit.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax return must be filed on or before 15 April following the close of the fiscal year (calendar year for most entities); a one-month automatic extension to 15 May applies upon submission of a request through SITIA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 1,000,000 in aggregate related-party transactions during the fiscal year. Annual simultaneous documentation (Local File) must be prepared and submitted to the DGI within 150 days after year-end; a Master File is additionally required when the MNE group exceeds USD 500 million in global consolidated revenue or the entity's related-party transactions exceed USD 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Panama has no general statutory Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) comparable to the BVI/Cayman framework for pure commercial trading vehicles. However, the DGI applies the 'economic substance doctrine' as an anti-avoidance principle: for a PIF engaged in cross-border apparel liquidation, the following must be demonstrable to protect the territorial exemption and treaty benefits: (a) an adequate Panamanian office with independent management, (b) the foundation's bank account and accounting records maintained in Panama, (c) substantive business decisions made in Panama, and (d) real commercial activity with sufficient risk-bearing. Free-trade-zone licensees, reinsurance companies, and financial institutions are expressly subject to published ESR guidelines. Where a PIF has no substance beyond the registered agent, the tax authorities may reclassify foreign-source revenue as taxable Panamanian-source income and invalidate the economic benefit of the territorial exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine of 100% to 500% of the omitted tax, plus default interest at the legal rate; disqualification from government contracts and the loss of the right to carry forward losses. Where the amount exceeds USD 250,000, the entity is permanently barred from operating as a foundation and its Panama commercial registration is cancelled.

Director personal liability: The foundation council and the registered manager become vicariously liable for restitution of the total evaded tax, may face a travel ban issued by the DGI in coordination with migration police, and can be imprisoned for up to 10 years under Panamanian Criminal Code Article 283. The DGI can pierce the corporate veil upon showing that the PIF is a mere instrumentality used to perpetrate fraud.
Shell Company and Artificial Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: The DGI may disregard the PIF as a sham entity, treat its shareholders/founders as the direct taxpayers, and impose the full CIT liability at 25% plus surcharges on their behalf. A certificate of 'no substance' issued by the DGI triggers the loss of the entity's beneficial status, cancellation of the PIF registration under Law 25 of 1995, and disqualification of the resident agent. Administrative fines of USD 5,000 to USD 50,000 may be imposed for each fiscal year of sham operation.

Director personal liability: Directors and the foundation council are jointly and severally liable for the reclassified tax liability. They are barred from serving as directors of any Panama-licensed entity for 5 years and may be subject to passport seizure and a prohibition on business travel to Panama while the assessment is pending.
Money Laundering (AML) and Unlicensed Remittance

Statutory sanction: Violations of Law 23 of 2015 carry fines from USD 10,000 up to USD 1,000,000 per incident, plus the freezing of bank account(s). Criminal charges for organized money laundering may result in imprisonment from 5 to 12 years, forfeiture of the foundation's assets, and dissolution of the entity. The PIF loses its benefit of confidentiality if used for criminal ends.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise a transaction that constitutes money laundering are individually subject to a prison term equal to the primary offender's sentence, a fine of USD 250,000, and asset seizure of personal property; they additionally face a 10-year travel ban from Panama and a prohibition on holding fiduciary licences in the Canal Zone.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment by the DGI to the arm's-length profit, application of a 20% accuracy penalty, plus interest at compound rates. If the adjustment results in tax larger than USD 300,000, the DGI may initiate criminal fraud proceedings. Loss of the territorial exemption for the manipulated transactions and retroactive assessment for the last 5 years.

Director personal liability: The foundation's legal representative who signs the inaccurate tax return is personally liable for the penalty equal to 25% of the entire adjusted tax liability, and if the mispricing exceeds USD 1 million, the representative is subject to imprisonment for up to 4 years and a civil restitution order in favour of the State. Travel restrictions may be applied pending the payment of the adjustment.
