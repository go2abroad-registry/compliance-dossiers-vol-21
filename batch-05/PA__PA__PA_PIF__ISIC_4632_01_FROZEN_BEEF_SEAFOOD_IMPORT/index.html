Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) of the Republic of Panama; online tax administration portal: Sistema Integrado de Tributación (SIT) at https://sit.mef.gob.pa. Customs enforcement for import/export ITBMS is handled by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) through its electronic customs declaration system, integrated with SIT.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Panamanian Private Interest Foundation (PIF, Ley 25 de 1995) must: (1) hold Public Registry registration of its Public Instrument of Foundation and maintain a resident agent in Panama; (2) appoint a resident legal representative and licensed CPA; (3) obtain a SIT digital signature (Firma Electrónica); (4) file Formulario de Inscripción via SIT or the DGI service center, submitting certified and Spanish-translated foundation charter, apostilled powers of attorney, Panama registered office address, and director/beneficiary identification; (5) upon approval, receive the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), which is the sole TIN/VAT number for ITBMS and income tax purposes; (6) register with ANA as importer of record for DUA filing; and (7) secure the municipal Aviso de Operación where local economic activity is conducted. The PIF also pays a flat annual foundation tax (currently B/.350) administered separately from income tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — Panama applies source-based territorial taxation. The PIF is an autonomous judicial patrimony with separate legal personality and is neither a pass-through nor a tax-exempt vehicle per se: it is taxable at entity level at the corporate income tax rates (15% on the first B/.50,000 of Panama-source taxable income and 25% above that amount) only on income derived from Panamanian sources, including import/trading margins from domestic wholesale sales of frozen beef and seafood. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — Panama applies source-based territorial taxation. The PIF is an autonomous judicial patrimony with separate legal personality and is neither a pass-through nor a tax-exempt vehicle per se: it is taxable at entity level at the corporate income tax rates (15% on the first B/.50,000 of Panama-source taxable income and 25% above that amount) only on income derived from Panamanian sources, including import/trading margins from domestic wholesale sales of frozen beef and seafood. Foreign-source income, foreign banking deposits, and foreign-source capital gains remain exempt in Panama, giving the structure a hybrid profile for cross-border planning but no domestic pass-through election.

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box or CFC election system. Applicable elections include: (i) election into the small-taxpayer simplified ITBMS regime (annual gross income up to B/.200,000) with quarterly settlement; (ii) election of ITBMS refund filing frequency for exporters in a credit position; (iii) election to operate under the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime (Ley 41 de 2007) for regional service income; and (iv) election by the PIF to be constituted as a public-interest foundation (Fundación de Interés Público) for certain exempt purposes. No domestic hybrid mismatch election is available; mismatches must be managed through entity classification differences recognized in the counterpart jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.200,000 (USD 200,000; balboa at 1:1 parity with USD) — annual gross income ceiling for the optional simplified ITBMS regime for small taxpayers. There is no zero-registration threshold: any person habitually selling taxable goods or services in Panama, and every importer of record, must register for the standard ITBMS regime. A reduced 2% rate applies to qualifying basic food basket items, including designated fresh/frozen meat and seafood lines, while alcoholic beverages and tobacco attract higher differential rates.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS zero-rating (0%) applies to (i) goods physically exported from Panama to a foreign destination with evidence of effective departure, (ii) goods delivered to a licensed free zone (e.g., Colón Free Zone or Panamá Pacífico) for re-export, and (iii) services performed and enjoyed outside Panama. An exporter holding a valid RUC and ANA exporter registration may credit or refund ITBMS borne on importation of frozen beef/seafood if the same goods are subsequently exported; meat and fishery consignments additionally require MIDA/ARAP sanitary certificates.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA) validated by ANA showing actual 'salida efectiva' (effective departure) of the goods
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill naming the foreign consignee and matching the declared HS codes (0202/0203 beef, 0303 pelagic fish)
  • Commercial export invoice and packing list reconciled with DUA quantities, weights, and Incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/MT202 bank credit advice proving collection of export proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • SIT zero-rate e-invoice record bearing the DGI-issued Código de Autorización
  • MIDA/ARAP sanitary and health export certificates for meat and fishery products, where applicable

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance through SIT electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica): all registered taxpayers must transmit invoice XML data to DGI via the SIT web-services API and receive a DGI validation and unique Código de Autorización before the invoice is legally issued; no physical fiscal stamping is used. Import declarations (DUA) are cleared electronically through ANA's single window, with ITBMS assessed and paid at the customs edge and linked to the SIT invoice record; zero-rated export invoices must be flagged as 'Exportación' and validated online.

DGI and ANA jointly perform post-clearance document verification and audits of zero-rating claims within the statutory limitation period. Where export substantiation fails, the full ITBMS (7% standard or 2% reduced rate) is retroactively assessed with statutory surcharges and monthly moratory interest, and any refund previously paid is clawed back with interest. Fraudulent documentation exposes the PIF, its legal representative, and advisors to Código Fiscal administrative fines proportional to the tax evaded, criminal tax-fraud prosecution, loss of exporting/e-invoicing privileges, and disqualification from the simplified regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized monetary system without any currency exchange control regime. There are no restrictions on capital account transactions, no current account convertibility licensing, and no profit remittance barriers for foreign investors. Dividends, interest, royalties, and trade settlements may be freely transferred abroad through the domestic banking system. The only practical hurdles are AML/CFT bank-level due diligence, documentary compliance with the SBP's know-your-customer (KYC) rules, and withholding tax obligations on Panamanian-source income as per the territorial tax code. Profits attributable to foreign-source income earned by a Private Interest Foundation (PIF) are generally outside Panamanian taxation, but bank remittances must still satisfy source-of-funds evidentiary standards.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds in Panama. The USD is both the domestic and settlement currency; therefore no surrender, repatriation, or forced conversion obligations apply. Exporters and importers may retain proceeds in any convertible currency within the Panamanian banking system; however, banks apply internal AML compliance filters and require the underlying trade documents to support all inbound and outbound wire activity.

Central bank supervision: No standing central bank in Panama; the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) acts as the prudential banking regulator, while Banco Nacional de Panamá serves as fiscal agent and clearing bank for the Republic; the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) oversees sovereign financial policy. The US dollar is legal tender, and the Balboa remains only a fractional coin unit pegged 1:1 to the USD.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore and unlicensed fintech digital EMI providers are legally prohibited from operating in Panama or opening deposit accounts for local or foreign-domiciled PIFs without obtaining a banking or fintech license from the SBP under Law 1 of 2018 and Decree 239 of 2021. Feasibility is rated Low for fully non-resident digital EMIs; however, a PIF may use a licensed Panamanian fintech or multi-bank platform only for payment initiation where funds ultimately clear through SBP-regulated commercial banks. Legal constraints include mandatory AML registration, beneficial ownership disclosure, transaction monitoring, and the operational absence of a Panama-specific e-money passporting regime from the EEA or other jurisdictions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from shareholders, settlors, or direct lenders must be wired exclusively to the PIF's named commercial bank account in Panama, using SWIFT MT103 for directly held funds or MT202 for intermediary transfers. The Panamanian receiving bank will require the underlying purpose code, e.g., 'Foundation capital contribution', 'Trade working capital loan', or 'Pre-export financing', together with a capital subscription resolution or loan agreement. Funds are held in USD; wire instructions must reference the IBAN-equivalent Panamanian account number (CLABE is not used) and the bank's SWIFT/BIC. The SBP requires banks to verify the identity of the ultimate originator and perform real-time screening, so all wire references should identify the direct remitter precisely to avoid rejection. For a PIF, the minimum capital requirement under the Private Interest Foundations Law must be funded at onboarding, and any subsequent injection must be evidenced by a foundation council resolution and, if above USD 100,000, a notarized declaration of source of funds.Outbound disbursements for frozen beef and seafood import are best executed through: (a) commercial documentary letters of credit opened by a Panamanian first-tier bank confirmed by a correspondent bank in the exporter's jurisdiction, with settlement via SWIFT MT700/MT710/MT754 and SWIFT MT103 for reimbursement; (b) direct SWIFT MT103 transfers for pre-negotiated open-account consignments; or (c) SWIFT MT202 for cross-border nostro account settlement. The PIF should structure payments according to Incoterms such as CIF or CNF, with documentary presentation including the full set of ocean bills of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, sanitary/phytosanitary certificate, and the Banco Nacional de Panamá or Banistmo trade finance desk's stamp. For recurring shipments, a revolving LC with maximum USD 2,000,000 per tranche is recommended, subject to margin deposits and commodity-specific goods inspection reports. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% flat rate on Panama-source taxable income for legal entities

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding on taxable Panama-source profits distributed by a Panamanian legal entity. Distributions by a Private Interest Foundation under Law 25/1995 are not legally characterized as dividends and generally fall outside the dividend withholding net unless they are re-characterized as Panama-source remuneration or taxable transfers.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before the last day of the third month after the fiscal year-end; calendar-year taxpayers must file no later than 31 March of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 1,000,000 in related-party transactions and USD 3,000,000 in consolidated annual turnover; Panama uses the US dollar as circulating legal tender, so the local-currency threshold is denominated in USD.

Economic substance scope: The Panamanian ESR framework applies to a legal entity conducting a relevant activity such as the import and distribution of frozen goods. The PIF must demonstrate substantial economic presence in Panama: physical premises, qualified local personnel, core income-generating activity performed in Panama, and annual expenditure adequate to its scale of operations. Failure to satisfy the ESR test entails a fine up to USD 1 million, a surcharge of twice the undeclared benefit, and potential deregistration of the foundation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and non-filing of Form 110

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to USD 5,000, a 25%-50% penalty on the omitted tax, monthly late-payment surcharges, and criminal liability with imprisonment of 3 to 6 years under the Panamanian Fiscal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors, foundation council members, and the resident agent may be jointly and severally liable for civil restitution, face freeze orders on personal assets, travel bans, and denial of exit permits where the foundation was used as a mere conduit for tax evasion.
Sham shell foundation and ESR failure

Statutory sanction: Cancellation of foundation registration, revocation of tax benefits, fines up to USD 1 million under Law 201/2021, and a deficiency assessment equal to twice the undeclared taxable benefit.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing applies: directors and officers become personally liable for cessation credits, face bans from acting as directors in any Panamanian entity for up to 10 years, and are subject to civil damages actions by creditors and third parties.
AML/invoice fraud and shadow financial engineering

Statutory sanction: Urgent freezing of assets, fines between USD 100,000 and USD 2 million under Ley 23/2015, criminal conspiracy charges with sentences up to 12 years, and seizure of the imported merchandise and the benefiting assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and foundation protectors may be subjected to travel bans, passport revocation, and personal criminal liability; the court may also order disqualification from managing regulated activities for up to 10 years and require personal restitution of the laundered amount.
