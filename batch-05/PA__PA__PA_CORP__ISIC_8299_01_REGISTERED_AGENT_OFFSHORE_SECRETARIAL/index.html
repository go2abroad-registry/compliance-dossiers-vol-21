Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) – Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; taxpayer portal: DGI Sede Electrónica via https://dgi.mef.gob.pa (e-Tax Panama)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) before invoicing Panamanian-source revenue. The application is filed before DGI through a local registered agent or an apostilled power of attorney, and must include corporate registration documents, beneficial ownership declaration, and a description of intended economic activities. Non-resident entities without a permanent establishment require a RUC only if they generate Panama-source income subject to ITBMS or corporate income tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign entity must obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) before invoicing Panamanian-source revenue. The application is filed before DGI through a local registered agent or an apostilled power of attorney, and must include corporate registration documents, beneficial ownership declaration, and a description of intended economic activities. Non-resident entities without a permanent establishment require a RUC only if they generate Panama-source income subject to ITBMS or corporate income tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A Panamanian Sociedad Anónima is inherently classified as a territorial taxpayer taxed on Panama-source income only. No statutory hybrid mismatch election exists; affiliated groups may elect consolidated filing under DGI administrative authorisation where wholly-owned groups meet statutory conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None (registration is mandatory upon the first taxable act; no monetary turnover threshold exists. A simplified monthly-payment regime is available for eligible micro/small taxpayers).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available when the exporter proves (i) movable goods physically left Panama under a valid customs export declaration, or (ii) services were rendered exclusively for use or consumption outside Panama and were received and paid by a non-resident without economic benefit in Panama. The exporter must maintain an active RUC, be registered for ITBMS, and issue an electronic invoice that expressly identifies the export nature of the transaction.

  • Panama Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) validated by the National Customs Authority (ANA)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or equivalent transport document evidencing physical departure from Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank deposit voucher proving payment in USD to the Panama-based exporter

Panama mandates digital invoicing ('Facturación Electrónica') through the DGI e-Tax platform. After a phased taxpayer-by-taxpayer rollout, all registered ITBMS taxpayers must issue digital invoices bearing a validated QR code and a DGI-generated authorization number. Invoices are transmitted in near real-time through the DGI API or web portal; fiscal authorization is obtained before serial activation and each emission is validated by a digital stamping service against the taxpayer's active registration status.

Fraudulent export zero-rating is subject to retrospective reassessment by DGI within the general five-year limitation period (extended to ten years in the case of fraud). The tax authority may claw back all input ITBMS credits previously offset against zero-rated exports, and impose fines of up to 200% of the omitted ITBMS, plus monthly interest and late-payment surcharges. Misdeclared exports also trigger suspension of the RUC, revocation of export authorisation, and potential criminal liability for tax fraud under Panamanian fiscal and criminal legislation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility without capital controls. The Panamanian balboa circulates at a fixed 1:1 exchange rate with the US dollar, and the US dollar itself is legal tender. Non-resident corporations face no exchange-control permit, no central bank approval, and no current- or capital-account restrictions on profit remittances, dividends, loan repayments, service fees, or registered-agent payments. The only frictions are AML/CFT reporting thresholds, bank-level due diligence, and documentary requirements imposed by SBP Rule 8-2017 and the FATF recommendations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no requirement to channel export settlements through local banks. Export receipts and all other inbound funds may be freely retained in USD or any convertible currency, subject to general AML/CFT record-keeping and suspicious-transaction reporting duties.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) — statutory banking, trust and financial-services regulator; note that Panama does not maintain a central bank, and the US dollar is legal tender under a full dollarization system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore electronic money institutions holding an EU/UK EMI license cannot passport into Panama. To offer local settlement, an EMI must either (i) establish a correspondent relationship with an SBP-licensed bank, (ii) operate through a local licensed bank under the SBP outsourcing rules, or (iii) apply for a non-bank financial-services authorization (electronic-money issuer / casa de valor) from the SBP. In practice, cross-border EMIs are accepted only as payment-introducer or payout-routing channels into a Panamanian omnibus account; the final treasury settlement must occur at a licensed local bank. Enhanced KYC, local registered-agent presence, and transactional reporting to the SBP are legally required.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident private interest foundation (PIF) or company with multi-layer shareholding, nominee directors, and unclear ultimate beneficial ownership.

Root cause: Non-resident private interest foundation (PIF) or company with multi-layer shareholding, nominee directors, and unclear ultimate beneficial ownership.

Defense evidence: Notarized and apostilled certificate of incorporation and foundation charter; certified copy of corporate registry entry (Registro Público de Panamá); full UBO chain with copies of passports; sworn source-of-wealth and source-of-funds statements; bank and professional references from jurisdictions of the principals; notarized board resolutions confirming the purpose; corporate good-standing certificate from the licensed registered agent.
High-volume cross-border wire traffic to or from jurisdictions perceived as high-risk, inconsistent with a staff outsourcing / secretarial-services business model.

Root cause: High-volume cross-border wire traffic to or from jurisdictions perceived as high-risk, inconsistent with a staff outsourcing / secretarial-services business model.

Defense evidence: Detailed business narrative describing the exact outsourced services; signed client service agreements and sub-contracting contracts; in-house AML risk assessment of the client portfolio; transaction flow diagrams explaining each payment leg; client on-boarding CDD records; billing and invoicing trail; evidence that all wire purpose codes match the commercial reality of the transaction.
Large unexplained inbound capital injection into a capital escrow or operational account shortly after incorporation, particularly if the company has no prior operating history.

Root cause: Large unexplained inbound capital injection into a capital escrow or operational account shortly after incorporation, particularly if the company has no prior operating history.

Defense evidence: Notarized capital subscription or contribution resolution; audited or management financial statements of the contributing party; bank statements of the remitting entity showing the origin of funds; declaration of non-repayable equity contribution; loan agreements if injected as shareholder debt; tax or fiscal-clearance certificates where applicable; legal opinion confirming that the company is not a dormant shell and that the capital will support active cross-border secretarial operations.
Use of crypto/fintech payment rails or settlement through a high-risk correspondent jurisdiction for a Panama private interest foundation or registered-agent fee collections.

Root cause: Use of crypto/fintech payment rails or settlement through a high-risk correspondent jurisdiction for a Panama private interest foundation or registered-agent fee collections.

Defense evidence: Complete source-of-funds documentation from the ultimate payor; chain-of-title records showing each intermediary transfer; settlement confirmation from a licensed Panamanian bank; client KYC/AML file with beneficial-ownership attestation; a local legal certificate addressing FATF and SBP Rule 8-2017 compliance; in-force letter of good standing issued by the licensed registered agent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing uses SWIFT MT103 or FedWire transfers to the bank's USD correspondent (abstractly, the Panama branch or its US correspondent), referencing the corporate RUC and, where applicable, the PIF registration number. Narrative must specify 'Capital contribution — non-repayable equity injection' and include the ultimate-stockholder name and tax identification (CRS/TIN). Equity injections should be directed into a dedicated Capital Escrow Account at the receiving SBP-licensed bank and must align with a notarized capital resolution previously presented at onboarding. The receiving bank registers the receipt under the capital-transaction code for national statistics and applies enhanced due diligence, including verification of the dispatching bank's licensing status and the transaction purpose.Clearing rails for outbound settlements: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border fees, service payments, dividends and management fees; (ii) commercial letters of credit under ICC UCP 600 using MT700/MT710/MT202 series where goods or invoiced services require title transfer; (iii) Panama's local ACH (ACH Panama / Cámara de Compensación) for USD-denominated domestic payments; (iv) cheques or straight wires for low-value disbursements. For an ISIC 8299 registered-agent and secretarial office, outward flows are predominantly operating expenses, statutory fees, dividends, and intra-group charges; every payment must be supported by an underlying contract, invoice, board resolution, and CRS/FATCA self-certification to satisfy the bank's travel-rule and suspicious-transaction monitoring obligations.Deliverable US-dollar forward contracts on the offshore interbank market where a local counterparty is availableNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on Latin American currency crosses for mismatched foreign-currency receivables or payablesCross-currency interest rate swaps and FX swaps to align USD functional-currency cash flowsVanilla currency options (puts and calls) executed through an SBP-licensed bank or authorized securities broker-dealer solely for treasury hedging, not speculative trading
