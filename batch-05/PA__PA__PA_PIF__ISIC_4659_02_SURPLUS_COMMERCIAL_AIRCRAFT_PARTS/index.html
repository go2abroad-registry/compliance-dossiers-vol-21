Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); taxpayer portal: SIT (Sistema Integrado de Tributación) at https://sit.mef.gob.pa/

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or Private Interest Foundation (PIF) engaged in taxable economic activities in Panama must obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) from the DGI. For a PIF, the foundation's constitutive documents must be legalized and, where appropriate, apostilled, together with the Public Registry of Panama founding inscription and a notarized power of attorney appointing a local resident agent and fiscal representative. The application must be filed through the DGI's SIT portal or at a local DGI branch, specifying the foundation's name, registered address, and intended activities. If no Panamanian-source income is derived and no ITBMS registration is required, the PIF may remain tax-unregistered for income tax purposes, but any import, export, or local commercial transaction in Panama will require an RUC and corresponding registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama has no U.S.-style 'check-the-box' classification election. A Private Interest Foundation is a juridical person with autonomous patrimony; for Panamanian income tax purposes, Panamanian-source income is taxable at the entity level, while foreign-source income falls outside the territorial scope. No hybrid mismatch election or pass-through classification is available under the current Code; however, a qualifying small taxpayer may elect the simplified ITBMS regime if annual taxable turnover is below the relevant statutory threshold.

Standard VAT / GST rate

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports from Panama of tangible movable goods, including surplus commercial civil aircraft rotables and avionics parts, qualify for the 0% ITBMS rate when the goods are physically exported from Panama to a foreign destination and the seller retains proof of export. Offshore services rendered to non-residents for use entirely outside Panama, and goods supplied into a Panamanian foreign trade zone for subsequent export, are also eligible. The exporter must be the registered seller, hold a valid RUC, and must not have consumed the goods within Panama before shipment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación).
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Panama.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent SWIFT/FX credit evidence confirming payment of the export proceeds into the seller's Panama bank account.

Panama does not currently operate a mandatory continuous transaction control clearance system, but the DGI requires electronic fiscal invoicing (factura electrónica) for most operationally active taxpayers, phased in by taxpayer category through the SIT portal. Invoices must include a DGI-issued digital fiscal code or authorized printed fiscal code where applicable, and transaction data, including export e-invoices, must be transmitted to the DGI on prescribed reporting cycles. Taxpayers must use DGI-authorized electronic invoicing software or the SIT web tool; real-time transactional interface is required for certain high-risk and large-taxpayer categories, while smaller taxpayers may issue electronic invoices with batch submission.

The DGI and Customs Authority can retroactively reverse zero-rated treatment and claw back uncollected ITBMS, VAT credits, and refunds if the export documentation is inconsistent or non-existent. Penalties include fines ranging from 100% to 300% of the tax evaded, surcharges and default interest, suspension or cancellation of the RUC and customs registry license, and criminal tax fraud charges under Panama's Fiscal Code and anti-money-laundering laws. Directors, resident agents, and de facto managers may be jointly and severally liable for fraudulent export schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Panamanian balboa exists purely as a unit of account and circulates only as coins pegged 1:1 to the U.S. dollar; the economy is fully dollarized with no separate legal tender. Accordingly, there is no discrete convertibility control over the U.S. dollar, and current-account payments for trade in goods and services are unrestricted. Capital account flows, including inbound equity or shareholder loans to a Private Interest Foundation (PIF), likewise do not require prior central bank approval because no foreign exchange surrender or licensing regime applies. However, for a PIF engaged in the distribution of certified civil aircraft rotables and avionics surplus, the practical profit-remittance constraints are not exchange-control related but arise from economic substance expectations, beneficial ownership documentation, and U.S. dollar clearing correspondent bank due-diligence requirements in jurisdictions where the ultimate beneficial owner or counterparties are located. Dividends or distributions to non-resident beneficiaries are not subject to Panamanian withholding tax under the territorial tax regime, provided the income derives from foreign sources or export-related activities, but banks will require full corporate and foundation documentation to distinguish the foundation's own assets from beneficiary interests for anti-money laundering and FATCA/CRS purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign currency receipts into local currency, as the U.S. dollar is legal tender and the balboa has no independent exchange rate. Exporters of aviation parts and rotables may retain all proceeds in U.S. dollar-denominated accounts at licensed banks or at specialized escrow institutions. The relevant compliance obligations are documentary: export declarations must be filed with the Dirección General de Aduanas (Customs Authority) via the SISCOMEX-type platform for the verification of the Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación), and banking records must evidence an underlying commercial contract, commercial invoice, airway bill, certificate of airworthiness, and End-User Statement where applicable. No mandatory conversion ratio exists, and no timeline applies for repatriation because the jurisdiction is a dollarized, non-surrender, non-exchange-control territory.

Central bank supervision: Banco Nacional de Panamá (National Bank of Panama) in coordination with the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Superintendency of Banks of Panama); the Republic of Panama does not maintain a conventional central bank issuing domestic currency, and the U.S. dollar serves as legal tender under Decree Law No. 9 of 1970. Currency control and anti-money laundering oversight are also exercised by the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) and the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Moderate feasibility. Panama's banking law restricts the taking of deposits from the public to entities licensed by the Superintendency of Banks; therefore, an offshore digital EMI or electronic money institution without a Panamanian banking or finance-company license cannot operate a deposit-taking account. A PIF may use a licensed Panamanian fintech or a specialized payment processor with a License B or License C general license only as a payment gateway, not as the primary liquidity depository. Cross-border EMIs regulated in the EEA or UK are generally unsuitable as the main account for U.S. dollar clearing of aviation escrow flows because of U.S. correspondent banking restrictions, Travel Rule thresholds, and the requirement for the underlying transaction to be clearly trade-related. The recommended viable structure is a Panamanian bank or trust-company escrow account for contractual aviation deposits, complemented by a U.S. dollar clearing account at a U.S. correspondent bank in the name of the Panamanian bank; direct use of an offshore EMI is advised only for non-core, pre-reconciliation administrative payments and must be avoided for regulated aviation escrow receipts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The recommended compliant routing for initial capital injection and subsequent shareholder/founder funding is a U.S. dollar wire from the grantor or shareholder's verified bank account to the PIF's Panamanian operational account using the Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication (SWIFT) message type MT103 for straight-through processing. The wire must clearly identify the beneficiary as the PIF with its full registered name and account IBAN or CLABE-style identifier as provided by the Panamanian bank; because Panama does not use IBAN, the bank's ABA/Chapa number and account number must be provided. The payment purpose code should be stated in the remittance field, such as 'GITS' or 'GITM' for transfers to non-resident entities if routed through a U.S. correspondent, or the appropriate ISO 20022/CFS purpose code; a Panamanian bank will generally accept a narrative like 'Capital contribution to Private Interest Foundation [Name] for aviation parts trading' or 'Shareholder loan for working capital – ISIC 4659'. Supporting verification must include a certified capital contribution resolution, a copy of the foundation deed, source-of-funds evidence (bank statements, sale agreement of assets, dividends, or gift letter), and a sworn statement from the grantor that the funds are not derived from proceeds of crime. For transfers exceeding certain thresholds, the bank will file a Currency Transaction Report with the UAF and may inquire into the economic substance of the foundation; thus the injection should be completed in a single transparent wire rather than structured into fragmented deposits to avoid structuring red flags.The primary clearing rail for outbound trade disbursements is a SWIFT MT103 wire from the Panamanian bank account to the supplier, refurbisher, logistics provider, or escrow agent. For larger aviation-consignment settlements, a commercial letter of credit (LC) issued under UCP 600 rules is appropriate, particularly when the counterparty is a non-U.S. airframe teardown facility or MRO provider; the recommended form is an irrevocable, confirmed (if possible) documentary credit with presentation of the commercial invoice, airway bill, certificate of airworthiness, and packing list. A standby LC or bank guarantee may be required when onboarding new consignment principals. To ensure liquidity clearing in the U.S. dollar system, the funding bank should maintain a nostro relationship with a U.S. clearing bank; payments settle through the Clearing House Interbank Payments System (CHIPS) or Fedwire, and correspondent banks will verify OFAC, anti-boycott, and sanctions screening. For high-value or non-standard disbursements to a third-party payee, the PIF must instruct the bank with a board-authorised payment instruction, and the bank will apply enhanced due diligence where the recipient is located in a higher-risk jurisdiction. The less preferred but viable rail is a commercial draft or cash-in-advance cross-border payment through a specialized aviation escrow provider that holds funds in a fiduciary account at a U.S. bank; such providers may require the PIF to be a named beneficiary and will generally impose a 0.5%–1.5% transaction fee for escrow, wire, and compliance handling.U.S. dollar-balboa spot transactions (automatic, no hedging required because the balboa is pegged 1:1 to the U.S. dollar and circulates only as coins)Cross-currency profit hedging using deliverable forward contracts (plain vanilla forwards) between the U.S. dollar and the customer's settlement currency, e.g., EUR/USD or MXN/USD, provided the Panamanian bank or an affiliated broker-dealer acts as counterparty and the transaction is classified as a trade-related hedge, not a speculative instrumentNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on Latin American emerging-market currencies such as the Colombian peso, Peruvian sol, or Argentine peso, available through a U.S. or regional bank with a Panamanian booking center, permitted only where the PIF can demonstrate a firm underlying aviation purchase or sale commitment in that currencyCross-currency swaps (FX swaps) and rolling spot contracts for managing temporary settlement mismatches between the receipt of U.S. dollars and the need to pay a foreign-currency MRO or teardown facility, subject to the bank's mark-to-market collateral requirementsOTC vanilla currency options (call or put) used as insurance against adverse exchange-rate movements on large bespoke aircraft-rotable purchases, although such options are not customarily offered to a PIF absent a structured relationship with the bank's global markets desk

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Moderate feasibility. Panama's banking law restricts the taking of deposits from the public to entities licensed by the Superintendency of Banks; therefore, an offshore digital EMI or electronic money institution without a Panamanian banking or finance-company license cannot operate a deposit-taking account. A PIF may use a licensed Panamanian fintech or a specialized payment processor with a License B or License C general license only as a payment gateway, not as the primary liquidity depository. Cross-border EMIs regulated in the EEA or UK are generally unsuitable as the main account for U.S. dollar clearing of aviation escrow flows because of U.S. correspondent banking restrictions, Travel Rule thresholds, and the requirement for the underlying transaction to be clearly trade-related. The recommended viable structure is a Panamanian bank or trust-company escrow account for contractual aviation deposits, complemented by a U.S. dollar clearing account at a U.S. correspondent bank in the name of the Panamanian bank; direct use of an offshore EMI is advised only for non-core, pre-reconciliation administrative payments and must be avoided for regulated aviation escrow receipts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and TP study mandatory where related-party transactions in a fiscal year exceed B/.2,000,000 (US$2,000,000; B/. is pegged 1:1 to USD); Master File required where the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent equals or exceeds B/.50,000,000; an abridged TP annexure is filed with Form 700 in all related-party transaction cases

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations apply (Law 107 of 2020 / implementing decrees) to entities carrying on geography-mobile 'relevant activities', including distribution of goods, financing, leasing and headquarters functions. A PIF trading in aircraft parts must demonstrate: (i) staff with suitable qualifications resident and physically present in Panama, (ii) physical premises and equipment, (iii) core income-generating activities (contract negotiation, inventory management, invoicing and risk control) performed in Panama, and (iv) annual economic substance return filed with the DGI; failure to meet substance exposes the entity to fines, reassessment, and treaty-benefit denial under the OECD/G20 principal-purpose test

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through misdeclared Panama-source income

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50%–100% of the evaded tax plus interest; administrative fine of B/.500–B/.5,000 per infraction; the 'Renta presunta' minimum tax (4% of gross) prevents underassessment; criminal liability pursued where the evasion exceeds statutory thresholds

Director personal liability: Members of the Foundation Council and officers who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the ISR, surcharges and fines; criminal conviction for tax fraud exposes them to imprisonment (up to 5 years), confiscation of assets, and travel bans/alerts in money-laundering-linked tax cases
Sham Private Interest Foundation / acting as a de facto trading corporation

Statutory sanction: The DGI and courts may disregard the foundation (veil piercing) and assess corporate ISR, dividend tax, ITBMS and penalties as if the business were a mercantile company; the foundation's assets may be attached for the unpaid fiscal liabilities; revocation of the foundation's registered status

Director personal liability: Council members are personally liable for distributions made in insolvency or from untaxed profits; the founder/beneficiaries may be treated as de facto shareholders and taxed on deemed distributions; striking off and 'neutralization' of the entity triggers personal liability for subsequent debts
Transfer pricing and economic substance failure

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income to the arm's-length result; penalty of 40%–50% on the tax understatement arising from TP adjustments; denial of treaty benefits; fines under the ESR (up to B/.1,000,000 in aggravated cases) and possible deregistration of the commercial license

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize non-arm's-length invoices without economic substance face personal 'responsabilidad solidaria' for the CNP/DGI claim; in cross-border cases, foreign tax authorities may invoke the DTA exchange-of-information clause, exposing the officers to parallel prosecutions and extradition where trade mispricing is linked to money laundering
AML: trade-based money laundering and sanctions circumvention

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015 (as amended): administrative fines of up to B/.1,000,000 per violation on the entity and responsible compliance officers; freezing/seizure of the aviation inventory and funds; criminal money-laundering conviction carries 5–12 years' imprisonment and asset forfeiture

Director personal liability: Foundation council members, signatories and beneficial owners face personal criminal prosecution, preventive custody and travel bans; bank-account freezes (escrow and Swift rails) are imposed by UAF orders during investigation; professional enablers (abogados, contadores) may also be indicted for facilitating the scheme
