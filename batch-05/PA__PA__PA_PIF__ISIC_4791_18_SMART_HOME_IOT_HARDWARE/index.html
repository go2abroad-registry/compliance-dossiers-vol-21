Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Panama prescribes no statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Private Interest Foundation, but Law 25 requires the initial endowment to be sufficient to fulfil the foundation's stated purposes; market practice adopts a US$10,000 endowment. The endowment is irrevocably assigned to the foundation's patrimony, may be held domestically or abroad at the Founder's discretion, and is not subject to escrow-release or capital amortization rules for registration purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No IRS-style check-the-box regime exists in Panama, but the following elections/classifications are available: (i) simplified ITBMS small-taxpayer election for gross annual turnover not exceeding B/. 300,000, replacing general ITBMS credit procedures with a 7% flat invoice supplement; (ii) small-retailer presumptive income-tax method for qualifying merchants, substituting the 25% standard corporate rate; (iii) territorial-source structural election: the PIF may keep all foreign-source e-commerce profits accumulated and unrepatriated as dividends, avoiding 25% ISR (only a 4% dividend/gratuity tax applies on actual distributions of foreign-source profits); (iv) Multinational HQ (SEM) elective regime — under Law 41 of 2007, a licensed SEM may claim a 5% rate on export services and ITBMS exemption, relevant only if the foundation adds cross-border B2B services; (v) practical PE election for online retail: if the PIF has no fixed place of business or dependent agent in Panama, its sales to foreign customers are treated as foreign-source and outside Panama's ITBMS/ISR net, so the foundation may elect to operate as a non-domiciled exporter, importing goods under customs ITBMS suspension with subsequent export credit.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 300,000 of gross annual taxable turnover (Balboa at parity with USD); however, registration as an ITBMS taxpayer is mandatory from the first import of IoT hardware or from the first Panama-source retail sale if the foundation maintains a fixed establishment. The 10% ITBMS rate applies to professional services, restaurants and similar service segments, while the 7% goods rate governs ISIC 4791 hardware retail.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS zero-rating (0%) is available where: (i) tangible IoT hardware is physically exported from Panama under a definitive customs export declaration, with ownership transferred and goods delivered outside Panama; (ii) the buyer of record is not a resident of Panama and the sale is settled from abroad; (iii) digital IoT software/firmware is delivered electronically to customers using the services outside Panama; or (iv) services are provided to a foreign counterparty without Panamanian use. DGI examines the cumulative file: real export declaration, pecuniary settlement evidence, and the PIF's accounting trail. Panama has no exchange-control repatriation deadline because the USD is the circulating currency, but the inward-remittance certificate and/or the Stripe/Shopify settlement ledger mapping to each export invoice is the substantive evidence required; free-trade-zone and maquila modalities benefit from ITBMS suspension with 0% on the re-export.

  • Panama Customs DUA export declaration (Salida Definitiva) or Free-Zone transfer document, with ANA validation stamp
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international courier/postal export receipt proving physical cross-border delivery
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the DUA and payment evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (boleta de remesa) or equivalent bank statement/Stripe-Shopify settlement ledger evidencing receipt from abroad
  • E-invoice with DGI authorization code showing ITBMS 0% and the export reason code

Mandatory real-time DGI-authorized e-invoicing (Factura Electrónica) is phased in by taxpayer category under Law 6 of 2018 and DGI technical standards through the SIF platform. The PIF retailer must: (a) obtain an 8/12-digit DGI Código de Autorización for each invoice in near-real time, either through the DGI REST/API gateway or DGI web forms; (b) transmit the invoice XML in the DGI schema and store the signed digital objects for audit; (c) print/show the authorization code and DGI fiscal legend on every Panamanian customer receipt; (d) integrate Stripe and Shopify Payments with a DGI-certified local e-invoicing adjunct or ERP API so each Panama-sourced sale is declared in the monthly ITBMS return; (e) adopt electronic sales/purchase books in SIF; (f) flag export transactions with the 'exportacion' reason to evidence 0% rates and support input-credit claims. Failure to transmit triggers rejection of input credits and administrative fines.

DGI, ANA and the banking system cross-match export declarations with paid ITBMS returns, customs departure data and banking inflows. Where zero-rating is improperly claimed: (1) the 7% ITBMS on the full gross amount is assessed retroactively together with a 10% surcharge and 1% monthly interest; (2) fiscal-code penalties of 50%-200% of the evaded tax apply for proven fraud; (3) refunded input credits are clawed back with interest for up to five years of statute of limitations; (4) criminal tax evasion (Law 8 of 2010 and Law 76 of 2019) is punishable with 2-5 years imprisonment and fines; (5) customs violations expose the PIF to fines of up to 300% of goods value, cancellation of the exporter license, and debarment from ANA authorized-operators programs; (6) foundation council members and the resident agent may face solidary liability for fraudulent export returns.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The U.S. dollar is unlimited legal tender under Executive Decree No. 154 of 1904, complemented by Law 84 of 1941; the balboa is a purely nominal accounting unit and no balboa paper currency is issued. Panama therefore has absolute convertibility on both current and capital account items. There are no profit repatriation restrictions, no capital controls, and no prior approval requirements for inbound or outbound transfers. For an offshore Private Interest Foundation conducting cross-border IoT retail, funds generated from foreign sales are considered territory-exclusive income and can be freely remitted to bank accounts worldwide. Compliance obligations derive solely from SBP AML rules (Law 23 of 2015) and require documentation of the underlying trade or capital contribution, not FX authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for export proceeds. The foundation may retain 100% of proceeds in USD, CNH, EUR, or any freely convertible currency in domestic or foreign accounts. For internal underwriting, the foundation should retain export invoices, bills of lading (AWB/SEAWAY), and marketplace payout reports to evidence the commercial provenance of inbound funds and mitigate any bank's perception of unexplained wealth.

Central bank supervision: Panama does not operate a central bank. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the sole banking and AML/CFT supervisory authority. Banco Nacional de Panamá (BNP) acts as fiscal agent for the Treasury but has no monetary policy power. Monetary conditions are set de facto by the U.S. Federal Reserve as a result of full dollarization.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate-Low. Panama enacted the Financial Innovation Regime (regulatory sandbox) under Law 70 of 2022 and the SBP regulates licensed fintech payment/electronic-money participants; however, no foreign offshore EMI (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Wise Business) holds a Panamanian payment-services or e-money license in good standing. Such EMIs may be used as auxiliary collection or disbursement tools for HKD/CNH and EUR payments to Chinese or European suppliers, but the foundation's primary liquidity anchor must be a licensed Panamanian bank account. Major domestic banks frequently reject inbound wires from third-party pooled settlement accounts of EMIs absent a clean beneficiary-owner name, an EMI bank reference, and a breakdown of each merchant payout. The recommended architecture is to use the EMI for supplier-side disbursements and to sweep all net merchant proceeds monthly via SWIFT MT103 into the Panamanian bank account, attaching Stripe/Shopify reconciliation reports.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the founder's capital injection, route a SWIFT MT103 from the founding family's own bank account to the foundation's Panama account with the beneficiary reference 'Contribution to Capital of PIF No. XX'; the transaction should be free of any goods description and supported by a Foundation Council capitalization resolution. For e-commerce settlement, use Stripe's payout engine to send USD to the Panama account via Stripe's U.S. or European banking partners (typically routed through Citibank/JPMorgan New York), and for smaller balances use Wise Business 'Business Multi-currency' only as a collection acceleration tool. Panama imposes no statutory purpose code, but best practice is to include descriptive remittance information such as 'FOUNDATION CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION', 'MERCHANT SETTLEMENT - STRIPE PAYOUTS', or 'REIMBURSEMENT OF TRADE ADVANCE' and attach the original payout report to the bank on request. All inbound wires from aggregators should arrive with the foundation as named beneficiary, never in the name of an individual council member, and should be matched to monthly payroll/settlement statements within 3 business days to satisfy the SBP's transaction monitoring expectations.Primary rail for supplier settlements is the SWIFT MT103 with fee division field SHA (charges shared) to prevent correspondent deductions from eroding the supplier's net payment. For regular procurement of smart IoT hardware from Shenzhen or Hong Kong manufacturers under USD 50,000 per invoice, use direct telegraphic transfer with attached purchase order. For consolidated bulk shipments exceeding USD 100,000, open an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700 issued by Banco General, Banistmo, or Multibank) with a 90-day usance tenor, payable at sight against bills of lading and commercial invoice. Alternative rails include trade financing facilities (import loans or trust receipts secured by the LC) and HKD/CNH disbursements via licensed EMIs for urgent top-up of supplier accounts. All cross-border payments must flow from the operational account only, with six-month settlement history and underlying trade invoices maintained and electronically retrievable for the bank's post-transaction compliance reviews.USD/CNH deliverable forward contracts (1M to 12M) transacted at the dealing desks of Banco General or Banistmo for settling Chinese suppliers without converting core USD liquidityCNY non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) entered into with U.S. or Hong Kong counterparties when the foundation has no onshore CNY bank account, based on standard international NDF documentationUSD/EUR FX swaps and rolling forward contracts to hedge European wholesale purchases and to convert EUR balances generated by Shopify MarketsUSD/EUR cross-currency swaps for term-aligned hedging of supplier credit lines, subject to ISDA and CSTA master agreements executed with a licensed bank dealerPlain-vanilla USD/CAD and USD/MXN forward contracts to hedge Canadian and Mexican retail payout repatriation if multi-currency collection accounts are later adopted

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderate-Low. Panama enacted the Financial Innovation Regime (regulatory sandbox) under Law 70 of 2022 and the SBP regulates licensed fintech payment/electronic-money participants; however, no foreign offshore EMI (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Wise Business) holds a Panamanian payment-services or e-money license in good standing. Such EMIs may be used as auxiliary collection or disbursement tools for HKD/CNH and EUR payments to Chinese or European suppliers, but the foundation's primary liquidity anchor must be a licensed Panamanian bank account. Major domestic banks frequently reject inbound wires from third-party pooled settlement accounts of EMIs absent a clean beneficiary-owner name, an EMI bank reference, and a breakdown of each merchant payout. The recommended architecture is to use the EMI for supplier-side disbursements and to sweep all net merchant proceeds monthly via SWIFT MT103 into the Panamanian bank account, attaching Stripe/Shopify reconciliation reports.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on net taxable income derived from Panamanian-source revenue (Código Fiscal Art. 699; small taxpayers under Law 84 of 2017 may benefit from a 15%–25% progressive bracket scale).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% final dividend withholding on distributions paid from Panamanian-taxed profits (Código Fiscal Art. 733); 10% withholding applies to distributions from non-taxed or fiscally transparent profits. A PIF distributing foreign-sourced foundation assets or foreign-derived income to beneficiaries is not subject to Panamanian dividend WHT by reason of territoriality; however, Panamanian-source commercial profits distributed through a PIF are subject to the 5% dividend tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed within three months following the fiscal year-end; a calendar-year PIF must file on or before 31 March of the succeeding year. Quarterly anticipos are due in the month following each quarter based on the prior year's tax liability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and annual TP Affidavit are mandatory in Balboas (B/., pegged 1:1 to USD) where the PIF's related-party transactions exceed B/. 350,000 in a fiscal year; Master File is required when the consolidated multinational group to which the PIF belongs reports prior-year gross revenue equal to or above B/. 50,000,000. Depending on DGI implementing resolutions, taxpayers with gross revenue above B/. 3,000,000 and intercompany goods or services may also be documented regardless of the 350k threshold.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not apply an offshore ESR statute of the BVI/UAE type, but the following substance rules bind a PIF: (1) the territorial exemption is available only if the IoT retail business is genuinely managed and executed abroad—Panama-point contacts, Panamanian fulfilment centers, or local managers can convert income into Panamanian source; (2) under the fiscal transparency trigger doctrine (Law 52 of 2016 and DGI rulings), a PIF whose Panamanian-source income falls below 50% of total global gross income may be forced to include foreign income in the taxable base unless it proves real economic activity, local registered office, Foundation Council minutes, local books, payroll, and decision-making records; (3) DGI audits require a logical substance file: foundation charter, council resolutions approving each commercial venture, distribution of e-commerce commissions, and bank statements. Absence of substance is treated as misuse of the PIF form, generating ex-officio assessments and 25% surcharges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Territorial Source Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal defraudación tributaria under Código Fiscal Arts. 758–761: fines ranging from 100% to 300% of the evaded tax and imprisonment from 2 to 5 years; civil reassessment including worldwide income retroactively, 1% monthly surcharge (capped at 60%), and nullification of tax-exemption certificates.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax debt and penalties; courts may order civil restitution to the foundation and beneficiaries, freeze personal assets, impose preventive travel bans (prohibición de salida del país), and sentence directors to imprisonment for fraudulent declarations.
Shell Foundation / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DGI re-characterization of the foundation as a taxable commercial entity, inclusion of foreign income under the fiscal transparency rule, 25% tax on the global base, surcharges of up to 60%, administrative fines up to B/.5,000, and denial of the PIF's statutory incompatibilities; the registry may cancel the foundation's inscription for manifest non-activity.

Director personal liability: Under Law 25 of 1995, Council members acting ultra vires or with gross negligence are personally liable for damages; in tax proceedings the corporate veil is pierced where the foundation is a mere alter ego—directors face personal assessments for the full reassessed liability, bankruptcy-restriction orders, and defensive custody while criminal tax charges are pending.
AML / Law 23 of 2015 Breach with Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Monetary fines of up to B/.1,000,000 for repeat or serious AML violations by non-financial subjects; violation of suspicious transaction reporting rules triggers administrative disqualification and criminal money-laundering charges under Law 23 of 2015, punishable by 5 to 12 years of imprisonment for predicate offences involving illicit funds.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members and compliance officers are criminally liable where they knowingly permit laundering through the retail pay in-flow; sanctions include personal fines, forfeiture of personal assets, travel restrictions, extradition exposure under AML treaties, and imprisonment of 5–12 years; directors are also subject to permanent debarment from founding or managing Panamanian legal entities.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: DGI may adjust the taxable base to the arm's length result and impose the 25% corporate tax on the upward adjustment, plus 1% monthly default surcharges and late interest; failure to submit TP documentation attracts administrative fines of B/. 1,000 to B/. 10,000 per infraction and increases the burden of proof to the taxpayer; repeat violations permit aggregate penalties of up to B/. 50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false TP affidavits face criminal liability for false statements (1–4 years imprisonment); civil liability extends to the entire TP adjustment where the Council approved artificial margins without economic justification, including restitution to the foundation and disgorgement of commissions; judges may impose personal preventive detention in aggravated fraud-linked TP audits.
