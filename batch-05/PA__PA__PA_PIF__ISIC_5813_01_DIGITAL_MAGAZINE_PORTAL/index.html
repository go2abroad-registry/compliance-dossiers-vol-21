Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); online e-Tax 2.0 portal at https://etax.mef.gob.pa. Ancillary registers: Registro Público de Panamá (foundation deed and foundation council registration), Caja de Seguro Social (payroll, if Panamanian staff are hired), and the Municipio of the registered office for the Aviso de Operación. Foreign digital service suppliers register through the DGI non-resident digital services module of e-Tax.

E-government filing portal

(2) Register the foundation with the Registro Público (Fundaciones section) to obtain the registration number and issued certificate. (6) Non-resident owners/beneficiaries require no physical presence; a local fiscal representative is mandatory and the resident agent may serve in that capacity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: The PIF is a hybrid legal entity (separate patrimony without shareholders) recognised for civil purposes; for Panamanian income tax it is a separate taxpayer only on Panama-source income. All foreign-source income — including digital advertising, subscription revenue collected abroad, and foreign bank interest — falls outside the territorial tax net and is exempt from ISR (the 25% corporate rate applies only to Panama-source profits). The PIF is not automatically tax-transparent domestically, but it has no mandatory income tax return if it derives no Panama-source income. Its only unavoidable national charge is the flat annual foundation tax of USD 300 (Law 25 of 1995 as amended, due 31 January; late-payment fine of USD 300). A PIF carrying on business in Panama (e.g., a local sales team selling ads to Panamanian advertisers) becomes subject to corporate ISR of 25% on that Panama-source income and must file annual returns; the endowment contribution is not taxed, and no net-wealth or municipal asset tax applies.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in Panama. Relevant elections and mechanisms: (a) early voluntary ITBMS registration before exceeding the registration threshold; (b) election to use the Régimen Simplificado del ITBMS (simplified fixed monthly payment) for taxpayers with annual turnover not exceeding USD 36,000; (c) advance tax ruling (consulta tributaria) before the DGI or the Tribunal Administrativo Tributario to confirm the export character of digital services; (d) contractual design of the foundation deed (reservation of powers to the founder, beneficiary designation, protector appointment) that may influence foreign jurisdictions to classify the PIF as a tax-transparent trust — this is a drafting election rather than a domestic statutory check-the-box; (e) OECD Pillar Two: for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million, the Income Inclusion Rule and Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax of 15% under Law 361 of 2024 may apply, but a standalone PIF falls outside that scope; (f) no Panamanian hybrid mismatch election is codified, but because the PIF is territorial and not subject to CFC rules, hybrid mismatch exposure is minimal.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 36,000 (B/.36,000) of annual gross taxable turnover distinguishes the simplified small-taxpayer regime from the general ITBMS regime; in practice, DGI expects registration as soon as taxable activity commences in Panama. Foreign digital service providers must register under the non-resident digital supplier regime from the first Panamanian B2C transaction with no de minimis threshold; once registered, monthly ITBMS returns are due. If the portal sells only to foreign consumers and foreign advertisers and no Panamanian consumption occurs, no ITBMS registration is required under the territoriality rule.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the territoriality principle, the sale of digital magazines and advertising inventory qualifies for the 0% export rate if the service is effectively used and exploited outside Panama and the counterparty is a non-resident. The DGI applies a use-and-enjoyment test: the contract must be executed abroad, the service recipient must be located outside Panama (foreign tax ID or passport evidence), and payment must originate from abroad. Subscription bundles combined with Panamanian-targeted advertising should be contractually split and allocated; the export character of digital advertising can be evidenced by IP-geolocation reports, advertising platform dashboards, and foreign advertiser country billing profiles. Domestic support services (local editing, local bank accounts) do not destroy the export character if the service is consumed offshore.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) for any direct export of goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (non-negotiable copy)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Remesa / SWIFT MT103 credit advice) proving receipt of foreign funds
  • Export sales contract and foreign customer residence certificate (foreign tax ID or passport copy)
  • For digital services: IP-geolocation and server access logs, plus Stripe/AdSense payout settlement statements identifying foreign counterparties

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta) applies to all ITBMS-registered taxpayers through the DGI e-Tax platform. Issuance requires DGI-accredited software producing XML/CAdES files with a DGI-authorised folio and security code (digital stamp). The clearance model is prior validation / pre-numbering by DGI, with real-time reporting capability via DGI web services/API for the largest taxpayers; foreign digital services suppliers must issue a simplified electronic invoice or receipt for each Panamanian transaction. Fiscal printers remain permitted only for small taxpayers under the simplified regime; QR-coded invoices are required, and the DGI can suspend issuers that fail validation.

Panama does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline because the official currency is the US dollar (Balboa at par); 180 days is adopted as the documentary benchmark. Failure to evidence inward remittance within 180 days allows the DGI to reverse the zero-rating and re-characterise the sale as domestic, assessing ITBMS at the applicable rate plus a 10% monthly surcharge (capped at 50%) and 1% monthly interest. False zero-rating claims attract administrative fines of 20%-40% of the omitted tax (Código Fiscal Articles 758-759); fraudulent claims constituting tax evasion under Law 70 of 2019 are punishable by 3-5 years of imprisonment and fines of USD 10,000-50,000. The DGI has a 3-year statute of limitations for assessments, extended up to 6 years for non-filing and unlimited in fraud cases, and may pursue joint and several liability of the resident agent and Foundation Council members for reckless filings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates under a fully dollarized economy; the US dollar is legal tender. There are no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Profits and dividends may be freely remitted abroad without conversion restrictions. However, banks apply their own compliance filters for source-of-funds verification and FATF-aligned AML checks. Corporate clients face no statutory convertibility hurdles, but must complete standard documentary KYC and beneficial-ownership disclosure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists; export proceeds may be retained in any currency and freely used. Banks may require the supporting commercial invoice, bill of lading, or customs declaration for regulatory reporting and suspicious-transaction threshold purposes, but there is no forced sale or mandatory conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as banking regulator; Banco Nacional de Panamá as fiscal agent; no central bank with monetary authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Offshore digital EMIs are not separately authorized in Panama and non-bank entities may not accept deposits or issue payment instruments without a license from the SBP. A PIF may use regulated Panamanian banks for local clearing and recognized international banks or licensed fintechs with interoperable IBAN/SWIFT rails for cross-border collection; however, unlicensed EMIs will be treated as high-risk counterparties and may be rejected for payment service provider access.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the PIF must be routed through a Panamanian commercial bank wire account using SWIFT MT103 with a clear beneficiary statement, referencing the foundation charter and foundation ID. Since Panama imposes no statutory purpose-code regime, the remitter should provide the bank with an investment intent letter, board resolution, or capital contribution resolution identifying the funds as paid-in capital or initial foundation endowment. For SaaS/advertising revenue, inbound receivables may arrive directly via Stripe or AdSense from global PSPs, then be swept to the Panamanian operating account through standard USD clearing.Outbound trade and operational disbursements use USD-clearing rails through Fedwire for domestic US settlements, SWIFT MT103 for foreign wire transfers, and commercial letters of credit for structured media/procurement transactions. Panamanian banks issue LC advices or confirm external LCs through correspondent relationships with US money-center banks. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% on net taxable income up to USD 1,500,000; 25% on net taxable income exceeding USD 1,500,000 per Law 76 of 2019.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% final withholding tax on dividends distributed out of profits already subjected to Panamanian corporate income tax; 10% final withholding tax on dividends distributed out of untaxed or exempt earnings, including foreign-source income of a standard corporation; 0% under the PIF special regime (Law 25 of 1995) for distributions of documented foreign-source assets and income.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within three months after fiscal year-end; calendar-year taxpayers must file no later than March 31 of the following year. A nil or fully exempt PIF must still submit Form 04 with a sworn declaration (Declaracion Jurada) evidencing the territorial exemption.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Advance income tax installments due on May 31, August 31, November 30, and February 28 for calendar-year taxpayers; each installment equals 25% of the prior year's final income tax liability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File are triggered when annual gross revenue equals or exceeds USD 1,000,000 or aggregate related-party transactions equal or exceed USD 2,500,000, per the 2022 implementing regulations of Law 198 of 2021; Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue above USD 85,000,000; entities below the documentation threshold must still file the annual transfer pricing informational annex to Form 04.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 254 of 2019, Decree 131 of 2020) apply to resident entities conducting relevant activities: IP holding, headquarters, distribution and service centers, financing, leasing, shipping, banking, and insurance. A digital magazine portal owning platform IP and earning AdSense/sponsored-media revenue may be classified as IP holding or distribution; the PIF must demonstrate substantive direction and management in Panama, physical premises, local qualified employees, and adequate operating expenditure. An annual economic substance declaration is due to the DGI within four months after fiscal year-end (calendar-year entities: April 30). Fully passive PIFs with no relevant activity file a nil/out-of-scope declaration with supporting analysis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudacion Tributaria)

Statutory sanction: DGI surcharge of 5% per month of late filing (max 25%), 10% per annum late payment interest, plus the alternative minimum tax recomputation; criminal tax fraud under the Penal Code carries imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and a fine equal to double the tax evaded; civil restitution of the full tax deficiency and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false sworn returns face personal criminal liability and can be indicted jointly; courts may pierce the corporate veil upon proof of fraud, rendering directors' personal bank accounts and assets subject to embargo; preventive travel bans may be imposed during criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of USD 50,000 for the first ESR violation and up to USD 100,000 for repeated violations; deregistration and forced dissolution by the DGI and the Public Registry; cancellation of the RUC; the foundation is barred from banking relationships and contract enforcement.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors ('aluminum directors') are personally liable for misleading ESR certifications; directors may be disqualified from serving on any Panamanian licensed entity, fined, and their assets attached for restitution of fees received.
AML / Money Laundering and Piracy-Linked Funds

Statutory sanction: Money laundering predicate offense under Law 23 of 2015 and Penal Code Article 254: imprisonment of 5 to 12 years, fines up to USD 1,000,000; asset forfeiture and freezing under Law 115 of 2019 (extincion de dominio); financial and administrative sanctions by the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF) of up to USD 1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the fictitious invoicing face predicate-offense money laundering charges, personal asset confiscation, imprisonment of 5 to 12 years, and travel bans; civil joint and several liability for bank losses as well as clawback of bridge loans and distributions follows under the veil-piercing doctrine.
Documentary Falsity (Falsedad Ideologica)

Statutory sanction: Penal Code falsification of private documents: imprisonment of 2 to 6 years; annulment of the challenged corporate resolutions; invalidation of tax filings; the DGI disallows all claimed exemptions and recharacterizes the foreign-source income as Panama-source taxable revenue, with a 150% tax adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable for the falsification; personal restitution orders cover evaded taxes and third-party losses; preventive detention and travel prohibitions may be ordered, and the director's professional license to act as a fiduciary or corporate director is revoked.
