Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope / Pass_Through_Eligible: The PIF is a separate legal person, yet Panama's income tax (ISR) reaches only Panama-source income. Foreign-source container-leasing rentals are outside the ISR base (0% effective entity-level tax), and distributions to non-resident beneficiaries trigger no Panama income tax or withholding. Panama-source business income of the PIF remains taxable at the standard corporate ISR rate; the PIF is not an exempt entity.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or entity classification election exists in Panama; territoriality is mandatory and not elective. Available mechanisms: (a) optional Simplified Small-Taxpayer ITBMS quota regime (Pequeño Contribuyente) for businesses below the DGI turnover ceiling; (b) optional exporter registration to substantiate 0% ITBMS and claim refunds; (c) under Law 25 of 1995, the PIF may elect a restricted scope of exclusively non-Panamanian assets/activities, eliminating the Aviso de Operación and ISR/ITBMS filing duties; (d) Panama has not implemented BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules, so no hybrid entity/instrument elections are available.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full effective dollarization: the US dollar is legal tender and the Balboa (PAB) is merely a 1:1 fractional unit of account. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible; profits, dividends, and Private Interest Foundation (PIF) distributions may be remitted abroad without central-bank approval or exchange-control permit. The only practical constraints are standard bank KYC/AML source-of-funds documentation, the foundation's RUC tax registration, and the final-beneficiary reporting obligations under Law 129 of 2020.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable - Panama imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no forced conversion ratio. USD container-lease receivables may be retained, swept, or disbursed without restriction; no repatriation certificate or central-bank formality is required.

Central bank supervision: Not applicable - the Republic of Panama does not operate a central bank or foreign exchange administration. Banking conduct and solvency are supervised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panama (SBP); Banco Nacional de Panama acts as the State's fiscal and clearing agent; anti-money-laundering monitoring is performed by the Unidad de Analisis Financiero (UAF) under Law 23 of 2015.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low - Panama issues no standalone EMI (e-money institution) license; only SBP-licensed banks may accept client deposits or operate payment accounts. Offshore digital EMIs and US-chartered virtual banks generally decline Private Interest Foundations as clients due to opacity risk, the sector's FATF-history sensitization, and correspondent-bank restrictive due-diligence policies. The PIF must therefore on-board with an SBP-licensed full-service bank; fintech-style payment functionalities may be layered only through that bank's own corporate card, prepaid, and API treasury products.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends paid from Panama-source taxable profits; profits of a Private Interest Foundation attributable to foreign-source income are not subject to Panamanian dividend withholding tax. A 20% withholding applies to non-distributed profits of a Panama entity deemed to be distributed if the profits are not distributed within 12 months from the fiscal year-end, but only to the extent derived from Panama-source taxable income.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax return must be filed within 4 months after the fiscal year-end (generally March 31 for calendar-year taxpayers following the October 31 statutory corporate deadline under Article 699 of the Fiscal Code; for calendar-year entities, the effective annual deadline is typically March 31 of the following year, with the DGI granting extended deadlines). For non-resident owned entities without Panama-source income, an annual sworn statement may still be required.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Identification data: RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes), corporate name, fiscal year, and registered address in Panama. Non-resident entities and PIFs must provide the RUC and the local registered agent information. The tax address may be the registered agent's office; foreign operations should be detailed in the annexes. Late filing penalties range from USD 100 to USD 10,000 depending on gross revenue; in addition, a monthly default surcharge of 0.5% on unpaid tax applies for the first month and 0.25% for each additional month, up to a maximum of 100% of the tax due. The DGI also imposes a 1.5% monthly interest on overdue tax amounts. Failure to provide complete information may trigger administrative fines and suspension of the tax ID number.
Box 5 / Gross Revenue Total gross income generated from Panamanian-source activities. Income from international container leasing is reported as exempt if it qualifies as foreign-source income derived from the lease of tangible movable property used entirely outside Panama. Report only Panama-source revenue as taxable. If the container leasing activities are contracted, managed, and performed outside Panama, classify all related gross revenue as foreign-source and, if necessary, attach an Exempt Income Reconciliation schedule. Late filing penalties range from USD 100 to USD 10,000 depending on gross revenue; in addition, a monthly default surcharge of 0.5% on unpaid tax applies for the first month and 0.25% for each additional month, up to a maximum of 100% of the tax due. The DGI also imposes a 1.5% monthly interest on overdue tax amounts. Failure to provide complete information may trigger administrative fines and suspension of the tax ID number.
Box 55 / Exempt Income Deduction Deduction of foreign-source income and other income exempt under the territorial tax system. Supporting documentation must identify the location where the underlying asset was used, the place of execution of the lease, and the origin of the payments. The non-resident PIF lessor must maintain a detailed schedule of container location, container move records, and lease counterparties, together with certificates proving that the container boxes are physically outside Panama, to support the deduction of foreign-source income. Late filing penalties range from USD 100 to USD 10,000 depending on gross revenue; in addition, a monthly default surcharge of 0.5% on unpaid tax applies for the first month and 0.25% for each additional month, up to a maximum of 100% of the tax due. The DGI also imposes a 1.5% monthly interest on overdue tax amounts. Failure to provide complete information may trigger administrative fines and suspension of the tax ID number.
Box 70 / Deductions Domestic allowable deductions: direct expenses attributable to Panamanian-source gross income, including depreciation, interest, repairs, and administrative expenses reasonably allocated to Panama activities. Foreign-cost allocation must be supported by an explanation of the apportionment method; only expenses relating to Panamanian-source income are deductible. Expenses financed or guaranteed by related parties may be scrutinized under thin capitalization and transfer pricing rules. Late filing penalties range from USD 100 to USD 10,000 depending on gross revenue; in addition, a monthly default surcharge of 0.5% on unpaid tax applies for the first month and 0.25% for each additional month, up to a maximum of 100% of the tax due. The DGI also imposes a 1.5% monthly interest on overdue tax amounts. Failure to provide complete information may trigger administrative fines and suspension of the tax ID number.
Box 85 / CAIR Determination Calculation of the Advance Corporate Income Tax (Cauca Electronica, normally 2% of gross taxable revenue for taxpayers subject to CAIR). Credits are available against the final income tax due. A PIF with no Panamanian-source gross revenue is generally not subject to CAIR, but if any domestic-source revenue arises, the CAIR regime applies. Non-resident taxpayers must file the related CAIR forms even if the advance tax is nil. Late filing penalties range from USD 100 to USD 10,000 depending on gross revenue; in addition, a monthly default surcharge of 0.5% on unpaid tax applies for the first month and 0.25% for each additional month, up to a maximum of 100% of the tax due. The DGI also imposes a 1.5% monthly interest on overdue tax amounts. Failure to provide complete information may trigger administrative fines and suspension of the tax ID number.
Box 90 / Tax Due Final corporate income tax at 25% on Panama-source net income, less advance payments and tax credits. In addition, the 10% dividend tax is imposed on dividends or deemed distributions of taxable profits. Non-resident PIFs distributing profits from Panama-source income are subject to the 10% dividend rate (or 20% for non-distributed profits deemed distributed if not distributed within 12 months, as applicable). Profits from foreign-source income should not be included. Late filing penalties range from USD 100 to USD 10,000 depending on gross revenue; in addition, a monthly default surcharge of 0.5% on unpaid tax applies for the first month and 0.25% for each additional month, up to a maximum of 100% of the tax due. The DGI also imposes a 1.5% monthly interest on overdue tax amounts. Failure to provide complete information may trigger administrative fines and suspension of the tax ID number.
Schedules and Annexes Supporting schedules: depreciation schedule, exempt income schedule, related-party transactions, and transfer pricing forms (Form SAR-100/Form 525 or equivalent DGI transfer pricing schedules) for transactions with related parties. Where international container leasing involves related-party lessees or foreign affiliates, the taxpayer must prepare a local file if the transaction exceeds USD 250,000 per year and file the annual transfer pricing informative return with DGI. Late filing penalties range from USD 100 to USD 10,000 depending on gross revenue; in addition, a monthly default surcharge of 0.5% on unpaid tax applies for the first month and 0.25% for each additional month, up to a maximum of 100% of the tax due. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Transactions with related parties require documentation when the aggregate value exceeds USD 250,000 (US Dollar) in a fiscal year. The Master File threshold is USD 250,000 for the aggregate related-party transactions and the Local File threshold is the same USD 250,000. Panama's transfer pricing rules apply to cross-border related-party transactions that affect Panama-source taxable income. Foreign-to-foreign pure contracts can fall outside the scope if they do not give rise to Panama-source income. The DGI may recharacterize a transaction if the economic substance of an arrangement is not demonstrated, particularly where a Panama PIF is interposed as a pure booking entity.

Economic substance scope: Applicability of Economic Substance Regulations (ESR): Panama does not currently apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations by statute; however, Panama has adopted substance requirements derived from tax information exchange agreements, the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) minimum standards, and the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) in the Fiscal Code. A Private Interest Foundation must demonstrate adequate substance in Panama for activities that generate Panama-source income, including physical office presence, local bank accounts, books and records held in Panama, and substantive decision-making in Panama for any domestic or treaty-claimed activities. For pure international container leasing with all assets located outside Panama and no Panama-source income, Panama's territorial regime does not impose local taxable substance. Nonetheless, if the PIF is a party to a Panama-registered vessel or the container operations are linked to Panama's international shipping registration regime (when the lease relates to ship containers shipped through Panama Canal ports and associated with a Panama-flag vessel), the PIF must maintain records demonstrating management, control, and the underlying commercial activity in Panama to resist foreign recharacterization and to comply with Panama's substance-related anti-BEPS measures. Additionally, the lease of intermodal shipping containers may be excluded from Panama's 'self-spreading' transaction rules if the container itself is not an asset located in Panama; the DGI can apply the permanent establishment threshold if the leasing activity is structured through a Panamanian agent or dependent representative.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Tributaria)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Panamanian Fiscal Code and Criminal Code include imprisonment from 2 to 7 years for tax evasion, plus fines of 2 to 5 times the evaded tax. The DGI may impose administrative penalties of 25% to 100% of the omitted tax, and the tax authority may seize assets and freeze bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors, foundation council members, and registered agents are jointly and severally liable for the entity's unpaid taxes when they knowingly participate in, approve, or conceal the evasion. Panama courts can pierce the corporate veil to impose civil restitution for the unpaid tax, interest, and penalties. The Superior Court may order travel bans (impedimento de salida), prevent directors from leaving the country while criminal proceedings are pending, and impose restrictions on their administrative capacities. Sentence enhancements apply for use of shell entities or fraudulent documentation.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under OECD-harmonized standards and Panama Law 52 of 2016 on tax transparency, the Superintendency of Banks and the DGI may classify the entity as a shell company, impose dissolution, delist the registered agent, and revoke the foundation status. Administrative fines under Law 52 can reach USD 100,000 for non-compliance with beneficial ownership registers. The entity may be recharacterized as a resident tax entity with all global income subject to Panamanian tax under the GAAR.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members and the resident agent are subject to fines and professional disqualification. If the DGI recharacterizes the entity and assesses tax on global income, the directors who signed the annual return can be held liable for up to 100% of the tax shortfall. Courts have also issued director disqualification orders and travel bans where the shell entity was used to facilitate international tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The DGI may apply a 4% penalty on the value of undocumented related-party transactions (capped at USD 400,000), and may adjust taxable income using the best available transfer pricing method. Adjustments can also impose a 25% income tax on the adjusted Panama-source profits, plus annual interest of 1.5% per month and a 0.5%-0.25% monthly surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incomplete transfer pricing forms are subject to personal fines under the Fiscal Code. The DGI can pursue the responsible directors for the penalty amounts if the company is insolvent and the directors failed to ensure adequate documentation. In criminal cases involving fraudulent transfer pricing reports, directors can face prosecution for fraud or tax evasion and may be subject to travel bans while the case is investigated.
AML / Money Laundering via Foundation

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is penalized under Panama Criminal Code Article 254 with imprisonment of 5 to 12 years and fines of 200 to 500 days (salaries). The UAF may suspend the account, the Superintendency of Banks may revoke licenses, and the DGI may cooperate with asset forfeiture. Corporate vehicles are subject to fines from USD 10,000 to USD 100,000 for AML program deficiencies.

Director personal liability: The resident agent and the foundation council face criminal liability if they execute transactions with knowledge of the illicit origin of funds. The Superintendency of Non-Financial Regulated Sectors (SRF) may impose personal fines, and courts can bar individuals from holding office in Panama. Travel bans are routinely imposed on money laundering suspects, and a convicted director may be imprisoned for up to 12 years, in addition to civil restitution for funds laundered through the foundation.
False Informative Return / Perjury

Statutory sanction: Falsifying tax documents constitutes perjury under Article 405 of the Criminal Code, punishable with 2 to 6 years imprisonment and a fine of 2 to 4 times the false benefit. DGI administrative penalties include suspension of the taxpayer's certificate and mandatory audits for up to five subsequent fiscal years.

Director personal liability: Directors and the accounting preparer who knowingly sign false returns can be convicted as authors of the crime of perjury. Each director potentially faces individual imprisonment and is personally liable for any tax difference determined (the corporate entity may be disregarded for the criminal penalty, with the individual held directly responsible). Travel restrictions can be imposed during criminal investigations.
Beneficial Ownership Register Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The General Directorate of Public Registries may impose fines of up to USD 50,000 for the entity and up to USD 10,000 for the registered agent. The registered agent's license can be suspended. The entity may lose its good standing certificate and be prevented from filing tax returns or resolving any administrative matter.

Director personal liability: The foundation council members and the registered agent are personally liable for the accuracy of the beneficial ownership information. Directors who knowingly provide false information can be prosecuted for perjury and money laundering facilitation. Persons named in administrative sanction proceedings may have their personal records flagged, and a court may order a travel ban if the case becomes criminal.
