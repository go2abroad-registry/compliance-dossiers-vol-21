Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A private interest foundation under Panamanian law is not required to operate a physical office at an address visible to public customers; however, it must maintain a registered agent in Panama (the Resident Agent) and, according to the Public Registry practice, a registered office address in Panama (which may be the office of the Resident Agent or the law-firm address). For a wholesale trading entity engaged exclusively in open-market brokerage/spot trading of SSDs and server processors, a virtual office is lawfully permissible as the address for receiving legal notices, as long as the Resident Agent accepts legal process service and maintains original Foundation books and records. The statutory mandate for substance under the local territorial-tax regime is not a dedicated commercial-presence requirement for non-resident foundations but the 'establecimiento permanente' (permanent establishment) analysis applies if the foundation exercises management or concludes wholesale contracts from Panama. The Registered Office must be physically in Panama during all times; however, the substantive management and decision-making for offshore trading may be conducted from China or other jurisdictions without triggering Panamanian taxation, provided the management is not effectively conducted in Panama.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; tax status derives from legal form and source of income. Available elections include: (i) 'Pequeño Contribuyente' simplified ITBMS regime for small taxpayers; (ii) Multinational Headquarters (SEM) special regime for qualifying regional MNE services; (iii) optional deemed 'territoriality' treatment confirming exclusion of foreign-source income. No hybrid mismatch election exists under Panamanian law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None -- no de minimis turnover threshold; any person performing taxable transfers of movable goods or services in Panama must register for ITBMS from the first taxable transaction. A simplified small-taxpayer regime is separately available to low-turnover eligible taxpayers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods and qualifying offshore services attract ITBMS at 0% (destination principle). Eligibility requires: (a) goods physically delivered to a foreign destination with Panamanian customs exit documentation; (b) services rendered, used, or exploited outside Panama or performed for a non-resident counterparty; (c) remittance of export proceeds in foreign currency through the Panamanian banking system; and (d) retention of contracts, customs declarations, bills of lading/airway bills, and bank inward-remittance records.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Panama mandates electronic fiscal invoicing through the DGI 'Sistema de Facturación Electrónica' (SFE) for most taxpayers; invoices require DGI authorization numbers and a quick-response (QR) code or digital seal. Submission of invoice master data (XML/JSON) is required via the DGI API within a short fiscal clearance window, with certain taxpayers permitted near-real-time asynchronous transmission.

The DGI may audit export-zero-rating claims retroactively within the general five-year assessment window (ten years for fraud/non-filing). Unsupported or fraudulent export zero-rating is clawed back with full VAT, statutory surcharge (up to 100% of omitted tax), default interest, and monetary fines; intentional document forgery or benefit fraud triggers criminal tax proceedings and criminal penalties under Panama's Tax Penal Regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized monetary system without a domestic monetary authority issuing paper currency; therefore there are no exchange-control permits, no prior authorisation gates, and no convertibility restrictions on either current or capital account transactions. Current account convertibility is absolute: trade settlements, brokerage commissions, service payments, and profit remittances by a Private Interest Foundation (PIF) may be effected freely in USD via SWIFT without central-bank approval. The capital account is equally open — inbound capital contributions, shareholder/founder loans, equity injections, and outbound distributions are unrestricted, subject only to (i) AML/CFT source-of-funds verification under Law 23 of 2015 as amended by Law 70 of 2019, (ii) CRS/FATCA beneficial-owner reporting, and (iii) Panama's UBO registry obligations for the foundation's ultimate beneficial owners. Profit remittance hurdles are purely documentary rather than regulatory: the PIF must maintain complete commercial documentation, an updated Certificate of Existence and Appointments, and a recorded UBO declaration; under Panama's territorial tax regime income derived from offshore SSD/CPU wholesale is outside the Panamanian tax net, so no withholding toll, stamp duty, or exchange permit exists on outbound profit flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: none. Panama imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no repatriation deadline, and no mandatory conversion ratio on export receipts. Because the country is dollarized, 100% of SSD/CPU wholesale proceeds are collected in USD — simultaneously the domestic legal tender and the invoice currency — and may be retained in full in the PIF's operational USD account, swept into an escrow sub-account, or applied to supplier and logistics payments without any SBP or customs authorisation. The only compliance duties are the general AML threshold (customer due diligence and transaction registration for flows at or above USD 10,000), complete commercial documentation for each export (commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading/airway bill, and the Panamanian customs export declaration), and a mandatory 5-year record-retention obligation under the Panamanian AML framework.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) — the banking and financial regulatory supervisor; Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent for government treasury clearing. Panama has no issuing central bank: the United States Dollar (USD) is legal tender and the Panamanian balboa (PAB) circulates only as coins and as an accounting unit pegged 1:1 to USD pursuant to Ley 84 de 1941.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for API-driven USD rails; legally constrained for unlicensed foreign operators. A foreign digital EMI without a physical Panamanian branch may lawfully provide USD virtual IBANs to a Panama PIF because settlement occurs in the U.S. correspondent network and the EMI does not solicit clients inside Panama. However, any EMI maintaining a place of business or actively soliciting Panamanian customers triggers the banking-licensing regime (Legislative Decree 9 of 1998, as amended) and would require an SBP banking licence or admission to the SBP Fintech Innovation Hub (sandbox). The strongest legal path is onboarding at an SBP-licensed digital-capable bank (Multibank or Towerbank) or a tier-1 bank with BaaS/API integration. Practical constraints: foreign EMIs commonly apply restrictive sectoral policies to semiconductor trading because of dual-use exposure and demand the identical UBO, end-user, and source-of-funds files required by conventional banks, so the EMI is a supplement, not a substitute, for a Panamanian banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for an inbound USD contribution to the PIF's Panama account: (i) the remitting bank sends an MT103 with field 50K naming the exact ordering customer, field 59 naming the beneficiary exactly as registered (e.g., 'Fundación [Name]'), field 70 containing the clear remittance narrative 'Capital contribution — SSD/CPU wholesale venture, ISIC 4651', and field 71A set to 'OUR' so the Panama beneficiary receives the full amount; (ii) the wire clears via U.S. Fedwire through the Panama bank's U.S. correspondent and is credited to the USD account after sanctions screening; (iii) because no Panama exchange-control exists, no SBP permit or import-foreign-exchange form is required, but the bank will request the Certificado de Existencia y Nombramientos, the UBO declaration, and a source-of-funds statement for any credit above USD 10,000; (iv) for escrow funding, the injection is addressed to the dedicated escrow sub-account maintained under the same foundation registration, with written escrow instructions filed at the bank.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 open-account settlement of SSD/CPU supplier invoices in USD, TWD, KRW, CNY, EUR, or JPY via Panama's correspondent banking network. Secondary rail: commercial Letters of Credit (MT700/MT707), confirmed by a U.S. or Asian bank, issued by the Panama bank for large or first-transaction consignments.
