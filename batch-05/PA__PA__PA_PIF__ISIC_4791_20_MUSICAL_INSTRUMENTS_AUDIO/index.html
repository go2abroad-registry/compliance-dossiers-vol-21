Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; electronic portal: Sede Electrónica DGI (SISE) and Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (SFEL)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must appoint a Panamanian resident agent and obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) by submitting the incorporation documents, foundation by-laws (or corporate charters), officer/resident agent acceptance, and stamp tax affidavit via the DGI's Sede Electrónica. For Private Interest Foundations, the RUC application is processed using the 'Aviso de Inscripción' and the foundation's digital certificate, followed by activation of the tax identification number for ITBMS and other obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign entity must appoint a Panamanian resident agent and obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) by submitting the incorporation documents, foundation by-laws (or corporate charters), officer/resident agent acceptance, and stamp tax affidavit via the DGI's Sede Electrónica. For Private Interest Foundations, the RUC application is processed using the 'Aviso de Inscripción' and the foundation's digital certificate, followed by activation of the tax identification number for ITBMS and other obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist under Panamanian law. A Private Interest Foundation is subject to separate taxpayer status if it carries on taxable commercial operations in Panama; otherwise it remains an inactive/territorial taxpayer. The foundation may elect simplified small-taxpayer status for ITBMS where annual gross turnover does not exceed PAB 200,000, subject to DGI approval.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis threshold: every person, domestic or foreign, making taxable commercial transactions in Panama must register for ITBMS. A simplified regime exists for taxpayers with annual gross turnover of not more than PAB 200,000 (USD 200,000), allowing quarterly payment instead of monthly returns.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) physical export of goods when the sale transfers ownership to a non-resident and the goods leave Panama; (ii) international transport and ancillary services directly linked to exportation; (iii) services rendered from Panama to non-resident persons for use or benefit wholly outside Panama; and (iv) services supplied to a non-resident in relation to goods to be exported. The taxpayer must prove the export with customs documentation, bills of lading, and evidence of payment in foreign currency or from a non-resident bank account.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación)
  • Bill of Lading (BL) or Airway Bill (AWB)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Deposit Confirmation

Digital invoices must be transmitted in real-time to the DGI through the Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (SFEL). The system requires prior registration of the transactional file, use of XBRL/XML-derived schemas, an electronic signature certificate issued by an authorized certifier, and DGI's immediate 'acuse de recibo' approval before the invoice acquires fiscal validity. Final consumers and cross-border operators must generate a digitally signed invoice with a QR code and the DGI's unique authorization code.

The DGI may audit export zero-rating claims for up to five fiscal years. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating triggers retroactive ITBMS liability plus a 10% monthly surcharge (capped at 50%), fixed fines under the Fiscal Code, and, in cases of deliberate tax fraud, criminal penalties of up to USD 30,000 or imprisonment for tax evasion. Exporters are liable to repay to DGI the full ITBMS not originally charged, with interest, and may have their export zero-rating authorization suspended or revoked.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. The U.S. Dollar (USD) is legal tender under Panamanian law; the Balboa (PAB) circulates solely as minted coinage at a 1:1 parity with the USD. There are no exchange-control restrictions on current-account or capital-account transactions, and residents and non-residents may freely convert, transfer, deposit, and repatriate USD, CNY, EUR, and other currencies through SBP-licensed banks. Panama applies territorial taxation: foreign-source income of a Private Interest Foundation is generally exempt from income tax, and distributions to non-resident beneficiaries are not subject to Panamanian withholding; the only material remittance hurdle is documentary - the paying bank must maintain in the KYC file the foundation charter, beneficial-ownership registry (RUB) confirmation, and a council resolution authorizing the distribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no surrender requirement, no repatriation deadline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. All receipts from online sales of musical instruments and audio equipment may be retained indefinitely in USD demand accounts at SBP-licensed banks; payment gateway settlements from Stripe and PayPal are treated as ordinary trade receivables, and the foundation must maintain gateway reconciliation and accounting records for fiscal-accuracy purposes only.

Central bank supervision: No central bank exists in Panama. Banking regulation and supervision are exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); the state-owned Banco Nacional de Panamá (BNP) acts as fiscal agent of the Republic and operator of the national clearing system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and payout; Low for treasury custody. Panama's electronic money framework authorizes licensed electronic-money companies (EDEs) to issue USD-denominated e-money, but the sector is still nascent and the dominant clearing and liquidity infrastructure remains with SBP-licensed commercial banks. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business) may serve as complementary settlement rails receiving Stripe/PayPal payouts or paying foreign suppliers; however, they are not regulated banks in Panama, their accounts are not covered by any Panamanian deposit guarantee, and concentrating foundation capital in an EMI will raise adverse-risk flags during periodic ODD review. Optimal topology: EMIs as pass-through transaction accounts beneath the tier-1 bank treasury account, which remains the sole concentration and disbursement point.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The founder instructs its originating bank to issue a SWIFT MT103 in USD to the Panamanian recipient bank's U.S. correspondent (CHIPS or FEDWIRE details), using field 57A for the beneficiary bank and field 59 for the foundation's account. Field 70 must articulate the purpose without reference to goods or services, e.g., 'FONDO FUNDACIONAL - APORTE INICIAL DEL FUNDADOR / FOUNDER CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO PRIVATE INTEREST FOUNDATION, NO COMMERCIAL INVOICE'; Panama has no centralized purpose-code validation, and no central-bank declaration is required. The account-opening bank must already hold in its KYC file the foundation charter, the RUB beneficial-ownership confirmation, and the council resolution approving the account and the expected inbound transfer; failure to match the SWIFT beneficiary name exactly with the registered foundation name will trigger rejection. For inbound amounts exceeding USD 1,000,000, the bank will apply enhanced due diligence and may suspend crediting for 24 to 48 hours pending verification.Primary clearing rail is the SWIFT MT103 gpi message for settlement of supplier invoices for musical instruments, audio gear, freight, insurance, and marketing services; banks require field 70 to show the invoice number and trade description (e.g., 'INV 2025-0123 / MUSICAL INSTRUMENTS AND PRO AUDIO EQUIPMENT / CIF PANAMA'), while the supporting commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, and insurance certificate are retained in the foundation's trade file for the bank's transactional audit. For repetitive high-value sourcing from Asian or European manufacturers, the recommended rail is an irrevocable, at-sight documentary letter of credit governed by ICC UCP 600, issued and advised through the tier-1 bank's trade desk; the LC is opened via SWIFT MT700 and settled via MT400 upon presentation of compliant documents. Domestic payments within Panama (customs brokers, logistics, warehousing, and local marketing) are cleared through the national automated clearing house operated by Banco Nacional de Panamá (ACH Panamá), while USD-denominated internal transfers are executed without FX conversion risk because USD is local legal tender.USD/EUR deliverable outright forwards up to 12 months tenor, executed with SBP-licensed commercial banks' dealing rooms and fully collateralized; used solely to hedge euro-denominated European supplier payables.USD/CNY and USD/CNH non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing the offshore CNH market; permitted as cash-settled hedging for China-sourced musical instrument manufacturing payables, with maturities of 1 to 6 months and documented underlying trade exposure.USD/CNH deliverable forwards and physically settled vanilla options (call and put) subject to bank-approved derivatives authorization and weekly mark-to-market collateral arrangements.Cross-currency swaps (USD-EUR, USD-CNY, and USD-GBP) for extended vendor-financing or purchase-order facilities exceeding 12 months; executable under an ISDA 2002 master agreement with a Panamanian law schedule and CSA documentation.Plain-vanilla USD interest-rate swaps to fix floating-rate trade or working-capital financing, offered within the bank's commercial treasury product suite and subject to SBP capital adequacy and counterparty risk limits.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (territorial system; Panama-source income only)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% on dividends paid from profits already subject to CIT; 20% on dividends or distributions from profits not previously taxed. Private Interest Foundations must apply these rates when making taxable distributions to beneficiaries.

Perform a reconciliation of the accounting profit (per foundation records) to the fiscal profit under Panama tax rules. Late filing: monthly surcharge of 5% of the final tax liability, capped at 100%; late payment: monthly interest of 1%; inaccurate return: penalty of 10–50% of the omitted tax, plus civil fines.
Box 6 - Tax Due at 25% Apply the 25% statutory rate to the net taxable income, less any advance tax payments and withholding tax credits. If no Panama-source income is reported, enter zero and state the foreign-source nature in the supporting statement. Late filing: monthly surcharge of 5% of the final tax liability, capped at 100%; late payment: monthly interest of 1%; inaccurate return: penalty of 10–50% of the omitted tax, plus civil fines.
Box A - Estimated Taxable Income Current-period projection of Panama-source taxable income used to compute the quarterly advance payment. Base the estimate on the previous year's Form 100 or the current year's actual cash flows from Panama-source sales. Failure to file quarterly advance returns triggers a 5% monthly surcharge on the unpaid advance amount plus interest at 1% monthly; failure to pay advances may increase the final Form 100 assessment by 20%.
Box B - Quarterly Payment Due One quarter of the estimated annual CIT, less any applicable credits. If the foundation has no Panama-source income, enter zero and retain evidence of the foreign-source origin. Failure to file quarterly advance returns triggers a 5% monthly surcharge on the unpaid advance amount plus interest at 1% monthly; failure to pay advances may increase the final Form 100 assessment by 20%.

Attach the relevant documentary support and request a refund if credits exceed the annual liability. Failure to file quarterly advance returns triggers a 5% monthly surcharge on the unpaid advance amount plus interest at 1% monthly; failure to pay advances may increase the final Form 100 assessment by 20%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD/PAB 2,000,000 of consolidated gross income or USD/PAB 1,000,000 of related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File and Master File documentation; lower thresholds may apply for intra-group services or financing transactions.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) under Law 70 of 2019 apply to all legal entities, including Private Interest Foundations. If the foundation conducts a 'relevant activity' (e.g., distribution and service centre, IP holding, financing, banking), it must maintain physical premises, qualified local staff, and active management control in Panama. The current online retail of musical instruments and audio gear is not a relevant activity if conducted only with third parties; however, the foundation must still file the annual sworn ESR statement and the resident agent must keep records demonstrating the absence of any geographic shell arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine equal to 2–3 times the evaded tax and imprisonment of 2 to 5 years under Panama's Criminal Code; civil reassessment to include a 100% surcharge on the unpaid tax, accumulated default interest, and prohibition from obtaining public contracts or tax benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors and foundation council members are jointly and severally liable for the PIF's unpaid taxes when they sign false returns, order asset transfers to frustrate collection, or consciously benefit from the evasion. Criminal consequences include personal fines, travel bans, asset freezes, and commercial registry disqualification.
Shell Company / Non-Compliance with Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines ranging from USD 5,000 to USD 1,000,000 per infraction depending on the type and recurrence of the violation; repeated non-compliance can lead to deregistration of the foundation, revocation of its resident agent appointment, and denial of tax treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members and the Resident Agent may receive separate administrative penalties and may be banned from acting as a resident agent in Panama; if the shell structure is used to disguise beneficial ownership or to facilitate fraud, personal criminal liability for tax evasion and money laundering can attach.
AML/CFT - Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015 and the Criminal Code, money laundering is punishable by imprisonment of 5 to 12 years, a fine of up to USD 1,000,000, seizure of the proceeds, restriction on banking licences, and cooling of the foundation's bank accounts if obliged entities report suspicious transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors, foundation council members, and the Resident Agent face personal criminal prosecution if they knowingly facilitate or tolerate laundered proceeds; civil liability includes damages to third-party victims, travel bans, and freezing orders against personal and corporate assets.
