Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; online portal: SISTEMA T@-PANET and Sede Electrónica (https://www.dgi.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Panamanian Private Interest Foundation must first register its Foundation Charter with the Public Registry of Panama, file the Aviso de Operación (Notice of Operation) with the Municipal Treasury, then obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) and RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) from DGI via the T@-PANET portal. Where the PIF derives only foreign-source income, the RUC is nonetheless required for export invoicing and potential ITBMS credit recovery; a resident legal representative must be appointed. The application requires a notarized foundation charter, certified identity documents, proof of registered address, and payment of the inscription fee; issuance typically occurs within 5-10 business days after Public Registry inscription.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box or full entity-reclassification election exists under Panamanian law. A PIF may elect voluntary ITBMS registration even below the B/.36,000 turnover threshold to claim input VAT credits on export purchases, and may file an annual Solvency/Income Affidavit (Suelto de Renta) confirming that all income is foreign-source and thus outside Panama's territorial income tax. Hybrid mismatch elections are unavailable under domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 (Panamanian Balboa, pegged 1:1 to USD 36,000) of gross annual taxable turnover; exporters may register voluntarily to recover input ITBMS credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A PIF exporting pre-packaged hotpot soup-base bricks qualifies for the 0% ITBMS rate when (i) the goods are physically shipped out of Panamanian customs territory, (ii) title and risk transfer to a non-resident buyer, (iii) the export is evidenced by an authorized customs registry, and (iv) the transaction is recorded in the DGI electronic system. Corresponding input ITBMS on local purchases becomes refundable (crédito fiscal) via Form 401 filed electronically through T@-PANET.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) filed via VUPE/SIUCA with DGI validation
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or air waybill) showing Panama as port of origin
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and commercial invoice evidencing payment from the foreign buyer
  • Packing list and certificate of origin for the seasoning base product line

Mandatory electronic invoicing under Ley 66 de 2020 and DGI resolutions on SEDI (Sistema Electrónico de Facturación) / T@-PANET. Taxpayers must issue XML-based e-invoices with digital signature, QR verification, an AIU fiscal ID code, and obtain a real-time DGI digital authorization code before clearance. Export invoices must bear the 'Exportación' type code and reference the supporting customs declaration.

DGI may retroactively reverse zero-rated export credits, assess 7% ITBMS plus monthly interest (1.5% per month), and impose fines of up to 100% of the understated tax for documentary deficiencies. False export declarations trigger criminal tax-fraud liability under the Código Penal (Ley 14 de 2007) carrying imprisonment of up to 5 years, exclusion from the exporter registry, and customs confiscation penalties ranging from 5 to 15 times the merchandise value in aggravated customs fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy: the United States dollar is legal tender and the balboa circulates only as 1:1 USD-pegged coinage. Both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible, with no restrictions on profit repatriation or dividend remittance. The PIF's foreign-source trading income falls under Panama's territorial tax system and may be distributed to non-resident beneficiaries without Panamanian withholding tax; the only practical frictions are banking KYC/AML compliance, not currency convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion requirement on export proceeds; exporters may retain and freely dispose of 100% of USD and foreign-currency receivables. The PIF must hold a valid RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), register the export-wholesale activity with the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), and file shipment-level export declarations with the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas, but no central-bank clearance certificate or conversion mandate applies.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no conventional central bank. Banking supervision is exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) under the banking law framework (Decreto Ley 9 de 1998), while Banco Nacional de Panamá serves as the fiscal agent of the State. No exchange-control authority or central-bank approval exists for foreign-exchange transactions, capital flows, or trade settlements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-low viability as the primary rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) are not licensed by the SBP to provide payment services in Panama and their internal client policies generally exclude private interest foundations from business-account onboarding. SBP-licensed domestic e-payment companies can hold USD client funds, but their limits and trade-finance capabilities are insufficient for LC-backed procurement of hotpot base from China. EMIs should therefore be used only for small B2B disbursements, never for the initial capital injection or documentary-credit settlements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on any dividend, distribution, or economic-distribution paid by a Panama-domiciled entity from Panama-source profits. The rate applies to resident and non-resident shareholders alike, and the distributing PIF is the statutory withholding agent.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing and full payment are due no later than 15 March of the year immediately following the close of a calendar-year corporate fiscal period. Non-calendar fiscal years require filing within three months of the fiscal year-end and the formal permission of the DGI.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Quarterly advance tax declarations are due on or before 15 April, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December for calendar-year taxpayers. Each quarter’s payment is calculated using prior-year taxable income adjusted for the official price index.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File must be contemporaneously prepared when annual related-party transactions exceed B/. 3,000,000 (USD 3,000,000). A Master File is mandatory for taxpayers forming part of a multinational group with consolidated group revenue equal to or greater than B/. 50,000,000 (USD 50,000,000). Any taxpayer with annual gross revenues above B/. 12,000,000 must maintain structured TP documentation even if the volume of related-party operations is lower.

Economic substance scope: Panama’s Economic Substance Regulations apply to entities that are resident in Panama and carry on one or more relevant activities. The export distribution of pre-packaged food items qualifies as a ‘distribution and service center’ activity, so the PIF must demonstrate in its Panama territory: (i) a physical office where core income-generating decisions are made; (ii) at least one full-time employee with sufficient qualification and competence; (iii) actual expenditure in Panama lawfully proportionate to the revenue; (iv) substantive board or foundation council meetings held in Panama; and (v) proper records and governance documentation evidencing the local direction of operations. A PIF that engages in inter-company purchase and resale of hotpot soup base while being managed entirely abroad is considered a shell and will be re-characterized for Nexus Tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Administrative sanction of 50% of the omitted tax up to three times the amount owed; criminal tax evasion under Panama’s Tax Procedure Law carries imprisonment from 2 to 5 years, monetary penalties equal to the tax evaded, and forfeiture of forged documents and related assets.

Director personal liability: The courts may pierce the foundation veil when the founder and directors act as mere formalities; directors are then personally liable for the evasion debt, subject to bank account freezes, judicial attachment of personal assets, travel bans, and entry into the DGI/public prosecutorial blacklist.
Shell Company and Substantive Presence Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Economic Substance Regulations range from a mandatory administrative fine of B/. 10,000 to B/. 500,000, plus potential late-interest at the statutory rate; the PIF registry may additionally cancel or suspend the foundation registration for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors are held jointly and severally for the ESR fines. During investigation, the Public Registry and DGI may impose exit bans on the registered director, and the courts may attach the director’s personal assets for the unpaid fine if the foundation lacks separate funds.
Money Laundering and Trade-Based AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to B/. 1,000,000 per violation for the PIF and its compliance officer; criminal sanctions for money laundering under Law 23 of 2015 include imprisonment from 3 to 5 years (aggravated up to 10 years), cumulative monetary penalties, and forfeiture of all accounts and warehouses tied to the hotpot base transaction chain.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and foundation directors are treated as principals by the UAF and Public Prosecutor’s Office; they face criminal indictment, passport suspension, exit bans, personal restitution orders, civil forfeiture of their personal property, and, upon conviction, maximum prison term enhancement for acting in the name of a private-interest vehicle.
