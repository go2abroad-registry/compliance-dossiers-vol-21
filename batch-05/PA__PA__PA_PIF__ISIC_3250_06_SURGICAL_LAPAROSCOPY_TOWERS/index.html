Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A private interest foundation is required to designate a resident agent in Panama who maintains the registered office (oficina registrada); the resident agent's physical office satisfies the registered-office obligation and a dedicated administrative virtual office is acceptable for the foundation's day-to-day file management, board communications, and record-keeping. A virtual office without any local substance is not sufficient if the foundation conducts regulated, licensing-dependent, or physical manufacturing activities in Panama; for the present ISIC 3250 medical-device manufacturing scope, a physical premises and local operational substance must be established if domestic production is contemplated, while a purely passive ownership holding of foreign operating subsidiaries may lawfully be administered remotely through the resident agent.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá, Sección de Fundaciones de Interés Privado (Public Registry of Panama, Private Interest Foundation Section), Archivo General de las Américas, Ancón, Panama City.

Governing statute

Ley 25 de 12 de junio de 1995 (Private Interest Foundation Law), Articles 1-19, as amended by Ley 82 de 2013 and related provisions of the Reglamento del Registro Público (Decreto 293 de 1996). In subsidiary matters, the Ley General de Sociedades (Law 32 of 1927) applies by analogy under Article 1, final paragraph.

Formation / constitutional filings

Escritura Pública de Constitución de Fundación de Interés Privado (public deed of incorporation of a private interest foundation), executed before a Panamanian Notary Public and recorded in the Public Registry.

There is no statutory escrow account requirement; the endowment must be expressly stated and contributed in the Public Deed, and the Foundation Council must document receipt in the Foundation's records before commencing business operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Panama; taxpayer portal: SITIO (Sistema Integrado de Información Tributaria) at dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first obtain a Panama Tax Identification Number (RUC) by appointing a resident agent (agente residente) and registering the entity with the Registro Único de Contribuyentes through the DGI, submitting certified articles of incorporation (escritura), proof of registered agent appointment, notarized identification of directors and beneficiary, and a local address; the RUC is issued before opening a local bank account or applying for an importer/exporter code (código de exportador/importador) via the DGI and Aduanas de Panamá.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign entity must first obtain a Panama Tax Identification Number (RUC) by appointing a resident agent (agente residente) and registering the entity with the Registro Único de Contribuyentes through the DGI, submitting certified articles of incorporation (escritura), proof of registered agent appointment, notarized identification of directors and beneficiary, and a local address; the RUC is issued before opening a local bank account or applying for an importer/exporter code (código de exportador/importador) via the DGI and Aduanas de Panamá.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Panama; entities are classified based on the territorial source of income, under which only Panamanian-source income is subject to ISR; taxpayers may elect the small taxpayer regime (régimen de pequeños contribuyentes) if gross turnover does not exceed the statutory threshold, and may elect for the simplified VAT payment scheme (cuota fija) when applicable; hybrid entity availability arises through Private Interest Foundation (PIF) flexibility, enabling perpetual existence and separate patrimony with foundation council control, but no pass-through election is available for Panama corporate income tax purposes other than the transparent treatment of partnerships and trusts under specific statutory rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The DGI mandates VAT registration for any person or entity whose taxable transactions exceed USD 36,000 annually or whose estimated taxable transactions exceed this threshold; businesses selling exoneration-listed goods or providing services are nonetheless required to register and obtain the RUC and VAT authorization before invoicing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Panama territorial taxation zero-rates exports of goods and services when the transaction is destined to a buyer abroad and income is sourced outside Panama; for ITBMS purposes, exports are subject to zero rate where the seller demonstrates that goods physically leave Panama under a customs export procedure (exportación definitiva) or services are provided to non-residents for use abroad, and the service is not connected with Panamanian real property or Panamanian-situs benefits; VAT registrants may claim input VAT refunds based on purchase invoices properly issued to the exporter's RUC, and refunds of ITBMS credits are subject to a 13% retention/withholding regime on the reimbursable balance.

  • Formulario Único de Aduanas (Customs Export Declaration / DUA) duly accepted by Aduanas de Panamá
  • Bill of Lading (original or electronic B/L evidencing departure from Panamanian port)
  • Commercial invoice with RUC and export code of the exporter
  • Contract or purchase order from non-resident customer
  • Packing list
  • Certificate of Origin (where applicable for tariff/vat relief)
  • Bank transfer receipt evidencing inward payment (comprobante de remesa)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Panamanian bank for the credits received

Panama operates a phased electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica) regime administered by the DGI, requiring generation of XML invoices with digital signature and immediate transmission through the SITIO platform; authorized taxpayers, including exporters and importers, must use DGI-authorised technological issuers (emisores tecnológicos), comply with schema validation (schema v1.0 / extension for ITBMS 7%), include the 'CF' (Comprobante Fiscal) electronic invoice code and AUI code, and transmit in real time; businesses over the VAT registration threshold must issue e-invoices with a printed or digital fiscal stamp (papel sellado / timbre fiscal) and transmit data in batches no later than 24 hours for top-tier obligors.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to DGI audit review requiring full documentary provenance; false zero-rating invoicing, use of forged export documents, under-declared taxes, or failure to remit export proceeds within 180 days of the transaction may trigger assessment of the 7% ITBMS with additions up to 100% of the unpaid tax, surcharge of 1% per month (or 10% on provisional calculations), plus administrative fines of up to five times the annual net taxable amount for serious noncompliance (DGI penalty scale) and criminal tax fraud sanctions of 2 to 5 years imprisonment where evasion exceeds the statutory criminal threshold; PIF directors and resident agents signing false export declarations may be jointly liable and subjected to cancellation of the exporter code and RUC based on the DGI's investigative rulings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully convertible, dollarized system with no exchange controls on either current-account or capital-account transactions. Capital injections, trade payments, hedging transactions and profit remittances may be executed freely without central-bank approval. The only remittance hurdle is fiscal: a 10% definitive dividend/distribution withholding tax applies to Panama-source profits distributed by a local payor to non-resident beneficiaries (5% for dividends paid on securities registered with the Panama Stock Exchange), and the payor must evidence tax compliance before remittance. Distributions from foreign-source assets held by a Private Interest Foundation fall outside Panama territorial taxation and are not subject to withholding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory FX surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Panama, as the USD is local legal tender and no central bank exists. Exporters of medical devices under ISIC 3250 must comply with documentary and customs discipline: file the export customs declaration (DUA) through the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) electronic platform at or prior to exit, maintain active export registration with the Ministerio de Comercio e Industrias (MICI) and the single window for foreign trade, and hold a sanitary export permit / sanitary registration from the Dirección Nacional de Farmacia y Drogas (DNFD) of the Ministerio de Salud. Export proceeds may be retained in any domestic or foreign USD account without conversion or repatriation obligation.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the competent banking and AML/CFT supervisor; Banco Nacional de Panamá operates the national clearinghouse and administers ACH Panamá. There is no central bank exercising monetary policy because Panama is fully dollarized, with the U.S. dollar as legal tender and the Balboa as a book-keeping unit at a 1:1 parity; exchange-rate and monetary-policy functions are effectively delegated to the U.S. Federal Reserve.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury or clearing venue, MEDIUM as an auxiliary receivable-collection channel. Panama does not issue EU-style electronic-money-institution (EMI) licenses; payment service providers must be authorized by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá under Law 23 of 2015 and the SBP fintech facilitation framework. Unlicensed offshore EMIs generally decline Private Interest Foundations due to complex beneficial-ownership analysis and cannot lawfully execute settlement, escrow, or trade-LC functions for a Panamanian entity. A locally authorized fintech wallet may accept export receivables only if balances are swept daily to the Tier-1 bank account, while escrow, LC, and mass-disbursement flows must remain exclusively on the SWIFT banking rail.

No exchange-control approval or import certificate is required in Panama; any mismatch between the remitter profile and the declared purpose will trigger a freeze and a Suspicious Transaction Report under Law 23 of 2015. Annual Foundation Tax (Tasa Anual de Fundación de Interés Privado) – mandatory payment to preserve legal existence under Law 25 of 1995 (as amended). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% flat corporate income tax (ISR – Impuesto sobre la Renta) imposed under the Panama Fiscal Code on the net taxable income of legal entities for all income sourced within Panama or derived from Panama permanent establishments.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding on dividends distributed by a Panama corporation out of profits not previously subjected to corporate income tax; 5% reduced withholding where the distributing company elects the credit-method (taxed-account) treatment and retains the relevant ISR paid; distributions by a Private Interest Foundation (PIF) are statutorily outside the dividend withholding regime when the foundation holds only exempt/foreign-source assets, but once the PIF realizes Panama-source commercial profits, the distribution to a foreign beneficiary is re-characterized as a deemed dividend and subjected to the 10% rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months after the close of the fiscal year (31 March for calendar-year taxpayers); the DGI extends the sunset deadline only when the due date falls on a non-business day; transitional quarterly estimated prepayment obligations apply when gross revenue exceeds USD 1,500,000.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Quarterly advance returns and prepayments due by 30 April, 31 July, 31 October, and 31 January, calculated on the basis of the prior final return; the DGI fixes the final annual prepayment adjustment in the Form 101 cycle.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (Informe Local) mandatory when cross-border related-party transactions, including component purchases, royalty payments, intercompany loans, or revenue-sharing for 4K endoscopy hardware, exceed USD 1,000,000 per fiscal year; Master File (Informe Maestro) and documentation consolidation required when the PIF/group consolidated gross revenue exceeds USD 5,000,000; Country-by-Country reporting applies if the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue is USD 750,000,000 or more; amounts are in Panamanian Balboas (PAB) which are pegged 1:1 to the USD.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations apply to PIFs, corporations, and hybrid entities conducting 'relevant activities' (including manufacturing, distribution, and pure holding of intangible assets). A PIF commercially manufacturing surgical laparoscopy towers must demonstrate in each fiscal year: (i) physical premises in Panama; (ii) resident employees commensurate with the activity; (iii) locally incurred operating expenditures; (iv) board meetings and decision-making for procurement, quality control, and export sales occurring in Panama; and (v) statutory books and records kept in Panama. The DGI requires submission of an annual substance attestation with the Form 101 package; a pure-asset-holding PIF is exempt from commercial substance only if it does not carry on any Panama trade or business.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudo Fiscal (Fiscal Fraud)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of two to five times the evaded tax; imprisonment of 2 to 5 years; automatic nullity of the false tax declaration; 3% monthly surcharge compounding on the evaded ISR; cancellation of the taxpayer's RUC and mandatory closure of the commercial establishment; inclusion in the DGI's Public Defaulter list and loss of all government procurement rights.

Director personal liability: Directors, the resident agent, and the financial officer face joint and several civil liability for the evaded tax plus penalties; the DGI and Public Ministry may impose travel bans, freeze corporate and personal bank accounts, request international asset seizure through mutual legal assistance, and obtain personal bankruptcy orders for the officers who signed the fraudulent return.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Fines up to USD 750,000 per fiscal year under the Economic Substance Regulations; deregistration of the PIF from the Foundation Registry; disqualification and personal sanction of the resident agent; re-integration of all foreign-source profits as Panama-source income; refusal by the DGI to honor treaty benefits; and revocation of the manufacturing license granted by the Ministry of Health.

Director personal liability: Officers are personally liable for unpaid taxes on re-characterized income; courts may impose civil restitution equal to the diverted profits; travel bans and prohibitions from acting as director of any Panama legal entity for up to 10 years; criminal money-laundering charges where the artificial structure was used to conceal the true beneficial owner.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: DGI full or partial re-characterization of the transactions at arm's length; assessment of the resulting additional ISR at 25% plus 3% monthly interest; a specific penalty equal to 5%–15% of the undocumented transaction value; potential double taxation without treaty MAP access where the adjustment is unilateral.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or signed intercompany agreements outside arm's-length parameters are personally liable for the tax differential in the event of corporate insolvency or liquidation; travel bans and seizure of personal real estate may be imposed during the coercive collection phase, and criminal liability arises when the transfer pricing documentation is found to be knowingly falsified.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to USD 1,000,000 per infraction under Law 23 of 2015 (AML), Law 254 of 2021 (Beneficial Ownership Registry), and the Criminal Code's money-laundering provisions; administrative seizure and freezing of Swift LC/Wire funds; cancellation of the foundation's registration; denial of banking licenses and corporate accounts; referral to the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF) for criminal prosecution with imprisonment of 5 to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal criminal liability, civil liability for laundering proceeds derived from the underlying fiscal offense, inclusion on international denial-of-benefit lists, extradition risk through Interpol red notices, and personal travel bans reinforced by the Panamanian Immigration Authority.
