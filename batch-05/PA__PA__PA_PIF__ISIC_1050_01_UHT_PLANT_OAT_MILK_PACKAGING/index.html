Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not maintain a domestic check-the-box regime; however, the PIF is not a per-se corporation for U.S. check-the-box purposes and may elect to be treated as a disregarded entity or pass-through under U.S. Treasury Reg. §301.7701-3 for U.S. federal tax reporting. Domestically, the PIF may elect to register as a voluntary 'contribuyente' for ITBMS and income tax if it conducts Panama-source commercial activities; alternatively, it may remain a non-taxpayer for foreign-source passive income. Small taxpayers with annual gross revenue not exceeding PAB 36,000 may elect the simplified tax regime ('Régimen de Tributación Simplificada') in lieu of the full ITBMS accounting regime. Hybrid mismatch elections are unavailable under Panamanian law; the PIF remains a distinct legal personality with shelter from its founders' creditors.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de facto dollarization ensures unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account. There are no exchange controls, currency quotas, or prior licences for any inward or outward remittance. Profit remittances to foreign beneficiaries of a Private Interest Foundation (PIF) are not blocked by FX regulation; however, the Panamanian territorial tax system imposes a 25% corporate income tax on net Panamanian-source income if the UHT plant operates inside Panama, and a 10% dividend tax applies when such after-tax income is distributed to the foundation's beneficiaries. Foreign-derived income remains outside the Panamanian taxing net. Capital injections from abroad are freely credited to the foundation's accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation exists for export receipts. Proceeds from the sale of UHT oat milk and related plant-based products may be held indefinitely in US Dollars or any freely convertible foreign currency. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation deadline, and no separate export FX declaration, although the exporter must comply with standard customs invoicing and statistical filings of the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas de Panamá (ANA).

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank issuing a national currency; the Balboa (PAB) exists only as a coin unit pegged 1:1 to the US Dollar, which is legal tender. The financial supervisor is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), the Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent of the state, and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) conducts external credit, fiscal, and debt management policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility for ancillary collection flows only. Off-shore digital EMIs without a Panamanian banking or fintech licence cannot lawfully take deposits, hold client money, or settle trade payments in Panama. SBP-regulated fintech institutions operating under Law 67 of 2021 (financial innovation regime) and the subsequent virtual-asset regulatory rules may provide e-commerce front-ends and low-value collection services, but all UHT plant capex, LC settlements, and treasury balances must repose in SBP-licensed tier-1 banks. Using an unlicensed offshore EMI for core operations creates AML and consumer-protection exposure and is not recommended.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound founder capital must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 with field 70 stipulating 'FOUNDER CONTRIBUTION - PIF CAPITAL ACCOUNT' and the foundation's Registro Público identification number. The remitter must be the founder or UBO of record, and the receiving bank will require the certified foundation charter, the registered agent's good-standing certificate, and a classification of the transfer as a non-repayable capital contribution. Accepted funds are segregated in the capital escrow account and later released only against verified UHT plant construction invoices, equipment pro-forma invoices, or LC margin calls.Outbound trade settlements will use (i) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for down payments, engineering fees, and minor spare-part purchases; and (ii) irrevocable documentary letters of credit governed by UCP 600 for major equipment (aseptic UHT filling lines, packaging rolls, filtration membranes), with confirmation by a US or EU bank to mitigate correspondent risk. LC documentation must include full original bills of lading, commercial invoices, packing lists, certificate of origin, and an independent pre-shipment inspection certificate. Box 121 Net taxable income after applying all deductions and the statutory net operating loss carryforward (maximum 5 years). Losses derived from foreign-source activities are non-deductible against Panamanian-source profits; only separately computed Panamanian losses may be carried. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 1% of the tax payable per month or fraction, capped at 20%, plus default interest at the statutory fiscal rate; after official notice, additional administrative fines from PAB 500 to PAB 5,000 apply. Persistent non-filing may lead to asset seizure and criminal tax-evasion charges.
Box 126 Income tax payable at the head-line 25% rate on Box 121. Non-resident taxpayer must compute the flat 25% rate on Panamanian-source income only; withheld credits and advance payments are reconciled in this box. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 1% of the tax payable per month or fraction, capped at 20%, plus default interest at the statutory fiscal rate; after official notice, additional administrative fines from PAB 500 to PAB 5,000 apply. Persistent non-filing may lead to asset seizure and criminal tax-evasion charges.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB 2,000,000 (US$2,000,000) in annual gross income; mandatory Local File and Master File are required for taxpayers exceeding this threshold, with additional Country-by-Country reporting for groups with consolidated revenue above US$750 million. Lower related-party transaction thresholds may still trigger contemporaneous documentation on a risk-based assessment.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 70 of 2019 and Decree 267 of 2019) apply to PIFs and other tax-resident entities carrying on 'relevant activities'. Manufacturing and operating an UHT oat-milk plant in Panama constitute real, mind-and-management presence; the foundation must maintain local premises, qualified staff, and actual decision-making records and file the annual economic substance declaration (Form 689). Pure holding PIFs are subject to lighter substance requirements but still must file and substantiate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Panamanian Tax Code (Articles 709-A et seq.): imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, fines ranging from 25% to 100% of the evaded tax, civil restitution, monthly surcharges, and cancellation of municipal permits.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members, signatories, and the Resident Agent who knowingly participate in filing fraudulent returns are jointly and severally liable; travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment are possible if the corporate vehicle is used as a concealment device.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance penalties under Law 70 of 2019: US$50,000 or 2% of gross revenue (whichever greater) for the first year; escalation to 10% of revenue or dissolution for persistent non-compliance, automatic reporting to international exchange-of-information authorities.

Director personal liability: The Resident Agent and Foundation Council members are personally liable for penalties if they fail to reconstitute economic substance within the statutory cure period; disqualification from holding Panamanian corporate positions may follow.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Omission

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under Ley 23 de 2015 and the UAF Regime: corporate fines up to USD 5,000,000, criminal liability for money laundering with prison terms up to 10 years, and international asset-restriction measures.

Director personal liability: Directors and Foundation Council members with effective control who knowingly omit true beneficial owners or facilitate laundering face personal criminal prosecution, travel bans, extradition risk, and unlimited civil damages, with the Panama court authorized to pierce the foundation's legal personality.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: DGI may reallocate taxable income, disallow excessive deductions, apply a 20% penalty on the understated tax, and impose interest from the original filing deadline; criminal liability arises where deliberate falsity is proven.

Director personal liability: Signing directors and Foundation Council members who approve unsupported transfer pricing files are jointly liable for the adjusted tax; evidence of wilful fraudulent pricing is treated as tax fraud, carrying imprisonment and personal criminal fines.
