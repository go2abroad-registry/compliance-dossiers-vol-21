Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Ley No. 25 de 12 de junio de 1995 (Private Interest Foundation Law), Articles 2, 5, 6, 8, 12, 18, 19 and 33; implementing Executive Decree No. 16 of 7 October 1999; subsidiarily, the Código de Comercio de la República de Panamá and the registration norms of the Public Registry.

Formation / constitutional filings

Carta Fundacional (Foundation Charter), executed before a Notary of the Circuit as a public instrument (Escritura Pública) and inscribed in the Special Registry of Private Interest Foundations

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Law 25 de 1995 imposes no statutory minimum; the Foundation must hold an initial endowment (patrimonio fundacional) adequate for its stated purpose, set at US$10,000 for this vehicle. No escrow or banker's deposit certificate is required; the endowment is contributed directly by the Founder and evidenced in the notarized Charter. The endowment may comprise cash, securities or software assets.

Funding & unlock path: Benemérito Cuerpo de Bomberos de la República de Panamá fire-safety inspection certificate for the registered office; structural load and occupancy inspection by the Dirección de Obras y Construcciones del Municipio de Panamá; workplace safety registration and medical clearance through the Ministerio de Trabajo y Desarrollo Laboral (MITRADEL) once employees are engaged.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; primary digital portal is SITIO (Sistema Integrado de Información Tributaria) at https://sitio.dgi.gob.pa, which consolidates RUC registration, taxpayer filings, electronic invoicing, and ITBMS declarations. Ancillary registries are the Registro Público de Panamá for the foundation charter and the relevant Municipio for the Aviso de Operación (business license).

E-government filing portal

For a Private Interest Foundation (PIF) established under Law 25 of 1995: (1) Notarize and register the foundation charter (Acta Constitutiva) with the Registro Público, declaring the initial endowment, which is customarily and statutorily expected to be at least USD 10,000; (2) appoint the resident agent and the Foundation Council (Consejo de la Fundación) with the required Panamanian officers; (3) after registration, apply for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) for legal entities through the DGI SITIO portal, filing the corporate registration certificate and identifying the legal representative; (4) where the PIF engages in taxable business in Panama, also register as an ITBMS taxpayer and obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación; (5) a non-resident digital service provider without a Panamanian permanent establishment may obtain a simplified ITBMS registration via a Panamanian fiscal representative, which serves as the statutory TIN/VAT-ID for app-store collections.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No US check-the-box election and no OECD hybrid-mismatch election exists in Panamanian law; the PIF is fixed by statute as a separate juridical person. Available elections include: (a) the simplified small-taxpayer regime for annual gross income up to B/. 36,000; (b) the standard ITBMS regime, including the exporter election to claim refunds of input ITBMS; (c) transitional elections under Law 52 of 2020 for entities migrating to the reformed income-tax code; and (d) charter elections on beneficiary designation and distribution structure, which determine the territorial-source treatment of distributions. Panama-source income of the PIF is taxed at the entity level; foreign-source income is outside the Panamanian tax net.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 36,000 (USD 36,000 equivalent at par) annual gross income for the simplified small-taxpayer fixed-quota regime; full ITBMS registration is mandatory from the first habitual taxable transfer in Panama, with no general de minimis exemption. Non-resident suppliers of digital services to consumers in Panama are subject to statutory ITBMS registration and collection duties. In-app sales through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing to Panamanian consumers are in-scope, while sales to non-resident users fall outside the ITBMS nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS is charged at 0% on (i) exports of tangible goods supported by an official Panamanian export declaration, and (ii) services rendered to non-resident customers that are wholly consumed or exploited outside Panama. For the health-fitness app tracker, zero-rating requires documentary proof that the end-user is non-resident (foreign billing address, non-Panamanian payment instrument, geolocation/IP data, and the license agreement) and that the digital service is consumed outside Panama. App-store commissions paid to Apple or Google are treated as imported services subject to reverse-charge ITBMS unless they support the PIF's own exported service; failure to substantiate the export converts the supply to a taxable 7% supply.

  • Panama Customs Export Declaration (Formulario Único de Exportación / DUE) for physical goods; for digital services, the export invoice, the license agreement, and the app-store transaction/delivery log
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill for goods; for services, app-store payout statements and platform transaction records evidencing the foreign recipient
  • Inward remittance certificate (Comprobante de Ingreso de Divisas) or bank/Apple/Google payout evidence demonstrating receipt of foreign exchange proceeds
  • RUC and ITBMS registration certificate plus the foundation charter excerpt confirming the PIF as the exporting counterparty

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica de Panamá) under DGI regulations, phased in by taxpayer category. VAT-registered businesses must issue invoices through the DGI-authorized platform or certified billing software using XML/API transmission, receiving a unique digital authorization code (stamp) in real time before issuance; each invoice must contain a QR code and the DGI tax identification of the issuer. Legacy fiscal printers remain temporarily permitted only for approved small taxpayers. Digital health-app operators in the standard ITBMS regime are on the mandatory electronic-issuance track, and input VAT credit or export refund claims generally require electronically authorized invoices.

DGI may audit export zero-rating claims within the statutory prescription period, which is generally five years and is suspended in cases of fraud or serious documentation defects. If export status is not substantiated, DGI assesses the 7% ITBMS on the gross supply, applies statutory default interest, and imposes fines up to 100% of the omitted tax depending on the degree of fault. Knowingly false export documentation constitutes tax fraud and may expose the PIF and its officers to criminal liability and to retrospective clawback of any refunded input ITBMS credits. The full documentation bundle, including FX remittance traceability and user-location evidence, must be retained for the entire prescription window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy; the U.S. dollar is legal tender and the Panamanian balboa exists only as a sub-unit coin at a fixed 1:1 exchange ratio. Both capital-account and current-account transactions are freely convertible and unrestricted in law. There are no exchange-control commissions, no central bank approval for foreign currency wires, and no hindrance to profit remittance. Dividends, distributions, and service fees may be repatriated freely, provided the bank obtains standard AML/KYC documentation and the relevant wire instruction is fully compliant with the Foreign Tax Information Exchange regulations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules in Panama. No timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or central-bank accounting requirement applies to export receipts. Export proceeds from the digital health-fitness app (Apple IAP/Google Play Billing settlements) may be retained in USD or any freely convertible currency and do not need to be converted into balboas or remitted to any Panamanian government authority.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama has no central bank in the conventional sense; the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the banking regulator, while Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the fiscal agent and treasury administrator for the State.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut Business) are not licensed banks in Panama and do not provide membership of Panama's local clearing or automatic SBP regulatory recognition. They may onboard Panama-registered Private Interest Foundations only if the EMI's own licensing regime permits this jurisdiction, but they cannot serve as exclusive treasury rails for collection or settlement in Panama. Their use is legally permissible as a secondary liquidity corridor, but local banks are required for statutory account structure and for receiving final settlement of subscription receipts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound initial and additional capital is routed by SWIFT MT103 wire from the founder's or subscriber's outside bank to the PIF's Panama bank account. The wire narrative must indicate 'Capital contribution – Panama Private Interest Foundation – Law 25 of 1995.' No central bank exchange-control approval is required. The receiving Panamanian bank will request evidence of the subscriber resolution, source of funds, and beneficial-owner identification, but there is no quantitative ceiling or waiting period for lawful capital injection.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements, infrastructure licensing, cloud-service fees, and merchant royalties are cleared through the SWIFT network using standard MT103 for wire transfers and MT700/MT710 for commercial letters of credit where appropriate. For low-value recurring SaaS and app-store-related settlements, MT103 payments to the relevant payment processors and vendors are sufficient. Panamanian banks subject commercial letters of credit to UCP600 rules and the bank's credit-availability relationship. Since Panama's local clearing system is essentially U.S.-dollar based, all domestic payments clear at par; cross-border payments are final in USD and may be routed via correspondent banks in New York or other financial centres.Deliverable forwards in USD/EUR, USD/GBP, and USD/JPY with SBP-licensed Panamanian banks where the company demonstrates real commercial exposure from non-USD subscription revenue.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) booked with international investment-grade counterparties outside Panama for currencies subject to foreign exchange restrictions.Cross-currency swaps for large multi-currency balances of app-store receivables and operational currency exposure.Plain-vanilla over-the-counter FX options executed through licensed Panamanian banks or an external broker-dealer permitted by SBP regulations.
