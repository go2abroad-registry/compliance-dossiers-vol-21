Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by Law No. 25 of 1995; nevertheless, the Foundation Act must declare the initial patrimony, which by banking market practice is fixed at no less than US$10,000 to meet know-your-customer requirements. No government escrow account is mandated; the subscribed patrimony is transferred directly to the Foundation's corporate bank account upon incorporation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Panama; e-filing and e-invoicing portal: Sistema de Información Tributaria (SIT) at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa. Customs counterpart for export clearance: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas de Panamá (ANA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned PIF first registers under Law 25 of 1995 with the Registro Público de Panamá (Public Registry) through a resident agent, stating the foundation endowment (local practice: minimum USD 100 endowment; no statutory maximum). It then obtains the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) by filing the taxpayer registration form with the DGI (in person or via SIT), attaching the foundation charter, the Public Registry certificate, the resident agent's acceptance, and identification of the foundation council members; the DGI assigns the RUC, the ISIC-based economic activity code (4669), and activates the ITBMS/VAT obligation. The PIF must also enroll in the mandatory electronic invoicing system and obtain a digital signature certificate. The statutory annual Panama PIF franchise tax (currently USD 400 under Law 52 of 2016) is payable to the National Treasury via authorized banks; foreign-source income is outside the Panamanian income tax net, but the RUC is mandatory before any Panamanian-source or export transaction is effected.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama has no U.S.-style check-the-box election; tax classification follows the statutory form. Relevant elections for a PIF: (i) irrevocable vs. revocable foundation status (irrevocable status fixes the foundation as the separate taxable person for Panamanian-source transactions); (ii) simplified small-taxpayer ITBMS regime election where annual turnover does not exceed USD 36,000; (iii) election of the zero-rated export treatment (rather than deemed exemption) to preserve input ITBMS refund rights; and (iv) periodic DGI refund application (devolución) for excess ITBMS credits of exporters. Panama has not enacted OECD hybrid mismatch rules; related-party transactions must be documented under Law 254 of 2021 transfer-pricing and annual information-return requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de minimis exemption: any person habitually carrying out taxable transfers of goods/services in Panama must register for ITBMS. A simplified small-taxpayer regime (quarterly filing, reduced documentation) is available only where annual gross turnover does not exceed B/. 36,000 (USD 36,000). The PIF's cross-border scrap consignment is a zero-rated export supply, so full ITBMS registration (RUC + e-invoicing) is required regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A sale of recycled scrap copper wire qualifies for the 0% ITBMS rate if: (i) the seller is an RUC-registered ITBMS taxpayer with the 4669 export activity code; (ii) the goods are physically exported from Panama via a customs procedure and the Export Declaration (DUA/DEX) is accepted by the ANA; (iii) title and risk pass to a non-resident buyer; (iv) a zero-rated e-invoice is issued with the DUA reference; and (v) the purchase price is collected from abroad (documented by bank remittance or letter-of-credit settlement). Services directly related to the export (international freight, insurance, port handling) are also zero-rated. Input ITBMS suffered on domestic scrap purchases and logistics (7%) is creditable; a resulting credit balance is refundable through the DGI devolución procedure for exporters.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA/DEX) accepted by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) in the SADM/SIGA system, including any non-ferrous scrap export authorization
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill naming the PIF as consignor and the non-resident buyer as consignee
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with full scrap grade, weight, container count, and INCO terms
  • Bank remittance evidence: SWIFT MT103/MT202 or documentary letter-of-credit settlement statement evidencing receipt of proceeds from abroad
  • Zero-rated ITBMS e-invoice (Factura Electrónica) with DGI validation QR and tariff code 0%
  • Independent survey/weight certificate with moisture deduction and copper-content analysis supporting customs valuation

Electronic invoicing is mandatory for all registered taxpayers (DGI Resolution 201-1197 of 2015 and phased migration completed by 2024). B2B/B2C e-invoices are issued in XML, digitally signed, timestamped and transmitted near-real-time to the DGI through an authorized fiscal service provider (Proveedor de Servicios de Facturación Electrónica); each invoice shows a QR/validation seal that the ANA and DGI cross-reference for export clearance. Export invoices must state the 0% (zero-rated) tariff code and the sale value in USD (B/.). Specified retail activities additionally require certified fiscal POS devices transmitting each transaction online; invoices not validated by the DGI cannot support an input VAT credit.

The DGI reconciles zero-rated e-invoices against ANA export declarations, carrier master bills, container terminal out-gate data and bank remittance records; the standard documentary benchmark for export proceeds is 180 days from shipment. On audit, unsupported input credits are disallowed and clawed back with compensatory interest from filing date, statutory late-payment surcharge (recargo) accruing monthly, and infringement fines under the Tax Code scale (typically USD 100–USD 10,000 per offense, higher for obstruction). Fabricated export documents or DUA/DEX misdescription triggers criminal tax-fraud and smuggling sanctions under Panamanian law, including confiscation, fines and imprisonment of responsible officers, plus RUC suspension and mandatory bank guarantees for future refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both current and capital account items. Panama is a fully dollarized jurisdiction: there is no exchange-control law, no approval requirement for capital entries or exits, and no restriction on current account payments. Profit remittances, dividends, and liquidation proceeds may be transferred abroad without Central Bank clearance. Practical hurdles are limited to the bank's own anti-money-laundering due diligence under Law 23 of 2015, sanctions screening, and, where Panamanian-source income is generated, documentary substantiation for withholding tax exemption (non-resident recipient). For a Private Interest Foundation conducting cross-border scrap metal trade, the capital account is unrestricted, and remittance speed is governed solely by banking compliance timelines.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panamanian law imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation, conversion, or holding period for export proceeds. Recycled copper scrap exporters and trading entities may retain USD receipts in Panama, in foreign banks, or in transit; they are only subject to record-keeping requirements under the AML/CFT regime (Law 23 of 2015) and the obligation to support all payments with identifiable commercial documentation.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank. Banking and foreign exchange activity is supervised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) under Decree Law No. 9 of 1998. The US Dollar is legal tender and circulates freely; the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) exists only as coinage at an irrevocable 1:1 peg. Monetary policy is effectively delegated by the 1904 Monetary Agreement with the United States; no sovereign exchange-control authority or monetary authority creates FX rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-moderate. Panama has no dedicated standalone electronic money institution (EMI) license; e-money and fintech payment services operate only under SBP's Banking Law licensing or the regulatory sandbox / Fintech Innovation Hub. Offshore digital EMIs based in the UK, EU, or Hong Kong cannot open settlement accounts with Panamanian licensed banks unless they themselves are licensed or registered with the SBP, and most tier-1 banks automatically reject such clients under de-risking policies. A licensed Panamanian fintech card issuers or a corporate wallet provider can be used as a secondary collection rail, but the foundation treasury must maintain at least one primary banking relationship with a licensed bank. Recommend using global EMIs only for low-balance operating expenses while preserving the main USD liquidity in a local bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommend using global EMIs only for low-balance operating expenses while preserving the main USD liquidity in a local bank account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 3,000,000 gross annual income in the preceding fiscal year or aggregate related-party transactions above USD 500,000 triggers the mandatory Form 233 and Local File. A Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds USD 50,000,000. All thresholds are in US dollars, which is the de facto and legal transactional currency in Panama (Balboa pegged 1:1 to USD).

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) under Executive Decree No. 133 of 2021 apply to Panamanian juridical persons carrying out 'Relevant Activities' such as distribution and service centres, headquarters, financing, insurance, IP-holding, and shipping. A PIF engaged in cross-border physical consignment and trading of recycled scrap copper wire is not automatically exempt: if the foundation performs management, purchasing, and logistical coordination from Panama, it will be treated as a distribution centre and must evidence local office premises, full-time employees, board-management meetings in Panama, and proportionate operating expenditure. Passive asset-holding foundations are excluded, but active commodity traders require an annual ESR filing and may be sanctioned for insufficient substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud penalties: imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and a fine equal to twice the amount of tax evaded. Civil tax assessment adds the underpaid tax, a 25% surcharge, monthly interest at 1.5%, and suspension or cancellation of the RUC.

Director personal liability: Directors and foundation council members who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, penalties, and interest. A court can order preventive seizure of personal assets, restrict re-entry or travel bans, and impose imprisonment for the fraud.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR non-compliance penalties up to USD 100,000 for non-filing or false certification; the DGI may disregard the entity and recharacterize the transactions as Panama-source income subject to 25% corporate income tax plus a 10% surcharge. Registry-level sanctions may include cancellation of the PIF enrollment.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is permitted in deliberate substance abuse; foundation council members can be held personally liable for restitution of the evaded tax and for wrongful signature of the substance declaration. Travel restrictions may be imposed pending criminal tax proceedings.
AML / Money Laundering (Scrap Trading Channel)

Statutory sanction: Fines under Law 23 of 2015 and Law 17 of 2022 range from USD 100,000 to USD 1,000,000 per violation, plus asset confiscation. Money laundering as a criminal predicate carries imprisonment of 3 to 10 years and disqualification from commercial activity in Panama.

Director personal liability: A director or foundation council member who knowingly facilitates layering through letters of credit or wire transfers faces personal criminal liability, travel bans, and freezing of personal and foundation assets. The corporate veil is pierced for sham transactions, and overseas extradition may follow.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Breach

Statutory sanction: DGI may impose a transfer-pricing adjustment plus a 10% surcharge and late-payment interest; penalties for non-filing of Form 233 range from USD 500 to USD 20,000, increasing for repeat violations and for multinational groups participating in aggressive pricing schemes.

Director personal liability: Directors whose false pricing certification causes the corporation to receive a tax benefit are jointly liable for the corrected tax and sanctions. In egregious cases, deliberate transfer-pricing manipulation is prosecuted as tax evasion, exposing directors to imprisonment and civil restitution orders.
