Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) - General Directorate of Revenue, Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; taxpayer portal: SIF (Sistema de Información Fiscal), https://sif.mef.gob.pa; official DGI website: https://www.dgi.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope - Under Law 25/1995, the PIF is a distinct legal person whose foreign-source and passive wealth-administration income, assets, transfers, and capital gains are not taxable in Panama (territorial tax system); if the PIF carries on commercial activity with Panamanian source, entity-level tax applies at standard corporate ISR rates (25%, with the Law 254/2023 progressive surcharges on profits exceeding B/.1.5 million) plus ITBMS at 7%. No pass-through of tax attributes to founders or beneficiaries exists.

Available election forms

Panama has no US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch passthrough election. Statutory elections relevant to the PIF: (i) small-taxpayer regime election (Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente - RPC; 2% of gross receipts in lieu of ISR and ITBMS, available where annual gross revenue does not exceed B/.200,000); (ii) election to remain an ordinary ITBMS registrant to recover input VAT on export costs; (iii) election to issue e-invoices through a certified third-party agent (agente facturador) versus direct SIF API integration; (iv) PIF charter elections allocating income and wealth to named beneficiaries abroad, which are not Panamanian source and therefore outside Panamanian withholding; and (v) transfer-pricing documentation elections for cross-border related-party transactions under Law 254/2023.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.07%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de-minimis threshold - ITBMS registration is triggered by the mere carrying-on of taxable transfers of goods or services in Panama. Optional simplified small-taxpayer regime (RPC) applies to annual gross revenue up to B/.200,000 (threshold raised from B/.36,000 by Law 254/2023). Non-resident digital platforms (e.g., foreign app stores) must register for digital-services ITBMS where consumers are Panamanian residents. A PIF with only foreign subscription customers has no ITBMS nexus in Panama.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply is charged at 0% ITBMS with entitlement to input VAT refund where: (a) goods are physically exported from Panama under a customs export declaration; or (b) services, including SaaS financial-portfolio-tracking subscriptions sold via Stripe and Apple IAP, meet all four conditions: (i) the recipient is a non-resident, (ii) the benefit is effectively used or consumed entirely outside Panama, (iii) the service is not connected to Panamanian real property, and (iv) the consideration is paid from abroad in foreign currency. For the fintech application, DGI accepts geo-located digital evidence (customer billing country, IP address logs, platform subscription records, and the signed service agreement) to evidence offshore consumption. Sales to Panamanian resident users remain taxable at 7% ITBMS.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera - DUA) - for physical goods exports only
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing physical export movement (consolidated logistics file)
  • Commercial invoice (FACTURA) stating 'ITBMS 0% - Exportación de Bienes/Servicios'
  • Signed service agreement or platform terms identifying the foreign recipient and the place of benefit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank certificate) proving foreign-currency proceeds credited to a Panamanian bank account
  • Customer non-residency evidence (billing address, foreign tax identification, passport/apostilled certificate, or geo-IP platform report)

Mandatory e-invoicing (Facturación Electrónica en Línea - FEL) administered through the DGI SIF platform under DGI Resolution 201-4458/2023, with a phased rollout: taxpayers with annual gross revenue above B/.5 million (FY2022), B/.250,000-B/.5 million (FY2023), and all remaining taxpayers on the 2024-2025 DGI calendar. Statutory model: real-time fiscal clearance via SIF REST/SOAP web services; taxpayer software or a certified agente facturador submits UBL 2.1 XML invoices to DGI and receives an immediate Aceptada/Rechazada authorization; printed invoice must carry a QR verification code and DGI digital stamp (sello electrónico). Offline contingency billing is limited to 48 hours using a 'C' prefix. Monthly electronic declaration of sales and purchases ledgers (Libro de Ventas / Libro de Compras) and e-filing of the ITBMS return are mandatory.

Export refund claims (saldo a favor) are automatically selected for DGI documentary verification before payment. If the substantiation bundle is incomplete or inconsistent, DGI re-characterizes the supply as domestic: collection of omitted ITBMS at 7%, monthly late-payment interest of 1% (12% annual), surcharge fines of 25%-200% of the omitted tax (Law 254/2023), and revocation of exporter refund status. Deliberate or false export zero-rating constitutes criminal tax fraud (defraudación tributaria), punishable by fines up to B/.250,000 and imprisonment for up to 10 years in aggravated cases, with joint and several liability of Foundation Council members and potential seizure of the PIF's assets. Assessments may be reopened within the statutory prescription period (generally 5 years, extended in cases of non-filing).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United States Dollar is full legal tender in Panama; the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) circulates only as token coinage at a fixed 1:1 parity and is not an independent monetary unit. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible with zero statutory restrictions, no surrender obligations, no central-bank prior-approval, and no settlement delays. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal: Panama applies a territorial income-tax model, so foreign-source revenue of non-resident-owned Private Interest Foundations is not subject to Panamanian corporate income tax; only Panamanian-source income is taxable. Dividends paid from Panamanian-source profits attract a 10% withholding tax, and branches remitting to foreign head offices attract a 10% remittance tax, but a pure fintech software and portfolio-tracking PIF generating revenue offshore faces no Panamanian remittance tax. No currency-conversion friction exists because the treasury operating and reporting currency is USD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panama imposes no statutory timeline for the repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio for export or service receipts. Software subscriptions, licensing revenue, and SaaS disbursements (ISIC 6201) may be retained offshore, settled in USD, or converted into third currencies at the operator's discretion without any central-bank filing or foreign-exchange declaration.

Central bank supervision: Panama operates under full dollarization and has no traditional central bank. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Superintendency of Banks of Panama, SBP), established under Legislative Decree No. 2 of 2008, is the sole banking and AML/CFT supervisor. Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent of the State, while the Ministry of Economy and Finance handles public-debt and liquidity policy in coordination with the international banking system. There is no dedicated foreign-exchange administration, no exchange-control agency, and no central-bank approval requirement for treasury operations; monetary policy is effectively delegated to the United States Federal Reserve through the dollarized currency regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a collection sub-rail and LOW as a primary treasury custody layer. Panamanian law has no Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing category; only SBP-licensed banks may lawfully take deposits in Panama. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, and Revolut Business may be engaged as payment-facilitation counterparts for inbound software sales and outbound vendor disbursements, but they are unregulated from a Panamanian perspective and typically subject the PIF to their own heightened AML screening. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI account does not constitute a Panamanian bank account and cannot satisfy the foundation's banking-reference requirement; (ii) all corporate identification materials, including the Foundation Charter, Certificate of Good Standing, and the final-beneficial-owner extract, must be apostilled and provided to the EMI; (iii) funds left in an EMI at year-end trigger no Panamanian tax but expose the PIF to PSP freezing risk, FX drift, and card-scheme reserve holds. Recommended architecture: use EMI rails exclusively for daily collections from Stripe and Apple IAP, with daily sweeping into an SBP-licensed USD operating account.

Defense evidence: Source-of-wealth file: audited financial statements or certified tax returns of the settlor for at least three years, bank statements evidencing accumulation of the foundation endowment, sale and purchase deeds for real property or intellectual property, a sworn declaration of legitimate origin, and a PEP-negative due-diligence report issued by an SBP-accredited vendor within 90 days of onboarding.

Defense evidence: Foundation Charter containing a registered beneficiary schedule, Council Resolution listing all current council members and the protector with nationality and residential address, an extract of the Registro de Beneficiarios Finales under Law 254 of 2021, and an irrevocable undertaking to the bank that any change to beneficiaries, council members, or the protector will be notified within five business days.

Root cause: Unusual source of the endowment capital, including cryptocurrency, intangible property, or intercompany loans

Defense evidence: Asset provenance report: chain-of-title records for contributed intellectual property, an independent appraiser's valuation report, executed loan agreements and intercompany balance confirmations, licensed-venue exchange withdrawal history and trading statements if cryptocurrency was used, and the certified public deed of the capital endowment recorded at the Registro Público.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the settlor executes a certified capital-endowment deed, and funds flow by SWIFT MT103, or MT202 for larger interbank transfers, from the settlor's bank to the PIF's USD account at an SBP-licensed Panamanian bank. The wire instruction must include: (i) the beneficiary PIF name exactly as registered, (ii) the foundation registry number, (iii) a purpose code or field 70 statement such as 'Foundation capital contribution under Law 25 of 1995' or 'Foundation endowment funding', and (iv) a reference to the endowment resolution. For amounts up to USD 100,000, the bank typically requires only the Foundation Charter and Certificate of Good Standing; above USD 500,000 it requires a source-of-wealth questionnaire; above USD 1,000,000 it requires an independent source-of-funds audit. Inbound remittances must originate from a bank account in the settlor's own name to avoid third-party-funding rejections. No central-bank approval or foreign-investment permit is required.Outward payments are cleared through the Panamanian bank's USD correspondent, typically Citibank N.A., JPMorgan Chase, or The Bank of New York Mellon in New York, using SWIFT MT103 for operating disbursements such as cloud-infrastructure bills, licensing fees, subscription refunds, salaries, and vendor payments. Commercial Letters of Credit are available for infrastructure procurement and are issued via SWIFT MT700, with settlement on MT202 or MT103 over corresponding account rails. EUR-denominated disbursements settle via TARGET2 using SWIFT MT103 in EUR. Domestic clearing in Panama is fully USD-denominated through the local ACH operator and the Clave system, which process only retail USD payments; no Balboa non-cash settlement exists. Recommended treasury policy: maintain banking redundancy across two tier-1 banks, perform daily sweeping of EMI balances into the licensed account, separate the settlement account from the custody and investment account, and pre-approve MT103 batch files with dual-authorization thresholds.Offshore deliverable FX forwards (USD/EUR and USD/GBP) executed with the Panamanian bank's treasury desk or a US prime broker under an ISDA Master AgreementNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on Latin American emerging currencies (USD/MXN, USD/BRL, USD/COP) with tier-1 global bank counterpartiesCross-currency swaps (USD/EUR interest-rate and FX basis swaps) under ISDA and CSA documentation for multi-year subscription-revenue hedgingVanilla FX options and zero-cost collars for EUR and GBP flow protectionFX spot interbank executed through the bank's dealing desk strictly for settlement, with no speculative position-taking

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a collection sub-rail and LOW as a primary treasury custody layer. Panamanian law has no Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing category; only SBP-licensed banks may lawfully take deposits in Panama. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, and Revolut Business may be engaged as payment-facilitation counterparts for inbound software sales and outbound vendor disbursements, but they are unregulated from a Panamanian perspective and typically subject the PIF to their own heightened AML screening. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI account does not constitute a Panamanian bank account and cannot satisfy the foundation's banking-reference requirement; (ii) all corporate identification materials, including the Foundation Charter, Certificate of Good Standing, and the final-beneficial-owner extract, must be apostilled and provided to the EMI; (iii) funds left in an EMI at year-end trigger no Panamanian tax but expose the PIF to PSP freezing risk, FX drift, and card-scheme reserve holds. Recommended architecture: use EMI rails exclusively for daily collections from Stripe and Apple IAP, with daily sweeping into an SBP-licensed USD operating account.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends or profit distributions paid out of profits that have been subject to corporate income tax; 20% withholding on dividends and deemed distributions paid out of untaxed or non-taxed profits, including Panamanian-source earnings not previously assessed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared and filed when annual consolidated gross revenue exceeds B/. 1,500,000 (USD equivalent). Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds B/. 75,000,000. Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) is required when the group's annual consolidated revenue exceeds B/. 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama has adopted Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) under Law 254 of 2021 applying to entities tax-resident in Panama engaged in relevant activities such as financing, leasing, holding of shares/securities, distribution, and service provision. A PIF engaged in passive foreign portfolio tracking for non-resident beneficiaries, with management and control exercised outside Panama, generally falls outside the scope of ESR. If management, staff, or revenue-generating activities are relocated to Panama, the entity must demonstrate adequate physical premises, a proportionate number of employees, and substantial expenditures in Panama. The general anti-avoidance rule established by Law 76 of 2019 permits DGI to disregard structures lacking economic substance, reallocate income to the real beneficial owner, and disallow treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudacion Tributaria)

Statutory sanction: Surcharge of up to 30% of the evaded tax, moratory interest, administrative fines of B/. 50 to B/. 500 per infraction, and criminal penalties of 2 to 5 years' imprisonment when the evaded tax exceeds B/. 300,000; aggravated circumstances may extend the term.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false declarations or approve fraudulent filings are jointly liable for the evaded tax and civil restitution; criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment, travel bans, and disqualification from serving as directors of any Panamanian legal entity.
Shell Company / Artificial Structure

Statutory sanction: DGI may disregard the legal form, reattribute income to the beneficial owner, deny treaty relief, impose a fine of one to two times the reassessed tax, and refer the matter for criminal tax fraud prosecution; failure to report ultimate beneficial owner data to the registry carries fines up to B/. 1,000,000 per violation.

Director personal liability: Directors who maintain a dormant or shell PIF without substance while facilitating tax avoidance face personal civil liability for restitution of the underpaid tax; prosecutorial measures may include asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment for complicity in tax fraud or money laundering.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines scaling from B/. 10,000 to B/. 1,000,000 per violation, forced closure of the fintech platform payment accounts, asset forfeiture of funds linked to suspicious transactions, and criminal prosecution of responsible officers with imprisonment of up to 5 years for money laundering complicity.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are individually liable for AML failures; penalties include civil fines, disqualification from financial activities, travel restrictions, and criminal imprisonment where the platform knowingly processes proceeds of unlawful activity.
Improper Distributions and Corporate Veil Piercing

Statutory sanction: Civil restitution of the full unlawful distribution plus interest, disallowance of deductions in the PIF's account, and a penalty of up to 30% of the improperly distributed amount; criminal liability arises if the distribution is part of a tax evasion scheme.

Director personal liability: Panamanian courts may pierce the PIF's juridical veil where the foundation is used fraudulently or abusively; directors face joint and several liability for the corporation's tax obligations, civil damages, travel bans, attachment of personal assets, and imprisonment of 2 to 5 years where fraud is proven.
