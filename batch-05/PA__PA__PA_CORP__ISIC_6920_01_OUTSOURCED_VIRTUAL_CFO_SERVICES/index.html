Governing statute

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); official electronic taxpayer portal: 'Sede Electrónica' (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa), including RUC issuance, e-filing, and the Sistema de Facturación Electrónica de Panamá (SFEP) for fiscal invoice validation.

E-government filing portal

A Panamanian Sociedad Anónima obtains its statutory tax ID through the following sequence: (1) file the incorporación deed with the Registro Público de Panamá and obtain the registered corporate existence certificate; (2) appoint a resident agent and legal representative as required by law; (3) apply online through the DGI Sede Electrónica for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes), attaching the public deed, identification documents of officers, and proof of registered address, using the digital eSignature granted by the DGI; (4) obtain the municipal patent and Aviso de Operación from the competent Alcaldía (Municipality); (5) for offshore service sellers, request DGI authorization to issue zero-rated ITBMS export invoices and register in the exporter services sub-ledger. Non-resident shareholding is unrestricted; Panama imposes no minimum capital for the S.A. and the RUC remains active even with zero Panamanian-source activity, subject to annual information filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation_with_Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Elections statutorily available include: (i) the Small Taxpayer Simplified Regime (Régimen Simplificado) for taxpayers with annual gross turnover not exceeding B/. 300,000, offering consolidated quarterly compliance; (ii) the Multinational Company Headquarters regime (Sede de Empresa Multinacional, SEM, under Law 45 of 2012) for cross-border group service centers, potentially exempting certain foreign-source service income and ITBMS on intercompany services; (iii) advance pricing agreements (APAs) with DGI to stabilize intercompany margins for the fractional CFO services; (iv) preferential-zone elections (Panamá Pacífico, City of Knowledge) granting full income tax and ITBMS exemptions for qualifying service tenants; and (v) written advance rulings on territorial-source classification of export services. A standard S.A. with foreign clients and remote service delivery remains, absent election, within the territorial regime: only Panamanian-source income is taxed at the 25% corporate rate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General rule: compulsory ITBMS registration from the first taxable transfer or service rendered in Panamanian territory; there is no general de-minimis exemption for a Panamanian S.A. issuing invoices. The B/. 300,000 (≈ USD 300,000) annual gross income threshold defines eligibility for the Simplified Small-Taxpayer Regime rather than exemption from registration. Non-resident digital service suppliers owe non-resident ITBMS registration under Law 192 of 2021 from their first Panamanian-source digital service revenue. A virtual-CFO consultancy holding a Panamanian RUC must be ITBMS-registered and file monthly returns irrespective of whether its revenue is entirely export-oriented.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports qualify at the 0% ITBMS rate where all of the following are satisfied: (i) the recipient is tax-resident or domiciled outside Panama and does not maintain a Panamanian permanent establishment benefiting from the service; (ii) the service is rendered to and 'used or exploited' entirely abroad — remote fractional CFO advisory, multicurrency bookkeeping and digitized management reporting delivered by the S.A. from Panama to foreign clients satisfy this territorial-export rule; (iii) the supplier is an ITBMS-registered taxpayer in good standing; and (iv) the supplier retains a complete substantiation file proving the foreign destination and payment. Input ITBMS on Panamanian overheads (bank charges, subscriptions, local legal and accounting services) is creditable against output ITBMS, and net credit balances arising from zero-rated exports are eligible for quarterly refund (devolución) from the DGI. Although Panama's dollarized economy imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline, prudent practice requires documented receipt of the foreign currency consideration within 180 days of invoice issuance to survive DGI export verification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) — required where the service deliverable incorporates tangible media or goods moved outside Panama
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exit of any exported goods; for pure remote services, a signed service delivery certificate and system/email logs evidencing offshore delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a Panamanian licensed bank or a foreign-currency receipt confirmation evidencing the US Dollar credit from the non-resident client
  • Zero-rated ITBMS e-invoice generated through SFEP, quoting the foreign client's tax identification number and a clear territorial-export rationale
  • Executed service agreement and statement of work defining the foreign place of supply, beneficial use outside Panama, and payment milestones

Electronic invoicing is mandatory through the SFEP platform under DGI Resolution 201-4699 of 2019 and subsequent amendments. The S.A. must integrate its billing stack (including Stripe Billing and Wise payment records) with the DGI API over HTTPS: commercial invoices are transmitted in standardized XML format to the DGI before issuance for most registered taxpayers ('validación previa' real-time pre-clearance), the DGI returns a unique authorization number and a QR-coded fiscal seal (Sello Fiscal Electrónico) to be printed on the customer-facing PDF; smaller taxpayers may use 'validación posterior' batch upload through the Sede Electrónica. All e-invoices must be rendered in USD (Panamanian balboa is pegged 1:1), must identify the foreign client's tax ID for export invoices, and must be stored for the statutory six-year audit period; invoice voiding requires DGI-approved credit-note XML (NC) transactions.

DGI conducts targeted export-verification audits for service exporters claiming 0% ITBMS, with focus on false documentation, circular payments, and shell foreign recipients. Adverse findings trigger: (i) retrospective reclassification of the export as a taxable local supply — the entity must self-assess 7% ITBMS on the full invoice value plus statutory surcharges and moratory interest from the original filing date; (ii) monetary fines per the Código Fiscal up to 50% of the omitted tax and, for falsified records, fines of up to ten times the amount fraudulently refunded; (iii) criminal 'defraudación tributaria' liability — aggravated tax fraud may expose the officers and resident agent to imprisonment terms (up to 5 years under Panamanian tax criminal law) plus fines of up to three times the tax involved; and (iv) administrative clawback of refunds already paid with interest, suspension of the exporter registration, and revocation of the RUC's authorization to render zero-rated invoices for up to two fiscal years, effectively terminating the offshore services business model.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Panamanian balboa (PAB) is pegged at par to the US dollar, and the USD circulates freely alongside it. There are no exchange controls or restrictions on current-account or capital-account transactions. Non-resident shareholders may remit dividends, royalties, and liquidation proceeds abroad without prior central bank approval. Panama applies a territorial tax system; Panama-source income is taxed, but outbound profit remittances are generally free from withholding taxes when properly documented under domestic law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no statutory surrender requirement, mandatory repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio on export proceeds. Exporters may retain funds in any currency and maintain USD-denominated bank accounts subject only to standard anti-money laundering (AML) reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Banco Nacional de Panamá (fiscal agent) in coordination with the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); Panama has no traditional central bank and operates under a fully dollarized monetary system with the US dollar as legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Moderate. A mandatory independent audit is triggered when: the entity is a listed/issuer under the Superintendencia del Mercado de Valores (SMV); the entity holds a regulated financial, insurance, trust, gaming, or fund-administration license; the DGI expressly requires audited financial statements during a verification or transfer-pricing procedure; or a bank/financing agreement imposes an audit covenant. Unregulated service companies may conduct a voluntary audit at the directors' discretion.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax; 15% on the first USD 50,000 of net taxable income and 25% on the excess; qualifying micro-taxpayers may access a simplified gross-receipts regime of up to 2%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% general dividend withholding tax; reduced to 5% when the distributed profits have been fully subject to the 25% net corporate income tax; distributions of exempt or foreign-source profits may attract a 15.5% compensating dividend withholding where applicable.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Form 142 Transfer Pricing information return where annual related-party transactions exceed USD 250,000; full Local File / Master File documentation maintained where Panamanian gross income exceeds USD 3,000,000 (Panama operates in USD).

Economic substance scope: Panama does not impose a standalone Economic Substance Regulation filing comparable to BVI or Cayman; however, the DGI applies general anti-abuse and business-purpose doctrines, the SEM regime (Law 41 of 2007) imposes substance conditions for qualifying multinational headquarters, and the beneficial-ownership register under Law 23 of 2015 requires real presence indicators. A Virtual CFO S.A. must evidence local office, genuine employees, executed engagement contracts, local bank arrangements and decisions taken in Panama to defend its territorial-source position.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Evasion and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Tax surcharge of 25%-50% of the evaded amount plus 1% monthly interest; administrative fine up to USD 5,000; criminal fines up to 10 times the evaded tax and imprisonment of 2-5 years under Law 76 of 2019 reforms.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, surcharges and fines; precautionary attachment of personal assets, travel bans and disqualification from serving as directors of Panamanian companies.
