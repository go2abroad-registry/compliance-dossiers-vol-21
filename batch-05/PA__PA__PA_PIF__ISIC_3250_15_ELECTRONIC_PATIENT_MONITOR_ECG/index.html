Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama. Primary e-Tax portal: 'Sede Electrónica de la DGI' at https://sede.dgi.gob.pa ; institutional website: https://www.dgi.gob.pa . The RUC and ITBMS registration, SIFEL electronic invoicing, monthly returns, and export zero-rating controls are administered exclusively through this portal and the SIGA customs platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must follow a five-step national tax registration procedure. Step 1: Execute the notarized foundation charter (Acta de Fundación) before a Panamanian Notary through a licensed resident agent (abogado idóneo) and endow the foundation with its statutory initial patrimony; Panama's Law 25 of 1995 does not fix a statutory minimum capital amount, but the charter must identify an initial patrimony and practice commonly uses at least B/.10,000 (Balboa/USD at parity). Step 2: Inscribe the PIF in the Registro Público de Panamá (Public Registry, Mercantile Section) to obtain the juridical-person certificate, which is the pre-requisite for any tax registration. Step 3: File the DGI 'Solicitud de Registro Único de Contribuyente' together with the registered foundation deed, resident-agent evidence, and the economic activity code (ISIC 3250) to obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente); the RUC is the single statutory tax identifier used for income tax, ITBMS, municipal operating permits, customs declarations, and export documentation. Step 4: Register as an ordinary ITBMS taxable person (contribuyente) because the PIF will perform taxable transfers (assembly and sale of ECG patient monitors) in Panama; there is no separate VAT ID — the RUC includes the ITBMS registration. Step 5: Apply to the DGI for SIFEL electronic-invoicing authorization, obtain the digital signature certificate, and, if components are imported, register as an importer/exporter in the SIGA customs system. A PIF whose activities are purely passive and foreign-source may remain a non-active RUC holder, but active manufacturing and sales require full registration, monthly ITBMS returns, and annual income-tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama applies a territorial income-tax system. A Private Interest Foundation is a juridical person for all Panamanian tax purposes and is taxable at entity level only on Panama-source income; foreign-source income and foreign capital gains are outside the domestic tax net. Panama does not operate a domestic 'check-the-box' or pass-through election: the PIF is never fiscally transparent under the Código Fiscal. No hybrid-mismatch election is available under Panamanian domestic law, and classification conflicts arising abroad (for example, an IRS Form 8832 election treating the PIF as a disregarded entity for U.S. federal income tax) have no effect on the PIF's Panamanian territorial classification. Commercial elective regimes conferring tax treatment — the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime and the Colon Free Zone license — require separate DGI/MICI approval and do not alter the territorial-tax characterization. The only directly available elective simplification is the small-taxpayer ITBMS quota, available while annual gross receipts remain within the statutory ceiling.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized: the Panamanian balboa is a non-issued unit of account at par with the US dollar, and the US dollar is legal tender. There are no exchange-control restrictions on current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and capital repatriation may be made freely in USD without prior central-bank authorization, conversion tax, or mandatory exchange rate applied. The only practical hurdles are bank-level documentary checks for source of funds, sanctions screening, and final beneficial owner identification imposed under SBP anti-money laundering rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panama imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no time limit for retaining foreign currency. Export receipts for multi-parameter ECG patient monitors and related ISIC 3250 hardware may be held indefinitely in USD or any currency, and may be used directly for inbound component procurement, trade settlement, or treasury investment at the PIF's discretion.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) — the banking regulator and de facto financial supervisory authority in the absence of a central bank; Banco Nacional de Panamá serves as the State's clearing agent and fiscal agent under the Panamanian Monetary Agreement with the United States.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, with a defensive posture: offshore digital EMI accounts may be used only as auxiliary treasury tools, e.g. for procurement cards or low-value disbursements, after a formal banking relationship is established. Under Panamanian law, only SBP-licensed banks and authorized payment service providers may accept deposits or carry out payment processing from the public; an unlicensed foreign EMI operating as a primary treasury or clearing hub would trigger unauthorized banking activity and AML risk. Therefore SWIFT wire and documentary credit operations must sit with a regulated Panamanian bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% withholding on dividends distributed from profits already taxed at the entity level under the territorial system; 10% withholding on dividend-equivalent distributions paid out of exempt or preferentially taxed Panamanian-source earnings. Distributions of foreign-source profits, capital corpus, or PIF patrimony to beneficiaries are beyond the Panamanian territorial nexus and are not subject to withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual return due within three months after the fiscal year-end; for a calendar-year taxpayer, the filing deadline is 15 March of the following year. Quarterly advance payments are due on the last business day of the month following each calendar quarter (April, July, October, January).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 (Renglón 1) – Ingresos Brutos de Fuente Panameña All gross sales, service revenue, assembly fees, calibration fees, and incidental receipts derived from economic activity carried out, exercised, or realized within Panamanian territory. The non-resident PIF must report only Panamanian-source gross receipts; foreign-source manufacturing or delivery income under FOB or CIF terms is excluded and should be shown in the exempt/foreign-source lines, not in this box. Late filing triggers a surcharge of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction of a month, capped at 50% of the tax; failure to pay timely accrues default interest at the statutory legal rate plus a 10% delinquency charge; electronic filing violations produce administrative fines of up to USD 100,000 and potential visa/certificate restrictions for taxpayers in arrears.
Box 2 (Renglón 2) – Ingresos Exentos y de Fuente Extranjera Non-Panamanian-source income, passive foreign dividends, interest from offshore banks, gains on disposal of foreign-listed shares, and distributions from foreign subsidiaries or trusts. A PIF holding the ECG monitor IP and purchasing finished goods from an affiliate manufacturing site outside Panama must be ready to demonstrate that title transfer, negotiation, and execution of sale contracts occurred outside the Republic of Panama. Late filing triggers a surcharge of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction of a month, capped at 50% of the tax; failure to pay timely accrues default interest at the statutory legal rate plus a 10% delinquency charge; electronic filing violations produce administrative fines of up to USD 100,000 and potential visa/certificate restrictions for taxpayers in arrears.
Box 3 (Renglón 3) – Deducciones Atribuibles a la Renta de Fuente Panameña Cost of goods sold, direct materials, labor, depreciation of fixed assets physically located in Panama, rent, local professional fees, insurance, and other unavoidable expenses exclusively necessary to produce Panamanian-source income. Cross-border management fees, headquarters recharges, and intercompany IP royalties are deductible only if supported by a valid transfer pricing file and tax identification of the payee; undocumented offshore recharges are non-deductible. Late filing triggers a surcharge of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction of a month, capped at 50% of the tax; failure to pay timely accrues default interest at the statutory legal rate plus a 10% delinquency charge; electronic filing violations produce administrative fines of up to USD 100,000 and potential visa/certificate restrictions for taxpayers in arrears.
Box 4 (Renglón 4) – Renta Neta Gravable Box 1 minus Box 3, being the net taxable income computed exclusively on Panamanian-source items; losses from foreign-source operations may not offset this balance. A non-resident PIF with no Panamanian-source income reports zero here and may file a nil return; any temporary local activity must be itemized and reconciled with the quarterly advance payments. Late filing triggers a surcharge of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction of a month, capped at 50% of the tax; failure to pay timely accrues default interest at the statutory legal rate plus a 10% delinquency charge; electronic filing violations produce administrative fines of up to USD 100,000 and potential visa/certificate restrictions for taxpayers in arrears.
Box 5 (Renglón 5) – Impuesto Determinado (25%) Result of applying the 25% statutory rate to the net taxable income in Box 4. Small taxpayers under the simplified optional regime may apply the applicable flat rate when eligible. Non-resident PIFs cannot invoke foreign tax credits against Panamanian-source tax; the full 25% is payable, subject only to DTA reductions if the PIF is the treaty resident of a qualified jurisdiction. Late filing triggers a surcharge of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction of a month, capped at 50% of the tax; failure to pay timely accrues default interest at the statutory legal rate plus a 10% delinquency charge; electronic filing violations produce administrative fines of up to USD 100,000 and potential visa/certificate restrictions for taxpayers in arrears.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 1,000,000 annual aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions (goods, services, royalties, financing, and cost sharing) triggers the mandatory filing of the Panama Transfer Pricing Informative Return (Formulario 2025) and the obligation to prepare and retain the Transfer Pricing Local File under Executive Decree 192 of 2011. The DGI may demand contemporaneous documentation below the threshold when transactions involve non-cooperative jurisdictions, recurring losses, or gross margin anomalies.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not codify Economic Substance Regulations of the BVI/Cayman/UAE model; however, the DGI applies the OECD/BEPS substance-over-form principle and the place of effective management test under Article 6 of the Tax Code. A PIF seeking to be a principal entity or IP owner of ECG monitor supply chains must demonstrate in Panama: physical office or registered agent premises with actual utilization, local bank accounts, its own or contracted full-time qualified staff, board or foundation council minutes evidencing decision-making in Panama, and audited financial records. Absent this substance, the DGI may reclassify the PIF as a sham or `cáscara` vehicle and reassess its worldwide commercial flows as Panamanian-source income, or alternatively deny the penalty reductions available to compliant taxpayers.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Mischaracterization of Panamanian-Source Transactions

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the full Panamanian-source income at 25% plus 5% monthly surcharge (up to 50%), legal interest, and a flagship penalty of up to 200% of the evaded tax; criminal conviction under the Tax Code and Criminal Code carries fines and imprisonment of 2 to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Founders, foundation council members, and resident agent signatories who knowingly prepared false declarations face joint and several civil liability for the uncovered tax and penalties; the commercial court may order injunctions, asset seizures, and travel bans pending criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: The DGI may apply anti-abuse recharacterization and assess tax on the total profit at 25%, deny the application of the arm's length standard entirely, impose documentation penalties of USD 1,000 per month for each missing transfer pricing record, and reject APAs; the registration of the foundation may be suspended for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors or foundation guardians who allow the entity to be used as a sham risk personal liability for the unpaid corporate taxes of the entity under the doctrine of piercing the veil, plus disqualification from serving as resident agents and monetary penalties of up to USD 50,000; criminal charges for fraudulent insolvency may carry imprisonment of 3 to 6 years.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Registry Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to USD 1,000,000 for the PIF and its resident agent; cancellation of the PIF's inscription in the commercial registry; prohibition from opening or maintaining banking relationships; criminal penalties under Law 23 of 2015 for wilful non-disclosure carry imprisonment of 2 to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members and the registered agent are personally exposed to civil fines, revocation of their agency license, immediate freezing of personal and corporate accounts, and arrest warrants leading to travel restrictions should the registry violations conceal illicit enrichment or sanctioned-party dealings.
Fraudulent Profit Repatriation and Evasion of Dividend-Equivalent Tax

Statutory sanction: The distributing PIF is held liable for the unwithheld amount plus a 50% surcharge, interest, and a specific penalty of 100% of the tax evaded when the mischaracterization is found to be intentional; the beneficiary may also be subject to gross-up taxation on the entire distribution;

Director personal liability: Signatories of the foundation council who authorized the disguised repatriation are personally liable for restitution of corporate assets and tax debts; criminal courts may impose sentences of 2 to 4 years, occupational bans for professional directors, and preventive seizure of personal property, yachts, or other luxury assets; travel bans are routinely granted at the DGI's request during pending criminal tax proceedings.
