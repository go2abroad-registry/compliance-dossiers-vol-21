Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), under the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) of the Republic of Panama; primary portal is the SICE (Sistema Integrado de Consulta Electrónica) at www.dgi.gob.pa, supplemented by the DGI e-tax platform (Tasas y Trámites Digitales) and the Mandatory Electronic Invoicing Portal 'Sistema de Facturación Electrónica'.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF) obtains its RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) as follows: (1) Appoint and legally empower a resident representative or agent in Panama; (2) File the foundation deed (Escritura Pública), registered statutes, and FIJA (Ficha Única de Identificación) with supporting apostilled/consularised corporate documents; (3) Submit the online RUC application via SICE, including the notarial certificate of existence and sworn statement of the resident agent; (4) Obtain the provisional RUC and then the definitive RUC once DGI verifies the domicile and legal representative; (5) Simultaneously activate the digital electronic signature (Firma Electrónica) issued through the Autoridad Nacional para la Innovación Gubernamental (AIG); (6) Register for ITBMS and for mandatory electronic invoicing if the PIF undertakes any Panamanian-source commercial activity; the definitive RUC is typically issued within five to ten business days of complete submission.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — Panama operates a strict territorial source-of-income tax system. A Private Interest Foundation created under Law 25 of 1995 is treated as a hybrid entity whose foreign-source income is outside the Panamanian tax net; the PIF is not a pass-through for domestic purposes but is exempt from corporate income tax and ITBMS when it restricts its activities to offshore trade, provided it does not habitually execute commercial acts within Panama or derive income from Panamanian sources. Trading profits on sulfur sold to non-resident buyers and delivered ex-Panama are therefore not Panamanian-source and are outside the taxable base.

Available election forms

Panama does not adopt US-style check-the-box elections. Available statutory elections include: (i) election to register the PIF as an ordinary ITBMS taxpayer versus remaining a non-taxpayer entity for offshore-only operations; (ii) election to be taxed under the simplified micro/small-taxpayer regime where qualifying turnover limits apply; (iii) election to treat cross-border service supplies as 0% 'export services' under DGI rules; (iv) election for the transfer-pricing documentation framework for multinational groups; and (v) election by the founder to define the PIF's activities in the foundation charter so as to preserve territorial non-taxation. No hybrid-mismatch matching election exists in domestic law; Panamanian entities are subject to the OECD BEPS-aligned transfer pricing rules and to reporting under the Multilateral Convention for the exchange of information.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

9%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory de minimis turnover threshold: any person habitually engaged in the transfer of movable goods or the rendering of services in Panama must register for ITBMS, regardless of annual revenue. A simplified regime applies to qualifying micro-entrepreneurs with limited turnover, but such mini-taxpayer status is generally unavailable or inappropriate for international commodity trading entities. A PIF conducting only offshore operations and having no Panamanian-source transactions is not required to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The definitive export of tangible goods from Panamanian customs territory, including dry bulk granular sulfur, is taxable at 0% ITBMS. Eligibility requires: (i) the seller to be a registered ITBMS taxpayer; (ii) the goods to physically leave Panama under a valid customs export declaration; (iii) transport by a duly documented vessel or carrier; (iv) the buyer to be a non-resident party and payment to be received from outside Panama; and (v) possession of the statutory documentation bundle. Services that are rendered to non-residents and effectively consumed abroad are also zero-rated as 'export services' where the economic benefit arises outside Panama. Input VAT attributable to the exported goods may be credited or refunded to the exporter on application to the DGI.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduana – DUA) duly stamped by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas de Panamá
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or equivalent ocean transport document confirming the cargo destined abroad
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/MT103 credit confirmation evidencing receipt of the export proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (or official weight certificate for bulk sulfur cargo)
  • Independent surveyor's Certificate of Analysis and weight report (e.g., SGS) substantiating quantity and quality

Mandatory electronic invoicing applies under the DGI 'Facturación Electrónica de Panamá' framework. Invoices must be transmitted in XML format through the DGI's digital-authorization service (AET – Autorización Electrónica de Facturas) using the taxpayer's digital signature; the system validates the invoice in near real-time, assigns a unique authorization code, and applies digital 'stamping' before the invoice is legally effective between the parties. Taxpayers are required to integrate their ERP or billing systems with the DGI's web services/API, transmit all sales documents electronically, and report the resulting ITBMS obligations through the electronic themise return. Paper invoicing is not permitted for general-regime taxpayers, and failing to obtain prior electronic authorization renders the invoice non-deductible and subject to fines.

DGI audit procedures systematically cross-match the DUA, port/terminal records, the electronic invoice authorization codes, and the banking remittance evidence. Failure to substantiate the physical departure of the goods or receipt of the foreign currency within the applicable period leads to retrospective revocation of the 0% rate and reassessment at 9% ITBMS on the full sales value, plus default surcharges, statutory monthly interest, and fines for false or incomplete declarations. Where the exporter knowingly simulates exports or uses back-to-back documentation to manufacture a refund, the conduct constitutes tax fraud under Panama's evasion legislation and may trigger criminal prosecution, imprisonment, and confiscation of the underlying goods or proceeds; the DGI may also claw back refunds already paid for the full statutory limitation period.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy: the United States Dollar (USD) is legal tender of unlimited acceptance, and the Balboa exists only as 1:1 token coinage with no electronic market. Both current-account flows (imports, exports, servicing, profit remittances) and capital-account flows (PIF endowment contributions, loans, repatriation) are free of convertibility licensing, central-bank approval, or outflow quotas. Practical barriers are strictly bank-level: AML, CFT, OFAC, and beneficial-owner checks under Law 23 of 2015. Profit remittances from a PIF to foreign beneficiaries are non-Panamanian-source income and are not taxed or restricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Because domestic legal tender is already USD, there is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no conversion quota, and no statutory timeline for retaining or repatriating receipts from granular sulfur ore sales. The obligations are administrative: export declarations via the national customs platform (SICE) through a licensed broker; five-year retention of fully documented export dossiers (commercial invoice, bill of lading, LC/MT103, surveyor certificate) under Law 23 of 2015; and Currency Transaction Reports plus unusual-transaction reports to the UAF for cash or flows breaching the USD 10,000 threshold or showing pattern mismatches.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no traditional central bank with monetary sovereignty. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), established under Decree-Law No. 9 of 26 February 1998 (General Banking Law), is the sole prudential banking and FX supervisor. Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent and quasi-central clearing counterparty, while the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) serves as the financial intelligence unit. No foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control board exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: LOW for any unlicensed offshore EMI as primary treasury rail. Panama has no passporting regime for EU/UK e-money institutions; Decree-Law 9 of 1998 restricts deposit-taking and payment services to SBP-licensed entities, so a foreign EMI cannot open settlement accounts at Tier-1 Panamanian banks without local licensing. Medium viability exists only as a supplementary collection channel: an authorized overseas EMI may gather small B2B receivables provided funds are re-cleared into the Panamanian operational account within 24 hours and the flow is disclosed at onboarding. For volumes below USD 30 million per annum, reliance exclusively on domestic licensed bank rails is the compliance-optimal design.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound endowment or shareholder-loan wires are executed via SWIFT MT103 in USD, with the beneficiary styled as the PIF's legal name and the reference clause reciting the foundation charter article governing the endowment. The receiving Panamanian bank places the credit on manual compliance hold until the remitter name is reconciled with the UBO declaration and the notarized endowment deed; release occurs typically within one business day. No SBP approval is required, but amounts exceeding the bank's threshold (commonly USD 10,000) generate a Currency Transaction Record to the UAF. A shareholder loan requires a formal council-resolution and written loan agreement; interest is exposed to Panama's 12.5% withholding only if characterized as Panamanian-source income, so offshore trade-financing loans should be documented as used exclusively outside Panama.Trade settlement uses two rails: (i) documentary credit rail by SWIFT MT700 issued by the Panamanian bank in favor of the sulfur supplier against commercial invoice, full-set clean-on-board bill of lading, and third-party inspection certificate; transferable LC mechanisms (MT720/MT710) are used where the PIF acts as middle broker; and (ii) open-account rail via MT103 for freight to vessel owners, port agents, surveyors, and post-shipment settlement of receivables, with MT202 COV for interbank cover when the beneficiary bank demands it. Domestic USD transfers use Panama's local ACH processor (Centro de Procesamiento Interbancario) and are irrelevant to cross-border flows. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% on dividends paid out of Panama-source profits; the paying entity must withhold and remit the 5% to DGI, and the shareholder bears no further Panamanian tax on the dividend. Dividends wholly sourced from foreign-source profits are outside territorial withholding.
