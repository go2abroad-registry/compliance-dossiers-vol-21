Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The Foundation Charter must allocate an initial endowment (patrimonio inicial) of at least US$10,000 under Article 8 of Law No. 25 of 1995. Panama does not mandate an escrow account for a Private Interest Foundation; the endowment is evidenced by the founder's asset inventory recorded in the Charter, and no capital deposit certificate is required for registry inscription.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Operating Permit issued by the Cuerpo de Bomberos de la República de Panamá following on-site inspection of fire exits, extinguishers, hydrants, and emergency lighting; structural and load-capacity clearance of warehouse mezzanines and racking systems certified by a licensed structural engineer; and an Occupational Health and Safety workplace clearance from MITRADEL covering accident-prevention plans, first-aid equipment, employee training, and general industrial hygiene per Panamanian workplace regulations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) — official portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa (e-services: Sede Electrónica)

E-government filing portal

For a Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) or any foreign entity with a Panamanian tax nexus, the statutory tax identification number is the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente). The DGI registration is conducted via the Sede Electrónica de la DGI (online portal). Steps: (1) submit the notarized and legalized/apostilled Certificate of Incorporation (Acta de Constitución), and for foreign entities, proof of registration in the home jurisdiction; (2) designate a resident legal representative (agente residente) with a Panama address; (3) complete the electronic Form RUC-01, stating the entity's business purpose and legal form; (4) attest to the territorial source characterization of income; (5) provide supporting documentation for value-chain activities; (6) the DGI verifies the information and issues the provisional RUC within 5 to 10 business days. Non-resident entities without a permanent establishment may alternatively request a non-resident TIN for withholding tax purposes via the same portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a check-the-box election regime. Entity classification follows the legal form and the territorial source rules. A PIF can elect to be organized for either non-commercial or qualified commercial purposes; the latter subjects it to normal income tax and ITBMS obligations for Panamanian-source activities. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized. Consolidated taxation is available only to related corporations under specific conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.07%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 (equivalent to USD 36,000) in annual gross taxable sales; annual transactions below this threshold are exempt from VAT registration unless voluntarily registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical goods leaving Panamanian territory and services performed abroad or rendered to non-residents (with limited exceptions) are zero-rated (0%) for ITBMS. The exporter must hold a valid RUC, be compliant with electronic invoicing, issue an export invoice reflecting zero rate, and transfer the goods out of the customs territory within 30 days of invoicing. Certification by the customs authority (Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas de Panamá) is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación de Aduanas)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (Conocimiento de Embarque)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing foreign revenue receipt (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas)

Panama operates a compulsory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) regime for businesses designated by the DGI. The system requires real-time fiscal authorization: every invoice must be validated and an authorization code obtained via the DGI web service or an authorized fiscal printer before issuance. Taxpayers must integrate their ERP or billing systems with the DGI API to submit electronic transactions, and attach a digital stamp (Código QR) to PDF or printed invoices.

The DGI conducts substantive audits of exported invoices and import/export matching. Fraudulent zero-rating claims are subject to retroactive recovery of the tax plus surcharges (average official interest rate plus 10%) and fines of 100% to 200% of the tax underpaid. Directors and representatives may be subject to criminal tax fraud liability under Panama's Tax Criminal Law (Ley 20 of 2018).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The U.S. dollar is the national currency and there are no exchange controls. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible for legal entities, including Private Interest Foundations. Profit and dividend repatriation to non-resident founders/beneficiaries is unrestricted. Panama applies a territorial tax system; however, if a PIF directly carries on a Panama-source commercial activity, including subscription merchandising delivered to or managed from Panama, Panama-source income may be subject to ISR (Corporate Income Tax) at 25%, while foreign-source revenue remains outside the taxable base subject to proper records.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion exists. Export invoices may be denominated in USD, Latin American currencies, or EUR; proceeds may remain offshore, be credited to Panama bank accounts, or be applied for outbound supplier settlements without any obligation to convert into local currency. Panamanian monetary law requires only that bank accounting be expressed in USD.

Central bank supervision: There is no central bank in the Republic of Panama. The Republic is fully dollarized with U.S. dollars serving as legal tender; banking and foreign-exchange operations are supervised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Panama has no comprehensive electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime analogous to the EU/UK PSD2; offshore EMI accounts (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) are usable as collection/disbursement rails but are not recognized as full bank deposits and may be de-risked by SBP-regulated banks. Ongoing balances must sweep to a Panamanian bank account. Use of EMIs requires contractual evidence of platform ownership and verified KYC data flowing to the PIF's registered agent and bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (top marginal rate on taxable income exceeding USD 1,500,000; progressive brackets: 10% on the first USD 50,000 and 15% on USD 50,001–1,500,000)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% standard Panama withholding tax on dividend distributions made by a Panamanian entity out of Panamanian-source taxable profits; 5% applies where the distributed profits have been fully subject to Panamanian corporate income tax at the gross-up standard rate. Dividends paid by a PIF out of foreign-source subscription-box profits are not subject to Panamanian withholding tax because such profits fall outside Panama's territorial tax net.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before 31 March of the year following the close of the fiscal year (within 3 months after fiscal year-end for non-calendar fiscal years). A PIF must file even if all income is foreign-source and no tax is due, or file a zero/surveillance declaration where required.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD/PAB 250,000 — mandatory Local File is required for any Panamanian taxpayer (including a PIF) that carries out controlled or related-party transactions exceeding USD 250,000 in a given fiscal year. A Master File is mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue equals or exceeds USD 50,000,000, or where cross-border related-party transactions exceed USD 2,000,000. Documentation must be filed with DGI within 6 months of the fiscal year-end and updated annually.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not operate a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation, but Law 254 of 2019 and its implementing decrees impose economic-substance reporting and effective-management requirements on entities carrying on 'relevant activities' (distribution, head-office, logistics, treasury, IP holding, and digital-commerce support). A PIF commercially managing a subscription-box business with fulfillment or distribution decisions in Panama must: (i) maintain physical premises or contracted fulfillment capacity in Panama, (ii) hold board council meetings in Panama with real decision-making minutes, (iii) employ or engage a Panama-based administrator or compliance officer, and (iv) file the annual economic-substance declaration through the CIGEx portal (Centro de Información de Sustancia Económica) identifying the nexus and employees. Failure to demonstrate substance may cause the DGI to deny a Tax Residence Certificate, reattribute foreign-source income as Panamanian-source, and expose the PIF to back assessments with interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Evasión Tributaria)

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 100%–300% of the tax evaded plus monthly late-payment interest; under Law 66 of 2017, criminal prosecution applies where the evaded tax exceeds USD 50,000 in a fiscal year, carrying imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and fines equal to the evaded amount.

Director personal liability: The foundation council member, resident agent, or fiscal representative who signs the false return is jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax; personal travel bans (impedimento de salida), freezing of personal bank accounts, and extradition exposure attach upon indictment; courts may lift the PIF's legal shield and order civil restitution from directors' personal estates.
Fraudulent Return / False Documentation

Statutory sanction: DGI may impose fines up to USD 250,000, cancel the RUC, revoke the resident agent's professional license, and refer the case to the Public Ministry; document forgery is punishable under the Panamanian Penal Code with 3 to 8 years' imprisonment and disqualification from commercial activity.

Director personal liability: Directors, the foundation council, and the appointed Panamanian attorney/registered agent face criminal liability for forgery and making false declarations before a public authority, including imprisonment, civil restitution of the refunded or evaded tax, and professional disbarment; travel bans are routinely issued during the criminal investigation.
AML / Money Laundering (Law 23 of 2015)

Statutory sanction: FIBA may impose administrative fines of USD 10,000 to USD 1,000,000 per infraction for violation of customer due diligence, record-keeping, and suspicious transaction reporting rules; money laundering as a predicate offense is punishable by 5 to 10 years' imprisonment, with aggravated penalties of up to 15 years for professional or cross-border laundering networks.

Director personal liability: Beneficiaries and directors convicted of laundering face imprisonment, civil and criminal forfeiture of the PIF's assets and personal assets, redesignation as OFAC/SDN targets where US payment rails are misused, and cross-border travel restrictions; directors who knowingly signed incomplete beneficial-ownership disclosures are personally liable for restitution and subject to asset freezing orders.
Shell Company / False Economic Substance Declaration

Statutory sanction: Violation of Law 254 of 2019 carries administrative fines of up to USD 200,000 for false or materially incomplete substance declarations; the DGI may revoke the Tax Residence Certificate, reclassify the entire revenue stream as Panamanian-source, and assess the 10%/15%/25% CIT with statutory interest and a 100% negligence surcharge.

Director personal liability: The foundation council and registered agent are personally liable for certifying false substance reports; sanctions include professional disqualification, civil restitution of the forgone tax amounts, travel bans during administrative or criminal recharacterization proceedings, and imprisonment for perjury or fraudulent misrepresentation where the false declaration exceeds the criminal threshold.
Corporate Veil Piercing and Director Personal Liability

Statutory sanction: Panamanian courts may judicially lift the corporate veil under Law 32 of 1927 and the Judicial Code, consolidating the PIF's assets with the director/beneficiary's personal estate; the court may order reassessment of unpaid corporate income tax, the full dividend withholding tax, transfer-pricing adjustments, plus a 100% statutory surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are held personally liable for civil restitution of the unpaid taxes and damages; travel bans are imposed pending the fraud investigation; imprisonment of 3 to 8 years applies for fraudulent misappropriation and fraud against the fisc, with additional penalties for using the PIF structure to obstruct tax collection.
