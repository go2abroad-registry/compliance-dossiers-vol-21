Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (Panama); electronic portal: SITIO e-Tax (https://etax.gob.pa).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-founded Private Interest Foundation (PIF), the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) is obtained as follows: (1) execute and register the foundation deed (acta de fundación) with the Registro Público de Panamá; (2) appoint a resident agent (abogado idóneo) with continuing legal capacity; (3) file the electronic registration application through SITIO e-Tax requesting a RUC under the territorial-source taxpayer segment; (4) attach the registered foundation deed, identification of the foundation council (Consejo de la Fundación), and the authenticated appointment of the resident agent; (5) obtain the taxpayer's digital signature (firma electrónica) and DGI user credentials; and (6) open a US-dollar bank account in Panama to support payment rails and remittance certificates. A purely passive PIF holding foreign assets with no Panamanian-source activity is not ordinarily required to hold a RUC; registration becomes mandatory upon the first taxable act (ITBMS invoicing, customs declarations, or receipt of Panamanian-source income).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a check-the-box election. The PIF is mandatorily classified as a separate juridical person taxed only on Panamanian-source income under Law 25 of 1995 and the territorial rules of the Código Fiscal. Available practical elections include: (i) income tax computation under the standard net-income method versus the statutory alternative minimum method established by Law 66 of 2017 and DGI regulations; (ii) voluntary ITBMS registration when the PIF purchases local inputs and intends to recover input VAT against zero-rated exports; (iii) advance pricing agreement applications to the DGI for transfer-pricing certainty; and (iv) election of monthly instalment payment methods for corporate income tax. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are governed by BEPS-aligned general anti-avoidance and economic-substance provisions; no per-entity hybrid classification election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 0 – no statutory economic turnover threshold; ITBMS registration is mandatory upon the first taxable act performed in Panama. A simplified small-taxpayer regime is available where annual gross receipts do not exceed approximately B/. 36,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A PIF distributing excess multilayer ceramic capacitors and resistors qualifies for the 0% ITBMS rate where the goods leave Panamanian customs territory (FOB, CIP, or equivalent delivery terms to a foreign buyer) and title and risk pass outside Panama. The zero rate also extends to directly attributable logistics or freight services physically executed in Panama in connection with the export shipment. Input VAT properly attributable to zero-rated exports is creditable monthly and refundable by the DGI upon substantiated quarterly reconciliations. Domestic resale of the same goods to Panamanian customers is standard-rated at 7%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera – DUA) accepted and stamped by the Servicio Nacional de Aduanas (SNA)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal of the capacitors/resistors from Panamanian territory
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Remesa / bank deposit certificate) issued by the Panamanian banking institution
  • Certified SNA cargo manifest (manifiesto de carga) where applicable

Mandatory electronic invoicing under the Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (SFE) operated by the DGI. All active taxpayers, including PIFs engaged in taxable supplies, must issue invoices through DGI-authorized service providers (Proveedores de Servicios de Facturación Electrónica – PSFE). Each invoice is generated in XML format with a unique fiscal control number, transmitted via REST/SOAP API integration or the SFE web portal, validated near-real-time by the DGI, and stamped with a digital security code (código de seguridad). Export transactions are declared with the export code, and supporting declarations are filed with the Servicio Nacional de Aduanas (SNA).

The DGI may reopen zero-rated export claims for 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of omission or fraud. If export substantiation is deficient, the 7% ITBMS is retroactively assessed with default interest and late-payment surcharges; fines range from 50% to 300% of the evaded tax under Articles 737–748 of the Código Fiscal. Willful falsification, sham export documentation, or failure to repatriate export proceeds is criminally prosecuted under Law 76 of 2019 (Régimen Penal Tributario), carrying imprisonment of up to 8 years, disqualification of foundation council members, and seizure of exported inventory proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account. The Panamanian Balboa (PAB) is a purely nominal unit pegged 1:1 to the U.S. dollar and circulates only as fractional coinage; U.S. dollar banknotes are the circulating medium. There are no capital controls, no exchange-rate licensing, no prior-approval requirements for dividend distributions, loan repayments, royalty remittances, or trade settlements, and no restrictions on holding balances or contracting credit in any foreign currency. A territorial-tax-resident PIF engaged in passive electronic component distribution therefore operates with zero convertibility friction and effectively zero FX settlement risk in USD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panamanian law imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export or trade proceeds. Export proceeds may be retained offshore or in any currency. The only binding compliance constraints are AML-driven: cash and international wire transactions of USD 10,000 or more are reportable by the financial institution to the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) under Ley 41 de 2007 and Ley 23 de 2015; SBP Acuerdo 008-2020 additionally mandates complete originator and beneficiary information on all cross-border remittances.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) — Panama has no conventional central bank or dedicated foreign exchange administration. Monetary and banking regulatory authority resides in the SBP; Banco Nacional de Panamá (BNP) acts solely as fiscal agent of the State. The U.S. dollar is legal tender and effective monetary policy is delegated to the U.S. Federal Reserve system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium — legally constrained. Panama has no dedicated electronic money institution (EMI) or neobank license; Ley 81 de 2019 regulates low-value payment service providers but the SBP has not issued a general-purpose EMI charter. Accordingly, foreign digital EMIs (e.g., U.S. or E.U.-licensed platforms) are not SBP-supervised and cannot independently serve as the PIF's primary treasury counterparty; they cannot provide regulated deposits nor the target 'Credit_Line' rail in Panama. EMIs remain viable as supplementary, low-balance collection and payables channels for the Asia-Pacific electronic components trade, with mandatory weekly sweeps to the primary Panamanian bank account to preserve audit trail integrity and avoid UAF red-flag concentration in unregulated accounts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 1,000,000 in controlled transactions per fiscal year triggers the Local File; Master File is required when group annual consolidated revenue exceeds USD 50,000,000. Panama uses the US dollar as legal tender.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not maintain a general Economic Substance Regulation; however, the PIF must maintain effective management, local accounting records, an appointed Foundation Council, and a Panama Resident Agent. For treaty residence and to avoid anti-abuse recharacterisation, the PIF must demonstrate substantive business functions (inventory management, distribution, invoicing) in Panama rather than being a mere passthrough.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion penalties under Código Fiscal: imprisonment from 2 to 5 years and a pecuniary fine of not less than the evaded tax, up to 300%; the tax authority may also apply civil charges and attach bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members and signatories are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax; prosecutorial seizure of personal assets, judicial travel bans, and possible extradition in cross-border cases.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of intercompany deductions, imposition of transfer-pricing penalties of up to 1% per month on undocumented transactions, cancellation of the tax-residency certificate, and inclusion of the PIF in published shell-company risk lists.

Director personal liability: Directors may be made liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; repeated non-substantive operation can trigger criminal charges of aiding tax fraud, with travel bans and up to 4 years' imprisonment.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Disregard

Statutory sanction: Banco Nacional de Panama / UAF enforcement under Law 23 of 2015: fines up to USD 5,000,000, suspension of registration, and criminal money-laundering penalties of 5–12 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Personal asset forfeiture, freezing orders, travel restrictions, and piercing of the foundation's legal veil to confiscate proceeds where the Foundation Council knowingly enabled laundering.
