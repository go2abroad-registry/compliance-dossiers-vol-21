Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); official electronic fiscal portal: 'Sede Electrónica' of the DGI — https://sede.mef.gob.pa (RUC registration, e-invoicing authorization, and ITBMS/ISR filings) complemented by https://dgi.mef.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

For the foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF): (1) incorporate the foundation under Ley 25 de 1995 through a licensed foundation agent and record the public deed at the Registro Público de Panamá; (2) appoint a resident legal/fiscal representative with a Panama address and obtain the representative's Firma Electrónica (digital signature); (3) file the single RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) application with the DGI, attaching the foundation deed, foundation reglamento, identification documents of founder and foundation council, appointment resolution, and proof of the Panamanian fiscal address; (4) DGI validates the ISIC 3091 economic-activity code (manufacture of electric cargo bicycles and scooters), assigns the permanent RUC, and activates ITBMS status if annual turnover equals or exceeds B/.300,000; (5) register the e-invoicing authorization in the same file. The standard processing window is 5-10 business days.

Default tax classification

Available election forms

Panama does not maintain a US-style check-the-box regime or an OECD hybrid-mismatch election. Material statutory elections available to the PIF: (1) charter election under Ley 25 de 1995 (Art. 23) to exercise economic activities (manufacturing and trading) — once elected, the PIF becomes an ordinary ITBMS/ISR taxpayer on Panamanian-source activities while remaining territorial for foreign-source income; (2) simplified ITBMS regime election under Ley 8 de 2010 for taxpayers whose annual taxable turnover does not exceed B/.300,000; and (3) irrevocable reglamento election to classify distributions as tax-neutral foundation benefits payable to non-resident beneficiaries free of Panamanian withholding tax — the standard PIF structuring election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.300,000 (Panamanian Balboa, at 1:1 parity with the U.S. dollar) in taxable turnover during the preceding 12 months; taxpayers commencing activities must register prospectively when expected turnover will exceed the threshold, and may register voluntarily below it.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment under ITBMS (Código Fiscal Arts. 1061-1063; regulations of Ley 8 de 2010) applies to the PIF's electric cargo e-bike sales when: (a) the PIF is an active ITBMS taxpayer in good standing; (b) the goods are physically removed from Panamanian customs territory under a valid export declaration; (c) risk and title pass to a foreign buyer outside Panama under written Incoterms (FOB/CIF); and (d) the export proceeds are received through the Panamanian banking system in convertible foreign currency. Offshore and international services (transport, port, logistics, services consumed abroad) enjoy the same zero rate or are outside the tax. Zero-rating neutralizes output tax, preserving input-credit neutrality; an actual refund arises only when attributable input ITBMS on components exceeds zero-rated output. Panama's fully dollarized system imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day inward remittance benchmark is the customary evidentiary period for confirming export proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Formulario de Exportación) validated and exit-confirmed through the SISCOA/VUPE single-window system of the Panamanian customs authority
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the shipment from Panamanian customs territory
  • Zero-rated commercial export invoice bearing the DGI electronic-invoicing constancia/hash and the 0% ITBMS line
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificación de Ingreso de Divisas) issued by the collecting Panamanian bank for the export proceeds
  • Packing list and certificate of origin supporting the customs file (goods classified under HS 8711.60/8716; origin certificate where required by bilateral trade instruments)
  • Export sales contract and foundation board resolution establishing Incoterms, destination, foreign buyer identity, and the territorial-source evidence chain

Mandatory certified electronic invoicing for every ITBMS taxpayer, including the PIF: (1) prior DGI authorization of the invoicing platform; (2) issuance of electronic invoices (machine-readable XML-equivalent format) carrying a unique DGI-validated 'constancia' (digital control token) and QR stamp — each invoice batch is transmitted to the DGI in near-real-time through the certified electronic-invoicing API/SOAP endpoints of the Sede Electrónica; (3) a specific electronic export invoice (factura de exportación) with zero-rate line, linked to the customs export declaration in the SISCOA/VUPE single window; and (4) retention of invoice transmissions and accounting books for at least five years. Invoices not transmitted to, or validated by, the DGI are null for input-credit purposes and trigger anti-evasion penalties.

DGI export audits automatically cross-match zero-rated invoices against customs exit records (SISCOA) and banking-system inward remittance records. Any discrepancy reclassifies the transaction as a taxable domestic supply from the invoice date: the PIF owes the applicable ITBMS (currently 7%) plus 12% annual interest, a surcharge of 100% of the omitted tax, and fines of B/.1,000 to B/.50,000 under Código Fiscal Articles 765-768. Fraudulent zero-rating (invoice-only exports, re-importation after zero-rated export, falsified export declarations, or unpaid input claims) is aggravated tax fraud: criminal imprisonment of 2 to 5 years for the responsible foundation-council member or officer, nullity of invoices, revocation of the exporter registration and foundation commercial license, and joint and several civil liability of the foundation council to the DGI and the Empresa Nacional de Aduanas.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized economy: the US Dollar is legal tender for all transactions, and the Panamanian Balboa exists solely as a fractional coin unit pegged 1:1 to the USD. Both current account and capital account transactions are fully and freely convertible, with zero exchange controls and no surrender or conversion obligations. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, capital repatriation, and trade settlements face no statutory hurdles, prior authorization, foreign investment screening, or withholding consent thresholds. The only practical friction is bank-level compliance (AML/KYC and FATF-related enhanced due diligence, given Panama's historical grey-listing), which does not restrict convertibility itself.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for export receipts in USD or foreign currency. Exporters may freely hold, transact, or repatriate proceeds through local or offshore bank accounts without central-bank reporting or licensing. The only administrative duty is compliance with anti-money-laundering recordkeeping and beneficial-ownership declaration under Law 23 of 2015 and Law 254 of 2021.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no traditional central bank. The Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent, while the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) exercises prudential regulation and supervision over banking institutions under Decree Law 2 of 2008. The Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) administers fiscal policy. No foreign exchange administration authority exists because the US Dollar is legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Panama does not license a dedicated Electronic Money Institution (EMI) category; non-bank payment entities operate only as licensed 'empresas de remesas' (money remittance businesses) under SBP Circular rules and cannot open full transactional accounts or issue clearing-capable IBANs in USD domestically. Offshore EMIs (e.g., UK FCA, Lithuanian ECB passported, or class-3 EMI licenses) generally reject Panama-domiciled Private Interest Foundations as high-risk clients, and any virtual USD IBAN they issue lacks direct access to Panama's local ACH/SWIFT clearing. A compliant strategy is to pair a local Panamanian bank account as the primary clearing anchor with an offshore EMI solely for European corridors (EUR), provided the EMI accepts Panama country risk; otherwise, use traditional correspondent banking via tier-1 local banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections (equity contribution, shareholder loan, or working-capital advance from the ultimate controlling shareholder) must be executed via SWIFT MT103 from the funding bank to the PIF's USD account at the Panamanian bank. The wire must clearly specify: (a) in field 50K, the ordering party's full legal name and account number (matching the entity's UBO declaration); (b) in field 59, the beneficiary as the exact PIF legal name per foundation deed; (c) in field 70 (Remittance Information), a precise purpose narrative such as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION FOR MANUFACTURING WORKING CAPITAL – ISIC 3091' or 'INTERCOMPANY LOAN FOR TRADE DISBURSEMENTS – ELEC CARGO EBIKE' plus the referenced loan agreement number. The mandatory documentation bundle: signed board/council resolution authorizing the account opening and the specific wire; copy of the foundation deed and UBO registry certificate (apostilled); source-of-wealth and source-of-funds affidavits; loan agreement (if loan, with interest rate and term); and the latest financial statements of the transferring entity if the amount exceeds USD 250,000. Panama requires no central-bank approval or purpose-code filing, but the receiving bank will apply SBP's enhanced due diligence, and the outbound credit advice must be retained as part of the AML file.Trade disbursements for component procurement (typical suppliers in China and the EU) should be cleared through: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic-transfer payments under USD 100,000 per invoice, with field 70 describing the invoice number and Incoterms; (2) SWIFT MT700 / MT710 for documentary Letters of Credit for larger or recurring supply contracts, allowing sight drafts against presented B/L, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and quality certificates; (3) SWIFT MT799 / MT760 for standby LCs or guarantees supporting pre-shipment deposits. All disbursements must reference the underlying purchase contract, utilize standard 3-digit currency codes (USD, EUR, CNY when permitted), and maintain at least 2-of-3 signature authorization by the foundation council to satisfy banking control requirements. Panama does not license a dedicated Electronic Money Institution (EMI) category; non-bank payment entities operate only as licensed 'empresas de remesas' (money remittance businesses) under SBP Circular rules and cannot open full transactional accounts or issue clearing-capable IBANs in USD domestically. Offshore EMIs (e.g., UK FCA, Lithuanian ECB passported, or class-3 EMI licenses) generally reject Panama-domiciled Private Interest Foundations as high-risk clients, and any virtual USD IBAN they issue lacks direct access to Panama's local ACH/SWIFT clearing. A compliant strategy is to pair a local Panamanian bank account as the primary clearing anchor with an offshore EMI solely for European corridors (EUR), provided the EMI accepts Panama country risk; otherwise, use traditional correspondent banking via tier-1 local banks.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% flat Panama-source corporate income tax (ISR) for S.A.s, corporations and Private Interest Foundations (PIFs) deriving Panamanian-source income; Panama applies a territorial system, so income from genuinely foreign sources is excluded from the taxable base. The 5-year loss carryforward exists but no carryback is permitted.
