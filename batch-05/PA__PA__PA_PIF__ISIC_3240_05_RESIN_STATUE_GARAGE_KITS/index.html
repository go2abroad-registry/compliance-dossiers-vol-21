Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas de la República de Panamá; official taxpayer portal: dgi.mef.gob.pa; electronic invoicing module: 'Facturación Electrónica' operated by DGI; foundation corporate registry: Registro Público de Panamá.

E-government filing portal

A foreign beneficiary establishing a Private Interest Foundation (PIF, Ley 25 de 1995) must: (1) execute and notarize the Foundation Protocol before a Panamanian attorney and register it in the Registro Público; (2) appoint a resident agent (Agente Residente) and file the notice of commencement of operations ('Aviso de Operación') with DGI; (3) obtain the 13-digit RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), which is the sole statutory tax identification number, by submitting the foundation protocol, corporate documents, identity documents of the Foundation Council members, and a notarized power of attorney to DGI; and (4) activate the e-invoicing issuer profile and obtain a DGI-accepted digital signature certificate. Non-resident founders may hold all economic rights, but local tax registration is mandatory before issuing invoices, importing production inputs, or exporting finished resin statue products.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope - Panama levies income tax exclusively on Panamanian-source income. A Private Interest Foundation is a hybrid vehicle that is a distinct juridical person for contractual and customs purposes but is fiscally transparent with respect to foundation patrimony income: the foundation itself is not subject to corporate income tax on capital gains or passive foreign-source income, and tax liability is generally deferred until distributions are made to founders/beneficiaries and only then taxed to the extent the distributed income has Panamanian source. The foundation remains a separate and taxable person for ITBMS purposes if it conducts commercial activities in Panama.

Available election forms

Panama does not operate an IRS-style 'check-the-box' election. Available statutory elections and regimes: (i) optional opaque/transparent election for Panama trusts (Law 1 of 1984) and analogous PIF distribution characterization; (ii) Tax Stability Agreements under Law 41 of 2007 protecting against Ius Variandi; (iii) election to join the Simplified Small Taxpayer Regime (Régimen Especial de Pequeño Contribuyente) when annual gross turnover does not exceed B/.36,000; (iv) Multinational Company Headquarters (SEM) special tax regime for regional administrative and logistics services; and (v) hybrid-mismatch aligned characterization by structuring the PIF as a non-resident-owned passive investment vehicle with beneficiary-level taxation in the beneficiaries' home jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 (equivalent to USD 36,000) of annual gross turnover; economic operators below this threshold may elect the simplified small taxpayer regime, while any person habitually trading goods or services in Panama at or above B/.36,000 must become a full ITBMS registrant with periodic DGI filing obligations. Registration is also mandatory without regard to threshold if the entity exports goods, because export zero-rating substantiation requires an active RUC-linked ITBMS registry account.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% ITBMS applies to (i) the definitive export of movable goods physically leaving Panamanian customs territory, supported by a valid customs export declaration and proof of exit, and (ii) services rendered from Panama for use and benefit exclusively outside Panama. A PIF exporting high-end resin statue garage kits must be a full ITBMS registrant, correctly classify the merchandise under the applicable HS tariff heading, issue the zero-rated electronic invoice, and file periodic ITBMS returns in which export line items are segregated. Input ITBMS borne on local purchases of resin, pigments, molds and packing materials may be creditable against domestic ITBMS or claimed as an export credit refund through DGI's refund procedure.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Exportación transmitted through the DEX/SIUCA customs platform)
  • Bill of Lading (Master or House) or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Panamanian customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Foreign-Currency Credit Advice (Panama has no exchange-control regime, but DGI and customs audits require bank certificates evidencing repatriation of export proceeds where export consideration is received abroad)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list reconciled with the zero-rated electronic invoice and the preorder deposit documents

Real-time fiscal e-invoicing is mandatory for all Panamanian taxpayers (fully phased in as of January 1, 2023). Every invoice, including preorder deposits received through Stripe or PayPal, must be issued through the DGI 'Facturación Electrónica' platform or a certified billing system integrated with DGI web-service APIs. The DGI platform validates each document in real time and assigns a unique fiscal authorization number ('número de comprobante') before the invoice is legally effective; issuance modes are (a) the free DGI web-based facturador, (b) DGI-certified billing software using XML/JSON API submission, and (c) certified fiscal POS terminals for retail sales. Unauthorized or untransmitted invoices are legally invalid and cause disallowance of ITBMS credits. Advance consideration on order preorders must be invoiced as receipt-of-payment advance documents and subsequently reconciled by credit note/debit note at dispatch.

DGI and the National Customs Authority cross-match DEX/SIUCA export declarations, ITBMS returns, e-invoice fiscal numbers and bank inward remittance records. Unsupported, false or backdated export documentation triggers: (i) immediate reversal of the 0% rate and re-rating of the gross invoice value at 7% ITBMS; (ii) imposition of statutory monthly recargos and moratory interest on the assessed tax; (iii) administrative fines per Código Fiscal Articles 753-758, ranging from B/.50 up to B/.50,000 per infraction; (iv) customs fraud penalties under the Ley de Aduanas (Law 30 of 2008), including confiscation of goods and suspension of exporter registration; and (v) criminal 'defraudación fiscal' liability of two to five years of imprisonment where the export scheme is proven wilful. Assessments may be raised retroactively within the five-year prescriptive window, extended to ten years where fraud or non-filing is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully dollarized economy with unrestricted convertibility. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are free of exchange-control approval; profit remittance, dividend distribution, license fee payments, and capital outflows are unimpeded. The only bottlenecks are standard bank AML/KYC documentation and Panama's FATCA IGA (Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act intergovernmental agreement) and CRS (Common Reporting Standard) information-exchange filings, plus the territorial income-tax compliance for any income sourced and remitted inside Panama.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Panama imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds. The PIF may retain receipts in USD or any freely convertible currency; there is no 180-day foreign-exchange surrender window, no forced central-bank remittance, and no export-invoice conversion certificate requirement.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank; the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) supervises and regulates commercial banks, while Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the state's fiscal agent. The regime is a fully dollarized system: the U.S. dollar is legal tender, the Panamanian balboa exists only as coins at a 1:1 peg, and no foreign-exchange administration or exchange control authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility with strict legal constraints. Panama has no dedicated EMI (Electronic Money Institution) license; an offshore digital EMI may not solicit or service Panamanian clients without an SBP payment-service authorization, and most foreign EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer) do not maintain physical presence in Panama. The practical strategy is to use EMIs only as auxiliary payout rails, while holding the primary treasury account at a Panamanian licensed bank; the PIF should avoid routing the majority of liquidity through an unregulated foreign EMI to prevent FATF-driven de-risking.
