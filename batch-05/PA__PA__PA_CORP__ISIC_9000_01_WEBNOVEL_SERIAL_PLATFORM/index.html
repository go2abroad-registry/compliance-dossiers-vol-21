Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF); online taxpayer portal is the 'Sistema E-Tax' at https://etax.dgi.gob.pa, with general guidance at www.dgi.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

After incorporation of the Sociedad Anónima in the Registro Público, the resident legal representative (or a registered public accountant with power of attorney) must file the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) application with the DGI, submitting the corporate charter, appointment resolution, and identity documents of shareholders and legal representative. The DGI assigns the RUC number, activates the taxpayer as 'Activo', issues the RUC certification with authentication code, and sets the tax regime and CAIP activity code. The applicant must also obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación before starting operations. Foreign shareholders are not required to appear in person; the local legal representative completes the procedure. No minimum capital is required, and the entire registration can be completed electronically through Sistema E-Tax with digital signature.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box election akin to the US regime exists. The S.A. is automatically a separate taxable person taxed at entity level only on Panama-source income under the territorial provisions of the Código Fiscal (Arts. 694 and 700). A qualifying corporation may elect into the 'Régimen de Tributación para Operaciones Internacionales' (Law 254 of 2021) for defined international activities, subject to economic substance and notification requirements. Small taxpayers with gross income below the ITBMS threshold may elect the simplified small-taxpayer treatment (pago definitivo). Taxpayers may also elect between the general ITBMS method and the simplified ITBMS method where eligible, and may voluntarily register for ITBMS despite falling under the threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.07%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PAB 36,000 (equivalent to USD 36,000) of annual gross revenue from taxable activities; registration becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded or upon the first importation of taxable goods, while registration is voluntary below the threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% ITBMS rate applies to exports of goods and to services rendered to non-resident persons or effectively consumed outside Panama, including serialized web fiction subscriptions and in-app digital content sold to foreign users. To qualify, the taxpayer must be ITBMS-registered and demonstrate that the digital service was supplied to a non-resident customer and paid for from abroad. Evidentiary criteria include the foreign user's registration country, IP-address and geolocation analytics, foreign billing address, app-store payout remittances received from Apple/Google, and contractual terms disqualifying Panama-based consumption; the export claim must be supported by corresponding foreign exchange receipts and a reconciliation of platform gross sales against tax returns.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) where goods are involved; for digital services, the zero-rated export invoice and platform settlement reports substitute for the customs declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical exports; for digital services, platform download logs and app-store transaction reports serve as evidence of cross-border delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT credit advice, or app-store payout statement evidencing collection of the export price from abroad
  • Export invoice annotated with the 0% ITBMS rate and the customer's foreign VAT/identification number where available
  • Service contract or Terms of Use binding the foreign end user to consumption outside Panama

Panama has progressively mandated the 'Régimen de Facturación Electrónica de Panamá (RFEP)' pursuant to Law 8 of 2010 and DGI implementing resolutions. All ITBMS-registered taxpayers integrate with the DGI through the official 'Facturador Electrónico' (the DGI's free web facturador, a commercial certified facturador, or a direct API/web-service connection to the DGI's E-Tax clearance platform). Every invoice and credit note must be transmitted to the DGI in real time for automated validation; the DGI issues a unique digital authorization and tracking code (código de seguimiento) that must appear on the printed invoice alongside a QR code. For low-value consumer sales executed through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, the platform must issue a simplified electronic VAT receipt, referencing the app-store transaction ID, for each sale; munitions, alcohol, tobacco, and gambling carry differentiated rates (10% and 15%) not applicable to digital content.

The DGI may scrutinize export declarations, ITBMS refund claims, and digital platform records; where zero-rating is claimed without adequate proof of foreign consumption or the service is found to be enjoyed in Panama, the DGI retroactively assesses the 7% ITBMS plus monthly interest and surcharges, with surcharges of up to 200% of the omitted tax for fraudulent or bad-faith filings. Fines per unsubstantiated invoice typically range from PAB 100 to PAB 10,000, the taxpayer's export authorization may be suspended, and the knowing issuance of false export documentation triggers criminal prosecution under Law 14 of 2004 (tax criminal code), which carries custodial penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility is inherent as the US dollar circulates as legal tender; the Panamanian Balboa exists only as a fractional unit at a 1:1 peg. Both current and capital account transactions are unrestricted with no prior approval, exchange control, or profit remittance restrictions. Dividends, royalties, and service fees may be freely transferred abroad.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender requirements, mandatory conversion ratios, or timelines for export/earnings repatriation. All proceeds may be retained in any currency and remitted without central bank approval, consistent with Panama's dollarized, open capital account regime.

Central bank supervision: Panama does not have a central bank; the US dollar is legal tender under the Monetary Agreement with the United States. Banking activities are supervised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) oversees fiscal matters. No foreign exchange administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High, subject to legal constraints. Panama's regulatory framework does not yet explicitly recognize standalone 'electronic money institutions' (EMIs). Digital payment providers generally require a banking license or an alliance with a licensed bank. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., non-Panamanian entities) cannot directly hold accounts in Panama's domestic banking system without a local presence or partnered bank. Operational viability is moderate for cross-border settlement via prepaid cards and payment gateways, but restrictions apply to accepting deposits or issuing electronic money without SBP authorization.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections (shareholder loans or equity) must be routed via standard SWIFT wire transfers to the Panama corporate account. No FX approval is required. The remittance should include clear payment purpose codes (e.g., 'Investment – Share Capital' and 'Intragroup Loan') and reference the Panamanian entity's RUC (tax ID). Supporting documents (shareholder resolution, loan agreement, or capital contribution deed) must be retained to comply with bank KYC/AML verification. The bank will typically perform a 'source of wealth' inquiry for amounts above USD 10,000 and may place a temporary hold pending compliance review.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements use SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers and Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) for documentary transactions. MT103 messages require detailed beneficiary bank codes (BIC/IBAN), and payment purpose codes aligned with the OCIMF/ISO 20022 standard (e.g., 'Royalty Payment', 'Digital Content License Fee', 'Software Services') when the bank supports structured remittance. For higher-value payments (e.g., content licensing to foreign authors or platform technology providers), banks may request underlying contracts and proof of service delivery. Commercial LC issuance is feasible for physical trade, but for digital web novel subscriptions, MT103 wires are the standard clearing rail.Plain-vanilla deliverable forwards (FX forwards) between USD and major currencies, executed via SBP-regulated banks or international broker dealers without requiring prior authorization.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for exotic-currency exposure (e.g., emerging market currencies) may be arranged by international banks, provided the Panamanian entity is a professional counterparty and the bank's internal suitability criteria are met.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps / interest rate swaps) are permissible for treasury management, subject to standard documentation (ISDA Master Agreement) and regulatory compliance under Panamanian banking practice.Options on foreign currencies (call/put options) are available through licensed international brokers. No derivative transaction is subject to exchange control approval in Panama.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends paid out of Panamanian-source taxable profits (5% for dividends from shares registered on a recognized Panamanian or regional stock exchange); 10% withholding also applies to dividends paid out of tax-exempt income; dividends sourced entirely from foreign-source profits are exempt from Panamanian dividend tax.

Box 1 - Ingresos brutos Total gross revenue recognized under Panama GAAP, including all worldwide receipts before deductions; for the webnovel platform, include App Store and Google Play settlement amounts (net of platform commissions) plus advertisement and licensing revenue. If the non-resident beneficial owner directly earns subscription income through a Panamanian S.A. acting as a conduit, DGI may recharacterize; the gross amount must be reported regardless of where the foreign shareholders reside. Late filing or non-filing: monthly surcharge of 10% of the tax due per month or fraction, capped at 50% of the tax; late payment interest accrues at the statutory rate referenced to the Banco Nacional de Panamá rate; repeated non-filing within 12 months triggers an additional administrative fine up to USD 5,000, the suspension of the paz y salvo (tax clearance), and the loss of good standing with the Public Registry; continued delinquency may lead to administrative dissolution and referral to the tax crimes prosecutor.
Box 2 - Ingresos exentos y no gravables Foreign-source income excluded under Panama's territorial tax regime; includes subscription and digital content revenue derived from services rendered and economically utilized outside Panama, supported by contracts, user location data, and payment-rail settlement reports. Exemption is allowed only where the service is rendered abroad and the payor is located abroad. App Store revenues from Panamanian consumers must be reclassified as Panamanian-source and taxed at 25%; documentary substantiation (geo-IP, customer billing addresses, foreign content distribution agreements) must be retained. Late filing or non-filing: monthly surcharge of 10% of the tax due per month or fraction, capped at 50% of the tax; late payment interest accrues at the statutory rate referenced to the Banco Nacional de Panamá rate; repeated non-filing within 12 months triggers an additional administrative fine up to USD 5,000, the suspension of the paz y salvo (tax clearance), and the loss of good standing with the Public Registry; continued delinquency may lead to administrative dissolution and referral to the tax crimes prosecutor.
Box 4 - Costos y gastos Deductible operating expenditures directly attributable to the production of gross income: content author royalties, translation and editing costs, server hosting, app maintenance, marketing, legal, and administrative expenses. Expenses are deductible only when economically linked to Panamanian-source income. If the entity's foreign-source income is exempt, related expenses must be proportionally excluded. Payments to foreign related parties for royalties or services must be arm's length and supported by transfer pricing documentation. Late filing or non-filing: monthly surcharge of 10% of the tax due per month or fraction, capped at 50% of the tax; late payment interest accrues at the statutory rate referenced to the Banco Nacional de Panamá rate; repeated non-filing within 12 months triggers an additional administrative fine up to USD 5,000, the suspension of the paz y salvo (tax clearance), and the loss of good standing with the Public Registry; continued delinquency may lead to administrative dissolution and referral to the tax crimes prosecutor.
Box 6 - Pérdidas compensables Net operating loss deduction (NOL) carried forward from prior fiscal years; Panama permits a maximum carryforward of 5 years. NOLs arising in Panama may offset only Panamanian-source income; losses of foreign branches or foreign disregarded entities cannot be transferred to the Panamanian S.A. Late filing or non-filing: monthly surcharge of 10% of the tax due per month or fraction, capped at 50% of the tax; late payment interest accrues at the statutory rate referenced to the Banco Nacional de Panamá rate; repeated non-filing within 12 months triggers an additional administrative fine up to USD 5,000, the suspension of the paz y salvo (tax clearance), and the loss of good standing with the Public Registry; continued delinquency may lead to administrative dissolution and referral to the tax crimes prosecutor.
Box 7 - Renta neta gravable Net taxable income after exempt revenues and allowable deductions; this is the tax base for the progressive corporate rate. A non-resident company without a permanent establishment in Panama is not assessed on Panama-source income unless it operates through a fixed base; the resident S.A. must nevertheless compute this line under the territorial rules. Late filing or non-filing: monthly surcharge of 10% of the tax due per month or fraction, capped at 50% of the tax; late payment interest accrues at the statutory rate referenced to the Banco Nacional de Panamá rate; repeated non-filing within 12 months triggers an additional administrative fine up to USD 5,000, the suspension of the paz y salvo (tax clearance), and the loss of good standing with the Public Registry; continued delinquency may lead to administrative dissolution and referral to the tax crimes prosecutor.
Box 8 - Impuesto computado Tax at 15% on the first USD 50,000 of net taxable income and 25% on the excess over USD 50,000. Non-resident shareholders cannot offset their home-country tax credits against Panama's corporate ITBMS credited; the corporate tax is an entity-level liability. Late filing or non-filing: monthly surcharge of 10% of the tax due per month or fraction, capped at 50% of the tax; late payment interest accrues at the statutory rate referenced to the Banco Nacional de Panamá rate; repeated non-filing within 12 months triggers an additional administrative fine up to USD 5,000, the suspension of the paz y salvo (tax clearance), and the loss of good standing with the Public Registry; continued delinquency may lead to administrative dissolution and referral to the tax crimes prosecutor.
Box 9 - Créditos y retenciones Credits including quarterly advance payments (pagos directos), third-party withholding certificates, and ITBMS credits deductible against income tax. Foreign tax credits are not granted by Panama; credit is limited to domestic withholdings and presumed dividend credits. Payments made to non-residents without Panamanian withholding will not generate creditable withholding. Late filing or non-filing: monthly surcharge of 10% of the tax due per month or fraction, capped at 50% of the tax; late payment interest accrues at the statutory rate referenced to the Banco Nacional de Panamá rate; repeated non-filing within 12 months triggers an additional administrative fine up to USD 5,000, the suspension of the paz y salvo (tax clearance), and the loss of good standing with the Public Registry; continued delinquency may lead to administrative dissolution and referral to the tax crimes prosecutor.
Box 10 - Impuesto a pagar / saldo a favor Excess of Box 8 over Box 9; balance due must be paid on filing. A saldo a favor may be claimed as a credit or refund via separate DGI procedure. The company cannot avoid tax by retaining earnings; undistributed taxable profits may trigger the presumed-dividend tax (10%) of the annual taxable income when distributions are made to foreign shareholders. Late filing or non-filing: monthly surcharge of 10% of the tax due per month or fraction, capped at 50% of the tax; late payment interest accrues at the statutory rate referenced to the Banco Nacional de Panamá rate; repeated non-filing within 12 months triggers an additional administrative fine up to USD 5,000, the suspension of the paz y salvo (tax clearance), and the loss of good standing with the Public Registry; continued delinquency may lead to administrative dissolution and referral to the tax crimes prosecutor.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD (Balboa at 1:1 with USD). Mandatory Local File: taxpayer whose gross income or total assets exceeded USD 2,000,000 in the fiscal year, or which transacts with related parties. Mandatory Master File: multinational group whose consolidated revenue exceeded USD 50,000,000. Country-by-Country Report: group consolidated revenue of at least USD 850,000,000. Documentation must be prepared within 6 months after the fiscal year-end and submitted to DGI upon request.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Requirements (Law 70 of 2019, effective for fiscal years beginning after 1 July 2020) apply to entities deriving income from relevant activities, including IP holding, distribution and services, and pure equity holding. A webnovel platform S.A. exploiting copyrighted content and earning subscription/licensing income is in scope. The entity must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (contract negotiation, billing, IP exploitation, server/content management decisions) are conducted in Panama, maintain a physical office and adequate qualified staff, and file the annual Declaration of Economic Substance with DGI. Non-compliance may trigger penalties up to USD 500,000, and the DGI may notify the Public Registry, effectively blocking the entity from conducting commercial activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction carries imprisonment of 2 to 5 years; financial penalty of up to 3 times the evaded tax; monthly surcharges of 10% (capped at 50%) plus late-payment interest; DGI has the power to embargo bank accounts and publish the taxpayer on the official delinquency registry.

Director personal liability: Directors and the legal representative who signed false returns face personal criminal prosecution, civil restitution of evaded taxes, asset embargoes, and travel bans (salida del país) imposed by the Fiscal Crimes Prosecutor while proceedings are pending.
Transfer Pricing / Royalty Stripping

Statutory sanction: DGI recharacterizes the related-party consideration, assesses the adjustment at the 25% corporate rate, applies a surcharge of 40% of the understatement where TP documentation was not filed, and imposes fines up to USD 50,000 for non-submission of the TP return or documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved non-arm's length agreements are jointly liable for the additional corporate tax; if the company is insolvent, directors face civil liability under the S.A. law for breach of fiduciary duty and fraudulent transfer actions.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: ESR monetary penalties from USD 5,000 to USD 500,000 per fiscal year; repeat or aggravated offenses can lead to the suspension of the commercial matrícula, the loss of tax residence status, and delisting from the Public Registry.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent are jointly and severally liable for the ESR annual filing obligation; the resident agent faces administrative sanctions and potential loss of accreditation, while directors may be personally assessed for penalties if they concealed the entity's activity.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Monetary fines from USD 1,000 to USD 50,000 for false, late, or omitted beneficial ownership declarations; where the structure facilitates money laundering, Law 23 of 2015 (as reformed) imposes imprisonment of 5 to 10 years and fines up to USD 5,000,000; the bank account and the corporation are listed on the UAF financial intelligence watchlist.

Director personal liability: Directors, the resident agent, and any shareholder acting as a front signatory face criminal prosecution for money laundering and organized crime participation; the corporate veil is pierced for restitution, funds are seized, and principals are subject to INTERPOL red notices and extradition proceedings.
Dividend WHT and Distribution Compliance

Statutory sanction: DGI assesses against the company the full 10% dividend withholding tax, plus monthly surcharges of 10% capped at 50% and statutory interest; the presumed-dividend regime may tax 10% of an imputed profit base when no timely return was filed; criminal referral follows for intentional omission.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable as withholding agents ('agentes de retención') for the un-withheld tax; DGI may initiate civil recovery against directors' personal assets, obtain travel bans, and, in cases of fraudulent bankruptcy, request imprisonment of up to 4 years.
ITBMS (VAT) Digital Services Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Assessment of 7% ITBMS on taxable Panamanian digital sales, monthly surcharges of 5% up to a maximum of 100%, and fines for omitted tax up to 100% of the liability on repeated violations; DGI may order the payment gateways to suspend distribution of settlement funds.

Director personal liability: The legal representative is jointly responsible for ITBMS remittances and faces criminal charges for destruction of consumption records and false invoicing; civil liability extends to the Board where revocation of the paz y salvo blocks future repatriation of dividends.
