Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), República de Panamá; official portal https://www.dgi.gob.pa; e-filing via E-Tax 2.0/SITWEB; electronic invoice clearance via SIFE (Sistema de Facturación Electrónica).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Panama S.A. obtains its statutory tax identifier (RUC — Registro Único de Contribuyentes) in the following sequence: (1) incorporation by Panamanian notary and inscription in the Registro Público de Panamá; (2) appointment of a resident legal representative and of a registered agent (agente residente) establishing a valid tax domicile in Panama; (3) filing of the Aviso de Operación (commencement-of-operations notice) before the DGI through the E-Tax/SITWEB portal; (4) issuance by DGI of the definitive RUC certificate; (5) procurement of the qualified electronic signature/digital certificate required for SIFE e-invoicing; and (6) completion of the ITBMS registration module (empresario) where taxable supplies will be made in or from Panama. For a purely extraterritorial holding/export vehicle, the same steps in abridged form apply, but the entity must still maintain a Panama-based resident agent and an up-to-date RUC file.

For a purely extraterritorial holding/export vehicle, the same steps in abridged form apply, but the entity must still maintain a Panama-based resident agent and an up-to-date RUC file.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No pass-through/check-the-box election exists for a Panama S.A.; the entity is taxable at entity level solely on Panama-source income and is not taxed on foreign-source income. The principal statutory elections available are: (i) selection of the simplified small-taxpayer regime (Régimen de Pequeños Contribuyentes) where annual gross revenue does not exceed B/. 36,000; (ii) admission to the Panama-Pacifico Special Economic Zone (Ley 41 de 2007) conferring income-tax, ITBMS and municipal-tax exemptions; (iii) the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime (Ley 41 de 2007) permitting 0% ITBMS on qualifying cross-border services; and (iv) hybrid-mismatch-driven elections in structuring the MNE chain such that foreign dividends, branch profits and capital gains sourced abroad remain outside Panamanian gross income under the territorial law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 36,000 (Panamanian balboa at 1:1 parity with the U.S. dollar) of estimated annual gross turnover: taxpayers whose supplies will exceed this amount are required to operate as regular ITBMS taxpayers and issue authorized fiscal invoices; taxpayers at or below this level may use the simplified small-taxpayer regime. Non-resident digital service platforms supplying Panamanian consumers are subject to mandatory ITBMS collection/registration even without a physical presence when their Panamanian-sourced digital supplies exceed the statutory threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and qualified offshore services are subject to ITBMS at the zero rate (0%). For goods, zero-rating requires the products to leave Panamanian customs territory and be covered by an accepted export customs declaration. For cross-border services — including stock-photo licensing and AI-generated visual-asset distribution — the zero rate applies only where the customer is a non-resident, the service is consumed and economically exploited abroad, the consideration is paid from abroad, and the income is remitted into the Panamanian banking system. Digital services that effectively benefit a Panamanian user cannot be zero-rated and remain within the standard 7% tax base.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Exportación / DUE) approved by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas
  • Bill of Lading (Conocimiento de Embarque) or Air Waybill evidencing physical exit of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice) demonstrating remittance of export proceeds in foreign currency into the Panamanian bank account
  • Commercial invoice and e-invoice bearing the 0% ITBMS export legend
  • Executed contract and customer certificate of non-residency for cross-border digital-asset services
  • Packing list, weight note, or electronic delivery log evidencing the foreign destination of the exported media/content

Panama operates a phased mandatory electronic invoicing mandate implemented through the DGI SIFE platform (Sistema de Facturación Electrónica de la DGI). Taxpayers transmit invoice data to DGI in XML/JSON through certified REST/SOAP API web services, obtain DGI authorization (digital stamp/sello digital) before invoice delivery in normal flow, and embed a validation QR code plus a qualified electronic signature on each e-invoice. Offline/contingency issuance is limited to a maximum of 24 hours, after which the invoice must be transmitted and sealed. Fiscal printers are accepted only for small taxpayers on a diminishing basis, and the DGI may suspend fiscal authorization where clearance APIs fail repeatedly.

Zero-rating claims are scrutinized by DGI and customs through bank remittance matching, customs-exit verification, and foreign-beneficial-use testing for digital services. An unsubstantiated or simulated export triggers immediate reversal of the zero rating with clawback of the 7% ITBMS otherwise payable, recapture of input ITBMS credits previously deducted on export-related costs, a statutory recargo of 10% on the omitted tax, monthly interest of 1% or fraction thereof, and fines of 50%–150% of the evaded amount. The filing of false export declarations is a criminal tax offense under Ley 70 de 2019 (delitos tributarios), carrying imprisonment of 2–5 years, fines equal to one to two times the tax deficit, and personal civil liability of the directors and the resident agent; DGI may also suspend the taxpayer's RUC and SIFE authorization pending full repayment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized: the US Dollar is unrestricted legal tender in parallel with the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) at an immovable 1:1 exchange rate, and the Balboa circulates only as fractional coinage. There are no exchange controls, no capital-account or current-account restrictions, no prior-authorization regimes, no conversion obligations, and no quantitative limits on any cross-border transfer. Capital contributions, digital-marketplace revenue settlements, royalty licencing payments, dividend distributions, intra-group loan repayments, and sale-of-business proceeds are freely convertible and transferable in either direction. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders faces no FX hurdle and no special tax clearance; the only legal limitations are (i) the standard Panamanian dividend withholding rules applicable strictly to profits derived from Panama-source activities (foreign-source income is outside the territorial tax base), and (ii) bank-level documentary gates such as board minutes, fiscal good-standing certificates, and Law No. 23 of 2015 ultimate-beneficial-owner declarations. For a stock-photo and generative-asset licencing entity operating through a Panama S.A., worldwide digital revenue routed to a USD account is fully convertible without central-bank intervention.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds, digital-service receipts, stock-photo royalties, or subscription/AI-asset licencing fees. The treasury may retain 100% of proceeds in USD or any other currency, disburse them abroad immediately, and maintain offshore balances without any central-bank reporting. The only reporting duties are standard AML thresholds (cash transactions at or above USD 10,000 and suspicious-transaction reports to the Unidad de Análisis Financiero), which are unrelated to FX surrender.

Central bank supervision: Banco Nacional de Panamá (National Bank of Panama) as fiscal agent of the State; prudential and AML/CFT supervision is exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Panama Banking Superintendency, SBP) under Decree-Law No. 3 of 1985, as amended. Panama has no traditional central bank, no foreign-exchange administration, and no monetary or FX policy authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High as an ancillary payment gateway; Low as a primary treasury host. Panama law does not recognise the EU/UK electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence category: any issuance of electronic money callable at par in Panama requires a licence or authorisation from the SBP, and offshore EMIs cannot lawfully provide regulated e-money services to the public inside Panama or hold the Panama S.A.'s principal settlement balances. The legally viable blueprint is to use licensed cross-border PSPs and EMIs (Stripe, PayPal, Wise Platform, Payoneer) strictly as collection forwarders that aggregate worldwide card/alternative-payment-method transactions in the United States or EU and wire net USD proceeds into the Panama operating account at a tier-1 local bank via SWIFT. This structure is compliant because the EMI performs payment facilitation in its own jurisdiction and never acts as a Panamanian bank. Banks will treat PSP-cleared flows as low-risk where a clean funds trail connects the PSP payout report to the Panama account; however, attempting to use an unlicensed EMI as the sole treasury host will trigger enhanced due diligence and likely account refusal.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital flows are USD-native and route through standard correspondent banking networks: (1) shareholder capital contributions are transmitted via SWIFT MT103 (or MT203 for multi-batch funding) from the parent or subscriber bank to the Panama S.A.'s USD operating account, with the Remittance Information narrative stating 'Aporte de capital / capital contribution' and referencing the capital-increase deed and RUC tax-identification number; (2) the receiving bank verifies the funds against an apostilled copy of the public deed of capital increase, the updated share registry, the shareholders' resolution, and registered-agent certification from the Public Registry; (3) Panama has no central-bank numeric purpose codes, so the narrative field constitutes the primary compliance classification and must be consistent with the corporate treasury documentation to prevent reclassification as a loan, royalty, or service fee; (4) for digital-marketplace receipts from Stripe and PayPal, funds settle from the PSP's USD accounts at US clearing institutions via ACH, Fedwire, or SWIFT and are credited to the Panama operating account, then matched against the platform's payout report to evidence the underlying transactions; (5) banks will refresh source-of-funds and UBO declarations before booking any large one-off capital injection, and legal counsel's letter confirming the payer's authority is generally required.Outbound treasury disbursements use five clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for individual supplier, contractor, licensor, and content-creator payments, executed over the correspondent network with New York CHIPS/Fedwire clearing banks as intermediaries; (ii) SWIFT MT202 COV for interbank treasury sweeps, parent dividends, and beneficiary-bank funding where cover payments are mandated; (iii) commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 (irrevocable, confirmed or unconfirmed) for documentary trade transactions, available through the trade desks of Banistmo and BAC for any physical or service-side supply chain, although digital stock-photo licencing is normally settled by invoice plus wire; (iv) PSP mass-payout engines (PayPal Mass Payout and Stripe Payouts) for disbursements to global content creators, where the PSP performs FX conversion and local-rail delivery outside Panama; and (v) the Panama local ACH system managed by the banking association for domestic B2B payments and Panamanian payroll. Because the corporate account is USD-denominated and Panama has no domestic FX market, outbound USD disbursements remain on the international correspondent clearing system without central-bank approval; non-USD beneficiary payments are converted by the bank's international desk at prevailing interbank rates, with no withholding tax on foreign-source profit remittance under the territorial tax system.Plain-vanilla FX forwards with international banking counterparties (Banistmo/Bancolombia international desks, Scotiabank Global FX) to fix EUR, GBP, or other conversion rates on content-licencing royalties and subscription receivables; no Balboa/USD forward market exists because the PAB is rigidly pegged at 1:1 to USDNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing Latin American currencies (COP, MXN, BRL) where regional marketplace receivables create documented exposure, transacted through parent-bank offices or international desks because local NDF market depth inside Panama is negligibleCross-currency swaps governed by an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with Credit Support Annex, converting EUR/GBP platform revenue into synthetic USD, permissible under Panamanian banking law as ordinary off-balance-sheet derivatives for corporate clients with verified underlying exposureEuropean-style OTC FX options for contingency hedging on large one-off licencing or asset-sale deals, permissible when supported by a realistic underlying exposure, board authorisation minutes, and a compliant derivatives-eligibility assessment by the SBP-regulated counterparty bankStrategic unhedged USD position as the default treasury policy, given that the entity's functional currency is USD and Panama's dollarised legal tender system eliminates domestic translation and convertibility risk

Panama law does not recognise the EU/UK electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence category: any issuance of electronic money callable at par in Panama requires a licence or authorisation from the SBP, and offshore EMIs cannot lawfully provide regulated e-money services to the public inside Panama or hold the Panama S.A.'s principal settlement balances. The legally viable blueprint is to use licensed cross-border PSPs and EMIs (Stripe, PayPal, Wise Platform, Payoneer) strictly as collection forwarders that aggregate worldwide card/alternative-payment-method transactions in the United States or EU and wire net USD proceeds into the Panama operating account at a tier-1 local bank via SWIFT. This structure is compliant because the EMI performs payment facilitation in its own jurisdiction and never acts as a Panamanian bank. Banks will treat PSP-cleared flows as low-risk where a clean funds trail connects the PSP payout report to the Panama account; however, attempting to use an unlicensed EMI as the sole treasury host will trigger enhanced due diligence and likely account refusal.
