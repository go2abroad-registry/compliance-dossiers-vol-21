Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; e-Tax online portal: https://etax.gob.pa; DGI informational portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa. Electronic invoicing administration is integrated into the same DGI 'Facturación Electrónica' platform.

E-government filing portal

A Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) operating an onshore Ghost Kitchen must obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente). Procedure: (1) attach the Foundation Charter and Public Registry inscription certificate; (2) file the DGI 'Pre-RUC' online application through e-Tax stating the expected business activity (ISIC 5610 restaurant/mobile food service) and the Panamanian resident agent's address; (3) DGI validates the foundation's existence and legal representative, then assigns a provisional RUC; (4) final RUC is issued after verification of fiscal domicile and acceptance of tax obligations. The same RUC simultaneously covers income tax (ISR) and ITBMS registration. Non-resident founders and beneficiaries are permitted, but a Panamanian resident agent is mandatory for service of process and tax notifications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope. The PIF is treated as a separate taxable person, not as a pass-through entity. Only Panamanian-source income is within the tax net; foreign-source income is outside the territorial scope and generally not taxed. A delivery-only cloud kitchen physically operating in Panama derives Panamanian-source income from food sales and delivery services, thus subject to entity-level corporate income tax (flat 25% ISR under the general regime, or simplified progressive rates if annual gross income does not exceed PAB 200,000).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists in Panama. Available statutory elections: (a) the 'Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente' elective simplified regime for taxpayers with annual gross income up to PAB 200,000 (ISR) and up to PAB 36,000 (ITBMS quota payments); (b) exporters may elect the ITBMS input-tax credit/refund recovery mechanism on export transactions; (c) free-zone establishment elections for operations inside Colón Free Zone or Panama Pacifico; (d) territorial-source attribution rules and CFC recharacterisation are mandatory, not elective; consolidation of several Panamanian taxpayers into one ISR return is not permitted — each PIF entity files separately.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

9%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general exemption threshold: any natural or legal person habitually carrying on taxable transfers of movable goods or services in Panama, including a PIF operating a ghost kitchen, must register for RUC and ITBMS before commencing activities (Código Fiscal Art. 1059), regardless of turnover. A reduced simplified-compliance regime is available only where annual gross income is at or below PAB 36,000 (quarterly payment quota system instead of monthly ITBMS filings).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% ITBMS rate applies only to: (i) physical export of goods that leave Panamanian territory; (ii) services provided to non-residents for use or enjoyment entirely abroad; and (iii) supplies delivered to a legally recognized free zone (Colón Free Zone, Panama Pacifico) treated as exports. A domestic ghost kitchen selling prepared food through delivery platforms to consumers located in Panama is not eligible for zero-rating: both the food supply and the delivery service are performed and consumed in Panama, attracting the 9% standard ITBMS. Exporter registration in the DGI Registro de Exportadores and customs exit documentation are mandatory to support any 0% claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Exportación - DUA)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exit from Panama
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Remesa) or Panamanian bank statement evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Exporter Register (Registro de Exportadores) inscription and, where applicable, certificate of origin or free-zone movement document
  • ITBMS export recovery workbook (supporting input VAT credits allocated to exported goods/services)

Mandatory phased electronic invoicing (DGI Facturación Electrónica): Phase 1 applied to taxpayers with annual billing above PAB 5,000,000 (2020); Phase 2 to those above PAB 1,000,000 (2021); Phase 3 extends to all remaining active RUC holders and new registrants (2022-2024). Invoices must be transmitted in XML via DGI-authorized software through e-factura web-service APIs, digitally signed, and validated by the DGI which returns a 'código de aceptación' (acceptance code). The model is online cycle validation rather than real-time point-of-sale fiscal clearance. Legacy fiscal printers remain permitted for qualifying small taxpayers until full migration deadlines are met.

DGI audits export zero-rating claims and ITBMS input-credit refunds with strict documentary checks, including customs exit evidence and proof of FX repatriation within 180 days. Non-compliance triggers: surcharge of 15% on unpaid tax (Código Fiscal Art. 749), moratory interest, fines ranging up to 100% of the evaded tax for false records, cancellation of export certificates and exclusion from the Exporters Register, and criminal liability for tax fraud or simulated exports under Law 8 of 2010 and the Código Penal. The ordinary DGI review period is five years, extendable to eight years in cases of omissions or non-filing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility with zero profit-remittance hurdles. The Balboa is pegged 1:1 to the USD and circulates only as fractional coins; the USD is legal tender for all obligations. Panama has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations since 1946, meaning no exchange controls, no capital-account authorization, no registration for inbound or outbound transfers, and no restrictions on dividends, foundation distributions, royalties, or loan repayments. Panama's constitutional and statutory territorial tax framework (Codigo Fiscal, as amended by Law 76 of 2019) additionally provides that income with foreign source, including the PIF's offshore asset income, falls outside Panamanian tax; the operating SPV's Panamanian-source commercial income is the only layer subject to local taxation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panama imposes no statutory timeline for export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts, and no central-bank reporting on trade settlement. Ghost-kitchen platform payouts from Uber Eats and DoorDash settle directly in USD into any Panamanian bank account without SBP approval, conversion, or foreign-exchange disclosure. The only operational obligation is the municipal Aviso de Operacion and sanitary licensing of the local operating entity.

Central bank supervision: Panama operates without a central bank. Banking supervision is exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panama (SBP), established under Decree Law 9 of 1998, while Banco Nacional de Panama serves only as the State's fiscal agent and clearing bank. Monetary policy is effectively displaced by full dollarization under Decree-Law 84 of 1941. The SBP additionally administers the Fintech Innovation Hub and the Regulatory Sandbox created by Law 68 of 2023.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Restricted and conditional. Law 68 of 2023 establishes a local fintech licensing and e-money framework under the SBP sandbox, but foreign offshore EMIs (UK FCA or EU e-money institutions) are not recognized as regulated deposit-taking institutions in Panama. Using an unlicensed offshore EMI as the treasury anchor would degrade the AML risk rating under SBP Agreement 04-2011 (as amended) and provoke de-risking by Tier 1 banks. A compliant architecture is acceptable: offshore EMI used only as a platform-ecosystem collection/attribution layer for Uber Eats and DoorDash payout aggregation, with a mandatory same-day USD sweep to the Panamanian operational account of the licensed SPV. All ultimate settlement must occur at a domestically licensed bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Red Flag 1 - Beneficial ownership opacity: A Private Interest Foundation under Law 25 of 1995 has no shareholder register, and nominee council members may obscure the founder's identity; this triggers enhanced due diligence (EDD) under SBP AML/CFT rules.

Root cause: Red Flag 1 - Beneficial ownership opacity: A Private Interest Foundation under Law 25 of 1995 has no shareholder register, and nominee council members may obscure the founder's identity; this triggers enhanced due diligence (EDD) under SBP AML/CFT rules.

Defense evidence: File the mandatory final beneficial-ownership declaration with the Registro de Beneficiario Final created by Law 254 of 2021; provide certified copies of the foundation charter, internal regulations, and the council resolution identifying founder, beneficiaries, and council members; provide apostilled declarations of source of funds for the statutory founding capital; add professional reference letters from the resident agent and Panamanian counsel; execute FATCA Form W-8BEN-E and the Panama CRS self-certification to close offshore transparency gaps.
Red Flag 2 - Source-of-funds opacity from marketplace settlements: Uber Eats and DoorDash payout flows arrive in aggregated net-commission amounts from foreign payment processors, lacking order-level traceability and any export-invoice foundation.

Root cause: Red Flag 2 - Source-of-funds opacity from marketplace settlements: Uber Eats and DoorDash payout flows arrive in aggregated net-commission amounts from foreign payment processors, lacking order-level traceability and any export-invoice foundation.

Defense evidence: Implement a dual-entity treasury protocol: the locally licensed SPV (S.A. or S de R.L.) contracts directly with the platforms and receives USD settlements into its operational account; the SPV then pays royalties or management fees to the PIF under a written brand-licence and services agreement; the compliance file includes daily order-level platform reports, settlement statements, inbound MT103 confirmations, intercompany invoices, and a five-year archive under Law 23 of 2015 record-keeping obligations.
Red Flag 3 - High-risk geopolitical profile: founder or beneficial owner holds residence or citizenship in a FATF grey-listed or sanctioned jurisdiction, or has negative adverse-media exposure.

Root cause: Red Flag 3 - High-risk geopolitical profile: founder or beneficial owner holds residence or citizenship in a FATF grey-listed or sanctioned jurisdiction, or has negative adverse-media exposure.

Defense evidence: Assemble: PEP declaration; apostilled police-clearance certificates from every jurisdiction of citizenship or residence; certified CV with a coherent wealth narrative; audited financial statements of the founder's related entities for the past two years; three original bank reference letters dated no more than ninety days before submission; notarized source-of-funds declaration backed by sales deeds, inheritance documents, or dividend vouchers; and a professional introduction letter from the PIF's resident agent.
Red Flag 4 - Ultra vires structure: direct operation of a commercial ghost-kitchen business by the PIF violates the passive-asset mandate of Law 25 of 1995 and lacks the local sanitary and municipal licences required for food operations.

Root cause: Red Flag 4 - Ultra vires structure: direct operation of a commercial ghost-kitchen business by the PIF violates the passive-asset mandate of Law 25 of 1995 and lacks the local sanitary and municipal licences required for food operations.

Defense evidence: Re-architecture proof: PIF holds 100% of a Panamanian operating SPV; the SPV is the registered holder of the municipal Aviso de Operacion, the MINSA sanitary inspection certificate, and the fire-safety permit; the PIF owns the virtual-brand trademark portfolio and licenses it to the SPV through a written IP licence agreement; the bank file contains the group chart, licence agreement, permits, and SPV board resolutions demonstrating commercial substance.
Red Flag 5 - Unsupported mid-cycle capital injections: external investors cannot subscribe equity in a foundation because the PIF issues no shares, so their funding appears as unexplained cash entries without corporate authority.

Root cause: Red Flag 5 - Unsupported mid-cycle capital injections: external investors cannot subscribe equity in a foundation because the PIF issues no shares, so their funding appears as unexplained cash entries without corporate authority.

Defense evidence: Route third-party investor funds as convertible SAFE instruments or shareholder loans into the SPV, not into the PIF; the PIF's own injections are supported by notarized loan agreements with fixed amortisation and an arm's-length SOFR-based spread; each wire is evidenced by SWIFT MT103, board minutes, and updated group capitalization schedules; retain a simplified transfer-pricing file supporting interest and royalty rates paid to the PIF.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital enters via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the selected Panamanian bank's US dollar correspondent (typically JPMorgan Chase New York, Bank of America, or Citibank). Because Panama has no exchange-control legislation, no SBP approval, no purpose-code registration, and no mandatory conversion is required. Recommended wire narratives: (i) founder endowment - 'Aporte de capital a Fundacion de Interes Privado [name]' with the PIF registration number; (ii) shareholder loan - 'Prestamo intercompania' citing the notarized loan agreement date; (iii) brand-royalty collection - 'Regalias por contrato de licencia' citing the licence; (iv) platform settlement sweeps - 'Settlement ghk [brand] - Uber Eats / DoorDash'. The evidentiary file for each injection must include the underlying contract, board resolution, originator bank statement, and the SWIFT message to satisfy SBP AML rule 5.3.USD is the functional and settlement currency. Vendor disbursements for packaging, raw-food imports, cloud software, and delivery-locker equipment are executed by SWIFT MT103 from the Tier 1 operational account. For equipment imports exceeding USD 50,000, open an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP 600), payable at sight in USD, with confirmation through a US correspondent. For Mexican and Colombian ingredient suppliers, settle USD through correspondent clearing or negotiate COP/MXN invoicing with a local bridge intermediary; avoid PayPal and similar wallets as the primary rail due to conversion fees and AML opacity. Platform payouts: Uber Eats settles gross-order balances to the SPV's USD virtual terminal; DoorDash (where operating from a US-domiciled marketplace entity) settles by ACH directly to the same USD account without conversion. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panama requires a mandatory Local File when the taxpayer's annual gross revenue equals or exceeds PAB/USD 3,000,000; a Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue reaches PAB/USD 50,000,000. Below these thresholds, a simplified TP documentation report is advisable for related-party royalty and brand fees.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Panama has adopted economic substance principles aligned with the OECD standards; a Private Interest Foundation conducting a ghost kitchen and virtual brand operation must demonstrate physical premises, local employees, strategic decision-making, and real management of delivery operations in Panama. A PIF that merely holds the brand rights or invoices delivery income without operational substance risks recharacterization and loss of territorial benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fiscal penalty of USD 10,000 to USD 100,000; imprisonment from 4 to 10 years for the responsible taxable persons; compulsory restatement and payment of the omitted 25% ISR plus default interest and a 200% surcharge on the evaded amount.

Director personal liability: Panamanian law authorises piercing the PIF veil where the foundation is a mere instrumentality; council members, the resident agent, and beneficial owner face joint civil liability, asset embargoes, travel bans, and, upon criminal conviction, imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: DGI may disregard the foundation's separate existence, assess Panama-source taxation on deemed revenues, apply surcharges up to 200% of tax, cancel the operating licence, and strike the PIF from the registry under Panama's economic substance enforcement rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and foundation council are liable for breach of fiduciary duty; Panamanian courts may impose civil restitution, travel bans, and penalty of up to two times the corporate deficiency; criminal sanctions may attach if the arrangement is used as a sham to evade tax.
Money Laundering / AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Panama's AML Law 23 of 2015 and CNB regulations, fines up to USD 1,000,000 or 200% of the assets involved; freezing and forfeiture of accounts; cancellation of the foundation registration; UAF administrative sanctions and criminal sanctions under the penal code.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent may face up to 12 years' imprisonment; travel bans and extended confiscation are routinely applied; the foundation's acts can be retrospectively nullified if vehicles and accounts were used to commit the offence.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: DGI will make a transfer pricing adjustment, re-characterize the royalty as constructive dividend, assess deficiency plus default interest, add a penalty of 20%–40% of the adjusted tax base, and place the entity on the tax-risk anti-substance list.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved the mispriced related-party transaction are personally liable for the underpayment with penalties equal to twice the tax understated; fraudulent documentation elevates liability to criminal tax evasion and travel bans.
