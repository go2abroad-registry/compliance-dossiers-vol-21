Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Panama S.A. legislation does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up equity or an escrow-account rule at formation; the charter must state authorized capital (commonly US$10,000). For the e-money licence, the SBP requires minimum paid-in capital and a safeguarding arrangement under which issued electronic money is backed by liquid reserve assets.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministry of Economy and Finance, Panama; online portal: SIME – Sistema Integrado de Mejoramiento de la Administración Tributaria (https://sime.com.pa); taxpayer e-services: SIRUC – Sistema de Información y Registro Único de Contribuyentes.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (including a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima with foreign shareholders) must obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) from DGI prior to commencing taxable activities in Panama. Procedure: (1) file notarized certificate of incorporation and registered bylaws with the Public Registry; (2) apply for an Aviso de Operación (business permit) from the relevant municipality; (3) register with DGI via the SIRUC or an authorized notary/agent using the DGI-RA-01 form, providing corporate documents, proof of legal representative's ID (cédula/passport), and a local fiscal address; (4) obtain RUC certificate and activate the SIME online account; (5) for a licensed EMI (electronic money institution) with virtual IBAN issuance activity, present the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panama (SBP) license or letter of no-objection for the Fintech license, and file the AML/KYC compliance annex. Processing time is normally 5–10 business days. Non-resident shareholders are permitted; a local statutory agent is mandatory for the S.A. entity. A foreign entity without a permanent establishment in Panama may require a simplified RUC only if deriving Panamanian-source income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Panamanian law. Taxation is territorial: a Panamanian S.A. is taxed only on Panamanian-source income and not on worldwide income. Available elections include: (a) election to apply the Small Taxpayer Regime (Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente) for annual gross income up to B/.300,000; (b) election under Law 41 of 2007 and Executive Decree 153 of 2005 for Multinational Company Headquarters (SEM) regime, enabling a reduced effective tax rate and whatever-case exemptions for qualifying EMIs; (c) election for tax consolidation of affiliated entities under DGI-approved rules; (d) election for foreign tax credit under tax treaties (limited to specific double taxation agreements). Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized; Panama does not apply OECD BEPS hybrid mismatch rules as national law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic turnover threshold for ITBMS registration; any person or entity habitually performing taxable acts (including digital and financial services) in Panama must register. A 'Small Taxpayer' simplified threshold applies to persons or entities with annual gross income of up to B/.300,000, allowing simplified filing and payment; the threshold is B/.300,000 Panamanian Balboas (B/.1.00 = USD 1.00).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (a) exports of tangible goods physically leaving Panama, and (b) services sold to non-residents that are performed, used, or exploited outside Panama, including offshore payment processing, digital wallet administration, virtual IBAN issuance, and cross-border fintech services (under Article 1565 of the Panama Tax Code and regulatory amendments). To claim 0% ITBMS, the EMI must establish the export nature of the service via a written contract with a non-resident recipient, plus proof that the service is not consumed in Panama. Direct transfer of goods or services to a Panamanian resident, even if booked as 'export', is disallowed. Export services must be invoiced using the DGI e-invoice export code; the corresponding input VAT (on local costs) is creditable and any excess is refundable through DGI.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA – Declaración Única Aduanera) for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill with proof of departure from Panamanian territory
  • Copy of the commercial invoice evidencing the export transaction
  • Proof of receipt and acceptance by the foreign buyer or service recipient
  • Payment certificate or inward remittance evidence (bank or SWIFT records) in the name of the Panamanian exporter
  • For electronic money services: SBP licensed EMI approval and KYC/destination report evidencing the non-resident status of the counterparty

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) for all registered taxpayers on a phased implementation schedule. Issuers must transmit structured electronic invoices (XML format) to the DGI platform in near-real time for validation and digital stamping; the DGI returns a unique authorization number (Código de Trazabilidad o Autorización) before the receipt can be legally issued to the client. The platform supports B2B and B2C models, enforced via SIME's 'Facturación Electrónica de Panamá' module. Financial entities, including EMI licensees, must use the sector-specific e-invoice scheme with extended fields for fees, virtual IBAN transactions, and electronic money reserve evidence.

Panama imposes substantial audit scrutiny on export zero-rating claims. If DGI finds fraudulent, false, or unsubstantiated export documentation, it may retrospectively reverse the zero-rating, levy the 7% ITBMS plus surcharge, late-payment interest, and a fine ranging from 100% to 300% of the omitted tax; criminal penalties for tax fraud include imprisonment under Panama's Tax Code (Articles 783–785) and the general criminal code for aggravated and repeated offenses. Additionally, the SBP may suspend or revoke the EMI license, and the financial institution's directors and compliance officers may be subject to personal liability and administrative sanctions. Under Panamanian law, failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days may be considered an indication of unrealized export; the tax administration may treat the transaction as non-exported if no reasonable explanation is provided, resulting in the same recapture and penalty regime. DGI applies a robust documentary and economic substance review for electronic money institutions due to the high-risk classification of digital wallet and virtual IBAN business.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized and open-economy monetary system with no exchange-control law, no prior-approval mechanism, and no compulsory buy/sell rates. Current-account items (goods, services, royalties, remittances) and capital-account items (equity injections, intercompany loans, foreign direct investment, portfolio flows) are freely convertible because transactions are executed in USD. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation are unrestricted in FX terms; they are subject only to Panama's territorial dividend withholding regime and standard corporate income taxation. The material FX exposure for an electronic money institution is limited to non-USD balances such as EUR, CNY, COP, MXN and BRL held for cross-border disbursement, which can be converted through licensed local banks without regulatory filing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Panama. Export receipts and all other foreign-currency earnings may be held indefinitely in USD or any convertible currency, transferred abroad without restriction, and never require conversion into balboa. There is no prescribed repatriation timeline for Panama-incorporated entities.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as banking, payment-systems and financial-license regulator; Banco Nacional de Panamá as state fiscal agent and clearing bank; no traditional central bank exists, and the U.S. dollar is the de facto legal tender with balboa only as nominal coinage.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Panama Law 23 of 2015 and the SBP Innovation Hub permit supervised non-bank electronic payment issuers to maintain USD settlement accounts with licensed Panamanian banks, but offshore digital EMIs are not granted direct access to U.S. Fedwire, CHIPS or ACH systems. A foreign EMI must contract a licensed local sponsor bank and may not join the SBP national ACH or RTGS settlement circuits without a full financial-company license. SBP restricts outsourcing of KYC functions to unregulated third parties; an approved outsourcing model with UAF registration and data-residency safeguards is mandatory.
