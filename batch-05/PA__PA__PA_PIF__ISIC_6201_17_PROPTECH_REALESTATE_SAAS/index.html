Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); taxpayer portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) deriving Panamanian-source taxable income must first obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC) from the DGI. The DGI does not issue a separate TIN; the RUC serves as the unified tax identification number for income tax, VAT (ITBMS), and payroll taxes. The foreign entity or PIF must: (1) complete and file the standardized RUC application form through the Sede Electrónica portal or at an authorized DGI office; (2) submit the Public Registry foundation deed and certificate of incorporation; (3) provide notarized proof of the entity's registered address in Panama; (4) appoint a resident legal representative or fiscal agent where the PIF has no domestic management; (5) submit identification documents of the foundation council members and protector (if any); and (6) obtain a digital taxpayer certification (token/firma digital) for e-filing. After RUC activation, the taxpayer must also register for electronic invoicing and ITBMS.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama's territorial tax system taxes only Panamanian-source income. There is no U.S.-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows the legal form of the PIF and its activity in Panama. A PIF is generally treated as a taxable legal entity for Panamanian-source income, but distributions to beneficiaries are not taxed at the foundation level if the foundation holds assets passively. Available elections include: (a) election to use the Código Fiscal cost basis and depreciation rules for asset holding; (b) election under Article 698 et seq. to be taxed on a remittance basis for foreign-source dividends and interest; and (c) limited transitional elections for relief under ratified tax treaties (no comprehensive double tax treaties except for specific multinational agreements). Hybrid mismatch elections are not statutorily available; Panama's participation in the BEPS Inclusive Framework does not incorporate EU ATAD-style hybrid rules into domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general exemption threshold. Any person or entity supplying taxable goods or services in Panama—including cross-border digital services to Panamanian customers—must register for ITBMS if they carry on habitual or occasional taxable activities. There is a simplified small-taxpayer regime for entities with annual gross receipts up to PAB 36,000 that substitutes for full ITBMS compliance. Foreign SaaS providers with no Panamanian establishment are required to register through the simplified e-Tax non-resident registration procedure if they make B2C supplies to Panamanian consumers; there is no numeric threshold beyond the small-taxpayer rule.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and certain internationally rendered services qualify for 0% ITBMS. For physical goods: the goods must be definitively exported from Panama and must be substantiated by a customs export declaration. For services: the service must be rendered to a non-resident recipient, effectively consumed outside Panama, and not directly connected to Panamanian real property. For a PropTech SaaS provider, hosted/cloud software services to foreign clients qualify for 0% where (i) the recipient is outside Panama, (ii) the service is delivered electronically from Panama, and (iii) no Panamanian real estate is directly managed or evaluated on behalf of the foreign client except on a purely incidental basis. Services connected to the management of real estate physically located in Panama remain subject to 7% ITBMS even if the client is abroad. The zero rate may only be applied after the exporter obtains the required documentation and files the export declaration with the DGI.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) duly accepted by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) for goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (where physical shipment is involved)
  • Final commercial invoice with explicit 'Exportación / 0% ITBMS' annotation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificación de Compensación de Divisas) issued by a Panamanian licensed bank showing receipt of foreign currency within the repatriation deadline
  • Preferential Certificate of Origin (if applicable under trade agreements)
  • Service agreement or contract with the non-resident client evidencing offshore service delivery and place-of-consumption clauses

Panama operates a mandatory fiscal electronic invoicing regime (Factura Electrónica) implemented progressively by DGI resolutions (especially Res. No. 055-2017 and subsequent enforcement resolutions). Taxpayers must: (1) obtain authorization as an electronic invoicing issuer; (2) use DGI-approved invoicing software or an authorized technology provider; (3) transmit invoices in near-real-time to DGI's electronic platform (Sede Electrónica) for validation; (4) include the DGI-assigned eight-digit fiscal folio, authorization string, and QR code; and (5) receive the DGI electronic acceptance/rejection response before issuing the final document to the customer. The regime also mandates a digital stamping and clearance mechanism for invoices above statutorily defined amounts. The DGI has imposed monetary fines and suspension of the RUC for non-compliant issuers.

The DGI and the Comisión de la Bolsa / AML authorities scrutinize export zero-rating claims through bank-foreign currency reconciliation. If the exporter fails to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days without valid justification or fails to provide the mandated documentation on audit, the zero rate is invalidated and the 7% ITBMS becomes due retroactively. Penalties include: (a) 100% of the omitted tax as a fine for fraudulent zero-rating; (b) a monthly 1.5% late interest surcharge on underpaid ITBMS; (c) suspension of the RUC and electronic invoicing authorization; and (d) potential criminal sanctions under the Código Fiscal for tax fraud, including imprisonment for amounts exceeding USD 100,000. The DGI may also impose fines of PAB 500–10,000 for documentary procedural irregularities, and the statutory limitation period for audit is six years, extendable to ten in fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the US dollar. Capital account transactions, profit repatriation, dividends, and current account transfers are freely permitted without prior exchange control approval. No surrender requirements, no repatriation delays, and no restrictions on foreign ownership of bank accounts. The legal tender is exclusively the US dollar; no local fiat currency exists to convert.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio on export receipts, SaaS subscription proceeds, or any inbound cross-border payments. All USD receipts may be held indefinitely, reinvested, or remitted abroad without exchange control filings.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); Panama has no separate central bank and operates on a full US dollar standard. Monetary and exchange rate policy are effectively determined by the US Federal Reserve, with SBP overseeing regulatory compliance for all banking and trust activities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium (restricted). Panama does not yet have a comprehensive Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Mercury, Payoneer, Wise Business) generally rely on correspondent arrangements through US or European banks and can open accounts for a Panama PIF on a discretionary basis. Key constraints include enhanced due diligence on the foundation's structure, the need for a Panamanian legal opinion, and the risk of sudden debanking without local regulatory recourse. A dedicated Panamanian bank account remains the legally safest and most stable solution.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Private Interest Foundation with undisclosed beneficiaries and broad object clauses.

Root cause: Private Interest Foundation with undisclosed beneficiaries and broad object clauses.

Defense evidence: Certified copy of the Foundation Charter (Pacto Fundacional), Rules Regulations (Reglamento), current certificate of registration from the Public Registry of Panama, legal opinion from a Panamanian counsel identifying settlor, foundation council members, beneficiary and protector, plus a detailed organizational chart showing ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs) and source of funds.
PropTech income inconsistent with the foundation's stated commercial profile.

Root cause: PropTech income inconsistent with the foundation's stated commercial profile.

Defense evidence: Audited or management-prepared financial statements for the last two years, commercial contracts with property management clients, software licensing agreements, SaaS platform user statistics, and a detailed business plan describing the digital real estate screening service and payment flows.
Inbound capital injection from jurisdictions classified as high-risk or lacking adequate KYC standards.

Root cause: Inbound capital injection from jurisdictions classified as high-risk or lacking adequate KYC standards.

Defense evidence: Bank statements from the originating institution, source-of-wealth declaration, tax residence certificates of all investors, and evidence of the economic substance in Panama (physical office, local board minutes, and operational records) to demonstrate legitimate cross-border capital movement.
Cross-border disbursements to third-party vendors in dissimilar industries or to high-risk geographies.

Root cause: Cross-border disbursements to third-party vendors in dissimilar industries or to high-risk geographies.

Defense evidence: Original invoices, executed vendor agreements, webhosting and cloud infrastructure contracts, and a funds flow matrix showing the commercial rationale. For payments to related parties, transfer pricing documentation and a consolidated organizational chart are required.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers in USD to the PIF's Panamanian bank account. The wire message must include a clear purpose code per the receiving bank's classification, typically 'Capital Contribution' (code 201) or 'Investment in Private Equity' (code 203). For foundations, the legal name on the wire must exactly match the registered corporate name on the foundation certificate. Verification requires the transferor to send proof of identity and entitlement (e.g., a board resolution for corporate investors) to the receiving bank prior to the first credit. Payment networks ACH and domestic Panama clearing (ACH-P) may be used only for local operating expenses from the same account.Trade settlements for subscription fees, licensing, cloud infrastructure, and vendor payments are executed through USD SWIFT MT103 wires, with the Panama clearing house (ACH-P) available only for local utility or payroll transactions. Letters of credit (Commercial LCs) are permissible for physical asset acquisitions but are uncommon for SaaS. For cross-border ACH disbursements, the PIF will need a US correspondent routing number or a fintech aggregate provider; direct US ACH is not natively available without a US-based account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 1,000,000 (USD 1,000,000) in cross-border related-party transactions per fiscal year, or classification as a Gran Contribuyente with gross annual revenue above B/. 10,000,000, triggers the Local File. Reporting groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million file a Master File and Country-by-Country Report under BEPS Action 13. The DGI may require contemporaneous documentation for smaller transactions where it identifies margin erosion or insufficient substance.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not maintain standalone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to the UAE. Nevertheless, the territorial tax system, anti-abuse principles, and judicial doctrine of simulation require a PIF to demonstrate substantive Panama-based presence: local management, payroll or contracted service providers, bank accounts, accounting records, and risk-bearing capacity. A PIF that merely holds legal title to the PropTech SaaS operation while all decisions and operations occur abroad is exposed to DGI re-characterization as a shell, at which point the foreign-source exclusion and the dividend withholding exemption may be disregarded and the underlying economic owners assessed at 25% on Panama-source receipts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction is punishable by imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and a fine of up to B/. 500,000; the civil re-assessment computes the 25% ISR plus a 10% late-payment surcharge and 1% monthly interest; an additional 100% penalty on the evaded tax is imposed for filing a false return.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members and officers are jointly and severally liable for withheld taxes not remitted. Directors who sign or direct a fraudulent declaration face criminal prosecution, personal civil restitution, DGI travel bans (restricción de salida), and embargo of personal assets.
