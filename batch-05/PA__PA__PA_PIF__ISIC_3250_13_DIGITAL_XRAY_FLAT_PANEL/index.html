Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Autoridad Nacional de Ingresos Públicos (ANIP) — Republic of Panama, succeeding the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas, with customs and internal revenue functions consolidated under Ley No. 365 de 2023. Official digital portal: Sede Electrónica ANIP at https://www.anip.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF): (1) Appoint a Panama-licensed registered agent and a tax-resident fiscal representative. (2) Register the foundation charter with the Registro Público de Panamá, Section of Private Interest Foundations, obtaining the foundation registration certificate and cargo (officer) appointments. (3) File the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) application through the ANIP Sede Electrónica portal, submitting the certified and apostilled foundation deed, valid identification of the foundation's beneficiaries or council, registered agent acceptance, and fiscal representative power of attorney. (4) Request simultaneous activation of the ITBMS (VAT) registration and designation as an exporter in the national customs exporter registry (VUPE). (5) Enroll as an electronic invoicing issuer with an ANIP-authorised e-invoicing operator. (6) Complete the foundation's mandatory annual flat tax payment of USD 400 through the ANIP system. Processing normally takes 10 to 20 business days after receipt of complete documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — The Panama PIF (Ley No. 25 de 1995) is a distinct legal person with segregated patrimony. Under Panama's territorial taxation model, income arising from Panamanian-source business activity is taxed at entity level under the Código Fiscal, while foreign-source income and offshore-held foundation assets remain outside the Panamanian tax net. A passive PIF that does not undertake commercial operations is liable only to the fixed USD 400 annual state fee. The foundation is not eligible for automatic pass-through or fiscal transparency treatment in Panama for income tax purposes.

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Available legal elections are governed by the Código Fiscal, Ley No. 25 de 1995, and ANIP administrative resolutions: (i) the foundation may, by charter amendment, elect to register as a commercial operator with the Registro Público and assume full income tax and ITBMS obligations for its Panama-source business profits; (ii) election to file as an exporter and apply zero-rated ITBMS treatment for qualifying export sales; (iii) election to maintain a passive patrimonial status while holding shares of an operating company; (iv) election to apply the special drawback or refund mechanism for VAT embedded in export production inputs; and (v) no hybrid-mismatch or check-the-box election exists under Panamanian law, so the PIF's classification is determined by its charter and registry status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 (USD 36,000) of cumulative annual onerous transfers of goods and services, expressed in Panamanian balboas at parity with the US dollar. Mandatory ITBMS registration is triggered when the taxable person's gross annual transfers exceed B/.36,000 or when the person habitually conducts business activities, imports goods, or renders specified taxable services regardless of amount.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods physically leaving Panamanian territory qualify for the 0% ITBMS rate, provided that: (i) the exporter holds a valid RUC and customs exporter code; (ii) the goods are sold to a non-resident foreign transferee for use or consumption outside Panama; (iii) the transfer is a genuine export transaction supported by a lawful customs export declaration filed through SICEA; (iv) the invoice is issued in the exporter's name in US dollars or foreign currency; (v) payment is received via Swift LC or TT wire transfer from the foreign buyer into a Panamanian bank account; and (vi) export proceeds are settled and repatriated within the maximum 180-day window from the date of bill of lading. For a manufacturer of digital X-ray flat panel detectors under ISIC 3250 (vapor-deposited amorphous silicon caesium iodide DR detectors), eligibility further requires that the exporter verify the tariff classification under Panama's HS Chapter 90.22, maintain the underlying production cost records, and retain proof that the goods were neither consumed, modified, nor sold locally in Panama. Qualified exporters may claim refund or credit for ITBMS borne on production inputs (amorphous silicon, caesium iodide, glass substrate, electronics, cleanroom consumables) under the export refund mechanism, subject to ANIP documentary review.

Panama operates a mandatory electronic invoicing regime known as Facturación Electrónica en Línea, governed by DGI Resolution No. 201-3572 de 2021 and subsequent ANIP implementing resolutions. Taxpayers with gross annual income exceeding USD 300,000 are required to issue fiscal e-invoices through ANIP-authorised electronic billing operators, with real-time or near-real-time digital validation by the Sede Electrónica, unique invoice serialisation, mandatory digital signature and QR code, and an electronic packing/transmission requirement (FEC format) before consular or customs clearance. Export invoices must reference the corresponding customs export declaration in the SICEA/VUPE platform and maintain a zero-rate digital file for audit purposes.

ANIP conducts automated cross-matching between customs declarations (SICEA/VUPE) and ITBMS returns; any export invoice without a matching customs export declaration or without compliant FX remittance evidence is recharacterised as taxable domestic sales. The legal consequences of fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating claims include: (i) immediate reversal of the 0% rate and reassessment of 7% ITBMS on the full gross value of the transaction from the shipment date; (ii) monthly surcharge of up to 3% and statutory interest for late payment; (iii) administrative fines ranging from one to two times the omitted tax; (iv) clawback of previously granted input VAT refunds, including retrospective recovery of export incentive benefits, plus harm (damages) of 100% of the refund; (v) for false customs declarations, criminal liability under Ley de Aduanas and the Código Penal for customs fraud, with potential imprisonment terms for directors and the fiscal representative; and (vi) suspension of electronic invoicing privileges and revocation of the exporter registration. Both the foundation and the responsible council members, registered agent, and fiscal representative may be jointly and severally liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Capital and current account transactions are fully convertible in U.S. dollars. Panama does not administer exchange controls, capital repatriation permits, or currency conversion quotas. A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) may receive foreign equity contributions, shareholder loans, trade proceeds, and client payments in USD and may remit dividends, loan repayments, supplier payments, and profit distributions to nonresidents without exchange-control authorization. The practical hurdles are exclusively compliance-driven: SBP-regulated banks require clear underlying transaction documentation, source-of-funds evidence, and ultimate beneficial ownership disclosure. Profit remittances by a nonresident PIF are generally free of Panamanian withholding if the underlying economic activity has no Panamanian situs; however, payments must correspond to documented trade, service, or financing transactions to avoid AML/CFT suspicion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio in Panama. Exporters and trading entities may retain dollar receipts freely in domestic or offshore accounts. For AML/CFT integrity, banks expect export receipts to be reconciled against sales contracts, commercial invoices, customs declarations, transport documents, and shipping records. The absence of surrender rules does not waive bank due diligence or the obligation to identify the origin of funds.

Central bank supervision: No formal central bank. The Republic of Panama uses the U.S. dollar as legal tender and maintains no domestic currency issuance authority. The banking and foreign-exchange supervisory authority is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), with Banco Nacional de Panamá acting as fiscal agent and the Ministry of Economy and Finance responsible for economic and financial policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for payment initiation and LOW for regulated treasury custody. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not recognized as Panamanian banks and cannot accept client deposits or provide local settlement accounts without being licensed or registered with the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá. A foreign EMI may be used as a payment gateway, card-issuing vehicle, or invoice-AP/AR tool, but all material clearing of medical X-ray flat-panel trade flows must settle through an SBP-licensed bank or its correspondent network. Treating an offshore EMI as a bank would create legal, AML, and counterparty risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must be routed through the SWIFT MT103 interbank network into the foundation's USD operational account at an SBP-licensed Panamanian bank. The initiating shareholder or lender must execute the wire from the same legal entity or natural person named in the PIF ownership or loan documentation; third-party payers are generally rejected. The payment should contain the beneficiary foundation's exact legal name, account number, beneficiary bank BIC, and a payment narrative referencing the foundation charter and trade purpose, such as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - PRIVATE INTEREST FOUNDATION - ISIC 3250 DIGITAL X-RAY FLAT-PANEL TRADE.' Panama does not maintain a government-assigned payment-purpose code framework, so the bank-validated SWIFT purpose narrative and underlying board resolution, subscription agreement, source-of-funds explanation, and sanctions/OFAC screening clearance form the compliance record. For trade prepayments, the narrative must cite the supplier invoice and contract number; for shareholder loans, cite the loan agreement and disbursement schedule.The primary outbound clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for open-account telegraphic transfers to verified suppliers, freight forwarders, and service providers. For large or strategically critical shipments, the foundation should use an irrevocable commercial documentary letter of credit issued by a Panamanian bank via SWIFT MT700, payable against a clean-on-board bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and quality or regulatory certificate. Confirmed or advised LC structures can reduce supply-chain payment risk for Chinese or regional manufacturers. Documentary collections using MT400/MT470 are permissible for lower-risk invoice-backed settlements. Domestic U.S.-dollar checks and local interbank debits are available through Panama's local clearing systems; cross-border USD clearing is executed through CHIPS or FEDWIRE via correspondent bank accounts. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File is triggered when annual related-party transactions exceed PAB 3,000,000; a Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue exceeding PAB 50,000,000. The Panamanian balboa (PAB) is pegged 1:1 to the USD.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), established under Law 70 of 2019, apply to entities conducting any 'relevant activity' such as distribution, intellectual property holding, financing or investment management. A Private Interest Foundation holding digital X-ray panel IP and earning royalties or intra-group distribution commissions must demonstrate adequate physical premises, full-time employees and strategic management in Panama. Passive asset-holding PIFs may rely on an licensed resident service provider, but must nevertheless file an annual ESR declaration. Inadequate substance may result in financial penalties, mandatory license revocation, automatic exchange of information and strike-off of the foundation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction carries imprisonment of 5 to 10 years, a fine of up to PAB 1,000,000, re-assessment of omitted tax, surcharge of 50% of the omitted amount, and monthly interest of 1%.

Director personal liability: The president, treasurer, legal representative and members of the foundation council who sign or cause the false filing are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution; courts may impose travel bans, assets embargo, disqualification from holding directorship in Panama, and, upon conviction, imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties of PAB 10,000 to PAB 100,000 per fiscal year, mandatory removal of the foundation from the registry, invalidation of the tax residence certificate, inclusion in EU/CRS adverse listings, and exchange of information with foreign tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Founders, council members and the resident agent may be held jointly liable for unpaid penalties and assessed taxes; investigations may trigger precautionary travel bans and six-month administrative arrest while the assets are traced.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The DGI may re-determine taxable income using the most appropriate OECD method, apply adjustment of 25% of the understated income, plus a late payment penalty of 1% per month and fines of up to PAB 200,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved unrealistic intercompany price lists are personally liable if the PIF cannot satisfy the adjustment; they face civil restitution orders, asset freezes, and disqualification from acting as tax representatives.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015, fines of PAB 5,000 to PAB 500,000 per violation, suspension of the PIF's registration, and for money laundering activity criminal penalties of up to 15 years imprisonment and forfeiture of the proceeds.

Director personal liability: Natural-person directors and the resident agent are personally liable for AML fines; they may be subject to travel bans, preventive arrest, civil forfeiture of personal assets, and long-term disqualification from operating regulated businesses in Panama.
