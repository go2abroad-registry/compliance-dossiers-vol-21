Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Foundation Charter must declare an initial endowment of at least USD 10,000 (B/.10,000) or equivalent assets. No mandatory escrow deposit or proof of blocked funds is required at registration, but the endowment assets must be identified in the charter and accepted by the first Foundation Council.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; online services portal: Sede Electrónica (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa) and Electronic Invoicing System (Facturación Electrónica de Panamá).

E-government filing portal

For a Private Interest Foundation (PIF), the resident agent and the foundation's designated representative must apply to the DGI for the Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC). The application is filed through the Sede Electrónica portal using the foundation's public deed and proof of appointment of the resident agent. The DGI issues a permanent RUC number (NIT) and registers the PIF as a territorial taxpayer. Foreign ownership does not require local presence beyond the resident agent, and the RUC application may be completed remotely with apostilled documents and a power of attorney.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — For a Private Interest Foundation (PIF), the resident agent and the foundation's designated representative must apply to the DGI for the Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC). The application is filed through the Sede Electrónica portal using the foundation's public deed and proof of appointment of the resident agent. The DGI issues a permanent RUC number (NIT) and registers the PIF as a territorial taxpayer. Foreign ownership does not require local presence beyond the resident agent, and the RUC application may be completed remotely with apostilled documents and a power of attorney.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a check-the-box regime. A PIF may elect to be treated as an active business taxpayer for Panamanian-source activities (thus subject to local income tax and ITBMS), or as a passive foreign-source income vehicle that is not subject to Panamanian income tax on non-Panamanian income. Hybrid mismatch elections are not statutorily available under Panamanian domestic law, but treaty and BEPS-consistent alignment may apply for cross-border payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.07%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No single numeric registration threshold for business entities; all persons engaged in commercial activities in Panama must obtain a RUC. Small taxpayers with annual gross income not exceeding PAB 36,000 (effectively USD 36,000) may apply for a simplified ITBMS payment method, while full registration and monthly/quarterly filing apply above this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% ITBMS applies to (i) the sale of movable goods physically exported from Panama, (ii) services rendered to non-resident persons for use or enjoyment entirely outside Panama, (iii) international transport of goods or persons, and (iv) goods and services supplied to duty-free zones or international transit regimes. The exporter must retain documentary evidence that the goods left Panamanian territory and were received abroad, and that payment was received from a non-resident or from export proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) accepted by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas de Panamá
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical shipment to the foreign buyer
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the Panamanian entity with a clear description of plush toys (ISIC 3240)
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer proof showing settlement of the export proceeds from a foreign bank account

Taxpayers authorized by the DGI must issue electronic invoices through the Facturación Electrónica de Panamá platform. The system requires real-time transmission of invoice data to the DGI, use of a DGI-authorized digital stamp (QR code with a unique validation code), and compliance with API integration rules. Foreign-owned exporters must use certified software or the free DGI web portal to file/report invoices; no separate pre-clearance of each invoice is generally required except for high-risk taxpayers, but data must be transmitted promptly upon issuance.

The DGI and Customs Authority may retroactively audit zero-rated export transactions for up to five fiscal years. If documentation is incomplete, fraudulent, or the goods are deemed to have been consumed domestically, the taxpayer is liable for the uncollected 7% ITBMS plus applicable surcharges, late payment interest, and fines of up to 200% of the omitted tax per the Fiscal Code. Criminal penalties, including imprisonment, may apply for false declarations, forged bills of lading, or fictitious export operations designed to obtain refunds or zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized monetary system with zero exchange controls; capital and current account transactions are freely convertible, with no quotas, approvals, or profit-remittance barriers for a Private Interest Foundation (PIF). Dividends, capital withdrawals, and trade payments may be remitted without central bank authorization; the sole friction is bank-level AML/CFT compliance pursuant to Law 23 of 2015 and SBP Acuerdo 4 de 2016.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory surrender, conversion window, or repatriation ratio on export receipts; proceeds from the sale of plush mascots may be retained in any jurisdiction or currency as determined by the foundation council.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) — Panama has no issuing central bank; the US dollar circulates as legal tender alongside the balboa (fixed 1:1) under Law 84 of 1941, and the SBP exercises banking supervision and prudential regulation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, with material legal constraints. Panama regulates fintech firms under Law 174 of 2020 and crypto businesses under Law 697 of 2023, but offshore digital EMIs are not licensed as Panamanian payment institutions; they may service the PIF only through bank-backed partnership rails or a local fintech registration. EMI accounts are acceptable for platform payouts and small USD flows, but are not accepted as the primary treasury rail for LC-backed or high-value OEM trade settlements because tier-1 originating banks require a clean bank-to-bank transfer trail.
