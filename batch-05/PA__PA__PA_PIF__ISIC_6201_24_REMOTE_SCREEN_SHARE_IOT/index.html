Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas – tax portal: 'e-Tax Panama' (https://etax.dgi.gob.pa; public information portal: https://www.dgi.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-held Private Interest Foundation (PIF), the statutory tax identifier is the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), which serves simultaneously as the income-tax and ITBMS (VAT) registration number. The foundation must first appoint a resident agent and be registered in the Registro Público of Panama. Thereafter, the foundation president or legal representative files Form RUC-L (persona jurídica) with the DGI through e-Tax or a Regional Office, attaching the foundation charter, public registration certificate, resident agent's notarized acceptance, and identity documents of the council members. A nominal processing fee (subject to DGI current tariff) is paid. Upon issuance, the DGI provides an 'Aviso de Inscripción' and activates VAT obligations if the foundation undertakes taxable commercial transactions in Panama. A foreign entity merely establishing or sponsoring a PIF does not itself require a Panamanian RUC; the foundation is the taxpayer.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a check-the-box entity classification system. A PIF is automatically a separate juridical person under Law 25 of 1995 and is taxable on Panama-source income only. No hybrid mismatch elections are available. The PIF may elect to satisfy its Panamanian tax presence through the flat annual foundation tax when it confines its activities to passive wealth management or foreign-source operations; if it carries on a commercial or industrial activity in Panama, it becomes a fully registered territorial taxpayer and must report Panama-source income under the general rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.07%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary de minimis threshold applies: any person or entity conducting taxable transfers of movable goods or taxable services within Panama must register for ITBMS. A simplified small-taxpayer regime is available only for domestic taxpayers with annual gross turnover not exceeding PAB 36,000; this does not exempt a foreign PIF from registration when it exports zero-rated services or makes taxable local supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for the physical export of tangible goods from Panama and for offshore services, including remote desktop access, unattended-machine telemetry, screen-sharing software subscriptions, and IoT remote support services, provided the service is supplied to a non-Panamanian customer, is effectively performed outside Panama, or the benefit/effective use occurs entirely abroad. The taxpayer must demonstrate from the contract, server location, user logs, and payment routing that the recipient is non-resident and that no use or entertainment of the service takes place in Panama. Services rendered to a Panamanian resident or effectively used in Panama will be subject to ITBMS at 7% irrespective of the digital delivery mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduanas de Exportación)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Alternative transport document for digital services evidencing delivery architecture
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/MT103 message showing payment received from the non-resident customer
  • Export service contract incorporating scope of remote screen share and IoT telemetry services
  • Commercial invoice annotated 'ZERO-RATED EXPORT – OFFSHORE SERVICE'
  • Certificate of non-residence or corporate registry/tax ID extract of the foreign customer

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) for all ITBMS-registered enterprises. Invoices must be issued in XML format, digitally signed using a certificate issued by a DGI-accredited certification authority, and transmitted in real time to DGI's e-Tax platform through web services/API for validation and clearance. Validated invoices are returned with a DGI verification code and QR code that must be printed or embedded on the customer copy. Zero-rated export invoices require prior electronic transmission of the export declaration (Computo de Exportación) to DGI; paper invoices are not accepted for export zero-rating.

DGI may audit zero-rated export claims for five years after filing. If the exporter cannot produce the customs, contractual, technical, and banking evidence proving foreign destination, offshore effective use, or receipt of foreign currency, the zero-rating is retroactively revoked. Reinstated ITBMS at 7% becomes exigible with monthly late-payment surcharges and adjusted interest. Administrative fines may range from a minimum monetary penalty to 100% of the evaded tax, and input VAT credits previously refunded may be clawed back with penalty. Wilful use of false export declarations or fabricated service contracts in a digital services context constitutes tax fraud, which may lead to criminal prosecution before Panamanian courts and suspension or cancellation of the taxpayer's electronic invoicing authorization.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility exists on both current and capital account. Panama imposes no exchange controls, no central bank approval for inward or outward remittances, and no restriction on profit or capital repatriation. A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) engaged in cross-border software exports may freely denominate contracts, hold multi-currency accounts, and remit founder or council distributions, subject only to the territorial source-based income tax regime of the Tax Code, DGI Resolution 201-9306 of 2019 on the digital economy, and standard DGI statistical/declaration obligations. Profit remittances are not taxed at source when the underlying income is derived exclusively from outside Panamanian territory, but outbound payments for services rendered into Panama may attract the 12.5% non-resident withholding tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panama imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate or sell foreign currency receipts to the state or banking system. Export proceeds from remote software and IoT telemetry services may be retained in USD or any convertible currency in domestic or foreign banks without reporting to a central bank.

Central bank supervision: Panama operates a fully dollarized economy with no central bank empowered to issue currency or impose exchange controls. Banking supervision and financial regulation are exercised by the Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá, SBP) under Decree Law No. 2 of 22 February 2008, while the Financial Analysis Unit (Unidad de Análisis Financiero, UAF) administers AML/CFT oversight. The Balboa (PAB) is a legal-tender unit of account at statutory 1:1 parity with the US Dollar, which circulates as the sole paper currency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility with structural constraints. Panama does not maintain a general-purpose Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing track for non-bank firms; the SBP operates an innovation hub and is developing a fintech licensing framework, but a PIF cannot act as its own PSP. Offshore digital EMIs generally decline PIF structures without strong economic-substance narratives and complete KYC bundles; the practical route is a local commercial bank account fed by PSP settlement from Stripe and Paddle via SWIFT, with direct EMI usage limited to treasury-float diversification below 15% of monthly receipts or emergency redundancy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Founder or shareholder capital wiring is executed via SWIFT MT103 to the PIF operational account, referencing 'Capital Contribution / PIF Funding'; Panama imposes no FX approval or purpose-code mandate, but the beneficiary field must include the 20-digit Código de Cuenta Cliente (CCC) and the PIF RUC to ensure straight-through processing. Single injections above USD 10,000 trigger UAF transaction-reporting thresholds under Law 23 of 2015, so a pre-assembled evidentiary bundle of the capital call, founder resolution, Council minute, source-of-funds statements, and independent counsel validation must be maintained at the bank and with the resident agent for DGI territorial filing.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for supplier invoices, SaaS royalty payments, and operating expenses, with MT202 covers used for internal liquidity sweeps. For software licensing and infrastructure procurement above USD 50,000, a commercial letter of credit may be opened, although PSP-based vendor remittances (Paddle payouts) render bank-issued LCs unnecessary in most cases. Each disbursement must carry a documented delegation of authority, beneficiary KYC, invoice-match, underlying contract, and FX conversion memo for non-USD payables; DGI records require payment vouchers and, where Panamanian withholding applies, tax-residency evidence for the counterparty.Natural-hedge policy: multi-currency settlement accounts (EUR, GBP, BRL, COP) with tier-1 banks to match PSP payout currencies and avoid derivatives for sub-USD 250,000 quarterly exposure.Deliverable FX forwards: permitted on cumulative non-USD exposure above USD 250,000 equivalent per quarter through SBP-licensed banks, with an aggregate notional cap of 20% of gross annual receipts.Cross-currency swaps: permissible for EUR/GBP-denominated PSP float held for 90 days or longer, executed under ISDA or a bank Master Agreement and confirmed by the Foundation Council treasury resolution.Non-deliverable forwards: permitted only for settlement against COP, BRL, or ARS through an offshore bank when local counterparty liquidity is absent; notional capped at USD 100,000 per trade and subject to monthly valuation monitoring.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding on dividends distributed from Panama-source profits; reduced to 5% where the underlying profits have already borne the full 25% corporate income tax; dividend distributions originating exclusively from foreign-source profits under the territorial regime are exempt from Panamanian withholding tax. For a Private Interest Foundation distributing cross-border software revenue to non-resident beneficiaries, the practical WHT exposure is 0% provided the income was foreign-source and no Panama corporate tax accrued.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months after the close of the fiscal year; for calendar-year taxpayers the return is due on or before 31 March of the following taxable year. Non-calendar electing entities must file no later than 31 May of the year in which the statutory three-month window matures.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than six months after the fiscal year-end; for calendar-year taxpayers the statutory filing window closes on 30 June of the following year. Local File/Master File documentation must be prepared and submitted to DGI only upon specific administrative request, but it must exist as of the filing date.

Box 1 – RUC / Registro Único de Contribuyente Unique taxpayer identification number of the Private Interest Foundation, as issued by the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI). The PIF must obtain a Panamanian RUC even when all shareholders/beneficiaries are non-residents; foreign beneficial owner information is collected through the RUC registration record. Box 5 – Ingresos Exentos / Fuente Extranjera (Exempt or Foreign-Source Income) Receipts earned outside Panama or from international markets, including SaaS revenue from non-resident subscribers, foreign-server telemetry data processing, and offshore remote-management fees. Non-resident PIFs rely on this line to reduce effective Panamanian tax to near zero. The exemption is defeated if the commercial activity is deemed to occur inside Panama, e.g., Panamanian-domiciled servers, local data-centre connectivity, or support services physically rendered in Panama. Only costs directly related to taxable Panama-source revenue are deductible. Foreign-source expenditures should not be allocated against Panama-source income unless a supported and consistent apportionment method is disclosed. Late filing penalty accrues at 2% of the tax due for each month or fraction of month, capped at 10%; failure to file within an additional six months may result in the DGI issuing administrative assessment, a fine of USD 500 to USD 5,000, suspension of the RUC, and criminal liability if fraudulent intent is established. Persistent non-filing of Form 510 exposes the PIF's appointed resident agent and directors to personal enforcement actions.
Box 7 – Renta Neta Imponible (Net Taxable Income) Resulting figure after subtracting Panama-source costs and deductions from Panama-source gross revenue. If the PIF is passive and earns no Panama-source revenue, this field should be zero or negative; negative balances are carried forward up to five taxable years. Late filing penalty accrues at 2% of the tax due for each month or fraction of month, capped at 10%; failure to file within an additional six months may result in the DGI issuing administrative assessment, a fine of USD 500 to USD 5,000, suspension of the RUC, and criminal liability if fraudulent intent is established. After offsets, any remaining balance must be paid on or before the filing deadline. A negative balance is not refunded automatically; it can be carried forward as a credit in the following taxable year. Failure to file Form 605 carries a fine of USD 1,000 to USD 10,000; inaccurate or incomplete filings attract an additional USD 5,000 penalty. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: US$1,000,000

Economic substance scope: Panama's Law 70 of 2019 and its Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) impose a substance-based standard on Panamanian tax-resident entities, including Private Interest Foundations, that carry on relevant activities such as distribution, service provision, financing, headquarters, shipping, holding, and intellectual-property holding. Remote screen-share software development, IOT telemetry processing, and related technical support will fall within the 'provision of services' relevant activity. The PIF must therefore demonstrate in Panama: (i) the active management and direction of the business; (ii) a physical office or data-centre presence commensurate with the level of activity; (iii) adequate number of full-time employees physically present in Panama; and (iv) operational expenditure in Panama proportionate to the foreign revenue booked. A pure passive holding PIF that owns IP rights without local staff or decision-making will be deemed to lack economic substance, triggering a regulatory review and possible denial of the territorial exemption or treaty access. The Panama ESR requires annual reporting to the Superintendencia de Sujetos No Financieros, and non-compliance is subject to fines of USD 5,000 for incorrect filings and up to USD 100,000 for intentional failure to meet substance thresholds.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 50% of the omitted tax plus accrued monthly surcharges (1% per month); sham structures are disregarded and the full 25% corporate income tax is reassessed. Criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment of 1 to 5 years and a fine of two to ten times the amount evaded, depending on the gravity of fraudulent intent.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent of the PIF may have the corporate veil pierced if they knowingly participated in the evasion scheme; the court can impose joint civil liability for the tax debt, travel bans, disqualification from acting as directors of Panamanian entities, and custodial sentences for ancillary offences under the Criminal Code.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The DGI and ESR authorities may deny the tax exemption, reclassify the income as Panama-source if telemetry equipment or management decision-making is deemed located in Panama, and impose administrative fines of USD 5,000 to USD 100,000. Repeat violations cause cancellation of the foundation's registration with the Registro Público.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and directors who sign statutory declarations of substance that are false are subject to criminal perjury-like sanctions; they may be ordered to pay the unpaid corporate taxes out of their personal estates, have their international travel barred, and be debarred from holding office in Panama for up to ten years.
Transfer Pricing Irregularities

Statutory sanction: The DGI may reassess taxable income under the arm's length principle, apply a 40% corrective penalty on the under-assessed tax, levy interest at the official rate plus a 10% per annum surcharge, and reject the PIF's requested treaty relief for the correlated foreign income. Repeat conduct qualifies as tax fraud for criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors who failed to establish and document a robust TP policy are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax assessed; the court can freeze personal assets, impose director disqualification, and in egregious cases order the imprisonment of the decision-makers who approved the non-arm's length contracts.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Failure to maintain and file accurate beneficial ownership information attracts monetary penalties of USD 1,000 to USD 1,000,000 under Law 23 of 2015 and its implementing decrees. Criminal laundering of subscription and remittance proceeds can independently warrant imprisonment of 5 to 12 years and forfeiture of all assets connected to the offence.

Director personal liability: The nominee directors and the registered agent face immediate personal criminal liability for knowingly allowing an undisclosed beneficial owner, including debarment, travel bans, extradition treaties activated, and incarceration. The civil veil of the PIF is automatically lifted so that the concealed owner becomes personally accountable for unpaid tax and money-laundering fines.
