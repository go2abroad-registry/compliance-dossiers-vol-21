Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas, Panama; official portal: https://www.dgi.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity acting through a Panamanian registered agent or legal representative must: (1) file the corresponding pre-registration form with the DGI, either online or through the taxpayer service office; (2) provide a certified copy of the entity's constitutive documents translated into Spanish, with notarization and Apostille or consular legalization as applicable; (3) execute and notarize a power of attorney appointing a resident agent; (4) submit a sworn affidavit identifying the business activity and the applicable Panama-source income and tax obligations; (5) obtain the provisional RUC, after which the DGI conducts a review, issues the permanent RUC certificate, and activates the taxpayer's electronic fiscal profile; and (6) request the fiscal authorization for electronic invoicing and, if required, the digital signature certificate.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign entity acting through a Panamanian registered agent or legal representative must: (1) file the corresponding pre-registration form with the DGI, either online or through the taxpayer service office; (2) provide a certified copy of the entity's constitutive documents translated into Spanish, with notarization and Apostille or consular legalization as applicable; (3) execute and notarize a power of attorney appointing a resident agent; (4) submit a sworn affidavit identifying the business activity and the applicable Panama-source income and tax obligations; (5) obtain the provisional RUC, after which the DGI conducts a review, issues the permanent RUC certificate, and activates the taxpayer's electronic fiscal profile; and (6) request the fiscal authorization for electronic invoicing and, if required, the digital signature certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available. Panama applies a territorial taxation model under which any legal entity, including a Private Interest Foundation (PIF), may elect to be taxed as a small taxpayer (pequeño contribuyente) if annual gross turnover is equal to or below B/.36,000. A PIF that carries out no commercial acts may remain outside the ITBMS and income tax nets; a PIF engaged in active trading of used machinery is treated as a general taxpayer at the corporate income tax rate of 25% on Panama-source profits. Mixed sourcing rules apply and no double tax relief treaty network is relied upon for export operations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold; all persons engaged in the commercial transfer of tangible movable goods or services in Panama must register for ITBMS irrespective of turnover. An optional small-taxpayer regime permits filing and remittance on a simplified basis for annual taxable turnover up to B/.36,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under ITBMS applies to used construction excavators if: (i) the goods are physically exported from Panama, evidenced by the customs unit load and bill of lading; (ii) title, ownership, and risk in the goods pass to a foreign buyer not resident in Panama; (iii) the sale is concluded under a purchase order or agreement issued by an offshore purchaser; and (iv) the consideration is received in a bank account outside Panama or through a licensed Panamanian bank in a clearing arrangement. Export-related services such as inland freight from Panama premises to the Panamanian port, inspection, and customs brokerage may also be zero-rated when directly linked to the exported goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) accepted and stamped by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas de Panamá
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill identifying the foreign receiver and Panama port of departure
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/SWIFT confirmation demonstrating receipt of the export proceeds from abroad
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued electronically with DGI fiscal seal
  • Purchase agreement or purchase order with the foreign customer
  • Fumigation or phytosanitary certificate where required for used machinery
  • DGI Form 302 for export zero-rating evidence, if specifically requested during audit

Panama requires mandatory electronic invoicing through the DGI-approved system (Facturación Electrónica). Taxpayers must transmit each invoice and credit note in UBL 2.1 XML format to the DGI in real time, obtain a digital fiscal seal (stamping) with an associated QR code, and publish accepted documents in the DGI's centralized electronic registry. All transactions, including exports, must be reflected in the electronic clearance platform; intermediate software providers and taxpayers themselves must use DGI-authorized API integrations or fiscal DSPs. The issuance of a non-electronic invoice is treated as a procedural infringement and may invalidate the corresponding input credit.

The DGI and customs authorities conduct joint audits of export zero-rating claims. Failure to maintain possession or timely submission of export evidence, or the absence of genuine foreign payment, results in retroactive clawback of all zero-rated VAT benefits, reassessment of the full 7% ITBMS on each invoice, and monetary surcharge of 10% per month of assessed tax (up to 100%). Additionally, penalties equal to 50% of the tax fraudulently omitted may be imposed, civil fines per false customs declaration and unauthorized electronic seal apply, and criminal liability may arise under Panama's Tax Procedure Code and Criminal Code, including jail terms for aggravated evasion. The taxpayer's digital invoicing authorization and export zero-rating eligibility may be suspended for a period of one to three years.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full official dollarization: the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) exists only as a 1:1 unit-of-account with USD coins; Federal Reserve notes circulate exclusively. There is no exchange-control law, no capital-account restriction, no prior-authorization requirement for FX conversion, and no restriction on the free movement of USD capital, dividends, or profits. Both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible by statute and practice; profit remittance is tax-exempt at source under Panama's territorial tax system, and no dividends withholding applies to distribution to non-resident beneficiaries provided the PIF has satisfied annual franchise tax and economic substance filings under Law 254 of 2020.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama has never enacted an export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion regime. Proceeds from the export of used construction machinery are freely retainable in USD, payable into any onshore or offshore USD account, or immediately disbursed. For bank credit of export proceeds the commercial bank typically requires the SIGA export declaration (Declaración de Mercancías) with the equipment serial numbers, the commercial invoice, and the ocean bill of lading; however this is a documentary KYC practice, not a legal surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: No Panamanian central bank exists; the US Dollar is legal tender under the 1904 Monetary Agreement and Decree 74 of 2004. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the sole banking and AML/CFT regulator, supervising banks and financial institutions. Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the fiscal agent and clearing administrator for the State, but does not set monetary policy or issue currency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Moderate. Panama enacted a Payment Institutions licensing regime under SBP Agreement No. 006-2023, which subjects any EMI-like activity to a full SBP license. Unlicensed offshore EMIs (EU/UK digital banks) may open a non-resident USD account to receive proceeds from international auction platforms, but cannot serve as the primary treasury settlement node because Law 254 of 2020 economic substance principles require the PIF's core treasury activity to be visible in Panama. The viable architecture is: offshore EMI as a collection-only side-purse for eBay/Ritchie Bros./IronPlanet payouts, with a 24-72 hour sweep to the Panamanian licensed bank; the domestic licensed account remains the operational and compliance nexus.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for initial capital: SWIFT MT103 from the founder's foreign bank to the PIF's Panama Capital Escrow Account, with Field 70 (Remittance Information) reading 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION – PRIVATE INTEREST FOUNDATION STATUTE ARTICLE [_X_] – FOUNDER ID [_name_]'; supporting upload of the notarized foundation statute, the RUAF certificate, and the founder's bank reference letter. For recurring trade proceeds: MT103 must include in Field 70 the commercial invoice number plus the used-equipment serial number; the bank's trade operations unit verifies the file against the pre-registered purchase agreement, SIGA export declaration, and bill of lading. For incoming SWIFT wires in non-USD currencies (EUR, JPY, GBP), the Panamanian receiving bank automatically converts at its desk rate; to avoid spread-added execution, the PIF instructs the sending bank to pre-convert into USD at the originating bank's rate before issuing the MT103, therefore the SBP-regulated Panama account receives only USD for final clearing.Primary clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 (OBI, Originator-to-Beneficiary Instruction) with matched reference to supplier name and machine purchase order, executed from the Panama Operational Account through the bank's New York correspondent (typically Bank of New York Mellon / Citibank / JPMorgan) with T+0/T+1 USD finality. For high-value used-equipment acquisitions from auction houses (Ritchie Bros., IronPlanet) and OEM rebuild centers, the PIF uses irrevocable commercial documentary Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) governed by UCP 600, issued by its Panamanian bank in favor of the supplier, payable against presentation of the commercial invoice, full set clean on-board bill of lading, packing list, and an independent inspection certificate. In-country disbursements (domiciliary agent, warehouse fees, transport within Panama) clear via Panama's national ACH/SPI retail clearing with MICR routing, linked to the same USD account without any conversion event.Outright USD/EUR and USD/JPY forward contracts with SBP-licensed commercial banks, underlying payables for European and Japanese used-excavator sourcing (auction house settlement in EUR/JPY).Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) in COP, MXN, BRL, CLP and PEN, hedgeable against forecast USD receivables from Latin American buyers; NDFs are universally used in Panama's dollarized system because no local market exists for the Latin American currencies.Cross-currency basis swaps (USD vs EUR, USD vs JPY) on notional amounts matching confirmed multi-unit sourcing contracts; transacted with the treasury desk of the primary relationship bank under ISDA/CSA documentation.Interest rate swaps on USD SOFR floating-rate debt, if the PIF's trade-finance line or LC discounting facilities carry floating exposure; permissible without SBP licensing because the corporate executes through a licensed counterparty bank.Simple USD/PAB currency symmetry is not required: Balboa exists only as coins and is pegged 1:1 with USD, therefore no intra-Panama FX hedge is needed or possible.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (Panama-source net taxable income only; territorial income tax system)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% on dividends or profit distributions paid from Panamanian corporate income tax profits; 10% on dividends or beneficiary distributions made from profits that were not subject to Panamanian ISR (for example, foreign-source income or exempt income).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed and any residual tax paid within three months after fiscal year-end, i.e. by 31 March for a calendar-year taxpayer.

If the PIF is engaged in active trading, do not select 'passive asset holding' or 'private foundation exempt activity'; erroneous classification is a trigger for economic substance and transfer pricing review. Box 4: Ingresos de Fuente Panameña Gross revenue derived from Panamanian-source trading, commissions, brokerage and sales of used excavators physically delivered or title-passed in Panama. Foreign-source revenue may be excluded under territoriality, but must be shown on the reconciliation annex with documentary support to avoid a DGI recharacterisation. Late filing and late payment subject the taxpayer to an administrative fine of USD 100 to USD 10,000 depending on the amount of tax involved, plus a monthly surcharge of 5% on the unpaid tax (maximum 50%) and statutory delay interest at approximately 1.5% per month.
Box 5: Costos y Gastos Deducibles Deductible expenses incurred for the generation of taxed Panama-source income: acquisition cost of used excavators, freight, marine insurance, repair and reconditioning costs, broker commissions, inspection fees and allocated overheads. Intercompany purchases from related suppliers are deductible only if priced at arm's length and accompanied by local transfer pricing documentation if the threshold is exceeded. Late filing and late payment subject the taxpayer to an administrative fine of USD 100 to USD 10,000 depending on the amount of tax involved, plus a monthly surcharge of 5% on the unpaid tax (maximum 50%) and statutory delay interest at approximately 1.5% per month.
Box 6: Pérdidas Tributarias Compensables Net operating losses from prior fiscal years applied against current taxable income, up to the five-year carryforward limit and any statutory annual caps. Attach a loss carryforward schedule and tax reconciliation computation; no carryback of losses is permitted. Late filing and late payment subject the taxpayer to an administrative fine of USD 100 to USD 10,000 depending on the amount of tax involved, plus a monthly surcharge of 5% on the unpaid tax (maximum 50%) and statutory delay interest at approximately 1.5% per month.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB 250,000 (Panamanian Balboa at parity with USD) or more in related-party transactions in a fiscal year triggers mandatory local file documentation and the annual transfer pricing disclosure. Group master file and country-by-country reporting obligations arise for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above PAN 250 million.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Decree Law 111 of 2020) require any tax-resident Panamanian entity performing a 'relevant activity' to show real physical presence, local employment, effective management and risk control in Panama, and to file an annual economic substance report. Active purchase and resale of used construction machinery is regarded as a distribution and service centre activity; therefore the PIF must demonstrate substantive Panamanian substance and not merely a registered address.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: DGI reassessment with accrued surcharges; administrative fine of 50% up to 300% of the omitted tax; seizure of bank accounts and assets; mandatory criminal referral to the Public Prosecutor's Office.

Director personal liability: Foundation council members and directors who sign false declarations are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and civil restitution; criminal conviction carries imprisonment of up to five years, judicial travel bans, disqualification from holding Panamanian director or resident agent positions.
Shell Company / Abusive Private Interest Foundation

Statutory sanction: Economic substance penalties, loss of territorial tax benefits, mandatory automatic exchange of financial account and foundation information, denial of tax residency certificates, and cancellation of the foundation's beneficial-ownership registry licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent may be held civilly liable for the principal's unpaid taxes; administrative travel bans and prohibition orders may be imposed; criminal convictions for sham facilitation carry imprisonment penalties of up to three years.
Trade-Based Money Laundering and AML Infractions

Statutory sanction: Criminal asset confiscation (extinción de dominio), fines of two to five times the laundered value, revocation of foundation registration, and cross-reporting by DGI and the Panamanian Financial Analysis Unit to the Public Prosecutor's Office.

Director personal liability: Officers, trustees and beneficial owners face imprisonment of up to twelve years under Panama's money-laundering provisions, preventive seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and permanent director disqualification.
