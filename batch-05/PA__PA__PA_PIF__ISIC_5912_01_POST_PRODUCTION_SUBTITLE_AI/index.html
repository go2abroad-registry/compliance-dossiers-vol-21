Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The Foundation must be endowed with an initial patrimony adequate to fulfill its stated purpose; a declared initial endowment of at least USD 10,000 is standard market practice. No statutory escrow account is required: the endowment may be contributed in cash or in kind, held at the Foundation's registered office or in any Panamanian or foreign bank account, and the capital need not be fully paid at formation provided the Patrimony (Fondo) is declared and made available to the Foundation Council.

Funding & unlock path: Dirección Nacional de Bomberos de Panamá — Certificado de Seguridad Contra Incendios (fire safety certificate) for the office/studio space; Ministerio de Salud (MINSA) workplace inspection under Resolución No. 457 de 2015 covering occupational health, lighting, and ergonomic requirements for screen-based work; structural load verification of server/rack room by a licensed engineer where such equipment exceeds 5 kPa floor load; additional municipal property usage certificate (Uso de Suelo) for the registered commercial address. For an AI voice-cloning studio, biometric/vocal data handling should adhere to the law on personal data and the Data Protection Law (Law No. 81 of 2019) as an occupational and consumer protection precondition.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; digital portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa). Customs export control: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) via the SICAA electronic system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) obtains its statutory TIN (RUC - Registro Único de Contribuyente) through a mandatory Panama-licensed resident agent. Procedure: (1) execute the foundation deed/acta under Law 25 of 1995; (2) present to DGI a completed RUC application, the foundation statute, notarized identity documents of the foundation council and beneficiaries, and a sworn local address declaration; (3) elect the applicable tax status: pure holding foundation (no Panamanian-source commercial activity), or commercial taxpayer with Panamanian operations; (4) append an ITBMS registration request when invoicing Panamanian customers is contemplated; (5) obtain digital-signature certificates and activate the DGI Sede Electrónica e-invoicing module. Registration is filed in person or by power of attorney; no physical office is required for an offshore service exporter.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A PIF is a hybrid instrument; the statutory default is that income with a Panamanian source is taxable (ISR corporate rate of 25%) while all foreign-source income falls outside Panamanian income tax. A PIF that does not conduct commercial or industrial activity in the Republic, does not transact with Panamanian-source assets, and pays its annual B/.300 foundation tax enjoys tax exemption over its offshore assets and distributions. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — A PIF is a hybrid instrument; the statutory default is that income with a Panamanian source is taxable (ISR corporate rate of 25%) while all foreign-source income falls outside Panamanian income tax. A PIF that does not conduct commercial or industrial activity in the Republic, does not transact with Panamanian-source assets, and pays its annual B/.300 foundation tax enjoys tax exemption over its offshore assets and distributions. No entity-level withholding applies to beneficiary distributions of foreign-source funds.

Available election forms

Panama does not maintain a domestic US-style check-the-box regime; instead, a PIF may: (i) elect pure settlement-vehicle status and remain outside the ISR/ITBMS net; (ii) elect commercial-enterprise status by registering a Panamanian branch/business in the RUC (triggering ISR at 25% and ITBMS at 7%); (iii) obtain an advance DGI exemption letter confirming non-taxability under Law 25 of 1995; (iv) align hybrid/treaty characterization for BEPS purposes on distributions; and (v) for U.S. tax owners, rely on the default corporate classification of a Panamanian PIF under Treasury Regulation Section 301.7701-3(b), unless affirmative partnership or trust classification is justified by facts. The AI studio may elect to be a pure exporter of IT services with zero-rate ITBMS invoicing and no domestic taxable base.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 (Panamanian Balboas, at par with USD) in annual gross revenue is the cap to qualify as a simplified small taxpayer (pequeño contribuyente), who is generally relieved from ITBMS and pays a simplified ISR. There is no general turnover threshold below which ITBMS registration is waived: any person habitually carrying out taxable transfers of goods/services in Panama, or importing goods, must obtain a RUC and register for ITBMS. A pure exporter of offshore AI post-production services (effective use outside Panama) is not required to charge Panamanian consumption tax, but must be registered to issue zero-rated export e-invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% ITBMS) is available for (i) goods physically exported from Panama via an authorized customs port, and (ii) services supplied by a Panamanian taxpayer to a non-resident recipient for effective use or exploitation entirely outside Panama (place-of-effective-use test). Automated subtitle transcription and AI voice-cloning delivered digitally to foreign clients qualifies when the contract identifies a non-resident beneficiary and the finished files are consumed in a foreign end-market. Supplies to a Panamanian branch of a foreign buyer, or services contracted through a local intermediary, fail the test and are taxable at 7%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / Documento Único de Aduana - DUA) filed in SICAA for physical exports; not required for pure service deliveries
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing exit from Panamanian customs territory
  • Zero-rated ITBMS electronic invoice (factura con tarifa 0%) with DGI validation code and QR
  • Signed service agreement / purchase order with the non-resident beneficiary and explicit offshore-use clause
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 / bank credit advice) proving collection through a Panamanian bank account
  • Digital delivery evidence for AI subtitle/dubbing services: handover logs, server metadata and IP-location records showing foreign consumption

Mandatory electronic invoicing via the DGI Sede Electrónica with real-time clearance and integration through DGI web services/API (or browser module). Every invoice, including 0% export invoices, must be generated in the DGI e-invoicing module, digitally signed, transmitted at issuance, and display a QR code and DGI-assigned validation code; customers receive the invoice only after DGI acceptance (digital stamping replaces the former physical fiscal seal). Fiscal printers are limited to DGI-authorized offline taxpayers and are not accepted for export invoicing. The studio must archive the DGI acuse de recibo for each zero-rated invoice.

DGI cross-matches zero-rated e-invoices, customs declarations and banking records. Where export status is disproven, DGI assesses the invoice at 7% (or the applicable special rate), adds statutory moratory interest (up to 1.5% monthly) and fines up to 100% of the omitted tax; refunded or credited ITBMS is clawed back with interest. False zero-rating documentation triggers criminal defraudación tributaria charges under Law 8 of 2010 (imprisonment of 3 to 6 years), debarment from the DGI e-invoicing zero-rate authorization, and mandatory reporting by the bank to the Unidad de Análisis Financiero as a suspicious transaction under Law 23 of 2015.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama imposes no exchange controls and maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. The absence of a domestic central bank means there is no active controlled FX market; commercial banks freely execute USD-denominated and foreign currency transactions. Profit and capital remittances to non-residents are generally unrestricted, provided that the underlying funds originate from Panama-source income that has complied with Panamanian territorial income tax laws and that anti-money laundering recordkeeping obligations are satisfied. For a Private Interest Foundation, distributions of foreign-source assets or income to foreign beneficiaries attract no Panamanian withholding taxes, whereas distributions sourced from Panama-productive operations may be subject to the applicable dividend or distribution tax under the Spanish-language territorial tax code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements in Panama. Because the country is dollarized and has no exchange control regime, all export and services receipts denominated in USD are freely retained by the exporting entity. No mandatory conversion or repatriation timeline applies to proceeds from international transcription, subtitle localization, AI voice cloning, or post-production services supplied to foreign clients.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama does not maintain a central bank. Banking supervision and prudential regulation is vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Superintendency of Banks of Panama). There is no separate foreign exchange administration or central monetary authority because Panama is a fully dollarized economy; the US dollar has legal tender status and the Panamanian balboa is an accounting unit issued only as low-denomination coinage.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI and payment gateway accounts have only conditional viability for a Panama-domiciled Private Interest Foundation operating the target AI post-production business. Stripe and Wise are not licensed or chartered as banks in Panama and do not maintain a Panama-registered operational branch that would permit direct seller-of-record treasury services. They can be engaged solely as low-value subscription collection rails where the foundation has a validated Tier-1 bank account, a signed merchant agreement, and no breach of the Panama Anti-Money Laundering regulations issued under Decreto 210/2017. Feasibility rating: Medium; legal constraint: any non-bank payment processor that handles cross-border flows on behalf of Panamanian legal entities must be registered with the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) as a remittance or payments company, and the PIF must maintain full audit evidence demonstrating that such processors are not being used as a substitute for regulated deposit-taking and treasury custody.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (with a preferential 15% compulsory rate for the first B/.50,000 of taxable income; the excess is taxed at 25%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% on dividends distributed from profits already subject to Panamanian corporate income tax; 10% on distributions made from untaxed or exempt profits; distributions of genuinely foreign-source accumulated profits to non-resident shareholders are outside Panama's territorial withholding charge.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is due within three months after the fiscal year-end; for a calendar-year taxpayer, the ordinary deadline is 31 March of the following calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.1,000,000 (one million Panamanian Balboas, at par with the US dollar) of aggregate controlled transactions in the preceding fiscal year; taxpayers with gross income exceeding B/.1,500,000 must additionally prepare the Master File and Local File and submit Form 600 together with the annual income tax return.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) under Law 254 of 2021 and its implementing executive decrees apply to a PIF conducting 'relevant activities', which include a service centre, distribution centre, headquarters, financing, pure equity holding, shipping, banking, and insurance. An AI subtitle transcription and voice cloning studio earning active commercial revenue must satisfy the full-economic-substance test: physical office presence in Panama, qualified employees in Panama, local direction and management, adequate expenditure proportionate to the activity, and genuine value-adding functions. A PIF used solely as a passive invoicing shell will fail ESR and may lose its territorial tax treatment and beneficial-ownership standing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Prosecution for fiscal fraud; fine equal to 100% to 300% of the evaded tax plus monthly interest surcharges; loss of the territoriality benefit; cancellation of the PIF's fiscal registration and potential criminal conviction under the Código Penal.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and formal 'fundador' representatives are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and civil restitution. Criminal conviction carries imprisonment of 3 to 8 years, asset seizure, and a travel ban pending satisfaction of the tax liability.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The DGI may recharacterize the PIF's foreign-source income as Panama-source under anti-abuse rules; ESR fines up to B/.100,000; suspension or revocation of the foundation charter for repeated non-compliance.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the PIF under Law 25 of 1995 where sham or abuse is proven, making directors personally liable for debts and tax. Directors may be subjected to civil restitution, prohibition on leaving Panama, and imprisonment for unlawful administration.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines from B/.10,000 to B/.1,000,000; registration of the scope of the foundation's assets may be suspended; the DGI and Public Registry may issue enforcement notices that freeze distributions and client payments.

Director personal liability: Directors and trustees who manage the PIF face criminal prosecution for money laundering, with imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, personal fines based on the value laundered, civil forfeiture of assets, and judicial travel bans during the investigation.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: 20% penalty imposed on the transfer-pricing adjustment; denial of deductions for undocumented payments; monthly interest of 1.5% on the adjusted deficiency; possible retroactive assessment within the five-year statute of limitations.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the return are personally liable to the extent the false documentation caused under-taxation. The DGI may obtain a court order attaching personal directors' assets and a travel interdiction until the adjusted tax is secured.
Treaty Abuse and Improper Withholding

Statutory sanction: The deferred withholding tax becomes immediately due and payable, plus a surcharge equal to 100% of the amount not withheld and monthly interest at 1.5%; treaty benefits are denied retroactively with re-characterization of the entire dividend as Panama-source.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the unwithheld distribution are personally liable for the gross withholding amount. Criminal proceedings for illegal enrichment or fraud may follow, carrying imprisonment, restitution orders, and travel bans pending payment.
