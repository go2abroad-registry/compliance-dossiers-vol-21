Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá – Dirección General, Sección de Fundaciones de Interés Privado (Public Registry of Panama; headquarters, Panama City; submission window for notarized instruments).

Governing statute

Ley No. 25 de 12 de junio de 1995 (Ley de Fundaciones de Interés Privado), as amended by Ley No. 8 de 2017 on international fiscal transparency; applicable provisions Arts. 1, 5–10, 16 and 28 (charter contents, endowment, registered agent, foundation council); supplemented by the Public Registry regulatory framework (Acuerdo No. 3 de 2014).

Formation / constitutional filings

Escritura Pública de Constitución de Fundación de Interés Privado (Foundation Charter Deed), protocolized before a Notary of the Public Circuit of Panama and inscribed in the Foundations Section of the Public Registry.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 17 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Law 25/1995 (Private Interest Foundations) mandates a declared initial endowment ('patrimonio inicial') of not less than USD 10,000.00 or its equivalent; no escrow account or bank deposit certification is required at formation. The Founder's declaration in the Foundation Charter suffices; the endowment may be cash, securities or in-kind assets. Registration fee of USD 250.00 is payable to the Public Registry, and the foundation is automatically assigned an RUC upon inscription (Law 8/2017).

Funding & unlock path: No civil-defense clearance is required for digital-only micro-SaaS operations. If an office is opened: (i) annual fire-safety inspection and certification by the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá; (ii) municipal structural occupancy certificate (permiso de uso) verifying load capacity and egress; and (iii) MITRADEL workplace safety compliance – occupational-risk induction, first-aid equipment and an internal safety handbook. These are administrative self-assessments, not substantive permits.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
First Resolution of the Foundation Council: (1) The Foundation Charter and its Regulations are ratified as binding constitutional instruments. (2) The declared initial endowment of USD 10,000.00 is accepted and recorded as the foundation's patrimony. (3) The registered office is fixed at the Resident Agent's address, Panama City, Republic of Panama; the Resident Agent is appointed under Law 25 of 1995. (4) The undersigned Council members are confirmed, and the Chairman and Secretary are elected. (5) The Chairman is authorized to execute banking, contractual, and regulatory filings for the foundation.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial licensing authority is triggered for a cloud software subscription business (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 511210). Service-sector reference authority: Ministerio de Comercio e Industrias (MICI) – Dirección Nacional de Empresas; any municipal 'Aviso de Operación' is administered by the Municipality of Panama (Alcaldía).

Category: Not applicable – remote digital micro-SaaS requires no national operating license. Only if a physical leased office is maintained, a municipal business license ('Aviso de Operación / Licencia Comercial de Negocio') is required; for the foundation itself, the Registry inscription serves as the legal personality document.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; official portals: www.dgi.gob.pa (Sede Electrónica / Sistema Tributario DGI) for RUC management and filings, and the Facturación Electrónica de Panamá (FEP) platform for digital tax documents.

E-government filing portal

The Private Interest Foundation (PIF) is constituted under Ley 25 de 1995 and obtains its juridical personality from the Registro Público de Panamá. For national tax registration: (1) register the foundation charter (Acta Constitutiva) and the appointment of the foundation council with the Public Registry; (2) obtain certified extracts of the Public Registry inscription and valid identity documents / power of attorney of the legal representative; (3) complete the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) application through the DGI's Sistema Tributario portal or at an authorized DGI attention center, submitting the foundation certificate, registered address proof, and representative appointment; (4) upon approval, the PIF is assigned a RUC with an ISR and ITBMS profile. A PIF that will only earn foreign-source SaaS subscription revenue (via Paddle or Lemon Squeezy as Merchant of Record) should register in the non-recurrent / export-services category, indicating no Panamanian-source taxable transactions. Note: the PIF is also liable to the annual Public Registry foundation fee of B/.300 (not an income tax) to remain in good standing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope (Panama taxes only Panamanian-source income; the PIF is a separate juridical person subject to entity-level taxation exclusively on Panama-source income, while foreign-source software subscription revenue is outside the Panamanian tax base; no pass-through treatment is available for domestic tax purposes)

Available election forms

Panama has no 'check-the-box' or hybrid mismatch election regime. Available statutory elections include: (a) Régimen Especial de Pequeño Contribuyente for taxpayers with annual gross income up to B/.36,000, replacing full ITBMS filing with a fixed monthly contribution and simplified ISR compliance; (b) voluntary registration as an ITBMS subject even absent Panama-source transactions (useful for zero-rated export invoicing); (c) election to file as a non-recurring exporter with documentary evidence of foreign-source revenue; (d) for larger operations, the Sede de Empresas Multinacionales (SEM) regime — not applicable to a consumer micro-SaaS PIF; the PIF may also request formal written tax consultations (consultas) from the DGI to confirm territorial treatment of specific revenue flows.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold for ITBMS registration; registration is required from the first taxable transfer of goods or rendering of services sourced in Panama. The special small-taxpayer threshold is B/.36,000 of annual gross income, allowing the simplified fixed-contribution regime instead of full ITBMS compliance. For this PIF, subscription services supplied to foreign end users and settled through foreign Merchant-of-Record platforms (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy) are not Panamanian-source and do not trigger ITBMS registration; if the PIF voluntarily registers, such export transactions must be invoiced at 0%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS is territorial: services rendered for use, benefit, or economic exploitation outside Panama are zero-rated (0%) under Código Fiscal exemptions for exported services. The consumer micro-SaaS PIF qualifies for 0% on subscription software revenue where (i) the contractual counterparty is the foreign Merchant of Record (Paddle or Lemon Squeezy) or a foreign end client; (ii) the software service is digitally delivered and effectively used/enjoyed outside Panama — server location alone does not create Panamanian nexus; (iii) export invoices are issued at 0% citing the statutory basis; and (iv) consideration is received from abroad into the PIF's bank account. Since the MoR is the seller of record to consumers, the PIF's cross-border flow is a B2B export of services, not a B2C Panama supply. VAT/GST on consumer sales is collected by Paddle/Lemon Squeezy under their own global compliance obligations, with no Panamanian ITBMS liability.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUS/Solicitud de Exportación) — required only for physical goods; not applicable to SaaS services
  • Factura de Exportación / FEP export e-invoice issued at 0% ITBMS with legal basis stated
  • International service contract between the PIF and the foreign Merchant of Record or end client (Paddle/Lemon Squeezy merchant agreement)
  • Proof of foreign-source receipt: SWIFT/foreign remittance into the PIF's Panamanian bank account
  • MoR settlement statements and subscription revenue reports evidencing foreign end-user consumption outside Panama

Mandatory electronic invoicing under the Facturación Electrónica de Panamá (FEP) framework established by Law 256 of 2022, Executive Decree 63 of 2023 and DGI Resolution 201-1129 of 2023. RUC holders are required to issue digitally signed e-invoices, credit/debit notes, final-consumer receipts, and export invoices in XML format, transmit them to the DGI FEP platform, obtain real-time validation, and print the DGI authorization code and QR/digital stamp on each document. Phased implementation applies by taxpayer category, beginning with large taxpayers in 2024 and extending to the remaining RUC population by 2025; deadlines have been periodically adjusted by DGI resolutions. A PIF with exclusively foreign-source revenue and no Panamanian taxable operations is not obliged to operate FEP, but any voluntarily registered zero-rated export invoices must transit the FEP system.

DGI audits export zero-rating on the basis of documentary proof of foreign use/exploitation and receipt of payment from abroad. Failure to substantiate the export triggers reassessment of the 7% ITBMS, late-payment surcharges (recargos) of 5% per month or fraction, up to 100%, plus moratory interest under Código Fiscal Articles 898-904; administrative fines under Article 897 may also apply, including penalties for non-compliant electronic invoicing. Fraudulent zero-rating claims expose the PIF and its representatives to criminal fiscal prosecution (Código Fiscal Articles 939-941) and predicate-offence treatment under the AML framework (Ley 23 de 2015). Documentary records must be retained for at least 5 years, and the DGI assessment window is typically 3 years, extended to 6 where no return is filed and up to 10 in fraud cases. Panama has no exchange-control regime because the US dollar is legal tender; the 180-day benchmark reflects the customs export-liquidation convention for goods and the DGI's evidentiary expectation for crediting export service proceeds. In the MoR model, the PIF should ensure settlement reports and bank inflows are consistent, traced, and archived to withstand clawback scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility in both current and capital accounts. The Panamanian Balboa (PAB) is a fixed 1:1 unit of account against the USD and circulates only as fractional coins; the USD is the operative clearing currency. There are no exchange controls, no central-bank approval requirements, and no restrictions on profit remittance or capital repatriation. The only practical friction is standard bank KYC, SBP transaction reporting, and U.S. OFAC screening during USD clearing through U.S. correspondent banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panama imposes no surrender, repurchase, conversion, or repatriation timelines on export proceeds, including cross-border SaaS subscription revenue. Exporters and service providers may leave foreign currency offshore, convert it freely, or use it for any lawful purpose without prior notification to the SBP; only standard AML/CTF threshold reports (monthly when cash or cash-equivalent amounts exceed USD 10,000) and the required accounting recognition of the revenue apply.

Central bank supervision: No central bank exists in Panama. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) supervises banking, credit, and AML/CFT conduct; the Banco Nacional de Panamá (a state-owned commercial bank) mints fractional Balboa coins and acts as fiscal agent but exercises zero monetary-policy or FX-control authority. The U.S. dollar is legal tender on a full dollarized basis.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a settlement-aggregator conduit; Low-Medium as a substitute for a Panamanian bank account. Paddle and Lemon Squeezy are merchant-of-record platforms that lawfully collect and remit SaaS subscription funds; this is the principal inbound settlement rail and is fully compatible with Panamanian law. However, offshore neobank/EMI accounts (e.g., UK FCA- or EU-licensed e-money institutions) are not regulated by the SBP; Panamanian licensed banks treat such EMIs as remittance originators rather than bank reference sources. The PIF should maintain at least one domestic USD operational account as the primary treasury vehicle, use EMIs only as inbound settlement conduits, and avoid permanently parking foundation cash in unaudited e-money wallets. Panama's financial-innovation framework provides a sandbox for licensed payment institutions, but established EMI-native treasury accounts are not yet available.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

No exchange-control approval is required because the Balboa is USD-equivalent. Field 70 should read 'PRIVATE INTEREST FOUNDATION CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION PER LAW 25 OF 1995 - SAAS TECHNOLOGY BUSINESS' and field 50K must reference the regulated originator institution. The bank will reconcile the wired amount against the signed contribution resolution and the Foundation's general ledger. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 2,500,000 (USD 2.5 million) of annual gross revenue, or B/. 750,000 (USD 750,000) of cross-border related-party transactions, triggers mandatory Local File preparation. A Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated gross revenue exceeds B/. 15 million.

Economic substance scope: Panama enacted Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) under Law 254 of 2021. A Private Interest Foundation engaged in holding intangible assets and invoicing SaaS subscriptions must demonstrate physical management presence in Panama if it claims Panamanian tax residency. Required substance indicators include local board meetings, bank accounts in Panama, accounting records, and compliance files. Non-compliance can result in administrative fines of up to USD 100,000 and removal from the tax register.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Panama Fiscal Code Article 756, criminal tax evasion is punishable with imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, plus a fine equal to 100% of the evaded tax. Civil liability includes full back tax, a monthly surcharge of 2%, and default interest of 0.9% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or direct the evasion can be held personally liable for restitution. Criminal liability extends to officeholders; foreign residents may face Interpol red-flag proceedings, travel bans, and extradition upon issuance of a Panama arrest warrant.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: ESR Law 254 of 2021 imposes administrative penalties of USD 5,000 to USD 100,000 for non-compliant entities. The tax administration may also disregard the foundation's separate legal personality for tax purposes and recalculate income at arm's length.

Director personal liability: Directors and foundation council members may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and costs where the foundation is deemed a shell. In aggravated cases, a director may face disqualification from serving as a company officer and imprisonment of up to 3 years for fraudulent insolvency arising from deliberately unpaid assessed tax.
AML / Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Panama's Law 23 of 2015, as reformed, criminalizes money laundering with penalties of 5 to 10 years of imprisonment and fines of B/. 1 million to B/. 5 million for predicate offenses. Financial regulators may also impose administrative penalties on the entity of up to B/. 10 million.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for failing to file Suspicious Activity Reports with the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF). Personal exposure includes asset forfeiture, travel restriction orders, and criminal conviction resulting in incarceration. CFO and COO officers who knowingly process flagged payments face both criminal and civil penalties.
Transfer Pricing Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Panama's transfer pricing regime permits the DGI to make primary or secondary adjustments and imposes a 35% penalty on the understated tax, plus interest. Large taxpayers may be automatically escalated to the DGI specialized audit division.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly prepare or rely on fabricated transfer pricing documentation may be charged with complicity in tax evasion, rendering them personally liable for adjusted tax and subject to director disqualification orders from the Registro Público.
Late Filing and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 2% of the tax due, capped at 50%. Submission of false statements carries a fine of USD 1,000 to USD 10,000 in addition to criminal sanctions for making false declarations before the tax administration.

Director personal liability: The responsible officer signing the return may be subject to personal fines and, where the false statement conceals tax evasion, a prison term of 2 to 5 years. Travel bans may be issued to secure the presence of the individual for investigation.
