E-government filing portal

The DGI must verify the physical or registered address, then issue a provisional RUC followed by a definitive RUC within the statutory period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT/ITBM (0%) applies to commercial export sales of tangible goods that physically depart Panamanian customs territory, and to certain offshore B2B services where the service is rendered and used outside Panama, provided the seller has a valid RUC, invoices under Panama tax rules, and retains export contracts and proof of foreign receipt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Aduanera de Exportación)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (B/L or AWB)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Collection Evidence (Swift or credit-dated remittance)

Panama mandates DGI-approved electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) with phased implementation. Covered taxpayers must use a certified fiscal invoicing system that transmits each invoice and credit note in real time to the DGI electronic clearing platform, applies a digital signature and unique confirmation code, and generates the required QR code prior to delivery of the document to the buyer.

DGI and Panama Customs (Aduanas) jointly audit export zero-rating claims. Failure to substantiate physical exit or collection within the 180-day repatriation period triggers reversal of the zero rate and collection of the omitted ITBM at 7%, plus statutory surcharges and interest. Intentional false declarations expose the taxpayer to minimum fines of 100% of the omitted tax, administrative penalties, and criminal tax evasion prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized economy without a domestic central-bank issued currency. The Panamanian balboa (PAB) exists only as a nominal unit of account pegged at 1:1 to the U.S. dollar, and USD is legal tender throughout the Republic. There are no foreign exchange controls, no restrictions on capital account or current account transactions, no prior central bank authorizations, and no limits on dividend or profit repatriation from a Panamanian Private Interest Foundation. All ordinary B2B MRO catalog sales proceeds may be received in USD or any freely convertible foreign currency, and retained offshore or in Panamanian banks without forced conversion. Profit remittance to non-resident foundation beneficiaries is restricted only by AML/KYC documentation requirements, not by exchange control regulations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama does not impose any statutory export proceeds surrender requirements, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines. Exporters and e-commerce operators, including a Private Interest Foundation, may retain all export earnings in foreign currency and deploy capital and profits freely through the international banking system.

Central bank supervision: Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá); Panama has no central bank, with Banco Nacional de Panamá acting as the state-owned fiscal agent and government treasury service provider.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Panama has adopted Law 75 of 2019 and the Superintendency of Banks' Regulatory Agreement for Innovation, but there is no comprehensive licensed electronic money institution (EMI) regime equivalent to the European Electronic Money Directive. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled in the UK, EU, or other jurisdictions are not recognized for Panamanian clearing, cannot open local settlement accounts without a Panamanian banking license, and may be treated as unregulated payment processors. They are viable as supplementary collection rails only, and must be supplemented by the foundation's own bank account at a Panamanian commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial capital injection into the Private Interest Foundation, use a SWIFT MT103 wire from the funding institution (typically the founder's personal or corporate bank) to the beneficiary account at a Panamanian commercial bank. The wire must include the Foundation's legal name, foundation identification number, account number, and a clear purpose code such as 'CAPITAL INCREASE' or 'SUBSCRIPTION OF FOUNDATION ASSETS'. The instruction must be supported by a notarized capital subscription resolution of the Foundation Council, a letter of instruction signed by the registered council, and evidence of the founder's legal identity and tax status. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on net taxable Panama-source income of juridical persons; the reduced small-taxpayer bracket of 15% may apply only to qualifying taxpayers falling strictly under Ley 49 de 2014 regimes.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding applies to dividends or profit distributions made by a Panamanian entity out of Panama-source taxable profits. Distributions paid from foreign-source profits are outside the territorial tax base and should not be subject to withholding if the foreign-source character can be demonstrated.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed within three months after the end of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the statutory deadline is 31 March of the following year; DGI may grant no extension by default unless a formal arrangement is made before the deadline.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Part I, Línea 1a Panama-source gross receipts: gross proceeds from industrial MRO catalog sales, commissions, logistics services and other business activity where the contract is concluded, the goods are delivered, or the economic use/exploitation occurs in Panama. Report only Panama-source revenue. Foreign e-commerce orders fulfilled from foreign warehouses to non-Panama customers are outside the territorial scope and should not be reported in this line even if collected through a Panama payment-acceptance account. Late filing and non-payment attract a 10% late-filing surcharge on the tax due plus 1% monthly interest on unpaid amounts. DGI may additionally impose administrative fines of approximately B/.100 to B/.10,000, block RUC operations, estimate tax by internal audit, and institute criminal tax proceedings for willful failure to file or false filing.

Attach a detailed foreign-source income schedule, bank statements from the foreign sales accounts, warehouse or drop-shipping contracts, and delivery documentation to substantiate the foreign sourcing. Do not net foreign losses against Panama-source income. Late filing and non-payment attract a 10% late-filing surcharge on the tax due plus 1% monthly interest on unpaid amounts. Part II, Línea 2 Deductions directly attributable to Panama-source income: purchases of MRO inventory, warehousing, shipping, sales commissions, chargeback losses, and operating expenses that are ordinary and necessary for the production of Panama-source taxable income. Foreign central costs and IP acquisition costs should be allocated conservatively using documented apportionment keys and maintained in the DGI file. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panama's transfer pricing law and implementing regulations require the Local File (and, where applicable, Master File) when the taxpayer's annual gross revenue exceeds B/.2,500,000 or the taxpayer engages in related-party transactions exceeding B/.1,000,000 in a fiscal period; Master File documentation is additionally required where the multinational group's consolidated gross revenue exceeds B/.13,000,000. The local currency is the Panamanian balboa (B/.), pegged at par to the U.S. dollar.

Economic substance scope: The Panamanian Economic Substance Regulations (Ley 254 de 2021 and implementing rules) require entities carrying on relevant activities, including holding/managing assets, distribution and service centers, and headquarters, to demonstrate adequate employees, physical premises, and board management in Panama. A PIF engaged as a pure B2B MRO e-commerce operator through foreign fulfillment, foreign bank accounts, and foreign customers must maintain substance documentation showing management outside Panama or otherwise justify non-residence; if it holds Panama operating assets or earns Panama-source income, it must file the annual economic substance report. Failure to do so can create a statutory shell-company presumption and trigger substantial penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Unreported Panama-Source Income

Statutory sanction: DGI may issue a tax assessment based on bank deposits and reconstructed sales, charge the 25% ISR plus 10% late-payment surcharge and 1% monthly interest, impose fines of up to B/.10,000, and refer the case to the Public Prosecutor for tax fraud. Criminal tax evasion carries imprisonment of two to five years and financial penalties of up to approximately B/.50,000.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members and the protector who sign or knowingly approve false tax returns become jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, surcharges, and interest. Criminal proceedings may also trigger preventive seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and judicial orders prohibiting departure from Panama pending final sentence.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Misstatement of Foreign-Source Margins

Statutory sanction: DGI may disregard the transactional price, make a primary or secondary adjustment to the Panama taxable income, apply the 25% corporate rate to the upward adjustment, and charge late-payment interest. Administrative penalties may include 40% of the understatement for consecutive non-compliant years and cancellation of the right to carry losses.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute intercompany contracts with non-arm's-length pricing or destroy transfer pricing documentation can be held personally liable for the resulting assessment. The courts may pierce the foundation veil, authorize attachment of directors' personal bank accounts, and order individual travel prohibitions where the transfer pricing scheme was used to facilitate tax evasion.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Panama's legal entity regime permits judicial disregard of the juridical personality, nullification of the foundation's protected patrimony, and recharacterization of the PIF as transparent for tax purposes. DGI may assess the founder personally on the foundation's worldwide income and impose fines under ESR for failure to file substance documentation, including penalties per annum and dissolution of the entity.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and the foundation council are civilly liable to creditors for failure to exercise real control; they may also face criminal liability for aiding the unlawful use of a legal entity. Judges may order personal restitution of diverted MRO sale proceeds, issue travel bans for persons participating in sham administration, and impose imprisonment of up to seven years for fraudulent insolvency or abuse of limited liability.
AML Violations / Money Laundering Through MRO E-Commerce Receipts

Statutory sanction: Panama's AML Law 23 of 2015, together with the Financial Information and Reporting Unit (Unidad de Análisis Financiero), authorizes severe administrative penalties, including fines of up to B/.1,000,000 per infraction, closure of accounts, revocation of the PIF's registration, and criminal prosecution for money laundering with sentences of five to twelve years of imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members, protectors, and beneficial owners who control the accounts or direct the transfers are personally subject to forfeiture of all proceeds, criminal fines, and imprisonment. Directors may be barred from holding banking or corporate positions, subjected to passport seizure and Interpol red notices, and ordered to make full civil restitution to the harmed commercial counterparties.
