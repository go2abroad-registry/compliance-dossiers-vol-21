Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas, Panama; online taxpayer portal SIA (Sistema Integrado de Atención al Contribuyente) / e-Tax platform (efactura.dgi.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must first be duly registered with the Public Registry of Panama through a licensed resident agent. To obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), the PIF must submit its foundation charter, the resident agent's notarized acceptance, identification documents of the founders and initial officers, registered address in Panama, and evidence of compliance with minimum capital requirements. The application is filed electronically through the DGI's SIA portal or in-person at a DGI attention center; a provisional or definitive RUC is issued upon verification, and the PIF is then enrolled in the national tax registry for ITBMS and income tax administrative purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must first be duly registered with the Public Registry of Panama through a licensed resident agent. To obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), the PIF must submit its foundation charter, the resident agent's notarized acceptance, identification documents of the founders and initial officers, registered address in Panama, and evidence of compliance with minimum capital requirements. The application is filed electronically through the DGI's SIA portal or in-person at a DGI attention center; a provisional or definitive RUC is issued upon verification, and the PIF is then enrolled in the national tax registry for ITBMS and income tax administrative purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a check-the-box regime or allow hybrid mismatch planning for PIFs. A Private Interest Foundation is a hybrid-risk entity treated as a separate taxpayer for domestic tax purposes but only taxed on Panama-source income; no pass-through election is available. A PIF may, however, elect into the simplified ITBMS regime (Small Taxpayer Regime) if its gross taxable turnover falls below the statutory threshold and may voluntarily register for ITBMS even when not legally required; foreign-tax-credit elections are unavailable in the absence of a tax treaty credit mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PAB 36,000 (Panamanian Balboa) annual gross turnover for the simplified small-taxpayer regime; any person engaged in taxable transfers of goods or services in Panama is generally required to register and apply ITBMS unless expressly exempt, and non-resident suppliers providing services used or exploited in Panama trigger VAT registration when liable for collection

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% ITBMS) applies to the export of tangible goods from Panama, including goods re-exported after being received through a return-merchandise reverse-logistics operation, provided title passes to a foreign buyer and the goods are physically removed from Panamanian customs territory within the legally permitted time. Also eligible are services rendered to non-residents and effectively used or exploited outside Panama, and services directly related to exported goods, subject to supporting evidence. Reverse logistics activities involving quality grading, repair, reconditioning, or consolidation of returned merchandise are eligible only if the services are expressly undertaken for and delivered to a foreign principal and the end-user/consumption occurs abroad; the zero-rated status must be documented at the time of invoicing.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera / DUA) demonstrating exit from Panama's customs jurisdiction
  • Bill of Lading (original or verified electronic equivalent) issued by the ocean or air carrier evidencing shipment of the returned merchandise to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) issued by a Panamanian bank or licensed exchange house evidencing receipt of the export proceeds from abroad

Panama mandates electronic invoicing through the DGI e-Factura system. Taxpayers must obtain an electronic authorization code (Código de Autorización Electrónico, CAE) from the DGI before issuing invoices. Invoicing software must comply with the official XML scheme and digital certificate requirements; full real-time per-transaction clearance to the tax authority is not yet implemented, but periodic transmission/listing of electronic invoices is required under the phased mandatory rollout. Digital stamping of fiscal invoices is also required for certain legacy printers. The system applies to ITBMS-registered taxpayers including PIFs undertaking commercial transactions in Panama.

The DGI rigorously audits export zero-rating claims, particularly cross-border reverse-logistics flows, to prevent fictitious exports or re-export schemes. A false zero-rating claim triggers the collector's demand for the omitted ITBMS (7%) plus monthly interest and late-payment surcharges, and fines ranging from 100% to 200% of the omitted tax under the Panama Tax Code. Knowingly issuing a fraudulent zero-rated export invoice may be prosecuted as tax fraud, leading to criminal penalties including imprisonment, disqualification from tax registration, and cancellation or suspension of the PIF's RUC. The DGI may retroactively reassess within five years for ordinary deficiencies and up to ten years in cases of fraud or omitted declarations; export documentation must be retained and produced on demand to avoid a full clawback of zero-rated treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy: the US dollar is legal tender, and the Balboa is only a fractional unit with no independent monetary issuance. There is no exchange control regime and no differentiation between current and capital account transactions. Capital injections, dividends, interest, fees, and profit remittances may be freely made in USD through licensed banks. The sole practical constraints are bank-level AML/KYC requirements, contractual documentation, and adherence to Panamanian territorial taxation rules on Panamanian-source income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline applies in Panama. Exporters retain USD and may allocate proceeds without central bank approval. For banking and customs purposes, trade documents (customs declarations, invoices, bills of lading/air waybills, goods grading certificates) must be retained, but they are not used for exchange-control-based surrender requirements.

Central bank supervision: None. The Republic of Panama has no central bank. Banking supervision is vested in the Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá), while the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF) administers AML/CFT obligations for financial intermediaries. Foreign exchange matters fall under the Ministry of Economy and Finance only for customs and statistical reporting, not for exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium and only as an ancillary payment-rail connector. An unlicensed offshore digital EMI may not offer banking or money transmission services to or from a Panamanian PIF without a banking licence under Decree-Law 9 of 1998, Article 4. Airwallex does not currently maintain a Panamanian banking licence and therefore cannot serve as the primary treasury node. The PIF must hold its main USD clearing account at a Panamanian licensed bank, using the EMI exclusively for cross-border reverse-logistics refunds to counterparties outside Panama where legal designation of the EMI as a collection and disbursement agent is permissible and not deemed prohibited Panamanian money transmission.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must route in US dollars via SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire to the PIF's licensed Panamanian bank account. Panama imposes no exchange-control purpose codes or central bank registration; however, banks apply their own underwriting and OFAC-facing controls. The MT103 field 70 should explicitly state the purpose, e.g. 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — PRIVATE INTEREST FOUNDATION'. The initial injection must be accompanied by a notarized foundation resolution authorising receipt, the foundation charter, and a source-of-funds declaration from the remitter. Funds from non-USD-sending jurisdictions may be converted by the sending bank, with the remitter's bank confirming lawful conversion and adherence to its own capital controls.Outbound reverse-logistics settlements are cleared primarily through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the Panamanian account to beneficiary banks. High-value or contractually structured transactions may use an irrevocable commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600) issued or confirmed by a Panamanian bank. Local USD clearing is available through Panamá ACH, which operates on a same-day or next-day cycle among domestic licensed banks. For mass return-refund payouts to non-Panamanian beneficiaries, the PIF may use an ancillary Airwallex virtual account only if the receiving party lawfully accepts such EMI payments and the Panamanian prime bank retains visibility of the transaction underlying invoice, return authorisation, and goods-received/quality-grade evidence.
