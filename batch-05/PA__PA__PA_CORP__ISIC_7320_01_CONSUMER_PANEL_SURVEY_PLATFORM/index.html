Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama. Principal portals: Sede Electrónica at https://sede.dgi.gob.pa (RUC registration, returns and payments); https://www.dgi.gob.pa for official guidance; mandatory electronic invoicing platform SIFED/DGI Patria with API integration.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.): (1) incorporate in the Registro Público under Ley 32 de 1927 and obtain the corporate Ficha Electronica; (2) appoint the statutory resident agent and directors; (3) file the Aviso de Operación with the municipality only if local business activity is carried out in Panama; (4) obtain a digital signature certificate (Firma Electrónica) and, via the DGI Sede Electrónica, submit the Solicitud de Inscripción de Contribuyente together with the Ficha, apostilled Spanish-translated corporate documents, shareholding structure, and intended ISIC 7320 activity to obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes); (5) request electronic invoicing authorisation (habilitación para facturación electrónica) in the same portal; (6) file Form 2410 (Declaración Jurada de Ingresos de Fuente Extranjera) annually and, where Panama-source revenue exists, the Form 2411 corporate income tax return, Form 217 ITBMS returns, and municipal statements. The procedure may be completed remotely through a licensed Panamanian attorney/CPA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope. The S.A. is a distinct taxable person subject to 25% corporate income tax only on Panama-source income; foreign-source SaaS and market-research income sits outside the Panamanian tax base. Dividends from Panama-source profits incur 10% withholding (20% in certain undistributed/unreported profit scenarios); no pass-through treatment is available; ITBMS zero-rating applies to exported services.

Available election forms

Panama has no US-style check-the-box and no flow-through election for S.A. companies. The territorial regime applies automatically; available elections include: (i) the Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente for annual turnover up to B/. 36,000 (presumptive ITBMS); (ii) the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007, granting preferential taxation on qualifying foreign-source services subject to an approved SEM licence and local substance; (iii) election of monthly vs quarterly ITBMS filing depending on taxable volume (B/. 300,000 threshold); (iv) no hybrid-mismatch election exists, although BEPS-aligned transfer-pricing rules mandate arm's-length documentation for controlled cross-border transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility in both current and capital accounts. The Balboa (PAB) is fixed at 1:1 to the USD and exists only as coins; USD banknotes circulate freely. There are no exchange controls, no central bank authorization for outward remittances, no limits on dividend/profit repatriation, and no restrictions on non-resident ownership of Panamanian S.A. shares or capital accounts. A foreign-owned S.A. may hold, receive, and disburse USD indefinitely inside or outside Panama without approval. The only practical hurdles are (i) proving to the DGI that income is territorial (non-Panamanian source) when repatriating, and (ii) satisfying bank source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership KYC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export or cross-border service receipts. Proceeds from consumer polling / market research SaaS services (ISIC 7320 / NAICS 541910) may be retained in USD in Panamanian or offshore accounts without central bank filing. For bank compliance, such proceeds are recorded as service-export income and must be supported by executed client master service agreements, itemized invoices, and audience-panel delivery reports; no Balboa conversion or repatriation deadline applies.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank. Monetary and FX framework is organized around the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panama (SBP), which supervises banks and enforces AML/CFT under Law 42 of 2001, and the Banco Nacional de Panama (state-owned), which issues the fractional Balboa coinage alongside the U.S. Dollar as legal tender. The SBP also operates the official payment/clearing infrastructure (ACH Panama and SWIFT environment), while the U.S. Federal Reserve de facto sets monetary conditions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Low as a bank substitute, Medium as a payment utility. Panama does not operate a full e-money institution license regime; Panama's banking law (Decree-Law 9 of 1998) requires any institution collecting funds from the public to be licensed by the SBP. Offshore EMIs (e.g., UK FCA or EU-licensed entities) cannot lawfully hold client money for a Panamanian S.A. as a domestic bank replacement. The SBP innovation sandbox (Law 76 of 2021) permits supervised fintech pilots but does not yet grant full EMIs to third-country firms. Practical approach: use Wise and PayPal Mass Payout as registered remittance/PSP channels that settle into a domestic SBP-licensed bank account, while treasury cash balances remain with a Tier-1 Panamanian commercial bank.
