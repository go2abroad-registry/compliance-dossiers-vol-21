Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Directorate of Revenues (Dirección General de Ingresos – DGI) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF), Panama; online taxpayer portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) is incorporated under Law 25 of 1995 and registered in the Public Registry through a Panamanian registered agent. After incorporation, the PIF must obtain its single taxpayer identification number (RUC) from the DGI by filing the DGI registration application, the notarized foundation charter and bylaws, the Public Registry certificate, identification of the founding council members and resident agent, and the notified Panamanian address. Non-resident founders and beneficiaries are permitted and may remain confidential. Once verified, the DGI issues a permanent RUC, which also integrates the foundation into the ITBMS taxpayer registry where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope – The PIF is a separate legal person subject to entity-level taxation only on Panama-source income; foreign-source income, including revenue from offshore digital game publishing and in-app purchases collected through Apple/Google payouts, is not within Panama's income tax net. Economic activities performed in Panama, such as local support, local server deployment, or local monetization, will trigger normal Panama-source entity-level taxation and ITBMS compliance.

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a US-style check-the-box election, pass-through election, or hybrid mismatch classification election for Private Interest Foundations. Tax classification follows the territorial-source rules of the Panama Fiscal Code and Law 25 of 1995. A PIF may elect its internal governance provisions under Law 25, and may voluntarily register for ITBMS where below the small-taxpayer threshold, but no elective pass-through or hybrid branch classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All legal entities carrying out taxable acts in Panama are required to register for ITBMS regardless of turnover. Panama maintains a small-taxpayer simplified regime for annual gross taxable turnover up to B/.36,000 (approximately US$36,000); businesses above that threshold are mandatory ITBMS-registered. For digital service suppliers, including MMORPG platform operators, Panamanian nexus arises from sales of electronically supplied services to consumers located in Panama, and registration under Law 20 of 2019 may apply from the first transaction without a de minimis threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated for ITBMS where title and possession pass to a non-Panama buyer and the goods physically depart Panama under a valid customs export declaration. For services, including offshore digital MMORPG subscription and in-app purchase revenues, zero-rating or non-taxability requires that the service is rendered to a non-resident recipient, is effectively used and enjoyed outside Panama, and is not attributable to a Panamanian business establishment. Platform settlement records from Apple App Store and Google Play should identify the non-Panama platform operator and non-Panama end-consumer pool. Income tax benefits under the territorial system likewise require that the economic activity be performed abroad and, for export-of-goods cases, that export proceeds be repatriated to Panama within 180 days.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUSCA or equivalent Panamanian customs exit document)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing physical exportation from Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/FX statement proving repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days
  • Commercial invoice and sale contract with the non-Panama buyer
  • Platform revenue statements and payout records from Apple App Store and Google Play where services are exported digitally
  • RUC and ITBMS registration certificates and, if applicable, the DGI electronic invoice authorization refs for export invoices

Panama has phased in mandatory electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica) through the DGI's Sede Electrónica platform. Registered taxpayers subject to the electronic-invoicing schedule must issue invoices using DGI-authorized software or qualified cloud/API service providers, transmit invoice datasets to the DGI in real time or immediately upon issuance, and include a DGI authorization code and QR code on each valid invoice. Manual invoices are not allowed for taxpayers in the mandatory electronic-invoicing regime for purposes of ITBMS credits, export substantiation, or refund claims.

The DGI audits export-related zero-rating claims by matching customs exit declarations, bills of lading, platform payment flows, bank transfers, and inward remittance certificates. If documentation is incomplete, contradictory, or the transaction is recharacterized as a Panamanian domestic supply, the DGI will assess ITBMS at 7% on the full invoice value, deny or claw back any refund previously granted, and charge monthly moratory interest plus administrative surcharges. Willful or fraudulent use of forged export documents, fictitious buyers, or false remittance certificates can trigger penalties of up to 100% of the omitted tax, criminal tax-fraud proceedings under Panamanian criminal tax law and Law 8 of 2010, and reopening of prior periods within the applicable five-year statute of limitations. Where the export qualification fails, the income may also be recharacterized as Panama-source and exposed to 25% corporate income tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama maintains unrestricted capital and current account convertibility. There is no legal distinction between capital and current account transactions; no exchange control law, no approval requirement, and no surrender obligation apply to residents or non-residents. The territorial taxation system and absence of exchange controls permit unconditional profit remittance, dividend distribution, and repatriation of foundation capital and earnings to any foreign beneficiary. No withholding tax is imposed on outbound distributions by a non-operating Panamanian Private Interest Foundation under Ley 25 de 1995, provided the foundation does not engage in Panamanian-sourced trade or business activities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline for the repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to channel export receipts through the domestic banking system. Digital service revenues, including Apple App Store and Google Play net settlements for MMORPG in-app purchases, are treated as exported services for territorial tax purposes and may be retained in any foreign financial institution or cleared through any correspondent channel. The only administrative obligation is the maintenance of accurate accounting records supporting the Panamanian-sourced versus foreign-sourced classification of income for the annual tax return (Declaración de Renta).

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama does not have an independent central bank. The US Dollar is constitutional legal tender under Decreto Ley 9 of 1998 regime, and monetary policy is effectively conducted by the US Federal Reserve through full dollarization. Banking supervision and licensing are exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). The state-owned Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the fiscal agent of the central government and provides lender-of-last-resort services, while Panama's coin-only national currency, the Balboa (PAB), circulates at an absolute fixed 1:1 parity with USD and has no independent monetary issuance or exchange rate mechanism.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low immediate viability. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) domiciled in the UK, EU, or Caribbean do not hold a Panamanian banking license and cannot directly provide a local USD account settlement rail that is recognized by Panamanian tax or banking authorities. Panama's SBP maintains an Innovation Hub (Módulo de Innovación Financiera) under Acuerdo 3 of 2023, but no comprehensive EMI licensing regime for digital payment institutions has been operationalized as of the current stage. A PIF is therefore advised to use regulated Panamanian commercial banks as the primary settlement gateway and may engage foreign EMIs only as auxiliary collection channels for global in-app purchase proceeds, provided the ultimate concentration account is held with an SBP-supervised bank. The 2022 Ley 21 crypto-asset authorization was partially declared unconstitutional by the Panamanian Supreme Court and offers no safe harbor for treasury clearing operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire routing for the initial endowment and subsequent capital contributions: (1) identify the beneficiary account by the exact foundation name as registered in Panama, the bank's SWIFT BIC, the USD account number, and the foundation's registered address; (2) remit via SWIFT MT103 from the sending bank; (3) include in field 70 the narrative 'Initial endowment contribution to Private Interest Foundation under Ley 25 de 1995' or 'Capital contribution – software publishing entity PA_PIF'; (4) Panama does not use IBAN, but the beneficiary bank's U.S. correspondent address (typically a U.S. Fed-wire-agented institution such as Wells Fargo, JPMorgan Chase, or Citibank) must be listed for a USD clearing; (5) obtain a pre-notification letter from the foundation's council confirming the expected amount, sender, and purpose to the bank's compliance department; (6) the initial endowment is not taxable as income under the Panamanian territorial system and must be recorded as a capital surplus entry in the foundation's books; (7) Apple and Google remittances arrive as separate MT103 credits from App Store and Google distribution entities, which must be matched daily against platform settlement conversion reports.Clearing rails for trade and operational settlement: (1) cross-border supplier payments for development, cloud hosting, marketing, and localized customer support are executed via SWIFT MT103 in USD, with each instruction carrying the exact foundation name, the beneficiary's full account data, and a commercially precise remittance narrative such as 'Licensed software development services' or 'Cloud infrastructure fees'; (2) commercial letters of credit (LC) for larger licensing or co-publishing commitments are issued through Banistmo or Banco General using the UCP 600 framework, advised by MT700/MT710, with proceeds settling via MT750/MT756; (3) local Panamanian disbursements are cleared through the Automated Clearing House of Panama (ACH Panama) operated by the Cámara Compensadora Automatizada, using the ABA equivalent, the domestic routing code, and the beneficiary's local account; (4) for intra-group transfers from the PIF to a foreign operating subsidiary or to the founder, only fixed-balance wire transfers with complete auditable trail are permitted, and the controller must record a formal council resolution authorizing each distribution under Ley 25 de 1995; (5) outbound USD wires clearing through the U.S. correspondent banking network require compliance with OFAC and the USA PATRIOT Act record retention, so the foundation must preserve full wire-support documentation for a minimum of five years.USD is the foundation's functional and reporting currency; the gold-plated treasury regulation of Panama allows the PIF to open foreign currency accounts (EUR, GBP, CNY) in domestic banks for operational payouts to foreign developers; FX risks from foreign-currency denominated trade liabilities may be hedged with plain vanilla deliverable forward contracts negotiated under a bilateral ISDA Master Agreement with any SBP-licensed commercial bank, booked under the foundation's ISDA Schedule and Credit Support Annex.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging market currencies without active offshore deliverable markets (e.g., Brazilian real BRL, Argentine peso ARS, Turkish lira TRY, Indian rupee INR, Egyptian pound EGP) may be transacted offshore with international banks through a Panamanian bank's offshore trading desk or directly with a foreign counterparty, provided the transaction is documented under a Panamanian law-governed confirmation and the resulting non-deliverable settlement in USD is booked in the foundation's treasury ledger.Permissible cross-currency swaps (USD/EUR and USD/GBP) for syndicated licensing or joint venture funding are authorized only for genuine economic hedge purposes, with mirror recognition in the foundation's accounting records under IAS/IFRS hedge accounting rules, and subject to a council-approved treasury policy limiting counterparty concentration to notional amounts below the registered banking group's lending ceiling; Panama's lack of a central bank imposes no stamp duty or regulatory approval on derivative contracts, and the SBP is not a hedging transaction counterparty.For operational exposure from the Apple and Google payout cycles, the foundation may enter into short-term rolling forwards of up to 90 days to cover guaranteed future platform settlement commitments denominated in EUR, GBP, or JPY, provided the notional netting is calculated from the net settlement schedules of Apple App Store and Google Play and each forward is mirrored by a matched treasury record; options, exotic structures, and speculative positions are prohibited under the model treasury policy.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low immediate viability. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) domiciled in the UK, EU, or Caribbean do not hold a Panamanian banking license and cannot directly provide a local USD account settlement rail that is recognized by Panamanian tax or banking authorities. Panama's SBP maintains an Innovation Hub (Módulo de Innovación Financiera) under Acuerdo 3 of 2023, but no comprehensive EMI licensing regime for digital payment institutions has been operationalized as of the current stage. A PIF is therefore advised to use regulated Panamanian commercial banks as the primary settlement gateway and may engage foreign EMIs only as auxiliary collection channels for global in-app purchase proceeds, provided the ultimate concentration account is held with an SBP-supervised bank. The 2022 Ley 21 crypto-asset authorization was partially declared unconstitutional by the Panamanian Supreme Court and offers no safe harbor for treasury clearing operations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% flat headline rate (Law 70 of 2019; progressive application: 15% on the first USD/PAB 50,000 of net taxable income and 25% on the excess above USD/PAB 50,000).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for distributions to non-resident beneficiaries out of foreign-source income (territorial rule); 5% dividend distribution tax on distributions made from Panama-source net taxable profits (Fiscal Code Article 730); 10% charge on distribution of foreign-source profits to Panamanian resident beneficiaries (Fiscal Code Article 733). The charge is borne by the beneficiary and withholdable at the foundation level; Panama does not impose a general dividend withholding tax on non-resident shareholders beyond these cases.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File, plus the annual related-party return (Form 311), when the aggregate value of related-party transactions equals or exceeds USD/PAB 1,500,000 in the fiscal year; taxpayers with transactions above USD/PAB 250,000 must maintain contemporaneous TP documentation available upon inspection (Fiscal Code Article 51F; Resolution 201-3942 of 2011). The threshold applies at the Panama legal person level, meaning the PIF or its permanent establishment.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance regime (Law 129 of 2019; Executive Decree 80 of 2020) applies to all Panamanian legal persons, including Private Interest Foundations engaged in commercial activity. A global MMORPG publisher/IP holder is classified under 'Intellectual Property', 'Distribution and Service Centre', or 'Headquarters' activities; the PIF must evidence a physical office in Panama, locally qualified employees, core income-generating activities (contract negotiation with Apple/Google, pricing, licensing, treasury management) executed in Panama, Council meeting minutes, and complete accounting records. Annual ESR filing is mandatory; failure to meet substance triggers fines of USD/PAB 1,000 to USD/PAB 25,000 per violation and, in repeated default, cancellation or dissolution of the entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Territorial Claim

Statutory sanction: Tax crime under Ley 76 of 2019 and Fiscal Code Articles 738-745: surcharge of 10% per month (cap 100%), evaded-tax fines of 50%-200%, and imprisonment from 2 to 5 years where the evaded amount exceeds approximately USD/PAB 100,000; chronic non-filing results in entity strike-off.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members and the administrator are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of evaded taxes; the DGI may obtain preventive asset attachment and travel bans (prohibición de salida) against the Council president; criminal conviction carries imprisonment and disqualification from holding directorships.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Rule violations: administrative fines of USD/PAB 1,000 to USD/PAB 25,000 per violation; the ESR authority may cancel the foundation's registration; foreign tax authorities may reclassify the PIF as a foreign tax resident, with ensuing back taxes, penalties, and denial of treaty benefits; loss of Article 723-A export-services shelter with full recomputation of tax at 25%.

Director personal liability: The public registry and courts pierce the foundation Council veil where the entity is a façade; directors are personally liable to the foundation's beneficiaries and third-party creditors for damages; repeated ESR non-compliance triggers a travel ban and disqualification from managing regulated entities.
