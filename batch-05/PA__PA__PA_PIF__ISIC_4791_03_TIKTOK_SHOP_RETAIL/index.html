Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Private Interest Foundation Law requires a minimum initial endowment of USD 10,000 (or equivalent in convertible currency) to be declared in the Foundation Charter. There is no escrow or immediate paid-in requirement as long as the founder signs a formal acknowledgment of asset assignment; the foundation's patrimony becomes a segregated fund under Law 25, Article 4.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection and evacuation certification under the National Civil Protection System (SINAPROC) and the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panama; structural load inspection and occupancy certificate from the Municipality of Panama for premises used above the ground floor; occupational workplace clearance issued by the Ministry of Labor and Workforce Development (MITRADEL) and Ministry of Health (MINSA) covering electrical installations, signage, and sanitary conditions.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Panama; electronic portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa (Sistema E-Tax / SIFDGI).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must appoint a resident legal representative and register with the DGI using Form 301 (Solicitud de Registro Único de Contribuyente). The representative must submit certified constitutional documents, proof of foreign registration, passport copies, a notarized power of attorney, and evidence of a local address. Upon approval, the DGI issues an RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) with a 11-digit numeric code; non-resident entities engaged in taxable activities without a permanent establishment may require simplified registration for withholding and digital services obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — Foreign entities must appoint a resident legal representative and register with the DGI using Form 301 (Solicitud de Registro Único de Contribuyente). The representative must submit certified constitutional documents, proof of foreign registration, passport copies, a notarized power of attorney, and evidence of a local address. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists under Panamanian law. Hybrid entity treatment is governed by the Private Interest Foundation Law (Law 25 of 1995, as amended) and tax regulations treating foundations as autonomous patrimonies without shareholders. Under the territorial system, only Panamanian-source income is taxable; no formal election is required to maintain pass-through or entity-level character for domestic purposes, but foundations may elect to register as a taxpayer and may opt into the simplified SME tax regime if qualifying under the relevant income thresholds. Cross-border hybrid mismatch elections are not available domestically; taxpayers rely on the Tax Code and the OECD position adopted through Panama's inclusion in the inclusive framework for information exchange.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PAB 150,000 (Panamanian Balboa, equivalent to USD 150,000) of annual gross revenue from taxable transfers and services for mandatory VAT registration; reduced thresholds apply for certain onerous transfers and for small taxpayers electing the simplified regime. Non-resident digital commerce operators may require registration irrespective of the threshold when supplying services to Panamanian consumers through local facilitating agents.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are generally zero-rated for ITBMS purposes when title passes outside Panama or the service is effectively used outside Panama. Offshore services provided by a Panamanian entity to non-residents, including digital commerce support services, are zero-rated if the recipient is outside Panama and the benefit of the service occurs outside the customs territory. Zero-rating is also available for goods removed from the tax jurisdiction under a valid customs export declaration and for services rendered to qualifying free-zone users.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA / Declaración Única de Aduanas approved by Panama Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill proving physical departure of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing foreign exchange repatriation
  • Commercial invoice issued by the exporter with explicit zero-rated ITBMS line
  • Export contract or purchase order identifying the non-resident buyer
  • Proof of payment of Panama-generated logistics charges or terminal handling certificates

Panama operates a phased electronic invoicing regime under Decree 53 of 2018 and DGI Resolution 201-1410. Taxpayers must issue electronic invoices ('factura electrónica') through DGI-authorized software using the XML format and submit invoice batches to the DGI in near-real-time according to the authorized transmitter scheme. The DGI assigns a digital stamp ('impresión fiscal') and validates the invoice before transmission to the buyer. Foreign sellers using platforms such as TikTok Shop must ensure that the platform or local payment agent issues e-invoices for Panamanian sales; API integration is required for direct DGI clearance and digital stamping.

The DGI may audit the complete export file, including customs logs, shipping manifests, banking records, and digital platform payout statements. Failure to substantiate zero-rating triggers retroactive collection of 7% ITBMS plus surcharges, monthly-adjusted interest, and fines ranging from 10% to 50% of the tax omitted depending on the degree of willful misconduct. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims involving false customs declarations, simulated offshore recipients, or unrepatriated foreign exchange may be prosecuted as tax evasion under the Fiscal Penal Code, with monetary penalties up to double the evaded tax and possible cancellation of the RUC, journalistic publication of the sanction, and restriction of the entity's ability to import or export through Panama.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a full dollarization regime with zero exchange-control legislation, meaning there are no limitations on capital-account or current-account convertibility, no requirement for central-bank approval to open or operate foreign-currency accounts, no prior license to repatriate profits, and no obligation to engage in government-brokered conversions. Any conversion between USD, CNY, EUR or other currencies is executed as a private market transaction through licensed banks and international dealers. Profit remittance from a Panamanian Private Interest Foundation is unrestricted at the FX level; the only applicable gate is taxation under the territorial income-tax principle, under which distributions sourced from foreign commercial activity are outside Panamanian tax incidence, whereas Panamanian-source net profits attract the standard corporate and dividend withholding rules. The practical barriers to convertibility are not regulatory but documentary: banks require complete source-of-funds declarations, commercial evidence of the underlying TikTok Shop settlement flows, and CRS/FATCA self-certifications.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline. A Panamanian PIF engaged in cross-border social commerce may retain 100% of TikTok Shop settlement proceeds in USD or any other currency in Panama or abroad, may hold them indefinitely, and may redeploy them to suppliers and beneficiaries without exchange-control authorizations. The only compliance conditions are general: every foreign receipt must be traceable to the platform settlement ledger and underlying invoices for income-tax territorial filing, and the bank's transaction-monitoring profile must reflect the declared e-commerce business volume to avoid money-laundering mischaracterization.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama has no central bank and no dedicated foreign-exchange administration. Banking supervision and licensing are exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), established under Banking Law No. 42 of 2001; AML/CFT supervision is vested in the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) under Law No. 23 of 2015; fiscal-agent and public-depository functions are performed by Banco Nacional de Panamá; fiscal policy is coordinated by the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas. Panama is effectively dollarized: the Balboa is a unit of account and coinage at 1:1 parity with the United States dollar under the 1904 Monetary Convention and the associated monetary statute, and the U.S. dollar circulates as full legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for operational settlement facilitation, MEDIUM for the treasury core, LOW for long-term capital custody. Wise Business (TransferWise), Airwallex, Payoneer and similar foreign EMIs do accept Panama-registered entities and PIFs, provide USD collection accounts, CNY and multi-currency wallets, and are strongly viable for TikTok Shop mass payout, ad-spend funding and recurring supplier payments. Legal constraints: these EMIs are not licensed by the SBP, are not eligible depositaries, and cannot function as the exclusive treasury home; balances above the short-term operating float must be swept to an SBP-licensed bank. CRS and FATCA self-certifications and complete UBO disclosure are mandatory, partially eroding the PIF's private-beneficiary design but remaining acceptable when transparency is confined to regulated compliance channels.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 300,000 (Panamanian Balboa equivalent to USD) in aggregate related-party transactions during a fiscal year triggers mandatory Form 930, Local File, and Master File documentation.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 254 of 2021, Executive Decree 68 of 2022) apply if the PIF carries out 'relevant activities' (mortgage/housing, banking, insurance, IP, services, commerce, shipping). For the TikTok Shop retail scope, the relevant activity is 'commerce and shipping' of goods from Panama; the PIF/trader must maintain permanent physical premises in Panama, sufficient local employees, and actual direction of revenue-generating activities within Panama. Entities engaged exclusively in foreign-source passive income or foreign-commerce activities outside Panama fall outside ESR reporting, but the DGI retains power to recharacterize substance if management or logistics are actually conducted from Panamanian soil.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fiscal Fraud

Statutory sanction: DGI assessment with 40% deficiency surcharge (Art. 748 Tax Code), default interest at DGI monthly rate; criminal tax fraud (Arts. 280-A to 280-C Criminal Code): 2 to 5 years imprisonment and fines of 1 to 3 times the evaded tax; administrative closure of commercial registration; forfeiture of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members (directors) are jointly and severally liable for evaded tax and penalties if they signed or directed the false filing; courts issue airline 'prohibición de salida' (travel ban), freeze personal bank accounts, and extradite under bilateral/Interpol channels; civil liability extended to the founder if the foundation is proven to be a fraud vehicle.
Shell Company / Insufficient Substance (ESR)

Statutory sanction: Fines of USD 10,000 to USD 1,000,000 for ESR non-compliance; DGI may request the Superintendency to cancel the Private Interest Foundation registration under Law 25 of 1995; automatic exchange of information with the protection's beneficiary country; loss of territorial exemption for related revenue due to source recharacterization.

Director personal liability: Directors of the PIF risk individual sanctions, disqualification from directing Panamanian entities for up to 5 years, and personal liability for unpaid taxes and employer social-security contributions when the foundation is classified as a sham/alter ego.
Anti-Money Laundering (Law 23 of 2015 & Criminal Code)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty for money laundering: 5 to 12 years imprisonment and fines up to B/. 10 million; asset forfeiture (extinción de dominio) of bank balances, inventory, and the PIF's assets; administrative penalties to the foundation's license under AML Law 23/2015.

Director personal liability: The beneficial owner-founder and Foundation Council members are criminally liable as 'autoblanqueo' (self-money laundering); they face arraigo (travel restriction), preventive detention, bank account freezes, and automatic reporting to the Panamanian UAF (Financial Analysis Unit); international cooperation via GAFILAT and Interpol Red Notices.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Specific fine of up to B/. 10,000 for missing/inaccurate Form 930; DGI adjustment with a 40% surcharge on the deficiency plus default interest; if the adjustment indicates intentional undervaluation, criminal tax-fraud charges apply; public disclosure of the tax demo (flag) in DGI registries.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate TP documentation face personal fines under Tax Code Art. 705-B; in fraudulent under-pricing, civil restitution of the artificially deflated profit is claimed personally from the directors; criminal prison term is elevated for coordinated acts across the PIF and affiliated seller entities.
