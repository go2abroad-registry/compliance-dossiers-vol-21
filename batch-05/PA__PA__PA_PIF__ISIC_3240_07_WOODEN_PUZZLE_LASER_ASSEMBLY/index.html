Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; official e-services portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa and SICE electronic invoicing/clearance platform; customs counterpart for exporter registration and export declarations: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas de Panamá (ANA), portal https://www.ana.gob.pa (SIGA system).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) with non-resident founders/beneficiaries: (1) execute and register the foundation charter (escritura constitutiva) at the Registro Público de Panamá through a licensed resident agent, thereby creating the PIF as a Panamanian legal person; (2) obtain the certified digital signature (Firma Electrónica) from a DGI-accredited certification authority, required for all electronic tax filings and invoicing; (3) file the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) activation application on the DGI e-Tax portal, annexing the registered charter, bylaws, resident-agent acceptance, and identity documentation of the foundation council members, and declaring the ISIC 3240 activity (manufacture of games and toys) together with the exporter ITBMS regime; (4) DGI assigns the RUC/NIT tax identification number and, where the PIF is commercially active, adds ITBMS registration, authorizes electronic invoice series, and issues the taxpayer certificate; (5) a foreign entity without local incorporation but with Panamanian-source activity follows the same RUC route through a resident agent; (6) for physical exports, register with ANA as an exporter in the SIGA customs platform and link the exporter code to the RUC; (7) a purely passive PIF performing no Panamanian-source commercial activity is not required to register for RUC/ITBMS but must nonetheless satisfy the annual juridical annuity and legal fee requirements of Law 25 of 1995 to remain in good standing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope - Panama taxes only income derived from Panamanian sources. The PIF is exempt from income tax on its worldwide passive income and on assets held outside Panama, but a PIF carrying on manufacturing, assembly, or any commercial business within Panama is subject to entity-level corporate income tax at the standard rate of 25% on Panamanian-source profits (production/origin test); export sales of goods manufactured in Panama are also treated as Panamanian-source income under the territorial rule and remain taxable, while non-Panamanian passive income of the PIF stays outside the charge.

Available election forms

Panama has no US-style check-the-box election and no domestic hybrid-mismatch (BEPS Action 2) legislation. Available statutory elections and mechanisms: (i) the Foundation Council may elect to engage in commercial activities in Panama, voluntarily subjecting the PIF to the ordinary corporate tax and ITBMS regimes; alternatively, it may elect to keep the PIF as a passive exempt foundation; (ii) under Article 25 of Law 25 of 1995, a foundation that performs taxable acts in Panama is automatically taxed under the rules applicable to corporations; (iii) election as a small taxpayer (pequeño contribuyente) to apply the simplified ITBMS quota (cuota fija) regime where annual gross revenue does not exceed B/.200,000; (iv) no unilateral domestic election is available to invoke tax treaty benefits; treaty application, where any, is administrative and case-specific.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Panama does not impose a minimum turnover threshold for ITBMS registration; any person or entity that habitually or occasionally performs taxable transfers of movable goods or services in Panamanian territory must register in the RUC and charge ITBMS. Exporters must register regardless of turnover and may apply the 0% rate without any threshold. A simplified small-taxpayer regime applies to businesses with annual gross revenue not exceeding B/.200,000 (approximately US$200,000), allowing fixed-quota ITBMS payments instead of full periodic accounting.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (tarifa 0%) applies to: (i) definitive exports of movable goods, including CO2 laser-cut wooden puzzle kits, that physically leave Panama under a customs DUA declared by the registered exporter; (ii) exported services performed in Panama but used exclusively abroad or benefiting non-residents outside Panama; and (iii) transfers of goods to duty-free zones and export-processing regimes (Colón Free Zone, Panamá Pacífico) where destined for re-export. The in-Panama assembly step does not disqualify zero-rating so long as the finished goods are exported within the permitted customs term and the export documents are in the exporter's name. To claim the 0% rate and the refund of input ITBMS credits, the PIF-exporter must hold a valid RUC/ITBMS registration, issue SICE-validated zero-rated export invoices, retain proof of physical departure, and evidence receipt of the foreign currency proceeds in a Panamanian bank account.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera - DUA) accepted and endorsed by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) in the SIGA system
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / multimodal transport document) evidencing the shipment, consignee, and the port/gateway of exit
  • SICE/DGI-validated commercial e-invoice at tarifa 0% identifying the export transaction and referencing the DUA number
  • Packing list and the purchase order or sales contract underlying the export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a Panamanian bank evidencing the export proceeds credited to the PIF's local account
  • SWIFT MT103 or bank credit confirmation proving receipt of payment from the foreign buyer
  • Certificate of Origin (where a preferential tariff treatment is claimed, including GSP and other trade agreements)

Electronic invoicing through the DGI SICE platform is mandatory, with phased roll-out to the entire RUC taxpayer base (major taxpayers from 2021-2022; all taxpayers by fiscal year 2025). The mandate requires real-time transmission of invoice data to DGI, DGI validation with an electronic seal and QR verification code before the invoice is legally effective, ERP/application-to-application integration via SICE APIs for high-volume issuers, digital certification (Firma Electrónica) for all issuers, and a distinct export-sales invoice category (tarifa 0%) that must reference the customs DUA. No paper invoicing remains for active commercial taxpayers; DGI automatically cross-matches invoicing, ITBMS returns, RUC status, and customs export records.

DGI and ANA verify zero-rating and refund claims by cross-matching DUA physical exit records, SICE e-invoice data, exporter bank statements, and upstream supplier invoices. A false or unsupported zero-rating claim triggers retrospective clawback of the full input ITBMS refunded, recapture of the standard 7% tax, the applicable statutory surcharge (recargo), monthly mora interest, and fines generally ranging from 50% to 300% of the credit fraudulently obtained. Additional consequences include removal from the DGI exporter refund list, suspension or cancellation of the RUC, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud under Panama Law 70 of 2019, carrying 5 to 10 years of imprisonment for intentional evasion or invoice falsification. The statutory prescription period for reassessment and clawback is three years, extended to five years in cases of fraud; foundation council members and the resident agent who sign export returns may incur joint liability for the penalties imposed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates under full de facto dollarization; the Balboa is a unit of account at fixed parity with the U.S. dollar. There are no capital controls, no exchange-control licensing, and no differentiated treatment between current and capital account transactions. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, royalty payments, and management fees are freely transferable abroad, subject only to bank-level AML/KYC due diligence and OFAC screening. Under Panama's territorial tax system, Panama-source income of a Private Interest Foundation is generally not subject to remittance blockage or non-resident withholding taxes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may freely hold, deposit, transfer, or reinvest U.S. dollar proceeds. Panamanian regulations do not impose a timeline for collecting or converting export proceeds; however, all export transactions must be reported to the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) for territorial tax assessment and to Ministerio de Comercio e Industrias for trade statistics.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the banking regulator; Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent; monetary authority is effectively the U.S. Federal Reserve because Panama has no central bank and uses the U.S. dollar as legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with legal constraints. Panama does not maintain a dedicated non-bank EMI license category equivalent to the EU's e-money institution framework; payment service providers must be authorized by the SBP or operate through licensed banks. Offshore digital EMIs cannot open direct multi-currency accounts for a Panamanian PIF unless they engage a licensed local correspondent bank. An offshore EMI may be used only as a payment gateway for Amazon Pay and Stripe settlement, with funds swept to a tier-1 local bank. The PIF must therefore maintain a physical banking anchor for capital escrow and trade disbursement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The remitter should identify the purpose in SWIFT field 70 as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'APORTE DE CAPITAL' and reference the foundation registration number. Panama does not use statutory government purpose codes, but banks require internal transaction reason codes and may request a predeposit letter, signed source-of-funds declaration, and proof of underlying commercial activity before accepting the wire. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 2,000,000 (annual gross revenue or aggregate related-party transactions exceeding USD 2,000,000 trigger mandatory Transfer Pricing Informative Return and Local File / Master File preparation)

Economic substance scope: Panama Law 254 of 2021 (Economic Substance Regulations) applies to juridical persons, including Private Interest Foundations. Entities conducting relevant activities such as manufacturing must maintain a local registered office, adequate premises, local employees, and local management. A physical CO2 laser assembly facility for wooden puzzles satisfies ESR by operating production in Panama. Passive PIFs holding only foreign investments or IP must file the annual economic substance return and meet the reduced substance requirements for pure holding entities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Fiscal Fraud / Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties of 2 to 5 times the evaded tax; imprisonment for 2 to 5 years; complete disallowance of fraudulent deductions; civil interest and late-payment surcharges of 10% to 50%.

Director personal liability: Directors, administrators, and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties. Panama courts may issue travel bans, freeze personal assets, and impose commercial disqualification.
Shell Company / ESR Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Economic Substance Regulations can trigger fines up to US$500,000 for serious infractions, automatic deregistration of the foundation, loss of tax-residency benefits, and suspension of the registered agent's license.

Director personal liability: Resident agents, directors, and administrators are personally liable for administrative penalties and third-party damages. The regulator may impose travel bans, strike the director from Panama's public registry, and order personal restitution.
AML/CFT Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to US$1,000,000 for non-compliant legal entities, freezing of bank accounts, administrative dissolution, and referral for criminal prosecution. Money laundering penalties include 5 to 10 years imprisonment and fines up to US$10,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers face personal criminal liability, civil restitution, travel bans, asset confiscation, and extradition risk. Non-resident founders lose the foundation's liability shield when they actively engage in laundering commercial proceeds.
