Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) — General Directorate of Revenue of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); taxpayer digital portal: 'Sistema T@ch' (https://tach.mef.gob.pa); customs authority for export controls: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-administered Private Interest Foundation (PIF) must (1) complete Public Registry inscription (Registro Público de Panamá) and obtain certified 'Personería Jurídica'/foundation charter evidence; (2) appoint a Panamanian resident agent and fiscal domicile; (3) file RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) application with DGI via T@ch portal or DGI window, attaching certified foundation documents, identity papers of the founder/administrators, proof of the agent's address, and a sworn inventory of Panamanian-source business; (4) register as ITBMS taxpayer irrespective of export-only activity when issuing invoices for services performed from Panama; (5) obtain digital e-invoicing credentials and a digital signature certificate; DGI issues the definitive RUC certificate and activates the e-invoicing 'sello digital'/authorization profile.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama has no U.S.-style check-the-box or entity-classification election. Law 25 of 1995 PIFs are autonomous juridical persons taxable only on Panama-source commercial income; offshore/export-derived income is exempt without election. Available elections: (i) ITBMS small-taxpayer simplified regime ('Pequeño Contribuyente') where annual taxable turnover is at or below the statutory small-taxpayer threshold; (ii) optional consolidated 'grupo de interés fiscal' filing for qualifying 60%-owned groups; (iii) election to claim export zero-rating and input ITBMS refunds by filing monthly ITBMS returns; (iv) irrevocable election of the 5-year statute-of-limitations period for offshore-activities records. No CFC or hybrid-mismatch elections apply to PIFs under current Panamanian law.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS zero-rating (0%) applies to (i) physical exports evidenced by ANA customs declarations and (ii) 'exportación de servicios' — services rendered to a client that is not resident/domiciled in Panama and whose economic benefit and legal use arise wholly outside Panama, including CGI/VFX and 3D character animation deliverables transferred by digital file and licensed to foreign studios/platforms. The PIF must hold a valid RUC and ITBMS registration, issue compliant e-invoices naming the non-resident client, conclude a written service agreement stipulating offshore delivery/use, and demonstrate payment received from abroad (Wise B2B and SWIFT MT103 wire certificates accepted). Export zero-rating permits retention and refund of Panamanian input ITBMS credit on Panamanian costs (equipment, cloud services, local subcontractors), claimable via monthly ITBMS return and audited DGI refund procedure.

Mandatory phased electronic invoicing under DGI Resolutions (Res. DGT 201-0149/2019 and amendments), administered on the T@ch platform. On-line model: real-time fiscal clearance via certified REST/SOAP APIs — each invoice must be digitally signed and receive a DGI authorization code before delivery; Off-line model: XML batches with DGI 'sello digital' (digital stamp) transmitted within statutory deadline (5 calendar days after issuance). Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized monetary system: the balboa (B/.) is a wholly fractional coin pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar, and USD is unlimited legal tender for all transactions. Both current-account flows (client invoice payments, royalty receipts, subcontractor disbursements) and capital-account flows (founder capital contributions, profit distributions, liquidation remittances) are convertible and freely remittable without SBP approval, foreign-exchange license, transaction volume caps, or surrender obligations. Profit-remittance hurdles are practical rather than regulatory: (i) the PIF must demonstrate a lawful source of funds and sufficient capital base under the Private Interest Foundation Law (Law 25 of 1995); (ii) CRS and FATCA reporting duties under Law 23 of 2015 may expose the ultimate beneficial owner to the tax authority of their country of residence; and (iii) the receiving bank applies enhanced due diligence and may require invoices or board minutes evidencing the commercial purpose of any outward dividend or treasury transfer. For a PIF operating under territorial-scope income principles, offshore-sourced animation production income may be distributed net of local levies without exchange-control hindrance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panamanian law imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation period for export receipts. Income earned by the PIF from commercial CGI/3D animation production (ISIC 5911) and VFX services may be retained in any currency at any bank domestically or offshore. Even when the PIF is treated as a Panamanian territorial taxpayer, the export of services generates no foreign-exchange surrender obligation; the only compliance duties are accurate bookkeeping of foreign-currency balances, retention of underlying client contracts and settlement statements, and transaction-reasonableness evidence for SBP-supervised bank due-diligence reviews.

Central bank supervision: No central bank is constituted in Panama; monetary and foreign-exchange administration is exercised de facto through US dollar legal tender (Article 1 of Law 84 of 1946, replacing Law 1 of 1914, together with the Panama-US Monetary Agreements), with licensing and AML/CFT supervision vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). There is no dedicated foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control ministry issuing currency approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High as a supplementary B2B payment gateway; Low as a primary treasury, escrow, or clearing rail. Panama does not maintain a distinct electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence; the SBP supervises digital payment services under Ley 21 de 2015, Acuerdo SBP No. 002-2020 and Acuerdo SBP No. 08-2017, reserving deposit-taking and money transmission to licensed banks. A foreign EMI such as Wise B2B (Wise PLC, London) may lawfully transmit USD to the PIF's Panamanian bank account via SWIFT/MT103 onward routing, but the PIF's bank must be able to identify the originating EMI customer; therefore the PIF must disclose the Wise account relationship in its opening risk profile. Legal constraints include: (i) an unlicensed EMI cannot open a settlement account in Panama without violating the Banking Law (Decreto Ley 9 de 1998); (ii) recurring high-value flows through Wise may trigger UBO-source verification by the SBP-supervised bank; and (iii) Wise business accounts are treated as non-regulated counterparties for amounts above USD 50,000. Recommended use-case: recurring low-value VOD/aggregator royalties and artist-payout batches between USD 5,000 and USD 50,000, with nightly sweeps by SWIFT MT103 into the primary Banco General/Banistmo treasury account. The PIF's actual account-opening identity and capital base must remain with the licensed Panamanian bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory final withholding tax on distributions of Panamanian-source profits by a Panama operating entity. A PIF's distributions to foreign beneficiaries out of accumulated foreign-source 3D animation income are not Panamanian-source dividends and fall outside this charge, producing a 0% effective Panama tax on such distributions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File documentation is required where aggregate related-party transactions exceed B/. 2,500,000 (USD 2,500,000) in a fiscal year. A Master File must be prepared where the PIF is part of an international group with aggregate consolidated revenue exceeding B/. 75,000,000, and Country-by-Country reporting is triggered for groups meeting the OECD BEPS threshold.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not maintain BVI/Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations for Private Interest Foundations, but the general anti-avoidance principle, the beneficial ownership registry, AML Law 23/2015, and CRS registration require the PIF to have genuine local administration, documented council meetings, accounting records, and substance consistent with its declared activity. A PIF earning commercial 3D animation production income must demonstrate real creative, managerial, and economic activity; a purely passive PIF collecting licence revenue from a CGI catalogue with no personnel or decision-making in Panama will be treated by the DGI as an artificial arrangement unless sufficient substance is established.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Improper Foreign-Source Characterisation

Statutory sanction: DGI re-assessment at 25% ISR, a 50% surcharge on the under-declared tax, annual interest from the due date, fines up to USD 5,000, and criminal tax evasion penalties of 2 to 4 years' imprisonment under the Panamanian Tax Code.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members, de facto directors, and founder-controlled officers are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax when they authorise false declarations; civil restitution, preventive asset attachment, travel bans, and imprisonment for fraud may be imposed despite the PIF's separate juridical personality.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's length result, a 25% penalty on the upward adjustment, deficiency interest, non-submission fines of USD 5,000 to USD 50,000 for Form 105, and criminal prosecution where false documentation is submitted.

Director personal liability: Directors and council members who approve mispriced intercompany agreements are personally liable for the under-declared profit, subject to disqualification from holding directorship for up to 5 years and to travel restrictions until the tax debt is fully settled.
Shell Company and AML Deficient Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Violation of Law 23/2015 and the Beneficial Ownership Cabinet Decree carries corporate fines from USD 10,000 to USD 1,000,000, cancellation of the foundation registration, forfeiture of assets involved, and up to 4 years' imprisonment for money laundering where the flow is linked to predicate offences.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the PIF's juridical veil where it is shown to be a sham; council members face criminal liability, joint civil restitution, freezing of personal bank accounts, and international travel bans including provisional arrest at the request of investigating authorities.
Employment, Social Security, and Municipal Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative closure of the studio, retroactive CSS and IDF assessments of 2 to 3 times the unpaid amounts, municipal tax liens, fines for unlicensed business operation, and seizure of bank accounts if compliance bonds are not posted.

Director personal liability: Under Panama labor and tax enforcement rules, directors and Foundation Council members are severally liable for unpaid wages and social security contributions; courts may order personal bankruptcy, travel bans, and director disqualification for repeated violations, irrespective of the foundation's corporate separation.
