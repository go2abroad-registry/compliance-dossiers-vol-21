Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); official portal: Sistema T@X (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa); e-invoicing clearance platform: Portal de Facturación Electrónica de la DGI; auxiliary registry: Municipio de Panamá (Aviso de Operación) and Registro Público de Panamá (corporate registration).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Sociedad Anónima at the Registro Público de Panamá (commercial registry, foil identification) and obtain the Certificado de Registro. Step 2: Obtain a municipal Aviso de Operación at the Municipalidad where the registered office is located. Step 3: File Form 'Solicitud de Inscripción de Contribuyente' with the DGI through the T@X portal (or physically at the DGI Customer Service branches), attaching the public deed, corporate certificate, proof of registered office, identification of directors/resident agent, and municipal notice. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Panama-source income only, territorial scope; no pass-through eligibility). The S.A. is a resident juridical person subject to corporation income tax (ISR) at progressive rates from 15% to 25% solely on income from Panamanian source. Income derived from services rendered and effectively used entirely outside Panama falls outside the territorial net and is not taxable in Panama. The SEO/ASO localization agency model remains carefully boundary-tested because services encoded as 'performed in Panama' may be treated as Panama-source even if the customer is abroad.

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box or any elective hybrid-mismatch regime. Statutory elections available to the S.A. include: (a) election between the 'coeficiente' (prior-year turnover ratio) and 'rentas estimadas' (estimated-profit) methods for quarterly advance ISR payments under Código Fiscal Art. 733; (b) election to apply the simplified 'pequeño contribuyente' invoicing/ITBMS regime where annual gross income does not exceed B/.36,000; (c) election to obtain 'Bono de Inversión' or accelerated depreciation for qualifying technology investment under Law 52 of 2019; (d) election under the SEM (Sede de Empresa Multinacional) regime under Law 41 of 2007 to lock in territorial treatment and special ITBMS export exemptions for intra-group service hubs; (e) election to pay the 10% shareholder-profit surcharge versus full dividend distribution treatment; tax consolidation is not permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary de-minimis threshold; ITBMS registration is mandatory for any person or legal entity habitually carrying out taxable commercial activities within the territory. The DGI requires registration of the RUC before the first invoice is issued. Foreign non-resident providers of digital services (including ASO/SEO platform services) create an ITBMS nexus when customers are located in Panama; foreign digital suppliers may be required to register through a legal representative under Decreto Ejecutivo 2 de 2022 where digital services are rendered to Panamanian consumers. Small domestic businesses with annual gross receipts not exceeding B/.36,000 may opt into the simplified peqeño contribuyente regime (which excludes certain ITBMS mechanics but does not waive registration).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Código Fiscal Art. 746-A and supporting regulations (Executive Decree 84 of 2005), a 0% ITBMS rate applies to: (a) tangible goods placed under an export customs regime, and (b) exported services rendered to clients domiciled, established or resident abroad, where the services are effectively used or exploited outside Panama. For the App Growth Agency (ISIC 7310/NAICS 541810), zero-rating requires: a written export service contract with a non-resident principal; the issuance of an electronic export invoice (with the QR/CTF code and annotation 'Exportación de Servicios'); deliverables demonstrably consumed abroad (campaign management, multilingual organic search, supplier outreach, App Store/ASO ranking services); and receipt of the consideration from foreign payment rails (Wise B2B, Payoneer, correspondent bank transfers) in a Panamanian bank account or licensed electronic-money account. The 0% rate preserves the right to full ITBMS input-tax credit or refund on local purchases used for the exported services. For income tax purposes, the corresponding service income is treated as foreign-source only where the DGI's source rules (Código Fiscal Art. 694 and DE 44 of 2019) are satisfied; to mitigate reclassification risk, the agency should maintain substance documentation and, where a multinational group is present, consider the SEM regime for definitive territorial exclusion.

Mandatory under Law 93 of 2019, Executive Decree 26 of 2020, and DGI Resolution 201-3246. All active RUC/ITBMS taxpayers must issue electronic invoices (Factura Electrónica) in XML format with a QR stamp containing the Código de Trazabilidad Fiscal (CTF). Two clearance modalities exist: (i) online real-time API/Web Services transmission through DGI-authorized SFE (Sociedades de Facturación Electrónica); (ii) offline-mode via DGI-approved billing software where invoices are transmitted within 24-48 hours. In both modalities, the DGI validates the invoice, returns a status (Aceptada/Rechazado) and assigns a unique folio. The CTF QR code and digital signature are required for all invoices, receipts, credit notes, and export documents. Taxpayers are also required to file periodic ITBMS declarations (Form 20) and monthly purchase/sales books electronically through the T@X system.

The DGI audits exporters under the 'Fiscalización al Sector Exportador' program. If the zero-rating is disallowed or documentation is deficient, the ITBMS is clawed back at 7% retroactively from the invoice date, plus statoll monthly interest (1.5% per month, capped as provided by law), a 10% 'recargo' surcharge, and fines under Código Fiscal Arts. 758-760 ranging from B/.500 to B/.20,000. Recurrent or fraudulent export zero-rating is classified as tax evasion under Law 70 of 2019 (Código Fiscal/Procedural) and may be prosecuted criminally with penalties of 2-5 years imprisonment for amounts above B/.50,000, exclusion from export-benefit regimes for five years, restitution of input VAT refunds, and administrative disqualification of directors/resident agents involved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Absolute de facto and de jure convertibility. The US Dollar is legal tender and circulates freely at par with the balboa (PAB), which exists only as fractional coins and has no independent exchange market. There are no capital-account controls, no current-account restrictions, no advance approvals for foreign investments, and no profit-remittance licensing. A Panamanian S.A. may inject capital, repatriate dividends, pay management or intercompany fees, and service shareholder loans to non-residents by ordinary SWIFT USD transfers without central-bank clearance. The only operational hurdles are bank-level enhanced due diligence, the UAF final-beneficiary disclosure framework, and tax-stage rules such as the 10% dividend withholding tax applicable to distributions from Panama-source profits to non-resident shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panama imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export proceeds. This rule is structurally moot because the balboa is pegged 1:1 to the US dollar and no paper balboa circulates; export receipts in USD are already domestic legal tender. For an ASO/SEO localization agency exporting digital services, incoming foreign remittances are classified as ordinary commercial credits and the receiving bank may require only an export invoice, a master service agreement, and a purpose code for AML documentation.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the sole banking and liquidity regulator under Decree-Law 9 of 1998; Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the fiscal agent and agent bank of the State; the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF), operating under the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas, supervises AML/CFT compliance. Panama has no central bank, no currency board, and no foreign-exchange control authority; monetary anchoring is supplied by the United States Federal Reserve through Panama's full dollarization regime established in 1904.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High as a secondary settlement rail but Low as a standalone treasury anchor. Wise B2B and Payoneer are foreign electronic money institutions / money services businesses, not SBP-licensed banks, and they cannot accept Panamanian retail deposits or offer Panamanian deposit protection. Wise B2B can onboard the Panamanian S.A. but applies its own 'digital marketing' handling rules and may demand a non-registered-agent physical operating address, UBO identity documentation, and executed client service agreements before enabling multi-currency receiving balances in USD, EUR, and GBP. Payoneer expressly supports Panama-registered service exporters for marketplace payouts. Legal constraint: neither EMI qualifies as the primary corporate bank for statutory audit evidence, escrow duties, or client-money segregation; both may hold only short-term settlement float and must be swept daily into the SBP-regulated operating account.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends distributed from Panama-source earnings. Dividends paid from a Panama corporation's foreign-source earnings are not subject to Panama withholding tax. For dividends distributed by a Panama entity derived from domestic earnings, the company must pay a 10% withholding tax; alternatively, a 20% rate applies if the 40% advance corporate tax credit mechanism is not followed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No specific statutory threshold expressed in PAB/USD; Panama's transfer pricing rules apply to taxpayers with cross-border transactions with related parties exceeding USD 250,000 per fiscal year. Documentation required: Local File when aggregate related-party transactions exceed USD 3 million; Master File when group annual consolidated revenue exceeds USD 50 million. Country-by-Country Report filing required for groups with consolidated revenue of at least USD 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Panama adopted Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) via Law 254 of 2021, effective for fiscal year 2022. All Panama legal entities engaged in relevant activities (including distribution and service centers, headquarters, and financing activities) must maintain substantial economic presence in Panama. For an ASO/SEO localization agency, the relevant activity is 'service business' if the entity provides services to non-resident affiliates. Essential requirements: (a) core income-generating activities performed in Panama; (b) adequate premises, employees, and equipment in Panama; (c) full compliance with corporate record-keeping. Entities failing ESR must report and pay a tax of 12.5% on total gross income (effective 2021 onward) and may have their corporate registrations suspended. A purely passive or shell corporation engaged in foreign-source ASO/SEO services with no local activity is exposed to ESR penalties and potential recharacterization of income as Panama-source.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Tributaria)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Tax Criminal Code (Ley 5 de 2021): imprisonment from 2 to 5 years; monetary fine equal to 50% to 100% of the evaded tax. Administrative penalties: 10% understatement penalty plus monthly surcharge and interest. Bankruptcy or dissolution may be ordered for asset concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and legal representatives who sign fraudulent returns are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties. Criminal court may impose travel bans, precautionary banking freezes, and restitution orders. If the company is deemed a facade, courts may pierce the corporate veil to hold personal assets.
Economic Substance Violation (Régimen de Sustancia Económica)

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalty: USD 50,000 to USD 1,000,000 per violation, plus a special 12.5% tax on all gross income of the entity. Suspension of the corporate legal personality (registro) after 90 days of non-compliance; subsequent cancellation of the registered agent.

Director personal liability: Directors and resident agents knowingly providing false statements may face fines of USD 10,000 to USD 50,000, disqualification from holding directorships in Panama, and criminal liability for false testimony (up to 2 years imprisonment). Personal liability extends to unpaid ESR special tax.
Money Laundering / AML Compliance Failure (Decreto Ejecutivo 363 de 2015)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to USD 1,000,000 for negligent AML compliance. If the entity is used as a vehicle for illicit transfer (e.g., invoicing fraud, VAT evasion, or terrorist financing), the assets are subject to confiscation and the company may be dissolved. Criminal penalties up to 12 years imprisonment for individuals convicted of money laundering under Law 23 of 2015.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML program failures. A court may issue a travel ban and freeze personal assets if the director knowingly participated in or permitted laundering through the corporation. Personal criminal liability cannot be indemnified by the corporation.
Transfer Pricing Penalty / Aggressive Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Administrative adjustment of taxable income plus 20% surcharge on the additional tax due, and monthly interest. If the adjustment stems from hidden transactions or false documentation, the penalty rises to 40% of the omitted tax. Failure to file documentation: USD 30,000 to USD 100,000 per omission.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives who sign tax returns with false transfer pricing documentation may face criminal tax evasion liability. Directors may be subject to a personal liability ruling for the 20%/40% surcharges if they vote to approve fraudulent intercompany invoices.
