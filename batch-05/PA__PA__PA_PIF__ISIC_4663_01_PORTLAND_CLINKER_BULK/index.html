Governing statute

Ley No. 25 de 1995 (Private Interest Foundations Law), as amended; Articles 14-22 (incorporation), Article 15 (endowment), Article 18 (foundation council), and Article 19 (registered office); supplementary application of the Código Civil and Código de Comercio for analogous corporate guaranties.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital and no escrow deposit requirement under Law 25 of 1995. The foundation charter must declare an initial endowment of assets sufficient to fulfill the foundation's purpose, customarily stated as at least USD 10,000 equivalent, although the minimum nominal stated amount may be USD 10 or B/.10.00 at formation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá for any storage, warehousing, or office premises; structural load inspection by a licensed engineering firm if cement bulk silos or heavy pallet racks are installed; workplace health-and-safety clearance from MITRADEL (Panama Ministry of Labor and Social Development) pursuant to Executive Decree No. 44 of 2016; additionally, if port-related operations occur, a facility compliance inspection with the Autoridad Marítima de Panamá (AMP) may be required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Autoridad Nacional de Ingresos Públicos (ANIP), Panama's integrated revenue, customs and tax administration succeeding the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) within the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas. Official portal: https://www.anip.gob.pa; taxpayer service portal (e-Tax / T@X portal) administered by ANIP.

E-government filing portal

For a Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) engaged in Panamanian-source wholesale or export trade: the PIF must first be duly inscribed in the Registro Público de Panamá through a licensed Panamanian attorney/fiscal agent. The RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) is then obtained by filing ANIP Form DGI-01/02 via the e-Tax portal, submitting the certified foundational deed, certificate of registered agent and domicile, passport copies of the founder/council members/protector, evidence of the appointed fiscal representative, and a sworn affidavit of Panamanian tax domicile. the RUC is issued in prototype within 1 to 3 business days. A PIF with no Panamanian-source income and no local activities is not obliged to register, but registration and fiscal representation are recommended for banking, ocean-freight contracting, ITBMS zero-rated export claims, and issuing compliant invoices to overseas cement buyers.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation_with_Territorial_Sourcing: Panama follows a territorial tax system; a Private Interest Foundation is a separate juridical person taxable at entity level only on income sourced within Panama (e.g., local freight-servicing activities, local storage, local sale execution). Foreign-source income and export proceeds qualifying as foreign-source are generally exempt. The PIF is not treated as a pass-through entity; distributions to beneficiaries are not deductible and are not taxable in Panama if sourced from exempt foreign income. — For a Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) engaged in Panamanian-source wholesale or export trade: the PIF must first be duly inscribed in the Registro Público de Panamá through a licensed Panamanian attorney/fiscal agent. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation_with_Territorial_Sourcing: Panama follows a territorial tax system; a Private Interest Foundation is a separate juridical person taxable at entity level only on income sourced within Panama (e.g., local freight-servicing activities, local storage, local sale execution). Foreign-source income and export proceeds qualifying as foreign-source are generally exempt. The PIF is not treated as a pass-through entity; distributions to beneficiaries are not deductible and are not taxable in Panama if sourced from exempt foreign income.

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity election. Available statutory/regulatory elections include: (i) the Multinational Headquarters Company (SEM) election permitting a 5% effective ISR rate on qualified Panamanian-source services; (ii) the simplified small-taxpayer regime election for taxpayers with annual gross income not exceeding B/.36,000; (iii) the election to apply accelerated depreciation/amortization under Panama's fiscal incentives; and (iv) the ITBMS monthly credit-and-debit election under which a registered taxpayer chooses to credit input ITBMS against output ITBMS rather than using the small-taxpayer presumptive method.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general ITBMS turnover threshold exempts habitual sellers: any natural or juridical person habitually/occupationally transferring movable goods (including bulk cement clinker), rendering services in Panama, or importing goods must obtain a RUC and register for ITBMS as of the first taxable transaction. A simplified small-taxpayer ITBMS regime is available for operators with annual gross income not exceeding B/.36,000 (Balboa at par with USD), but exporters, importers, holders of customs records, and taxpayers subject to withholding obligations must be formally registered regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of bulk portland cement clinker qualify for the 0% ITBMS rate when: the goods are sold to a non-resident buyer; the goods physically leave Panamanian customs territory under a valid export declaration (DUA); the Panamanian registered taxpayer is the exporter of record on the DUA and shipping documents; title and risk pass to the foreign buyer under the relevant Incoterms (FOB/CIF etc.); and the sale proceeds are collected from abroad in foreign currency. Services performed and enjoyed wholly outside Panama, including ocean-vessel freight services supplied to non-residents, are likewise eligible for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera - DUA) evidencing the physical departure of the bulk clinker from Panama
  • Original or electronically issued ocean Bill of Lading in the name of the non-resident buyer or its nominee
  • Commercial invoice in Spanish/English describing the goods under HS 2523.30 and the quantity/weight of bulk clinker
  • Packing list and/or weight certificate/draft survey report evidencing the bulk cargo shipped
  • Sales contract or purchase order containing Incoterms, quantity, price, and risk-of-loss clauses
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank MT103/credit advice demonstrating receipt of the export sale proceeds in Panamanian or foreign banking accounts

Mandatory electronic invoicing (factura electrónica) has been phased in for all RUC/ITBMS taxpayers and is required in the wholesale construction-materials and ocean-freight trade. Taxpayers must issue e-invoices/credit notes through ANIP-certified software or the ANIP e-Tax web portal applying the approved XML/JSON invoice schema, sign invoices with a valid digital certificate, transmit or validate each invoice in near real time with the ANIP central tax-clearance platform before or at issuance, and include a unique QR fiscal code. Invoices lacking valid ANIP clearance are inadmissible for input-credit, zero-rating, or refund purposes.

ANIP audits zero-rated export claims through cross-checks between the DUA, customs departure records, shipping manifests, vessel itineraries, e-invoice files, corporate filings, and bank proceeds records. Where the exporter cannot substantiate the physical export and foreign remittance, ANIP will claw back the unpaid ITBMS with the applicable statutory monthly interest surcharge, impose administrative fines of up to 100% of the omitted tax (increased penalties for repeated/reckless conduct or amounts exceeding thresholds), suspend the RUC and export privileges, and refer the case for criminal tax-evasion prosecution (defraudación tributaria), which is punishable by imprisonment of 2 to 5 years under the Código Fiscal and applicable Panamanian criminal/anti-money-laundering statutes; the legal burden rests on the exporter to demonstrate both the factual export and the inward remittance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both capital and current account transactions are fully convertible without prior SBP or executive approval. Panama does not impose exchange controls, surrender obligations, or capital-flow licensing. Profit remittance is unrestricted: income from foreign-source commercial activity of a Panama Private Interest Foundation (including international ocean freight and wholesale distribution) is outside the territorial income tax base to the extent properly sourced, and remittance to beneficiaries is not subject to Panamanian dividend withholding. The only practical remittance hurdle is submission of an accurate source-of-funds and source-of-wealth rationale during bank compliance review, not an exchange-control barrier. The U.S. Dollar as the transacting currency eliminates convertibility delay and FX liquidity risk within the PIF's clearing structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rule exists in Panama. Exporters and commercial intermediaries may retain 100% of proceeds in U.S. Dollars, subject only to AML/CFT reporting thresholds: cash transactions of USD 10,000 or more must be reported to the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) under Cabinet Decree No. 33 of 2016 and Law 23 of 2015. Non-cash wire and letter-of-credit proceeds are freely disposable, subject to standard bank records, OFAC screening and the receiving bank's verification regime. There is no mandated repatriation period, no obligatory conversion ratio, and no central-bank registry of export contracts.

Central bank supervision: Panama operates a fully dollarized monetary system with the U.S. Dollar as legal tender; the Balboa is a nominal unit pegged at par. The Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the State fiscal agent and clearing agent, while banking supervision and prudential regulation rest with the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). There is no currency-issuing central bank and no centralized exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-Low, ancillary use only. Panamanian law does not create a separate electronic money institution (EMI) license; non-bank payment processors and digital wallets must obtain SBP authorization under Decree Law No. 2 of 2008 and Acuerdo No. 3 of 2015. Offshore EMIs supervised in foreign jurisdictions are not permitted to carry out deposit-taking or payment services through a Panamanian establishment, and SBP-licensed clearing banks may reject credits drawn through an unlicensed EMI. For the clinker and ocean-freight trade, offshore EMIs may be used only as a supplementary collection channel for small advance payments or post-shipment receivables; principal disbursements for vessel charter and clinker purchase must move through full SWIFT bank rails to preserve documentary evidence, LC capacity and payment provenance. Virtual IBAN products from EMIs cannot substitute for the PIF's primary Panamanian bank account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Because Panama is dollarized, capital injection is settled in USD via the U.S. correspondent clearing system (Fedwire/CHIPS) without conversion or Central Bank approval. The founder or parent entity should issue a SWIFT MT103 identifying the PIF, its bank and account number, with Remittance Information in field 70 stating 'Capital Contribution to Private Interest Foundation No. [Registry Number]' and referencing the Foundation Charter date; the ordering customer in field 50 must match the account from which funds are debited to prevent misattribution. Pre-wiring compliance: corroborate the UBO identity, obtain an SBP-compliant source-of-funds declaration, and ensure the settlement debit originates from a banking account in good standing in the founder's jurisdiction of residence. For capital injection above USD 1,000,000, a compliance interview and evidence of the underlying wealth generation event are required; funds must not be commingled with proceeds from any sanctioned jurisdiction.Primary clearing rails are (i) a commercial documentary Letter of Credit established via SWIFT MT700 with amendments via MT707 for imported portland clinker purchases, issued by the Panamanian bank and confirmed by the supplier's bank, with settlement executed through SWIFT MT103 in USD; and (ii) plain telegraphic transfer via SWIFT MT103 for vessel charter hire, demurrage, port dues and Panama Canal tolls. Field 70 of the MT103 should carry a concise purpose such as 'Payment under B/L No. [X] under LC [Y]' or 'Charter hire Vessel [Z]'; field 50 must present the PIF or its designated ordering party as the payer. All outbound payments are routed through the bank's U.S. correspondent account in USD, subject to the bank's internal sanctions screening, and underlying commercial contracts and bills of lading may be required for payments above USD 250,000. Beneficiary banks in non-dollar jurisdictions convert on the sender's instruction; where a fixed rate is required, a correspondent-level FX purchase or forward will be secured at the PIF's request.USD/EUR and USD/CNY offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for cement-supply purchase contracts invoiced in Euro or Renminbi.Cross-currency swaps between USD and EUR, or USD and CNY, executed through an SBP-licensed international banking center branch for long-term charter or cargo-pricing exposure.Plain vanilla foreign exchange options (call/put) permitted under Decree Law No. 2 of 2008, executed as OTC derivatives with a tier-1 Panamanian bank or its international broker, subject to Foundation Council authorization.Synthetic hedging structures embedded in dual-currency deposit products offered by international banks in Panama, enabling the PIF to convert maturing deposits at a pre-set strike rate.Commodity- and freight-linked contracts with embedded FX elements used solely for matching the ocean freight and clinker purchase legs; these are permitted as trade-related hedging and not as speculative leverage.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A panamanian Private Interest Foundation is not required to appoint an independent auditor solely on the basis of its legal form. A certified statutory audit becomes mandatory only where the foundation is operated as a commercial taxpayer and exceeds Panama's annual gross revenue threshold of approximately USD 1,000,000, is classified as a 'large taxpayer' by the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), or holds a financial intermediation license; a non-commercial asset-holding PIF is generally exempt from audit.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three (3) months after the fiscal year-end; for a calendar-year PIF the deadline is March 31 of the following year. Payment is due concurrently and no extension is available; quarterly advance payments (pagos a cuenta) in April, July, October and January are owed when prior-year tax exceeded B/. 100,000.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Panel A - Identificacion (Casilla 01-06) RUC number, legal name, fiscal period, ISIC/NAICS activity code (4663 / 423320) and the foundation council identification of the PIF. A non-resident PIF with no Panamanian-source income files a nil declaration (declaracion sin movimiento) with the same identification blocks; the RUC must be obtained before filing even when no tax is due. Late filing triggers a 10% surcharge on the tax due (minimum B/. 100), monthly default interest of 1% on unpaid amounts (Tax Code Art. 712), and administrative fines from B/. 50 to B/. 5,000; failure after official notice permits DGI closure of the establishment and prosecution for tax evasion (imprisonment of 2 to 5 years).
Casilla 101 - Ingresos brutos obtenidos dentro de la Republica de Panama Gross receipts from wholesale cement or clinker sales where delivery, passage of title, or the port bills of lading evidence a Panamanian destination, plus domestic cabotage freight. Non-residents report only Panamanian-source revenue; deliveries discharged in Panama must not be recharacterized as foreign transactions. Late filing triggers a 10% surcharge on the tax due (minimum B/. 100), monthly default interest of 1% on unpaid amounts (Tax Code Art. 712), and administrative fines from B/. 50 to B/. 5,000; failure after official notice permits DGI closure of the establishment and prosecution for tax evasion (imprisonment of 2 to 5 years).
Casilla 101-B - Ingresos brutos por actividades realizadas fuera de Panama (exentos) Gross income from international ocean vessel freight and wholesale hydraulic cement sales delivered outside Panama, substantiated by bills of lading, container manifests, and foreign purchase orders. The exemption is claimed for foreign-source income under the territorial rule; voyage logs, port-of-discharge evidence, and transfer pricing documentation must be retained where intercompany sales are involved. Late filing triggers a 10% surcharge on the tax due (minimum B/. 100), monthly default interest of 1% on unpaid amounts (Tax Code Art. 712), and administrative fines from B/. 50 to B/. 5,000; failure after official notice permits DGI closure of the establishment and prosecution for tax evasion (imprisonment of 2 to 5 years).
Casilla 120 - Total de ingresos brutos Aggregate of Panamanian-source taxable income and exempt foreign-source income; the entry is informational for territorial sourcing reconciliation. The reported total must reconcile with bank statements and audited financial records; mismatches trigger DGI cross-check flags. Late filing triggers a 10% surcharge on the tax due (minimum B/. 100), monthly default interest of 1% on unpaid amounts (Tax Code Art. 712), and administrative fines from B/. 50 to B/. 5,000; failure after official notice permits DGI closure of the establishment and prosecution for tax evasion (imprisonment of 2 to 5 years).
Casilla 130 - Costos y gastos incurridos para obtener los ingresos gravables Deductible costs such as cost of clinker and cement, port handling, charter hire attributable to Panamanian voyages, salaries, and depreciation incurred for Panamanian-source income; expenses allocable to exempt foreign revenue are non-deductible. A reasonable apportionment of warehouse and freight costs must be prepared and signed by the external accountant; the allocation working paper must be kept for audit. Late filing triggers a 10% surcharge on the tax due (minimum B/. 100), monthly default interest of 1% on unpaid amounts (Tax Code Art. 712), and administrative fines from B/. 50 to B/. 5,000; failure after official notice permits DGI closure of the establishment and prosecution for tax evasion (imprisonment of 2 to 5 years).
Casilla 150 - Renta neta gravable Net taxable income after statutory deductions and allowable reserves. A loss year still requires filing; the 5-year loss carryforward is claimed prospectively in subsequent years. Late filing triggers a 10% surcharge on the tax due (minimum B/. 100), monthly default interest of 1% on unpaid amounts (Tax Code Art. 712), and administrative fines from B/. 50 to B/. 5,000; failure after official notice permits DGI closure of the establishment and prosecution for tax evasion (imprisonment of 2 to 5 years).
Casilla 170 - Impuesto computado (ISR) Tax computed at the progressive rate table up to 25% for net taxable income not exceeding B/. 1,500,000, or at the flat 25% rate for net taxable income above B/. 1,500,000. Only net Panamanian-source taxable income enters the calculation; exempt foreign-source income must be excluded to avoid overpayment. Late filing triggers a 10% surcharge on the tax due (minimum B/. 100), monthly default interest of 1% on unpaid amounts (Tax Code Art. 712), and administrative fines from B/. 50 to B/. 5,000; failure after official notice permits DGI closure of the establishment and prosecution for tax evasion (imprisonment of 2 to 5 years).
Casilla 180 - Pagos adelantados y retenciones sufridas Quarterly advance payments and Panamanian withholding tax credits available to the PIF, including ITBMS credits and any treaty-reduced withholding credits. Credits must be supported by withholding certificates; unmatched credits are subject to audit rejection. Late filing triggers a 10% surcharge on the tax due (minimum B/. 100), monthly default interest of 1% on unpaid amounts (Tax Code Art. 712), and administrative fines from B/. 50 to B/. 5,000; failure after official notice permits DGI closure of the establishment and prosecution for tax evasion (imprisonment of 2 to 5 years).
Casilla 200 - Impuesto por pagar o saldo a favor Final tax payable or refundable balance after application of credits and advance payments. A zero balance combined with substantial exempt foreign-source flows is acceptable only if the sourcing analysis is complete, documented, and retained for five years. Late filing triggers a 10% surcharge on the tax due (minimum B/. 100), monthly default interest of 1% on unpaid amounts (Tax Code Art. 712), and administrative fines from B/. 50 to B/. 5,000; failure after official notice permits DGI closure of the establishment and prosecution for tax evasion (imprisonment of 2 to 5 years).
