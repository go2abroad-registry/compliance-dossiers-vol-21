Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama. Functional portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI (https://sedeelectronica.dgi.gob.pa) for RUC (TIN) applications, e-filing of ISR and ITBMS, e-invoicing, and tax certificates; auxiliary platforms: DGI e-Tax, the electronic invoicing portal, call center and regional tax offices.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Panamanian S.A.: 1) Appoint a resident attorney and a registered agent with a physical office in Panama (mandatory). 2) Submit the RUC pre-registration via the DGI Sede Electrónica or through the National Single Window for Investment (VUI), attaching: (a) certified copy of the Public Deed of Incorporation and by-laws; (b) apostilled/legalized incorporation certificates of any foreign parent entity, with Spanish translation; (c) notarized power of attorney in favor of the local agent; (d) final beneficial-owner sworn declaration (for CRS and economic substance); (e) valid identification documents of directors, officers and shareholders; (f) proof of the registered office address; and (g) the Aviso de Operación (commencement notice). 3) DGI issues the 20-digit RUC, simultaneously activating the ITBMS registration and municipal business license registration; the procedure is completed upon downloading the digital Registro Único de Contribuyentes certificate. 4) Activate the DGI e-Tax credentials and mandatory electronic invoicing account; complete the process within approximately 5–10 business days. There is no minimum capital requirement and no statutory share-capital deposit.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope — the Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) is a separately taxed legal person; no pass-through or transparent treatment exists. The S.A. pays corporate income tax (standard rate 25%; reduced statutory rates apply to qualifying SEM/CEP export-service income and specific licensed financial activities) only on Panamanian-source income and on profits derived from assets used or rights economically exercised in Panama. Foreign-source income (including fees from virtual card issuing, processing and spend-management services for non-resident merchants and foreign issuing banks) falls outside the Panamanian tax net where the service is performed and the economic use occurs abroad. Dividends distributed to shareholders are subject to a final 10% withholding tax at the corporate level; an alternative 4% minimum tax on undistributed taxable profits operates as a mechanism in lieu of actual distribution. A zero-tax status is available only within statutorily recognized tax-exempt zones (e.g., Colón Free Zone, SEM/CEM regional headquarters, Ciudad del Saber). — For a foreign-owned Panamanian S.A.: 1) Appoint a resident attorney and a registered agent with a physical office in Panama (mandatory). 2) Submit the RUC pre-registration via the DGI Sede Electrónica or through the National Single Window for Investment (VUI), attaching: (a) certified copy of the Public Deed of Incorporation and by-laws; (b) apostilled/legalized incorporation certificates of any foreign parent entity, with Spanish translation; (c) notarized power of attorney in favor of the local agent; (d) final beneficial-owner sworn declaration (for CRS and economic substance); (e) valid identification documents of directors, officers and shareholders; (f) proof of the registered office address; and (g) the Aviso de Operación (commencement notice). 3) DGI issues the 20-digit RUC, simultaneously activating the ITBMS registration and municipal business license registration; the procedure is completed upon downloading the digital Registro Único de Contribuyentes certificate. 4) Activate the DGI e-Tax credentials and mandatory electronic invoicing account; complete the process within approximately 5–10 business days. There is no minimum capital requirement and no statutory share-capital deposit.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope — the Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) is a separately taxed legal person; no pass-through or transparent treatment exists. The S.A. pays corporate income tax (standard rate 25%; reduced statutory rates apply to qualifying SEM/CEP export-service income and specific licensed financial activities) only on Panamanian-source income and on profits derived from assets used or rights economically exercised in Panama. Foreign-source income (including fees from virtual card issuing, processing and spend-management services for non-resident merchants and foreign issuing banks) falls outside the Panamanian tax net where the service is performed and the economic use occurs abroad. Dividends distributed to shareholders are subject to a final 10% withholding tax at the corporate level; an alternative 4% minimum tax on undistributed taxable profits operates as a mechanism in lieu of actual distribution. A zero-tax status is available only within statutorily recognized tax-exempt zones (e.g., Colón Free Zone, SEM/CEM regional headquarters, Ciudad del Saber).

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections exist under Panamanian law; classification follows the Código Fiscal and DGI rulings. Available elections: (i) Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente (RPC) — optional for gross annual income up to B/. 36,000, substituting ISR and ITBMS filings with a fixed monthly quota (B/. 50–B/. 300); (ii) SEM/CEM Multinational Headquarters Regime (Law 41 of 2007, Law 21 of 2017) — election for a 5% income tax rate on qualifying export services, 0% ITBMS on intra-group services and full foreign-source characterization; (iii) Advance Pricing Agreements (APAs) and written advance tax rulings on transfer-pricing treatments for cross-border card-network flows; (iv) the Innovative Enterprises (fintech) licensing regime under Law 242 of 2021, under which qualified digital-payment companies may obtain special administrative and fiscal treatment through an advance DGI ruling.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de minimis registration threshold: every person carrying out taxable transfers or services in Panama must register for ITBMS from the first taxable event, regardless of turnover. A statutory simplified regime threshold applies for small taxpayers: annual gross income up to B/. 36,000 (Balboa, pegged 1:1 to the US dollar) can elect the fixed-monthly-quota RPC instead of the standard ITBMS/ISR filing. A special simplified ITBMS registration regime is also applicable to non-domiciled digital-service providers supplying services to Panamanian consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% ITBMS applies where tangible goods are physically exported from Panamanian customs territory, the Customs Export Declaration is issued in the exporter's name, and the commercial invoice is addressed to a non-resident purchaser. Services: 0% applies where (i) the service is performed and the benefit is enjoyed wholly outside Panama, (ii) the service is directly related to exported goods, or (iii) the recipient is a qualifying SEM/CEM regional-headquarters company. For a corporate virtual-card issuer, network, processing, interchange and spend-management fees invoiced to foreign merchants, foreign issuing banks and non-resident cardholders qualify as zero-rated export services when the processing location, merchant establishment, card account or bank is outside Panama and no Panamanian fixed establishment economically consumes the service. Claimants must hold a written contract evidencing the non-resident beneficiary, demonstrate commercial-economic substance, maintain transfer-pricing documentation at arm's length, and show foreign-currency collection normally within 180 days of invoice.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación de Mercancías) with exit authorization and customs seal
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing despatch to a non-resident consignee
  • Commercial invoice with RUC, Incoterms, full description, quantity and value
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT / Foreign Currency Receipt Voucher evidencing the foreign-currency payment credited to the exporter's account
  • Supporting service-export proof: signed contract or service order, card-network settlement statements (Mastercard/Visa Direct records), and certification of non-resident use or consumption for zero-rated services

Mandatory electronic invoicing (eFactura) applies to all ITBMS-registered taxpayers under the DGI electronic-invoicing technical standard introduced by DGI Resolution and phased in since 2020. Issuers must use DGI-authorized software or the free DGI portal; each invoice is filed to the DGI for near-real-time validation and receives a unique digital authorization code (digital stamp) before or at the moment of issuance. The system requires XML-format invoicing, digital signatures, and periodic reporting (daily, weekly or quarterly depending on taxpayer category). The DGI automatically cross-reconciles the e-invoicing database with ITBMS returns, customs records, banking information and card-settlement data, effectively operating as a centralized fiscal clearance mechanism.

DGI automatically cross-matches zero-rated ITBMS declarations with customs cargo manifests, banking records and card-network settlement data; discrepancies generate immediate audit flags. Sanctions: (i) restitution of the unpaid 7% ITBMS plus statutory overdue interest and late-payment surcharges (generally 10%–50% depending on delay); (ii) administrative fines from B/. 1,000 to B/. 100,000 against the taxpayer and its legal representative; (iii) publication of the penalty and disqualification from future export-refund claims; (iv) criminal tax-fraud prosecution under Código Penal Articles 310–313 with imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and disqualification from public contracts. The DGI may reopen the last 5 fiscal years (or 10 years where no return was filed) and claw back previously granted refunds with interest; the registered agent and shareholders may incur joint and several liability for gross negligence in the filing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized and freely convertible monetary system. Both current-account and capital-account transactions may be settled without exchange-control approval. Profit, dividend, and capital remittances are unrestricted; only normal AML/CFT documentary due diligence applies to outbound and inbound USD transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio apply to export receipts. Exporters may retain proceeds in USD either abroad or in Panamanian local banks. Panama imposes no export-proceeds surrender or repatriation duty, although banks may request supporting export documents under AML/CFT procedures.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); Panama has no traditional central bank. The U.S. dollar is unrestricted legal tender, and Banco Nacional de Panamá operates as a state-owned commercial bank rather than a monetary authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium. An offshore digital EMI without a Panamanian license cannot directly access local USD clearing or ACH Panama except through a licensed sponsor bank. International EMIs may onboard the Panamanian S.A. as a corporate client, but local merchant payouts require a sponsor relationship with an SBP-licensed bank or a locally licensed payment service provider. Foreign EMI passporting is not recognized; a local payment institution or sponsored banking agent is the legally prudent route.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Panama taxpayer must prepare and file a Transfer Pricing Local File/informative return when annual gross revenue exceeds B/.3 million; a Master File is additionally required for multinational groups with consolidated group annual revenue above B/.100 million. Controlled cross-border transactions above B/.1 million require transaction-level functional and economic analysis.

Economic substance scope: Panama ESR Law 254 of 2021 applies. A virtual card issuer engaged in financial services must perform core income-generating activities in Panama, maintain a licensed physical office and locally resident employees, keep books in Panama, hold board meetings in Panama, and demonstrate that card-issuance, risk, AML/KYC, network contract, and settlement decisions are managed from Panama.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to 100%-300% of the evaded tax, surcharges, monthly interest, criminal prosecution under Panama's Código Fiscal, and imprisonment of 3 to 6 years in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false returns or direct the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, subject to civil restitution, travel bans, and conviction with imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Registered agent de-registration, loss of tax benefits, DGI recharacterization of all revenue, fines under Law 254 of 2021, and revocation of the corporate license and tax identification number.

Director personal liability: Panamanian courts will pierce the corporate veil. Directors and ultimate beneficial owners must personally restore diverted funds, face civil restitution, and may be subjected to travel bans and criminal prosecution.
AML / Unlicensed Payment Activity

Statutory sanction: Fines under Law 23 of 2015 and SBP financial regulations ranging from B/.150,000 to B/.5 million per violation, mandatory closure, confiscation of settlement funds, and criminal referral for money-laundering offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners may be individually fined, removed from the financial system, denied banking access, banned from travel, and imprisoned for money-laundering or terrorist-financing offenses.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: DGI may re-price the controlled transactions, impose a 25% CIT adjustment, and levy penalties of up to 100% of the adjusted tax plus interest. Transfer-pricing documentation failures carry additional fixed monetary fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's-length intercompany terms are jointly liable for the additional assessment; deliberate profit-stripping can be prosecuted as criminal tax evasion and leads to director travel bans and imprisonment.
