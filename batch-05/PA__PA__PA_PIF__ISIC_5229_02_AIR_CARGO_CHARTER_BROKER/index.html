Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), with its Sede Electrónica DGI electronic tax portal at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) under Law 25 of 1995 is a Panamanian legal person and must: (1) be incorporated and registered at the Registro Público de Panamá through a Panama-licensed resident agent and a duly appointed foundation council; (2) file the Aviso de Operación with the DGI declaring the ISIC 5229 air cargo charter brokerage activity; (3) apply for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) through the Sede Electrónica DGI, submitting the foundation charter, resident agent acceptance, registered address, and foundation council documentation; (4) enroll simultaneously in the ITBMS (VAT) taxpayer registry and obtain electronic invoicing authorization from the DGI; (5) open a US Dollar bank account for receiving broker commissions via SWIFT Wire or Fedwire and file monthly ITBMS returns and the annual income tax return through the DGI portal. A foreign entity without a Panamanian fixed place of business is not required to register solely for offshore brokerage activities; if it operates through a permanent establishment in Panama, it must obtain its own RUC and register for ITBMS.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope; Entity_Level_Taxation: the PIF is a separate taxpayer subject to the 25% corporate income tax only upon Panamanian-source income. Foreign-source fees derived from cross-border air cargo charter brokerage remain outside the Panamanian tax base unless the income is sourced to Panama through local performance or a Panamanian permanent establishment.

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections and regimes include: (1) the Régimen Especial Simplificado (simplified small-taxpayer tax method for annual gross income below USD 300,000); (2) the Sede de Empresa Multinacional (SEM) election under Law 41 of 2007 for qualified multinational headquarters operations; (3) advance pricing agreements with the DGI for transfer pricing certainty; and (4) for hybrid mismatch exposure, the CFC, economic substance, and transparency rules introduced by Panama Law 254 of 2019. A PIF cannot elect pass-through status, but non-Panamanian-source income is excluded from the taxable base under territoriality.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no monetary turnover threshold triggering registration: every person habitually performing taxable acts in Panama must register for ITBMS and obtain an RUC. A USD 300,000 annual gross income ceiling is only the eligibility boundary for the optional simplified small-taxpayer regime, not a registration exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for the 0% ITBMS export rate where they physically leave Panama under a customs export declaration (DUA) in the exporter's RUC, are destined to a foreign market, and are paid from abroad. Services, including air cargo charter brokerage commissions, qualify where the service is supplied to a non-resident counterparty, is not effectively used or consumed in Panama, confers its benefit outside Panama, and the fee is received from a foreign bank. Eligible zero-rated exporters may also recover attributable input ITBMS credits.

  • Certified copy of the ANA Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera, DUA)
  • Original Air Waybill (AWB) or Bill of Lading evidencing cargo uplift and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of USD proceeds from the foreign counterparty via SWIFT Wire or Fedwire
  • Executed export service contract and the DGI-approved 0% ITBMS export invoice
  • Foundation council statement certifying that the brokerage service is consumed outside Panama and that no Panamanian recipient exists

Panama mandates real-time fiscal clearance: all active RUC holders must issue electronic invoices through DGI-certified integrators, transmit invoice data via API (REST/SOAP) to the Sede Electrónica DGI for pre-validation, obtain a DGI digital stamp and QR verification code, and deliver the cleared document to the buyer. Paper or PDF invoices are admissible only under certified DGI contingency procedures; invoices issued without DGI clearance are invalid and cannot support ITBMS input credits.

The DGI may audit and reassess within the general six-year prescription period. If export zero-rating is unsupported, fraudulent, or not repatriated within 180 days, the DGI retroactively claws back the 7% ITBMS from the invoice date with monthly statutory interest, imposes fines of 25% to 100% of the omitted tax, excludes the taxpayer from the 0% export procedure register, cancels the Aviso de Operación, and may pursue criminal tax fraud charges carrying two to five years' imprisonment and fines up to USD 200,000 for wilful evasion.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility exists on both capital and current account transactions. There are no exchange control laws, no currency exchange license requirements, and no centralized foreign exchange market. Dividends, profit remittances, loan repayments, equity capital returns, and service payments to non-residents may be freely effected in U.S. dollars. The only practical constraints are bank-level AML/KYC requirements, source-of-funds verification, tax attestations, and evidence of the underlying cargo charter brokerage transaction for unusual or large wire flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline for surrendering export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign currency receipts. Air cargo charter brokerage income may remain in U.S. dollars in any authorized bank account, be used directly for outbound disbursements, or be repatriated without prior approval from any Panamanian authority.

Central bank supervision: Panama operates without an independent central bank; the U.S. dollar is legal tender. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panama (SBP) is the primary banking and foreign-exchange-related regulator, while Banco Nacional de Panama acts as the fiscal agent of the State with no monetary authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Panama recognizes fintech operators under Law 125 of 2020, but there is no standalone e-money institution (EMI) license identical to the EU or UK regimes. Offshore digital EMI platforms may be utilized for outbound trade disbursements and collection of cross-border charter brokerage fees only when the EMI settles through an SBP-licensed bank or a U.S./international correspondent bank acceptable to the Panamanian banking system. Such EMIs are not a substitute for an onshore Panama account for capital injection and are not treated as regulated deposit-taking institutions. The viability rating is Medium: permissible for treasury optimization and cross-border disbursement speed, but the PM/PIF must maintain a primary onshore account with a licensed Panamanian bank and ensure that all EMIs are subject to sanctions screening, AML monitoring, and transaction traceability.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

PIF beneficial ownership opacity and ambiguous interest structure

Root cause: PIF beneficial ownership opacity and ambiguous interest structure

Defense evidence: Notarized and apostilled foundation deed, articles of incorporation, registered agent certificate, foundation council resolution appointing the cargo charter broker manager, updated beneficial ownership register filed in accordance with Law 23 of 2015 and Decree 41 of 2018, valid passports and other national identity documents of all natural-person beneficial owners, and a notarized source-of-wealth declaration.
High-risk air cargo charter contracts with payments to offshore, tax-haven, or non-transparent counterparties

Root cause: High-risk air cargo charter contracts with payments to offshore, tax-haven, or non-transparent counterparties

Defense evidence: Signed charter agreements with verified counterparties, commercial and pro forma invoices, aircraft registration documents (Panama Aircraft Registry where applicable), air waybills (AWB) and cargo manifests, commission calculation summaries, correspondence trail showing commercial negotiation, and a written AML risk assessment of each high-risk counterparty.
Unusually large inbound contributions or funding from unrelated offshore jurisdictions

Root cause: Unusually large inbound contributions or funding from unrelated offshore jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Source-of-funds bank statements, PIF board resolution authorizing the capital injection, executed loan or contribution agreements, SWIFT payment evidence, capital account ledger entries in the PIF's accounting records, and a professional legal opinion confirming the transaction is consistent with the foundation's registered purpose.
OFAC, sanctions, and PEP exposure through cargo routes or client jurisdictions

Root cause: OFAC, sanctions, and PEP exposure through cargo routes or client jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Automated sanctions and PEP screening results, OFAC SDN list checks, denied-party screening logs, IP and geolocation evidence for digital transactions, beneficial owner identification of all material counterparties, contractual sanctions and embargo representations, and a compliance certificate issued by the PIF's designated AML officer.
Co-mingling of personal, foundation, and sub-broker funds within the same account

Root cause: Co-mingling of personal, foundation, and sub-broker funds within the same account

Defense evidence: Maintenance of segregated accounts in the sole name of the PIF, board-approved banking mandate containing an anti-mingling policy, detailed accounting ledgers by client and charter contract, annual audited financial statements (even where not statutorily required), and a transaction-monitoring procedure identifying expected activity volumes for the air cargo brokerage business.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections are structured through SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire transfers from the beneficial owner, a related finance company, or an investor account to the PIF's designated U.S.-dollar account in Panama. The wire must clearly state the beneficiary: 'Private Interest Foundation PA_PIF', the beneficiary bank in Panama, the full account number, and a purpose narrative such as 'Capital Contribution to PIF PA_PIF under Foundation Deed dated [date]' or 'Intercompany Loan to PIF PA_PIF for Working Capital'. The remitting bank must transmit the complete ordering customer chain (name, address, account number) and, when required, the sourcing financial institution. The receiving Panamanian bank will require the PIF's registered agent certificate, foundation deed excerpt, and supporting board resolution; inclusion of the commercial invoice, capital account entry, and good-standing certificate reduces clearing delays. For contributions in EUR, GBP, CNY, or other non-USD currencies, the remittance may be credited to a multicurrency account or spot-converted by the receiving licensed bank; conversion must be evidenced by a trade confirmation and recorded in the PIF's books.Outbound trade disbursements are cleared using: (1) SWIFT MT103 for high-value USD/EUR/GBP payments to charter operators, fuel supplliers, airport authorities, sub-brokers, and aircraft maintenance providers; (2) SWIFT MT202 COV for downstream interbank cover payments executed by the intermediary bank; (3) Fedwire for same-day clearing requiring US-dollar finality to domestic U.S. beneficiaries; (4) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600/ISBP) issued through SWIFT MT700/MT710 for aircraft charter and cargo-service purchase obligations; (5) Documentary collections (DP/DA) through the correspondent banking network for less liquid freight jurisdictions; and (6) ACH Panama for low-value domestic U.S.-dollar payments to Panamanian vendors. Each disbursement must reference the underlying charter agreement, carrier commission invoice, and air waybill; no exchange-control approval is required, but the PIF must preserve the full transaction trail for bank audit and OFAC/SIU review.Outright FX forward contracts with licensed Panamanian and international banks matching EUR/GBP/CNY charter brokerage receivables and payables, with tenors of up to 24 monthsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing USD for emerging-market currencies where physical delivery is restricted or illiquid, including fuel and routing corridor exposuresCross-currency swaps (USD vs EUR/GBP/CNY) executed under an ISDA Master Agreement to hedge structural foreign-currency cash-flow mismatchesPlain vanilla FX call and put options and zero-cost collars for protection against adverse exchange-rate movements on large lease payments and aircraft charter feesFX forward roll-over facilities and netting arrangements provided by the PIF's primary relationship bank, subject to credit approval and ISDA documentation
