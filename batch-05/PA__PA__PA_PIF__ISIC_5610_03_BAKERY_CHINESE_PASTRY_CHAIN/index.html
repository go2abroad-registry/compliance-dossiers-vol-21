Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama. Official e-Tax portal: https://etax.mef.gob.pa; institutional website: www.dgi.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

A Private Interest Foundation (PIF) constituted under Law 25 of 1995 is a legal person but is not automatically a registered taxpayer. Registration in the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) is compelled only when the foundation engages in Panama-source economic activity, trades through a local establishment, or elects to operate a commercial business. SOP for foreign-owned PIFs: (1) formalize the Foundation Charter before a notary and file it at the Registro Público, appointing a resident foundation agent; (2) access the e-Tax portal and file the 'Solicitud de Inscripción de Contribuyente' (DGI form 600-1) with the foundation charter, the agent's notarized acceptance, and identification of the founder and council members; (3) if the bakery franchise chain operates a central kitchen or master franchise office in Panama, complete the physical premises inspection and obtain a local patent (patente) from the corresponding Alcaldía as a precursor to RUC activation; (4) receive the RUC certificate with the 13-digit taxpayer number and enroll in the Social Security registry (CSS) if employees will be hired; (5) procure the digital signature certificate (firma digital) issued by authorized certification providers to activate electronic filing and electronic invoicing credentials. The DGI does not apply a check-the-box or look-through election at registration; the PIF''s fiscal identity is determined by the territorial-source classification of the activity it actually executes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections affecting the PIF and its bakery chain structure include: (a) the PIF's foundational election under Law 25/1995 to remain a private-interest vehicle holding assets solely abroad, keeping it outside the income tax net and outside ITBMS; (b) the election to convert the PIF's operation into an active commercial undertaking, which subjects its Panama-source revenue to corporate income tax; (c) the Optional Simplified Regime (Régimen Opcional Simplificado, ROS) for small taxpayers with gross annual turnover up to B/. 300,000; (d) the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41/2007 and the Multinational Business Hub (SNM) regime under Law 254/2019 for managing royalty, franchise, and regional service income; and (e) Zona Libre de Colón and processing-zone classification for the manufacturing/re-export wing of the pastry chain, which confers import-duty exemption and ITBMS deferral on re-export. Hybrid mismatch elections are not separately codified; Panama's territorial system and absence of general anti-avoidance hybrid rules make the PIF's offshore-fee flows eligible for favorable treatment if danger of DGI re-characterization is managed through substance.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account; no exchange controls distinguish capital or current transactions. The U.S. dollar is freely usable, and the Balboa exists only as coinage. Profit remittances are unrestricted; no prior Central Bank or SBP approval is required. Any applicable taxes, such as capital gains or dividend withholding for territorial-source earnings, are assessed separately and do not block transfer of funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export proceeds surrender exists under Panamanian law. There are no statutory surrender timelines, no required conversion ratios into local currency, and no requirement to register export receipts with the SBP. Export proceeds may be retained in U.S. dollars or another convertible currency, credited to a Panamanian account, and used freely for payments abroad or repatriation of profits.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the banking and foreign-exchange supervising authority; Panama does not maintain a classic central bank or issue monetary policy, as the United States dollar is legal tender and the Balboa is a unit of account only.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Pure offshore fintech EMI entities not supervised by the SBP have limited viability for opening a full corporate account or executing significant transaction flows due to correspondent-bank de-risking. Panama's Financial Innovation Law (Law No. 320 of 2023) provides a sandbox regime, and only licensed fintech participants or banks partnering with regulated EMIs may provide treasury services. A rational structure is to use a licensed local bank as the primary clearing agent and a sandbox-approved fintech facilitator only for point-of-sale settlement and merchant wallet receipts.

Root cause: Off-shore incorporator or shell-company risk from a third-country jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Copy of the valid registered-agent licence; incorporation certificates of any intermediate holding vehicles; legalized commercial extract from the jurisdiction of the founder; complete chain of ownership from PIF to bakery franchise entity; audited or reviewed financial statements of the bakery operating company; and clear source-of-wealth documentation for initial endowment.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foundation endowment and shareholder capital injections from abroad will be channelled by SWIFT MT103 priority transfers to a designated Capital Escrow Account maintained at a Top-Tier Panamanian bank. The beneficiary must include the full certified PIF name and the purpose field should state 'FOUNDATION INITIAL CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / SUBSCRIPTION TO PRIVATE INTEREST FOUNDATION UNDER PANAMA LAW 25 OF 1995'. The remitter must appear uniformly on all corporate records. Beneficiary bank will require a legalized copy of the foundation deed, a certificate of legal existence, a registered-agent letter, the KYC acceptance form, and the bank's specific 'Confirmation of Source of Funds' executed by the contributor. Because Panama is dollarized, no foreign-exchange conversion occurs for USD->USD movement. For non-USD original currencies, the transferring bank will convert at market rate and communicate the SWIFT MT202 cover payment through a correspondent U.S. or Panamanian clearing bank. The inbound remittance should also include the IPT (Impuesto de Transferencias de Bienes Muebles y Prestación de Servicios) invoice number if the capital brings a taxable asset, or, where exempt, a letter from outside counsel listing the applicable exemption.Outbound clearing for trade settlements is primarily performed through (a) SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers for raw ingredients, franchise royalties, supplier invoices, and equipment deposits; (b) irrevocable at-sight commercial letters of credit (LC) confirmed by a U.S. or European correspondent for larger kitchen fixtures and central-kitchen machinery; and (c) ACH debits or payroll in Panama using the national clearing house accompanying the 'Cámara de Compensación' for local vendors and franchise operators. For CNY, EUR or other currency settlements, the Panamanian bank will issue MT103 US-treasury-backed messages via a New York or Hong Kong correspondent, with full SWIFT travel-rule fields. Trade payment instructions must identify the ISIC 5610 bakery master-franchise operating company as the remitter and the exact purchase order number, LC applicant, and product classification code. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% (statutory rate on dividends paid to any shareholder; an alternative 5% rate applies where the distributing profits have already borne Panamanian corporate income tax at the full 25% rate and are separately identified in the reserves).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 90 calendar days after the fiscal year-end. For calendar-year taxpayers, the statutory deadline is 31 March of the following year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same statutory deadline as Form 101; a written extension may be requested prior to the due date, but approval is discretionary.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and supporting transfer pricing study are mandatory where the aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed B/.250,000 (USD 250,000) or where gross annual revenue exceeds B/.1,000,000 (USD 1,000,000). A Master File is triggered when the consolidated group revenue exceeds B/.50,000,000 (USD 50,000,000).

Economic substance scope: Yes. Panama’s Economic Substance Regulations apply to PIFs and commercial entities performing geographically mobile activities, including intellectual property holding, distribution, financing, and service center functions. A bakery franchise PIF that holds trademarks and receives royalties from the local operating chain must demonstrate continuous economic substance: physical presence in Panama, personnel with appropriate qualifications, active board decisions, and operating expenditure proportionate to the income. Pure holding PIFs with no active intra-group management may be exempt if they do not conduct mobile businesses and simply hold shares and dividends; however, any active royalty licensing or central-kitchen management activities require substance reporting.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 50% to 100% of the evaded tax, penal interest, loss of tax clearance, seizure of bank accounts, and criminal prosecution under Panama’s Criminal and Tax Code. Criminal punishment may range from three to ten years’ imprisonment, disqualification from acting as a director, and forfeiture of assets traceable to the evaded funds.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the PIF’s corporate veil where the foundation is used as a mere instrument of tax evasion. Directors, trustees, and legal representatives are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, face civil restitution, travel bans, precautionary asset attachment, and imprisonment in case of intentional fraud.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The DGI may reprice the controlled transaction, disallow excess deductions, impose a penalty of up to 20% of the transfer pricing adjustment, levy annual interest on the under-withheld or underpaid tax, and charge specific fines for non-filing of Form 910 (up to B/.5,000).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the arbitrary pricing scheme can be held jointly and severally liable for the additional tax and penalties. During TP audits, responsible officers may be prohibited from leaving Panama and must appear before the tax court; intentional submission of materially false documentation may trigger criminal sanction.
Shell Company / ESR Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Violation of Panama’s economic substance regulation exposes the entity to cumulative financial penalties up to USD 500,000, mandatory exchange of information with foreign tax authorities, loss of the PIF’s license, and strike-off from the registry after continued non-compliance. The DGI also denies treaty benefits from a hollow foundation and treats the income as Panamanian-source.

Director personal liability: Officers and foundation founders who signed false substance declarations are personally liable to civil fines and damages. A travel ban may be imposed against foreign directors during a criminal investigation, and Panama’s cross-border cooperation can lead to extradition or placement on an Interpol notice for serious cases.
AML / Money Laundering Through Cash-Heavy Business

Statutory sanction: Under Panama’s Law 23 of 2015 (AML/CFT), licensed subjects face fines ranging from USD 100,000 to USD 2,000,000 per violation, suspension or revocation of licenses, closure of the local operations, criminal money-laundering penalties up to twelve years’ imprisonment, and forfeiture of laundered assets.

Director personal liability: Directors, trustees, and beneficial owners who knowingly allow money laundering through the PIF or the franchise chain incur personal criminal liability, travel bans, freezing of personal bank accounts, and confiscation of residential or commercial assets held through the foundation. The corporate veil is not recognized for illicit proceeds; principals may be prosecuted extraterritorially.
