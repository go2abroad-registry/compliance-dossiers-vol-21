Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Panama; official online portal: Sede Electrónica DGI (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities and Private Interest Foundations must first appoint and maintain a resident agent or legal representative in Panama. For a PIF, the foundation must be duly registered in the Registro Público de Panamá, after which the resident agent may apply for the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) before the DGI. The procurement process requires filing the Aviso de Operación, providing certified foundation by-laws, and submitting identity documents of the founder and resident agent through a licensed Panamanian CPA or legal counsel using the DGI Sede Electrónica. If the foundation undertakes Panamanian-source commercial activity in the designer toy sector, it must also obtain an Impuesto de Transferencia de Bienes Muebles y Servicios (ITBMS) registration and enroll in the electronic invoicing system. If importing raw materials or exporting finished vinyl art collectibles, the foundation must separately register with the National Customs Authority (Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas). A PIF with no Panamanian-source income and no local commercial activity is not automatically required to obtain a tax identification number solely by virtue of its existence, but must request a clearance certificate if it wishes to rely on tax-exempt status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election comparable to the U.S. or EU regimes. A Private Interest Foundation may, however, elect in its charter to adopt a trust-like distribution structure among beneficiaries, but this does not change its status as a separate taxpaying entity for Panamanian tax purposes. Under Panama's territorial tax system, Panama-source income derived through a commercial business is taxable at the entity level, while foreign-source income is outside the DGI's scope unless repatriated and specifically made taxable by law. No OECD-style hybrid mismatch deductions are recognized. Eligible small taxpayers may elect the simplified 'pequeño contribuyente' collection mechanism if annual gross receipts are not more than B/.36,000, but that election is a fiscal administration regime, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No minimum turnover threshold is set by law: any person or entity carrying out taxable transfers of movable goods or services in Panama must register as an ITBMS taxpayer before commencing operations. A simplified small-taxpayer treatment is available for annual gross receipts of B/.36,000 or less, but registration is still required where the economic activity is performed in Panama.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods from Panama are zero-rated for ITBMS purposes. The producer or exporter must be the registered consignor on the official customs export declaration and must demonstrate that the goods physically left Panamanian customs territory within the applicable export term. Services, including design and airbrush finishing services incorporated into exported collectible toys, may also qualify for 0% where the economic benefit is realized outside Panama and the service recipient is a non-resident. To claim the zero rate, the exporter must maintain reliable books and records showing the export invoice, customs documentation, transport contracts, and actual receipt of payment from a non-resident buyer. Input VAT directly attributable to exported goods may be claimed as a credit or refund, subject to DGI audit verification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAE / DUA) stamped by the National Customs Authority of Panama
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Panama
  • Commercial invoice issued to the non-resident buyer
  • Packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Swift/MT103 payment evidence showing foreign currency repatriation
  • Certificate of Origin (where applicable)
  • Export agent loco or warehouse receipts supporting the export chain

Panama mandates phased electronic fiscal invoicing through the DGI. Taxpayers enrolled in the electronic invoicing system must issue invoices using DGI-approved technological platforms and transmit invoice content to the DGI's Sede Electrónica for validation and approval. Each valid e-invoice is digitally stamped with a unique authorization correlative number and must contain a QR code and the taxpayer's electronic signature. The transmission is effectively near real-time for large taxpayers, with fraud audits performed through automated cross-matching against import and export customs declarations. Invoices not cleared or validated by the DGI cannot support ITBMS input VAT credits or refunds. Digital servicing and online sales of designer blind-box toys through payment rails such as Shopify Payments or Stripe are subject to the same e-invoicing rules where the taxable activity is deemed to occur in Panama.

The DGI and National Customs Authority jointly verify export zero-rating claims through admission audits and post-customs verification. Instances where the export declaration is fraudulent, overvalued, or not supported by actual departure and foreign payment will trigger immediate clawback of any VAT credit or refund taken, plus interest at the statutory monthly rate, late-payment surcharges, and fines under Articles 708 and 712 of the Código Fiscal. Penalties for deliberate documentary fraud may reach 300% of the omitted ITBMS, and criminal tax fraud charges may result in imprisonment for responsible officers or trustees. The limitation period for backward clawback in fraudulent export schemes is generally six years from the date of the return, with an extended possibility where falsified private documentary evidence is used to alter taxable events.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility under an open capital account. Panama imposes no exchange controls, no prior-approval mechanisms, and no differentiation between current and capital account transactions. Profit remittances, foundation distributions, loan repayments, royalties and capital injections are freely transferable in USD or any foreign currency; a Private Interest Foundation (PIF) is exempt from Panamanian tax on foreign-source income, and no withholding applies to distributions to non-resident beneficiaries. Practical constraint: correspondent-bank due diligence (nostro limits, USD clearing restrictions) governs convertibility, not statutory limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory repatriation timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Designer-toy export proceeds may remain in USD at any SBP-licensed bank, be held at offshore bank accounts, or be applied directly to pay foreign suppliers, subject only to the bank's internal AML transaction-monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Superintendency of Banks of Panama, SBP) as banking and AML/CFT regulator. No domestic central bank exists: Panama is fully dollarized (Law 84 of 1941), the US Federal Reserve serves as monetary authority, the Balboa circulates only as sub-dollar coins, and Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent of the Republic.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium (ancillary use only). Panama has no EU-style e-money institution licence; fintech operators require SBP authorization under the 2022 Fintech Law (Law 69 of 2022). Offshore EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Mercury, Dukascopy) are not passportable into Panama; a PIF may use them only where their terms accept Panama-incorporated entities and the EMI's banking partner tolerates a PIF legal form. Payoneer and Airwallex generally accept Panamanian corporate clients; Wise Business usually rejects Panama PIFs; Mercury accepts only US LLCs/C-Corps. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial endowment injection: founder wires from personal bank account to the PIF's Panama account via SWIFT MT103; remittance field 70 (Beneficiary Information) must state 'INITIAL ENDOWMENT – PIF CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION'; the purpose-of-transfer field in the bank form must recite 'Aporte de Capital Inicial de la Fundacion'. Supporting evidence: notarized Foundation Deed, resident-agent certificate, and source-of-funds statement. Because neither Stripe nor Shopify Payments directly supports Panama-incorporated entities, e-commerce sales of designer blind boxes must be routed through a compliant merchant-of-record architecture: (a) a Delaware LLC owned by the Foundation serves as MoR for Stripe/Shopify Payments, with funds settled at a US bank account; (b) the LLC remits residual profits to the Panama PIF by MT103 with purpose 'DISTRIBUTION / ROYALTY UNDER IP LICENCE AGREEMENT', accompanied by the licence agreement and board minutes; (c) local Panamanian POS sales settle directly in USD via Banco General Yappy or Multibank acquiring (T+1). Verification evidence: payment-platform payout confirmations, MoR intercompany agreement, e-invoices (Factura Electronica where applicable), and quarterly treasury reconciliation statements.For manufacturing and raw-material purchases (vinyl, rotocasting compounds, packaging): SWIFT MT103 for settlements under USD 50,000 with field 70 referencing 'INV <invoice number>' and corresponding commercial invoice; Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700, amendments by MT707) for production runs exceeding USD 50,000 issued by the Panama bank in favour of Chinese factories (typical tenor 30–60 days); documentary collections (DP/DA) for trusted recurring suppliers; bank payment obligation (BPO) as an optional commitment instrument. Rationale for the tiered rail: LC reduces advance-payment risk on tooling; MT103 preserves speed for finished-goods balancing orders. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium (ancillary use only). Panama has no EU-style e-money institution licence; fintech operators require SBP authorization under the 2022 Fintech Law (Law 69 of 2022). Offshore EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Mercury, Dukascopy) are not passportable into Panama; a PIF may use them only where their terms accept Panama-incorporated entities and the EMI's banking partner tolerates a PIF legal form. Payoneer and Airwallex generally accept Panamanian corporate clients; Wise Business usually rejects Panama PIFs; Mercury accepts only US LLCs/C-Corps. The legally compliant design is to use a Delaware LLC as merchant-of-record for Shopify Payments/Stripe and repatriate residual funds to the Panama PIF by MT103, while keeping all regulatory liquidity in SBP-licensed banks.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0% (Impuesto Sobre la Renta, ISR) on net taxable income of Panamanian-source only; foreign-source income excluded under the territorial regime.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10.0% WHT on dividends distributed from Panamanian-source profits that have borne ISR; distribution of untaxed/foreign-source PIF patrimony incurs no Panama dividend WHT (territoriality).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form 96 due 31 March for calendar-year entities (within three months after the fiscal year-end); PIF flat fee due 30 June; monthly returns due no later than ten days after the month-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.1,000,000 (≈US$1,000,000) annual related-party transactions (or B/.4,000,000 gross revenue) triggers mandatory Local File and Form 96 TP annex; Master File required for multinational groups exceeding B/.40,000,000 consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not impose Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) on PIFs; nevertheless, OECD BEPS/Global Forum and AML Law 23/2015 substance expectations apply. A PIF claiming foreign-source exemption must demonstrate effective management: qualified Foundation Council in Panama, resident registered agent, minutes/accounts, and documented decision-making. Pure 'paper' PIFs distributing foreign e-commerce profits risk re-characterization as a Panamanian PE and retroactive 25% ISR.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Administrative: 50%-100% fine on evaded ISR plus 1% monthly interest. Criminal: Defraudación Tributaria under Código Penal Art. 259-A: imprisonment of 3-6 years for evading over B/.250,000.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax plus surcharges, face travel bans (prohibición de salida) issued by the Caja/Fiscalía, and criminal sentences up to 6 years.
Shell/Artificial Structure (Substance Over Form)

Statutory sanction: DGI re-characterizes the PIF as a taxable commercial enterprise retroactively for up to 3 prior years, with 25% ISR, 10% dividend WHT on deemed distributions, 5%-per-month late surcharges, and 1% monthly interest.

Director personal liability: Civil: personal liability for de facto management (abuso del patrimonio autónomo); criminal: criminal association for sham documentation; travel bans and freezing orders on personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DGI intra-group adjustment using the most appropriate method; penalty of 10% of the non-compliant related-party transaction value, plus re-computed tax, 5% monthly late fee (max. 50%), and 1% monthly interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved unrealistic intercompany pricing are jointly liable for the TP adjustment when the PIF is insolvent; tax collection proceedings may include personal asset seizure and travel restrictions.
AML / Beneficial Ownership (UAF & BO Registry)

Statutory sanction: Law 23/2015 (as amended): fines from US$100,000 to US$1,000,000 for severe AML violations; BO Registry: US$500-US$5,000 per unfiled record, escalating to US$50,000 for repeat offenders; prohibition of beneficial distributions.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal money-laundering liability (5-10 years imprisonment) for Foundation Council members who conceal ultimate beneficiaries; civil fines apply to directors individually; travel bans and asset freezes ordered by the UAF/Fiscalía anticorrupción.
