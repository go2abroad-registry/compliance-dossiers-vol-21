Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The Foundation must be funded with an initial endowment or foundation capital. Law 25 of 1995 does not prescribe a fixed amount, but Public Registry practice requires the endowment to be real and adequate for the stated purpose; USD 10,000 is the recommended standard for an operating medical-device manufacturing foundation. No mandatory escrow account is required; the endowment may be evidenced by a founder's certificate or bank acknowledgment, and it must be segregated as a separate patrimony.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety clearance issued by the Benemérito Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá before commencement of operations; municipal structural and dangerous-use permit from the District Municipality; occupational health and safety workplace clearance under Law No. 4 of 1989 and COPASIT Resolution No. 302-2003; for buildings over 500 square meters, an approved fire-protection engineering plan.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Autoridad Nacional de Ingresos Públicos (ANIP) — the merged tax and customs authority (successor to the Dirección General de Ingresos, DGI); official e-portal: https://www.anip.gob.pa (RUC enrollment, e-invoicing, and customs single window).

E-government filing portal

A Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) governed by Law 25 of 1995 obtains its RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) — the single statutory TIN used for both corporate income tax (ISR) and ITBMS (VAT) — by filing the tax registration form for juridical entities (Formulario DGI-01) with ANIP, accompanied by: (i) certified copy of the foundation charter (acta constitutiva) registered with the Registro Público de Panamá; (ii) certificate of legal existence and registered agent; (iii) declaration of fiscal domicile (Domicilio Fiscal) in Panama; and (iv) copy of the resident representative's/board's ID. Foreign entities without a Panama permanent establishment are not required to obtain a RUC, but a PIF with Panama-source business or export-manufacturing activity must register; a foreign principal must appoint a Panama resident fiscal representative to enroll. The ANIP portal issues the RUC certificate within approximately 5–10 business days after completeness review.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama has no US-style check-the-box or formal hybrid-mismatch election. A PIF is an opaque, entity-level taxpayer on Panama-source income only; the available administrative elections are: (i) election to be treated as an ITBMS 'exportador' to access zero-rating and refunds; (ii) election of the simplified small-taxpayer ITBMS regime (2% gross-receipts method) where annual turnover does not exceed B/. 50,000; (iii) election of quarterly ISR prepayment under the normal 25% regime; and (iv) for a foreign-owned PIF, an explicit territorial-scope claim excluding foreign-source income and foreign assets from the Panamanian tax base, supported by the foundation charter.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 10,000 (annual gross taxable turnover in Balboas/USD) triggering mandatory ITBMS registration; a simplified small-taxpayer regime applies up to B/. 50,000 annual turnover; exporters must register regardless of turnover to validate zero-rated export sales and recover input credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% ITBMS applies if (1) the exporter holds an active RUC and is enrolled for ITBMS as exporter; (2) the goods (color Doppler ultrasound consoles) are physically and definitively exported from Panamanian territory; (3) the export is supported by an accepted Single Export Declaration (DUE) in the name of the PIF exporter; (4) the sale is made to a non-resident purchaser; and (5) export proceeds are received in convertible foreign currency (USD acceptable), evidenced within the 180-day administrative verification window. Services: 0% applies to services performed for a non-resident beneficiary and effectively consumed outside Panama; input ITBMS paid on local purchases may be offset or claimed as a periodic refund (devolución de saldos a favor) irrespective of the small-taxpayer simplification.

  • Declaración Única de Exportación (DUE) — accepted by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) via the SIGA single window
  • Factura Comercial (Commercial Invoice) issued by the PIF exporter referencing RUC and DUE
  • Conocimiento de Embarque (Bill of Lading) or Air Waybill evidencing international carriage of the ultrasound consoles
  • Lista de Empaque (Packing List)
  • Certificado de Remesa de Divisas / SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds
  • Certificado de Origen (if claiming a preferential tariff under Panama's trade agreements)
  • Health/technical export authorization (MINSA or ANA import-export registry) for the medical device, as applicable

Mandatory electronic invoicing through ANIP's Fiscal Clearance Platform (Facturación Electrónica). Invoices are transmitted to ANIP in real time or by batch; ANIP validates, stamps a sequential authorization number, and returns the invoice bearing a digital fiscal seal and two-dimensional QR code. B2B transactions run on the express/tacit acceptance model, and credit notes are likewise cleared. Export sales must reference the customs export declaration (DUE) transmitted through the SIGA customs single window; print of fiscal-equivalent invoices is discontinued for enrolled taxpayers.

ANIP may jointly verify export destinations and cargo routing with ANA; if the export trail is defective or the goods re-enter Panama without lawful re-import declaration, ANIP retroactively reverses the zero rating, claws back input credits, and assesses the 7% (or applicable higher) ITBMS on the gross invoice value, plus compounding interest and surcharge. Civil fines range from 100% up to 150% of the omitted tax; the RUC may be suspended and fiscal domicile closed; a false export claim also triggers 'defraudación tributaria' under Law 70 of 2019, carrying imprisonment, and the statute of limitations for reassessment extends from 5 years to 10 years in cases of fraud. Export documents must be retained for at least 5 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized jurisdiction. The US dollar is the sole legal tender under Law 84 of 1946, while the Panamanian balboa is a nominal 1:1 accounting unit with no issuing institution and no physical paper currency. There are no exchange controls, no capital-account restrictions, and no differentiation between current and capital account convertibility. Profit remittances and dividend distributions from foreign-source income are freely transferable via SWIFT without prior SBP approval. Panamanian-source profit distributions to non-residents may attract a 10% dividend withholding tax when the underlying income was taxed under the territorial system; cross-border remittance itself remains unrestricted. Transfers of cash exceeding USD10,000 entering or leaving Panama trigger reporting obligations under SBP Form 200 and Financial Analysis Unit (UAF) threshold rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. Panama imposes no mandatory conversion, no repatriation timeline, and no surrender quota for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in USD or in any convertible currency in a Panamanian or foreign bank account. The only obligations are documentary: the exporter must submit the export declaration through the VUCE (Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior) single-window platform, and the inward credit to the exporter's operational account must be supported by the commercial invoice, packing list, transport document (bill of lading or air waybill), and the bank's own KYC record of the underlying trade transaction.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no currency-issuing central bank. Monetary policy coordination is performed by the Consejo de Coordinación Financiera (Financial Coordination Council) chaired by the Minister of Economy and Finance. Prudential banking regulation and AML/CFT supervision rest exclusively with the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), while Banco Nacional de Panamá operates as the fiscal agent of the State and operator of the national clearing system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: LIMITED / SUBORDINATE (Low-Medium). Panama's Banking Law (Decreto Ley 9 de 1998, amended by Law 42 of 2001) reserves deposit-taking and payment account services to SBP-licensed institutions. Foreign fintech EMIs (e.g., UK FCA, Lithuanian, or Hong Kong licensed e-money institutions) may not lawfully hold Panamanian client deposits or provide primary settlement accounts to a Panamanian foundation. Their viable role is restricted to complementary payment facilitation such as virtual IBAN issuance and outgoing transfer origination via APIs, provided the funds are routed through a sponsor account at an SBP-licensed tier-1 bank. The Law 5 of 2022 Fintech Hub sandbox allows authorised innovation testing but does not supersede the vigorous SBP deposit-taking monopoly. For the PA_PIF treasury structure, the primary account must remain at a licensed Panamanian bank to ensure LC operability, clean audit trail, and territorial tax integrity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound USD capital injections route to the PA_PIF operational account at a Panamanian tier-1 bank via SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer, settled over the Panamanian bank's US correspondent (CHIPS or Fedwire). Recommended ISO 20022 external purpose code: 'EQUI' for equity/capital injection into the foundation; use 'CINT' for intercompany loans. The wire reference must include the foundation's Panamanian fiscal identifier (RUC) and the full account name to avoid funds-blocking. Supporting verification for amounts above USD250,000: capitalisation resolution of the Foundation Council, source-of-funds statement of the injecting entity, and confirmation of the beneficiary in the RBDF registry. No exchange control approval or SBP pre-authorization is required; funds typically clear in 1-3 business days.Outbound settlements for the medical device trade are executed through two primary rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for open-account TT Wire payments, citing the commercial invoice and purchase order in the narrative; and (b) irrevocable documentary commercial letters of credit issued by the Panamanian bank under UCP 600 (MT700/MT707), payable at sight and settled by MT103 cover or MT202COV for interbank USD concentration. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.10,000,000 (Balboa) in annual gross revenue, or where related-party cross-border transactions exceed B/.5,000,000 in the fiscal year. Obligated taxpayers must prepare, update and file a Transfer Pricing Study (Local File). A Master File and CbC Report are required for PIFs that form part of a multinational group with consolidated annual revenue equal to or greater than EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Law 70 of 2019, as amended by the Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), applies to Panamanian entities engaged in relevant activities. Manufacturing and export of color Doppler ultrasound diagnostic consoles under ISIC 3250 is considered a distribution and service center activity; therefore the PIF must demonstrate economic substance in Panama including: (i) holding of board or foundation council meetings in Panama; (ii) strategic management exercised in Panama; (iii) adequate physical premises, employees and expenditures proportionate to the activity; and (iv) filing of an annual ESR return report with the DGI. Failure to satisfy ESR results in fines from B/.5,000 to B/.100,000 per fiscal year and can lead to an order to strike the foundation off the Public Registry for repeat breaches.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Restitution of the evaded tax plus 10% surcharge and 1% monthly interest; administrative fines up to 100% of the tax evaded; criminal prosecution under the Panamanian Fiscal Code for defraudación fiscal may carry imprisonment from 2 to 10 years depending on the amount evaded.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members, legal representatives, and signatories who participated in or failed to prevent the fraud are jointly and severally liable for the tax debt. Upon criminal conviction, personal assets can be confiscated under the Criminal Assets Confiscation Law; the court may impose a travel ban, and non-resident directors are subject to extradition procedures where applicable.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the ESR Law, non-compliance with economic substance requirements attracts fines from B/.5,000 to B/.50,000 for the first offence and up to B/.100,000 per year for repeat non-compliance. Continuous failure can result in administrative dissolution and permanent disqualification of the registered agent.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who certify false ESR filings are personally liable for the resulting penalties. They may also be deemed to have committed a criminal false-statement offence and can be subject to imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, with visas and resident permits cancelled, and civil liability for damages to creditors.
AML/CFT Violation

Statutory sanction: Panamanian Anti-Money Laundering Law (Law 23 of 2015) imposes fines up to B/.1,000,000 for institutions and individuals who breach AML duties. Money laundering predicate offences for international commercial fraud can lead to 5 to 15 years of imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Foundation Council members may be personally fined and criminally prosecuted if they knowingly execute transfers from proceeds derived from unlawful activity. Directors convicted of money laundering face extended prison terms, mandatory asset forfeiture, a prohibition on holding corporate office, and deportation if the convict is not a Panamanian national.
Fraudulent Filing of the Annual Return

Statutory sanction: DGI may reassess the full tax, apply a penalty of up to 300% of the underpaid tax in cases of serious misrepresentation, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution. The refusal to provide records can result in seizure of assets and suspension of the PIF's tax identification number.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and resident agent who sign the fraudulent return are personally liable for restitution of the damage caused. Panamanian courts have pierced the corporate veil of PIFs in such cases, ordering directors to transfer assets back to the foundation and imposing a travel ban pending resolution of the case.
