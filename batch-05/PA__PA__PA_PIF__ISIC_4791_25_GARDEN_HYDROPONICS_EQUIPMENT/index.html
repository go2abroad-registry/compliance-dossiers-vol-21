Governing statute

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; official e-services portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa (SIE – Sistema de Facturación Electrónica and RUC module)

E-government filing portal

A foreign settlor or resident agent must (1) register the Private Interest Foundation (PIF) with the Registro Público de Panamá under Ley 25 de 1995; (2) obtain the Aviso de Operación (business notice) and RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) via the DGI portal, submitting the foundation charter, Registered Agent license proof, copy of identity documents, and the Aviso de Operación issued by the municipality; (3) activate the DGI e-Tax account and obtain the fiscal digital certificate for electronic invoicing. No local residency or economic substance is required for the PIF, but the local Registered Agent is mandatory.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style pass-through election exists under Panamanian law. The PIF is a hybrid legal person: settlor retains control unless the charter provides otherwise, and assets/income are segregated from the founder's patrimony. For CRS/FATCA classification the PIF may elect its status as a passive NFE or investment entity, affecting reporting only, not taxation. The foundation charter (Pacto Constitutivo) can elect the distribution mechanism (interest/participation) and the governing law, but territorial taxation applies regardless. No hybrid mismatch election is available under Panama's domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.0 – no ITBMS registration threshold; any person habitually engaged in taxable commerce inside Panama must register for RUC and ITBMS. A small taxpayer simplified regime applies for annual gross turnover up to B/.36,000, where a fixed quarterly quota replaces the 7% general rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods (including B2C online retail shipments of hydroponics and gardening equipment sold by the Panamanian PIF to foreign buyers) qualify for the 0% ITBMS rate when title passes to a non-resident and the goods physically leave the Panamanian customs territory. Sales of services rendered to non-residents for use entirely abroad are also zero-rated. The PIF must maintain evidence that proceeds derive from foreign buyers and that the goods left Panama within the statutory export window; e-commerce marketplaces (Stripe/PayPal) settlement records must be retained.

  • Panama Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) submitted via SIGA/VUPA portal, referencing the SIE export invoice
  • Commercial invoice issued through SIE with 0% ITBMS and the export validation code
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from Panamanian territory
  • Packing list and cargo manifest
  • Inward remittance certificate or payment settlement report from the payment processor (Stripe/PayPal) confirming funds received from abroad

Mandatory nationwide e-invoicing per Law 75 de 2019 and DGI Resolution 230-3446 de 2019. The PIF must issue XML e-invoices through the DGI'SIE platform or an authorized Electronic Invoicing Service Provider. Each invoice receives a real-time DGI digital acknowledgment and a validation code (Código de Verificación) for the transaction; the customs module cross-references export declarations. Full API integration is required for marketplaces/payment rails, including Stripe and PayPal. The SIE is also used for issuing zero-rated export invoices and the 'Comprobante de Exportación'.

The DGI cross-validates SIE e-invoices against the customs SIGA/VUPA database; unjustified zero-rating triggers retroactive ITBMS assessment at 7% plus monthly 10% late-payment recargo (Código Fiscal), fines ranging from 10% to 300% of the evaded tax, and clawback of any input tax credits claimed. False export documentation constitutes fiscal and customs fraud under Ley 45 de 2023, carrying criminal penalties and joint liability of the Registered Agent and PIF directors.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama applies full convertibility on both current account and capital account. There is no central-bank authorization, no investment-registration gate, and no surrender or repatriation permission required for capital injections, profits, dividends, or Private Interest Foundation (PIF) distributions; the only practical compliance gates are AML/CFT documentation and FATF travel-rule data on wire transfers. Under territorial taxation, Panama-source income earned by a local operating e-commerce entity is taxable in Panama (25% corporate income tax, 7% ITBMS value-added tax where applicable, and 10% dividend withholding on distributions of taxed Panama-source profits), while foreign-source income held or earned through the PIF is exempt. Non-resident beneficiaries receiving PIF distributions from foreign-source assets carry no Panama tax liability, and USD/PAB conversion is absolute at 1:1 with no remittance hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no export-proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no statutory timeframe for repatriating export receipts. The PIF and its trading subsidiary may lawfully retain 100% of export and e-commerce proceeds in USD, or convert and hold them in any designated bank inside or outside Panama. For AML and audit defense, the commercial trail (commercial invoices, packing lists, Panamanian customs export declarations, and payment-processor settlement statements) should be retained for at least five years.

Central bank supervision: Panama operates without a traditional central bank or domestic currency-issuing authority. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the prudential regulator with oversight of banking and foreign-exchange operations; Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the fiscal agent of the State; and the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) coordinates financial policy. The monetary system is fully dollarized: the US dollar is legal tender and the Balboa (PAB) circulates only as coins at a fixed 1:1 parity, so de facto monetary conditions are anchored by the monetary policy of the US Federal Reserve.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium overall viability with a mandatory structural workaround for Stripe. PayPal fully supports Panama-domiciled business accounts and serves as the primary local e-commerce payment rail. Stripe does not support direct onboarding of a Panama-domiciled entity, so the PIF must establish a foreign-incorporated SPV (for example, a Delaware LLC incorporated through Stripe Atlas or local counsel) to hold the Stripe merchant account, with SPV profits distributed to the PIF via a documented capital and distribution trail. Offshore EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Mercury) are viable as complementary collection and vendor-payout rails, but Panamanian Tier-1 banks treat EMI inflows as heightened-risk and require underlying transaction evidence; some EMIs internally decline private-interest foundations. The SBP Fintech Hub sandbox does not yet provide a practical full EMI license for this business model; the recommended architecture is PayPal Panama plus Stripe through a US SPV plus a Tier-1 Panamanian USD master clearing account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from founders or external investors should be executed by SWIFT MT103 in USD with the remittance information (field 70) reading Capital contribution to Private Interest Foundation or aporte de capital, and with complete FATF travel-rule fields: field 50K for the ordering customer, field 57A for the intermediary bank where applicable, and field 59 for the beneficiary with the foundation's exact legal name and account number. No SBP or central-bank approval is required for the transfer; the bank's KYC/AML committee will request the registered foundation deed showing the endowment capital, a council resolution authorizing receipt, RUC certificates, and source-of-funds statements for wires above enhanced due-diligence thresholds (commonly USD 100,000 or at account opening). E-commerce operating proceeds should flow through PayPal and Stripe settlement sweeps into the same USD master operating account to avoid third-party funnel-account patterns that trigger compliance red flags.Supplier settlements for hydroponic hardware are executed through: (a) SWIFT MT103 for single foreign supplier payments and MT101 batch files for periodic multi-vendor cycles; (b) UCP600-compliant sight letters of credit issued through Banistmo or BAC for bulk procurement from China and Taiwan, with the required document set (commercial invoice, full-set bills of lading, packing list, certificate of origin) strictly matching LC terms; (c) standby letters of credit under ISP98 for critical contract vendors; and (d) domestic ACH and Fedwire clearing through the bank's US correspondent network for US-based couriers and third-party logistics providers. All USD flows clear through the US banking system (Fedwire/CHIPS), so OFAC/SDN screening and Panamanian sanctions enforcement apply automatically. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 3,000,000 (Balboa, pegged 1:1 to USD 3,000,000) of annual gross revenue: taxpayers at or above this threshold executing cross-border related-party transactions (e.g., purchase of hydroponics equipment from an affiliated supplier, management fees, or IP licensing) must prepare and file the Local File, Master File, and the annual transfer pricing informative return with the income tax return. Below-threshold taxpayers remain subject to the arm's length standard and must maintain contemporaneous records.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Requirements (Law 254 of 2021, effective fiscal year 2022) apply to all legal entities, including Private Interest Foundations, performing relevant activities. Online distribution of hydroponics and garden equipment constitutes a distribution and service center relevant activity, requiring: (i) annual sworn economic substance declaration filed with the DGI; (ii) core income-generating activities (management, procurement, inventory decisions) conducted in Panama; (iii) physical presence, local employees or outsourced arrangements with local substance, and complete accounting records maintained in Panama; (iv) adequate local expenditure proportional to income. A PIF lacking substance is presumptively a shell entity and may lose territorial tax treatment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion (Criminal Code Art. 277-A): imprisonment of 2 to 5 years; fines up to 10 times the tax evaded; retroactive reassessment of expired periods upon proof of fraud; civil liability for unpaid tax, recargo, and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors, founder, and the resident agent who sign or cause the false return may be jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency, penalties, and defense costs; criminal imprisonment and judicial travel bans may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to B/. 1,000,000; classification as a shell entity allowing the DGI/ULAF to disregard separate legal personality; cancellation of foundation registration; retroactive taxation of foreign-source income; inclusion on international blacklists.

Director personal liability: Officers and the resident agent sustaining the shell structure face personal civil liability for tax assessments, closure orders, regulatory debarment, and potential money laundering charges with 5-12 years' imprisonment where funds disguise illicit origin.
Transfer Pricing / Related-Party Misstatement

Statutory sanction: DGI recharacterization of pricing with upward taxable income adjustment; penalty of 5% to 15% of the under-declared tax difference plus deficiency interest; failure to submit transfer pricing documentation triggers separate fines up to B/. 50,000 per omission.

Director personal liability: Approving officers may be held jointly liable for the transfer pricing adjustment, fines, and, if intentional, tax evasion sanctions including personal bankruptcy, director disqualification, and criminal prosecution.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Transparency

Statutory sanction: AML fines under Law 23 of 2015 and UBO registry penalties up to B/. 50,000 per violation; criminal money laundering under Criminal Code Art. 254 carries 5-12 years' imprisonment and asset seizure; professional facilitators face aggravated penalties; jurisdiction-level FATF/EU grey-listing consequences may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors, the resident agent, and the guardian (Protector) face personal criminal liability for money laundering of 5-12 years' imprisonment, fines, travel bans, asset freeze orders, disqualification from office, and civil restitution claims; professional advisors who fail to report suspicious transactions are also exposed.
