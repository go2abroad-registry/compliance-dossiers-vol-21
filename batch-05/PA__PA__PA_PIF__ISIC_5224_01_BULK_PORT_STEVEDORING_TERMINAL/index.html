Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; electronic portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa) and the ATENEA virtual taxpayer office.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation (PIF) obtains the statutory Unified Taxpayer Registry number (Registro Único de Contribuyente – RUC) as follows: (1) notarize and public-register the foundation charter before the Registro Público de Panamá, appointing a Panamanian resident agent and statutory officers, with the minimum foundation capital evidenced; (2) request the RUC through the DGI Sede Electrónica/ATENEA, filing the certified registry plate (ficha registral), foundation deed, statutory officers' identification, and proof of the resident agent's appointment; (3) if the PIF will render Panamanian-source taxable services, i.e., port stevedoring, simultaneously enroll for ITBMS as a 'sujeto pasivo' and as an 'agente retenedor', and obtain the municipal patent (Aviso de Operación) from the relevant municipality; (4) if the foundation has no Panamanian-source income and remains non-operative, no RUC is mandatory, but DGI practice requires a formal status election and a registered-agent certificate to evidence exemption; (5) after issuance of the RUC digital certificate, activate the electronic-signature certificate and e-invoicing profile in the DGI fiscal-clearance platform before issuing tax-compliant invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not maintain a U.S.-style check-the-box regime, partnership classification, or hybrid-mismatch election. The operative elections for a PIF engaged in stevedoring are: (i) the election under Article 25 of Law 25 of 1995 to exercise commercial activities inside Panama, which brings the foundation's Panamanian-source business income within DGI jurisdiction while preserving exemption for foreign-source income; (ii) the small-taxpayer election (Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente) for annual gross turnover not exceeding B/.36,000, substituting a 2% quarterly tax for the ordinary ITBMS and advance-payment regime; (iii) DGI-authorized elections between accrual and cash accounting for ITBMS and income-tax purposes; (iv) optional conclusive-payment agreements with the DGI; and (v) a non-operative/foreign-source status election, keeping the PIF outside the Panamanian tax net (no RUC, no ITBMS), provided no services or assets are realized within Panama. A separate election to 'opt in' to entity-level taxation is unnecessary: entity-level territorial taxation is the default rule.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary threshold for ITBMS registration: any person habitually transferring taxable goods or rendering taxable services in Panama must enroll with the RUC. The only de minimis is the small-taxpayer regime for annual gross turnover not exceeding B/.36,000 (Balboas/USD 36,000), which pays a 2% substitute tax; the bulk-port stevedoring terminal, by scale, exceeds this de minimis and is therefore a mandatory VAT registrant and withholding agent.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% ITBMS rate applies to qualifying exports of goods and services. For bulk-port stevedoring, the service must: (a) be supplied to a non-resident principal (shipowner, charterer, or recognized foreign cargo/forwarding agent) under a written contract; (b) relate to a vessel engaged in international navigation, with cargo loaded or unloaded at a Panamanian terminal for transportation between foreign ports; (c) be effectively consumed outside Panama; (d) be declared to the DGI under the exporter's registration (Registro de Exportador de Servicios); and (e) be evidenced by the foreign-currency inward remittance. Qualifying services may be invoiced at 0% with a full input-VAT credit or reclaim right; non-qualifying domestic stevedoring or cabotage services are subject to the 7% rate.

  • Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) electronic export declaration (Declaración Única de Exportación – DUEX) validated through the VUCEM/SIGA foreign-trade single window, identifying the terminal, cargo, vessel, and customs regime
  • Commercial stevedoring invoice issued by the PIF, marked '0% Export – ITBMS' and bearing the DGI validation/QR code
  • Original bill of lading (or multimodal bill of lading / sea waybill) showing the vessel, Panamanian loading/discharge port, foreign destination, cargo description, and tonnage
  • Port authority or Panama Canal departure/tally certificates and the terminal operator's signed stevedoring service report (work order, equipment, and gang-hour records)
  • Inward remittance certificate (Comprobante de Ingreso de Divisas) or SWIFT MT103 / bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the export consideration within 180 days
  • DGI exporter registration certificate and the foreign recipient's corporate/tax identification and address

Real-time electronic invoicing ('Facturación Electrónica') is mandatory for active taxpayers. The DGI requires structured XML/JSON invoices transmitted via the Sede Electrónica web services/API or an accredited e-invoicing operator immediately upon issuance, signed with an approved digital-signature certificate, and carrying the DGI validation code (código de validación) printed as a QR code; physical fiscal printers are being phased out. Port terminal operators must issue an e-invoice per stevedoring service, serialize by terminal contract, and maintain an electronic audit log; the DGI performs automated fiscal clearance, rejecting non-compliant invoices in real time.

The DGI cross-matches DUEX filings, port-authority vessel call data, e-invoice fiscal logs, and bank foreign-currency receipts. Any zero-rated claim that fails substantiation triggers immediate refund clawback plus default interest and fines under the fiscal penalty provisions of the Código Fiscal (as amended); repeated or fraudulent claims may lead to cancellation of exporter registration and criminal prosecution under the tax-criminal regime (Law 70 of 2019, as amended), with fines of up to B/.50,000 and imprisonment for aggravated evasion; officers and the foundation are jointly liable for reversed credits and sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized, open-economy regime with absolute convertibility between the balboa and the US dollar. Current-account transactions (trade in stevedoring and marine terminal services, bunkering, payroll, canal tolls, subcontractor dispatches) and capital-account transactions (foundation capital endowment from the founder, capital increases, intercompany loans, beneficiary distributions, profit remittances) are entirely free from exchange-control licensing, prior authorization, or central-bank surrender obligations. Profit remittance hurdles are limited exclusively to bank-level anti-money-laundering source-of-funds verification under Law 23 of 2015; there are no fiscal approval hurdles, no withholding taxes on payments by a Panamanian private interest foundation to foreign beneficiaries under territorial taxation, and no convertibility restrictions. The practical complexity is the documentation burden: port-terminal revenues in USD follow standard 'customer due diligence' protocols, and any perception of mismatch between the foundation's stated purpose and its actual commercial activity will trigger enhanced due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists. Export proceeds and port-terminal fees earned by the stevedoring and bulk cargo handling operation may be retained 100% in USD in domestic or offshore accounts. Panama imposes neither a requirement to repatriate within a fixed period nor an obligation to convert into balboas, and no quantitative limits apply to foreign-currency holding. The only compliance overlay is the UAF's hard-cash reporting threshold of USD 10,000 (or its equivalent) per transaction or series of related transactions, requiring a Suspicious Transaction Report in accordance with Law 23 of 2015 and SBP agreements; electronic USD wires above this threshold must carry an accurate reference and underlying contract file to survive the bank's OFAC/UAF screening.

Central bank supervision: Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the Republic's fiscal agent, settlement institution, and provider of treasury services, but Panama has no issuing central bank and no traditional monetary authority; the banking supervisor is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). The monetary system is fully dollarized: United States banknotes are unlimited legal tender, the Panamanian balboa (PAB) is a merely nominal unit of account issued exclusively as US-equivalent coinage at a fixed 1:1 parity, and there is no national central-bank balance sheet for FX intervention. Foreign exchange policy and financial-intelligence matters fall respectively to the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) and the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a standalone treasury rail. Under SBP regulations, a foreign fintech or off-shore Electronic Money Institution (EMI) that does not hold a Panamanian banking or e-money license may not open a domestic clearing account and may not present itself as a bank; its utility is confined to non-repatriating, low-value cross-border payables (e.g., supplier disbursements to niche service providers) where the EMI operates as a payment instruction channel. A UK/EU-licensed EMI can lawfully execute MT103-equivalent transfers on behalf of the PIF without a Panamanian license, provided it does not 'carry on banking activities' in Panama and the liquidity never touches the Panamanian port-authority clearing system. The portal cannot replace a Tier-1 USD correspondent account for the stevedoring terminal because port concession agreements (Autoridad Marítima de Panamá), canal invoicing, and syndicated equipment LCs require a regulated Panamanian bank. Viability: Low for primary treasury, Medium for satellite cross-border payments above the USD 5,000 per wire threshold. Legal constraint: the EMI must complete the PIF's beneficial-ownership attestation and cannot rely on foundation confidentiality or nominee arrangements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Sole compliant rail is a SWIFT MT103 wire (or MT202 COV for aggregation) from the founder's or foreign corporate parent's account into the PIF's designated USD account at Banco Nacional de Panamá, Banco General, or Towerbank. Settlement is cleared via the receiving bank's US correspondent (Fedwire/CHIPS) and automatically credited in USD, requiring no conversion because Panama is dollarized. The SWIFT field 70 (remittance information) must read 'Capital Contribution to Private Interest Foundation [Name], registered under Law 25 of 1995 — ISIC 5224 stevedoring and port terminal operation', for FATCA/CRS consistency. The bank's KYC unit will internally map the transaction to the foundation's capital account, apply the Purpose Code 'P101' (equity contribution / replenishment of capital) on the Panamanian electronic-transfer record, and retain a notarized Council Resolution authorizing the inflow. All injections above USD 50,000 require the source-of-funds file to be pre-uploaded in the bank's secure onboarding portal; funds held in the Capital Escrow Account cannot be commingled with operating revenue before the Port Authority's commercial go-live certificate is verified.Port Authority clearing and domestic cascos use Panama's local clearing house (TEF / ACH) for payments to the Autoridad Marítima de Panamá, port landlord fees, towage, mooring, stevedore subcontractors and payroll. Cross-border operational disbursements—stevedore equipment purchases, spare parts, crane rentals, marine insurance, international subcontracting—are executed via SWIFT MT103 direct wires. For high-value capital equipment and engine supplies, Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) are the preferred clearing rail, often advised through Banistmo or BAC International, subject to UCP600. All outbound transfers above USD 10,000 are reported to the UAF through the bank's transactional form, and beneficiary names must match exactly the invoice/contract screens. For disbursements to US ports or liners, MT103 settled over CHIPS/Fedwire with the Panama PIF as originator; for European/Asian terminals, the same MT103 rail sends USD through the bank's chain but may use a cover payment (MT202 COV). Under SBP regulations, a foreign fintech or off-shore Electronic Money Institution (EMI) that does not hold a Panamanian banking or e-money license may not open a domestic clearing account and may not present itself as a bank; its utility is confined to non-repatriating, low-value cross-border payables (e.g., supplier disbursements to niche service providers) where the EMI operates as a payment instruction channel. A UK/EU-licensed EMI can lawfully execute MT103-equivalent transfers on behalf of the PIF without a Panamanian license, provided it does not 'carry on banking activities' in Panama and the liquidity never touches the Panamanian port-authority clearing system. The portal cannot replace a Tier-1 USD correspondent account for the stevedoring terminal because port concession agreements (Autoridad Marítima de Panamá), canal invoicing, and syndicated equipment LCs require a regulated Panamanian bank. Viability: Low for primary treasury, Medium for satellite cross-border payments above the USD 5,000 per wire threshold. Legal constraint: the EMI must complete the PIF's beneficial-ownership attestation and cannot rely on foundation confidentiality or nominee arrangements.
