Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá — Sección de Fundaciones de Interés Privado (Public Registry of Panama, Private Interest Foundations Section).

Governing statute

Law No. 25 of 12 June 1995 (Private Interest Foundations), as amended by Law No. 13 of 2008 and Law No. 52 of 2013; specifically Articles 2, 5, 18, 25 and 32 on formation, endowment, Foundation Council and registered agent.

Formation / constitutional filings

Escritura Constitutiva de Fundación de Interés Privado (Foundation Charter), executed before a Panamanian Notary Public and inscribed in the Public Registry.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 16 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by Panamanian law; the Foundation must declare an initial endowment of at least one asset or economic right, commonly US$100 or a single participating share. No escrow account is required for this private interest foundation vehicle.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office must obtain a current fire safety compliance certificate from the Benemérito Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá (Panama Fire Department). Structural load and occupancy safety are evidenced by the municipal Certificate of Use and Occupancy issued by the Municipality of Panama; if employees are engaged, the Foundation must also satisfy the occupational health and hygiene obligations under Panamanian labor and social security law.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by Panamanian law; the Foundation must declare an initial endowment of at least one asset or economic right, commonly US$100 or a single participating share. No escrow account is required for this private interest foundation vehicle.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The initial Board (Foundation Council), being duly constituted pursuant to Law 25, adopts the following resolutions. First, Endowment Subscription: the Foundation accepts the irrevocable endowment declared in the Foundation Charter and confirms that the minimum endowment requirement is satisfied. Second, Registered Office: the registered office of the Foundation is fixed at [Registered Address], Panama City, for the receipt of all legal and administrative notices. Third, Officers: the Council appoints [Name] as Chairperson, [Name] as Secretary, and [Name] as Treasurer, with full authority to open bank accounts, execute software service agreements, engage the registered agent, and represent the Foundation before the Public Registry and tax authorities. This Resolution is binding and effective immediately.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — ISIC 6201 / NAICS 541511 corresponds to a non-physical software development and data analytics service; no industrial licensing authority exercises direct competence over this activity.

Category: Not applicable. No industrial operating or manufacturing permit is required. A municipal Aviso de Operación may be obtained only if the Foundation elects to conduct active commercial billing operations in Panama.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) — Panama's General Directorate of Revenue; official portal: SERTRACEN (https://sertracen.dgi.gob.pa) and the DGI e-Invoicing/SAE platform (https://www.dgi.gob.pa).

E-government filing portal

A Panama Private Interest Foundation (PIF) without Panamanian-source activity is not obliged to hold a RUC; to commercialize the SEO analytics SaaS, the foundation first registers its charter in the Registro Público, appoints the resident agent/fiscal attorney, then files the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) application with the DGI via SERTRACEN, attaching the foundation deed, public inscription certificate, identification of Foundation Council members, fiscal address confirmation, and a sworn economic-activity declaration; the ITBMS taxpayer registration is issued together with the RUC within 3-10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope — the PIF is taxed as a separate juridical person solely on Panamanian-source income under the territorial system; foreign-source revenue of the foundation stays outside Panama's income tax; no pass-through treatment is recognized for PIFs; tax-exempt zone treatment applies only if the PIF establishes operations in a registered free zone (e.g., SEM Panama Pacifico) and obtains zone authorization.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or equivalent classification election is available in Panamanian law; a PIF may, however, structure its actual business conduct to remain a non-commercial Law 25 foundation (no Panamanian-source profits and therefore no income tax or ITBMS obligation), or elect admitted ITBMS taxable-person status to operate a zero-rated digital export business; hybrid-mismatch and transparent-entity elections are not statutorily recognized.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. (balboa at parity with USD): no minimum turnover threshold; registration is mandatory as soon as the PIF habitually carries out taxable transfers or renders services in Panamanian territory; the simplified small-taxpayer regime is available for annual gross income up to B/. 200,000; non-resident digital service providers are registrable without local turnover under the remote digital services rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Panamanian customs territory and services rendered to non-resident customers — with performance, use, or enjoyment occurring abroad — qualify for the 0% ITBMS rate; the MarTech SEO content analytics SaaS qualifies when the subscriber is a foreign entity, the payment originates from outside Panama, and the analytics data is consumed abroad; the PIF must retain the executed SaaS agreement, non-resident customer documentation, and bank credit evidence; customary DGI practice expects the export consideration to be credited within 180 days of invoice date (not a positive-law deadline in Panama).

  • Panama Electronic Customs Export Declaration (SIEC) — or a sworn Service Export Statement where digital delivery involves no physical goods movement
  • Commercial invoice and completed SaaS subscription/analytics agreement with the non-resident customer
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, where physical goods or media are exported
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice from the overseas collection account evidencing receipt of the export proceeds
  • Beneficial ownership and end-customer register of the PIF demonstrating absence of Panamanian-domiciled users

Mandatory electronic invoicing and centralized fiscal clearance through the DGI's SAE platform: each ITBMS invoice must be transmitted as a digitally signed XML document in real time to the DGI before delivery, must pass automated validation, and must contain the DGI-issued Sello Fiscal/QR clearance code; export invoices are additionally linked to the SIEC customs single-window; failures to clear invoices in advance trigger administrative fines, blocked input-credit claims, and presumption of non-issuance.

The DGI's export-audit unit verifies SIEC declarations, foundation banking records, and the foreign-destination nexus; if zero-rating is reversed, the full 10% ITBMS becomes payable with statutory interest computed from the invoice date; under Law 76 of 2019 tax-procedure rules, penalties range from 50% to 300% of the tax evaded plus administrative fines up to B/. 50,000; knowingly false export declarations expose the Foundation Council to criminal tax-fraud prosecution and clawback of input ITBMS credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates under a fully dollarised monetary system. The US dollar is legal tender and freely circulated alongside the on-demand fixed-balboa coinage. There are no exchange controls, and both current and capital account convertibility are unrestricted. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and capital repatriation can be effected without FX approval or mandatory conversion. The sole practical hurdles are Panamanian withholding tax obligations on certain payments, documentary substantiation of underlying transactions, and AML/KYC verification standards imposed by the SBP-regulated banking system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios in Panama. Exporters may retain foreign exchange earnings in any currency, with no prescribed timeline for conversion or repatriation. The absence of a surrender burden is supported by the free use of the US dollar. Reporting duties under AML/CFT rules, such as declaration of cash and electronic transfers above thresholds, remain applicable to exporters and their financial institutions.

Central bank supervision: Banco Nacional de Panamá (National Bank of Panama) serves as the government's fiscal agent; the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the primary banking and foreign exchange regulator. Panama does not operate a traditional central bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating for offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., Mercury, Airwallex, Payoneer) is Low for the PA_PIF because Panamanian law treats money transmission and payment services as banking activities requiring an SBP licence and local presence. Unlicensed offshore EMIs typically reject private interest foundations due to heightened overseas KYC friction and correspondent-bank constraints. Domestically licensed fintech payment providers operating under the SBP framework may achieve a Medium rating and can support cross-border clearing via BaaS arrangements with tier-1 banks, but they do not replace a physical banking relationship for foundation treasury management.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque ownership arising from nominee foundation members and bearer shares

Root cause: Opaque ownership arising from nominee foundation members and bearer shares

Defense evidence: Complete foundation charter with registration deed, updated council member register, notarised and apostilled identification of all beneficial owners, explicit written waiver of bearer instrument privileges, and a signed statement confirming the source and purpose of funds.
Ambiguous source of wealth or high-risk jurisdiction exposure

Root cause: Ambiguous source of wealth or high-risk jurisdiction exposure

Defense evidence: Bank statements covering a substantive lookback period (12–36 months), audited financial statements of the foundation, tax returns, commercial contracts evidencing legitimate MarTech/SaaS revenue streams, and a narrative of wealth origination supported by external counsel or an independent wealth-management advisor.
Mismatch between declared business model and expected transaction flows

Root cause: Mismatch between declared business model and expected transaction flows

Defense evidence: Detailed business plan describing the SEO content analytics platform, subscription/client contracts, third-party payment processor statements (Stripe/Paddle), a projected transaction profile with expected counterparties and settlement percentages, and supporting licensing/exemption confirmations typical for low-risk software activities.
Sanctions, PEP status, or adverse media screening flags

Root cause: Sanctions, PEP status, or adverse media screening flags

Defense evidence: Clear, tracible corporate genealogy and ultimate beneficial ownership map, OFAC/EU/UN sanctions screening report from a licensed provider, comprehensive adverse-media search results, and, where applicable, a qualified compliance opinion or clarifying legal memorandum addressing any PEP association.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the PIF's USD-denominated account at a Panamanian tier-1 bank. The wire must clearly specify the correct purpose code (e.g., 'Capital contribution' or 'Transfers under Article 13 of Foundation Charter') and identify the foundation's registered name and account number. To prevent rejection, the originator should provide a notarised board resolution recording the capital contribution, the foundation's charter excerpt, and a source-of-funds questionnaire. The Panamanian bank will verify the wiring instructions and, where the transfer exceeds regulatory thresholds, file an electronic money-laundering report.
