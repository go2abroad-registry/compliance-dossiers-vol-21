Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; taxpayer portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Interest Foundation must appoint a resident legal representative and register for the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) at DGI. The procedure requires submission of the foundation charter (protocolized and notarized), resident agent certificate, registered address, and identification of the legal representative. If the foundation performs no Panama-source taxable activity, no income tax filing duty arises; however, any ITBMS-taxable export transaction requires a RUC and electronic invoicing capacity before invoicing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 0 — no de minimis registration threshold; any habitual taxable transfer of goods or provision of services in Panama triggers compulsory ITBMS registration. A simplified small-taxpayer regime is available for annual gross receipts up to B/. 200,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for 0% ITBMS where the RPG dice are physically exported from Panama to a foreign purchaser, clear Panamanian customs, and are not consumed, resold, or modified within Panama. Services may qualify as zero-rated offshore services only if rendered for non-resident recipients and used exclusively outside Panama. Evidence of collection in foreign currency and foreign destination is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) formally lodged with Panama Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign banking credit evidence showing payment by the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • E-export invoice authorized by DGI with the zero-rated export marker
  • Transport contract and customs seal / exit certificate issued by the customs authority

Mandatory electronic invoicing for active RUC holders. Export invoices must be issued through DGI-approved software and transmitted to DGI's Sede Electrónica via API before delivery; each invoice receives a DGI fiscal authorization code and a CSV verification/QR stamp. A zero-rated export transaction must be explicitly marked as 'Exportación' and linked to the corresponding customs export declaration.

DGI and Panama Customs may cross-match export declarations, ITBMS e-invoices, transport documents, and incoming bank remittances. Failure to remit or document foreign payment within the 180-day period causes reversal of the 0% rate, immediate reassessment at 7% ITBMS, plus interest, late-payment surcharges, and fines. Fraudulent zero-rating claims, fictitious exports, or forged customs documentation expose the foundation and its signatories to tax evasion penalties and criminal liability under the Panamanian Tax Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Convertibility is complete and unrestricted. Panama maintains no foreign-exchange control law, no capital-account approval system, and no currency rationing. Current-account flows from the PIF's online sale of handmade epoxy-resin RPG dice through Kickstarter, Stripe, and PayPal may be received, retained, and remitted abroad in USD or any freely convertible currency. Capital-account movements, including the settlement of the Foundation's initial patrimony, distributions to beneficiaries, and dissolution surpluses, are likewise free of central-bank clearance. Under the territorial-source tax doctrine preserved and modernized by Law 254 of 2024, extraterritorial income generated by sales perfected outside Panama to non-resident customers through foreign payment platforms is not subject to Panamanian income tax; consequently no profit-remittance tax, dividend withholding, or repatriation authorization applies to foreign-source proceeds. Practical constraints are limited to bank-level KYC/AML verification, not monetary regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline. Export receipts from the dice business may remain indefinitely in USD or any other freely chosen currency in the PIF's accounts. The applicable regime is AML notification rather than surrender: banks must record cash transactions above USD 10,000, preserve wire-transfer records for at least five years, and report suspicious or unusually aggregated movements to the UAF in accordance with Law 23 of 2015 and SBP Agreement 3 of 2015. Retaining the crowdfunding settlement reports, trade contracts, and shipping documentation fully satisfies these obligations without converting any funds.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama operates without a central bank empowered to issue national currency; the United States Dollar circulates as legal tender under Law 84 of 1904, while the Balboa exists only as a fractional coin. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), established by Decreto Ley No. 9 of 1998, is the exclusive banking regulator and supervisor. The Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF), created by Law 23 of 2015, exercises AML/CFT supervision and acts as the financial intelligence unit. The Banco Nacional de Panamá serves as fiscal agent of the Republic but performs no monetary-policy function; no Panamanian authority issues banknotes or grants foreign-exchange authorizations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 1,500,000 annual gross revenue of the Panamanian entity triggers mandatory transfer pricing documentation (Local File and contemporaneous TP study); groups with consolidated revenues above USD 50,000,000 must additionally prepare a Master File and, when applicable, Country-by-Country Reporting.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) require any entity engaged in 'relevant activities'—including IP holding, distribution and service centres, financing, leasing, or headquarters—to demonstrate proportionate staff, premises and effective management in Panama. A PIF holding only foreign financial assets and not conducting Panamanian commercial activity may fall within the 'pure equity holding entity' exemption; an active dice-casting or IP-licensing operation in Panama must satisfy ESR reporting and substance evidence to avoid reclassification as a shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Civil reassessment with a 100%-200% fraud penalty, default interest, and criminal conviction carrying imprisonment and fines under the Panama Penal Tax Regime; the foundation's bank accounts can be seized.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign or cause false declarations are jointly and severally liable for the omitted taxes, may be prohibited from leaving Panama, and face confinement of up to eight years.
Shell Foundation and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Panama may impose ESR penalties (monthly fines for non-compliance), strike the foundation from the Registro Público, and deny all tax benefits; the DGI may recharacterise the foundation as a sham and tax the beneficial owner directly.

Director personal liability: Civil courts may pierce the foundation veil against directors and officers, order restitution of distributed assets to creditors, impose travel bans and disqualification from serving as director in any Panamanian entity.
Money Laundering / AML Offences

Statutory sanction: Under Panama Law No. 23 of 2015, money laundering carries imprisonment of 5 to 12 years and fines up to USD 5 million; convicted assets are forfeited and the foundation's license is revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors, trustees and the beneficial owner can be detained pre-trial, have personal and corporate assets frozen, be subjected to travel bans and surrender of passports, and face extradition to the predicate-crime jurisdiction.
Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines from USD 500 up to USD 50,000, recurring penalties for continued non-compliance, and temporary suspension of the foundation's rights to distribute assets until filing is complete.

Director personal liability: Directors and the compliance officer are personally liable for false filings, may be fined and disqualified, and may face criminal punishment if the omission is used to circumvent AML controls.
