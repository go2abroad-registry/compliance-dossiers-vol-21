Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Secretariat General of Taxation, Sultanate of Oman; official e-portal: taxoman.gov.om

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) must first register with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion to obtain the Commercial Registration certificate, then apply online via the taxoman.gov.om portal for a Tax Registration Number (TIN). The application requires the Commercial Registration certificate, constitutional documents, Oman address evidence, and identity documents of the shareholder and manager. The SPC is then registered for income tax, VAT, and employer withholding obligations. A VAT registration certificate is issued separately where the taxable turnover threshold is met or exceeded.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) must first register with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion to obtain the Commercial Registration certificate, then apply online via the taxoman.gov.om portal for a Tax Registration Number (TIN). The application requires the Commercial Registration certificate, constitutional documents, Oman address evidence, and identity documents of the shareholder and manager. The SPC is then registered for income tax, VAT, and employer withholding obligations. A VAT registration certificate is issued separately where the taxable turnover threshold is met or exceeded.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Oman has no general check-the-box flow-through election for SPCs. An SPC is taxed at entity level as a limited liability company. Hybrid entities may be subject to specific treatment under the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting standards with respect to double taxation and treaty relief, but no domestic statutory hybrid mismatch election or check-the-box regime exists in Oman.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 500,000 annual taxable supplies (higher threshold for businesses with annual taxable supplies of OMR 500,000 or less may choose voluntary registration subject to conditions; mandatory registration applies at OMR 500,000 and above for resident persons and certain non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Oman).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of goods outside the GCC common VAT area and to certain international transportation and related services. For a manufacturer of surgical laparoscopy towers classified under ISIC 3250, the export of physical medical devices overseas is zero-rated provided that the supplier retains evidence of export within the prescribed timeframes, the goods leave Oman and are consigned to a non-GCC customer, and the SPC holds a valid VAT registration. Direct export sales are zero-rated; explicit documentary evidence of export is required for the VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document equivalent or Oman Customs declaration)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing dispatch from Oman
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Bank Credit Advice showing foreign currency payment from outside Oman
  • Certificate of Origin
  • Sultanate of Oman Customs Export Certificate

Oman operates a structured, clearance-based electronic invoicing framework under the VAT Department. Persons registered for VAT must issue e-invoices compliant with the Oman VAT E-invoicing specifications, transmit invoice data in near real time through certified service providers or authorised tax technology providers, and use cryptographic digital stamping where required. Existing national digital stamping mandates for excise and goods controls are supplemented by VAT-specific e-invoicing clearance protocols and document-level integrity controls. The phased implementation requires API-based system-to-system integration for high-risk and large taxpayers, with timestamps and validation tokens assigned to each invoice.

The Oman Tax Authority may scrutinise export zero-rating claims through post-clearance audits and refusal of refunds where documentation is incomplete or the export timeline is not met. Refunds improperly claimed are clawed back, and the SPC is liable for VAT at 5% plus a late payment penalty for each tax period involved. Fraudulent claims or deliberate document falsification invoke tax evasion penalties, fines up to OMR 5,000 or more per invoice and imprisonment of up to three years, in addition to publication of penalty decisions. Where the 180-day foreign currency repatriation deadline is missed without an approved waiver, the export may be recharacterised as a domestic supply, with VAT, late payment penalties, and tax evasion penalties applied.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current account transactions under IMF Article VIII obligations. Oman maintains a pegged exchange rate regime of OMR 0.3845 per USD with a narrow band. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment capital injections, registered trade finance, and remittance of profits by 100% foreign-owned entities, are permitted under the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2019) and CBO regulations. Profit remittance requires audited financial statements, board resolution, proof of settled Omani tax and social insurance obligations, and certification by a licensed public accountant. There are no systemic exchange control approvals for bona fide trade-related settlements, but commercial banks apply know-your-customer, source-of-funds, and ultimate-beneficial-owner due diligence. Endoscopy hardware manufacturing and related service imports/exports are treated as ordinary goods and services, thus eligible for full current account convertibility without remittance caps, provided the underlying transactions are supported by commercial contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and shipping documents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose a mandatory export proceeds surrender requirement or any statutory percentage conversion obligation into Omani Rial. Exporters are permitted to retain foreign currency proceeds in onshore accounts or receive funds in offshore collection accounts, subject to CBO circular requirements on documentation and reporting. CBO Directive and the banking law framework require that export proceeds be repatriated within a commercially reasonable period, generally up to 180 days from the date of export or as stipulated in the underlying letter of credit/contract; banks may require an extension letter for longer open-account terms. There is no compulsory sale or conversion ratio; the exporter may hold, use, or convert foreign currency balances in accordance with prevailing exchange rate regulations and internal compliance policies of the remitting bank. All export collection entries must be properly coded under CBO/IMF BPM6 purpose codes, and the corporate must maintain underlying customs export declarations for AML/CFT verification.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) in coordination with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion and the Capital Market Authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and payment institutions have restricted viability for core treasury operations in Oman. The CBO requires any entity conducting payment services, money transfer, or digital wallet activity in Oman to obtain a CBO license or operate through a licensed bank/authorized payment services provider. Offshore EMIs are not permitted to open payment accounts on behalf of an Omani SPC without an Omani banking partner. They may be used narrowly for supplementary foreign-currency vendor payments or employee travel cards if licensed in their home jurisdiction and compliant with FATF standards; however, primary import settlement, LC opening, export collection, and tax escrow accounts must be held with CBO-licensed commercial banks. Legal constraints under the Omani Electronic Transactions Law and CBO AML/CFT guidelines require data localization for customer files; therefore, the treasury cannot rely on an unlicensed non-resident EMI as the principal settlement rail. Feasibility rating: Low-Medium (limited use only as auxiliary card provider, not as custody/settlement account).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the ultimate shareholder's or parent company's bank account through an international correspondent clearing network to the Oman SPC's designated operational bank account at a CBO-licensed bank. The remittance must be denominated in USD or OMR; USD transfers are settled through the US clearing system and converted to OMR at prevailing CBO-driven rates. The bank will require the beneficiary account name to exactly match the Commercial Registration certificate and the beneficiary 'SPC' to reflect 'Oman Single Person Company' or 'Single Person Company LLC' as registered. The wire should include a clear purpose code aligned with CBO balance-of-payments statistics: principal FDI equity contribution (category 'direct investment – equity capital' under BPM6), intercompany loan (category 'direct investment – intercompany debt'), or advance against future exports (category 'trade advances'). Supporting documentation before opening the account comprises the executed share-capital subscription statement, capital adequacy confirmation if the CBO/ROC regime applies (no minimum legal capital for an SPC unless licensed activities require it), source-of-funds declaration, and the Commercial Registration certificate. All capital injection funds must be credited to the account as 'capital inflow' and the bank will issue a Foreign Investment Registration Certificate; the company must record the foreign investment with the Ministry of Commerce via the investment portal. Verification steps include SWIFT UETR tracking, remitter–beneficiary name matching, and anti-money-laundering screening of the remitter's bank SWIFT/BIC against sanctions and PEP databases. After clearance, the bank will provide a crediting advice with the CBO-mandated balance-of-payments purpose code, which must be retained for tax, audit, and repatriation evidence.
