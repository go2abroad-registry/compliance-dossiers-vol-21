Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Single Person Company; however, share capital must be stated in the MOA, subscribed for by the sole shareholder, and paid into an Omani bank account. No public escrow account is required for an SPC, although the Ministry may require a suitable capital or bank guarantee where a freight forwarding activity is subject to MTCIT licensing.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and evacuation plan must be issued and inspected by the Civil Defense – Royal Oman Police; the registered office must pass a structural load inspection confirming safe floor load for records and sample containers; occupational safety clearance for the workplace is issued by the Ministry of Labour after a site inspection under the Occupational Safety and Health Rules.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Single Person Company; however, share capital must be stated in the MOA, subscribed for by the sole shareholder, and paid into an Omani bank account. No public escrow account is required for an SPC, although the Ministry may require a suitable capital or bank guarantee where a freight forwarding activity is subject to MTCIT licensing.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), Ministry of Finance; unified national e-portal: https://tax.gov.om; VAT e-services via https://taxportal.tax.gov.om

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Omani SPC, the entity must first obtain a Commercial Registration (CR) from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry & Investment Promotion. Following CR issuance, the entity must register for Corporate Tax and VAT through the OTA Tax Portal by submitting the CR certificate, constitutional documents, financial projections, and beneficial owner identification. The TIN/VAT number is issued electronically after verification; non-resident foreign entities without local presence may apply directly and must appoint a VAT tax representative when making taxable supplies in Oman.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Oman does not operate a check-the-box election regime. Corporate income tax is assessed at entity level for the SPC. For VAT, a resident taxable person may elect to form a VAT tax group with related persons under Article 16 of the VAT Law, subject to OTA approval. No hybrid mismatch elections are available under Omani tax legislation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 38,500 annual taxable supplies for Omani resident entities; non-resident entities making taxable supplies in Oman are required to register regardless of threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported outside Oman and the GCC are zero-rated when the supplier retains sufficient documentary evidence of export. Freight forwarding and NVOCC services directly connected to goods exported outside Oman, or supplied to a non-resident recipient, qualify for zero-rating if the place-of-supply rules and supporting shipment documentation are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent banking settlement proof)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Contract / Freight Forwarding Instruction
  • Proof of freight payment and clearance from Oman Customs (Bayan / Risk Management)

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing or digital stamping regime has been activated as of 2025. Tax invoices may be issued in paper or electronic format and must include all VAT law particulars. The OTA has announced a future 'Muwafaq' e-invoicing framework, but API-based fiscal clearance is not yet obligatory for freight forwarding or NVOCC operations.

Under the Omani VAT Law, zero-rating claims lacking adequate export evidence are subject to output VAT reassessment, late-payment penalties, and clawback of any input VAT recovered on the related costs. Penalties include an administrative fine of up to OMR 50,000 and, for fraudulent or sham export transactions, imprisonment of up to three years. Forged or falsified customs documentation may also trigger anti-money-laundering and criminal finance penalties under Central Bank of Oman regulations.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current account transactions, and Oman operates a de jure liberal regime for capital account transactions connected to foreign direct investment. Since the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree No. 50/2019 implementing the Foreign Investment Law regime under the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion), a 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) may lawfully establish an NVOCC freight forwarding operation without an Omani partner. Dividend and profit remittances are permitted freely through CBO-licensed commercial banks, subject only to standard AML/KYC verification, audited financial statements or tax clearance evidence, and CBO balance-of-payments statistical reporting. The OMR is pegged at approximately 1 OMR = USD 2.6008, so currency convertibility risk is low, although banks apply a commercial spread.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory Omani obligation to surrender export service proceeds to the Central Bank, and no mandatory percentage of export receipts must be converted into OMR. However, export proceeds, including fees earned from ocean freight forwarding, NVOCC brokerage, and ancillary logistics services, must be repatriated through a licensed Omani commercial banking channel and must be traceable to the underlying contract, bill of lading, house bill, or freight invoice. CBO banking supervision and anti-money-laundering rules require the SPC to maintain documentary evidence of the commercial transaction for at least five years, and the remitting bank must report each transaction for balance-of-payments aggregation purposes. Late repatriation without commercial justification creates an AML red flag and may trigger a CBO inquiry.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary banking rail. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Airwallex, Wise, etc.) do not hold a CBO banking license, do not operate on the Omani local clearing system, and cannot provide an OMR IBAN for domestic operational collections. They may be used only as an ancillary foreign-currency concentration or disbursement tool for overseas agent receipts and carrier payments, subject to preserving full Omani corporate tax substance and AML traceability. Any EMI that solicits payment services from Oman residents must itself be licensed or authorized by the CBO under the payment services regulatory framework; otherwise the arrangement creates a legal, tax and sanctions-compliance exposure.

Defense evidence: Share subscription agreement, foreign parent bank statements for the prior six months, audited or management accounts of the parent, remittance bank SWIFT records, tax residence certificate, and a formal statement of source of wealth demonstrating commercial profitability rather than crypto or third-party pooling.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity should be wired from the foreign shareholder's corporate bank account to the Oman SPC's operational bank account via SWIFT MT103 with field 70 identifying 'FDI Equity Subscription / Share Capital under Foreign Capital Investment Law — OM_SPC Commercial Registration [CR number]'. The bank will verify the source of funds, the share subscription document, and the identity of the remitter. Oman does not generally require prior CBO approval for a foreign equity contribution to a permitted FDI activity, but the bank must submit and retain the remittance under CBO balance-of-payments reporting. Where capital is contributed in foreign currency, conversion to OMR or retention in a foreign-currency account is elective; the official peg of OMR to USD applies, and the relevant rate is the bank's daily spot rate.Outbound payments for ocean freight forwarding and NVOCC operations are primarily executed by SWIFT MT103 out of the SPC's local account to overseas carriers, feeder operators, container depots, port terminals, and overseas agents. For interbank funding or simultaneous cover, MT202 covers the underlying bank-to-bank leg. Local OMR disbursements to Omani ports, customs agents, and domestic truckers should be made through the Oman Electronic Clearing System (OECS), which in practice is reached via the bank's ACH Direct channel. Commercial letters of credit are uncommon in NVOCC freight brokerage but may be used for vessel chartering, container guarantees, or large cross-border terminal handling contracts; standby LCs are occasionally required by shipping lines or port operators. All disbursements must be supported by the original invoice, evidence of service performance, tax invoice or withholding-tax certificate if Omani VAT applies, and CBO-compliant trade-reason codes: e.g., 'payment for ocean freight services', 'container handling charges', 'port charges', or 'agency commission'.Plain-vanilla OTC forward contracts (USD/OMR and cross-currency pairs) executed with a CBO-licensed bank under an ISDA/FX trade confirmation frameworkFX swaps between USD and OMR, USD and EUR, or USD and UAE dirham for treasury liquidity managementCross-currency interest rate swaps for any long-term foreign-currency financing used by the SPCBank-quoted currency options with credit-line approval, including plain put/call and zero-cost collarsAnti-Netting and close-out netting arrangements under an ISDA Master Agreement subject to Omani legal opinionOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not available or permissible as the primary hedging rail for OMR exposure; any offshore NDF must be excluded from the onshore SPC accounting records or ring-fenced in a fully documented foreign-currency account and disclosed to the Omani bank

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 1,000,000 annual gross revenue, or aggregate controlled transaction value above OMR 100,000 in a tax year; such taxpayers must prepare and maintain a Master File, Local File and arm's-length benchmarking documentation.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not yet apply a standalone ESR law to NVOCCs; however, the tax anti-abuse and commercial-law tests require the SPC to be real: registered office, operational staff, Omani bank account, board/manager decisions made in Oman and audited accounts. A captive forwarding vehicle that books paper profits only with no local decision-making will be treated as artificial, may lose tax residency and treaty protection, and can be recharacterised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Fine of 50%-300% of the tax evaded, interest on deferred tax, cancellation of the entity's tax card and commercial licence, and criminal referral to the Oman Public Prosecution.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil where the SPC is used as a mere instrumentality: directors and managers are jointly liable for restored tax, face travel bans, asset freezes and imprisonment for up to five years.
Transfer Pricing / Artificial Shell Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Oman Tax Authority may recharacterise transactions, impute the arm's-length profit, deny intercompany deductions, assess additional tax at 15%, impose administrative penalties and interest, and initiate compulsory strike-off under the Commercial Companies Law.

Director personal liability: Where the SPC is a sham, the corporate veil is pierced: directors and shareholders may be personally assessed for the unpaid corporate tax, banned from managing Omani companies, subjected to travel restrictions and exposed to confiscation orders.
AML and Trade Sanctions

Statutory sanction: Fines under Oman's AML Law (Royal Decree 30/2016 as amended), confiscation of tainted cargo and bank balances, suspension of Swift/Wire and ACH payment rails, and criminal prosecution of the undertaking.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers, directors and beneficial owners face personal criminal fines, imprisonment up to ten years, asset confiscation, entry bans and, for expatriates, cancellation of Oman residence and driving permits.
