Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is mandated for a Single Person Company under Royal Decree No. 18/2019. The sole shareholder must state the subscribed share capital in the Articles of Association; the capital must be fully paid before or immediately after registration and evidenced by a bank certificate. No escrow deposit is required for ordinary plush-toy manufacturing, unless the chosen activity is separately regulated.

Funding & unlock path: The facility must obtain a Civil Defense and Ambulance Authority (CDAA) fire safety clearance before operation, including approved fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and electrical safety certification. Structural load approval for the storage and finished-goods area, municipal building inspection, and a Ministry of Labour workplace environmental clearance are also required before production begins.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is mandated for a Single Person Company under Royal Decree No. 18/2019. The sole shareholder must state the subscribed share capital in the Articles of Association; the capital must be fully paid before or immediately after registration and evidenced by a bank certificate. No escrow deposit is required for ordinary plush-toy manufacturing, unless the chosen activity is separately regulated.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) – e-services portal: https://taxa.gov.om (formerly Secretariat General of Taxation; integrated within the Ministry of Finance for administrative purposes)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) must first complete Commercial Registration (CR) with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion. The SPC then applies through the OTA e-services portal for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) using the CR certificate, Foreign Investment Licence (where applicable), constitutional documents, audited/pending incorporation details, director/resident manager details, and registered address. The TIN is issued prior to VAT registration if the annual gross taxable supplies threshold is met or anticipated.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) must first complete Commercial Registration (CR) with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion. The SPC then applies through the OTA e-services portal for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) using the CR certificate, Foreign Investment Licence (where applicable), constitutional documents, audited/pending incorporation details, director/resident manager details, and registered address. The TIN is issued prior to VAT registration if the annual gross taxable supplies threshold is met or anticipated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Oman does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election. An Omani SPC is compulsorily treated as a corporate tax resident under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree No. 28/2009, as amended). No fiscal unity/group consolidation election is available for separate legal entities. No hybrid or reverse-hybrid entity classification election is currently prescribed; the entity's civil-law personality determines tax treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 38,500 (approximately USD 100,000) of annual taxable supplies; voluntary registration: OMR 19,250 (approximately USD 50,000). Once turnover threshold is exceeded, registration must be completed within 30 days.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to direct exports of goods physically delivered outside Oman (including to free zones and customs-excised destinations) provided the exporter is the supplier, the goods leave Oman within the statutory/administrative limit, and documentary proof of export is retained. The export must be evidenced by customs declaration, transport documentation, and payment/remittance records tracing the transaction to the foreign buyer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (single administrative document / manifest data) clearly showing the plush maternity supplies and destination
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Omani territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/TT advice proving foreign-currency settlement from the customer

Oman has phased mandatory electronic invoicing (B2B and B2G). The OTA requires approved e-invoicing systems and structured formats, but does not currently operate a continuous transaction-control clearance (real-time API fiscal stamping) obligation for all taxpayers. Exporters must however issue valid tax invoices containing the VAT registration number and, when exporting, retain underlying customs and shipment records for possible digital audit.

The OTA may conduct post-shipment audits requiring reconciliation of physical export, customs clearance, freight movement, and foreign currency inflow within a 180-day window. Falsely claiming zero-rating or failing to provide the export bundle triggers retrospective VAT recovery at 5% on the invoice value, late payment interest at the statutory rate, administrative penalty ranges (typically OMR 500–25,000 per invoice), and in fraud cases criminal penalties including imprisonment. The exporter bears the evidential burden; insufficient documentary linkage leads to assessment and clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current account transactions, and capital account convertibility is substantially open for direct investment flows. Oman is an IMF Article VIII member and does not impose exchange controls on cross-border current transfers. Profit and dividend repatriation by a foreign-owned SPC is freely permitted without CBO pre-approval, subject to bank-level due diligence, audited financial statements, and absence of tax or legal encumbrances. Withholding tax on dividend distributions is zero under the Unified Tax Law applied in Oman. There is no mandatory conversion of foreign currency into OMR and no restriction on maintaining concurrent OMR and foreign-currency accounts, making the regime comparable to fully liberalized GCC jurisdictions. The only procedural friction is the standard bank requirement to evidence the commercial or investment purpose of each significant remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no mandatory surrender requirement, statutory conversion ratio, or minimum repatriation timeline on export proceeds. The SPC may freely retain USD, EUR, or CNH receivables in foreign-currency accounts held with licensed Omani banks. However, all exports must be reported through the Oman Customs 'Bayaan' single-window platform and the National Centre for Statistics and Information (NCSI). The SPC must maintain underlying shipping documentation (commercial invoice, packing list, air waybill or bill of lading, certificate of origin) for a minimum of five years for bank and tax audit verification. Exports of plush mascot toys under HS 9503.00 are not subject to any export license, and CBO does not require pre-export declaration beyond normal customs clearance.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), established under the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000) as amended, and acting as the sole regulatory authority for commercial banking, exchange houses, payment systems, and the national clearing infrastructure. CBO administers Oman's fixed exchange rate regime and issues circulars and AML/CFT directives that bind all licensed financial institutions in the Sultanate.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for primary treasury routing; Medium as an ancillary collections or disbursement rail. Foreign digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Mercury) are not licensed by CBO and cannot issue OMR IBANs or open resident accounts in Oman. CBO-published rules require all payments in OMR and, in practice, most material USD flows affecting an Omani commercial entity to settle through a licensed Omani bank or an authorized exchange house. Use of a foreign EMI is legally possible for supplier payments, but creates three compliance frictions: (i) Omani banks may reject the beneficiary's inbound SWIFT-related advice when 'EMI' appears as the ordering institution; (ii) the SPC's auditor will require additional substantiation since EMI statements lack a SWIFT BIC and are not bank statements under IFRS audit standards; and (iii) CBO AML examinations will trigger enhanced due diligence if more than 10-15% of monthly treasury volume flows through unlicensed non-bank rails. Recommended architecture: keep a licensed Omani bank as the sole treasury anchor and restrict the external EMI to processing platform collections from small marketplace buyers with a monthly de minimis cap below USD 10,000.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound FDI capital for the SPC must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the parent's corporate bank in the originating jurisdiction (e.g., China, UAE, or Hong Kong) directly to the SPC's operational account at a licensed Omani bank. The remittance must include the following compliance reference string in Field 70: 'SHARE CAPITAL INJECTION OM_SPC CR NO [insert Commercial Registration number]'. The purpose must map to the IMF BPM6 code DI4 (Direct Investment - Equity Capital) or the equivalent Omani bank internal purpose code. The receiving Omani bank will verify the subscription agreement, board resolution, MoCIIP CR certificate, and the notarized Memorandum of Association before crediting funds. If the account is newly opened, a prior account-receivable letter from the bank's head-office compliance unit is mandatory. No CBO approval is required, but all inbound capital amounts exceeding OMR 10,000 equivalent must be declared via the bank's electronic transaction reporting system to CBO.Primary clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 for open-account supplier settlements, with the SPC's Omani bank acting as the instructing party. The Field 70 must contain the invoice number, SI/Bill of Lading reference, and the HS code of the underlying goods. For higher-value raw-material purchases exceeding USD 50,000, a commercial documentary Letter of Credit (MT700) issued by Bank Muscat or HSBC Bank Oman is the recommended instrument, drawn on the Chinese or South-East Asian supplier under Incoterms FOB or EXW, with beneficiary presentation of the commercial invoice, clean-on-board bill of lading, packing list, certificate of origin, and inspection certificate where required. For low-value consignments under USD 10,000, CBO's Electronic Clearing Settlement and Transfers system enables same-day settlement. A standby LC or bank guarantee is not required for the plush-toy OEM segment. Single transactions greater than USD 100,000 must be supported by a fully executed purchase contract retained in the bank's trade-finance file for post-facto audit.Onshore Deliverable FX Forward (USD/OMR): available from licensed banks' treasury desks; given the USD peg, the forward curve is tightly pegged but usable for term-pricing certainty on known USD receivables.Cross-Currency Swap (USD/CNH or USD/EUR): permitted for matching currency cash flows on supplier and buyer legs; available in tenors up to 24 months subject to CBO credit-risk exposures on the counterparty bank.Foreign Exchange Swap (Spot-Forward Combination): commonly used to convert seasonal USD receipts into OMR for working capital while committing to reconvert on a future date without creating a net open position.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) for OMR/INR or OMR/PHP: available for labor-cost hedging where underlying wage/contractual obligations are denominated in Indian Rupees or Philippine Pesos; must be evidenced by an underlying commercial contract and settled via the licensed bank's treasury desk, not an exchange house.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 500,000 aggregate related-party transactions in the tax year triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation; a TP disclosure form must be attached to the annual CIT return.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not have a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code like the UAE, but the new Income Tax Law imposes a general substance-over-form rule and a comprehensive anti-avoidance provision. An SPC engaged in plush toy manufacturing must maintain real economic substance in Oman—office/plant premises, full-time employees, and production activity—otherwise the Tax Authority may disregard its existence, strip it of treaty benefits, and reallocate profits to the non-resident owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: A fine of OMR 10,000–OMR 50,000 (or 100%–300% of the evaded tax, whichever is greater) and imprisonment up to 5 years, plus reassessment of omitted CIT and daily late-payment surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or authorize the false return are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax. A court may issue a travel ban, freeze personal assets, order civil restitution, and sentence directors to prison.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits, disallowance of deductible expenses, recharacterization of profit at the parent level, and revocation of the commercial registration under the Commercial Companies Law.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced. Directors and the sole shareholder (SPC member) are personally liable for the understated tax and may face travel bans and asset attachment through civil execution.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment with addition of taxable income, a 10% additional tax on the adjustment, 1% monthly late-payment penalty, and a fine of OMR 5,000 for missing TP documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly set or approve manipulated prices are personally responsible for the additional tax if the company defaults; they can also incur accountancy malpractice fines and extended travel restrictions.
Anti-Money Laundering / Financial Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: A fine between OMR 50,000 and OMR 100,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years under Oman’s AML Law (Royal Decree 30/2016 as amended). The license may be revoked and all funds in the SPC account frozen.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are individually criminally liable, subject to confiscation of personal assets, passport cancellation and travel bans, and strict personal accountability for the source of funds.
