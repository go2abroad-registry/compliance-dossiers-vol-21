Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for a Single Person Company under Royal Decree 18/2019; the sole shareholder must declare the entire share capital in the constitutive documents, which may be subscribed in Omani Rial or a foreign currency at the prevailing exchange rate. The subscribed capital must be fully paid within five years from incorporation, with no separate escrow account obligation for ordinary trading activities.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The Tax Authority of the Sultanate of Oman, operating under the Ministry of Finance; official e-services portal: taxoman.gov.om. It is the sole national administration for corporate income tax, value added tax, and withholding tax, with no sub-national tax divisions.

E-government filing portal

Following MOCIIP incorporation of OM_SPC as a Single Person Company (Commercial Registration certificate) and licensing under ISIC 4669 for wholesale of granular sulphur mineral ore, the foreign shareholder must: (1) open a corporate bank account in Oman; (2) log on to taxoman.gov.om and submit the corporate income tax registration for the resident SPC to obtain the unified Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN); (3) file a VAT registration application on the same portal — compulsory once taxable supplies/imports exceed OMR 500,000 in any 12-month period and electable from OMR 100,000 — using the CR and TIN references; (4) activate the e-invoicing profile and obtain the Authority's digital invoice credentials for zero-rated export B2B invoices; and (5) appoint a tax representative or file a non-resident withholding registration only if a foreign principal, distinct from the SPC, separately derives Oman-source income without a permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Omani law; entity classification follows the Commercial Companies Law and the Income Tax Law. Contractually available elections include: (i) voluntary VAT registration at the OMR 100,000 annual turnover threshold; (ii) application for VAT tax-group treatment for closely related resident entities; (iii) the statutory small-business income tax concession, where the SPC's ownership, payroll and turnover satisfy the published thresholds; (iv) application for an advance pricing agreement for cross-border related-party brokerage margins; and (v) the designated free-zone VAT treatment election, to the extent the SPC supplies goods into licensed Omani economic zones.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 500,000 of taxable supplies and imports in any 12-month period. Voluntary registration: OMR 100,000 of taxable supplies or taxable expenses in any 12-month period, provided the entity is established in Oman or makes taxable supplies in Oman.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies to supplies of goods (including granular elemental sulphur, HS 2503) that leave Oman for a recipient outside Oman and the GCC within the statutory period, in the same or substantially the same condition, with the exporter proving export by approved customs records, bill of lading, and commercial delivery terms. Directly connected export facilitation services (ocean freight, port handling, export brokerage) supplied as part of the export chain to the overseas buyer are zero-rated where they are supplied to enable the physical export. Goods supplied to licensed designated zones and international transport of passengers or goods may also attract zero-rating under the VAT Executive Regulations.

  • Customs export declaration (Bayan) bearing the Royal Oman Police Customs exit stamp
  • Bill of Lading (negotiable or electronic) evidencing loading of the bulk sulphur cargo at an Omani port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT/MT103 credit advice evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and cargo tally sheet / packing list
  • Zero-rated tax e-invoice referencing the Bayan ID and bill of lading number
  • Port authority or stevedore's export certificate and mate's receipt
  • Letter of Credit or SWIFT LC documentation, where the payment rail is a letter of credit
  • Certificate of origin, if required by the importing jurisdiction

Oman is implementing a phased mandatory e-invoicing regime on the Taxoman E-Services portal. Businesses above the mandatory VAT registration threshold must issue structured electronic tax invoices (XML/JSON with authenticity, integrity and legibility controls), transmit invoice datasets through the Tax Authority's e-invoice API and clearance module before the published phase deadlines, and maintain the full e-invoice audit file. National real-time fiscal clearance is not yet comprehensive for all live invoices; however, export B2B invoices must be digitally signed, cross-referenced to the customs Bayan export declaration, and made available for machine-readable verification by the Tax Authority and customs authorities.

The Tax Authority cross-matches VAT returns against customs Bayan exit records, bill-of-lading data, and bank remittance systems. If export or payment evidence is not retained for the statutory period, the Tax Authority retrospectively re-characterizes the zero-rated supply as taxable, issuing an assessment for 5% output VAT plus late-payment sanctions. Fraudulent documentation or knowingly false zero-rating returns trigger the VAT Law's penalty matrix, including administrative fines for record-keeping and invoice violations, escalated criminal fines and imprisonment where evasion is prosecuted, exclusion from refund fast-track treatment, and retrospective clawback of any input VAT refunds already paid.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR, ISO 4217) is fully convertible for current international transactions and Oman complies with IMF Article VIII obligations since 1973. The currency is hard-pegged to the US Dollar (USD 1 = OMR 0.3845 buying / OMR 0.3850 selling; OMR 1 = USD 2.6008), which effectively eliminates T+2 settlement forex risk for USD-denominated sulfur trade. Capital account convertibility is liberal: 100% foreign ownership of the SPC is permitted, and profit repatriation, dividend distribution, shareholder loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds may be transferred freely without CBO pre-approval, subject only to standard AML/CFT verification, the submission of audited financial statements, clearance of corporate tax liabilities from the Oman Tax Authority (formerly the Secretariat General of Taxation), and compliance with the CBO Balance of Payments Reporting Directive. In practice, dividend remittances require a certified board resolution, a tax clearance certificate, and an approved audited profit figure. There are no dual exchange-rate tiers and no import surcharge or advance import deposit requirements. The OMR remained pegged throughout regional shocks; the main structural hurdle is the scarce availability of deep OMR forward liquidity beyond 12 months for exotic currency pairs, but USD-OMR conversion is unconstrained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio to OMR. Exporters of granular elemental sulfur and other mineral ores are permitted to retain foreign currency receipts in USD/EUR Accounts of Export Proceeds at licensed Omani banks without CBO approval. However, the CBO's anti-money laundering and reporting framework requires every export receipt to be traceable to a legitimate customs declaration (Bayan single-window system), a commercial invoice, a bill of lading, and, for letter-of-credit collections, the SWIFT MT700 documentary credit and settlement messages. Commercial practice imposes a 180-day documentary realization window for export financing eligibility; if proceeds are not collected within that period, the bank may raise a qualitative reporting flag to CBO but does not compel enforced surrender or penalty. Receipts may be converted to OMR at the bank's prevailing market rate, and the SPC must file a monthly BOP-7 export proceeds declaration with the relationship bank to support CBO statistical monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) / Al-Markaz al-Watani lil-Masrif (established under the Banking Law of 1974, Royal Decree No. 114/2000 as amended, and the Financial Institutions Law, Royal Decree No. 37/2021).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW as a primary settlement rail and MEDIUM only as an ancillary foreign-exchange conversion channel. Legal constraints: the Central Bank of Oman does not license pure e-money institutions (EMIs) for holding OMR-denominated corporate transaction accounts; any payment service provider collecting deposits or undertaking remittances in Oman must hold a CBO license under Law No. 37/2021 and the Payment Systems Regulation. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut, and UAE-based fintechs without CBO branch licenses) cannot connect to the Oman RTGS/gravity clearing system, cannot issue a local IBAN for purposes of CBO reporting, and will not be accepted by Omani commercial banks as conclusive proof of trade settlement for LC-backed sulfur transactions. The SPC may use an offshore EMI exclusively for small incidental disbursements (surveyor fees, insurance premia below USD 10,000, sub-agent commissions) but all trade proceeds and LC settlements must flow through a CBO-licensed Tier-1 Omani bank to satisfy customs, tax, and exchange-control auditability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 (with MT103 cover) from the UBO's or shareholder's account at a G7 or GCC-regulated bank to the SPC's Capital Escrow Account at the chosen Omani Tier-1 bank. The remittance reference field must state 'SPC-OM-SCM equity injection — [shareholder name] — ISIC 4669'; the SWIFT transaction type code should be 'G14' or, if the Omani bank requires a BOP code, 'G-14 Investment Equity' (as mapped in the CBO Balance of Payments manual). The correspondent bank's message must clearly identify the order customer and the beneficiary as OM_SPC with the Oman Commercial Registration number. Disbursement of the escrowed OMR balance is only permitted after the CBO reporting form is submitted, the bank receives the certified Commercial Register extract showing paid-up capital, and the on-boarding compliance unit releases a Funds Activation Certificate. Incoming USD is converted to OMR at the bank's spot rate on a T+1 basis; the SPC may simultaneously maintain a USD current account beside the OMR operational account for freight settlementsFor outward trade settlements, the preferred rail is the irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) issued under ICC UCP600 with Oman as the issuing jurisdiction and the beneficiary's bank as confirming bank; brokerage/tolling structures should opt for Transferable LC (MT720) or Back-to-Back LC instruments. Straight-forward payments for freight, insurance, port charges, and surveyor fees must be sent via SWIFT MT103 to the beneficiary's account, or MT202/202COV for interbank transfers, cleared in USD through the Omani bank's New York correspondent (CHIPS or Fedwire) and for OMR-denominated local taxes, through the Central Bank of Oman's Real-Time Gross Settlement system (OCSS-RTGS). The end-to-end LC workflow requires: (1) MT700 issuance; (2) MT710/MT720 negotiation of the shipping documents (B/L, certificate of origin, sulfur quality certificate); (3) MT754 advice of payment or acceptance; and (4) MT999/MT799 telex reimbursement for discrepancies. The SPC should select the CBO's 'Oman Integrated Payment System' for any OMR local counterparty settlements, and maintain a USD clearing ledger below the bank's Sanctions and Trade-Based Money Laundering transaction monitoring threshold.Plain-vanilla OMR/USD and OMR/EUR FX forwards on a spot-against-forward basis executed with CBO-licensed domestic banks (tenor up to 12 months; the OMR hard peg to USD at 0.3845 means USD/OMR forwards are quoted with near-zero premium).Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (FX swap line simultaneously exchanging spot USD for OMR and forward USD against OMR) to hedge the funding leg of the Trade Finance Facility.USD/EUR, USD/CNY and USD/JPY forwards used to hedge the purchase price of sulfur sourced from ex-USA, ex-Gulf, or ex-CIS suppliers when the invoice currency differs from the sale currency.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) with approved offshore counterparties on exotic converting currencies (e.g., African and South Asian imports) where the OMR leg is synthetic and settled in USD; a full NDF market for OMR itself does not exist due to the official peg.Commodity-linked price protection via costless collars or simple call/put options on ISDA agreements, permissible only with a CBO-licensed bank acting as principal, and subject to the SPC's risk-management mandate registered with the bank's Treasury Division.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Oman's Income Tax Law does not set a specific OMR threshold for mandatory transfer pricing documentation. However, the Oman Tax Authority may request contemporaneous evidence for any related-party transaction, and robust Local File/Master File documentation is strongly recommended when aggregate related-party turnover exceeds OMR 500,000.

Economic substance scope: Oman has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations as implemented in the UAE, but the Tax Authority applies anti-avoidance principles and the place-of-effective-management test. The sulfur-trading SPC must demonstrate genuine business presence in Oman: physical office, qualified staff, appropriate board minutes, local bank account, and operational control over trading transactions. The absence of such substance may lead to the recharacterization of profits as belonging to the foreign shareholder or to the creation of a foreign permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to three years and a fine of OMR 1,000 to OMR 5,000; in addition, 100% administrative penalty on the evaded tax and 7% annual interest on overdue tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false accounts are personally liable for unpaid tax, may be disqualified from management, and are subject to travel bans and asset attachment orders.
Shell Company Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Tax Authority may disregard the legal personality, recharacterize the SPC as a branch of the foreign parent, and reassess 15% tax on the full commercial profit, together with penalties of 25% to 100% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil when the SPC is a mere facade, exposing directors and the beneficial owner to unlimited liability for the company's tax obligations and civil restitution.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to OMR 200,000 and imprisonment up to seven years under Oman AML Law (Royal Decree 30/2016), with confiscation of the underlying cargo and shipping proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable if they knew or ought to have known of money-laundering or sanctions violations; they face travel restrictions, personal fines, and asset seizure.
Failure to File or Material Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of up to OMR 1,000; late payment interest of 7% per annum; under-declaration penalty of 25% to 100% of the additional tax assessed.

Director personal liability: If the company becomes insolvent and collection from the SPC fails, Oman's Companies Law permits the Tax Authority to recover unpaid tax personally from the directors who caused or allowed the non-filing or short-declaration.
