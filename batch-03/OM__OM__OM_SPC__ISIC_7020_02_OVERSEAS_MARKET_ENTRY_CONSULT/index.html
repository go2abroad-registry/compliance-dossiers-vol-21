Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) — official portal: https://tms.taxoman.gov.om (Tax Management System); the OTA is the successor to the Secretariat General for Taxation under the Ministry of Finance and administers income tax, VAT, and excise tax in the Sultanate of Oman.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) must first obtain a Commercial Registration (CR) certificate from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MCIIP). Thereafter, the SPC must register with the Oman Tax Authority through the Tax Management System (TMS) for income tax purposes, obtaining an Income Tax Registration Number. For VAT purposes, any resident person, including an SPC established in Oman, making taxable supplies in Oman must register for VAT through the OTA's TMS portal if the annual taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed the mandatory registration threshold of OMR 500,000; voluntary registration is permitted when taxable supplies exceed the voluntary threshold of OMR 50,000. The VAT registration application must be submitted electronically, accompanied by the CR certificate, Articles of Association, copy of the founders' and directors' passports, proof of registered address (EJARI or equivalent tenancy contract), and authorization letter appointing a tax agent where applicable. Upon approval, the OTA issues a VAT Registration Certificate displaying the VAT ID with a standard format. Where the SPC has no taxable supplies yet but plans future taxable activities, a pre-registration for tax residence may be required; the SPC must also notify the OTA within 60 days of any changes to CR details, directors, or registered address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Oman does not provide a 'check-the-box' classification election comparable to the United States. The SPC is statutorily classified as a resident juridical person taxable at entity level on its worldwide income. No partnership pass-through election is available for an Omani SPC. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized under Omani domestic tax law; Oman has not enacted CIT-specific hybrid mismatch rules and relies instead on general anti-abuse provisions. No group taxation or fiscal unity election is available. The only meaningful statutory elections available are the irrevocable VAT standard-rate voluntary registration election once the OMR 50,000 threshold is exceeded, and the option to apply the simplified accounting method for VAT if annual supplies do not exceed OMR 5,000,000, subject to OTA approval.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration threshold: OMR 500,000 in annual taxable supplies; voluntary registration threshold: OMR 50,000 in annual taxable supplies or annual taxable expenses. Both thresholds calculated on a 12-month look-forward or look-back basis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of goods is zero-rated when the exporter is a VAT-registered person, the goods are exported outside Oman either directly or under the GCC Customs Transit regime, the exporter retains proof of export accepted by the Oman Tax Authority, and the destination of the goods is outside the GCC member states or otherwise qualifies under the GCC Common VAT Agreement. All zero-rated supplies must be supported by a valid tax invoice issued in the supplier's own name showing the zero rate, the export documentation, customs declaration, and the proof that title to the goods passed outside Oman. For offshore services and consultancy services, the zero-rating / cross-border services treatment applies under international services rules when the recipient of the supply is a non-resident person who is not registered for VAT in Oman, the recipient is outside Oman at the time of supply, and the services are effectively used and consumed outside Oman. For market-entry consultancy services, an SPC may zero-rate its fees to non-resident clients if it retains contemporaneous documentation evidencing the non-resident status of the client (e.g., VAT registration certificate, commercial register extract or a declaration on the client's letterhead) and the actual performance of the services occurred outside the Sultanate, with no direct use of services within Oman. Export-related ancillary services such as transportation, insurance, inspection, and agency services tied to exported goods are also zero-rated only to the extent the underlying main supply is export-geared and documentation is retained. The OTA may require a zero-rated supply declaration submitted within 90 days of the supply date.

Oman does not currently impose a mandatory real-time e-invoicing clearance regime as of 2025, but the Oman Tax Authority has announced a phased implementation of the Mersal e-invoicing system for B2B and B2G transactions. Taxable persons are required to issue tax invoices containing the mandatory VAT invoice fields, retain invoices and supporting records for 10 years, and ensure the machine-readable QR code requirements as introduced in phases for B2C transactions. Mersal pilot phases are in development; upon full implementation, invoices must be transmitted digitally to the OTA for fiscal validation before clearance. Until the mandatory clearance regime becomes effective, certified fiscal data storage and CSV/XML structured e-invoicing are recommended. VAT returns and payments must be submitted electronically via the TMS portal on a quarterly basis for persons above the OMR 50,000 annual supplies threshold; no separate electronic stamping requirement is currently in force.

The Oman Tax Authority may reject, reduce, or clawback input VAT credits previously claimed in respect of zero-rated supplies where documentary substantiation is incomplete, delayed, or inconsistent. If export proceeds are not repatriated to an Omani licensed bank within 180 days of the export date (or of the invoice due date, in accordance with the OTA's interpretation and Oman's foreign exchange and commercial laws), the SPC is required to treat the export as subject to VAT at the standard rate of 5% and pay the relevant output VAT, plus interest on late payment, retroactively. Penalties for VAT non-compliance or false zero-rating claims are cumulative: a tax evasion penalty under Oman's Tax Law may range from up to 100% of tax evaded; a statutory late penalty of 5% per month or part month on unpaid tax up to 50% maximum; additional delay penalties doubling in cases of prolonged non-submission of VAT returns; administrative fines from OMR 500 to OMR 5,000 per zero-rating documentation breach; penalties for e-invoicing non-compliance after Mersal goes live from OMR 100 to OMR 1,000 per instance; and criminal liability in cases of fraudulent or forged documents, with imprisonment in serious evasion cases. The OTA has a 10-year record retention period for zero-rated export claims and may reopen assessments within 5 years (or 10 years with intent to evade). No VAT refund claim is processed until the refund application, underlying customs evidence, and FX repatriation certificates are submitted through the TMS.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is pegged to the US dollar at a fixed rate of 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD. Oman operates a unified and liberalised exchange regime under the CBO Foreign Exchange Regulation Act; both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible and do not require advance approval from the central bank. Profit remittance by a foreign-owned SPC is permitted without a specific licensing hurdle, provided the SPC possesses a valid Commercial Register (CR), has filed annual financial statements, and obtains a tax clearance certificate from the Oman Tax Authority when applicable. Practical constraints arise primarily from bank-level due diligence on source of funds and the requirement to document the underlying contractual or equity basis; there is no general restriction on conversion of OMR receipts into foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters and service providers, including consultancy SPCs, may retain export receipts in foreign-currency accounts maintained with CBO-licensed commercial banks, subject to normal AML/CFT due diligence. Incoming cross-border funds must be repatriated through a licensed bank and are reflected in the bank's electronic reporting to the CBO; for services exports, the underlying contract and invoice must be retained to demonstrate the transaction is a genuine current-account payment. There is no fixed timeline for repatriation or exchange, although prudent treasury policy should repatriate within 30 days of receipt to avoid bank compliance queries.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (Foreign Exchange Department) and the Oman Tax Authority for payment reporting under the Oman Commercial Companies Law (SD 18/2019) and CBO Circular 3/2020 (AML/CFT guidelines).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: Medium (supplementary use); not viable as a primary treasury anchor. The Central Bank of Oman does not license offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Revolut, or Payoneer to hold OMR accounts, issue local IBANs, or execute domestic payment services within Oman without a CBO payment-service-provider license. Legal constraints arise under the CBO Payment System Rules and the prohibition on unauthorised money transmission; therefore an Omani SPC may utilise an offshore EMI wallet only for low-value, outward-bound international consultancy disbursements, while all inbound business receivables must be settled into a licensed Omani bank account. The SPC should obtain its own legal opinion if the EMI onboarding agreement requires the SPC to disclaim compliance with Omani electronic payment regulations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for foreign shareholder capital injection into the Omani SPC is a SWIFT MT103 wire from the parent company's account. The wire must reference the Omani company's Commercial Register number and the designating account; the bank must be provided an executed Capital Subscription Resolution, the notarised Certificate of Incorporation, the Articles of Association (MOI), and a shareholding structure chart identifying the source of funds. Under Oman's electronic reporting framework, banks classify the payment as 'Direct Investment — Share Capital'; typical purpose-code wording is 'P013 Capital Injection / Direct Investment' or ISO 20022 category code 'BD' (Business Development). The clearing rail is FTGS (Fast Transfers Gulf System) or SWIFT gpi to the receiving bank. Funds are initially held in a segregated capital sub-ledger until utilisation; the SPC must record the inward remittance in its accounting ledger and reflect the issued share capital on its Commercial Register.Outbound consultancy disbursements and trade settlements are executed primarily via SWIFT MT103/MT102 with SWIFT gpi tracking for cost efficiency. For transactions related to trade agency or procurement under advisory mandates, a commercial letter of credit (UCP 600) is permissible when the payment size exceeds USD 100,000 or upon a counterparty request. Intra-GCC payments can alternatively be settled via Oman's Real-Time Gross Settlement (O-RTGS) / MEPS duplicate or UAE FTS for quiet high-speed processing. All outbound wires must be supported by an underlying commercial invoice, service agreement, or supplier pro-forma invoice, and the bank will perform sanctions screening of the beneficiary name and rating. Where disbursements require FX conversion, Omani banks apply the CBO closing mid-rate plus a standard margin; the SPC should obtain a forward-looking FX quote at the time of mandate to lock in settlement risk.Deliverable outright forward contracts between OMR and USD, EUR, and GBP with maturities up to 12 months, freely quoteable by CBO-licensed banks under their market-making licences.Cross-currency swaps (OMR/USD and OMR/EUR) used to hedge recurring cashflow streams, subject to ISDA documentation and CBO counterparty credit limits.FX call and put options for participating in overseas tender bids where currencies are non-USD, provided the bank lists the instrument as a permissible hedge for the SPC's underlying commercial exposure.Onshore OMR NDFs are not offered and should not be used; the OMR is pegged to the USD and freely convertible, so banks instead structure deliverable forwards referencing the fixed peg.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Medium (supplementary use); not viable as a primary treasury anchor. The Central Bank of Oman does not license offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Revolut, or Payoneer to hold OMR accounts, issue local IBANs, or execute domestic payment services within Oman without a CBO payment-service-provider license. Legal constraints arise under the CBO Payment System Rules and the prohibition on unauthorised money transmission; therefore an Omani SPC may utilise an offshore EMI wallet only for low-value, outward-bound international consultancy disbursements, while all inbound business receivables must be settled into a licensed Omani bank account. The SPC should obtain its own legal opinion if the EMI onboarding agreement requires the SPC to disclaim compliance with Omani electronic payment regulations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: where the aggregate amount of controlled (related-party) transactions exceeds OMR 1,000,000 in a tax year. Master File: where the consolidated group’s annual revenue exceeds OMR 20,000,000 and the Omani entity participates in cross-border related-party transactions exceeding OMR 3,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Although Oman does not maintain a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) notice modelled on the UAE-style beneficial-ownership regime, the Commercial Companies Law and income-tax law effectively mandate economic substance: every foreign-owned SPC must maintain a real administrative office in Oman, have resident executive staff, make strategic management decisions in Oman, and transact through local bank accounts. The OTA can displace the SPC’s tax-resident status if the place of effective management is outside Oman, or disregard the SPC as a sham and attribute its Oman-source revenue to a foreign permanent establishment. For any treaty-rate claim or statutory loss carryforward, a substance file containing employment contracts, office lease, board resolutions, and Omani bank statements must be available.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Oman income-tax law imposes a fine of not less than 100% and up to 300% of the evaded tax, plus recovery of the principal tax and monthly late-payment surcharges. The OTA may reopen assessments for up to six years on fraudulent filings, revoke the SPC’s commercial license, and refer the conduct for criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors and the single shareholder who signed or approved a knowingly false return are jointly and severally liable for the full tax loss. A court may impose a travel ban, freeze personal bank accounts and movable estate, and, upon criminal conviction, imprisonment for a term not exceeding three years. The corporate veil will be pierced for the entirety of the evaded tax and penalties.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Oman Tax Authority may disregard the SPC as a sham, reattribute the entire Oman-source revenue to the foreign parent as a permanent establishment in Oman, and assess corporate income tax at 15% on the full profit, plus late-payment surcharges and a penalty of up to 100% of the tax due. Trade-licence renewal is denied and any treaty relief previously granted is clawed back with prosecution costs.

Director personal liability: Directors who collude in a shell structure face personal liability for all taxes and penalties under the Commercial Companies Law and the newly enhanced tax-beneficial-ownership rules; the OTA may obtain a personal civil judgment against the directors, an international asset freeze, and a travel ban to prevent escape before the tax is paid.
Money Laundering / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Oman’s Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism Law (as amended by Royal Decree 30/2016), the SPC is subject to fines up to OMR 50,000 and the commercial license is struck off; bank accounts are frozen; the responsible AML officer faces personal criminal fines and imprisonment up to ten years if the breach is proven to be wilful.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allowed the SPC to transmit unexplained funds to foreign beneficiaries are criminally liable as principals; they may be barred from any future directorship, personally fined, subjected to travel restrictions, and required to forfeit the proceeds in rem. The court can pierce the veil and confiscate all assets attributable to the money-laidered consulting revenue.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Oman Tax Authority will make a transfer-pricing adjustment to an arm’s-length price: the additional net profit is taxed at 15%; a late-payment surcharge of 2% per month is added; if TP documentation is not filed on time, a further penalty of up to 25% of the adjusted tax amount is imposed. Repeated mispricing is treated as fraudulent tax evasion, leading to the 100%-300% fraud penalty and criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved false intercompany invoices and TP documentation are jointly and severally liable to the extent of the tax-adjusted profit distributed to them. A civil court may order restitution, an accounting, and a travel ban pending full payment; where criminal dishonesty is established, imprisonment up to five years can be imposed.
Persistent Late Filing / Repeated Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: For each annual return, the SPC incurs a late-filing penalty of 10% of the tax due, a fixed fine of OMR 100, and a monthly surcharge of 2% until filing; where no return is filed, OTA issues a best-judgment assessment up to three times the declared turnover and the SPC is deregistered; the license to operate is revoked and customs/registration clearances are suspended.

Director personal liability: Directors and the sole shareholder remain personally accountable for all accumulated taxes and penalties after liquidation; they may be prevented from leaving Oman under a travel ban, their personal assets may be attached in restitution proceedings, and they may be disqualified from acting as directors in any Omani commercial company for up to five years.
