Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Single Person Company under Omani law; the sole shareholder declares the capital, which must be fully paid before filing, with a capital deposit certificate issued by an Oman-licensed bank confirming the amount is credited. No escrow account requirement applies for non-regulated corporate formation; the maritime operator licence may require proof of insurance and net worth.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Civil Defence and Ambulance Authority (CDAA) for the head office and port mooring offices, including smoke detection, extinguishers and emergency evacuation plans; structural load inspection report for any dock-side or shore office; occupational safety clearance for stevedoring supervisory personnel and work-at-height certificates for RoRo deck inspection teams under the Ministry of Labour Occupational Safety and Health Regulations.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Single Person Company under Omani law; the sole shareholder declares the capital, which must be fully paid before filing, with a capital deposit certificate issued by an Oman-licensed bank confirming the amount is credited. Pro-forma First Board Resolution — Single Person Company. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), established by Royal Decree No. 31/2021 as the successor to the Secretariat General of Taxation of the Ministry of Finance. Digital registration and filing portal: Taxoman (https://taxoman.gov.om). The OTA interoperates with the MoCIIP InvestEasy company incorporation platform and the Oman Customs Bayan single window. Commercial registration of the SPC is administered by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) performing deep-sea RoRo car-carrier operations: (1) Incorporate the SPC through MoCIIP InvestEasy and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate, which carries the unified national company ID (no minimum paid-up capital is required under the Omani Commercial Companies Law); (2) Register with Oman Customs (Bayan single window) as exporter and declarant to obtain the customs code required for vehicle export declarations from Sohar, Salalah, Duqm and Port Sultan Qaboos; (3) Register for Corporate Income Tax (CIT) with OTA on Taxoman immediately upon CR issuance, submitting the deed of incorporation, CR, registered office proof, shareholder and beneficial owner declarations and the board resolution appointing the SPC tax representative; CIT registration is a precondition for port operating licenses and fishing? no - for port clearances and trade licenses; (4) Perform the VAT registration assessment on Taxoman: mandatory registration within 30 days after annual taxable supplies including zero-rated RoRo freight exceed OMR 1,000,000 and voluntary registration when supplies exceed OMR 500,000; (5) Obtain the OTA Tax Registration Number (TRN), which must appear on all VAT invoices, customs declarations, charter-party invoices and terminal billing correspondence; (6) Trigger CIT annual return filing, VAT return filing (monthly or quarterly as assigned) and the 10% withholding tax obligations on remittances to non-resident counterparties, all anchored to the same TRN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Territorial_Scope; no pass-through eligibility). The Omani SPC is an opaque resident corporate taxpayer: CIT is charged at the entity level on Omani-source taxable income at 0% on the first OMR 30,000 of taxable profit and 15% on the excess. Outbound RoRo international-transport income attributable to the Omani port leg and the Omani-source share of the voyage is taxable in Oman; pure foreign-to-foreign voyage legs generally fall outside Omani source rules. Distributions and certain fees paid to foreign shareholders attract 10% withholding tax subject to double tax treaty relief. No income tax consolidation or fiscal unity is available between the SPC and foreign affiliates.

Available election forms

Oman has no check-the-box, pass-through or hybrid mismatch election regime. Statutory elections relevant to the SPC include: (i) VAT grouping election available under the VAT Law for related Omani resident entities, allowing one TRN, a single VAT return and joint liability, provided OTA grants approval; this is useful where the SPC controls an Omani terminal or stevedoring affiliate; (ii) input VAT recovery method elections: full attribution, standard proportional recovery or an OTA-approved special apportionment method for mixed-use vessel, port and overhead costs; (iii) VAT return frequency election between monthly and quarterly filing based on annual turnover and OTA assignment; (iv) election to carry forward Omani tax losses for up to five tax years; (v) Authorized Economic Operator (AEO) status election with Oman Customs to streamline RoRo export clearance; and (vi) DTA election to claim treaty relief on outbound dividend, interest, royalty, charter and technical service payments. No group loss surrender or tax consolidation election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 1,000,000 of annual taxable supplies triggers mandatory VAT registration within 30 days of exceeding the threshold; voluntary registration is available at OMR 500,000. Zero-rated RoRo freight and export supplies count toward both thresholds. Non-resident ship operators deriving Omani-source voyage or port income are assessed on the same threshold where a fixed Omani establishment or agent gives rise to supply presence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The RoRo operator may apply 0% VAT to: (1) export supplies of vehicles physically removed from Oman via ocean ports, evidenced by customs export declarations, bills of lading and vessel manifests, provided the goods do not return into Oman; (2) international sea transport services for the deep-sea leg from an Omani loading port to a named foreign discharge port; (3) services directly connected to the exported vehicles or the international transport, including RoRo terminal handling, stevedoring, vehicle lashing, export customs brokerage and vessel agency fees for the outbound international voyage; (4) ancillary freight-related charges invoiced to the overseas buyer or charterer where covered by the transport contract. Zero-rating is denied where the supply is consumed in Oman, including cabotage between Omani ports, domestic storage beyond the export period, terminal services for a domestic voyage, and any voyage where the vehicle is discharged in an Omani free zone and subsequently sold into the Omani domestic market; those supplies are taxable at 5%. The SPC must hold all transport and customs evidence at the date of filing the VAT return in which the zero-rated supply is declared.

  • Customs export declaration lodged electronically in the Oman Customs Bayan single window
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill evidencing carriage from the Omani loading port to the named foreign discharge port
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for the exported vehicles
  • RoRo vessel manifest and departure certificate showing consignees and export cargo
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank statement evidencing foreign-currency settlement by the overseas buyer or charterer
  • Zero-rated VAT export invoice bearing the OTA Tax Registration Number and recipient details

Oman has not yet activated a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance regime. Current obligations: issue statutory VAT invoices containing TRN of supplier and recipient, invoice date, unique sequential number, description, quantity, unit price, tax-exclusive consideration, the 5% VAT shown separately, and a zero-rating statement for export RoRo invoices; issue Arabic or bilingual invoices for domestic supplies; retain electronic invoice records accessible to OTA. The OTA has announced a phased national e-invoicing mandate with a centralized validation platform, API-based Peppol-aligned XML/JSON submission, digital stamping and near-real-time clearance of B2B and B2G invoices; large taxpayers including shipping groups are designated for the first migration wave, but the compulsory go-live date is not yet fixed in legislation. The SPC should keep its billing and ERP architecture API-ready for the future clearance regime.

OTA may reassess VAT for the preceding five calendar years. If the zero-rating evidence package (customs export declaration, bill of lading, vessel manifest, foreign remittance) is missing or inconsistent, the supply is reclassified as taxable at 5%, with monthly late-payment penalties accruing on the underpaid VAT and fixed administrative fines imposed per material violation, including failure to register, issuance of incorrect invoices and failure to retain records. Where the SPC deliberately invoices zero-rated exports without actual loading, uses round-tripping RoRo calls or fabricates terminal and customs documents, the conduct is treated as tax evasion carrying criminal penalties of imprisonment of up to five years and fines of up to OMR 10,000, confiscation of vehicles and vessels involved in the evasion scheme, public disclosure on the OTA non-compliant taxpayer list, and revocation of AEO status and tax good-standing certificates, which in turn blocks port clearances, bank foreign exchange services, vessel agency licensing and CR renewal. Retrospective clawback of input VAT recovered on direct costs is triggered where export documents are not reconciled with the vessel manifest or where proceeds are repatriated later than the 180-day benchmark, and shipping agents and terminal operators may be held jointly liable where they knowingly issue zero-rated port invoices without underlying physical export.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current account transactions and capital account transactions for foreign direct investment, with profit and capital repatriation permitted for 100% foreign-owned entities under the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2019). Dividends, branch remittances, and liquidation proceeds may be freely remitted provided the entity submits audited financial statements, tax clearance from the Oman Tax Authority, and evidence of settlement of all commercial obligations. No exchange control approval is required for trade-related payments or for the conversion of OMR into USD/EUR, as the CBO pegs the Rial to the US Dollar at a fixed rate of 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD with no exchange restrictions on current or capital transactions. Practical hurdles result from the bank's internal compliance procedures, particularly the need to provide underlying trade documentation, beneficiary due diligence files, and OECD CRS declarations, rather than from statutory convertibility controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose any mandatory surrender or conversion requirements on export proceeds. A deep-sea Ro-Ro vessel operator may retain export receipts in USD or other convertible currencies in onshore or offshore accounts, provided the Central Bank of Oman's Foreign Exchange Circulars are respected and foreign currency accounts are opened through a licensed Omani commercial bank. Where the Oman entity invoices a foreign affiliate for shipping services, the proceeds must be credited and supported by a commercial invoice, bill of lading, and a CBO Form for trade settlement if the bank requires a purpose code; however, there is no statutory timeline or percentage-based surrender obligation for export earnings under Omani law.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) and the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) in coordination with the National Centre for Statistics and Information (NCSI) for trade data reconciliation

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to Medium operational viability for routine treasury payments, but insufficient as a primary banking solution. Oman's Central Bank prohibits unlicensed electronic money institutions and payment service providers from servicing residents without a CBO authorization. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Currenxie cannot maintain local clearing routing or provide OMR accounts, and must partner with a licensed Omani bank or a correspondent bank. A cross-border Ro-Ro carrier may use an EMI to remit charter hire or settle bunker procurement from the UAE or Singapore, but the Oman SPC must maintain its principal operational account at a licensed Omani commercial bank so that local regulatory reporting under the CBO's Anti-Money Laundering and Combatting Financing of Terrorism Circular and tax authority access to banking records are fully satisfied. The highest compliance use is for non-Oman-related disbursements, although even then the bank account of the SPC should be the account of record for invoicing and collection purposes.

Root cause: Profit remittance and dividend distribution to a foreign parent within the first year, which is structurally permissible under Oman law but will trigger a high-risk review because no prior audited financial evidence exists

Defense evidence: Interim management accounts prepared and signed by the Oman auditor, Oman Tax Authority declaration confirming no pending tax liabilities or tax card, CBO remittance Form / Foreign Exchange Paper declaring the basis of the profit distribution, Reserve Bank or origin-country regulatory approval for outward remittance, and minutes of the shareholders' meeting resolving the distribution. If the parent is in a tax-haven or non-cooperative jurisdiction, the bank will request OECD CRS self-certification and detailed economic-substance documentation showing employees, chartering assets, or operational management in Oman.

Root cause: Reliance on a non-UAE or non-Oman fintech EMI for international payments may trigger a politically exposed person / PEP status or correspondent-bank rejection because the digital EMI lacks a valid relationship with the Omani banking system and may route USD through unregulated corridors

Defense evidence: Written New Product/Service approval from the CBO or a prominent contractor confirming that the EMI is licensed in the EEA/Singapore and does not handle OMR clearings; a bringing letter from the Omani bank that the EMI's payment services are deemed acceptable under the bank's outsourcing policy; corporate chart and AML controls of the EMI; UBO declaration of the digital payment provider; and last six months of settlement statements from the EMI proving the origin of funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign parent of the Oman SPC must inject the minimum share capital (no minimum for SPC) and operational equity/debt funding via SWIFT transfer from a bank account in the parent's jurisdiction to the SPC's USD operational account at an Omani licensed bank. The wire should be tagged with the correct purpose code, typically 'Direct Investment: Equity Capital' (IMF BPM6 code for equity investment in an SPV) or 'Share Capital Injection under Foreign Capital Investment Law' with the beneficiary reference containing the commercial registration number. The Omani receiving bank will normally require a capital injection declaration showing the source of funds, the shareholder resolution, and the share confirmation in the SPC's books. The MoCIIP is notified via the 'Investor Relationship' process and the Oman Tax Authority is not required to pre-approve inbound equity, but the company must update the commercial license's paid-in capital record at the Commercial Registry. Loan subordination and shareholder loans should be routed to a separate OMR or USD loan account to avoid commingling with operating revenues and should be documented by a loan agreement and director resolution referencing the Common Reporting Standard and transfer pricing regulations.For vessel disbursements, bunker payments, port costs, sub-hire, and agent payments, the Oman SPC should pay by SWIFT MT103 through its Omani commercial bank using the beneficiary's IBAN and the proper purpose codes, e.g. 'Transport - Freight and Charter Hire' or 'Operating Expenses Related to Ocean Shipping'. For the purchase or bareboat charter of Ro-Ro vessels and high-value technical services (ship management contracts or IT systems), a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) issued by the Omani bank and advised/confirmed by Singapore, UAE, or European correspondent banks is acceptable. Where the Omani entity sells Ro-Ro transportation services to a foreign automobile manufacturer or shipping client, the payable should be received by wire under 'Omani Export of Services / International Transport of Goods' and the Oman VAT rules on freight must be handled separately. Rial-denominated operating expenses (Omani port disbursements under Asyad, Oman Shipping, or Port Sultan Qaboos) may settle by local wire transfer through the Domestic Payment System operated by the Central Bank of Oman, while USD receivables should be concentrated in the USD currency account to avoid constant conversions.FX Forwards of up to 12 months with an Omani licensed bank or approved international counterparty, subject to board authorization and the bank's collateral policy.Participation in the OMR/USD pegged-rate forwards offered by Bank Muscat, HSBC Bank Oman, and National Bank of Oman for receivables or payables in OMR or USD.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) in OMR when counterparties prefer offshore settlement outside the Omani interbank system, legally available only through an international bank licensed to book derivatives in the UAE, Singapore, or London, and not through an unhedged local broker.Cross-currency swaps (USD/EUR/GBP) for vessel acquisition debt and operating lease commitments, executed under ISDA Master Agreements with bilateral credit support annexes.Commodity hedging linked to bunker procurement permitted through Omani bank trade desks, but subject to risk management review; fuel risk is outside the CBO FX capital controls and should be treated under pure market risk policy.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Medium operational viability for routine treasury payments, but insufficient as a primary banking solution. Oman's Central Bank prohibits unlicensed electronic money institutions and payment service providers from servicing residents without a CBO authorization. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Currenxie cannot maintain local clearing routing or provide OMR accounts, and must partner with a licensed Omani bank or a correspondent bank. A cross-border Ro-Ro carrier may use an EMI to remit charter hire or settle bunker procurement from the UAE or Singapore, but the Oman SPC must maintain its principal operational account at a licensed Omani commercial bank so that local regulatory reporting under the CBO's Anti-Money Laundering and Combatting Financing of Terrorism Circular and tax authority access to banking records are fully satisfied. The highest compliance use is for non-Oman-related disbursements, although even then the bank account of the SPC should be the account of record for invoicing and collection purposes.
