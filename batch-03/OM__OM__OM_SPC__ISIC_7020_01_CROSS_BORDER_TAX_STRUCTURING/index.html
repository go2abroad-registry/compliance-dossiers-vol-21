Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Single Person Company under Royal Decree No. 18/2019; however the subscribed capital must be fully paid at incorporation and must be adequate for the intended consultancy operations. No escrow account or capital deposit certificate is required by the Companies Law for this commercial professional services activity.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from General Directorate of Civil Defense (Royal Oman Police), fire extinguisher maintenance and emergency exit conformity; structural load inspection certificate issued by the building owner or municipality confirming approved office occupancy load; occupational health and workplace clearance from Ministry of Labour for the number of office employees.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Single Person Company under Royal Decree No. 18/2019; however the subscribed capital must be fully paid at incorporation and must be adequate for the intended consultancy operations. No escrow account or capital deposit certificate is required by the Companies Law for this commercial professional services activity.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) — national revenue administration succeeding the Secretariat General of Taxation of the Ministry of Finance; statutory e-portal for income tax, VAT, withholding declarations, and e-invoicing clearance: www.taxoman.gov.om, integrated with the Bayan national customs single window.

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors must first incorporate the SPC at the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR). With the CR issued, the SPC must register for Corporate Income Tax with the OTA within three months from commencement of business activity to obtain the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN / tax card). A non-resident company carrying on activities through an Omani permanent establishment (PE) or deriving Omani-source income without a local office must itself register with the OTA and, where required, instruct an Omani tax agent. VAT registration is effected separately through the same OTA e-portal: within 30 days after taxable supplies or imports cross the mandatory OMR 38,500 annual threshold, or voluntarily at OMR 19,250, the OTA issues a distinct 15-digit VAT TIN. The Omani SPC must also file a tax clearance / no-objection certificate before CR cancellation, share transfer, or cessation of activities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — mandatory for an Omani SPC; the single-person limited company is an opaque resident corporate taxpayer, not eligible for pass-through or partnership classification. Territorial_Scope — resident SPC is taxed on worldwide income at the 15% CIT headline rate, with a 0% band on the first OMR 100,000 of taxable income; non-resident persons and foreign PEs are taxed on Oman-source income only, with outward dividends, interest, royalties, and management/technical fees subject to 10% withholding tax unless reduced by an applicable Double Tax Treaty. Tax_Exempt_Zone — available only where the SPC obtains a licence in a designated free or economic zone (e.g., Duqm SEZAD, Sohar Port and Freezone, Salalah Free Zone), in which case zone incentive laws provide defined multi-year CIT holidays and special VAT treatment. — Foreign investors must first incorporate the SPC at the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — mandatory for an Omani SPC; the single-person limited company is an opaque resident corporate taxpayer, not eligible for pass-through or partnership classification. Territorial_Scope — resident SPC is taxed on worldwide income at the 15% CIT headline rate, with a 0% band on the first OMR 100,000 of taxable income; non-resident persons and foreign PEs are taxed on Oman-source income only, with outward dividends, interest, royalties, and management/technical fees subject to 10% withholding tax unless reduced by an applicable Double Tax Treaty. Tax_Exempt_Zone — available only where the SPC obtains a licence in a designated free or economic zone (e.g., Duqm SEZAD, Sohar Port and Freezone, Salalah Free Zone), in which case zone incentive laws provide defined multi-year CIT holidays and special VAT treatment.

Available election forms

No check-the-box, hybrid-mismatch, fiscal unity, or transparent-entity election exists under Omani law; the SPC is per se taxable. Available statutory elections and options include: (i) the 0% CIT band election for taxable income not exceeding OMR 100,000 per year; (ii) voluntary VAT registration at the OMR 19,250 threshold; (iii) VAT grouping between affiliated entities subject to OTA pre-approval and joint-and-several liability; (iv) accounting-period alignment election to align the Omani subsidiary with the ultimate foreign parent's reporting year; (v) income-tax loss carryforward utilisation election within the statutory five-year limitation window. No group relief or loss-transfer regime is recognised.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 38,500 of annual taxable supplies during any consecutive 12-month period, or OMR 38,500 of cumulative imports into Oman in the same window (approximately USD 100,100 at the pegged exchange rate of OMR 1 = USD 2.6008). Voluntary registration: OMR 19,250 of annual taxable supplies or taxable expenses. Non-resident suppliers of distance sales or electronically supplied services to Omani customers must register at the mandatory threshold unless a VAT representative has been appointed and the place-of-supply rules allocate taxation elsewhere in the GCC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies where title and physical possession pass to a non-resident buyer, the goods are consigned to a destination outside Oman and outside the GCC Implementing States, export is completed within 90 days from the date of supply, and the supplier retains a valid Customs Export Declaration, commercial invoice, transport documents, and payment evidence. Services: zero-rating applies to (i) international transport of passengers and goods; (ii) services directly connected with exported goods, such as loading, handling, freight, inspection, and marine insurance; and (iii) services supplied to a non-resident customer that are effectively consumed and benefit outside Oman and the GCC. For the cross-border tax-structuring advisory SPC, zero-rating is permitted only where the consultancy is performed and used entirely outside Omani territory, the recipient is outside the GCC, no Omani PE or fixed base is involved, and no application of the advice occurs inside Oman; advisory services evaluating or implementing Omani domestic structures remain taxable at the standard 5% rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan national single-window record) evidencing physical export from an Omani port, free zone, or approved customs territory
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Multimodal Transport Document showing despatch to a destination outside Oman and the GCC Implementing States
  • Commercial Invoice containing the zero-rating statement, the exporter and consignee VAT/TIN identifiers, Incoterms, and the unique customs reference (UCR)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank-issued, cross-referenced to SWIFT MT103/MT202) evidencing receipt of export proceeds within the repatriation benchmark
  • Packing list, cargo manifest, and freight-forwarder or insurer certification where multimodal or indirect export movement has been used

Oman has introduced a phased national e-invoicing mandate under the OTA. VAT-registered persons in the designated tranches must issue e-invoices in a machine-readable structured XML format, transmit invoices to the OTA's e-invoice clearance portal in near real time before issuance to the buyer, obtain an approved clearance identifier, and affix a cryptographic QR code / digital stamp. Businesses integrate their ERP and billing systems with the OTA-approved API communication protocol; B2G and large-taxpayer waves are first in sequence, with subsequent rollout for all VAT registrants. Paper invoices remain lawful outside the active waves, but once the mandate applies, an unrecorded or un-cleared invoice is inadmissible as a VAT recovery document, input tax is denied, and qualifying export documentation must be linked digitally to the Bayan customs records and archived for OTA audit.

Export zero-rating claims are a priority audit area for the OTA. Unsupported, late-filed, or fictitious export substantiation exposes the exporter to retrospective VAT assessment at the standard 5% rate with late-payment penalties of 1% per month of the unpaid tax, fixed administrative fines ranging from OMR 500 to OMR 10,000 per defective document, denial of input VAT recovery, and suspension of pending refunds. Where the Authority establishes deliberate fraud — including false export declarations, circular fund flows, or re-introduction of exported goods into Oman — the clawback period extends to the statutory limitation period, criminal fines up to OMR 20,000 or double the evaded tax may be imposed, and responsible directors or officers may be imprisoned for up to three years. Refunds founded on zero-rated export transactions are auditable within the limitation window and are automatically frozen while a tax dispute is under review or objection.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman is a fully convertible current-account jurisdiction under IMF Article VIII, without exchange controls on payments and transfers for trade in goods or services or on profit and capital remittances by a 100% foreign-owned SPC. The OMR is pegged to the US dollar at the CBO reference rate of US$1 = OMR 0.3845 with a narrow intervention band, eliminating material USD/OMR risk and ensuring on-demand convertibility. Capital-account operations are generally open, subject to CBO statistical reporting; practical profit-remittance hurdles are documentary rather than regulatory, requiring audited accounts, treasury validation of the commercial reality, tax-clearance confirmation and, where a double-taxation treaty is invoked, the relevant residence certificate and relief form.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no export-proceeds surrender obligation in Omani law: the mandatory conversion ratio is 0% and there is no statutory repatriation deadline. Exporters may retain 100% of export receipts in foreign-currency accounts at CBO-licensed banks, provided each receipt is received through the banking system and captured in CBO Balance-of-Payments reporting. For an ISIC 7020 consultancy SPC, foreign-currency fee collections may lawfully be held offshore or swept into the Omani operational account without conversion penalties.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) - the sole monetary authority, banking supervisor and official foreign-exchange administrator of the Sultanate of Oman; the CBO Banking Supervision Department regulates licensed banks and the CBO Financial Intelligence Unit exercises the national AML/CFT enforcement mandate, complemented for remittances by the Oman Tax Authority (tax clearance) and MOCIIP (Foreign Capital Investment Law registration). Legal basis: Banking Law (RD 114/2000), AML Law (RD 30/2016) and CBO foreign-exchange directives.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Oman does not impose any statutory withholding tax on dividends, profit distributions, or branch profit remittances to resident or non-resident shareholders. The absence of DWT is a deliberate fiscal policy to attract foreign capital; therefore no domestic withholding obstacle exists for an SPC repatriating dividends to its foreign parent. The transfer must still be supported by audited distributable reserves and board minutes to avoid reclassification as a loan or undeclared remuneration.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return is due within four months after the end of the SPC's financial year; for calendar-year companies the deadline is 30 April. A written extension of up to one additional month may be granted by the OTA if application is made before the original due date. Payment of the final liability is due on the same deadline; deferred payment accrues a 1 percent monthly late-payment surcharge.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Oman Transfer Pricing Regulations (Ministerial Decision No. 57/2023), effective for tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2024, impose: (i) Local File obligation where the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with related parties equals or exceeds OMR 500,000; (ii) Master File obligation where the multinational group's consolidated worldwide revenue equals or exceeds OMR 50 million; (iii) Country-by-Country Report and notification where the MNE group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds OMR 375 million. A listed taxpayer must also file a TP disclosure statement with the annual CIT return when controlled transactions exceed OMR 500,000. Even below the thresholds, a written TP policy and contemporaneous benchmarking must be made available to the OTA on request within 30 days; the SPC is required to be ready to demonstrate arm's-length pricing for all cross-border management fees, advisory fees and cost recharges.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime, but economic substance is embedded in multiple legal layers. The Commercial Companies Law (Royal Decree 18/2019) requires every SPC to maintain a registered office in Oman, a resident manager, board decision-making records, local bank accounts, payroll, and physical premises consistent with its licensed activity. For ISIC 7020 tax structuring services, the OTA will evaluate substance by evidence of actual consultancy work performed in Oman, qualified professional staff, execution of engagement letters, and control over cross-border transactions. The Tax Procedures Law (Royal Decree 31/2023) contains a General Anti-Avoidance Rule and substance-based recharacterization powers, and Oman's commitment to the OECD BEPS framework means shell companies with no local decision-making are disregarded, with profits reattributed to the ultimate parent jurisdiction. The OTA may also deny treaty benefits under the principal purpose test if the SPC was created primarily to obtain Omani tax benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Procedures Law (RD 31/2023) and Income Tax Law: imprisonment up to five years, fines up to OMR 100,000, confiscation of proceeds, assessment of all evaded tax, a 25 percent evasion penalty, and a 1 percent monthly late-payment surcharge. The OTA is empowered to publish the names of convicted evaders.

Director personal liability: The SPC manager, directors, and controlling shareholders shadow-direct the entity are personally exposed: criminal conviction on Gross Negligence grounds, civil restitution of the full evaded tax, OTA-issued travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and disqualification from holding future board or management positions in Oman.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization: the OTA disregards the SPC, reattributes profit to the foreign parent, denies all deductions claimed under the sham contract, imposes 15 percent CIT plus 1 percent monthly interest, and applies penal fines up to 50 percent of the additional tax. The company may also be struck off by the commercial registrar.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: shareholders who authorized the sham structure are jointly and severally liable for the reattributed tax and penalties; directors face personal travel bans, confiscation of personal property, and imprisonment where the sham is coupled with fraudulent intent to evade tax or launder proceeds.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties of up to OMR 20,000 for each missing or defective TP document, plus separate OMR 20,000 penalties for CbCR non-compliance; the OTA simultaneously makes an upward adjustment to taxable income, recharacterizes the fees as deemed dividends or royalties subject to 10 percent WHT, and adds the 1 percent monthly late-payment charge on the resulting CIT.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign TP-out-of-scope disclaimers without factual basis face personal liability for the penalty where the documentation was knowingly false; travel bans and disqualification of the responsible chief financial officer or tax manager can be imposed by court order in aggravated cases.
AML and Anti-Terrorist Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Oman's Anti-Money Laundering Law (RD 30/2016, as amended): imprisonment up to seven years, fines up to OMR 200,000, forfeiture of funds and instrumentalities, revocation of the commercial licence, and compulsory de-risking by Omani banks. The financial intelligence unit may freeze all SPC bank accounts pending investigation.

Director personal liability: AML compliance is a statutory duty of the SPC manager and directors: personal criminal liability arises without proof of personal enrichment if the officer knew or should have known of the illicit funds; sanctions include sequestration of personal assets, travel bans, extradition exposure, and complete loss of the right to manage any Omani company.
Withholding Tax and Remittance Defaults

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the full un-withheld WHT from the payer, a penalty of 25 percent of the unrecovered amount, and the 1 percent monthly surcharge; the corresponding expense is disallowed for CIT purposes until evidence of the tax payment is produced; repeat offences trigger criminal fines and imprisonment of responsible officers.

Director personal liability: The directors and the senior finance officer are jointly liable for the un-collected WHT as personal guarantors of corporate fiscal obligations; the OTA may seize personal bank accounts, issue travel bans, and extend criminal liability to all signatories of the payment instructions that circumvented the WHT regime.
