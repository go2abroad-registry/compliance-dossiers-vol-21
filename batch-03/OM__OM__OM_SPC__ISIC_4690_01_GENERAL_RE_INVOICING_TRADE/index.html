Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital requirement for a foreign-owned SPC under Royal Decree 18/2019; the subscribed capital is fixed in the constituent contract and must be deposited into a licensed Omani commercial bank before registration. A banker's certificate is required at filing. No escrow account is statutorily mandated for a general re-invoicing trading business, but movements in corporate accounts must comply with CBO AML/CFT regulations.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the Oman Civil Defence and Ambulance Authority for the registered office and warehouse; structural load inspection and safe capacity statement for any mezzanine or storage rack system; workplace clearance from the Ministry of Labour confirming occupational health compliance, fire exit pathway, and the availability of a minimum of two authorised first-aid staff per worksite.

A banker's certificate is required at filing. No escrow account is statutorily mandated for a general re-invoicing trading business, but movements in corporate accounts must comply with CBO AML/CFT regulations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) — the national revenue authority responsible for corporate income tax (CIT) and VAT; official E-Services portal: https://eservices.taxoman.gov.om (main information site: https://www.tax.gov.om). Commercial registration is supervised by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP); customs control is executed by the Royal Oman Police Directorate General of Customs (ROP-DG Customs).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the SPC under the Commercial Companies Law (RD 18/2019), execute a notarized English/Arabic set of Articles of Association, and register with MoCIIP to obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate and municipal license; foreign investors may hold 100% of the SPC without a local partner. Step 2 — CIT registration: within 3 months from the CR issuance/commencement of business, access the OTA E-Services portal, create the taxpayer profile containing CR number, shareholder identity, registered address and activity codes, and obtain the Oman Tax Registration Number (TRN) which serves as the primary tax identity for CIT, withholding tax and VAT. Step 3 — VAT registration: if the annual taxable supplies exceed the mandatory threshold of OMR 500,000, or if voluntary registration is sought at OMR 250,000, submit the VAT registration application within 30 days from the end of the month in which the threshold is exceeded; the OTA will issue a distinct 15-digit VAT registration number prefixed with 'OM'. Step 4 — Documentary pack: certified copies of passports/CR of the sole shareholder and manager, notarized Articles, CR certificate, proof of registered physical address, bank confirmation, and audited financial statements where applicable; all non-Arabic documents require legalized Arabic translation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the Omani Single Person Company is a tax-resident juridical person (incorporation-based residence) and is fully taxable at entity level under the Income Tax Law (RD 28/2009, as amended by RD 9/2017). CIT is levied at 15% of taxable income with no pass-through treatment. The SPC's taxable scope is worldwide income derived through the Omani entity; no mainland free-zone or tax-exempt-zone status is available for this trading SPC. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Omani Single Person Company is a tax-resident juridical person (incorporation-based residence) and is fully taxable at entity level under the Income Tax Law (RD 28/2009, as amended by RD 9/2017). CIT is levied at 15% of taxable income with no pass-through treatment. The SPC's taxable scope is worldwide income derived through the Omani entity; no mainland free-zone or tax-exempt-zone status is available for this trading SPC. For VAT, the SPC is a separate taxable person and its standard-rated and zero-rated supplies, plus imports, are aggregated for registration-test purposes.

Available election forms

Not available. Oman does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid classification election; there is no consolidated or group CIT filing, no VAT group registration, and no option to tax otherwise-exempt financial supplies. The general anti-avoidance provisions of the Income Tax Law allow the OTA to disregard artificial transactions and re-characterize arrangements based on economic substance, so the SPC cannot legitimately achieve hybrid-mismatch outcomes through internal elections.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial is de facto pegged to the US Dollar at a CBO reference rate of 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD and is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. Oman does not require prior exchange-control authorization for foreign direct investment; an SPC may be 100% foreign-owned under the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree No. 50/2019) and the Commercial Companies Law (Royal Decree No. 18/2019). Dividend remittances, share-capital repatriation, and loan servicing are freely permitted subject only to bank-level documentary verification and AML/CFT screening. The residual friction for a re-invoicing entity is not exchange control but 'substance' review: because the goods do not cross Omani customs, banks will require a demonstrable commercial rationale, a physical contractual presence, and a transparent beneficial-ownership chain before approving outward remittance volumes commensurate with gross-turnover re-invoicing models. There are no quantitative limits on foreign-currency account balances or on conversion amounts for licensed corporates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio exists under Omani law. An exporter or re-invoicing intermediary may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign-currency accounts at CBO-licensed banks. The operative compliance duties are: (i) all trade-related proceeds must eventually flow through the Omani banking system so that the CBO can compile accurate BOP statistics; (ii) the proceeds must be repatriated within the credit period evidenced in the underlying commercial contract, typically net 90 to 180 days for general re-invoicing trades; and (iii) each receipt must be supported by a reconciled commercial dossier (invoice, bill of lading, packing list, and settlement statement) available to the bank on request. Non-repatriation without a documented trade rationale is treated as a red flag for concealed capital flight but is not penalized by a formal surrender ratio.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), the exclusive monetary authority and banking regulator established under Royal Decree No. 23/1974, operating under the Banking Law promulgated by Royal Decree No. 37/2022. Foreign-exchange administration is exercised through CBO licensing of banks and exchange bureaux, issuance of AML/CFT circulars, and mandatory balance-of-payments (BOP) reporting by all licensed banks; no separate exchange-control law or ministry-level approval exists for routine trade transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium with strict structural constraints. The CBO does not license pure non-bank electronic money institutions (EMIs) or neobanks within Oman; the banking perimeter confines deposit-taking and payment execution to licensed banks and approved payment service providers under the CBO Oversight Framework for Payment Systems and Card Schemes. Consequently, foreign EMIs (e.g., EU or UK-licensed equivalents, global multi-currency wallets) cannot hold OMR settlement accounts and are not recognized as correspondent beneficiaries by Omani banks for trade settlements involving the SPC; many Omani compliance teams classify such transfers as unhosted, third-party or high-risk and reject them unless the beneficiary is a fully regulated bank. The SPC may lawfully maintain a foreign-currency account at an overseas licensed bank or an offshore payment institution for ancillary disbursement purposes, but such a rail must be supplementary only. The primary clearing architecture must remain onshore at a CBO-licensed bank to preserve BOP reporting integrity, tax substance, and the documentary defense of the re-invoicing model.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital must be remitted to the SPC's segregated Capital Escrow Account at the CBO-licensed bank via SWIFT MT103. The bank will record the remittance under the BOP category for direct-investment equity capital (BPM6 DI.2) and the instruction text must state 'Equity Capital Injection - Share Capital Contribution' with reference to the SPC name and CR number. The remitter must be the registered shareholder named in the Commercial Register; if the shareholder is a corporate entity, its certificate of incorporation, register of directors, and UBO identification must be provided. The shareholder resolution approving the injection, the CR certificate, and a shareholding-structure declaration are mandatory attachments. Because Omani law imposes no minimum capital on an SPC, the injection amount should be calibrated to working-capital and trade-volume needs; the funds may be swept to the operational account only after the bank records the capital and the SPC presents an auditor's confirmation of capital utilization where the injection is designated as paid-up capital. Any inbound remittance not matching the shareholder's identity or lacking the equity-purpose reference will be rejected as a suspicious transfer.Settlement rails are selected by trade leg and risk tier: (i) Standard wire settlements use SWIFT MT103 for USD/EUR gross disbursements to suppliers; MT103 is the fully auditable, BOP-coded standard and should be used as the default rail. (ii) Commercial letters of credit are issued through SWIFT MT700 (advised via MT707 amendments; MT710 for transferable LCs in back-to-back structures) with payment flows executed by the nominated bank upon presentation of compliant transport documents. (iii) Express/XT (external cross-border transfer) formats are permissible for low-value, pre-approved supplier payments below USD 50,000 where the beneficiary bank is a regulated tier-1 institution and the purpose code matches the underlying invoice; XT rails are not acceptable for first-time counterparties or high-risk corridors. (iv) FX-conversion legs between the invoice currency (e.g., CNY, EUR) and the funding currency (USD/OMR) execute as spot conversions on the same value date, or as deliverable forwards when the settlement date exceeds 48 hours. (v) For sanctioned-adjacent or enhanced-risk corridors, disbursements must be routed through Omani correspondent relationships using restricted-purpose transfers, vessel pre-screening, and pre-approved beneficiary lists; the bank may require a five-business-day pre-notification window. All outbound disbursements must mirror the three-way matching dossier retained on file for a minimum of five years per CBO record-retention obligations.Deliverable FX Forward (USD/OMR and cross-currency pairs) - permitted on genuine trade-backed exposure for tenors up to 12 months, collateralized against the SPC's credit line, with settlement in OMR or the contractual currency at the CBO/forex fixing rate.Cross-Currency Swap (CCS) - permissible for term conversion of USD/EUR receivables into OMR or CNY/EUR disbursement currencies, provided the notional and maturity profile match the underlying re-invoicing portfolio and no speculative open position is created.Offshore Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) - legally permissible but operationally limited; offshore OMR NDF liquidity is negligible due to the hard USD peg, so onshore banks offer non-deliverable structures referencing regional pairs (e.g., USD/CNY NDF based on the offshore CNH fixing) exclusively to hedge disbursement legs to Asian suppliers; speculative NDF positions are prohibited for the SPC under CBO derivative-appropriateness guidance.FX Swap (spot/forward combination) - available for rolling settlement of supplier payments across value dates and for temporary conversion of OMR operating liquidity into USD disbursement balances, structured as sell-buy swaps with matched maturity.Islamic Treasury Product (Reverse Wa'd based FX forward) - available through Islamic windows (e.g., Bank Nizwa and Islamic units of conventional banks) as a Shari'ah-compliant forward undertaking, permissible only where the re-invoicing contracts and trade documentation satisfy the underlying asset-test requirement.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Oman does not impose a withholding tax on dividends distributed by an onshore SPC to its sole foreign shareholder; profit remittance may proceed via bank transfer without OTA WHT clearance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 120 days after the fiscal year-end under Royal Decree 15/2017 as amended by Royal Decree 4/2023. For tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2025, the Unified Corporate Tax Law deadline, as administratively confirmed by the OTA, is aligned to the 120-day filing window with payment due on the same date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File and Master File are mandatory where the SPC's gross revenue exceeded OMR 10,000,000 in the relevant tax period or where aggregate related-party re-invoicing transactions surpassed OMR 500,000 in the tax year; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to MNE groups whose consolidated revenue exceeds the OTA-published OECD-equivalent threshold (approximately OMR 460,000,000). The SPC in a general re-invoicing operation will almost invariably exceed the OMR 500,000 transaction threshold and must maintain full OECD-aligned TP documentation.

Economic substance scope: Oman has not enacted UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the anti-abuse provisions of the Income Tax Law, the Unified Corporate Tax Law, the OECD 'substance over form' doctrine, and OTA audit practice require the SPC to demonstrate real commercial substance in Oman: physical office premises, qualified resident staff, independent board and management decision-making, local bank accounts and invoicing systems, and assumption of commercial risk. A passive letterbox re-invoicing vehicle with no operational substance in Oman is vulnerable to treaty-benefit denial, GAAR recharacterisation and profit reallocation to the foreign parent's jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Fabricated Re-Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Recomputed 15% corporate tax on the undeclared profit, a monthly late-payment surcharge of 1%, an evasion penalty of 50%-100% of the evaded tax under the Tax Procedures Law, and criminal prosecution of the responsible persons.

Director personal liability: Directors and the beneficial owner are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the tax evaded; courts may issue travel bans, attach personal bank accounts and impose imprisonment for fraudulent invoicing and false declarations.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation on Re-Invoicing Margins

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to arm's length profit, additional tax at the 15% rate, 1% monthly late-interest until settlement, and an administrative penalty of 25%-40% of the additional tax assessed; contemporaneous TP documents are demanded at audit.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the pricing policy may be summoned for individual examination; continued TP default can lead to asset-freezing orders, disqualification from managing any Omani company, and travel restraint pending tax recovery.
Shell / Letterbox Re-Invoicing Company and DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and Tax Residency Certificates, recharacterisation of the margin as a taxable service fee, full 15% assessment on the reallocated profit, and potential imposition of penalties under the GAAR provisions of the Unified Corporate Tax Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who certified false substance declarations are personally liable under the Commercial Companies Law for damages caused to the state treasury; travel bans and passport restrictions are commonly imposed during OTA substance examinations.
Trade-Based Money Laundering / AML Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Penalties under Oman's Anti-Money Laundering Law (Royal Decree 30/2016): fines from OMR 1,000 up to OMR 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment; the OTA may freeze bank accounts and the Ministry of Commerce may deregister the commercial license.

Director personal liability: The beneficial owner and directors face criminal conviction carrying up to 7 years' imprisonment, confiscation of laundered assets, and prohibition from operating any licensed trade in Oman.
Breach of Tax Administration Duties / Late Filing

Statutory sanction: OMR 100 per month late-filing penalty, 1% monthly late-payment surcharge, 15%-25% document-production penalties, suspension of loss carryforward benefits, and publication of the taxpayer's default status.

Director personal liability: Foreign directors who neglect tax duties can be personally joined to enforcement proceedings; the OTA may obtain a receivership order over the SPC's assets and request police travel bans until all taxes, fines and surcharges are settled.
