Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an SPC under Royal Decree 18/2019; the share capital, stated in Omani Rial in the MOA, must be fully subscribed and, depending on the MOA terms, at least the paid portion must be deposited in a temporary bank account of a licensed Omani bank and evidenced by a bank certificate before commercial registration. 100% foreign ownership is permitted under RD 50/2019 for wholesale trading activities not on the negative list.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) - the national tax administration within the Ministry of Finance, constituted as the statutory successor to the Secretariat General of Taxation under Royal Decree No. 54/2024 (Tax Procedures Law); official taxpayer e-services and filings are transacted through the OTA Tax Portal at www.tax.gov.om, covering TIN issuance, VAT registration, periodic VAT returns, and the electronic invoice clearance platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: incorporate the Foreign-Owned Single Person Company (SPC) with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) via the Invest Easy platform and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) classified under ISIC 4661 / NAICS 424720. Step 2: upon CR issuance, file the OTA tax registration application through the OTA Tax Portal submitting the CR certificate, constitutional documents (AOA), Oman lease/place-of-business evidence, passport copies of the sole shareholder and governor, and the ultimate beneficial owner declaration. Step 3: simultaneously assess VAT exposure; if taxable supplies in Oman (including imports and deemed supplies) equal or exceed OMR 38,500 in the preceding 12-month period, file a mandatory VAT registration application within 30 days to obtain the VAT Registration Number, or elect voluntary VAT registration once taxable supplies or deductible expenses reach OMR 19,250. Step 4: complete the employer registration with the Public Authority for Social Insurance and confirm any sectoral trade licence requirements prior to first import or export customs declaration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the SPC is a resident juridical person incorporated in Oman and is taxed as a standalone taxpayer under Royal Decree No. 28/2009 (Income Tax Law); taxable income up to OMR 30,000 is taxed at 0% and income above OMR 30,000 at 15%; no flow-through to the sole foreign shareholder is permitted, and the entity cannot elect pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

None - Oman does not provide any check-the-box, flow-through, tax consolidation, group relief, or hybrid mismatch election; each legal person, including the 100% foreign-owned SPC, is mandatorily assessed at entity level. Available administrative elections are limited to: (i) adoption of a non-calendar fiscal year with prior OTA approval; (ii) voluntary VAT registration before the mandatory threshold is crossed once taxable turnover exceeds OMR 19,250; and (iii) voluntary amended return filing to self-correct errors before the OTA issues an assessment, which mitigates but does not eliminate penalties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration at OMR 38,500 (approx. USD 100,000) of cumulative taxable supplies and taxable imports over the preceding 12 months, with the registration application due within 30 days after crossing the threshold; voluntary registration from OMR 19,250 (approx. USD 50,000) of taxable supplies or deductible expenses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of drummed and bagged penetration-grade bitumen is zero-rated at 0% VAT where (i) the SPC exports the goods to a destination outside the GCC territory, (ii) the goods are physically dispatched from Oman within the statutory period (generally 90 days from the tax invoice date), (iii) title and risk pass to the non-resident buyer under the agreed Incoterms, and (iv) the SPC retains the complete documentary evidence trail proving physical exit from Oman and receipt of consideration. Supplies of services directly connected to the exported cargo (such as international ocean freight and handling of bitumen in containers) also qualify for zero-rating provided no benefit is enjoyed in Oman. Sales of bitumen to another GCC member state are not zero-rated as exports for this purpose; they are treated as intra-GCC transfers subject to the customer's reverse-charge reporting with the buyer's GCC VAT number and proof of arrival.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan) duly cleared by Royal Oman Police Customs at the port or border of exit
  • Original negotiable Bill of Lading (or equivalent multimodal transport document) evidencing shipment to the non-GCC destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/TT bank credit advice proving repatriation of export proceeds into the SPC's Oman bank account
  • Zero-rated tax invoice (e-invoice) carrying the export zero-rating code, OTA fiscal mark, and cross-reference to the customs declaration number
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, Certificate of Origin, and insurance certificate evidencing Incoterms and cargo description for the containerized bitumen shipment

Oman is implementing a phased mandatory e-invoicing regime under the Tax Procedures Law (RD 54/2024) and OTA executive regulations. In-scope taxpayers must issue tax invoices in an OTA-approved structured electronic format, apply digital signatures, and transmit invoice datasets to the OTA platform or an OTA-certified clearance provider via secure API at the prescribed filing cadence; issued invoices carry an OTA digital stamp (QR-code based fiscal marking). For export zero-rated bitumen sales, the e-invoice must incorporate the customs export declaration reference and the zero-rating indicator. The phased rollout applies by taxpayer turnover, and wholesale commodity exporters are required to integrate their billing systems with the OTA API upon formal notification.

Export zero-rating claims in the wholesale petroleum-products sector attract heightened OTA post-clearance audit scrutiny. The SPC bears the burden of proof and must evidence physical export and receipt of foreign consideration within 180 days. Failure to substantiate any element triggers: (i) reversal of the zero rate with VAT assessed at 5% on the supply; (ii) retrospective clawback of input VAT credits and any refunds paid, with statutory late-payment charges accruing; (iii) fines under the VAT Law ranging from OMR 100 to OMR 50,000 per violation and up to 100% of the VAT evaded for deliberate misuse; and (iv) referral of fraudulent cases to the Public Prosecution, carrying criminal penalties including imprisonment of up to 5 years. Circular shipments, round-tripping, missing-trader chain indicators, or over-valuation of the bitumen cargo may additionally expose the sole shareholder, directors, and the licensed tax auditor to joint liability and publication by the OTA.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions, with a hard peg of 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD. No exchange control approval or foreign investment board clearance is required for bona fide trade payments, capital injections, or profit repatriation for a 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC). Dividend and profit remittance hurdles are exclusively fiscal, not exchange-control based: a 10% withholding tax applies on dividends and interest remitted to non-resident shareholders unless reduced under an applicable double tax treaty, and the remitting bank will require an audited financial statement, an Oman Tax Authority clearance certificate confirming no outstanding tax liabilities, and the resolution of the sole shareholder authorising distribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no mandated repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign-currency (primarily USD) receipts in export proceeds accounts at licensed Omani banks. However, all cross-border flows are captured for balance-of-payments statistics: banks must report inward and outward remittances through the CBO Monthly International Transactions Report (MITR) framework, and exporters transacting above OMR 10,000 equivalent are required to provide a Foreign Inward/Outward Remittance form referencing the underlying commercial contract, invoice, and bill of lading. For bitumen/asphalt containerized wholesale operations, proceeds may be held in USD without conversion to OMR, and no central bank approval is triggered.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), operating under the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000), in coordination with the Oman Tax Authority and the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP); payment clearing infrastructure is administered through the Oman Clearing System (OCS) operated by Oman Clear LLC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility. The CBO does not maintain a licensing pathway for non-bank offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) to operate independently in Oman. Under the Payment and Settlement Systems Law (Royal Decree 18/2020) and CBO e-money rules, any entity performing account origination, IBAN issuance, or payment execution in OMR must be a licensed bank, payment service provider, or licensed e-money issuer supervised by the CBO. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) cannot directly access the Oman Clearing System, cannot issue local OMR IBANs, and may not accept deposits denominated in OMR. They may be engaged only as ancillary payment-initiation or FX-conversion service providers channeling funds through a licensed Omani bank acting as the clearing and settlement bank; the SPC must maintain a primary account with a domestic tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol: the foreign shareholder transmits funds by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for aggregated transfers) to the SPC's Capital Escrow Account at the chosen Omani bank. The remittance instruction must reference the SPC legal name, Commercial Register number, and the purpose code 'CA' (Capital Contribution), with the underlying narrative 'Share Capital Injection under the Oman Commercial Companies Law'. The receiving bank will require the Foreign Inward Remittance Form, the MoCIIP capital verification record, and a copy of the SPC's incorporation certificate before crediting the capital account. For inflows exceeding OMR 10,000 equivalent, the bank applies normal Enhanced Due Diligence for the first lodgement, verifies that the sender is the registered shareholder, and cross-matches the SWIFT sender BIC with the UBO/anti-money-laundering file. The funds are then transferred internally from the capital escrow account to the operational USD/OMR current account once the CBO-relevant transaction reporting and source-of-funds documentation are completed.Outbound trade settlements use two primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for routine supplier payments, freight forwarding settlements, and port handling charges, executed in USD via the bank's correspondent network (typically J.P. Morgan, Citi, HSBC, or Standard Chartered nostro accounts) with dual-phase authentication and monthly reconciliation against the trade ledger; (2) Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (Irrevocable and optionally Confirmed) issued via SWIFT MT700 for high-value bitumen procurement from suppliers in India, UAE, and China, providing payment against presentation of the full shipping set: clean bill of lading, commercial invoice, certificate of origin, penetration-grade quality certificate (ASTM D946 / AASHTO M20), and certificate of quantity. No CBO prior approval is required for outbound trade payments, but each disbursement above OMR 10,000 must be reported on the bank's MITR filing and supported by the underlying overseas purchase contract to satisfy trade-based AML scrutiny.USD/OMR FX Forward Contracts (up to 12 months) executed with a licensed Omani bank on a deliverable basis, recognising the fixed peg of 2.6008 OMR per USD and used to lock operational cash-flow conversion certainty.Cross-Currency Swaps (USD/CNY and USD/INR) available under the bank's treasury dealing line to allow the SPC to match bitumen procurement costs in renminbi or Indian rupees while receiving USD from export sales.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) referencing INR, PKR, or LKR for regional trade counterparties whose currencies are non-fully convertible; such contracts are cash-settled in USD against reference fixings confirmed on the Reuters/EMTA page and subject to CBO prudential position limits.FX Spot Contracts executed through the licensed bank's dealing room for immediate settlement of operational foreign-currency conversions between USD, OMR, and EUR; spot trades are unrestricted and do not require CBO approval.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Omani Riyal (OMR). Transfer pricing documentation obligations apply where the taxpayer's gross revenue exceeds OMR 10 million per tax year, or where revenues from related party transactions exceed OMR 300,000 per tax year. The Oman Tax Authority issued Transfer Pricing Rules under PD 35/2020 requiring contemporaneous Local File and Master File for taxpayers meeting these thresholds; a country-by-country report is required for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding OMR 75 million (approximately EUR 750 million threshold alignment) where Oman is the group's ultimate parent jurisdiction or a secondary filing threshold applies. For a bitumen wholesale SPC below these thresholds, a simplified transfer pricing disclosure and arm's length analysis should nevertheless be maintained for audit defense.

Economic substance scope: Oman has not enacted a distinct Economic Substance Regulations regime onshore in the same manner as the UAE DIFC or Abu Dhabi Global Market. However, Oman’s Income Tax Law and anti-avoidance principles require that a taxpayer must have actual commercial substance and purpose. The OTA applies a general anti-abuse rule under the Tax Procedures Law (Royal Decree 42/2015) and transfer pricing rules expecting: (1) non-resident shareholders cannot be shell conduits; (2) the Oman SPC must maintain physical office, bank account, staff or contracted management in Oman, and decision-making in Oman; (3) '̃Substance over form' is applied to deny the 0% dividend exemption or treaty benefits where the SPC is a mere booking entity without commercial substance or where the arrangement is structured to circumvent Omani tax. Additionally, the Oman Commercial Companies Law requires the SPC to have at least one director and a resident agent/government representative; failure to evidence substance exposes the SPC to recharacterization penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Willful Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Fine of 50% of the tax understated or additional tax up to 100% under Article 33 of Royal Decree 28/2009; orders to pay the full tax differential; forfeiture of tax loss carryforward claims; criminal prosecution under the Tax Procedures Law with imprisonment for a term of up to 2 years for deliberate tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Civil restitution of the evaded tax and fines; travel bans imposed by OTA or Directorate General of Income Tax; attachment of personal bank accounts where the Omani Commercial Companies Law veil piercing applies for misuse of the limited liability shield, fraudulent intermingling of SPC accounts with the shareholder's personal account, and complicity of the director and resident agent. Criminal imprisonment applies against individuals who signed, approved, or directed the false return.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalization Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income in the amount equal to non-arm's length margin; penalty of 10% of the adjustment amount under the Oman TP Rules if contemporaneous documentation is not provided or is inadequate; disallowance of interest exceeding Oman's debt-equity thresholds and arms' length interest rate; late payment interest on adjusted tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and the commercial manager are jointly liable for penalties where they signed transfer pricing declarations or falsely certified the absence of related party transactions; the OTA may pierce the corporate veil to recover the altered tax from the foreign parent's Omani assets and may issue personal guarantee demands against directors who authorized the artificial margin structure.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of beneficial treaty access; deemed-source assessment taxing profits in Oman notwithstanding nominal trust or agency agreements; liquidation of the company under the Commercial Companies Law upon repeated violation; administrative fine of OMR 500 to OMR 5,000 in addition to tax recharacterization; de-registration of the SPC's Commercial Registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident '"commercial agent'’ (government office representative) face personal liability for the debts of the SPC under Commercial Companies Law Article 15 where the company operates as a facade or in the interest of the foreign owner; the Omani authorities may revoke residency permits and impose travel bans where they fail to present books and records during a substance audit.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Violations (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: For companies: fine of not less than OMR 5,000 and not more than OMR 50,000 per violation; suspension or revocation of commercial license; freezing of bank accounts; confiscation of the bitumen containers and proceeds; referral to the Financial Crimes Prosecution; criminal imprisonment for officers. For foreign exchange/bank payment rails: customer due diligence failures trigger additional CBO penalties of up to OMR 100,000 for the SPC if it is treated as a non-financial business and profession subject to AML registration.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and the sole shareholder are personally criminally liable where they wilfully disregard AML reporting obligations (STR filing to the Financial Intelligence Unit), benefit from the laundered trade, or cause the SPC to become a conduit for a sanctioned owner. Remedies include seizure of personal assets, travel bans under the AML law, extradition within GCC, and imprisonment up to seven years.
Failure to Keep Records and Audited Financial Statements

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of OMR 1,000 to OMR 10,000; arbitrary tax assessment computed from estimated margins if accounts are not presented; increase of assessed tax by 10%; refusal of license renewal for SPC CR by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion.

Director personal liability: Directors bear civil liability to the SPC and third-party creditors where missing accounts prevent the reconstruction of financial position; the auditor's limitation of liability is void as to fraudulent omissions; the director also becomes indefinitely liable for tax beyond the normal statute of limitations when concealment is established; travel and exit permit denial is enforceable.
