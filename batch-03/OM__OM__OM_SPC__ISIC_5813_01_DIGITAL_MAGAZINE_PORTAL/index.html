Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Authority of the Sultanate of Oman (Oman Tax Authority), established by Royal Decree No. 39/2020, succeeding the Secretariat General of Taxation; national e-administration portal: https://taxoman.gov.om.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the foreign-owned SPC under the Omani Commercial Companies Law through the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) Invest Easy portal to obtain the Commercial Registration (CR); 100% foreign ownership is allowed and no minimum paid-up capital applies. Step 2 - Register for corporate income tax with the Oman Tax Authority promptly after CR issuance (before the first taxable invoice) by filing the TIN/Tax Card application on taxoman.gov.om; attach the CR, notarized Arabic Articles of Association, passport copies and power of attorney of the shareholder and local manager, lease contract for the registered office, and bank reference letter. Step 3 - Apply for VAT registration separately when taxable supplies reach OMR 38,500 in a 12-month period, or voluntarily from OMR 19,250; a non-resident supplier without an Omani establishment may use the simplified direct VAT registration from the first Rial of remote B2C digital supplies. Step 4 - Upon approval, the Tax Authority issues the Tax Card and a single TIN used for CIT, VAT and withholding filings; the SPC then files monthly/quarterly VAT returns and the annual CIT return through the same portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the SPC is a separate juridical person and the Omani resident taxpayer, liable to corporate income tax under Royal Decree No. 28/2009 (as amended) on its taxable income, including worldwide income; no partnership-style pass-through, transparency or tax consolidation is available; shareholders are not taxed on undistributed profits.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or entity-classification election exists under Omani law; tax classification follows the statutory form. Procedural elections available: (i) voluntary VAT registration at OMR 19,250; (ii) a non-calendar fiscal year for CIT subject to Tax Authority approval; (iii) grouping/aggregation of related-party supplies for the VAT registration threshold; (iv) use of the annual VAT refund stream. OECD Pillar Two and hybrid-mismatch elections are not applicable because no implementing domestic legislation is in force.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 38,500 of annual taxable supplies (measured over the preceding 12 months or expected next 12 months). Voluntary registration: OMR 19,250. Non-resident suppliers of remote digital services (B2C) must register from the first Omani Rial of supplies; the ordinary threshold does not apply to them.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Digital magazine subscriptions, sponsored-media placements and related content-licensing supplies qualify for 0% export VAT where the place of supply is outside Oman and the conditions are evidenced: (i) B2B services supplied to a recipient established outside Oman, evidenced by the customer's foreign VAT/GST/TIN certificate or tax residence certificate; (ii) B2C e-services supplied to a consumer whose usual country of residence is outside Oman, proven by IP geolocation, device/network country codes, payment-card BIN country and billing address captured at checkout; (iii) the service is not effectively used or enjoyed in Oman; and (iv) consideration is settled into the Omani bank account (SWIFT) in foreign currency or OMR. Advertising placements sold to non-resident principals for campaigns aimed exclusively abroad are zero-rated; campaigns targeting Omani audiences are standard-rated. Supplies to recipients in other GCC states follow the destination-state/reverse-charge mechanisms and are not automatically zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (required only where physical media or collateral is exported)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill (physical exports only; not applicable to pure digital deliveries)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export consideration in Oman
  • Executed service agreement specifying the non-Omani recipient and the place-of-supply clause
  • Customer tax residence certificate or foreign VAT/GST registration certificate evidencing non-resident status
  • IP-address geolocation and payment-card BIN country logs generated by the digital portal
  • Stripe/AdSense settlement and payout reports mapping each offshore transaction to the export invoice

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API e-stamping or government clearance server is currently imposed on SPC invoices. All registered persons must nonetheless issue VAT invoices containing the statutory data fields (Arabic and English), keep machine-readable invoice registers, and e-file VAT returns via taxoman.gov.om. The Tax Authority has announced a phased national e-invoicing program with future API-based clearance and digital stamping, commencing with large taxpayers before full rollout; the SPC should therefore deploy certified e-invoicing software capable of QR-coded receipts, bulk API submission, and seamless Stripe/AdSense reconciliation. Simplified invoices are permitted for B2C supplies up to OMR 1,000 (e.g., magazine subscriptions).

Zero-rating is a strict evidentiary privilege. If the Tax Authority reassesses within the 5-year limitation window (extended to 10 years for fraud), an unsubstantiated export claim is reclassified as standard-rated, 5% VAT becomes immediately payable with late-payment penalties, and input VAT attributable to the export is clawed back. Administrative fines of up to OMR 10,000 per false invoice or record apply. Deliberate fabrication of export evidence constitutes tax evasion under Royal Decree No. 121/2020, attracting imprisonment of up to 3 years and/or fines of up to OMR 10,000, an additional penalty scaling to the tax evaded, and suspension or cancellation of the VAT registration; directors and controlling shareholders can be pursued jointly. Repeat or aggravated cases are published by the Tax Authority.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations since 1974; the Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current and capital account transactions. The currency is de jure pegged to the US dollar at 1 USD = 0.3845 OMR, with no exchange-control quotas, prior approval requirements, repatriation taxes, or capital-flow restrictions imposed by the CBO. Digital publishing revenue and sponsored media placements may be earned in foreign currency and freely converted or retained. Profit remittance to the foreign sole shareholder requires only standard documented corporate mechanics (board resolution, audited financial statements, tax clearance where applicable) and is not subject to CBO exchange-control approval. A 10% Omani withholding tax may apply to dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders unless reduced under an applicable double tax treaty; prudent treasury practice requires reserving 10% of any proposed distribution pending Omani Tax Authority verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Oman. There is no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no percentage of export or digital-service proceeds that must be converted into OMR. Advertising revenue, subscription collections, AdSense payouts and sponsorship receipts may be freely credited to the SPC's OMR or foreign-currency accounts. Banks will require accurate SWIFT purpose coding and underlying contractual evidence for statistical and AML reporting, but no CBO repatriation or surrender obligation exists.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) under the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000), supported by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) for corporate registration and foreign investment licensing. The CBO exclusively supervises banking, payment systems, foreign exchange operations and AML/CFT compliance for all onshore deposit-taking institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for primary treasury use. Offshore fintech EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut, Dukascopy and similar) do not hold a CBO licence as payment service providers or acquiring/settlement banks in Oman and cannot lawfully act as the primary settlement rail for Omani merchant traffic or trade payments. An unlicensed foreign EMI may be used only for immaterial low-value disbursements to non-Omani independent contractors, provided the ultimate operational liquidity remains in a CBO-regulated Omani bank. Routing core B2B settlements, equity injections, or significant advertising payouts through unlicensed EMIs creates an outsized risk of blocked funds, regulatory de-risking, and rejection by Omani auditors and tax authorities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant rail is a SWIFT MT103 wired from the foreign shareholder's own bank account to the SPC's Bank Muscat capital escrow account. The MT103 must carry the ISO 20022 purpose classification of direct investment equity capital, with the beneficiary field showing the full SPC legal name, Omani Commercial Registration number, and registered address. A copy of the notarized SPC charter, share-capital resolution, and shareholder identification in the bank KYC file is required before settlement. No CBO permit or exchange-control approval is needed. Once credited, the funds are journalized as share capital by the external auditor; any tax-number registration under the Oman Tax Authority and VAT registration threshold assessment should be completed to avoid mismatches on outbound bank reconciliation.Primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 messages for cross-border contractor payments, ad-tech vendor settlements, and software licensing fees. Domestic OMR payments use the CBO national clearing/RTGS channels, which corporate banking users access through ordinary bank transfers. Commercial letters of credit (MT700) are technically permissible but commercially inappropriate for digital magazine publishing and sponsored media placements, which are service flows settled at high frequency; the entity should rely instead on MT103 with beneficiary-name, address, and IBAN structured remittance information. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for primary treasury use. Offshore fintech EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut, Dukascopy and similar) do not hold a CBO licence as payment service providers or acquiring/settlement banks in Oman and cannot lawfully act as the primary settlement rail for Omani merchant traffic or trade payments. An unlicensed foreign EMI may be used only for immaterial low-value disbursements to non-Omani independent contractors, provided the ultimate operational liquidity remains in a CBO-regulated Omani bank. Routing core B2B settlements, equity injections, or significant advertising payouts through unlicensed EMIs creates an outsized risk of blocked funds, regulatory de-risking, and rejection by Omani auditors and tax authorities.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% flat rate for a tax-resident Omani Single Person Company (SPC) under the Oman Income Tax Law (RD 85/2001, as amended).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends and other profit distributions paid by an Oman-resident SPC to a non-resident shareholder. Distributions between Omani tax-resident companies or to the Oman Government are outside the withholding net.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return and final tax payment are due no later than four months after the end of the SPC's fiscal year; for a calendar-year SPC, the deadline is 30 April of the following year. Advance CIT instalments are due on 30 June and 31 December where projected annual tax liability exceeds OMR 5,000.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The TP Declaration is due on the same date as Form CIT-1, no later than four months after the fiscal year-end. Local File and Master File must be contemporaneous and available within 30 days of a Tax Authority notice.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation is triggered when the Oman-resident taxpayer's annual gross turnover exceeds OMR 5,000,000. Additionally, contemporaneous documentation is required for any related-party controlled transaction that individually exceeds OMR 500,000 per fiscal year. The Oman SPC must still maintain ordinary transfer pricing benchmarking for smaller connected-party transactions if they materially affect taxable income.

Economic substance scope: Oman has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to UAE or Bahrain. However, the Oman Tax Authority applies general anti-abuse principles, substance-over-form doctrine, and Oman's MLI principal-purpose test when deciding treaty entitlement. To sustain Oman as the place of effective management and to avoid being classified as a shell vehicle for profit stripping, the SPC must maintain a physical office, locally resident decision-makers, independent board minutes, active bank accounts, and Omani substantive control over the digital publishing, advertising inventory, and commercial contracts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Filing

Statutory sanction: The Oman Tax Authority may issue an estimated assessment, recover all unpaid tax, impose a penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution. Criminal penalties include fines up to OMR 100,000 and imprisonment for a period of up to five years under the amended Oman Income Tax Law and related anti-fraud provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors and the SPC manager are personally liable where the company is operated as a mere facade, where corporate assets are stripped before settlement, or where they knowingly signed false returns. Courts may lift the corporate veil, impose civil restitution, issue travel bans, and order freezing of personal and corporate bank accounts. A director convicted of tax fraud may be imprisoned.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: The Tax Authority will adjust the taxable profit to the arm's length amount, assess a 10% secondary penalty on the transfer pricing adjustment, and impose documentation fines in the range of OMR 5,000–10,000 for missing or inadequate TP files. Repeated offences may lead to a criminal referral for wilful tax evasion.

Director personal liability: A director who sets or approves non-arm's length pricing with a related foreign entity while holding the SPC's only management authority may be held jointly liable for the additional tax and penalties. Travel restrictions and bans on leaving Oman may be imposed during active tax investigations where evasion appears intentional.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Oman courts and the Tax Authority may disregard the SPC and tax the economic owner directly, deny DTA benefits, disallow deductible payments, and treat the arrangement as an artificial avoidance structure. The SPC may also be struck off or penalised under the Omani Commercial Companies Law for failure to carry on business with a genuine local existence.

Director personal liability: The veil piercing principle makes the sole beneficial owner and the resident nominee director civilly liable for all taxes, penalties, and interest if the SPC is proven to be a sham. The Oman Tax Authority can pursue personal bankruptcy proceedings, obtain travel bans, and impose imprisonment where the shell was used to evade tax or launder proceeds from advertising revenues.
Money Laundering and Unexplained Wealth

Statutory sanction: Under the Omani Anti-Money Laundering Law, violations attract fines of up to OMR 100,000, imprisonment of up to seven years, and confiscation of funds and instruments associated with the offence. Professional firms and payment intermediaries may be sanctioned for reporting failures. The Tax Authority may also treat suspicious revenues as undisclosed taxable income and impose up to 100% penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors are subject to personal criminal liability where they knowingly direct payments through unauthorised channels. Courts may impose asset freezing, travel bans, and seizure of passports during proceedings. A director cannot hide behind the SPC's limited liability when personal involvement in the money flow is established, and imprisonment may follow upon conviction.
