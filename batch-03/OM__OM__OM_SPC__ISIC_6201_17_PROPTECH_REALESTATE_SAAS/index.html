Governing statute

Commercial Companies Law of the Sultanate of Oman, Royal Decree No. 18/2019 (Single Person Company provisions, Articles 48-62, and applicable LLC provisions, Articles 249-268); Foreign Capital Investment Law, Royal Decree No. 50/2019; Income Tax Law, Royal Decree No. 28/2009; Value Added Tax Law, Royal Decree No. 121/2020.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up share capital under Royal Decree 18/2019. The sole shareholder must declare capital commensurate with the business purpose, fully subscribed at incorporation; no escrow account or bank capital deposit certificate is required for non-regulated IT/software activities, though the Commercial Registrar may request a capital confirmation statement.

Funding & unlock path: PACDA Fire Safety and Means-of-Escape Certificate is mandatory before staff occupancy; Muscat Municipality issues the Building Occupancy and Land Use Clearance after structural inspection under the Oman Unified Building Code (Royal Decree 55/2017). Occupational health obligations under the Labor Law (Royal Decree 35/2003) apply once the first employee is engaged, including first-aid provisioning, fire drills, and workplace safety organization.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), an independent body established under Royal Decree No. 39/2021 and successor to the Secretariat General of Taxation of the Ministry of Finance. Registration portal: OTA e-Services Tax Portal at https://taxy.gov.om (legacy gateway: eservices.tax.gov.om).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC): (1) Incorporate at the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP), obtain Commercial Registration (CR) certificate and authenticated Articles of Association; (2) Appoint an OTA-approved tax agent and file the registration application on the Tax Portal with the CR certificate, capital declaration (no minimum capital statutory requirement), registered office lease, passport copies of the shareholder/directors and POA; (3) OTA verifies and issues a Tax Card containing a single unified Tax Identification Number (TIN, 'Al-Raqm Al-Dharibi') that is concurrently used for Corporate Income Tax and VAT; (4) Corporate registration is due within 60 days of commencement of business; VAT registration is due within 30 days from the end of the month in which the mandatory threshold is exceeded or before commencement when registration is compulsory; (5) Registration e-payment and filings thereafter occur through the OTA Tax Portal; foreign entities acting without an Omani place of business must additionally apply for a non-resident VAT ID before selling via local PSPs.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The SPC (Limited Liability company with one corporate or natural shareholder, RD 18/2019 Commercial Companies Law) is a juridical person and tax resident of Oman; subject to Corporate Income Tax at 15% on worldwide income as adjusted by Omani law (RD 28/2009 as amended by RD 9/2017), with Place of Effective Management (POEM) and incorporation as residency tie-breakers. No pass-through treatment is available to an SPC; Oman-source and PE-attributable income of non-residents are taxed territorially. The PropTech SaaS scope is outside oil/gas, thus no 55% hydrocarbon rate applies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The SPC (Limited Liability company with one corporate or natural shareholder, RD 18/2019 Commercial Companies Law) is a juridical person and tax resident of Oman; subject to Corporate Income Tax at 15% on worldwide income as adjusted by Omani law (RD 28/2009 as amended by RD 9/2017), with Place of Effective Management (POEM) and incorporation as residency tie-breakers. No pass-through treatment is available to an SPC; Oman-source and PE-attributable income of non-residents are taxed territorially. The PropTech SaaS scope is outside oil/gas, thus no 55% hydrocarbon rate applies. No free-zone incentive is assumed for this entity.

Available election forms

Oman does NOT operate a US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) reduced 3% CIT rate election for qualifying small and medium enterprises with taxable income up to OMR 100,000 (per RD 9/2017 and MOF clarifications); (ii) unilateral foreign tax credit election on a per-country basis for foreign income taxes paid; (iii) VAT grouping election under the VAT Executive Regulations (RD 52/2021) with OTA approval (joint liability regime); (iv) intra-GCC VAT relief/refund election for established foreigners (copy of foreign registration + annual refund form); (v) free-zone tax holiday election (10 years, Duqm 30 years) not exercised here; (vi) no group loss relief except for specified oil and gas joint operations. Oman endorses the OECD BEPS Inclusive Framework, and GCC VAT treaty rules are applied as the primary indirect-tax anti-hybrid overlay.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration threshold: OMR 38,500 (approx. USD 100,000) of annual taxable supplies (including zero-rated export supplies; excluding exempt supplies), tested over the preceding 12 months or expected next 12 months. Voluntary registration threshold: OMR 19,250. For a PropTech SaaS exporter selling to non-resident real-estate clients via Stripe/ACH, all zero-ratable export revenue counts toward the threshold, so registration is generally compulsory once billings exceed OMR 38,500 in a rolling year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the international supply of services (export of services), including cloud-based PropTech/SaaS subscription, where: (i) the recipient is a non-resident without an establishment in Oman and is not a citizen/resident for VAT purposes; (ii) the place of supply is governed by the recipient-location rule (B2B) or effective use and enjoyment occurs wholly outside Oman (B2C); (iii) the supplier retains contemporaneous documentary evidence. For SaaS (ISIC 6201/NAICS 531390), B2B sales to remote US/foreign real-estate operators are generally outside the scope of Omani VAT (reported in the export box and zero-rated), while sales to Omani landlords or Omani payment-accepting users are standard-rated at 5%. Express statutory conditions include a written contract, foreign customer VAT-identification or residence certificate, delivery/access evidence from servers outside Oman, and proceeds received in a foreign or licensed Omani bank account in a verifiable manner.

  • OTA-compliant zero-rated tax invoice (e-invoice XML/PDF containing OTA TIN, supplier C.R. number, VAT amount '0%', and description of the SaaS subscription delivered to the offshore customer)
  • Fully executed Software-as-a-Service agreement / order form / property-management platform subscription schedule with non-resident counterparty and invoicing address outside Oman
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank credit advice from a licensed Omani or foreign bank evidencing receipt of the export proceeds within the repatriation window (for ACH/Stripe settlements: consolidated payout and deposit confirmations mapped to each subscription)
  • Non-resident customer evidence: foreign Commercial Registration, VAT registration certificate, or equivalent tax residence certificate (e.g., W-8BEN-E for US PropTech clients; UK/EU VAT certificate for overseas operators)
  • Offshore consumption and delivery evidence: SaaS access logs, server location/IP verifications, admin console data attributes proving presence and use outside Oman
  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD) and transport documents (e.g., Bill of Lading/Airway Bill) - applicability note: required ONLY when the transaction includes physical media or hardware bundled with software; for purely electronic cloud delivery this document is substituted by the digital delivery report above

Oman is implementing a phased National E-Invoicing Framework: the OTA has published technical specifications (structured XML-based e-invoices, machine-readable e-archive, API clearance to the OTA platform) and has piloted the regime with large taxpayers (annual turnover >= OMR 10 million); full mandatory application for all VAT-registered persons, including B2B cross-border SaaS supplies, is being rolled out from 2025-2027 (large-taxpayer phase already effective; SME phase subject to OTA gazettal). Until the mandate fully binds, taxpayers must issue tax invoices in Arabic or English containing all statutory elements, retain electronic records, and support real-time OTA inspection via the Tax Portal. No separate fiscal digital stamp or excise-style stamping applies to software services; API-based clearance will replace download-based e-return submission once the framework becomes mandatory for this SPC.

Under Central Bank of Oman exchange-control practice and VAT regulation, proceeds of exported services must be repatriated into an Omani bank account within 180 days; non-repatriation or late collection triggers reassessment of the supply as taxable at 5% plus interest at 15% per annum on any unpaid VAT and fixed penalties (OMR 500-5,000 per erroneous return or invoice; up to OMR 10,000 per registration breach). Fraudulent zero-rating claims (forged contracts, false import/export certificates, circular payments, sham offshore recipients) constitute VAT evasion under RD 121/2020, punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years and fine of OMR 10,000-100,000, extended look-back period of up to 10 years for deliberate concealment, retroactive clawback of credited refunds, and public Blacklist disqualification from future zero-rating or voluntary compliance schemes; OTA special audit desks emphasize invoice-cash-flow-performance congruence for 100% export digital service providers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current-account purposes under Oman's IMF Article VIII obligations, and capital-account inflows (foreign equity and shareholder loans into an SPC) are freely admitted without prior CBO licensing or exchange-control permission. The SPC may open foreign-currency accounts (USD, EUR, GBP) and repatriate profits in the original currency, subject to the bank's verification of audited financial statements, a valid tax-clearance certificate from the Oman Tax Authority, and the CBO outward-remittance declaration. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal, but a 10% withholding tax applies to dividends, royalties, management fees, and certain service fees paid to non-residents, with treaty rate reductions available under Oman's double-taxation treaty network; no additional capital-gains or repatriation levy applies to a duly capitalized SPC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; a SaaS exporter may retain USD, EUR, or GBP balances in a foreign-currency account with a CBO-licensed bank. CBO exchange-control reporting, however, requires that export proceeds be repatriated to the Omani banking system within a commercially reasonable period, with the prevailing bank practice set at 180 days for service exports; the supporting dossier consists of the signed SaaS master agreement, the Stripe/ACH settlement statements, and the periodic foreign-account declaration filed annually with the SPC's relationship bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), exercising exclusive regulatory authority under the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000), the CBO Exchange Control Rules, and the Combating Money Laundering and Terrorism Financing Law (Royal Decree 30/2016); the CBO supervises inward/outward remittance reporting, foreign-currency account eligibility, and the licensed banking system in the Sultanate of Oman, assisted by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) for corporate licensing and by the Oman Tax Authority for tax-clearance-linked remittance approvals.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 100,000 — an aggregate annual controlled-transaction threshold for maintaining Local File and Master File in Omani tax practice; statutory law does not publish a fixed Oman threshold, but the OTA will request transfer-pricing documentation for intra-group services, SaaS rights, and software licensing above this risk threshold.

Economic substance scope: Oman's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) under Royal Decree 54/2020 require entities conducting relevant activities — including headquarter, distribution, financing, leasing, and high-risk sectors — to maintain adequate direction, management, physical presence, and employment in Oman. A PropTech SaaS SPC engaged in ISIC 6201 software and real-estate property-management services is not a categorised relevant activity unless it also performs distribution or headquarter functions for a foreign group; nevertheless, the OTA will scrutinise substance to distinguish a genuine trading company from a shell, so physical premises, Omani employees, board decision materiality, and core revenue-generating functions in Oman are required.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Oman's Income Tax Law, tax evasion on conviction carries imprisonment of up to three years and/or a fine not exceeding OMR 50,000, plus the entire evaded tax, a 50% penalty on the under-assessed tax, and monthly late-payment surcharges from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the SPC's corporate veil if the sole shareholder or manager personally directed the evasion; criminal liability extends to the natural person, and civil recovery can reach the shareholder's personal assets, with travel bans and freezing orders available to the OTA during investigation.
Shell Company (No Economic Substance)

Statutory sanction: Designation as an artificial shell results in denial of tax residence, reassessment of Oman-source income, a penalty equivalent to 10% of revenue or OMR 10,000 under ESR non-compliance powers, cancellation of the commercial licence, and loss of Omani tax treaty protection.

Director personal liability: The single shareholder and authorised manager are jointly and severely liable for the shell's tax deficiencies if assets are intermingled; the OTA can issue a personal assessment, freeze personal bank accounts, and impose a five-year ban on serving as a director in Oman.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The OTA has authority to adjust income using the arm's-length standard; the adjustment attracts a 25% penalty on the additional tax, a daily late-payment surcharge, and, in case of non-disclosure of related-party transactions, an additional administrative fine of up to OMR 20,000.

Director personal liability: Managers who knowingly approved unsubstantiated intercompany prices are personally liable for the tax adjustment where they benefit directly from the shifted funds; the OTA may restrain their travel and impose a criminal penalty if the documentation is false.
Disguised Profit Repatriation / Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation under substance-over-form principles triggers the 10% withholding tax plus a penalty of 25% of the unpaid withholding; continuing non-compliance leads to daily surcharge and denial of deductions for the unpaid payment, increasing corporate income tax at 15%.

Director personal liability: The director who signs a false withholding declaration commits tax perjury and is personally liable for withholding remittances; a criminal conviction can impose imprisonment up to two years and prohibition from acting as an officer of any Omani company.
AML / Payment Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Violation of Oman's Anti-Money Laundering Law (Royal Decree 30/2016) exposes the SPC to fines up to OMR 50,000 per breach, suspension or revocation of electronic-payment authorisation, freeze of settlement accounts, and administrative fines by the National Centre for Financial Information.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be held personally criminally liable if they knew or recklessly ignored the laundering risk; penalties include imprisonment of up to seven years, confiscation of assets, and a permanent ban from financial-sector management.
