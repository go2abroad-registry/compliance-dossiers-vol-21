Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (Hay'at Al-Dhariba), established by Royal Decree No. 48/2020 as successor to the Secretariat General of Taxation; digital e-services portal: Taxoman (https://taxoman.gov.om). Export customs administration and the national single-window (Bayan) sit with the Royal Oman Police – Directorate General of Customs, which electronically shares exit data with the Tax Authority.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the Single Person Company under the Commercial Companies Law with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion via the Invest Easy portal; 100% foreign ownership is permitted for logistics/hub activity and no minimum paid-up share capital applies. Step 2 - Register for Corporate Income Tax with the Oman Tax Authority through Taxoman immediately upon commencement of business; there is no turnover threshold, and the Tax Authority issues the unified Tax Registration Number (TRN) used for both CIT and VAT. Step 3 - Complete a separate VAT registration when annual taxable supplies in Oman equal or exceed OMR 38,500 (mandatory) or OMR 19,250 (voluntary). Step 4 - Obtain a Customs operator identifier via the Bayan single-window for export declarations. Step 5 - The foreign sole member provides its home-country tax identification for dividend withholding administration; the SPC must file CIT and VAT returns electronically and designate an officer for the Tax Authority's correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the SPC is a resident juridical person and is the taxable person on its worldwide income; Oman does not permit pass-through of SPC income. Tax rate: 3% on the first OMR 30,000 of taxable income and 15% on the excess. The non-resident shareholder is taxed only on Oman-source items, such as dividends and certain cross-border payments, and is not the taxpayer in respect of the SPC's active consolidated-hub income.

Available election forms

Oman recognises no check-the-box, fiscally transparent entity, or hybrid-mismatch election; SPC classification is per se. Available statutory elections: (i) choice of the SPC's accounting financial year as the CIT tax year, subject to Tax Authority approval; (ii) carry-forward of assessed tax losses for 5 years from the loss year, subject to continuity of business and shareholder conditions; (iii) voluntary VAT registration from OMR 19,250; (iv) quarterly VAT return filing where annual taxable supplies do not exceed OMR 1,000,000, with monthly filing mandatory above that level; (v) no fiscal unity or group relief is available, so the SPC files standalone.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 38,500 of annual taxable supplies (mandatory registration); OMR 19,250 (voluntary registration). A foreign supplier has an Oman VAT nexus where it supplies B2C services performable in Oman, or where the Omani SPC receives B2B freight and consolidation inputs under reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated when physically exported from Oman within 90 days of supply; the parcel consolidation hub must hold customs exit evidence. Services are zero-rated when (a) provided to a non-resident recipient located outside Oman and the services are actually performed outside Oman, (b) directly connected to exported goods, including sorting, repacking, storage and handling of parcels ultimately removed from Oman, or (c) ancillary to the international transport of goods, including linehaul and direct-injection haulage. The hub may not zero-rate domestic sorting, or storage or handling services performed for an Omani principal or for goods not leaving Oman; such supplies bear 5% VAT. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated export activity is creditable and repayable via the VAT refund mechanism upon substantiation.

  • Customs export declaration (Bayan single-window) evidencing H.S. classification, weight and exit of each consolidated consignment
  • Bill of Lading or Master/House Airway Bill evidencing direct injection to the destination country
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 credit voucher evidencing receipt of export consideration
  • Consolidation manifest and hub gate-out/warehouse release certificate
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and overseas principal's shipping instruction

The Oman Tax Authority is implementing a phased e-invoicing programme under its digital transformation roadmap; a binding real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping module is not yet operative for logistics and parcel services. Currently the Tax Authority accepts paper, PDF and voluntary structured e-invoices through Taxoman, provided the invoice carries the mandatory VAT fields: supplier and recipient names with TRNs, sequential invoice number, supply date, description of service, consideration exclusive of VAT, VAT rate and VAT amount. Digital tax stamps are confined to excise goods, such as tobacco and energy drinks, and do not apply to parcel consolidation, sorting or linehaul services unless excised goods form part of a consolidated lot.

The Tax Authority cross-checks Bayan exit records, transport documentation dates and bank remittance evidence against VAT returns; where zero-rating evidence is absent or false, the supply is recharacterised as taxable in Oman, VAT at 5% is clawed back with statutory late-payment additions, and CIT re-assessment at 15% may follow. Administrative fines are imposed per the VAT violations schedule, with evasion punishable by imprisonment of up to 3 years and/or a fine of up to OMR 50,000 for intentionally falsified export documents. The ordinary assessment window is 5 years, extended to 10 years for fraud. Oman imposes no exchange-control or statutory FX-repatriation deadline as an Article VIII IMF current-account liberalised jurisdiction; the 180-day benchmark is observed as the documentary window for inward remittance confirmation in export substantiation and transfer-pricing reviews. Exporters must preserve the complete customs documentation bundle for at least 10 years.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is pegged to the USD (approximately 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD). Current account transactions are fully convertible and free of exchange controls; authorisation to deal in foreign exchange is reserved to licensed banks and approved exchange houses. Capital account convertibility is liberalised for foreign-owned licensed enterprises in connection with inward foreign direct investment, outward loan servicing, trade financing, and liquidation proceeds. Profit and dividend remittances are permitted without prior exchange-control approval, provided the company is tax-compliant and has submitted an audited unqualified financial statement to the Oman Tax Authority documenting distributable reserves. There are no restrictions on maintaining foreign-currency bank accounts for operational treasury management, but all repatriations must be executed via commercial banks in the Sultanate of Oman and evidenced by the underlying commercial or financial document.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman applies no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds settlement must be completed and credited to the exporting entity's bank account within 180 days from the date of the bill of lading or equivalent transport document, unless a longer period is approved by the Central Bank of Oman. Settlement in foreign currency may be retained in an OMR or foreign-currency account at a licenced commercial bank; conversion to OMR is driven by commercial need rather than a statutory requirement. The bank must be provided with the invoice, customs declaration, and air waybill or bill of lading for the underlying parcel consolidation export; non-timing compliance requires a documented justification and may trigger CBO reporting and higher scrutiny on future trade transactions.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM, WITH CONDITIONAL LEGAL CONSTRAINTS. Oman's banking and payment system is tightly regulated by the Central Bank of Oman; unauthorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) and offshore digital payment platforms cannot hold OMR-denominated deposits, issue local payment cards, or act as a primary settlement account for Oman-registered companies. They may be used only as auxiliary treasury rails for inbound collections from international e-commerce marketplaces or for outbound mass payouts to foreign contractors, provided funds are passed through a CBO-licenced Omani bank account for statutory record-keeping and tax trail. No decisive reliance on a non-Omani EMI as an isolated liquidity repository is legally viable for the Omani SPC's statutory treasury function.

Root cause: Thin capitalisation or insufficient capital to maintain the projected transaction volume

Defense evidence: Shareholder statement confirming additional paid-in capital, financing agreement, fixed-asset purchase invoices, payroll register, and a treasury projection model demonstrating that transaction volume is commensurate with the capitalised substance and normal trade payment cycles.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection from the foreign shareholder must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's corporate account in its home jurisdiction to the Omani SPC's operational account at a licensed commercial bank. The wire must clearly bear the purpose code for capital contribution and include the following reference text: "PAID-UP CAPITAL / OM_SPC / ISIC_5229_10". The bank will require a certified copy of the incorporation certificate, the shareholders resolution approving the capital injection, the company registration file held by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion, and a source-of-funds certificate confirming the commercial provenance of the monies. Under Omani law no minimum paid-up capital is formally required for the SPC, so even a nominal transfer is acceptable; however, the bank may internally apply a minimum deposit to activate the account. No pre-approval from the Central Bank of Oman is needed for the inbound remittance unless the aggregate deposit exceeds the licensing threshold for authorised money-changer activity, which is inapplicable for this treasury function.For local operational payments in OMR, use the domestic clearing rails of the Oman Clearing House (Operational Settlement System) through internet banking, direct debit, and FTS (Funds Transfer System) interfaces. To preserve liquidity and avoid FX conversion leakage, maintain foreign-currency settlement accounts at the same Omani bank for USD/EUR receipts and disbursements. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 500,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with related parties triggers the mandatory submission of the Oman Transfer Pricing Disclosure Form and contemporaneous Local File documentation; Master File preparation is additionally required for a multinational group with consolidated global revenue exceeding OMR 30 million.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not have a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, beneficial ownership requirements, and treaty abuse prevention rules require the SPC to maintain real commercial substance in Oman: physical premises, local management decisions, operational staff, and actual logistics capability. A mere foreign-owned mail-box company that lacks substance will be recharacterized and denied treaty benefits or tax transparency.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 50% of the evaded tax as an additional assessment, criminal fine of up to OMR 25,000, and imprisonment for up to three years. All underpaid tax, interest, and penalties remain payable.

Director personal liability: Directors and the manager of the SPC may be personally assessed for evaded corporate tax where the corporate veil is pierced for fraud. Personal civil restitution may be ordered, foreign travel may be banned, and responsible individuals are subject to imprisonment. Assets held through the SPC can be attached.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Tax Authority is empowered to adjust taxable income to the arm's-length position and impose a penalty of 10% of the upward adjustment or 25% of the underpaid tax. Late-payment interest at 1% per month also accrues on the deficiency.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly liable for the adjusted tax if they signed or approved false TP documentation. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed for outstanding assessments, and willful document forgery carries criminal liability.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Tax Authority may disregard the legal personality and recharacterize the operation as a foreign company carrying on business in Oman, resulting in branch taxation at a 15% rate and denial of expenses allocation. Commercial registration may be revoked, the entity struck off, and a fine of up to OMR 5,000 imposed.

Director personal liability: The sole shareholder and director of the SPC risk personal liability for all corporate obligations if the entity is treated as a sham or if continued trading without substance/in good standing exposes personal liability. A travel ban may be issued for unpaid assessments and the director may be disqualified from managing Omani entities.
AML and Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Omani Anti-Money Laundering Law include fines of up to OMR 100,000 and imprisonment of up to seven years. The financial crime court may order forfeiture of assets, closure of business, and delisting from the commercial register.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners with knowledge of or negligence in facilitating trade-based money laundering face personal criminal prosecution, personal fines, travel bans, and confiscation of personal assets. Exemptions from liability do not apply merely because the conduct was carried out through an SPC.
