Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), the national revenue authority succeeding the former Secretariat General for Taxation of the Ministry of Finance; principal e-filing and administrative portal: OTA e-Services (taxoman.gov.om), which issues the CIT Tax Card, VAT registration certificate, and accepts CIT/VAT returns and refund applications. No sub-federal tax authority exists; taxation is administered nationally (unitary system).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the SPC as a Single Person Company (LLC) at the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) under the Commercial Companies Law (RD 18/2019) and the Foreign Capital Investment Law (RD 50/2019); the sole foreign corporate shareholder may hold 100% and no minimum share capital is imposed. Step 2 - After issuance of the Commercial Registration (CR), register the SPC with the OTA within the statutory period (generally within three months from the commencement of activity or CR date) to obtain the corporate Tax Card (TIN); the registration is filed online via the OTA e-Services portal with the CR certificate, constitutional deed, leases (if any), and shareholder identity documents. Step 3 - VAT registration: apply for VAT registration (in addition to the CIT TIN) if the rolling 12-month taxable supplies exceed OMR 38,500 or are expected to exceed that amount in the next 30 days; zero-rated export supplies count toward the threshold. The VAT registration certificate and VAT identification number are issued electronically within the statutory processing window. Step 4 - Update the CR and OTA records for any change in the SPC's manager, registered address, or ownership structure; for a foreign-owned SPC, no separate federal or regional tax permit is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The Omani SPC is a juridical person resident in Oman and is taxed at entity level on worldwide income under the Income Tax Law (RD 23/2023, effective tax years beginning 1 January 2024; previously RD 28/2009, as amended). No pass-through, look-through, or fiscal transparency applies; the SPC files its own CIT return at the flat 15% rate (foreign-owned SPCs are outside the old 3% SME concession, which in any event was phased out). The SPC is not a tax-exempt zone vehicle: even if the business were licensed inside a special economic zone (e.g., Duqm), the CIT exemption would require a separate incentive grant, and no free-zone tax holiday applies to the standard SPC at a mainland address. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Omani SPC is a juridical person resident in Oman and is taxed at entity level on worldwide income under the Income Tax Law (RD 23/2023, effective tax years beginning 1 January 2024; previously RD 28/2009, as amended). No pass-through, look-through, or fiscal transparency applies; the SPC files its own CIT return at the flat 15% rate (foreign-owned SPCs are outside the old 3% SME concession, which in any event was phased out). The SPC is not a tax-exempt zone vehicle: even if the business were licensed inside a special economic zone (e.g., Duqm), the CIT exemption would require a separate incentive grant, and no free-zone tax holiday applies to the standard SPC at a mainland address. If the SPC belongs to an MNE group with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more, an Omani domestic minimum top-up tax (GloBE/Pillar Two) may apply from fiscal years aligned with the 2025 implementation timetable, but this does not change the entity's classification.

Available election forms

Oman has no check-the-box or entity classification election; all incorporated SPCs are automatically taxable companies. Available elections and claims relevant to the SPC: (i) participation exemption - elective claim for exemption of qualifying dividends and capital gains on disposals of shares in a subsidiary held at least 12 months with at least a 10% interest, where the subsidiary is subject to a comparable corporate tax rate, as provided under RD 23/2023; (ii) VAT voluntary registration (turnover between OMR 19,250 and OMR 38,500) and, where approved by the OTA, VAT grouping of related resident persons; (iii) tax depreciation: election to adopt straight-line tax depreciation rates for fixed assets rather than accounting life; (iv) loss utilization: no consolidation election exists, but unrelieved tax losses may be carried forward (up to 10 years under RD 23/2023, subject to continuity-of-business conditions). Hybrid mismatch or OECD anti-hybrid elections are not available in Oman's domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration when taxable supplies in Oman exceed OMR 38,500 over any rolling 12-month period, or where the 30-day forward-looking test indicates the threshold will be met; voluntary registration from OMR 19,250. Zero-rated exports of newswire/data services are taxable supplies and therefore count toward the threshold calculation; exempt financial services do not. Registration application must be lodged within 30 days of the date the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of services: a supply of digital financial-market news/data services by the SPC is zero-rated where (i) the recipient belongs in a non-Omani/non-GCC territory (per the place-of-establishment/fixed-establishment rules), (ii) the place of supply is outside Oman - i.e., the effective use and enjoyment of the API feed occurs outside Oman, and (iii) the supply is not physically performed in Oman. If the customer is registered for VAT in another GCC state and discloses its effective GCC VAT ID, Omani VAT is still 0% (reverse charge in the customer's state). Zero-rating is denied if the contract/API delivery points to Omani consumption, e.g., the customer's Omani branch or a Gulf-based trading desk receiving the feed in Muscat. Substantiation requirements for each export invoice: recipient legal name, non-Omani establishment address, copy of the foreign VAT ID or foreign commercial registration/certificate of residence, executed subscription/API services agreement, delivery and usage logs showing off-shore consumption, and the bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of the subscription fee. For goods components (if the feed is delivered via branded terminals/printed media [not typical]), the customs export declaration chain applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (customs gateway barcode for goods-based exports; for pure digital service exports this is replaced by the export service contract, commercial invoice, and supply/delivery log)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (for physical media or terminal hardware movements; for API/digital delivery, a server dispatch log or digital delivery receipt is substituted)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice (confirmed by the receiving Omani bank, evidencing the foreign-currency subscription payment was received and converted/credited to the SPC)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or QR-code mandate for standard B2B tax invoices is in force for the current VAT regime. The SPC must issue a full B2B tax invoice within 14 days of the supply date; the invoice may be issued in paper or electronic form (PDF/XML), and e-archiving is required. The OTA has publicly signaled a phased national e-invoicing mandate (structural reform road map), but until the go-live, continuous integration with a Government clearance gateway is not required. For the newswire API business, the tax invoice should state the subscription period, the applicable rates (5% for Omani customers; 0% for export supplies), and the recipient's VAT ID where the recipient is a registered GCC taxable person, to support reverse-charge treatment.

Export zero-rating claims are high-risk in OTA audits. Documentation must be contemporaneous; missing recipient-residence or effective-use evidence triggers immediate input VAT disallowance and re-issued VAT assessment at 5%. Under RD 121/2020 (as amended), knowingly or negligently submitting false zero-rating/export documentation subjects the SPC to a penalty of up to 100% of the evaded VAT, fixed administrative fines (statutory caps generally up to OMR 10,000 for registration/record-keeping violations), and, for tax-evasion offenses, criminal liability including imprisonment; the OTA may reassess within the statutory limitation period (5 years, extended in fraud cases). Retroactive clawback also applies to input VAT recovered on costs that funded the export sale if the underlying export evidence is invalidated. For CIT, false export characterization does not affect CIT rate, but a tax shortfall triggers a monthly late-payment addition (up to 1% per month on the unpaid tax) plus reassessment; misstatement penalties under RD 23/2023 apply for fraudulent returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman maintains an open, fully convertible current account regime; the Omani Rial (OMR) is pegged to the US dollar (USD) at 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD. There is no legal restriction on the conversion or repatriation of profits, dividends, or liquidation proceeds for fully foreign-owned Single Person Companies (SPCs), provided that all OTA tax liabilities and Zakat/prepaid charges are settled, and the MoCIIP's electronic Commercial Registry records are compliant. The capital account is de facto liberalised for 100% foreign ownership under the Foreign Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2019, effective January 2021), with no mandatory capital conversion requirement for non-oil/export services. However, banks may impose a temporary compliance hold on first capital injection until the source-of-wealth file is fully cleared and the MoCIIP investment certificate is validated. There are no divergent restrictions between current and capital account for a low-risk financial news aggregation business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement in Oman for either goods or services. All overseas sale receivables for the news/API subscription services may be retained in retained foreign currency accounts at licensed Omani banks or converted to OMR at market rates. In practice, the Central Bank of Oman requires licensed banks to submit daily spot FX turnover data, including any service-export receipts, for market monitoring; SPCs are not obligated to repatriate or convert 100% of their export proceeds, but must report the receipts on their audited financial statements for corporate tax and transfer-pricing benchmarking. For service exports exceeding OMR 10,000 equivalent, the remitting bank is required to verify the underlying invoice and contractual documentation under anti-money laundering (AML) procedures.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) holds exclusive regulatory competence over foreign exchange operations, payment systems, and the licensing of commercial banks and payment providers. The Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) administers foreign investment registration and capital flow documentation; the Oman Tax Authority (OTA) supervises tax clearance for outbound profit remittances and capital repatriation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium direct feasibility. The Central Bank of Oman has not issued a standalone electronic-money-institution (EMI) or payment-service-operator licence to any offshore or foreign-incorporated digital EMI. Therefore, non-bank desert-based EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) cannot legally maintain settlement infrastructure in Oman without a local commercial bank sponsor. Stripe is not a CBO-licensed payment bank; however, Stripe may be used as an international card-facilitating gateway for the SPC's client subscriptions, subject to a fallback remittance rail: Stripe's foreign PSP settlement account must be a designated USD account in a supported jurisdiction, from which funds are then wired via MT103 to the SPC's Omani bank. The SPC should contract with a CBO-regulated local payment facilitator (e.g., Telr or PayTabs, acting as an integrated payment broker) to obtain merchant-of-record status and ensure settlement directly into the Omani operational account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 30,000 (approximately USD 78,000) in aggregate annual related-party transactions per counterparty triggers the mandatory preparation and filing of transfer pricing documentation consistent with the Oman Tax Authority Transfer Pricing Guidelines. Taxpayers above this threshold must complete the dedicated related-party transaction schedules in the Corporate Income Tax Return and maintain a contemporaneous Local File; group entities in multinational groups are expected to maintain a Master File and, where group consolidated revenue exceeds the OECD Country-by-Country Reporting threshold, file the CbC notification. Transactions below the threshold remain subject to arm's-length adjustment on audit.

Economic substance scope: Oman applies the substance-over-form doctrine and a statutory General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR): the Tax Authority may recharacterize, disregard, or adjust any arrangement where an SPC lacks genuine commercial substance, including entities incorporated in Oman but managed, staffed, or controlled exclusively from abroad. Oman has not enacted a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) framework; however, the Income Tax Law taxes the 'place of effective management' and requires profits to be attributed to real functions, assets, and risks. For an SPC conducting financial market news aggregation and syndication, demonstrable local substance means Omani-registered premises, resident operational and editorial staff, data-license and API-infrastructure contracts executed from Oman, and board decisions documented in Oman. Absence of such substance invites GAAR recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, and potential classification of the SPC as a sham shell, with retroactive tax assessments and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to OMR 20,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years, in addition to full recovery of the evaded tax, a 1% monthly late-payment surcharge, and a civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax shortfall.

Director personal liability: Directors and the non-resident sole shareholder who sign or cause false returns to be filed are jointly and severally liable for the SPC's unpaid tax; the OTA may attach personal assets, issue travel bans, and refer the individuals for criminal prosecution.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization: the whole or part of the arrangement is disregarded, profits are adjusted upward, treaty or exemption benefits are denied, and a penalty of up to 100% of the adjusted tax plus 1% monthly interest is imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for the penalty tax, disqualified from incorporating or managing Omani companies for a fixed period, and in aggravated cases charged with tax evasion carrying imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Upward transfer pricing adjustment, disallowance of the excessive deduction, a fixed penalty of OMR 1,000 for non-filing or incomplete TP schedules, and additional monthly surcharges on the adjusted tax; transaction-level penalties may be applied on repeat offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved artificial intra-group pricing without functional or economic substance expose themselves to personal liability for the adjusted tax under the veil-piercing provisions, plus travel bans pending payment.
Dividend and Cross-Border WHT Failure

Statutory sanction: The SPC, as withholding agent, is personally liable for the full unrecovered WHT amount, plus the 1% monthly late-payment surcharge and a fixed penalty for late remittance; the non-deductibility of the underlying expense is asserted where applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the un-withheld or mis-relieved WHT; enforcement includes garnishment of bank accounts, attachment of personal assets, and a travel ban against the directors and the non-resident sole shareholder until the liability is settled.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to OMR 100,000 for UBO non-disclosure and AML record-keeping breaches, suspension of the Commercial Register license, and referral to the Public Prosecutor under the Omani Anti-Money Laundering Law (RD 30/2016); proceeds of the suspected offense are subject to confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors and the beneficial owner face imprisonment of up to 3 years for willful AML violations, personal monetary fines, seizure of personal assets, and a travel ban pending investigation and trial.
