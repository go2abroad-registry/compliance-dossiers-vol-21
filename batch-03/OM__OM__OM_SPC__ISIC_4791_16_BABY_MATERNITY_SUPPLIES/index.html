Governing statute

Oman Companies Law promulgated by Royal Decree No. 18/2019, as amended by Royal Decree No. 68/2020, particularly Chapter Five on Single Person Companies (Articles 81-95), Articles 13, 24-25 on formation, and Article 195 on mandatory commercial registration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum and Articles of Association (MOA/AOA) in bilingual Arabic-English form, notarized before a Notary Public of the Ministry of Justice and Legal Affairs and submitted through the InvestEasy portal.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Single Person Company under RD 18/2019; the sole shareholder must fully pay the capital amount fixed in the MOA/AOA. No escrow or bank-capital-blocking account rule applies to a non-regulated retail SPC.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Clearance Certificate from the Civil Defense and Ambulance Authority (Royal Oman Police) confirming fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and proper storage of flammable packing materials; structural load inspection is required for the storage rack system; if permanent staff exceed 10 persons, approval of the internal occupational health and safety plan by the Ministry of Labour is required.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Single Person Company under RD 18/2019; the sole shareholder must fully pay the capital amount fixed in the MOA/AOA. No escrow or bank-capital-blocking account rule applies to a non-regulated retail SPC.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) — official e-portal: https://taxoman.gov.om; CIT and VAT administration, enforcement and e-services via the Oman Tax e-Portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned SPC is incorporated with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) to obtain the Commercial Registration (CR). The OTA automatically registers the SPC for Corporate Income Tax (CIT) and assigns a Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon CR issuance. For VAT, an application is filed separately via the OTA e-Portal, requiring: (i) CR certificate, (ii) memorandum and articles of association, (iii) passport copy of the foreign sole shareholder, (iv) proof of local address, and (v) 12-month taxable turnover forecast. The OTA will issue a VAT registration number (VRIN) after review; a tax representative may be required if the shareholder is a non-resident and the SPC has no readily accessible local management.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned SPC is incorporated with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) to obtain the Commercial Registration (CR). The OTA automatically registers the SPC for Corporate Income Tax (CIT) and assigns a Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon CR issuance. For VAT, an application is filed separately via the OTA e-Portal, requiring: (i) CR certificate, (ii) memorandum and articles of association, (iii) passport copy of the foreign sole shareholder, (iv) proof of local address, and (v) 12-month taxable turnover forecast. The OTA will issue a VAT registration number (VRIN) after review; a tax representative may be required if the shareholder is a non-resident and the SPC has no readily accessible local management.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Neither the Omani tax law nor the OTA provides a 'check-the-box' election or pass-through election for SPCs. The entity is taxed on its profits at CIT rates of 3% on taxable income up to OMR 30,000 and 15% above that threshold; no hybrid mismatch election under BEPS Action 2 is available; the only optional election relevant is voluntary VAT registration once taxable supplies exceed OMR 19,250.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration is required when taxable supplies (excluding exempt supplies, excluding capital assets) exceed OMR 38,500 within any 12-month period; voluntary registration is available when taxable supplies exceed OMR 19,250. For non-resident sellers of baby/maternity goods into Oman, the OTA applies the same OMR 38,500 cumulative turnover threshold, and may require a tax representative where no fixed establishment exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to export of goods where the seller exports goods outside Oman (outside the GCC, or to non-GCC destinations) as evidenced by customs declarations; to services supplied to a non-resident if not effectively used or enjoyed in Oman; to international transport and related services. For the e-commerce SPC, shipments of baby and maternity supplies to foreign customers will be zero-rated only if physical exports are documented and leave Oman within the statutory period (normally 90 days), with proof that the beneficial recipient is located abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List with HS codes
  • Courier/Carrier Proof of Export (e.g., airway bill or master bill of lading)
  • Bank Credit Advice / Final Inward Remittance Certificate

Oman has not yet introduced a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance / e-invoicing stamping regime for all taxpayers; however, the OTA is developing a national e-invoicing framework and has powers to require API-based data transmission by large taxpayers. SPCs must issue compliant electronic tax invoices (with all mandatory VAT fields), maintain actual e-invoice data in structured XML/JSON format, and grant OTA digital access upon notice. Until mandatory e-invoicing is fully enforced, paper/PDF invoices are accepted if they contain the statutory particulars.

Under the Oman VAT Law (RD 121/2020) Arts. 57 and 60, a false zero-rating claim constitutes tax evasion or willful misdeclaration. The OTA can re-assess the VAT at 5% on the export value, impose a late-payment penalty of 1% per month or part thereof (capped at 100% of tax), and levy administrative fines up to OMR 100,000; the responsibility extends to imprisonment not exceeding 3 years for individuals involved. The OTA may retroactively claw back any input VAT credits previously recovered on goods/services attributable to the claimed export and may extend the ordinary 5-year assessment period to 10 years for fraudulent cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. Oman applies a liberalized exchange-control regime, and no general CBO approval is required for inward equity injection, profit repatriation, or capital withdrawal by a foreign-owned SPC. However, all cross-border payments must pass through licensed banks, be supported by genuine commercial or investment documentation, and satisfy CBO/FATF anti-money-laundering, sanctions-screening, and statistical reporting obligations. Profit remittances are subject to the SPC's audited accounts, Oman Tax Authority clearance, and the absence of outstanding CBO or government levies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose any statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters must ensure that proceeds are received through a licensed Omani bank, or through an approved foreign-currency account where prior CBO authorization exists, and must retain underlying trade documentation for CBO and tax audit purposes. Inward receipts are reported as part of Oman's balance-of-payments statistics and AML/CFT cross-border transfer monitoring framework.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) in coordination with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) for foreign direct investment registration and capital entry monitoring.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital EMI and e-wallet operators in Oman. CBO licensing is mandatory for payment service providers and stored-value facilitators under Oman's Payment Service Provider regulations. Offshore EMIs do not receive direct access to Oman's national clearing and settlement infrastructure and can only provide services through a CBO-licensed sponsor bank. A local branch or subsidiary, tangible economic substance, and CBO authorisation are required in virtually all cases.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing is executed via SWIFT MT103, with MT202 for correspondent cover where amounts exceed local thresholds, from the shareholder's banking relationship to the SPC's OMR or foreign-currency account at a licensed Omani bank. The payment narrative must identify the remitter, the SPC, and the purpose as 'Equity capital contribution for Omani SPC – online trade in maternity and infant products', and include the MOCIIP FDI registration or investment approval number where available. The receiving Omani bank will verify the SPC's commercial registration, match the beneficiary name, apply CBO cross-border reporting and sanctions filters, and may request the contribution agreement, board resolution, and audited financial statements before crediting the capital account.Outbound disbursements for imported baby and maternity supplies are settled through authenticated SWIFT MT103 messages (or MT101/MT202 for cover) and bank-issued documentary instruments, principally irrevocable Letters of Credit under UCP600 for large supplier transactions and Documentary Collections for recurring replenishment. For open-account supplier payments, the Omani bank requires import documentation, commercial invoices, packing lists, bill of lading or airway bill, cargo insurance certificates, and customs declarations. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions triggers mandatory Local File and Oman Master File preparation (per Oman Income Tax Law and TP Rules); lower threshold of OMR 250,000 applies for related-party services; CbCR filing obligation arises for groups consolidated revenue above OMR 30,000,000 with the ultimate parent in Oman, or for Omani entities designated as surrogate parent constituents.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not have an ESR notice regime identical to UAE ESR, but the Omani income tax system requires genuine economic activity and risk-bearing capacity for a foreign-owned SPC. The OTA may deny tax residence claims, treaty benefits, intercompany payments, or loss carryforward where the SPC does not maintain a fixed physical office, employs sufficient staff in Oman, exercises core decision-making (logistics, procurement, sales control) locally, and documents the actual assumption of risks. The 'substance over form' doctrine is applied through the anti-abuse rules of the Income Tax Law and the OECD Inclusive Framework; a passive SPC serving only as invoice conduit will be treated as a shell and payments to it may be disregarded.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to three years and a fine not exceeding OMR 100,000 under the Omani Income Tax Law as amended by Sultan Decree 28/2009; also restitution of unpaid tax plus 15% negligent misstatement penalty and non-deductibility of the underlying expense. Criminal prosecution may be instigated by the Public Prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors of the SPC who knowingly sign a false return, direct concealment of revenue, or divert corporate payments for personal benefit face civil restitution for the unpaid corporate tax, freezing of personal bank accounts, travel bans from Oman, and the possibility of a default imprisonment sentence until the tax debt is settled. The Omani Court of Appeal has upheld piercing of the corporate veil in cases where the SPC was used as a façade for the shareholder's personal tax fraud.
Shell Company / Zero-Substance Entity

Statutory sanction: The OTA may disregard the SPC’s separate personality and assess tax on the foreign parent directly in Oman, retroactively disallow all intercompany deductions, impose the 2% monthly surcharge on all deemed undeclared profits, and revoke the commercial registration through the Ministry of Commerce. The entity may lose all treaty benefits and be deemed a 'transparent' taxpayer.

Director personal liability: A director who allows the company to be managed exclusively abroad or fails to maintain local records and staff will be personally responsible for the enterprise tax arrears to the extent the company is unable to pay. Travel bans and asset confiscation orders can be issued under the Enforcement Law, and the director may be disqualified from operating in Oman for a period of five years.
AML / Economic Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Heavy fines under the Omani Anti-Money Laundering Law (Sultan Decree 30/2016) ranging from OMR 10,000 to OMR 1,000,000, confiscation of equipment and bank accounts, and imprisonment of directors up to seven years. Repeated violations also trigger immediate revocation of the commercial registration and exclusion from the Chamber of Commerce.

Director personal liability: Company directors are individually and severally liable for AML compliance failures; they may face criminal conviction, disqualification as company directors in Oman or any GCC state, seizure of personal assets, and if a cross-border conspiracy is proven, up to 10 years' imprisonment and extradition risk under mutual legal assistance treaties.
