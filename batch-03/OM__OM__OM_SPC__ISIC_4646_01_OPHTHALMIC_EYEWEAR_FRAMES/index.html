Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) - هيئة الضرائب; unified national tax e-portal and e-services platform: https://taxes.gov.om

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the Single Person Company (SPC) through the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) 'Invest Easy' portal to obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate. Step 2 - File the online tax registration application with the OTA using the CR, constitutional documents, shareholders'/directors' passport copies, leased premises contract (Ejara) and, if required, audited financial projections; the OTA issues a single Tax Identification Number (TIN) for corporate income tax, VAT and excise duties. Step 3 - Submit the VAT registration application through the OTA VAT module where annual taxable supplies equal or exceed the OTA threshold of OMR 38,000 (mandatory) or OMR 19,000 (voluntary); the OTA issues a VAT registration certificate with a separate VAT ID linked to the same TIN. Step 4 - Update the OTA profile after any change in activity, ownership, or branch openings within the statutory notification period.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the foreign-owned SPC is a separate tax-resident juridical person liable to Omani corporate income tax (currently 15% on taxable income; certain prescribed categories may apply) on Oman-source and attributable foreign income; no pass-through or fiscal-transparency regime applies to foreign shareholders, and Oman does not levy shareholder-level income tax.

Available election forms

Oman has no US-style 'check-the-box' or hybrid-mismatch election. The available statutory elections/arrangements in practice are: (a) selection of the SPC's fiscal year-end (calendar-year default; a different year-end requires prior OTA approval); (b) election to adopt optional capital-allowance/depreciation methods within the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 28/2009, as amended) boundaries; (c) application of the 5-year tax-loss carry-forward regime with continuity-of-activity conditions; (d) application for a binding advance tax ruling or interpretation ruling from the OTA on the treatment of a specific arrangement; and (e) for VAT, option to self-assess import VAT and account for output VAT on the accrual/adjusted basis as prescribed in the Unified VAT Agreement and Oman VAT Law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 38,000 (annual taxable supplies in Oman); Voluntary registration: OMR 19,000; non-resident businesses supplying taxable goods/services in Oman must register regardless of threshold; imports into Oman are taxable without threshold exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A wholesale exporter of ophthalmic eyeglass frames may apply 0% VAT if: (a) the SPC is VAT-registered and issues a proper tax invoice showing the 0% charge; (b) the goods are physically exported from Oman to a destination outside the GCC and this is substantiated by customs documentation; (c) export occurs within 90 days of the supply date (or within an extended period approved by the OTA); (d) the buyer is not a VAT-registered person in Oman or the GCC; and (e) the SPC retains complete transport and payment evidence in its VAT records. Offshore services supplied to a non-resident customer for the benefit of that customer outside Oman may also be zero-rated under the Regulation 52 type conditions.

  • Certified Customs Export Declaration (Bayan) endorsed by the Royal Oman Police Customs
  • Original Bill of Lading / Multimodal or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Oman
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT/MT103 credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in Oman
  • Valid VAT registration certificate of the Omani exporter and the original zero-rated VAT tax invoice
  • Signed commercial contract/purchase order, packing list and Certificate of Origin
  • GCC/ETA movement proof where transiting through another GCC state to a non-GCC destination

Oman does not currently enforce a real-time continuous-transaction-control (CTC) e-invoice clearance or digital stamping system for general OMR Vatable supplies. VAT-invoicing obligations under the Executive Regulations require a sequential Arabic-compliant VAT invoice (with seller/buyer TINs, taxable amount, VAT rate and amount) issued within the prescribed period; OTA has implemented fully electronic tax return filing and is developing a phased e-invoicing data-exchange framework. Electronic fiscal marks exist only for excise-controlled products (e.g., tobacco or alcohol), not for ophthalmic frame wholesale supplies.

The OTA may conduct refund-audits over a 5-year limitation period; any zero-rated export claim found unsupported, artificially arranged, or fraudulently documented is retrospectively clawed back together with interest, administrative penalties (from OMR 50 to OMR 1,000 per violation or up to 100% of the VAT amount involved), blacklisting and publication of the taxpayer in OTA enforcement notices; deliberate evasion, forgery of customs export documents, or false refund claims additionally expose directors to criminal liability, imprisonment, suspension of the Commercial Registration, and prohibition from obtaining future tax clearances.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current-account transactions, and the capital account is liberalized with no multiple-currency practices or exchange restrictions. Non-resident shareholders of the SPC may remit profits, dividends, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds through the licensed banking system under the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2019) and Ministerial Decision 209/2020, provided the SPC holds a General Foreign Investment Certificate issued by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP). Remittance is conditioned on full settlement of corporate income tax liabilities, filing of audited financial statements, and obtaining a tax clearance certificate from the Omani Tax Authority. The OMR is pegged to the US dollar at a fixed rate, eliminating translation risk for USD-denominated trade settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The CBO does not mandate conversion or surrender of export proceeds into OMR. Exporters may retain export receipts in OMR or in foreign-currency accounts with licensed banks; however, all export proceeds must be repatriated through the Omani banking system and credited to the exporter''s account within six months from the date of shipment or within the contractual credit term, whichever is earlier. Receipts must be reported to the CBO for balance-of-payments compilation when formally requested. No fixed percentage of mandatory conversion applies.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), the single monetary and foreign-exchange authority under the CBO Law (Royal Decree 114/2000); all exchange-control administration and licensing of foreign exchange activities is vested exclusively in the CBO.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. The CBO does not operate a licensing regime for foreign digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs), and offshore EMIs cannot access local clearing, hold omnibus accounts, or issue Omani-domiciled IBANs. A non-resident EMI may be used only as a supplementary collection channel for platform-based payments, and any payment service provider must be registered under the National Payment Systems Law (Royal Decree 18/2019) and the CBO Payment Service Provider Regulations, which in practice imposes a local establishment and full AML/CFT licensing burden. Consequently, the SPC''s primary treasury rails should rely on licensed Omani banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign direct investment capital for the SPC must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 in USD or OMR to a licensed Omani commercial bank, with the purpose code classified as 'P801 - Direct Investment / Equity Capital' (or the equivalent CBO balance-of-payments coding, as directed by the beneficiary bank). The remittance instruction must reference the SPC name, Commercial Registration number, and the purpose 'Share Capital Injection under General Foreign Investment Certificate No. [X]'. Funds are credited to a temporary capital-subscription escrow account pending issuance or verification of the MOCIIP General Foreign Investment Certificate, after which they are released to the operational OMR/USD account. The beneficiary bank automatically files the Inward Remittance Notice with the CBO, satisfying the regulatory reporting requirement; no separate prior CBO approval is required.Payments to overseas acetate eyeglass frame suppliers and optical component vendors are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 bank telegraphic transfers for advance-purchase or open-account settlements. Large-volume import contracts are settled through Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit issued under UCP600, either sight or 30-to-90-day usance, with confirmation capability through the issuing Omani bank. OMR-denominated legs clear through the Oman Clearing and Settlement System; USD and other foreign-currency legs clear via the SWIFT network and correspondent banking. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% flat corporate income tax rate on taxable income of companies, effective under Royal Decree No. 9/2023 for tax years commencing on or after 1 January 2024.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding tax on gross dividends and profits distributed by an Oman-resident company to a non-resident shareholder, introduced by Royal Decree No. 9/2023 and effective for distributions relating to tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2024.
