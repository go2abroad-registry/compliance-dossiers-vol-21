Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum subscribed or paid-up capital for an SPC under Royal Decree 18/2019; the sole member fixes the capital in the MoA. For the discretionary portfolio-management licence, the Financial Services Authority (FSA) imposes a minimum regulatory capital (typically not less than OMR 250,000 or such higher amount as FSA determines), which must be deposited with a Central Bank of Oman (CBO) regulated bank and evidenced by a certificate; pending licence issuance the capital remains held in a blocked escrow-style account releasable only with FSA consent.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), established as the independent national tax administration by Royal Decree 12/2023, succeeding the Secretariat General of Taxation within the Ministry of Finance; exclusively competent for corporate income tax (Royal Decree 28/2009 as amended), Value Added Tax (Royal Decree 121/2020 effective 16 April 2021), selective/excise tax, withholding tax compliance, and taxpayer registration. Unified electronic portal: 'Emtaaz' at https://emtaaz.ota.gov.om, interoperable with the Invest Easy company registration portal, the MOCIIP commercial register, and the Bayan customs clearance platform; the OTA issues the TIN, the annual tax card, VAT certificate, and export clearance attestations. No sub-national or municipal tax authority exists; the Sultanate is a Unitary_National jurisdiction for indirect tax purposes.

E-government filing portal

Precondition: a foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) engaged as a Multi-Family Office (MFO) must first obtain the specialized statutory license from the Financial Services Authority (FSA) under Royal Decree 30/2023, or the Central Bank of Oman where deposit-taking or custodial activities are combined, because the tax file will not be approved without the underlying CR and FSA license. Step 1: incorporate the SPC with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) through Invest Easy; reserve the name and file the single-member LLC constitutional documents under the Commercial Companies Law (Royal Decree 18/2019); obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate. Step 2: within 30 days of CR issuance, apply through Emtaaz for income tax registration / the tax card; upload the CR, audited incorporation documents, the 100% foreign shareholder's passport and utility-lease evidence of the Oman office, the FSA license, the AML/CFT compliance framework, and bank reference from the Omani custodian bank; OTA then issues the Omani TIN and annual tax card. Step 3: VAT registration through the same Emtaaz profile is mandatory within 30 days after taxable supplies exceed OMR 38,500 in the current or immediately preceding 12-month period, or within 30 days after the SPC expects to exceed that threshold; the OTA issues the VAT certificate with the VAT identification number. Step 4: supplementary registrations include PASI social insurance, the Municipality fee account, and Oman Chamber of Commerce membership; the tax identification is automatically suspended if the FSA license or CR lapses, so the SPC must keep the professional license current at all times.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Omani SPC is a separate juridical and tax person, wholly distinct from its non-resident foreign shareholder; it is taxed as a resident Omani company under Article 2 of the Income Tax Law (RD 28/2009 as amended), with the headline corporate income tax rate of 15% applied to its worldwide/territorial profits arising from services managed, performed, and delivered in Oman. No pass-through treatment is available; the MFO cannot be treated as a transparent partnership or disregarded entity; there is no consolidated/group filing regime, so each SPC files separately. Territorial_Scope note: a resident SPC may exclude demonstrably non-Omani portfolio income only where management and control are exercised outside Oman and revenues are not attributable to Omani activity, but discretionary portfolio advisory physically performed by Oman-based investment staff remains Oman-sourced. Tax_Exempt_Zone: the mainland SPC is not automatically within the OPAZ free-zone regime; an MFO would not ordinarily qualify for free-zone income tax holidays (10-30 years) because financial services are not a qualifying core free-zone activity unless specific OPAZ approval and physical free-zone substance are first obtained, with all VAT and CIT consequences confirmed by binding OTA rulings.

Available election forms

Oman does not operate a US-style check-the-box or tax classification election; entity tax status follows the legal form. Available statutory elections and administrative mechanisms: (i) VAT group registration under Article 9 of the VAT Law (RD 121/2020) and Article 14 of the VAT Executive Regulations, allowing the SPC and related Omani-resident entities with 50% or greater common control to be treated as a single VAT taxable person so that intra-group supplies are disregarded; (ii) voluntary VAT registration at the OMR 19,250 annual threshold; (iii) VAT special scheme for imported services under reverse-charge mechanisms (no Omani registration needed for the non-resident supplier); (iv) income tax depreciation method election between straight-line and declining-balance methods, applied consistently for fixed assets; (v) automatic five-year tax loss carry-forward under Article 35 of the Income Tax Law, with no formal election and no carry-back; (vi) accounting period election to a non-calendar fiscal year, subject to OTA approval; (vii) free-zone incentive application filed through OPAZ where the MFO can prove qualifying industrial/logistics activity; (viii) treaty relief / double-taxation-avoidance election for the foreign shareholder is a separate claim under Oman's tax treaty network (currently limited treaties; Oman's domestic law also grants relief for foreign tax paid on doubly taxed income where a DTT exists); no hybrid mismatch elections are available or required under Omani law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration threshold: OMR 38,500 of annual taxable supplies in the last 12 months or reasonably expected in the next 12 months; voluntary registration threshold: OMR 19,250; non-resident suppliers have no threshold where the reverse charge applies, although the Omani recipient is liable to account for the VAT. An MFO advising multiple private family entities must aggregate the taxable value of all its supplies on a single taxable-person basis whenever connected family SPVs are supplied, and should consider VAT grouping to avoid break-up of the threshold; supply of exempt financial services is excluded from the taxable turnover computation but counts for monitoring the threshold where taxable supplies are mixed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating is available where the goods are physically exported from Oman under the Bayan customs declaration within 90 days of supply, with title passing to a non-resident buyer. Services: for the MFO, the international B2B place-of-supply rule places discretionary portfolio management, asset allocation advisory, family-governance consulting, and reporting services supplied to a non-resident recipient outside Oman where the recipient is established outside Oman and is not present in Oman at the time of supply; such services are either zero-rated or treated as outside the scope of Omani VAT, preserving the MFO's recovery of input VAT on professional, licensing, and technology costs. To claim the 0% rate, the MFO must demonstrate that: (i) the client is non-resident (tax residency certificate or equivalent KYC documentation); (ii) benefit and performance are enjoyed outside Oman; (iii) services are not connected with Omani immovable property or an Omani fixed establishment of the client; and (iv) the fee consideration is received from abroad. Zero-rating is denied where the service contract is with an Omani-resident family trust, an Omani citizen resident in Oman, or a local SPV whose ultimate beneficiaries are Oman-domiciled, even if the family office is operated through offshore holding structures.

  • Bayan customs export declaration (electronic export declaration number and acceptance receipt), required only for physical goods but maintained as part of the complete VAT export file for any mixed service/product engagement
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or equivalent multimodal transport document evidencing physical departure from Omani territory within the 90-day export window
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 payment confirmation / custodian wire advice evidencing receipt of the export or service fee from outside Oman within the 180-day repatriation benchmark
  • Executed investment management agreement or service agreement identifying the non-resident client family, the offshore situs of the assets under management, and the fee basis for the zero-rated or outside-scope supply
  • E-invoice (OTA-cleared fiscal invoice) showing the zero-rated supply classification and the client's foreign tax identification number or certificate of tax residence
  • Client KYC/AML file with beneficial ownership documentation, the MFO's FSA license, and the custody mandate / power of attorney proving control over non-Omani custodian accounts

The OTA is operating a phased national electronic invoicing (fiscal invoicing) mandate for VAT-registered persons, commencing with large taxpayers and progressively extending to all taxpayers including professional service companies such as an MFO. Compliant e-invoices must be issued and transmitted in real time through the OTA's approved e-invoice API/clearance environment in the prescribed structured data format (including TIN, buyer TIN or foreign tax ID, VAT amount, and supply classification); the system requires an OTA digital signature, a machine-readable QR code, and validation of the fiscal invoice before issuance to the client; post-clearance stamping returns a unique fiscal invoice identifier that must be included on the customer copy. The MFO must integrate its billing/ERP system with the OTA's clearance APIs and cannot rely on offline or paper invoicing for business-to-business supplies after its implementation wave; invoices to private family clients and to the Omani custodian bank are business-to-business fiscal invoices requiring full compliance; failure to clear e-invoices before issuance triggers rejection of the input VAT credit by the client and administrative penalties for the issuer.

The OTA actively audits export zero-rating claims by MFOs and financial service providers. Where the exporter cannot produce the complete documentation bundle, fails to demonstrate the non-resident status of the recipient, or does not receive the consideration within the referenced 180-day window, the OTA will reverse the 0% treatment, charge 5% VAT on the gross fee, disallow any associated input VAT credits, and issue an additional-assessment with a monthly late-payment charge running from the original due date of the VAT return; administrative fines of up to OMR 10,000 per contravention may be imposed under the VAT Executive Regulations. In cases of deliberate misrepresentation, forged export documents, or circular offshore payment arrangements, the VAT Law (RD 121/2020) provides for criminal penalties of imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines up to OMR 50,000, in addition to full recovery of the evaded tax and related interest; statute of limitations is five years in ordinary cases and longer where fraud is alleged. The OTA coordinates with the Central Bank of Oman and the FSA under AML/CFT channels, so cross-border fee repatriation for a High_Risk_Special_License MFO is examined for both tax authenticity and sanctions/beneficial-ownership integrity; any restructuring of fees by the foreign shareholder or related offshore entity is also subject to Omani transfer pricing adjustments and secondary disallowance.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is pegged to the USD at 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD. Oman accepts Article VIII obligations of the IMF, guaranteeing free convertibility for current international transactions. Capital account transactions are largely liberal but certain regime-bound items (bank borrowing, securities issuance) require CBO or FSA approval. Profit remittance and capital repatriation are permitted without exchange controls, subject to documentary compliance (tax clearance, audited financials, and purpose codes). No general restriction exists for non-resident ownership of Omani Single Person Companies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists under Omani law. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic licensed accounts indefinitely. Nevertheless, all export receipts must be routed through a licensed bank in the Sultanate; CBO AML/CFT directives require proper trade documentation (e.g., Bill of Lading, Invoice, Export Declaration) and repatriation within the commercial credit cycle. No fixed 30/60/90-day disposal timeline is imposed; however, banks apply a de facto 180-day expectation for routine trade transactions unless exceptional circumstances are justified.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) in conjunction with the Financial Services Authority (FSA) for securities-related fintech activities; the National Centre for Financial Information and the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) oversee AML/CFT reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not authorized by the Central Bank of Oman and cannot provide custodial or escrow services for regulated Omani entities. They may be used as supplementary outbound payment instruments for non-settlement transactions, but they are not acceptable for statutory capital deposits, tax disbursements, or high-value family office transfers. Practical limitations include absence of OMR clearing, lack of CBO oversight, and high probability of correspondent de-risking. A licensed Omani commercial bank account remains the sole viable treasury spine.

Root cause: Unidentified or opaque Ultimate Beneficial Owner structure in the family office trust or foundation

Defense evidence: Certified trust deed/foundation charter; UBO declaration under CBO EDD form; passport copies of all beneficiaries; tax-residency certificates; a written explanation of the ownership waterfall; independent legal opinion confirming control and economic substance.
Unclear source of wealth for initial capital injection from overseas family members

Root cause: Unclear source of wealth for initial capital injection from overseas family members

Defense evidence: 12-24 months audited bank statements; sale-and-purchase agreements; inheritance documents; audited financial statements of source companies; notarized source-of-wealth report prepared by an external auditor; evidence of tax clearance or settlement in the source jurisdiction.
Sanctions/PEP screening hit on a family member or indirect shareholder

Root cause: Sanctions/PEP screening hit on a family member or indirect shareholder

Defense evidence: Enhanced screening output from Dow Jones / World-Check; explanatory narrative from the compliance institution; official police/court clearance; risk-mitigation plan; no-facilitation letter from the concerned family head; independent risk counsel advisory.
MFO authorization/license under suspicion (CA or CMA activity beyond registered scope)

Root cause: MFO authorization/license under suspicion (CA or CMA activity beyond registered scope)

Defense evidence: Official license (e.g., FSA/CMA registration or exemption letter); CBO no-objection correspondence; board resolution confirming business scope; AML/CFT policy manual; designation of a compliance officer and MLRO with CBO-approved qualifications.
High-risk jurisdiction clients embedded in the discretionary portfolio

Root cause: High-risk jurisdiction clients embedded in the discretionary portfolio

Defense evidence: Client-specific risk assessment; transactional source-of-funds documentation; sanctions screening of all secondary beneficiaries; residence/visa proof; escalated approval from bank's head-office compliance; retention of a third-party due diligence report (e.g., KYC360 or Refinitiv).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 with a purpose code indicating 'Capital Injection for SPC Equity' (e.g., C1 or S100 equivalent). The beneficiary must be the Omani SPC's CBO-licensed bank account, and the remittance must include the beneficiary's IBAN, Bank Muscat/NBO, and a valid tax identification number. The receiving bank will verify the source of funds against the EDD file; post-receipt, the funds are settled through Oman's RTGS (Central Bank of Oman's Electronic Clearing System) and can be transferred to an operational account after fulfilling capital escrow release conditions. No CBO prior authorization is required for non-resident equity injection, but banks must submit a domestic money-laundering report if the amount exceeds OMR 20,000 (or USD equivalent) until the source is established.Outbound disbursements use SWIFT MT103 for non-documentary transfers (e.g., investment management fees, profit distributions). Documentary trade settlements are structured as commercial letters of credit under UCP600 (sight or term) with the Omani issuing bank routing through CBO's trade finance lane. For cross-currency settlement, the transaction chain is an OMR/USD FX conversion followed by a USD correspondent transfer via CHIPS or Fedwire. Local OMR-based payments within Oman are cleared via the Oman Electronic Clearing System (OECS); cross-GCC payments can be routed using the GCC-RTGS interoperability, but SWIFT remains the primary rail. Banks require a valid export/import invoice, bill of lading, and the corresponding CBO declaration for any trade disbursement.OMR/USD outright forward (on-premised deliverable, tenor up to 24 months)OMR/USD FX swap (swap-in and swap-out)Cross-currency ISDA-compliant swap between OMR and USD (interest rate + principal, permitted for notional hedging)Non-deliverable forward (NDF) on EUR/GBP/JPY when paired with USD as the underlying external exposure of the family officeForwards on Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) currencies (SAR, AED, KWD) via established correspondent bank linesStructured Shari'ah-compliant hedging forwards (Wa'ad / Murabaha) for Islamic finance booking and jumbo asset protection

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not authorized by the Central Bank of Oman and cannot provide custodial or escrow services for regulated Omani entities. They may be used as supplementary outbound payment instruments for non-settlement transactions, but they are not acceptable for statutory capital deposits, tax disbursements, or high-value family office transfers. Practical limitations include absence of OMR clearing, lack of CBO oversight, and high probability of correspondent de-risking. A licensed Omani commercial bank account remains the sole viable treasury spine.
