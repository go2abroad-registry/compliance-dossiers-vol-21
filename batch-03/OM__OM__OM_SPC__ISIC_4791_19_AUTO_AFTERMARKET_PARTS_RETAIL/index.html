Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (السلطة الضريبية العمانية); online portal: OTA e-Services (taxoman.om / est.taxoman.om).

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned SPC must obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) through the Oman Tax Authority e-Services portal after commercial registration and obtaining the Foreign Investment Licence from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP). The application submits the CR certificate, Articles of Association, ownership passport copies, and lease proof. The OTA issues the TIN without a standard physical certificate; the TIN is linked to the Registered Taxpayer Profile. For VAT, the SPC must separately register via the OTA portal for a VAT Registration Number (VAR) if taxable supplies exceed the mandatory threshold, or voluntarily above the voluntary threshold, or for businesses requiring refund claims.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available under Omani income tax law. An SPC may elect tax consolidation/group relief for VAT only by meeting the VAT Law’s Tax Group registration conditions, which require related parties sharing management or substantial control. No income tax fiscal unity election exists for foreign-owned SPCs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration threshold: OMR 500,000 of annual taxable supplies and imports. Voluntary registration threshold: OMR 100,000 of annual taxable supplies or expenses subject to VAT. No registration threshold for non-resident suppliers supplying goods or services in Oman, who must register for VAT regardless of value.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the GCC, and exports to GCC countries where the goods are transported to a non-GCC destination, are zero-rated if the supplier retains valid proof of export. Services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside Oman and not registered for VAT in Oman may be zero-rated when the services are directly linked to exported goods or when the benefit is effectively consumed outside Oman. The SPC must verify and document the commercial export status and customs outer departure.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Single Window / Bayan)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Certificate of Origin (GCC/Chamber of Commerce)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRS/IMTS) evidencing foreign currency payment
  • Shipping / Transport documentation (shipping order and packing list)

Oman has not yet implemented a fully compulsory e-invoicing clearance framework as of 2025. The OTA is progressively digitalising VAT filings and has adopted the GCC-linked clearance system for excise goods. VAT invoices must be issued electronically in the taxable person’s electronic system in an approved XML/PDF format for audit; however real-time digital stamping/API clearance is not yet legally mandated for all taxpayers. The OTA requires monthly or quarterly VAT returns filed purely electronically through the e-Services portal, with a 5-day extension period after the filing deadline.

The OTA may deny zero-rating where supporting export documents are incomplete or inconsistent; the VAT becomes due plus late payment penalties (1% per month of unpaid VAT) and administrative fines (minimum OMR 200, maximum OMR 1,000 per contravention; for non-compliance with invoices OMR 300). Fraudulent zero-rating claims with forged customs documents may trigger criminal proceedings under the VAT Law and Royal Decree on Anti-Fraud (Royal Decree 40/2012 as amended), with imprisonment up to three years and/or fines of up to OMR 5,000, plus retrospective clawback of any refunded VAT and disgorgement of offender’s gains.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account. Oman has accepted Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement; consequently there are no exchange controls on trade-related payments, dividends, profit repatriation, loan repayments, or foreign direct investment remittances. The OMR is pegged to the USD at a fixed mid-rate of 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD (1 USD = 0.3845 OMR), which eliminates USD/OMR conversion risk for the auto-parts e-commerce treasury. Profit and capital repatriation by a foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) requires no CBO approval; the only friction points are the remitting bank's documentary verification (shareholder distribution resolution, audited financial statements, and tax registration card where applicable) and standard sanctions screening. No general profit-remittance tax or exchange-surrender levy applies under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 28/2009, as amended).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement. There is no statutory time limit within which export receipts must be converted into OMR, and there is no minimum conversion ratio. Exporters may retain any foreign currency, including USD and EUR, in designated export proceeds accounts at licensed Omani banks, may apply such balances directly against import disbursements, may convert them freely at market rates, or may hold them indefinitely on a corporate balance sheet. The only obligations are statistical: the receiving bank must report the inflow to the CBO's balance-of-payments data collection framework, and the exporter must retain underlying commercial documentation (customs declarations, invoices, bills of lading) for audit purposes. For the SPC's online retail business, card acquiring and marketplace settlement inflows received through licensed payment aggregators may be credited to the operational account in the settlement currency without CBO conversion directives.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), the sole monetary authority and prudential supervisor of licensed banks and payment systems under the Banking Business Law (Royal Decree 114/2000, as amended) and the Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism Law (Royal Decree 80/2016, as amended). The CBO administers exchange rate policy, operates the national RTGS and payment settlement infrastructure, and supervises all foreign exchange activities through licensed Omani commercial banks and exchange houses.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low to Medium, with strict legal constraints. The CBO does not license offshore digital banks or pure-play foreign Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) to open resident corporate accounts or to hold OMR deposits in Oman. Under the Payment and Settlement Systems Law (Royal Decree 38/2022), any entity providing payment services to Omani-resident persons or collecting consumer payments in OMR must hold a CBO-issued payment service provider license; unlicensed offshore EMIs (including Wise, Airwallex, and Currencycloud-equivalent platforms) therefore cannot be used as the primary treasury settlement rail, cannot hold the SPC's customer receivables as a local custody account, and cannot issue OMR-denominated float. However, such EMIs remain viable as supplementary cross-border disbursement rails for low-value supplier payments in EUR, GBP, or AED originating from funds already credited to the SPC's licensed Omani bank account. The recommended architecture is a licensed Omani commercial bank as the operating and escrow account holder, with an offshore EMI used only for payables automation in non-OMR currencies, subject to full reconciliation and no CBO-regulated float accumulation in Oman. Stripe and PayPal are regarded as checkout aggregation rails, not banking rails, for the online storefront; for Omani-resident merchant settlement, Stripe is not a supported acquirer in Oman as of the current country matrix, while PayPal Business can settle funds to a verified Omani bank account subject to PayPal's cross-border payout limitations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder's capital contribution must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own bank account to the SPC's designated capital escrow or operational account at the licensed Omani bank. The wire instruction must include: (i) explicit purpose text in field 70 or 77B, e.g., 'Foreign Direct Investment - Capital Contribution into Single Person Company CR No. [XX/YYYY]', (ii) the SPC's legal name and CR number in field 59, and (iii) the beneficiary bank's BIC and the SPC's account IBAN. The receiving Omani bank will apply no exchange-control approval requirement because Oman is fully liberalized on the capital account, but will conduct internal CDD checks and may request the corporate documentation described in the AML/KYC defense dossier, including the capital contribution resolution, source-of-funds evidence, and the beneficiary entity registration pack. Once credited, funds are freely available in OMR, USD, or another major currency on the same business day through the CBO-operated RTGS. The SPC must ensure that the injected capital is booked in the statutory financial statements as paid-up share capital or shareholder current account, and the bank will generally restrict onward disbursement until the ownership and source-of-funds file is fully cleared. A minimum share capital is not statutorily required under the Commercial Companies Law (Royal Decree 18/2019), but the bank may impose an internal threshold for the initial escrow deposit to activate trade finance limits.Supplier payments and trade settlements should be executed through the following rails in priority order: (1) SWIFT MT103 direct credit transfers from the SPC's Omani bank account for routine payables below USD 100,000 per beneficiary per month, routed through the bank's USD correspondent clearing account in New York or its EUR correspondent in Germany/France; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (sight, at-sight or 30-90 days usance) under UCP600, transmitted via SWIFT MT700/MT710, for bulk auto-parts inventory replenishment above USD 100,000, with the underlying invoice, packing list, and transport documents conforming to the LC terms to avoid discrepancies; (3) SWIFT MT103 for GCC and regional payables in AED, SAR, or OMR, using the local OMR RTGS and the regional GCC interbank clearing network for same-day domestic settlement where the beneficiary holds an Omani account; and (4) card acquiring and refund rails via the licensed payment gateway or marketplace aggregator (PayPal Business or equivalent) for low-dollar consumer refunds and chargebacks, always backed by a separate GL reconciliation against the operational bank account. Every outbound disbursement must be supported by a valid purchase order or pro-forma invoice, the seller's banking details, the commercial contract, and positive sanctions screening against CBO, UN, and OFAC-denied-party lists. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Medium, with strict legal constraints. The CBO does not license offshore digital banks or pure-play foreign Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) to open resident corporate accounts or to hold OMR deposits in Oman. Under the Payment and Settlement Systems Law (Royal Decree 38/2022), any entity providing payment services to Omani-resident persons or collecting consumer payments in OMR must hold a CBO-issued payment service provider license; unlicensed offshore EMIs (including Wise, Airwallex, and Currencycloud-equivalent platforms) therefore cannot be used as the primary treasury settlement rail, cannot hold the SPC's customer receivables as a local custody account, and cannot issue OMR-denominated float. However, such EMIs remain viable as supplementary cross-border disbursement rails for low-value supplier payments in EUR, GBP, or AED originating from funds already credited to the SPC's licensed Omani bank account. The recommended architecture is a licensed Omani commercial bank as the operating and escrow account holder, with an offshore EMI used only for payables automation in non-OMR currencies, subject to full reconciliation and no CBO-regulated float accumulation in Oman. Stripe and PayPal are regarded as checkout aggregation rails, not banking rails, for the online storefront; for Omani-resident merchant settlement, Stripe is not a supported acquirer in Oman as of the current country matrix, while PayPal Business can settle funds to a verified Omani bank account subject to PayPal's cross-border payout limitations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File maintenance is mandatory when aggregate controlled transactions exceed OMR 300,000 per tax year. Master File is required for a group with consolidated group revenue exceeding OMR 15 million. Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) is required when consolidated group revenue exceeds OMR 375 million.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations law akin to the UAE ESR framework. However, the Income Tax Law and Tax Procedures Law adopt a substance-over-form doctrine: the Tax Authority may disregard an arrangement that lacks genuine economic activity. A foreign-owned SPC engaged in active e-commerce with an Oman office, inventory, employees and real sales records will satisfy substance; a passive letter-box SPC claiming DTA benefits or low-tax profits may trigger anti-abuse and TP recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Underreporting E-Commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion penalties under the Tax Procedures Law include imprisonment up to 5 years and fines of OMR 500 – OMR 25,000, plus civil penalty of 25% of the tax understated and monthly late-payment interest of 1%. The Tax Authority may also withdraw the taxpayer’s good-standing status and suspend the commercial registration.

Director personal liability: The SPC manager/director who signs the false return is directly exposed to criminal prosecution. In cases of fraud, the Omani courts may pierce the corporate veil, rendering the director and de facto shareholder jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, with travel bans and asset freezes under the enforcement powers of the Tax Authority.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance and DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Tax Authority may recharacterize the arrangement under the substance-over-form rule, disallow deductions, impose an upward adjustment to taxable income under the arm’s-length principle, and apply a 10% deficiency penalty plus 1% monthly interest. The SPC may be classified as a 'shell company' and denied all tax treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors who certified the sham structure can be ordered to repay diverted funds under civil fiduciary liability. Oman’s authorities have powers to impose travel bans on directors and to dissolve the SPC after administrative revocation of the commercial registration; if AML charges arise due to non-tax reporting of beneficial ownership, imprisonment is possible.
AML / Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Under Oman’s Anti-Money Laundering Law (Royal Decree 30/2016), penalties include imprisonment of 10 years to life and a fine of OMR 10,000 to OMR 100,000 for individuals; the entity may be fined OMR 100,000 – OMR 500,000. In addition, the relevant payment acquirer will terminate the merchant agreement and blacklist the company from all payment rails.

Director personal liability: The SPC director/manager and the beneficial foreign shareholder may be prosecuted personally. Omani courts allow personal asset confiscation, travel bans and freezing orders. The corporate veil is expressly lifted for AML violations, making owners personally liable for fines and forfeiture.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Cross-Border Misinvoicing

Statutory sanction: The Tax Authority may disregard the pricing, compute taxable income on an arm’s-length basis, impose an additional tax of 10% of the under-declared tax, and apply a monthly 1% interest charge. Non-documentation itself attracts an administrative penalty of OMR 1,000 – OMR 5,000 in addition to any TP adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed inaccurate group company declarations and knowingly approved mispriced invoices may be subject to personal civil liability to the SPC for breach of fiduciary duty. In egregious schemes the authorities may treat the TP fraud as tax evasion, leading to imprisonment and travel bans.
