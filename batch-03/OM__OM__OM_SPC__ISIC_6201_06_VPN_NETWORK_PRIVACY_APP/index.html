Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), established under Royal Decree 2/2021 as the successor to the Secretariat General of Taxation; official e-portal: https://taxoman.gov.om (Taxoman electronic services).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) and Companies Registry number. Step 2 - Within the statutory filing period after incorporation, submit the income tax registration application to Oman Tax Authority (OTA) via the Taxoman portal and obtain the Taxpayer Registration Number (TRN), which is the corporate tax file identifier. Step 3 - Register for VAT under Royal Decree 121/2020 and Executive Regulations 53/2021 within 30 days of exceeding the mandatory threshold (OMR 38,500 of annual taxable supplies); voluntary VAT registration is permitted once annual taxable supplies exceed OMR 19,250. Step 4 - Upload the CR, constitutional documents, board/shareholder resolutions, power of attorney, passport copies of the sole foreign shareholder and proof of registered office in Oman to complete the OTA e-services profile. Step 5 - Where the SPC has employees, complete PASI (Public Authority for Social Insurance) registration and the municipal/commercial premises licensing in parallel.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the resident SPC is a distinct taxable entity for Omani income tax; the company is taxed on taxable business income at the statutory corporate rate (generally 15% above the statutory lump-sum relief, where applicable), and no flow-through/pass-through of income to the non-resident shareholder applies. The Omani mainland jurisdiction does not confer free-zone tax exemption on this SPC; non-resident shareholders without a permanent establishment in Oman are otherwise taxed only on Oman-source income.

Available election forms

Oman does not provide a US-style check-the-box election, a CFC classification election or a hybrid-mismatch election. Available statutory elections and options: (i) voluntary VAT registration at the OMR 19,250 threshold; (ii) VAT group registration for related Omani entities with OTA approval; (iii) application of small-taxpayer relief and annual accounting/tax-year alignment under Income Tax Law Royal Decree 28/2009; and (iv) reverse-charge application for specified imported services where the recipient is a VAT-registered SPC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 38,500 of taxable supplies in the preceding or next 12 months; voluntary registration: OMR 19,250; the OTA may require registration of a non-resident supplier of cross-border digital/e-services to Omani consumers without applying the domestic turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating at 0% VAT is available for exported services supplied to a recipient who belongs outside Oman (including the SPC's VPN/Network Privacy SaaS subscriptions) where the OTA place-of-supply rules and effective use-and-enjoyment criterion demonstrate that the service is consumed outside Oman. B2B supplies to non-resident VAT-registered businesses may be zero-rated on customer status and contract evidence; B2C app subscriptions and automatic digital downloads qualify when the recipient's residence, payment instrument and IP-based geolocation evidence place the use outside Oman. Services consumed or received in Oman are standard-rated at 5%, so the zero-rating claim is a documentary gating condition, not an elective treatment.

  • Customs export declaration / single administrative document evidencing departure from Oman (for physical deliverables, where any hardware components are exported)
  • Bill of lading or airway bill / carrier certificate evidencing physical export movement outside Oman
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice confirming receipt of the foreign sales proceeds from the non-resident customer
  • Executed export sales contract or purchase order identifying the non-resident recipient, the VPN/software service scope and the zero-rated consideration
  • Time- and date-stamped digital delivery logs (subscription activation records, API usage reports, IP-address evidence of recipient location outside Oman)
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice referencing the statutory export evidence set and the applicable zero-rating provision of the VAT Law
  • Country-of-receipt bank and corporate identification data (tax number, passport/CR extract, intermediary payment-provider settlement statements from Stripe/Paymentwall)

The OTA has a phased e-invoicing mandate: VAT-registered persons must issue tax invoices with the full statutory dataset of Executive Regulations 53/2021 and must retain them in a legible, machine-readable electronic format capable of extraction on audit. VAT returns are filed electronically through the Taxoman portal, and API-based integration is available for advanced taxpayers. No real-time fiscal clearance (continuous control) API or mandatory digital stamping regime is currently enforced for SaaS/VPN exported services; cross-border export invoices remain paper-less, but e-invoice exchange in the domestic B2B segment is expected to migrate into the national e-invoicing framework.

Under the Oman VAT Law (Royal Decree 121/2020), unsupported zero-rating claims are reversed prospectively and retrospectively: the OTA assesses VAT at the headline 5% rate from the original supply date plus statutory late-payment additions, and may impose separate administrative fines on the SPC, its responsible officers and the sole shareholder for invoice, record-keeping and return violations. Fraudulent export documentation (including forged customs declarations, fabricated foreign payments or false customer-location evidence) attracts enhanced evasion penalties and criminal liability under the VAT Law and Oman Penal Code, with personal liability of directors/owners for the VAT due. The OTA may conduct post-clearance audits within the statutory record-retention period and require full reconstruction of the export chain - customs declarations, foreign payment confirmations, digital delivery logs and contract files - before any refund or 0% output treatment is finally accepted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is freely convertible under Oman's liberalised exchange-control regime; current account payments and profit/dividend remittances are unrestricted for a licensed Omani Single Person Company (SPC) subject to standard bank-level documentation comprising audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, the SPC commercial registration certificate and, where applicable, the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Sultani Decree 50/2019) approval. The OMR is pegged to the USD at 1 OMR = 2.60028 USD, eliminating bilateral conversion risk on the anchor currency. Capital account inflows are permitted without exchange-control approval; banks must file Balance of Payments (BOP) statistics with the CBO under the Exchange Operations and Foreign Currency Accounting Rules. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to documentary evidentiary requirements; no separate central bank approval is required for ordinary profit repatriation once withholding tax obligations (if any) are discharged and tax clearance is presented.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed surrender timeline for export proceeds or export-of-services receipts. The SPC may retain 100% of export receipts in foreign currency accounts (USD/OMR) with licensed commercial banks. Receipts are reportable to the CBO through the bank's Balance of Payments (BOP) reporting channel on a transaction-completion basis; there is no legal obligation to sell foreign currency to the central bank or local banks.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., FCA- or PSD2-authorised) are not permitted to take deposits, domicile customer accounts, or provide payment services in Oman; doing so without a CBO licence constitutes an offence under the National Payment Systems Law (Royal Decree 5/2019) and the CBO Payment Services Rules. Such EMIs are feasible only as downstream collection or payout rails for foreign-currency merchant revenue (e.g., Stripe/Paymentwall settlement originating outside Oman), never as the primary treasury account or for OMR settlement. The SPC must maintain its principal liquidity, corporate tax substance, and CBO-reportable balances in a licensed Omani bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for equity injection: inbound SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank credited to the SPC's OMR/USD account at a CBO-licensed bank; SWIFT field 70 must carry the ISO 20022 purpose code 'Equity Capital Injection / Direct Investment' (IMF BOP code for Direct Investment); the SPC commercial registration certificate, Foreign Capital Investment Law approval (where applicable), and anti-money-laundering onboarding release must be presented to the receiving bank before credit availability. For shareholder loans, the bank requires a written facility agreement, board resolution, and full CBO BOP reporting; any OMR funding is credited only after compliance review and immediate BOP reporting. OMR is not an internationally cleared currency, so USD correspondent routing via Bank Muscat, NBO, or HSBC Oman is mandatory. Stripe and Paymentwall merchant payouts arrive as MT103 credits to the SPC's Omani account and are re-swept to OMR via spot FX. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Omani transfer pricing regulations currently impose transfer pricing documentation obligations on taxpayers with transactions with foreign related parties; documentation is specifically required if annual transactions exceed OMR 500,000 (approximately) for a given class of related-party transaction, with lower or higher thresholds for related-party payments and royalties; existing SGT guidance requires a contemporaneous transfer pricing study (Local File) and, for larger multinational groups, a Master File.

Economic substance scope: Oman applies the OECD BEPS Action 5 standard through its Exchange of Information framework and Royal Decree requirements requiring economic substance for entities claiming treaty benefits; the Oman Tax Authority may disregard shell entities and treaty-shopping arrangements lacking adequate staff, office, and decision-making in Oman, and the Central Bank of Oman AML framework subjects VPN/SaaS payment flows to beneficial-ownership scrutiny.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Filing Fraud

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine of up to OMR 25,000; additional civil tax penalty of 25% of the underpaid tax, plus interest charged at the statutory rate (monthly compound) and payment of all evaded taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers who knowingly cause or permit tax evasion are personally liable for the unpaid tax and fines; Oman courts may issue travel bans and freezing orders; individuals may be sentenced to imprisonment alongside the company fine.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and economic-substance relief; reclassification of profits to the actual beneficial owner; additional tax, penalties up to 200% of the tax deficiency, and deregistration of the company under Commercial Companies Law; potential listing on a tax-haven blacklist.

Director personal liability: Nominee and foreign directors are jointly and severally liable for taxes and penalties; Oman may restrain their bank accounts in Oman; penalties for failure to maintain substance may extend to criminal director liability where fraud is proved.
Anti-Money Laundering / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Fines up to OMR 100,000 per violation, forfeiture of assets, suspension of the Commercial Registration; imprisonment for individuals up to 7 years under Oman's AML Law (Royal Decree 30/2016, as amended); banks must freeze accounts and report to the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers are personally subject to criminal prosecution, asset confiscation and travel restrictions; directors cannot hide behind the corporate veil for wilful money-laundering facilitation.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary tax adjustment reallocating profits to Oman; a transfer-pricing penalty of up to 40% or more of the additional tax determined (depending on the quantum of base erosion); daily late-payment penalties and up to 200% of the total tax deficiency.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incorrect group transfer-pricing filings or knowingly enter into artificial internal invoicing may be sued for restitution by the Oman Tax Authority; travel bans can be issued for substantial cross-border tax claims.
Late Filing, Withholding and Return Defaults

Statutory sanction: Late registration penalty of OMR 1,000 and up; late filing penalty of OMR 1,000 plus 10% monthly penalty of the assessed tax, capped at 200%; withholding tax penalty for non-deduction at 30% of the amount not withheld; possible daily fines and tax liens on bank accounts.

Director personal liability: The penalty for non-withholding is expressly imposed on the person responsible, i.e., the SPC director or resident agent; civil liability extends to the full unpaid tax, and criminal conviction can cause personal bankruptcy and travel bans.
