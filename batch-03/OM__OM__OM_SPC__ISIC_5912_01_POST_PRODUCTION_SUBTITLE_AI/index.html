Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), the single national tax agency administering corporate income tax, VAT, and the e-invoicing clearance platform under Royal Decree No. 26/2023 (Unified Tax Law) and Royal Decree No. 121/2020 (VAT Law). Official e-services portal: https://taxoman.gov.om (unified TIN, VAT registration, tax filings, e-invoice clearance).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Single Person Company (SPC) at the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate. Step 2: Register for the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) through the OTA Taxoman e-services portal using the CR number, notarized memorandum or articles, passport copy of the sole non-resident shareholder, capital contribution bank certificate, registered office lease proof, and a business description covering ISIC 5912 automated subtitle transcription and AI voice cloning services. Step 3: Receive the OTA TIN, which also serves as the VAT registration identifier; the foreign-owned SPC is treated as a resident juridical person from the date of CR issue. Step 4: File a separate VAT registration application within 30 days after exceeding OMR 38,500 of taxable supplies, or voluntarily from OMR 19,250; a non-resident digital supplier of subtitle or AI voice services to Omani consumers must register for reverse-charge VAT from the first OMR of taxable supply even without a local permanent establishment. TIN issuance is completed within 5-10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SPC is a resident juridical person taxed at the entity level with no pass-through to the sole shareholder. Under the Unified Tax Law (RD 26/2023), taxable income up to OMR 100,000 is taxed at 0% and income above OMR 100,000 at 15%. Income from business activity carried on in Oman is within scope; genuinely foreign-source offline revenue is outside Omani territorial tax unless attributable to an Omani establishment or effectively connected activity.

Available election forms

Oman does not operate a check-the-box classification regime and has no domestic hybrid-mismatch elections; the SPC is mandatorily an entity-level taxpayer. Available elections: (i) automatic application of the OMR 100,000 zero-rate CIT band and annual return certification without a separate election; (ii) election to carry forward net tax losses subject to the 5-year statutory limitation and continuity-of-business test under RD 26/2023; (iii) application to account for VAT on a cash basis where annual turnover does not exceed OMR 500,000; (iv) application to OTA for a pre-approval of recurring zero-rated export supplies of subtitle transcription and AI voice services to non-GCC principals.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration at OMR 38,500 of annual taxable supplies or taxable reverse-charge expenses; voluntary registration at OMR 19,250; zero OMR threshold for non-resident suppliers of digital and electronically supplied services, including AI subtitle and voice-cloning services, when consumed in Oman.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% is available for: (i) goods physically exported from Oman to a non-GCC destination within 90 days of supply, evidenced by customs exit records; and (ii) services, including ISIC 5912 post-production, automated subtitle transcription, and AI voice-cloning services, supplied to a recipient belonging outside the GCC where the service is effectively used and enjoyed outside Oman. Eligibility criteria include: a B2B contract with a foreign principal documenting the buyer's non-GCC place of establishment, delivery and acceptance records outside Oman, no use of the service for a taxable Omani supply, and issuance of an OTA-cleared zero-rated e-invoice referencing the export or foreign-remittance evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan / GCC customs clearance record) for goods exports
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical exit from Omani territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 foreign-currency receipt statement evidencing repatriation of export consideration
  • Commercial invoice and OTA-cleared zero-rated VAT e-invoice with QR fiscal stamp
  • Service agreement and foreign buyer place-of-establishment and effective-use documentation for subtitle and AI voice services
  • Delivery and acceptance records, including subtitle master file delivery logs, IP usage reports, or platform licence access records

OTA Decision No. 31/2024 mandates the national e-invoicing regime under the Murasalat clearance model: all VAT invoices and credit notes must be issued in OTA-prescribed structured XML or JSON format with a PDF/A visual representation, carry a QR fiscal stamp, be digitally signed with an OmanTrust e-certificate, and be cleared in near real-time through the OTA API before delivery to the customer; phased rollout commences with large taxpayers in 2025-2026, followed by mid-sized and small taxpayers; API integration requires OAUTH2 authentication, SHA-256 payload digest, invoice sequence watermarking, and retention of clearance receipts for at least 5 years.

OTA may audit zero-rating claims for a period of 5 years, extended to 10 years for deliberate concealment. Deficiencies trigger: reassessment of output VAT at 5%, late-payment interest of 1.5% per month, clawback of any refund already paid with statutory interest, refusal of pending refunds until documentation completion, tax-evasion fines of not less than 200% of evaded tax and imprisonment of up to 5 years under Royal Decree No. 121/2020, and additional penalties of up to 100% of the tax difference for artificial or abusive arrangements under the Unified Tax Law (RD 26/2023).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman has maintained IMF Article VIII current-account convertibility since 1974, and the Omani Rial (OMR) operates a de jure fixed peg to the US Dollar at 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD with a narrow CBO intervention band. The capital account is equally open for foreign investors: Royal Decree 59/2020 (Foreign Capital Investment Law) permits a foreign natural or legal person to hold 100% equity in an Omani Single Person Company (SPC) without any exchange-control licensing. Profit and dividend repatriation requires no CBO convertibility permit; the only practical hurdles are corporate tax clearance under the Income Tax Law (RD 28/2009), the filing of audited financial statements, and the presenting bank's AML source-of-funds verification. Intangible service revenues from AI subtitle transcription and voice cloning are freely convertible as current-account exports, provided the underlying intercompany service agreement and, where applicable, the IP licensing document for the AI engine are maintained and produced to the bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose any statutory export-proceeds surrender ratio or mandatory OMR conversion timeline under the CBO exchange-control regime. Exporters may retain proceeds in Foreign Currency Accounts within the Omani banking system, although CBO's Foreign Exchange Operations Guidelines require receipts from exports and cross-border services to enter the Omani banking system within 180 days of the invoice or shipment date; longer deferrals must be supported by a written commercial explanation filed with the bank's compliance function. All export proceeds are convertible into OMR at the pegged CBO rate without restriction. For services rendered under ISIC 5912, the evidential base is a signed client statement of work, the SWIFT credit cover showing the payee as the Omani SPC, and a reconciliation report from the payment processor (Stripe or Wise) identifying each end-client invoice.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), with exchange-control and AML/CFT supervisory authority derived from the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000); the formal foreign-exchange administration function is carried out by the CBO Supervision Department and the National Committee for Combating Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium as an ancillary payout channel; Low as the primary operating account. The CBO Fintech Regulatory Framework (2020) and the CBO Regulatory Sandbox allow newly licensed payment service providers, but no offshore digital EMI is currently licensed or passported in Oman. Wise and Stripe are not Omani-licensed payment institutions; Stripe does not provide acquiring services to Omani-incorporated merchants, and Wise's business-account capability in OMR is not locally regulated. A defensible legal arrangement is to route low-ticket global streaming receipts through a compliant third-country settlement account established in the legal name of the Omani SPC (for example, a Wise multi-currency USD account), with an enforced zero-balance sweep to the CBO-licensed Omani bank account at least monthly. This avoids the CBO anti-pass-through regulatory trigger, ensures that no OMR deposit-taking occurs outside Oman, and preserves the Omani account as the treasury anchor for payroll, tax, and statutory payments. The Omani bank must be informed of the EMI arrangement and the monthly SWIFT MT103 sweeps must be accompanied by invoice-level reconciliation to prevent the filing of a suspicious-transaction report.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Stepwise compliant route: first, the foreign shareholder's bank issues an SWIFT MT103 credit to the SPC's Omani bank account with the remittance narrative 'Equity contribution / paid-in capital' and ISO 20022 purpose code G300. Second, the receiving bank matches the funds against the SPC's board resolution, the foreign-investment certificate issued under Royal Decree 59/2020, and the statutory UBO declaration before any credit is made available. Third, the proceeds are booked as paid-in capital in the SPC's statutory registers and, where the onboarding bank so requires, are held in a segregated Capital Escrow Account until the first annual audit verifies the receipt. Fourth, any amount received above the authorized capital is reclassified as a shareholder loan requiring a proper loan agreement and thin-capitalization documentation. Fifth, the bank records the transaction in Oman's balance-of-payments statistics; injections above OMR 500,000 trigger enhanced due diligence and a CBO information note. The injection must not be routed through Stripe, Wise, or any unregulated EMI, and no OMR disbursement may occur until the account is fully activated and the AML release is granted.Two acceptable clearing rails are available. First, SWIFT MT103 for routine trade settlement, used to pay foreign subtitle freelancers, translation agencies, cloud GPU and AI transcription API vendors, and SaaS subscription providers. Each disbursement is executed at the CBO pegged OMR/USD rate and must be supported by an underlying service invoice, a freelancer contract, or a purchase order, together with withholding-tax proof where the payee is a non-resident with Oman-source income. Second, a commercial letter of credit (MT700 or MT760 series) for high-value equipment or software license purchases above the bank's benchmark threshold of approximately OMR 250,000 to OMR 1,000,000, requiring a master supply agreement, a proforma invoice, and the SPC's treasury approval memorandum. Local operating expenses and Omani payroll clear through the CBO-operated RTGS and the Oman Electronic Clearing System for cheques and direct debits, with same-day OMR/USD conversion available at the fixed CBO midpoint. Cross-border outbound payments are not subject to licensing thresholds; however, CBO statistical reporting under the balance-of-payments and international investment-position surveys, as well as OFAC/UN sanctions screening, remain mandatory.

Wise and Stripe are not Omani-licensed payment institutions; Stripe does not provide acquiring services to Omani-incorporated merchants, and Wise's business-account capability in OMR is not locally regulated. A defensible legal arrangement is to route low-ticket global streaming receipts through a compliant third-country settlement account established in the legal name of the Omani SPC (for example, a Wise multi-currency USD account), with an enforced zero-balance sweep to the CBO-licensed Omani bank account at least monthly. This avoids the CBO anti-pass-through regulatory trigger, ensures that no OMR deposit-taking occurs outside Oman, and preserves the Omani account as the treasury anchor for payroll, tax, and statutory payments. The Omani bank must be informed of the EMI arrangement and the monthly SWIFT MT103 sweeps must be accompanied by invoice-level reconciliation to prevent the filing of a suspicious-transaction report.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 500,000

Economic substance scope: Oman has not enacted a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to the UAE ESR. However, the Income Tax Law contains a general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) requiring that transactions are not artificial or fictitious, and treaty benefits are conditioned on substantive business activity in the state of residence. A shell Omani SPC with no real office, employees, or decision-making in Oman will not be recognised as claiming exemption or treaty reduction, and the SGT may recharacterise revenue as Omani-source, impose 15% tax, and add penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the full tax evaded, a 1% monthly interest charge for late payment, and an administrative penalty of up to 100% of the underpaid tax. Criminal prosecution may result in a fine up to OMR 100,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or participate in concealment are jointly and severally liable for restitution of State revenue. The SGT may impose a travel ban, freeze personal bank accounts, and seek personal bankruptcy orders against foreign nominee directors.
Shell Company / Artificial Structure

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA relief; recharacterisation of income to the foreign beneficial owner; mandatory reclassification as an Omani permanent establishment; imposition of 15% tax on the full amount, together with 1% monthly late-payment interest and a discretionary penalty of up to OMR 1,000 for failure to notify a change in activities.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under fraud and sham doctrine; directors can be made personally liable for the unpaid tax and for third-party claims if they have knowingly undercapitalised the SPC. Travel bans and asset attachment are standard recovery tools.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: An upward adjustment to Omani taxable income, competent authority risk, and a penalty of up to 10% of the adjusted profit in addition to interest; in aggravated cases the SGT may impose a penalty equal to 100% of the TP adjustment.

Director personal liability: Financial officers and directors who authorised the transfer price become jointly liable for the resulting tax deficiency, and the SGT may issue personal liability notices against executive directors for failure to uphold arm’s length standards.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Oman’s Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Terrorism Financing Law (Royal Decree No. 30/2016), fines up to OMR 50,000 per violation, seizure and forfeiture of transaction proceeds, and cancellation of the SPC’s commercial registration. Criminal prosecutions can lead to imprisonment of 1 to 5 years for serious offences.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally indictable for money laundering if they knew or ought to have known of the origin of the funds. Penalties include confiscation of personal assets, travel bans, freezing of UAE/Oman bank accounts, and legal extradition in cases involving cross-border criminal networks.
Withholding Tax Aggregation

Statutory sanction: The SPC becomes personally liable for the uncollected WHT, plus a 1% monthly late payment penalty for each month of delay. Additional penalties of OMR 100 to OMR 1,000 may be imposed for filing an incorrect withholding return.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the payment without ensuring WHT compliance are co-liable for the amount due to the SGT. In practice, the SGT will issue demand letters to the directors personally and, upon continued non-payment, impose travel bans and seize company and personal assets.
