Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Secretariat General of Taxation (SGT), Ministry of Finance, Sultanate of Oman. E-filing, tax cards, VAT registration, rulings, and taxpayer services are administered via the Oman Tax Portal (https://taxoman.gov.om). Oman Customs, as part of the Royal Oman Police, operates the Bayan single-window system for import VAT and customs clearance. The Central Bank of Oman (CBO) regulates the banking channels relevant to inward remittance certificates supporting export zero-rating.

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) must complete four registration steps. Step 1: incorporation under the Commercial Companies Law (Royal Decree 18/2019) through the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP), obtaining a Commercial Registration (CR), with 100% non-resident ownership permitted for the specified fast-casual licensing activity. Step 2: CIT registration with the SGT within three months of commencing activity, resulting in the statutory TIN/Tax Card, which is a precondition for CR renewal, bank account opening, and Omani licensing. Step 3: VAT registration through the SGT VAT e-portal where the OMR 386,000 mandatory or OMR 193,000 voluntary threshold is met; royalty and master-franchise fee inflows must be assessed as taxable supplies. Step 4: registration with Oman Customs (Bayan) if the SPC itself imports or exports goods. Foreign shareholder identity documents, notarization, and authentication are required given the SPC's non-resident ownership profile.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Oman-incorporated SPC is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to the unified 15% corporate income tax on worldwide income. No pass-through classification is available to the foreign shareholder. Non-resident enterprises, if taxable in Oman, are taxed on a Territorial_Scope basis and only on Oman-source income. The SPC does not automatically qualify for any Tax_Exempt_Zone regime unless it obtains a separate license within a designated special economic zone, which is not presupposed for this licensing SPC.

Available election forms

Oman does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime and provides no hybrid-mismatch or transparent-entity election. Available statutory elections include: (i) consolidated CIT filing for Omani group companies subject to SGT approval; (ii) VAT single-taxable-person grouping for closely related legal persons; (iii) VAT input-tax offset and branch head-office allocation elections; (iv) advance pricing arrangements and unilateral/bilateral transfer-pricing determinations; (v) small-business de minimis treatment where annual gross turnover does not exceed OMR 100,000. The SPC cannot elect to be treated as a pass-through vehicle.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 386,000 (approximately USD 1,003,600) of annual taxable supplies and taxable imports over any consecutive 12-month period. Voluntary registration: OMR 193,000 (approximately USD 501,800). Registration is turnover-triggered rather than capital-based; the SPC's franchise and master-license royalty income is a taxable supply subject to these thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports are zero-rated where the goods are physically removed from Oman under a valid customs export declaration, the exporter holds the full documentary chain of title and transport, and export occurs within the statutory period. Services, including franchise/master-license royalties and international logistics services, are zero-rated only where the place of supply is outside Oman and the effective use and enjoyment of the service occurs entirely outside Oman. The SPC must prove that the overseas franchisee/licensee is a non-resident recipient, maintain the executed licensing agreement, and demonstrate that franchise fees are paid to an Oman bank account from overseas. Zero-rating is subject to anti-abuse safeguards and requires the retention of the recipient's non-resident status evidence, foreign TIN or equivalent, and date-stamped departure records.

  • Customs Export Declaration and exit certificate issued through the Oman Customs Bayan single-window system
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical dispatch from Oman
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or equivalent bank credit confirmation, issued by an Oman-licensed bank
  • Commercial invoice in OMR or convertible foreign currency referencing the zero-rated supply and the SPC's VAT/TIN identifiers
  • Packing list and Certificate of Origin, where required by the importing jurisdiction
  • Executed master-license/franchise agreement and offshore royalty invoice substantiating the non-resident recipient for zero-rated services

Oman is implementing a mandatory national e-invoicing framework under the SGT Electronic Invoicing Initiative, on a phased rollout beginning with large taxpayers and extending to all registered taxable persons. E-invoices must be issued in structured XML/JSON formats, digitally signed, and transmitted through SGT-certified fiscal middleware/ERP APIs to the national fiscal clearance gateway. Digital stamping by means of a cryptographic QR code is required on B2B and B2C invoices. In the transitional phases, near-real-time transmission is required and invoices must be cleared within the prescribed statutory window; input VAT recovery is denied where an invoice has not been successfully cleared and stamped.

Export zero-rating claims are a high-priority SGT audit area. Where documentation is defective, the SGT may retrospectively claw back the uncharged 5% VAT and impose a late-payment penalty of 1% per month on the outstanding amount, together with administrative fines from the statutorily prescribed penalty schedule. Fraudulent zero-rating, forged customs declarations, counterfeit bills of lading, or false IRCs are treated as tax evasion and attract criminal prosecution, fines up to treble the evaded tax, and imprisonment. Additional consequences include denial of input VAT recovery for non-cleared e-invoices, civil recovery of wrongly refunded VAT, reference to the Public Prosecution, and delisting or suspension of the SPC's Commercial Registration by MOCIIP.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible and pegged to the US Dollar at approximately OMR 1 = USD 2.6008, equivalent to USD 1 = OMR 0.3845. Oman has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and imposes no exchange-control licensing, prior approval or surrender duty on current international payments, profit remittances, royalty payments, loan repayments or repatriation of liquidation proceeds. A 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) registered under the Unified Investment Law (Royal Decree 71/2023) enjoys unrestricted capital-account convertibility for bona fide commercial operations. Foreign investors may remit after-tax profits and capital freely; the practical constraint is upstream Chinese regulatory approval (SAFE/MOFCOM outward-investment filing) rather than any Omani restriction. Banking evidence required for remittances includes audited financial statements, tax clearance or tax card, and a standard CBO Balance-of-Payments reporting form for transactions exceeding OMR 20,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists in Oman. CBO regulations allow exporters and service providers to retain 100% of export receipts in any foreign-currency account indefinitely without a mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline. For the target fast-casual restaurant operation, revenue is domestic OMR store-level POS income, so no export surrender provisions apply; intercompany franchise invoices may be settled in USD and retained offshore in a foreign-currency account, if desired.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO; Al-Bank al-Markazi al-'Umani), established under the Banking Law Royal Decree 114/2000; sole monetary, banking, foreign-exchange and payment-system regulator for the Sultanate of Oman.

Acceptable secondary use only: global treasury payment layer for converting CNY to USD and expediting the parent-side outflow (e.g., feeding an MT103 via the EMI's GBP/USD rails) before crediting the Omani bank; the Omani SPC must still maintain a licensed domestic bank account for all legal statutory deposits.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 500,000 aggregate annual cross-border related-party transaction value. A Local File and Master File are mandatory when the threshold is exceeded; the Country-by-Country notification threshold is set at OMR 60 million consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations code comparable to UAE ESR, but the Income Tax Law contains an economic-substance and anti-avoidance doctrine requiring real business presence. The Chinese-owned SPC operating a fast-casual Malatang franchise must maintain physical premises, locally employed staff, active board decisions, a local bank account, and evidence of franchise management in Oman. A bare-license holding structure will be recharacterised, and free-zone tax holiday benefits may be revoked retroactively.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Oman Income Tax Law, tax evasion triggers a penalty up to OMR 50,000 and not less than 100% of the evaded tax, in addition to restitution of all unpaid tax and interest. The SPC's commercial licence and tax card may be suspended or revoked by the Oman Tax Authority.

Director personal liability: The foreign sole director may be held personally liable when the SPC is used as a veil; liability extends to personal assets in Oman and abroad, court-imposed travel bans, and imprisonment of up to three years under the Penal and Tax Fraud provisions.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Tax Authority may disregard the intermediary SPC, attribute the entire business profits to the China-headquartered franchise system, disallow deductible royalty payments, and assess under-declared corporate income tax at 15% with 100% penalty and monthly 2% late-payment surcharge. DTA benefits may be denied under the principal purpose test.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under the Omani Commercial Companies Law and Income Tax Law, rendering the director and ultimate Chinese shareholder jointly and severally liable for the full reassessed tax, penalties, and interest. The director may face asset freezing, restriction from leaving the Sultanate, and imprisonment for intentional sham structuring.
AML / Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: The Oman AML Law (Royal Decree 30/2016) imposes administrative fines up to OMR 100,000 per violation, freezing orders on bank accounts, confiscation of illegal funds, and suspension of the tax card and commercial registration. Referral to the Public Prosecution can trigger criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors can be prosecuted for money laundering crimes carrying imprisonment up to seven years and personal fines up to OMR 200,000; civil restitution equal to all laundered commercial proceeds is mandatory. The Financial Intelligence Unit may activate GCC-wide border alert mechanisms against the director and beneficial shareholder for immediate enforcement.
