Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic Application for Commercial Registration and Certificate of Incorporation, together with the notarized Memorandum and Articles of Association (MOA/AOA).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Single Person Company under RD 18/2019; the capital is fixed in the MOA and must be fully paid if the MOA so requires. A cash capital contribution must be evidenced by a deposit certificate from an Omani bank. No escrow account is statutorily required for SPC formation.

Funding & unlock path: Not required for a purely online business without public access or physical inventory. If a conventional retail/showroom or storage space is used, obtain a Civil Defense and Ambulance Authority fire safety certificate, a structural occupancy inspection, and a municipal occupational clearance prior to commencement.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Single Person Company under RD 18/2019; the capital is fixed in the MOA and must be fully paid if the MOA so requires. A cash capital contribution must be evidenced by a deposit certificate from an Omani bank. No escrow account is statutorily required for SPC formation.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Authority of the Sultanate of Oman (Hai'at Al-Dara'ib) — national e-Services Portal: https://taxoman.gov.om (Arabic/English); unified foreign-investor onboarding also runs through the Oman Business Platform / InvestEasy (https://istithmar.om) operated with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) and Oman Customs (Bayan system).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Single Person Company (SPC) via the Oman Business Platform to obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) number and municipal license; 100% foreign ownership is allowed with no statutory minimum paid-up capital. Step 2: Complete Income Tax registration on the Taxoman e-portal using the CR number; the Tax Authority issues the Tax Card / Tax Identification Number (TIN) covering annual Corporate Income Tax obligations under Royal Decree 28/2009 as amended by RD 9/2017. Step 3: Register separately for VAT under Royal Decree 121/2020 via the same Taxoman portal once annual taxable supplies exceed OMR 38,500 (mandatory threshold) or OMR 19,250 (voluntary threshold); non-resident suppliers of e-services and online retail to non-registered Omani consumers must register with no turnover threshold and may appoint a tax agent resident in Oman.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists under Omani law; the SPC is a juridical person taxed at entity level at 15% of taxable income, with a statutory reduced 3% rate election available for small enterprises whose annual taxable income does not exceed OMR 100,000. No domestic hybrid-mismatch election is provided; Oman aligns with BEPS minimum standards, including transfer-pricing documentation requirements (RD 9/2017, Article 9), substance over form rules, and the availability of advance pricing agreements and binding tax rulings. A foreign-owned SPC may not classify itself as a disregarded branch for Omani tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 38,500 (approx. USD 100,000) annual taxable supplies for mandatory resident registration; OMR 19,250 for voluntary registration; non-resident suppliers of e-services, distance selling, and online retail are subject to mandatory registration with no turnover threshold when supplying non-registered customers in Oman.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to goods physically exported by the SPC from Oman to a destination outside the GCC where export occurs within 90 days of supply, and to services supplied to non-residents that are wholly benefited outside Oman, as set out in the GCC VAT Agreement and Omani VAT Executive Regulations. The DTC operator must hold valid VAT registration, ship the goods under an accepted customs export procedure, retain independent transport and delivery evidence, and show the zero-rated export supplies separately on the quarterly VAT return in order to claim input VAT refunds on related purchases.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan) certified by Oman Customs evidencing physical departure
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing consignee and routing outside the GCC
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT or payment-rail settlement evidence from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration
  • Certificate of Origin (recommended for GCC customs verification and preference claims)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or mandatory digital stamping regime is currently enforced in Oman; however, electronic VAT invoices are compulsory and must contain all particulars under Article 74 of the VAT Executive Regulations, be issued in Arabic (or Arabic plus another language), and be retained in XML/PDF format. The Tax Authority is progressively adopting structured e-invoicing; for the beauty DTC retail model, Shopify Payments and PayPal must generate a compliant VAT tax invoice/receipt per B2C supply, preserve traceable order-level electronic logs, and align the invoicing record with the customs export declaration and transport documentation for physical cross-border shipments via the Oman Customs Bayan single window.

Zero-rating claims are strictly audited by the Tax Authority; failure to substantiate physical export within 90 days triggers retrospective reversal of the 0% treatment and reassessment at 5% VAT with a 25% late-payment addition. Fraudulent export zero-rating, forged customs declarations, or fake bills of lading expose the SPC to clawback of all input VAT recovered, fines up to OMR 10,000, imprisonment for up to 5 years under VAT Law RD 121/2020, blacklisting by the Tax Authority, and personal liability of directors/shareholders under the Omani Commercial Companies Law.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible under the IMF Article VIII framework and is pegged to the US dollar at 1 USD = 0.3845 OMR. Current account transactions, including trade in goods, services, and profit remittances, are unrestricted. Capital account transactions are subject to compliance with the Omani Commercial Companies Law and Foreign Capital Investment Law. A foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) may freely remit distributable profits after tax clearance and upon submission of audited financial statements and director resolutions. Administrative hurdles to profit remittance are limited to standard documentary checks by the banking system: valid commercial registration, certified financial statements, tax status certificate, and proof of distributable reserves.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts held with CBO-licensed banks. For customs and balance-of-payments reporting, exporters must be able to produce underlying export invoices and shipping documents. No CBO circular requires mandatory conversion of USD, EUR, or other export-denominated currencies into OMR; the full convertibility regime applies.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), Sultanate of Oman

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 1,000,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File preparation; groups with consolidated revenue exceeding OMR 30,000,000 must also prepare a Master File. The Oman Tax Authority may voluntarily request documentation below these thresholds in audit.

Economic substance scope: Oman has not enacted a stand-alone ESR regime, but the Income Tax Law contains general anti-avoidance, substance-over-form, and beneficial ownership rules. The SPC must demonstrate actual commercial activity through an Omani office, employees, bank accounts, and board decisions. Lack of substance may result in denial of treaty relief, recharacterization of transactions, and reclassification of the SPC as a shell taxable in Oman.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to OMR 100,000, additional tax equal to the evaded amount, monthly 2% late-payment surcharge, deprivation of tax clearance certificate, and up to three years’ imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who know of or procure the evasion are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties; the court may issue civil compensation orders, travel bans, and a disqualification from managing companies in Oman.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income by the Oman Tax Authority, additional tax up to the full amount understated, a monthly late-payment surcharge, and penalties of 5%–10% of the adjustment for inaccurate reporting.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate TP schedules may be assessed personally if the Oman Tax Authority establishes willful blind-eye or direct involvement, including attachment of personal bank accounts and restraint orders.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the Oman SPC as a non-resident’s permanent establishment, full tax assessment for the foreign parent, denial of treaty and withholding benefits, and fines up to OMR 20,000 for failure to maintain substance records.

Director personal liability: Ombur directors may be personally liable if they permit the company to act as a mere instrumentality without commercial substance; courts can lift the corporate veil, ordering restitution of unpaid tax from shareholder assets, and may impose a travel ban pending investigation.
Anti-Money Laundering Violation in Payment Raily

Statutory sanction: Under Oman’s AML Law and related regulations, fines up to OMR 50,000, confiscation of proceeds, suspension of commercial licence, and imprisonment for responsible officers.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to implement AML compliance are criminally liable, subject to personal fines, imprisonment up to five years, and disqualification; they may also be sued for third-party losses in civil courts.
