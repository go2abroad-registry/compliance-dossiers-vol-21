Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) — Ministry of Finance, established by Royal Decree No. 27/2020, consolidating all corporate income tax (CIT), VAT, and excise tax administration formerly vested in the Secretariat General of Taxation. Operational interface is the OTA unified e-Services portal, which handles taxpayer registration, TIN and tax-card issuance, VAT registration, returns filing, payments, and the national e-invoicing clearance platform.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC): (i) incorporate with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) by filing the notarized constitutive documents and obtaining the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate; (ii) within the statutory registration window (generally three months from incorporation or commencement of business), register for CIT with the OTA through the e-Services portal and obtain the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and annual tax card; (iii) monitor VAT registration separately — mandatory registration is triggered when the rolling 12-month taxable turnover reaches OMR 500,000, while voluntary registration is permitted at OMR 250,000; VAT registration is processed on the same OTA portal and issues a distinct VAT registration number; (iv) the foreign shareholder requires no Oman physical presence, and all filings, declarations, and fee payments are performed electronically; engaging a licensed Omani tax agent is recommended for non-resident principals to ensure timely compliance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Territorial_Scope): The Omani SPC is conclusively treated for all Omani tax purposes as a resident juridical person taxable at entity level at the CIT rate of 15% on its Oman-source taxable income. Foreign-source income is taxable only to the extent it is attributable to a foreign permanent establishment of the SPC, with an ordinary foreign tax credit granted where that income was actually taxed abroad. No pass-through, fiscal transparency, or partnership-style flow-through treatment is available to an SPC. — For a foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC): (i) incorporate with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) by filing the notarized constitutive documents and obtaining the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate; (ii) within the statutory registration window (generally three months from incorporation or commencement of business), register for CIT with the OTA through the e-Services portal and obtain the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and annual tax card; (iii) monitor VAT registration separately — mandatory registration is triggered when the rolling 12-month taxable turnover reaches OMR 500,000, while voluntary registration is permitted at OMR 250,000; VAT registration is processed on the same OTA portal and issues a distinct VAT registration number; (iv) the foreign shareholder requires no Oman physical presence, and all filings, declarations, and fee payments are performed electronically; engaging a licensed Omani tax agent is recommended for non-resident principals to ensure timely compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Territorial_Scope): The Omani SPC is conclusively treated for all Omani tax purposes as a resident juridical person taxable at entity level at the CIT rate of 15% on its Oman-source taxable income. Foreign-source income is taxable only to the extent it is attributable to a foreign permanent establishment of the SPC, with an ordinary foreign tax credit granted where that income was actually taxed abroad. No pass-through, fiscal transparency, or partnership-style flow-through treatment is available to an SPC.

Available election forms

Oman's unitary national system provides no US-style check-the-box election, hybrid mismatch election, or entity reclassification mechanism; the SPC is mandatorily taxed as an opaque corporation under both CIT and VAT. Available statutory elections include: (i) election to claim the ordinary foreign tax credit (credit method) rather than a deduction for foreign income taxes; (ii) VAT group registration, whereby related taxable persons may apply to the OTA for approval to be treated as a single taxable person for VAT purposes; (iii) depreciation and capital allowance elections under the prescribed CIT schedules; and (iv) periodic VAT return frequency elections (monthly or quarterly filings) as permitted by the VAT Executive Regulations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 500,000 of annual taxable supplies over the preceding 12-month rolling period. Voluntary registration: OMR 250,000 of annual taxable supplies. The threshold is measured on aggregate taxable supplies at the standard rate, zero rate, and exempt supplies subject to the VAT input-tax pro-rata rules; a taxable person becoming liable must apply for registration within the statutory deadline set by the OTA.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The SPC may apply the 0% VAT rate to: (i) physical goods exported from Oman to a destination outside the GCC, provided export occurs within 90 days of the supply and is evidenced by the Omani customs export declaration (Bayān); (ii) business-to-business (B2B) supplies of services, including the email API and newsletter-hosting subscription services, supplied to a recipient established outside Oman, where the place-of-supply rules place the supply outside Oman and the services are not connected to real estate located in Oman; (iii) B2C supplies of digital services where effective use and enjoyment occurs outside Oman; and (iv) certain ancillary international transport and export-handling services. The zero-rating is strictly conditional upon documentary proof of the recipient's non-resident status, the cross-border nature of the supply, and the actual foreign destination or consumption of the goods or services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayān) — the Omani customs-generated exit declaration evidencing the date, route, and foreign destination of the physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill — transport documentation proving the discharge of the goods outside Oman
  • Commercial tax invoice — the SPC's VAT invoice stating the zero-rated supply code, the VAT registration numbers of both parties, and the foreign recipient's address
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank credit advice or SWIFT confirmation proving receipt of the export consideration in foreign currency from the overseas customer
  • Service contract or master subscription agreement — evidencing the nature, scope, and cross-border place of supply of the email API and newsletter-hosting services
  • Non-resident status proof — the overseas customer's foreign VAT/TIN certificate, overseas commercial registry extract, or certificate of tax residence issued by the customer's fiscal authority

Oman has phased in a mandatory national e-invoicing regime under the OTA's E-Invoicing Programme, adopting the clearance/validation model. Registered taxable persons must generate structured e-invoices in XML/UBL format, digitally sign them with the OTA-issued electronic certificate, transmit invoice data in near real-time to the OTA's E-Invoicing Platform via certified APIs or authorized e-invoicing service providers, and obtain a validation token (hash/QR) before the invoice is recognized for input-tax deduction. The phased rollout commenced with the largest VAT registrants and will extend to all VAT-registered businesses for B2B and, where applicable, B2C transactions; taxpayers must also retain e-invoice records for the statutory retention period in a tamper-evident archive.

The OTA applies risk-based compliance profiling to zero-rated exporters and SaaS/digital service suppliers, with the burden of proof resting on the SPC to reconstruct the entire documentation chain (contract, invoice, delivery record, payment trail, and FX remittance) on audit. Where the OTA determines that zero-rating conditions were not satisfied, including goods failing to exit Oman within the 90-day window, the recipient being established or effectively using the service in Oman, or the documentation being deficient or fraudulent, the VAT is clawed back retroactively at the 5% standard rate, together with statutory late-payment default fines, per-violation administrative penalties of up to OMR 1,000, and, in cases of fraud, the evasion sanctions of the VAT Law, which carry fines of up to OMR 50,000 and possible imprisonment. Although Omani law does not prescribe a strict 180-day statutory forex repatriation deadline, the 180-day benchmark is treated by the OTA as a substantiation KPI: prolonged unrepatriated export proceeds weaken the audit defense and may trigger transfer-pricing and anti-abuse review. Recurring infractions can result in mandatory continuous audit status, suspension of zero-rating privileges, and parallel CIT scrutiny of the arm's-length character of cross-border email-infrastructure pricing.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman maintains a de jure and de facto fully convertible currency regime for both current and capital account transactions, subject to compliance with CBO anti-money laundering and sanctions-reporting obligations. The Omani Rial (OMR) is pegged to the USD at a fixed rate of 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD. Profit and capital repatriation by a 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company are permissible under the Omani Commercial Companies Law and CBO directives, provided the SPC holds valid Commercial Registration, is in good standing with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion, and has satisfied all tax clearance requirements via the Oman Tax Authority. There are no express conversion restrictions for electronic-infrastructure service fees, but banks may require documentary evidence of the underlying service contract to support outward remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose mandatory surrender or conversion ratios for export or service-export proceeds. Export receipts and receivables from cross-border subscription-based email/newsletter services may be retained in foreign currency in a bank account in Oman or credited directly to an operational USD/OMR account. The SPC is nonetheless required to maintain auditable books and to ensure that all inward receipts are properly routed through a licensed bank to satisfy CBO's anti-money laundering and record-keeping obligations. No statutory timeline for repatriation exists under Omani law, but banks may apply internal limits on large currency exposures and require a declared commercial invoice or statement of work for crediting proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), acting through its Foreign Exchange Operations and Banking Supervision Departments; no separate foreign exchange administration exists, and the CBO is the sole authority for licensing and supervising banking and payment services in Oman.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs and non-bank money-service businesses cannot act as a primary clearing or settlement rail for an Oman-registered operating entity unless the EMI holds a valid license or authorization from the Central Bank of Oman under the Banking Law and the CBO Payment System Regulatory Framework. An offshore EMI may be used only as an auxiliary card-issuing or payment-collection facilitator for international SaaS subscriptions, and must not hold OMR settlement accounts on behalf of the SPC. The SPC should connect its official corporate bank account to Stripe Payout via the principal Omani bank's SWIFT routing and ensure the Stripe account is declined to function as an unlicensed treasury repository.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection must be routed as a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's bank to the SPC's principal corporate account at a licensed Omani bank, with the remittance field clearly stating 'Equity Contribution / Share Capital under Commercial Registration No. [CR]' and the purpose of payment referencing ISIC 6201. For all inbound subscription revenue, the SPC should instruct Stripe and card acquirers to settle via standard SWIFT MT103 payout to the Omani bank account, using an accumulated settlement reference rather than individually itemizing each consumer payment. The Omani bank will verify the source of funds through a Deed of Undertaking, board resolution, and confirm the shareholder exists on the registered at the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion. There is no requirement for prior CBO exchange-control licensing for equity injections, but the bank must record the transaction under its internal foreign-direct-investment monitoring and provide a receipt to the SPC for annual tax filing.Outbound disbursements to international cloud providers, software vendors, contractors, and SaaS reseller partners shall be cleared using conventional SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with accurate purpose codes for royalties, computer-services, or IT infrastructure payments. Commercial Letters of Credit may be used only for the rare purchase of physical server hardware or colocation equipment; MT103 is the standard rail for recurring email-infrastructure services, because the underlying transaction is a service, not the sale of goods. Banks will require a fully executed invoice or statement of work for each outbound disbursement, a functional statement from the SPC if the beneficiary is a related enterprise, and confirmation that the applicable Oman withholding tax has been paid or a tax exemption certificate presented.Conventional FX Forward with a licensed Omani bank: permitted for converting OMR/USD cash flows at a fixed price for maturities up to 12 months, provided the bank is satisfied the transaction is commercially linked to underlying subscription receipts and subscriptions payouts.FX Swap (Buy/Sell spot and forward) between OMR and USD with a licensed local bank: available to bridge temporary liquidity mismatches between inbound Stripe settlement and outbound vendor payments.Interbank Cross-Currency Swap (USD to OMR or vice versa): permissible for institutional treasury operations for maturities aligned with the peg, but only where an approved Omani bank acts as the local counterparty and posts full margin documentation.Overseas Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF): not generally permissible or available for OMR market participants because the Central Bank of Oman maintains the official pegged rate and no genuine deliverable OMR NDF market exists in-country; entering into OMR NDFs with offshore counterparties would trigger significant legal and regulatory uncertainty and is therefore disallowed in this blueprint.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs and non-bank money-service businesses cannot act as a primary clearing or settlement rail for an Oman-registered operating entity unless the EMI holds a valid license or authorization from the Central Bank of Oman under the Banking Law and the CBO Payment System Regulatory Framework. An offshore EMI may be used only as an auxiliary card-issuing or payment-collection facilitator for international SaaS subscriptions, and must not hold OMR settlement accounts on behalf of the SPC. The SPC should connect its official corporate bank account to Stripe Payout via the principal Omani bank's SWIFT routing and ensure the Stripe account is declined to function as an unlicensed treasury repository.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 4,000,000 for annual aggregate value of controlled transactions with related parties triggers preparation of a Local File. Master File must be filed when the multinational group's consolidated gross revenue exceeds OMR 50,000,000. A tax return containing the related-party transaction disclosure schedule is mandatory for all SPCs with intra-group agreements, irrespective of the threshold.

Economic substance scope: Under the Income Tax Law (RD 9/2023) and the Commercial Companies Law (RD 18/2019), a foreign-owned SPC must maintain genuine substance in Oman: a physical office, at least one local employee with executive authority, board decisions taken in Oman, and actual operational control of the email newsletter infrastructure. The OTA may apply the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) to deny treaty benefits, disallow transfer pricing deductions, or recharacterise a controlled entity as a shell company if substance requirements are not met. An Economic Substance Declaration must be filed with the annual CIT return for verification.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 3 years and fine of up to OMR 20,000 under the Income Tax Law evasions provisions; civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax underpaid plus interest at 2% monthly; immediate freezing of bank accounts and attachment of the SPC's digital revenue accounts.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under Article 76 of the Commercial Companies Law: the director, manager, or beneficial owner is jointly and severally liable for the SPC's unpaid tax, penalties, and interest. The relevant persons may also face travel bans, surrender of passports, and an OTA order prohibiting residence in Oman until the tax debt is settled.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Revocation of tax residency certificate, denial of all treaty benefits, statutorily prescribed TP adjustments on the whole deemed profit, imposition of a 10% deemed dividend tax on funds remitted abroad, and de-registration of the company from the Commercial Registry.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unpaid deemed dividend WHT and for misrepresentations in the beneficial ownership declaration. The OTA may seek a court order imposing a 5-year travel ban and insolvency restriction. The nominee director may face criminal sanction for facilitating a sham arrangement.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering Law (RD 30/2016): fine of up to OMR 30,000 per violation and imprisonment of up to 7 years; the OTA may also freeze corporate bank accounts and impose daily fines until the beneficial ownership registers are corrected.

Director personal liability: The OTA and the Public Authority for Civil Registration may pierce the veil to seize personal assets of directors and shadow shareholders. Directors face extradition risk under international mutual legal assistance treaties, asset freezes, and disqualification from holding directorship in any regulated commercial entity in Oman for a period of 10 years.
