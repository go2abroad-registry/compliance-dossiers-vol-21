Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA; formerly the Secretariat General of Taxation) under the Ministry of Finance; single national-level tax administration with no sub-jurisdictional tax authorities; official electronic portal: https://tax.gov.om

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Single Person Company (SPC) under the Omani Commercial Companies Law (Royal Decree 18/2019) and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP); no minimum paid-up capital is required and 100% foreign ownership is permitted. Step 2: Register the SPC for income tax with the OTA via the tax.gov.om portal within three months of commencing business to obtain the Tax Identification Number (TIN); resident status is determined by incorporation and effective management in Oman. Step 3: Complete VAT registration on the same portal; registration is mandatory if annual taxable supplies and imports exceed OMR 500,000, and voluntary if they exceed OMR 38,000; a non-resident person making taxable supplies in Oman must register without a turnover threshold and must appoint a tax representative. Step 4: Maintain CR/TIN linkage, board resolutions, accounting records, bank account details, and statutory registrations (customs, social insurance, regulatory) prior to invoicing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Omani SPC is a resident juridical person taxed separately from its sole foreign shareholder under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 28/2009 as amended); profits are taxed at the standard 15% corporate rate, with a reduced 3% rate on the first OMR 100,000 of taxable income for qualifying small businesses (paid-up capital not exceeding OMR 50,000). The SPC is subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide business income; no pass-through, transparency, or consolidated group treatment exists, and there is no territorial ring-fencing for resident companies.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Omani domestic law; the SPC cannot elect pass-through treatment. Available elections include: (i) voluntary VAT registration under Article 13 of the VAT Law (Royal Decree 121/2020) where annual taxable supplies and imports exceed OMR 38,000 but remain below the OMR 500,000 mandatory threshold; (ii) election to adopt a non-calendar fiscal year subject to prior OTA approval, used consistently for CIT and VAT; (iii) discretionary instalment-payment facility for assessed corporate tax; and (iv) time-of-supply elections (invoice versus payment basis) permitted by the VAT Executive Regulations. No group relief, tax-transparent election, or hybrid-mismatch alignment mechanism is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration where annual taxable supplies plus taxable imports exceed OMR 500,000 (VAT Law Article 13); voluntary registration where annual taxable supplies and imports exceed OMR 38,000; non-resident suppliers carrying on taxable activity in Oman must register without turnover threshold and appoint a tax representative; imported digital services supplied by non-residents to an Omani taxable person are subject to reverse charge at the 10% standard rate, with the SPC acting as the accounting party.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies where ownership and risk pass to a non-resident customer, the goods physically depart Oman within 90 days of supply, and contemporaneous documents evidence the export. Services: zero-rating applies to a supply made to a taxable person located outside Oman (e.g., Google Ireland Limited or AppLovin Ireland) where the place-of-supply rules situate the supply outside the Sultanate and the benefit is received outside Oman. The hyper-casual game publisher's ad-monetisation payouts received from AdMob and AppLovin qualify as exported advertising-inventory services (B2B supplies to non-resident ad-tech platforms) taxable at 0% VAT, preserving the SPC's entitlement to reclaim input VAT incurred on Omani server hosting, development, and distribution costs. The claim requires proof of the customer's foreign taxable-person status, a signed ad-network agreement, IP-geolocation evidence, and inward foreign-currency receipts.

  • Customs export declaration (Bayān) and export manifest validated by Oman Customs evidencing departure from Oman (for goods)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill with corresponding freight delivery and port/terminal export evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank credit advice from the SPC's Omani bank showing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident customer
  • Commercial invoice, export contract, and, for zero-rated services: signed ad-network agreement (AdMob/AppLovin payout statements), non-resident VAT or legal-entity registration evidence, and service-performance/geolocation records

Oman does not currently operate a statutory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, API-based e-invoicing, or digital-stamping regime comparable to Saudi ZATCA Phase 2 or Egyptian e-invoicing. The OTA has announced a phased roadmap toward mandatory e-invoicing, but as of this dossier's reference date the mandate is not yet in force. Taxpayers may issue machine-readable electronic tax invoices in XML/PDF with sequential unique numbers and VAT registration details under the VAT Executive Regulations and must retain all electronic records for at least ten years for OTA inspection. A transition and onboarding period will apply once the formal e-invoicing mandate is enacted.

The OTA reconciles zero-rating claims against customs Bayān data, bank foreign-currency receipts, and ad-tech payout reports; missing or inconsistent substantiation triggers recharacterisation of the supply as a domestic taxable supply at 10%, disallowance of input VAT credits, late-payment penalties, and administrative fines under the VAT Executive Regulations ranging from OMR 100 to OMR 100,000 per violation. In deliberate or fraudulent export schemes, the OTA may issue retrospective clawback assessments extending beyond the ordinary limitation period in cases of falsification, impose additional penalties of up to 100% of evaded VAT, and refer the matter to the Public Prosecution; the VAT Law provides for imprisonment of up to five years and/or fines of up to OMR 20,000, with personal liability attaching to the SPC's directors, managers, and signatories.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions under the framework of Oman's acceptance of IMF Article VIII obligations. OMR is pegged to the US Dollar (USD) at 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD, eliminating bilateral USD/OMR exchange-rate risk. Foreign-owned entities, including an Omani Single Person Company (SPC), may freely repatriate profits, dividends, and capital after meeting corporate tax filings and obtaining the required tax clearance certificate from the Oman Tax Authority. No general approval from the Central Bank is required for profit remittances, provided that transfers are executed through the local commercial banking system and valid supporting documents are retained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rule or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts under Omani law. Export proceeds, including advertising revenues from cross-border hyper-casual gaming services, may be credited to foreign-currency accounts at licensed Omani banks and held indefinitely in USD or any major currency. The CBO requires all export-related inflows to be declared to the bank for balance-of-payments statistical reporting (using CBO-governed purpose codes), but the proceeds are not required to be repatriated within a specific period nor converted into OMR. The SPC should promptly reconcile incoming AdMob and AppLovin payouts against invoice records in order to satisfy materiality and monitoring expectations of the receiving bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) - the sole authority responsible for monetary stability, banking supervision, and foreign exchange regulation in the Sultanate of Oman

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs). Oman has no separate EMI licensing regime under the Banking Law; non-bank payment service providers are not currently allowed to open virtual accounts or provide multi-currency IBAN-based shell accounts to Omani corporate entities. Using an unlicensed offshore EMI to sweep gaming revenues would create inbound wire risk, violate CBO capital and money-laundering regulations, and cause the receiving Omani bank to reject transactions. The SPC should route all treasury flows exclusively through licensed Omani banks or well-regulated cross-border correspondent banks supporting the Omani Rial.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial capital contribution and any subsequent equity injections into the Omani SPC must be routed through an authorized Omani commercial bank using SWIFT MT103 or local real-time gross settlement (RTGS) if initiated within the GCC. Corporate remittances from abroad must include a proper purpose code under the CBO's Balance of Payments classification; for equity capital this is typically 'Equity capital other than retained earnings' (purpose code 1000) for FDI entry, and the receiving bank will require a copy of the CR, the board resolution authorizing the capital contribution, and proof of the investor's banking provenance. The Omani bank will issue a Foreign Investment Report or E-form entry for central bank statistics. If the injection exceeds OMR 10,000 (approx. USD 26,000), enhanced due diligence may apply, requiring bank statements, source-of-funds evidence for the remitter, and confirmation that the amount aligns with the stated paid-up capital level.Outbound settlements for international trade or services are executed through the Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication (SWIFT) network using MT103 single payment messages, or MT202 for bank-to-bank transfers. For cross-border service disbursements (e.g., licensing fees, software subscriptions, cloud infrastructure storage, affiliate commissions), the standard rail is a SWIFT MT103 with an application header identifying the beneficiary bank plus the CBO-coded purpose (e.g., 'Data processing and hosting services' or 'Royalties and license fees'). Commercial Letters of Credit (LCs) are generally not required for intangible digital gaming transactions, but an LC may be used for physical marketing materials, hardware purchases, or co-publishing agreements; for LCs, only irrevocable, confirmed documentary credits conforming to UCP600 are accepted. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are mandatory where annual gross revenue exceeds OMR 10,000,000 (approx. USD 26,000,000) and cross-border related-party transactions exist; Country-by-Country Report notification applies to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue equal to or exceeding OMR 375,000,000 (approx. USD 975,000,000); contemporaneous documentation must be prepared by the tax filing deadline and produced to OTA within 30 days of request

Economic substance scope: Oman has no standalone annual Economic Substance Regulation filing (unlike UAE), but Royal Decree 25/2023 contains a general anti-avoidance rule and substance-based recharacterisation power: arrangements lacking commercial rationality or real economic substance (employees, offices, board decisions, administrative and strategic functions in Oman) may be disregarded by OTA. The SPC must evidence central management and control in Oman, active participation in game publishing decisions, local staff, and substantive procurement of AdMob/AppLovin monetisation services; a mere letterbox SPC holding foreign IP licences and channelling ad revenue into offshore accounts will be deemed a sham and all income will be reattributed to the SPC, erasing treaty benefits and foreign tax credits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Royal Decree 25/2023, Article 105 and Exec. Regs)

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax plus OMR 5,000–OMR 100,000 administrative penalty; criminal referral to the Omani public prosecutor with imprisonment up to 5 years; the SPC's CR licence may be suspended or revoked; audit and confiscation of proceeds from all connected bank accounts

Director personal liability: Directors and the single foreign shareholder who signed or knowingly caused false returns are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, civil restitution, and criminal fines; OTA may issue a travel ban and freeze personal bank accounts in Oman; imprisonment up to 5 years applies to individuals; the corporate veil is pierced where the SPC is proven to be a mere façade for the shareholder's personal tax avoidance
Shell Company / Sham Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: OTA disregards the interposed entity and reattributes all income to the SPC; additional tax at 15% plus a 50% deliberate-negligence penalty; denial of treaty relief and foreign tax credits; cancellation of the SPC's commercial registration; blacklisting of the foreign shareholder from Omani business activities

Director personal liability: Personal liability for the entire unpaid corporate tax where the SPC is treated as the shareholder's alter ego; directors face civil restitution claims, confiscation of personal assets used in the operation, travel bans, and, under the Omani Commercial Companies Law, imprisonment for fraud where the corporate form is used to evade public obligations
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: OTA imposes a TP adjustment on deemed arm's-length income; additional tax plus a penalty of up to 30% of the adjusted tax differential; fixed fine up to OMR 5,000 for missing documentation; monthly default interest of 1.5% on the additional assessment until paid

Director personal liability: Directors who sign TP declarations knowing documentation is absent or false are personally fined up to OMR 10,000; where an incorrect TP position strips profit from the Omani taxing base, OTA may pierce the veil and hold the foreign parent liable for the SPC's tax debts as a de facto shadow director; travel restrictions apply during investigations
AML and Payment Rail Misuse (Royal Decree 30/2016 AML Law and Central Bank of Oman enforcement)

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty up to OMR 250,000 per AML violation and up to double the illicit transaction value; freezing of all payment accounts; suspension of banking relationships in Oman; criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 7 years for money laundering; revocation of the SPC's commercial licence

Director personal liability: The single director and beneficial owner face personal criminal liability, seizure of personal and corporate bank balances, travel bans, and imprisonment; failure to appoint an AML compliance officer (where required) constitutes director negligence; civil liability extends to the full amount laundered plus legal costs; OTA and the Central Bank of Oman coordinate asset-forfeiture actions against nominee shareholders
