Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Companies Law of 2022 prescribes no universal statutory minimum paid-up capital for a service SPC; capital is fixed in the Articles and must be adequate for the corporate object. For ISIC 6611 securities brokerage, the Capital Market Authority requires paid-up capital of not less than OMR 250,000 (approx. USD 650,000), fully subscribed and deposited in a blocked escrow account at a licensed Omani bank, with an escrow confirmation certificate submitted to the CMA before issuance of the brokerage licence; the CMA thereafter imposes continuous net-capital and aggregate-risk rules.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Clearance from the General Directorate of Civil Defence and Ambulance for the brokerage floor (automatic detection, manual call points, emergency exits, portable extinguishers, and emergency lighting); structural and electrical-safety certificates from licensed engineers; Ministry of Labour occupational-health registration and workplace safety plan; CMA-required physical access controls and information-security measures for client trading systems.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA); e-services portal: 'Emara' (tax portal at tax.oman.om).

E-government filing portal

After incorporation of the Single Person Company in the Sultanate's commercial registry, the SPC must complete online tax registration via the Emara portal; the OTA automatically issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) for income tax and a VAT Identification Number (VAT ID) once the VAT registration threshold is met. Foreign-owned SPCs with no local permanent establishment but deriving Oman-source income or making taxable supplies in Oman must register directly through the same portal; a non-resident is registered for VAT regardless of turnover when supplying taxable goods or services to non-registered persons in Oman.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — After incorporation of the Single Person Company in the Sultanate's commercial registry, the SPC must complete online tax registration via the Emara portal; the OTA automatically issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) for income tax and a VAT Identification Number (VAT ID) once the VAT registration threshold is met. Foreign-owned SPCs with no local permanent establishment but deriving Oman-source income or making taxable supplies in Oman must register directly through the same portal; a non-resident is registered for VAT regardless of turnover when supplying taxable goods or services to non-registered persons in Oman.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Oman has no check-the-box or flow-through election; an SPC is always a separate taxable juridical person. For VAT, a group registration election is available to closely connected taxable persons. There is no hybrid mismatch election under domestic Omani law, and anti-hybrid rules apply only in accordance with OECD BEPS-aligned transfer pricing documentation and mutual agreement procedures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 500,000 gross taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months; voluntary registration: OMR 50,000; non-resident taxable persons making taxable supplies in Oman must register without threshold, subject to reverse-charge relief.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Oman and cleared by Omani customs within the statutory deadline are zero-rated. Services (including offshore securities brokerage commissions) are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient located outside Oman and the services are not used or enjoyed within Oman; the broker must evidence the offshore destination, the non-resident status of the client, and the actual provision of brokerage service from Oman. Certain financial services may be exempt from VAT rather than zero-rated, in which case input VAT recovery is restricted; supplies effected in the established Special Economic Zones may qualify for preferential VAT treatment.

  • Omani Customs Export Declaration (Bayan) or exit confirmation for goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Oman
  • Inward Foreign Currency Remittance Certificate (bank credit advice) proving receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident
  • Zero-rated VAT export invoice bearing the supplier's VAT ID and clear 'exported supply' / '0% VAT' statement
  • Non-resident recipient status evidence and contracts, mandates or trade confirmations for brokerage services

Oman currently requires VAT invoices in electronic or paper form but has not implemented mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. Invoices must contain statutory particulars (supplier VAT ID, recipient TIN, date, description, net amount, VAT amount) and be retained for 10 years for on-demand OTA inspection. The Tax Authority is advancing an e-invoicing reform; a phased issuance and digital-clearing obligation is anticipated but not yet legally operative.

The OTA may reassess unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating claims at the standard 5% VAT rate, impose late-payment penalties (where applicable to the due tax), and add administrative fines for incorrect declarations. Willful evasion carries criminal liability under the Oman VAT Law and the Oman Tax Procedure Law; retrospective clawbacks may extend to open years within their respective statute of limitation, and repeated non-compliance may result in registration suspension or revocation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman operates under a unitary legal framework with the Omani Rial pegged at 1 USD = 0.3845 OMR. The CBO maintains full convertibility for both current account payments and the vast majority of capital account transactions. Profit remittance is permitted once the corporate books are audited, a board resolution for distribution is adopted, and the bank has verified the underlying commercial documentation; there is no discretionary CBO approval requiring economic benefit test. Dividend repatriation by a 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company is lawful under the Oman Commercial Companies Law, and Oman imposes no dividend withholding tax. Non-resident investors may transfer capital and profits freely. The only practical hurdles are bank-level due diligence, CMA charter/licensing conditions on regulated broker activities, and the filing of a final audited financial statement that supports the distributable profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not statutorily mandate surrender or mandatory conversion of export receipts. Export proceeds may be credited in foreign currency to an Omani bank account and retained without a forced conversion ratio. The CBO requires all inward remittances to be reported by the receiving licensed bank through the electronic reporting system, and the underlying commercial or capital transaction must be evidenced. There is no prescribed statutory timeline for surrender; however, for securities brokerage services, commission receipts must be booked in compliance with the CMA’s client asset segregation rules and the bank’s anti-money laundering monitoring framework.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), in coordination with the Capital Market Authority of Oman (CMA) for securities brokerage licensing and the Oman Tax Authority for fiscal clearances

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital electronic money institutions and non-resident payment service providers are not licensed to hold client treasury accounts for an Oman-regulated broker without establishing a licensed local payment service operation under CBO’s Payment Systems Law. An overseas EMI may function only as a downstream employee expense or card-issuing agent after the principal operating account is maintained at an Omani licensed bank. Direct EMI opening of accounts for the Omani Single Person Company is generally rejected due to the absence of an Omani license and CBO’s prohibition on unlicensed payment services in Oman.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital is wired through SWIFT MT103 from the overseas parent or shareholder to the Omani bank account. The transfer must be marked with a clear corporate purpose, usually ‘equity contribution under capital increase’ or ‘paid-up share capital for SPC ‘, and the remittance advice must include the Omani Commercial Registration number, the company’s investment declaration, and the bank-selected BPM6 purpose code for direct investment equity. CBO reporting is handled by the receiving licensed bank on an automatic electronic basis, but the corporate treasury must provide the underlying agreement, the board resolution, and the Oman Commercial Registry filing to permit the posting of the credit and the issue of the capital certificate. No prior CBO exchange-control approval is required for capital injections, but the bank will carry out a beneficial ownership check before the funds are made available.Trade and operating disbursements from Oman are settled primarily through the SWIFT network using MT103 for commercial payments, MT202 for interbank cover, and MT700/MT707 for documentary letters of credit where the counterparty requires trade finance against physical goods or software licensing on bank terms. Domestic OMR legs execute through the Central Bank of Oman’s Real-Time Gross Settlement system and the local Electronic Funds Settlement System. Cross-border fund transfers for brokerage margin, client withdrawals, liquidity provider collateral, and foreign fee payments must be supported by the underlying contract and transaction register; if the recipient account is located outside Oman, the bank applies CBO-sanction screening and may request an attestation from the Compliance Officer that the payment is not a masked retail client withdrawal or unlicensed investment product. Visa/Mastercard rails are reserved for small-value operational payments, specifically exchange data fees, cloud service subscriptions, and card-issuer charges.Deliverable OMR/USD forward contracts with CBO-licensed banks, permissible only when the broker has a documented underlying treasury exposure for settlement and working capital; no speculative positions are allowedCross-currency swaps (USD/OMR; USD/EUR) used for asset-liability hedging on shareholder advances or long-term infrastructure liabilities, subject to CBO-bank risk and underwriting approvalSight and time forward contracts on major convertible currencies, supported by audited forecast cash flows and executed under the local bank’s ISDA or Master AgreementOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not prohibited per se, but the Omani bank will require a negative confirmation that the hedge corresponds to genuine commercial flows and that the counterparty is a CBO-recognized dealer; the NDF must be documented as a hedge of an underlying OMR/USD receivable rather than as a separate treasury profit center

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 30,000

Economic substance scope: Oman does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the tax authority consistently requires genuine local substance: real office premises, qualified staff, local board decision-making, and documented risk-bearing by the SPC. A foreign-owned SPC without these indicia may be treated as a sham or a conduit, causing denial of corporate limited liability, reassessment of taxable profits in Oman, or recharacterization of the SPC as a non-resident entity for tax purposes. Commercial Companies Law and the Financial Services Authority enforce substance requirements in the licensing and annual compliance phases.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Oman Income Tax Law (Royal Decree No. 28/2009), the offender is liable to a fine of OMR 1,000 to twice the tax evaded, plus an additional assessment of up to 60% of understated tax. Criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment for up to seven years and the freezing or confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: The general manager, directors, and the person signing the tax return are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax when the SPC has knowingly engaged in evasion; travel bans and passport confiscation are commonly imposed during investigation, and the corporate veil will be pierced if the SPC is used merely as a vehicle for a foreign parent's fraudulent scheme.
Shell Company / Sham Entity

Statutory sanction: The Oman Financial Services Authority may revoke the securities brokerage licence and request judicial dissolution under the Commercial Companies Law. The Oman Tax Authority may also disregard the legal personality of the SPC, reassess profits in the hands of the foreign owner, deny treaty relief, and impose late payment fines and criminal penalties for carrying on unlicensed financial activity.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit the shell arrangement are subject to individual director-disqualification orders, civil joint and several liability for corporate liabilities, and potential criminal prosecution for fraud; travel bans may be issued for failing to answer tax litigation or enforcement summonses.
AML / CFT Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Oman's Anti-Money Laundering Law (Royal Decree No. 30/2014), the penalty can be imprisonment for three to seven years and a fine up to OMR 50,000; the Financial Services Authority is empowered to impose additional fines up to OMR 200,000, suspend or revoke the licence, and require confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and the appointed money-laundering reporting officer face personal criminal liability, asset freezes, travel bans and imprisonment if they acted knowingly or with gross negligence. The court can pierce the corporate veil if the SPC was a tool for laundering third-party funds.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Oman Tax Authority may adjust the taxable income to align with the arm's-length principle and impose tax on the reallocated profit, plus late-payment surcharges and a penalty for failure to produce documentation when formally requested.

Director personal liability: Directors and the tax representative who sign the disclosure schedule without verifying underlying comparability analysis are jointly liable for the resulting tax adjustment and penalties; deliberate transfer-pricing manipulation is treated as tax evasion, exposing directors to criminal prosecution and travel bans in the course of collection.
