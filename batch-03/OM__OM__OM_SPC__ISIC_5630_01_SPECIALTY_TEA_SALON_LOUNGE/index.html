Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), Sultanate of Oman — national e-services portal available at https://taxoman.gov.om

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Omani Single Person Company (SPC) must obtain an Oman Tax ID (TIN) by submitting the OTA registration application through the Authorised Tax Agent or the OTA e-services portal, accompanied by the commercial registration certificate (CR), constitutional documents, and each ultimate shareholder's identification and passport copies. In parallel, VAT registration must be undertaken with OTA using the same portal if the projected annual taxable turnover exceeds the statutory mandatory VAT registration threshold or the SPC is required to register because of importation activities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Omani Single Person Company (SPC) must obtain an Oman Tax ID (TIN) by submitting the OTA registration application through the Authorised Tax Agent or the OTA e-services portal, accompanied by the commercial registration certificate (CR), constitutional documents, and each ultimate shareholder's identification and passport copies. In parallel, VAT registration must be undertaken with OTA using the same portal if the projected annual taxable turnover exceeds the statutory mandatory VAT registration threshold or the SPC is required to register because of importation activities.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Oman's corporate income tax system does not provide an entity-classification 'check-the-box' election; the SPC is statutorily opaque and subject to entity-level taxation. Partnership and similar hybrid transparency options are not generally available for an Omani SPC. No Oman-specific hybrid mismatch election has been enacted for the SPC tax profile.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 38,500 mandatory annual taxable supplies; optional (voluntary) registration allowed for persons with annual taxable supplies exceeding OMR 19,250

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating may be claimed for goods physically exported outside Oman to a recipient outside the Sultanate, provided the goods are conveyed out of Oman within the statutory time frame, the export is evidenced by a customs export declaration and transport documents in the taxpayer's name, and the underlying consideration is settled in accordance with normal commercial terms. Zero rating may also apply to offshore services supplied to a non-resident that are not directly connected with goods or services in Oman; excessive or unsupported zero-rating claims are rejected by the Oman Tax Authority.

  • Customs Export Declaration (filed with the Royal Oman Police – Customs)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or equivalent road manifest evidencing physical export outside Oman
  • Commercial Invoice and the zero-rated VAT tax invoice
  • Packing List and certificate of origin where applicable
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export consideration in foreign currency

Oman does not currently enforce a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-injection regime for VAT invoices. Taxpayers must issue compliant statutory VAT invoices and records, and the Oman Tax Authority's e-services portal is used for returns and transactions. OTA has publicly announced a phased national e-invoicing initiative, with technical integration obligations expected to apply once the mandatory implementation timeline takes effect.

A fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating claim constitutes VAT evasion: the Oman Tax Authority will re-raise the invoice at 5% VAT, impose late-payment penalties of 14% per annum, and subject the taxpayer to administrative fines applicable under the VAT Law (including per-invoice penalties and, for deliberate fraud, statutory evasion fines up to OMR 20,000 or an amount linked to the tax evaded; imprisonment may also apply in severe cases). The OTA may retrospectively reopen the relevant tax periods within the statutory limitation window (generally up to five years for assessments other than fraud, with longer periods for fraudulent conduct) and claw back the entire VAT amount plus penalties and fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman maintains full de jure and de facto convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions; no CBO pre-approval is required for profit remittance, capital repatriation, or liquidation proceeds of a 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC). The Foreign Investment Law (Royal Decree No. 50/2019) guarantees free repatriation subject only to tax clearance and statutory record-keeping obligations. The Omani Rial is pegged to the U.S. Dollar at a fixed central rate, eliminating bilateral exchange risk for USD-denominated trade and permitting seamless USD clearing. No dividend, interest, or royalty withholding tax is imposed on non-residents under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree No. 9/2017 as amended by Royal Decree No. 66/2021); furthermore, a five-year SME concession pegs the effective corporate tax at 0% for annual taxable income up to OMR 100,000, rendering the specialty tea salon a demonstrably low-friction remittance corridor.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The CBO imposes no export proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation deadline for export receipts. The SPC may hold the full proceeds of loose-leaf tea and cultural ceramic exports in any currency within its multi-currency account at a licensed Omani bank, or apply such funds directly to offset import payables and overseas operating expenses. Outbound settlement merely requires the underlying trade or services documentation and an appropriate IMF BPM6 transaction code on the bank transmission form.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), Muscat; the sole monetary and regulatory authority under the Banking Law (Royal Decree No. 114/2000), the Exchange Control Law (Royal Decree No. 6/1974), and the National Payment Systems Law (Royal Decree No. 74/2014). The CBO licenses and supervises all commercial banks, exchange houses, and payment service providers, and administers the Omani Rial (OMR) peg of 1 OMR = 2.60028 USD prevailing since 1986.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. Oman does not recognize foreign offshore digital EMI licenses, and a non-bank provider must obtain a Payment Service Provider license from the CBO under the National Payment Systems Law and the CBO Payment Service Provider Rules. Borderless fintech platforms cannot acquire the salon's Local POS or Credit Card rails, hold OMR/USD merchant floats, or settle directly with the Omani tax authority; their utility is confined to supplementary collection wallets for overseas tourists, with all core liquidity routed through a CBO-licensed acquiring bank. The treasury blueprint therefore mandates that no operational float be parked on an unlicensed cross-border EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity injections and shareholder loans must be transmitted exclusively by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own bank to the SPC's operational or escrow account at the chosen Omani bank, quoting the beneficiary legal name exactly as registered with the receiving bank, the beneficiary IBAN (national prefix 'OM'), and the CBO-mapped BPM6 purpose code for direct investment share capital or intercompany loan. The onward funding must be cleared into the SPC account through the CBO's Real-Time Gross Settlement window within the standard posting cycle; the SPC then maintains a sequential financing schedule, bank credit advices, and audited capital certificates to satisfy the Oman Tax Authority, CRS/FATCA obligations, and any MoCIIP capital-confirmation request. Third-country fintech aggregators and unlicensed EMI platforms must be avoided, as any deviation from the confirmed beneficiary IBAN or purpose code triggers heightened CDD and potential suspicious-transaction reporting.Trade settlements for tea, ceramics, and freight are effected via SWIFT MT103 and MT202 COV with the BPM6 general-merchandise-imports transaction code, supported by underlying invoices and contracts. Structured payment instruments include irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600 issued by the CBO-licensed Omani bank in favor of Chinese, Indian, and UK exporters, documentary collections (DP/DA) for repeat shipments, and domestic clearing through the CBO-operated electronic cheque clearing and RTGS rails for local suppliers. All Visa/Mastercard Local POS and Credit Card day-end settlements are remitted by the Omani acquiring bank directly into the SPC operational pool at T+0/T+1, with the acquiring statement reconcilable to the POS terminal ledger and the salon's target payment rails. The treasury policy retains approximately 20% of projected monthly turnover in the OMR/USD operational account as a float buffer against settlement timing variance and LC margin calls.Plain-vanilla FX Forwards on USD/OMR, EUR/OMR, and GBP/OMR with tenors up to 12 months, executed by a CBO-authorized bank and supported by documentary evidence of underlying tea-import or equipment-purchase exposure; USD/OMR forwards trade at the fixed pegged midpoint with a narrow dealing margin.FX Swaps (simultaneous spot and forward) in USD/OMR and EUR/OMR for liquidity re-profiling across the salon's funding, LC margin, and payroll cycles.Cross-Currency Swaps on EUR/OMR and GBP/OMR matched to the notional, coupon, and tenor of invoiced trade liabilities, available under a negotiated ISDA Master Agreement and subject to CBO documentation thresholds.European-style OTC FX Options (puts and calls) on EUR/OMR, GBP/OMR, and USD/CNY legs with up-front premium payment, offered under the bank's treasury mandate with a signed Risk Disclosure and Suitability Acknowledgement, writing solely against confirmed trade exposure.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (flat rate effective for tax years commencing on or after 1 January 2024, introduced under Royal Decree No. 1/2023 amending the Income Tax Law, Royal Decree No. 28/2009; the former 3% rate on the first OMR 30,000 of taxable income has been abolished)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Oman does not impose any withholding tax on dividends distributed by an Omani SPC to a non-resident sole shareholder. No tax is required to be withheld at the point of profit distribution, and the dividend receipt is not taxable in Oman for the recipient.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Omani Transfer Pricing Rules (Executive Regulations and Tax Authority guidelines): a Local File is mandatory where the aggregate value of related-party transactions in the fiscal year exceeds OMR 250,000 OR where the resident taxpayer's annual gross revenue exceeds OMR 15 million; a Master File is additionally required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds OMR 37.5 million; the Country-by-Country Report filing is triggered for ultimate-parent groups with consolidated revenue above OMR 75 million. A single-owner foreign tea-salon SPC paying brand royalties or management fees to the parent must therefore test each transaction against the OMR 250,000 aggregation threshold annually.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not operate the separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime maintained in the UAE or certain other GCC free-zone centers. However, the SPC is subject to the substance-based anti-avoidance clause in the Income Tax Law (Art. 55, RD 28/2009) and the Foreign Capital Investment Law (RD 50/2019). The tea-salon SPC must sustain genuine commercial presence in Oman: a physically leased salon, Omani payroll and social-insurance contributions, locally executed management decisions, bank accounts with Omani banks, and commercial-license renewal through the Invest Easy portal. Passive routing of image rights or brewing-method royalties through an offshore connected company without local operations would constitute a permanent-establishment or fictitious-arrangement risk and would expose the SPC to recharacterization of the entire gross profit as Oman-sourced taxable income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Suppression of Sales

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of up to OMR 20,000 plus 100% of the evaded tax; the SPC must pay the full additional assessment with a monthly 1% arrears surcharge; repeated or willful falsification triggers mandatory criminal referral to the Public Prosecution under the Income Tax Law, with additional penalty exposure under the Commercial Fraud and Anti-Money-Laundering framework.

Director personal liability: The sole shareholder and the registered SPC manager are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and fines where suppression of sales occurred on their instruction or with their knowledge; the Oman Tax Authority may obtain a court-issued travel ban, an asset-freeze order, and, following criminal conviction, imprisonment for the de facto controller for a term of up to three years. Corporate-veil protection is lost because the offenses are treated as fraud rather than mere corporate tax non-compliance.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Oman Tax Authority may disregard the commission arrangement, recharacterize the entire Omani gross receipts as taxable income of the SPC, impose a 15% assessment on the full profit margin plus a penalty of 10% to 100% of the tax undercharged for failure to maintain a transfer-pricing documentation file; the Ministry of Commerce may suspend or revoke the commercial license under the Foreign Capital Investment Law for failure to maintain genuine economic activity.

Director personal liability: The outward nominee manager who signs returns for a shell arrangement faces individual liability for false declarations; where the corporate form is used to divert income improperly, Omani courts will pierce the corporate veil and render the foreign shareholder directly liable for the SPC's tax debts, civil restitution, and administrative fines. Residency revocation and travel interdiction may be issued for the manager while the shareholder remains exposed to extradition and enforcement of an Omani civil judgment through mutual legal assistance.
Improper Profit Repatriation and Withholding-Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the remittance as a taxable deemed dividend or as Omani-source services income; disallowance of the deduction in full (and not merely the WHT amount); a 10% WHT assessment on the gross outflow; monthly 1% late-payment penalties on the withheld amount from the remittance date; fixed fines of up to OMR 5,000 for the failure to file the WHT statement with the Oman Tax Authority.

Director personal liability: The SPC manager who signs and executes the foreign remittance is personally liable for the un-withheld tax when the SPC fails to pay, under the withholding provisions of the Income Tax Law; personal bank accounts and assets of the manager may be attached, and a travel ban may be enforced for the outstanding WHT liability. The shareholder receiving the recharacterized distribution bears secondary exposure for the grossed-up tax where the payer becomes insolvent.
AML / Abusive Use of Merchant Payment Rails and False Accounting

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money-Laundering Law (RD 30/2016), receipt of tainted proceeds through the merchant facility constitutes a criminal offense: fines up to OMR 10,000 per transaction for the entity plus confiscation of the proceeds; the Oman Tax Authority additionally raises the correct tax, applies the 10% penalty for inaccurate returns, and the merchant bank may permanently terminate the POS and credit-card acquiring agreement; the Financial Investigation Unit may suspend the commercial license of the SPC.

Director personal liability: Directors engaged in laundering a predicate tax-evasion offense are personally prosecutable with imprisonment between six months and three years; where phantom-supplier invoices are fabricated, the exclusive shareholder-manager loses limited-liability protection and must make full civil restitution of the unpaid tax, penalties, and legal costs. The travel ban and asset-freeze powers apply from the moment the criminal investigation is opened and are not discretionary at settlement stage.
