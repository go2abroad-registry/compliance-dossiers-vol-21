A pure virtual office address is not permissible for a Single Person Company registration under MOCIIP practice. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), Sultanate of Oman; official e-services portal available at https://taxoman.gov.om

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Single Person Company must first complete commercial registration with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion. The SPC then registers for tax purposes with the Oman Tax Authority through its e-services portal, submitting the commercial registration certificate, constitutional documents, details of shareholders and beneficial owners, and authorised signatory information. The OTA issues a Tax Identification Number and, separately assigns a VAT number on mandatory or voluntary VAT registration. Registration may be completed online via the OTA portal, with physical submission accepted at OTA service centres for document authentication.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial source taxation: the foreign-owned SPC is taxable in Oman as a juridical person on Omani-source income; no pass-through treatment is available — A foreign-owned Single Person Company must first complete commercial registration with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial source taxation: the foreign-owned SPC is taxable in Oman as a juridical person on Omani-source income; no pass-through treatment is available

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Omani law. The SPC is automatically treated as a taxable juridical person. No general entity classification election or hybrid mismatch election is available. Tax grouping/consolidation is not permitted for income tax. VAT registration carries a voluntary registration right for persons whose taxable supplies exceed OMR 150,000 annually, with mandatory registration once the OMR 500,000 threshold is crossed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration is required when the annual value of taxable supplies exceeds OMR 500,000; voluntary registration is available when taxable supplies exceed OMR 150,000. Non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Oman are required to register if they derive taxable revenue and have no Omani establishment, subject to the same threshold rules and the liability may be shifted to the recipient under reverse charge in specified circumstances.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the direct export of goods from Oman to a destination outside the GCC implementing states, provided the supplier retains evidence that the goods have physically left Oman within the prescribed period. International transport services and directly connected services are also zero-rated. For cross-border e-commerce mail-order retail with cash-on-delivery settlement, the supplier must demonstrate that title and physical control of the goods pass to a non-resident customer outside Oman and that the goods are not installed or made available in Oman. Services rendered to a non-resident outside Oman and enjoyed outside the implementing territories may qualify as zero-rated if the identified supporting documentation is preserved.

  • Customs Export Declaration duly endorsed by the Royal Oman Police Customs Department
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or internationally recognised courier receipt proving physical departure from Oman
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the overseas sales proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order matching the exported goods description, quantity, and value
  • Proof of delivery signed by or on behalf of the foreign customer evidencing delivery outside Oman

Oman is progressively implementing electronic invoicing and reporting. The Oman Tax Authority has issued technical guidance on e-invoicing with an architectural model involving structured invoice data, digital signature, and integration through online and offline modes. Real-time clearance and API-based fiscal stamping are being phased in for selected categories, with mandatory adoption required by large taxpayers in the initial rollout phases. In addition, digital tax stamp (‘Muqataa’) requirements already apply to tobacco products and are being extended to other excisable goods. All VAT registrants must retain invoices and accounting records in readable form for at least ten years and must transmit data to the OTA as and when the phased timetable requires.

The Oman Tax Authority will scrutinise zero-rating claims and may claw back VAT previously treated as zero-rated where the exporter fails to provide the prescribed export evidence, does not receive foreign consideration, or does not repatriate proceeds within the applicable timeframe. Penalties include the assessment of unrecovered output VAT, late-payment charges of 1% per month or any higher percentage set by OTA regulations, and administrative fines up to OMR 10,000 for incorrect records. Fraudulent or abusive zero-rating claims may additionally trigger a district-level criminal penalty of imprisonment for up to five years and fines of up to OMR 100,000, together with liability for all evaded VAT and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII (accepted 1974), pegged at 1 USD = 0.3845 OMR within a +/-0.15% band. Current-account flows (trade, services, dividends, profit remittance) are unrestricted and require no CBO pre-approval when routed through licensed commercial banks. Capital-account inflows are liberal, with 100% foreign ownership permitted under the Foreign Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2019) and the Companies Law (Royal Decree-Law 18/2019). Remittance hurdles are practical rather than legal: an Oman Tax Authority (OTA) tax clearance certificate is required for dividend/loan/interest repatriation, transfer pricing documentation is needed for intra-company flows, and UBO registry filing is mandatory. No surrender requirements apply to current or capital receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory mandatory conversion ratio or surrender percentage on export or COD trade receipts. Exporters and SPCs may retain 100% of proceeds in OMR or foreign-currency accounts with licensed banks. CBO prudent banking practice requires all trade/COD receipts to be repatriated and credited to a licensed local bank within 6 months of the shipping/collection date; monthly Balance of Payments (BOP) reporting and AML transaction declarations apply to any transfer equal to or exceeding OMR 6,000 equivalent. Late repatriation without documented justification triggers bank-wide de-risking review and potential corrective action plans by CBO examiners.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) — Banking Supervision Directorate, Exchange Control and Payment Systems Departments; Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) issues investment licensing for foreign-owned SPCs.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM for treasury supplementation; LOW for primary local settlement. Airwallex/Wise-class offshore digital EMIs may lawfully provide foreign multi-currency wallets (USD, EUR, GBP, CNH) to the Omani SPC for vendor payouts, receivables aggregation from overseas marketplace entities, and FX margin reduction; they cannot hold OMR balances, issue local account numbers, participate in the CBO National Payment System, or operate COD collection loops without a CBO payment-service-provider license or a licensed bank sponsorship. Legal constraints: Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000) reserves payment business to licensed banks; unlicensed EMI routing of substantial Omani trade settlements creates AML red flags and suspension risk of inward remittances. Recommended architecture: use Airwallex only as a non-resident disbursement pipe; keep OMR receipts and trade settlement in a Tier 1 Omani bank.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% on taxable income exceeding OMR 30,000; reduced rate of 0% on the first OMR 30,000 of taxable profit for qualifying small businesses

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% withholding tax on dividends paid by an Oman resident SPC to non-resident shareholders under the Income Tax Law; no domestic dividend deduction is allowed to the distributing company.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within nine months after the close of the taxable fiscal year; for a 31 December year-end, the statutory filing deadline is 30 September. A fiscal year approval must be obtained from the Tax Authority if a different year-end is used.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 100,000 aggregate related-party transactions per fiscal year triggers a mandatory Transfer Pricing Local File; groups with consolidated revenue of OMR 75 million or more must additionally prepare a Master File, and Country-by-Country Reporting is required for multinational groups exceeding the Oman Tax Authority threshold.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations apply to all Oman licensed entities engaged in relevant activities, including distribution and logistics services. A mail-order COD e-commerce SPC must demonstrate an adequate number of employees, local fulfilment premises, physical presence in Oman, and active decision-making on procurement, pricing, and COD settlement. The SPC cannot rely exclusively on a foreign related party; the profit from online sales must remain proportionate to Oman business substance. Inadequate substance will cause remittances to be recharacterised as taxable distributions or fees and may lead to registration cancellation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Financial Statement Filing

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax, automatic disallowance of false deductions, and imprisonment of up to 5 years under the Income Tax Law and Penal Code. The Tax Authority is also empowered to issue a 30-day jeopardy assessment before the statutory due date when fraud is suspected.

Director personal liability: Directors and the sole shareholder of the SPC are jointly and severally liable for taxes evaded, and civil restitution can be enforced against personal assets through a travel ban, asset seizure, and freezing of bank accounts. Criminal imprisonment may be imposed on any managing director who signed the return knowing it was false.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting and Concealed Related-Party Remittances

Statutory sanction: The Tax Authority will recharacterise profit, impose an upward adjustment to taxable income, deny deduction of undisclosed fees, and apply a penalty of 10% to 50% of the additional tax assessed. A late filing penalty and a monthly delayed payment surcharge are also applied to the resulting amended liability.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved transfer pricing manipulations are personally liable for the assessed tax under the joint liability provisions; additionally, the Oman Tax Authority may seek disqualification of directors and revocation of the SPC's commercial registration, while overseas founders can be named in a tax recovery order.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Regulations, non-compliant entities face administrative penalties from OMR 5,000 to OMR 20,000, compulsory de-registration, cancellation of the commercial licence, and a ban on obtaining tax clearance or new trade activities for two years. Returns may be rejected and the entity classified as a shell company.

Director personal liability: Directors and nominee shareholders are jointly liable for unpaid taxes and can be held personally responsible for restitution to creditors. Travel bans may be imposed for unpaid fines, and the Tax Authority may notify financial authorities to freeze all bank accounts linked to the shell company. Deliberate use of a shell for international tax avoidance may subject each director to criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years.
AML Violations and Cash Settlement Without Regulatory Controls

Statutory sanction: Under Oman's Anti-Money Laundering Law, the SPC and its senior management face fines from OMR 10,000 to OMR 50,000 per violation and imprisonment of up to 10 years for laundering funds linked to unlicensed cross-border settlement. The SPC can also be made ineligible for banking services and subject to compulsory corporate dissolution.

Director personal liability: The Principal Officer / Money Laundering Reporting Officer is personally criminally liable for failing to file STRs; directors who ignored compliance protocols can be subjected to asset forfeiture, travel restrictions, and imprisonment. Corporate veil-piercing is applied when the SPC is a facade for informal money movement, leaving the foreign beneficial owner exposed to extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties.
