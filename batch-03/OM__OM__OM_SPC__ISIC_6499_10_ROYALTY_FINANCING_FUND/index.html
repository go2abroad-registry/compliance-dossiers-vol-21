Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Secretariat General of Taxation (SGT), Ministry of Finance, Sultanate of Oman; unified e-Services portal: https://taxes.gov.om

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) is first incorporated at the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) to obtain a Commercial Registration (CR) and Oman Chamber of Commerce card; no minimum capital is required and 100% foreign shareholding is permitted. Thereafter, the SPC registers with the SGT through the unified taxes.gov.om portal by uploading the CR, certified Memorandum/Articles of Association, passport copies of the sole shareholder and manager, notarized power of attorney and a board/owner resolution. SGT returns a Taxpayer Identity Number (TIN) for corporate income tax purposes and, where the VAT registration threshold is met or reasonably expected to be met in the next 30 days, a separate VAT registration number. Non-resident persons whose only nexus is Oman-source royalty or financing service revenue must register for VAT if they make taxable supplies in Oman, and may appoint an Omani tax representative as required by the VAT Law (Royal Decree 121/2020) and Executive Regulations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope. The Oman SPC is a juridical person subject to entity-level corporate income taxation under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 28/2009 as amended) at 15% on Oman-source taxable income; qualifying small taxpayers pay 3% on the first OMR 100,000 of taxable income. No pass-through or fiscal transparency exists for income tax purposes. Distributions or profit remittances to the foreign shareholder are not subject to additional entity-level tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope. The Oman SPC is a juridical person subject to entity-level corporate income taxation under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 28/2009 as amended) at 15% on Oman-source taxable income; qualifying small taxpayers pay 3% on the first OMR 100,000 of taxable income. No pass-through or fiscal transparency exists for income tax purposes. Distributions or profit remittances to the foreign shareholder are not subject to additional entity-level tax. For VAT purposes the SPC is a separate taxable person; a revenue-based financing/royalty fund will generally treat financing margins as exempt financial services and royalty-for-rights income as taxable supplies if rendered to Omani-resident counterparties, while exports of qualifying services to non-resident recipients are zero-rated.

Available election forms

Oman does not offer a US-style check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election. Available statutory elections: (i) VAT tax group registration for related parties under Article 15 of the Executive Regulations, which permits aggregated returns and zero intra-group VAT accounting; (ii) election between invoice-basis and cash-basis VAT accounting, the latter available where SGT criteria for small or cash-based businesses are satisfied; (iii) election of an input VAT apportionment method for mixed taxable/exempt activity, subject to SGT approval; and (iv) utilization of tax losses carried forward against future taxable profits under the normal CIT framework. These elections are made in writing or via the taxes.gov.om dashboard and, once adopted, bind the SPC for a minimum statutory period.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 500,000 of annual taxable supplies in the current or immediately preceding 12-month period (mandatory registration); voluntary registration is available above OMR 250,000 but remains subject to SGT discretion and, during the initial implementation period, was suspended for many sectors. Non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies to non-registrant customers in Oman are required to register within 30 days of the first in-scope supply unless another registered supplier is liable for the tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Article 5 of the VAT Law and Executive Regulations Articles 21-22 where: (i) goods are physically exported out of Oman within 90 days of the date of supply and the export is evidenced by customs clearance records; (ii) services are supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside Oman and the services are not considered connected to immovable property or tangible goods situated in Oman at the time of supply; (iii) services are directly related to exported goods or to international transport of passengers or goods; or (iv) the financing or royalty service qualifies as an offshore supply contract where the benefit of the service is fully enjoyed outside Oman and the customer is not an Oman-resident taxable person. For an ISIC 6499 revenue-based financing/royalty fund, the SPC must distinguish exempt financing returns from zero-rated royalty/technology service exports; input VAT attributable to exempt supplies is unrecoverable, whereas input VAT on zero-rated exports is fully claimable by quarterly VAT refund.

  • Export Customs Declaration - approved Bayan/ECCAS export declaration from Royal Oman Police Customs showing exporter TIN, tariff classification, quantity, value and the exit customs point for goods exports.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR - independent transport document evidencing physical departure of the goods from Oman within 90 days of supply; for pure services exports, a signed statement of work, delivery/acceptance log and IT service records performing the same evidentiary function.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank credit advice or SWIFT MT103 evidencing collection of the export consideration from the foreign customer (or, for offset transactions, a bilateral confirmation acceptable to SGT).
  • Commercial Invoice and Export Contract / Financing and Royalty Agreement - detailing the parties, royalty or financing fee base, payment schedule, place-of-supply position and the zero-rating rationale relied upon.
  • Non-resident Evidence - VAT or tax registration certificate, certificate of residence, or equivalent proof of the foreign recipient's status and absence of an Oman permanent establishment.

Oman is progressively implementing a national e-invoicing framework oriented toward continuous transaction control (CTC). As of the current operative period, no universal real-time fiscal clearance mandate or mandatory digital stamping/envelope is in force; the SGT has piloted API-based electronic reporting for selected large taxpayers and business-to-government supplies through the taxes.gov.om platform, with a phased mandatory rollout expected from 2026 onward. Until the universal mandate commences, the SPC must issue VAT invoices in paper or electronic (XML/PDF) format containing all mandatory fields, transmit summary data through VAT returns, and retain the underlying invoice files for the statutory record-keeping period.

SGT conducts post-export audit verification by cross-checking the export declaration, transport movement, payment receipt and beneficiary identity; zero-rated treatment is reversed where the goods do not leave Oman within 90 days, the services are not enjoyed wholly offshore, or documentation is incomplete. On reversal, the SPC is reassessed at 5% VAT plus additional tax of 1% per month on the unpaid amount up to a maximum of 100% of the tax due, together with administrative fines in the OMR 500 to OMR 3,000 range per violation. Refund claims found to be fabricated or supported by forged invoices trigger clawback of the refunded VAT with interest and penalties, and may expose directors and the tax representative to criminal prosecution, imprisonment of up to three years and confiscation of the tax evaded. Oman's law does not impose a rigid 180-day forced FX repatriation deadline for exporters; the 180-day benchmark is treated by auditors as the expected period within which export proceeds must be received and credited to the Omani bank account, and receipts after that window will ordinarily require additional justification to avoid a zero-rating challenge.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman operates an open and fully convertible currency regime under the Central Bank of Oman. Current-account payments and transfers for current international transactions are unrestricted; profit, dividend and royalty remittances are freely permissible once the applicable Omani tax clearance certificate is obtained and the appropriate CBO balance-of-payments purpose code is declared. Capital-account transactions are equally liberal under the Foreign Capital Investment Law, with no mandatory prior approval for a 100% foreign-owned single person company, although inward equity injections and outward capital reductions are subject to reporting through the licensed banking system for statistical and sanctions-screening purposes. No convertibility restriction or surrender queue applies to the Omani rial, which is pegged to the US dollar at approximately USD 2.6008 per OMR; operational FX mismatch risk is therefore limited. The practical hurdles for a royalty-financing fund relate to bank-level economic substance reviews, source-of-funds verification, and tax clearance rather than exchange-control restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters and service suppliers may retain foreign-currency proceeds in onshore foreign-currency accounts with licensed Omani banks, may apply such balances to approved outbound payments, or may convert into Omani rial through the interbank market at the prevailing pegged rate. The only administrative obligations are accurate declaration of export or business receipts in the bank's financial reporting and retention of underlying trade invoices or proof-of-service documentation for CBO and AML/CFT examination. The royalty-financing fund should treat collections from digital brands as services or current-account receipts; no forced conversion of USD proceeds into OMR is required before reinvestment or distribution.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. The Central Bank of Oman regulates payment service providers and electronic money institutions through the National Payment Systems Law, and no comprehensive independent EMI-licensing track is available for non-resident fintech operators. Offshore digital EMIs cannot legally open domestic settlement accounts in Oman solely on the basis of a home-state EMI licence; they require either a CBO-recognised bank-partner arrangement or a locally incorporated payment service provider. For treasury purposes, the SPC should rely on regulated tier-1 Omani banks for OMR clearing and use SWIFT/MT103 correspondent rails rather than third-party EMI payment accounts for principal flows. Offshore EMIs may be used only for marginal collection micro-routing where the underlying seller is outside Oman, and even then consolidated repatriation into the Omani bank account should occur within the same value cycle to avoid unlicensed payment-facility risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and debt funding must be wired through an authorised Omani bank using SWIFT MT103 or MT202 and the CBO balance-of-payments purpose code for inward direct investment or equity capital. The remitting bank must include the SPC full legal name, bank IBAN, a reference such as 'INCAP-OM_SPC', and the ownership percentage if relevant. Bank Muscat or HSBC Oman will verify the corporate mandate, screen the remitter against OFAC/UN/EU sanctions, and obtain a signed source-of-funds and purpose-of-payment declaration. No prior CBO approval is required for a wholly foreign-owned SPC, but the incoming transfer must be supported by the certificate of incorporation, the investment declaration filed with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (if required), a board resolution approving the capital injection, and underlying subscription or contribution documents where the SPC issues profit-sharing units.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements, revenue-purchase payouts, and service fees will use the correspondent-banking network of the onboarding Omani bank. Primary rails are SWIFT MT103 for direct commercial payments, MT202 COV for interbank cover where required, and documentary letters of credit under UCP 600 through SWIFT MT700/MT707 for structured trade imports. OMR-denominated local payments to Omani merchants are settled through the Central Bank of Oman clearing infrastructure and direct debit schemes; USD payments are settled through the bank's New York correspondents via CHIPS or Fedwire. All outbound payments must carry a CBO purpose code and be matched to an underlying signed commercial agreement; cash disbursements are prohibited. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium. The Central Bank of Oman regulates payment service providers and electronic money institutions through the National Payment Systems Law, and no comprehensive independent EMI-licensing track is available for non-resident fintech operators. Offshore digital EMIs cannot legally open domestic settlement accounts in Oman solely on the basis of a home-state EMI licence; they require either a CBO-recognised bank-partner arrangement or a locally incorporated payment service provider. For treasury purposes, the SPC should rely on regulated tier-1 Omani banks for OMR clearing and use SWIFT/MT103 correspondent rails rather than third-party EMI payment accounts for principal flows. Offshore EMIs may be used only for marginal collection micro-routing where the underlying seller is outside Oman, and even then consolidated repatriation into the Omani bank account should occur within the same value cycle to avoid unlicensed payment-facility risk.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 10,000,000

Economic substance scope: Oman does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime analogous to the UAE. Substance is nonetheless assessed via the place of effective management (POEM) test, the existence of a permanent establishment, and the beneficial ownership requirement under double tax treaties. A royalty financing fund that merely passes income through Oman without business activity, physical presence, or management and control risks being treated as a sham or a conduit vehicle, exposing its transactions to disregard and its shareholders to penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of not less than OMR 1,000 or not more than OMR 50,000, in addition to full restitution of evaded tax and late-payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors and the beneficial owner may be jointly and severally liable for the SPC's tax debts if they knowingly engaged in concealment; travel bans and criminal prosecution apply upon indictment.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disregard the entity as sham, re-characterize transactions to the actual economic owner, and impose penalties of up to 100% of the avoided tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders are personally liable for the re-determined tax and any fines; travel bans may be issued to secure collection.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Under Oman's Combating Money Laundering and Terrorism Financing Law (Royal Decree No. 30/2016 as amended), fines of up to OMR 450,000 and criminal imprisonment for up to 7 years for deliberate breaches; assets may also be frozen or confiscated.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and shadow principals face criminal prosecution, professional disqualification, asset forfeiture, and travel bans. Corporate veil-piercing is permitted where the SPC is found to be a mere instrumentality of crime.
Transfer Pricing (TP) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income to the arm's-length result, imposition of a 15% deficiency tax, and administrative penalty of up to OMR 2,000 for failure to file TP documentation; significant upward adjustments may be treated as evasion leading to criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors who certified and signed off on the transfer pricing return face personal fines and restitution orders; where allocations involve concealment or misuse of corporate assets, civil claims against them may be brought by creditors and shareholders.
Failure to Register or File

Statutory sanction: Failure to register: administrative fine of up to OMR 1,000. Failure to file a return: up to OMR 1,000 per return. In addition, a late-payment surcharge of 2% per month on outstanding tax is imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors and the foreign owner may not immigrate or exit Oman until outstanding fines are settled; a court order can pierce the liability shield to collect tax debts from the natural persons controlling the SPC.
