Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Secretariat General of Taxation (SGT), Ministry of Finance, Sultanate of Oman; online filing and registration via the Tax Portal at https://tax-portal.gov.om

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the foreign-owned Single Person Company, the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) issues a Commercial Registration Number, which serves as the corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN). For VAT, the SPC must apply through the SGT Tax Portal using its CR number and Omani shareholder/manager documents, declare expected taxable supplies over the next 12 months, and provide bank and establishment details. The SGT issues the VAT Registration Number (VRN) and a VAT-related TIN within 20 working days. A foreign entity without a domestic establishment or permanent establishment making taxable supplies in Oman must file the VAT registration application via the Tax Portal, appoint a tax representative/agent under a notarized power of attorney, and submit the foreign incorporation certificate, evidence of supplies in Oman, and banking details.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available under Omani law; entity classiﬁcation follows the Commercial Companies Law and is per se taxable. Available elections are limited to voluntary VAT registration, selection of the tax accounting period with prior SGT approval, and treaty-based application for tax residence certificates.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 38,500 mandatory registration on a rolling 12-month taxable-supply turnover basis; voluntary registration at OMR 19,250

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for goods physically exported from Oman to a destination outside the GCC VAT implementing states, provided export occurs within 90 days of the supply and sufficient evidence of physical departure is retained. Certain cross-border services supplied to a non-resident directly and consumed wholly outside Oman, plus international transport of goods/persons out of Oman, are also zero-rated. Supplies to GCC countries are generally treated as switched/removal to another GCC territory and may be taxable in the receiving GCC state rather than zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan system)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT confirmation of export proceeds

Oman currently applies a post-audit VAT model without mandatory real-time fiscal clearance; VAT-registered businesses must issue tax invoices electronically or in paper form showing prescribed data elements, retain electronic/paper records for at least 5 years, and submit VAT returns electronically via the SGT Tax Portal. No government API-based real-time clearance or QR-code digital stamping is mandatory as of the current effective tax period.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims trigger SGT audit re-characterization and retrospective assessment of the erroneously zero-rated VAT, with late-payment penalties of up to 2% per month of delay. Evasion penalties under the VAT Law may reach 100% of the tax evaded with a minimum fine of OMR 1,000, plus possible imprisonment up to 3 years; the SGT may also claw back prior refunds, impose publication of the violation, and disqualify the taxpayer from future export zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial is fully convertible for current international transactions; Oman has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. The currency has been pegged to the US dollar at USD 1 = OMR 0.3845 since 1986, which eliminates OMR/USD conversion risk while leaving EUR, GBP, THB, CNY, VND and other Asian cross-currency exposure. Capital-account transactions are liberalized for this SPC: the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2019) permits 100% foreign ownership and free profit repatriation. No CBO exchange-control permit is required for equity injection, import payments, or dividend remittances. Practical hurdles are documentary and tax-driven: outbound profit or dividend remittances require an Oman Tax Authority (OTA) tax-clearance certificate and settled corporate income tax (progressive rates under Royal Decree 9/2017, as amended: OMR 30,000 exempt, 12% on the next OMR 970,000, and 15% above OMR 1,000,000); VAT at 5% applies to supermarket retail sales. Banks must sight the underlying commercial invoice, import customs declaration, or tax clearance before executing any cross-border payment. No ceiling or percentage restriction applies to current or capital transfers; bank transfers of any size are lawful subject to AML screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not operate any statutory export-proceeds surrender regime: there is no mandated percentage of export receipts that must be converted into OMR and no codified repatriation deadline under the Banking Law, CBO exchange-control rules, or Income Tax Law. Exporters may hold foreign-currency proceeds indefinitely in licensed foreign-currency accounts at CBO-authorized dealers. The binding obligations are (i) all export or trade proceeds must be received through a CBO-licensed bank so the monthly balance-of-payments reporting return captures the transaction; and (ii) banks apply a trade-finance and AML prudential review when export receivables remain outstanding beyond 180 days from the bill of lading date. For the SPC's re-export of imported Asian condiments, proceeds must land in the Omani corporate account and be supported by the export invoice and outbound customs entry; conversion to OMR, if any, is made at the bank's published market spread with zero compulsory ratio.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), the sole statutory monetary, banking and foreign-exchange regulator established by the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000, as amended); supervises all licensed banks and authorized dealers, administers Oman's IMF Article VIII convertibility obligations, the OMR/USD peg (USD 1 = OMR 0.3845, i.e., OMR 1 = USD 2.6008), and the foreign-exchange and balance-of-payments statistical reporting framework enforced through commercial banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as the primary banking rail; Medium as a strictly supplementary small-value supplier payment corridor. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) hold no CBO banking license, are not authorized dealers, cannot maintain OMR settlement accounts, cannot access the Oman Clearing House or local POS acquiring network, and must not be used to receive trade or export proceeds without triggering an AML red flag and breaching the Omani bank's account terms. The CBO Regulatory Sandbox Framework (2019) and the payments-by-non-bank circulars allow fintech pilots only through a CBO-licensed sponsor bank; a standalone foreign EMI license confers no in-country legal status. If used at all, the EMI must be restricted to minor supplier disbursements with full disclosure to the Omani bank, each EMI credit matched to source documentation, and same-day onward settlement into the CBO-licensed operating account to avoid layering inferences.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% for companies resident in Oman under Oman Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 28/2009, as amended)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends distributed by an Omani single person company to its corporate shareholder; Oman does not impose a statutory dividend withholding tax on profit distributions made by a resident SPC out of taxed profits

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within four months after the fiscal year-end, i.e., by 30 April for a 31 December year-end, unless an extension is granted in writing by the Oman Tax Authority

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 1,000,000 annual value of related-party transactions for Local File; OMR 10,000,000 group consolidated turnover for Master File; a TP disclosure in the annual tax return is required whenever controlled transactions occur

Economic substance scope: Oman does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations as a standalone code, but the Oman Tax Authority applies an economic-substance and beneficial-ownership test in deriving treaty relief and in scrutinising transactions involving non-resident related parties. A supermarket SPC that is a mere letterbox, has no local management or purchasing decisions, carries no inventory in Oman, or books all profit to a non-resident related party without substantive commercial activity will have intercompany transactions disregarded, adjusted at the source, or denied treaty benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Additional tax equal to up to 100% of the under-declared liability, administrative fines of OMR 5,000 to OMR 100,000, loss of the right to offset losses, criminal prosecution under Royal Decree 28/2009 and the Omani Penal Code, and a ban on receiving future government or free-zone licences

Director personal liability: A director or sole manager who signs a knowingly false return is personally liable to the Oman Tax Authority for the additional tax and fines; piercing the corporate veil allows the Tax Authority to recover from personal assets, impose a travel ban, and obtain imprisonment of the responsible officers
Failure to Withhold / Pay WHT on Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: The Oman SPC is personally liable to the Tax Authority for the gross amount of unpaid WHT, plus the 1% monthly surcharge until settled, plus an administrative penalty of up to 100% of the withheld amount; the withholding obligation is not extinguished by the beneficiary's residence in a treaty country until the DTA certificate is approved

Director personal liability: The local director and the company secretary are jointly and severally liable for the un-withheld tax if they caused the payment instruction to be issued without the WHT approval; civil restitution, attachment of personal bank accounts, and the freezing of exit rights from Oman may be imposed
Shell Company / Conduit Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Tax Authority is empowered to disregard the interposed SPC, reattribute income to the beneficial owner, deny the entitlement to the 0% dividend WHT and treaty exemptions, impose a 15% CIT reassessment on the reattributed profit, and apply administrative penalties of up to OMR 100,000 for the filing of inaccurate declarations

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced when the SPC transacts with no real independent management; courts may declare the SPC a sham and hold the non-resident owner and local nominee director jointly liable for unpaid corporate tax, civil damages, and criminal fraud penalties, including imprisonment
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Breaches

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under the Oman Anti-Money Laundering and Combating of Financing Terrorism Law (Royal Decree 30/2016 and amendments): monetary fines of OMR 10,000 to OMR 1,000,000, temporary or permanent revocation of the SPC's commercial registration and supermarket permits, asset seizure, and referral for criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: A director, manager or shadow owner who knowingly facilitates the UBO concealment or cash layering is subject to criminal liability, personal financial restitution, a travel ban, disqualification from managerial office, and imprisonment up to the statutory maximum for AML fraud; the limited liability shield is lifted where the company is proven to be an instrument of crime
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Aggressive Intercompany Pricing

Statutory sanction: The Oman Tax Authority may make a primary transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income to the arm's length gross margin, impose a secondary adjustment on the deemed dividend or loan to the shareholder, levy a 15% additional tax on the adjusted income, and add a penalty equal to up to 100% of the tax under-assessed for the missing TP documentation

Director personal liability: The director who signs the return and the preparer who refuses to file TP documentation are personally liable for fines and assessment increases; where the inflated purchase price was knowingly used to strip profits to a foreign related party, the court may revisit the corporate personality and require the director plus the beneficial owner to make good the tax difference from personal assets
