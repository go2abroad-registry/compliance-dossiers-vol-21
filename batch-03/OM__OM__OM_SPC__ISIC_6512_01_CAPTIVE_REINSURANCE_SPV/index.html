Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), electronic services portal at https://taxoman.gov.om

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) must first obtain Commercial Registration from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion; thereafter, the entity must register with the Oman Tax Authority through the OTA e-services portal to obtain the Tax Identification Number (TIN). If the SPC makes taxable supplies exceeding the VAT registration threshold, it must also complete a separate VAT registration through the same portal. Foreign entities not yet established in Oman but making taxable supplies from Oman may obtain a non-resident TIN and VAT registration via the OTA portal; a local tax representative is required where the law mandates it.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the SPC is treated as a resident juridical person taxable in Oman on Oman-source income and on foreign-source income derived through activity attributable to the SPC; no flow-through status is available for an SPC. — The foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) must first obtain Commercial Registration from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion; thereafter, the entity must register with the Oman Tax Authority through the OTA e-services portal to obtain the Tax Identification Number (TIN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the SPC is treated as a resident juridical person taxable in Oman on Oman-source income and on foreign-source income derived through activity attributable to the SPC; no flow-through status is available for an SPC.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available under Oman domestic law. A taxpayer may elect small business relief if annual taxable turnover does not exceed OMR 100,000 and such election is available for a captive reinsurance only if the activity is not otherwise excluded by law. No general entity classification election exists for an SPC; corporate income tax liability follows its juridical form.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 500,000 mandatory registration threshold on aggregate annual taxable supplies; OMR 250,000 threshold for voluntary registration in respect of future taxable supplies. Special provisions may apply to financial services and captive reinsurance supply chains, and the insurance/reinsurance sector is generally subject to VAT on fees and commissions with exemptions for certain insurance transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated when they are exported from Oman to a non-GCC destination and the exporter retains evidence of physical export within the prescribed period. Services supplied to a non-resident person are zero-rated where the service is directly connected to exported goods, directly performed outside Oman, or enjoyed outside the GCC; captive reinsurance services supplied to foreign affiliates may be zero-rated if the recipient is outside the GCC and the benefit of the service is received outside Oman, subject to anti-abuse and VAT place-of-supply rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (E-Export / Single Customs Declaration through Oman Customs / Bayan)
  • Commercial invoice issued by the SPC
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing export from Oman
  • Signed export-contract or insurance policy/underwriting agreement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit confirmation evidencing receipt of export consideration
  • Transport/shipping evidence, including container tracking or port/intermodal transfer records

The Oman Tax Authority has introduced mandatory electronic invoicing in phases. Affected taxpayers, including VAT-registered large taxpayers and entities directed by OTA, must generate invoices through OTA-approved e-invoicing software or the Muqayyad platform, transmit invoice datasets to the Tax Authority in near-real time, and assign a tax authority-approved digital identifier or QR stamp. Invoicing systems must use OTA-compliant APIs and preserve an auditable integrity chain; no local clearance or tax stamp is required for pure B2B services outside the e-invoicing scope until the relevant phase applies.

Exports zero-rated only after substantive compliance; the Tax Authority has the right to conduct post-clearance audits and, upon irregularity, to assess VAT with late-payment penalties of 1% per month on the unpaid VAT, a maximum fixed penalty of OMR 500 per non-compliance declaration, supplementary administrative fines, and criminal penalties including imprisonment and fines not exceeding OMR 20,000 in cases of fraudulent evasion or knowingly false zero-rating certifications. Retrospective clawback of output VAT at 5% applies where the exporter fails to prove that goods left Oman or that services were genuinely supplied to a non-resident recipient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade-related payments, reinsurance premiums, claims settlements, and profit remittances, subject to submission of underlying documentary evidence to the authorized dealer bank. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment injections, are generally liberalized for qualifying entities, but may require CBO reporting or prior approval where financing involves cross-border guarantees, foreign currency borrowing, or repatriation of liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance by a foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) is permitted without a profits-repatriation tax or dividend withholding tax under Omani tax law, provided the entity has filed its corporate tax return and obtained the requisite tax clearance certificate from the Oman Tax Authority. There are no exchange control restrictions on the conversion of OMR into major foreign currencies for bona fide business purposes, nor any requirement to route remittances through a central pool; however, banks apply standard anti-money laundering (AML) and know-your-customer (KYC) checks against the Central Bank of Oman's consolidated instructions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose mandatory export proceeds surrender requirements. Exporters, including captive reinsurance SPVs generating fee or premium income from offshore group entities, are not required to convert foreign currency receipts into OMR or to repatriate such receipts within a statutory timeline. The CBO requires only that export and reinsurance-related foreign currency receipts be received through a licensed bank in Oman if the underlying contract is booked in Oman, and that the bank retains the underlying transaction documents for audit and reporting purposes. Reinsurance premium income may be retained offshore, but should be reflected in the Omani entity's audited financial statements for corporate tax and CBO statistical reporting.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) and, for licensing and foreign exchange policy coordination, the Sultanate of Oman's Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) and the Capital Market Authority (CMA) where insurance/reinsurance regulation applies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Oman does not maintain a dedicated fintech electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime comparable to the UK FCA or European EEA EMI passport. The Central Bank of Oman regulates payment service providers under the Banking Law and the National Payment Systems Law, and any non-bank entity providing cross-border payment or electronic money services must obtain a CBO license or operate under the CBO Regulatory Sandbox. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-regulated EMIs, EU EMIs, or Singapore MPI licensees) may not open operational accounts for Omani entities in the absence of a physical or supervised presence in Oman, and in-bound and out-bound flows must clear through a licensed Omani bank. Use of offshore EMIs as primary treasury clearing rails is therefore legally questionable and operationally fragile; they may be used only as supplementary collection tools if the Omani SPC maintains a fully compliant local bank account as the main treasury node.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

UBO or shareholder is a holding company domiciled in a jurisdiction considered tax-haven or higher-risk (e.g., BVI, Cayman, certain UAE free zones).

Root cause: UBO or shareholder is a holding company domiciled in a jurisdiction considered tax-haven or higher-risk (e.g., BVI, Cayman, certain UAE free zones).

Defense evidence: Entity chart showing the full upstream chain to the ultimate natural-person UBO; notarized and apostilled certificate of good standing; consolidated group financial statements; audited financial statements of the immediate parent; commercial invoice or reinsurance agreement evidencing the ownership rationale; confirmation that the UBO has a substantive residence and tax nexus in a reputable jurisdiction.
The captive reinsurance SPV has a very low capital base relative to the maximum assumed risk (premium leverage red flag).

Root cause: The captive reinsurance SPV has a very low capital base relative to the maximum assumed risk (premium leverage red flag).

Defense evidence: Actuarial opinion or independent reserve report issued by a licensed actuary; reinsurance agreement with defined coverage limits; solvency calculation under Omani insurance/reinsurance guidelines; group parent guarantee and letter of comfort; capital maintenance commitment resolution of the sole shareholder; pro-forma balance sheet showing liquid assets that could absorb claims shock.
Premiums are paid from group entities in restricted or high-risk jurisdictions (e.g., sanctioned countries, high-risk FATF jurisdictions).

Root cause: Premiums are paid from group entities in restricted or high-risk jurisdictions (e.g., sanctioned countries, high-risk FATF jurisdictions).

Defense evidence: FATF jurisdiction risk assessment; sanctions screening report from a reputable compliance database (e.g., Dow Jones, Refinitiv, or World-Check) showing no sanctioned counterparties; confirmatory letters from each parent group entity stating the source of premium funds; chain of underlying policyholder documentation; certificate of residence or tax identification number of each premium payer.
The SPV has no office or substantive staff, or the registered office is a corporate service provider's address shared with many other companies.

Root cause: The SPV has no office or substantive staff, or the registered office is a corporate service provider's address shared with many other companies.

Defense evidence: Signed lease agreement for Omani business premises; employment contracts and civil ID copies of the general manager and finance officer; Omani social insurance and Omani tax registrations; audited financial statements confirming payroll and administrative expenditure; proof of physical signage and utility bills at the stated address.
Expected transaction velocity and counterparty mix are inconsistent with a captive reinsurance SPV (e.g., high volume of third-party, non-affiliate premiums).

Root cause: Expected transaction velocity and counterparty mix are inconsistent with a captive reinsurance SPV (e.g., high volume of third-party, non-affiliate premiums).

Defense evidence: Copy of the reinsurance master agreement or cover notes; risk participation schedule listing only affiliated and approved cedants; board resolution confirming that no third-party risk is assumed; detailed narrative explaining the business rationale of the captive; independent legal opinion confirming the captive is not used as a conduit for third-party money movement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection into the Omani SPC's share capital or additional paid-in capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 from the parent company's bank to the SPC's operational account at a licensed Omani bank. The remitter must state the correct purpose code, generally 'G210' for direct investment capital under the Balance of Payments classification (or a bank-specific code approved by the CBO), and the wire must be accompanied by a board resolution of the parent authorizing the transfer, a copy of the SPC's commercial registration certificate, and the bank's 'Capital Contribution/KYC' form. Funds should be denominated in USD or EUR (or OMR equivalent) and should be named strictly as 'Equity Injection' or 'Share Capital Contribution' on the wire. The receiving bank will perform a source-of-funds verification and issue a Foreign Investment Declaration (if required) for MOCIIP statistical reporting; partial redemption or repatriation of the capital injection requires prior tax clearance and CBO reporting.Outbound trade disbursements for reinsurance claims letters, periodic risk-bearing premium distributions, or claims settlement payments are cleared through the correspondent banking network using SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) for non-documentary payments, or via Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700/MT707) where the underlying contract requires documentary conditions, including presentation of loss confirmation certificates, claim adjustment reports, or proof of loss from an independent adjuster. The Omani bank will debit the SPC's OMR or foreign currency account, convert currencies at prevailing market rates plus a disclosed commission, and retain the underlying reinsurance contract, bordereau, loss report, and tax invoice. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A mandatory Local File is required when the Omani taxpayer's annual gross revenue exceeds OMR 15,000,000; a Master File is additionally required when the multinational group's worldwide revenue exceeds OMR 75,000,000 and the Omani entity is included. Related-party transactions below the revenue threshold are still subject to documentation in a proportionate disclosure file.

Economic substance scope: Captive reinsurance is a relevant activity under the Oman Economic Substance Regulations. The SPC must demonstrate substantive activity in Oman: a physical licensed office, locally resident directors/managers, actuarial staff, decision-making on risk underwriting, claims reserves, and operational expenditure proportionate to premium income, and annual substance reporting to the OTA. Failure to satisfy these tests causes the tax authority to recharacterise the SPC as a shell and to nullify tax relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Additional assessment up to the full amount of evaded tax, fine of 25% of the unpaid tax (minimum OMR 500, maximum OMR 20,000), and daily late-payment surcharge at 15% per annum. Courts may impose imprisonment not exceeding three years for repeated evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives are personally and jointly liable for the unpaid corporate tax if they caused or allowed the evasion; civil restitution orders, freezing of personal bank accounts, travel bans and imprisonment are enforceable.
Transfer Pricing / Arm's-length Violation

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment based on the most appropriate transfer pricing method, penalty of 10% of the value of the under-reported related-party adjustment, late-payment interest of 15% per annum, and loss of treaty relief for improper documentation.

Director personal liability: The chief financial officer and authorised signatories may be held personally liable for the compliance failure where they knowingly signed a false TP declaration; penalties can attach to personal assets and directors may face travel restrictions.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Oman can deny treaty benefits, reassess taxable income at the full 15% without deductions for branch payments, impose penalties of up to OMR 100,000, and order the striking-off of the company from the commercial register.

Director personal liability: Non-resident directors and beneficial owners are treated as de facto directors; they may be served through the OTA international cooperation mechanisms, banned from UAE/GCC travel, and be subject to civil liability for all unpaid taxes.
AML / Sanctions / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Oman's anti-money laundering law: fine of OMR 50,000 to OMR 250,000, imprisonment up to seven years, confiscation of assets, revocation of the economic licence, and cancellation of tax registration.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, and ultimate beneficial owners face criminal prosecution; travel bans are automatic on pending AML charges; personal accounts and property can be frozen within 24 hours of a FIU order.
