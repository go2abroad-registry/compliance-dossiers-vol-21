Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) — Commercial Registration and Licenses Department, Muscat, Sultanate of Oman; consolidated e-filing channel: Invest Easy (investeasy.gov.om).

Governing statute

Royal Decree (RD) No. 18/2019 promulgating the Commercial Companies Law — Articles 1, 2, 13–16 (incorporation procedure) and 43–51 (Single Person Company provisions); Foreign Capital Investment Law RD No. 50/2019 (100% foreign ownership); MoCIIP Executive Regulations under the Commercial Companies Law; Civil Defense Law RD No. 43/2007 for premises clearance.

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum and Articles of Association (MoA/Statutes) of the Single Person Company, executed by the sole shareholder before the authorized MoCIIP notary, accompanied by the electronic Commercial Registration application in the Invest Easy registry.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an SPC under RD 18/2019 Article 43; the MoA states the adopted share capital, which must be fully subscribed by the sole shareholder and deposited into an Omani corporate bank account before CR issuance. The capital-deposit certificate is attached to the notarized MoA; foreign-currency capitalization is converted at the Omani-rial peg (1 OMR ≈ USD 2.60).

Funding & unlock path: PACDA fire-safety clearance requires verified ABC dry-chemical extinguishers (minimum two per 200 m²), emergency exit signage and lighting, and smoke detectors; buildings of four floors or more require annually tested alarm/sprinkler systems. Structural-load inspection by a licensed Omani engineer is mandatory for studio lighting and server racks. Occupational compliance under Royal Decree 45/80 (Labour Law) and the Occupational Safety Regulations: first-aid kit, posted evacuation plan, adequate ventilation and illumination, and electrical-safety clearance where applicable.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an SPC under RD 18/2019 Article 43; the MoA states the adopted share capital, which must be fully subscribed by the sole shareholder and deposited into an Omani corporate bank account before CR issuance. The capital-deposit certificate is attached to the notarized MoA; foreign-currency capitalization is converted at the Omani-rial peg (1 OMR ≈ USD 2.60).

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned SPC: (1) incorporate via MoCIIP 'Invest in Oman' e-registration and obtain Commercial Registration (CR) and Chamber of Commerce card; (2) the income-tax TIN is assigned automatically upon CR data integration with the Tax Authority; (3) register for VAT ID in TMS within 30 days after taxable supplies/imports exceed OMR 38,500 in any rolling 12-month period (voluntary registration from OMR 19,250; non-residents making taxable supplies in Oman must register without threshold); (4) open an Omani bank account, appoint a local VAT/tax representative if required, and maintain authenticated TMS e-services access. Estimated completion: 5–15 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Omani law; the SPC is mandatorily a taxable juridical person. Available statutory elections: (i) VAT group registration for related Oman-resident entities under VAT Law Art. 13; (ii) small-business CIT instalment/return treatment where profits are within legislated SME thresholds; (iii) designated free-zone tax incentive election (reduced or 0% income tax and VAT special regimes); (iv) advance pricing agreement (APA) application for cross-border digital-value transfers; (v) VAT reverse-charge election for specified imported services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 38,500 of taxable supplies or imports in any 12-month period; voluntary registration: OMR 19,250; non-resident suppliers with Omani taxable nexus must register irrespective of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods exported physically from Oman (or to designated free zones) with customs evidence, and to 'exported services' supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside Oman at the time of supply, where the benefit and use of the digital stock-photo/AI licensing service occur outside Oman and the supply does not relate to Omani immovable property or goods located in Oman. For B2B digital licensing, the supplier must hold the license/use agreement, customer jurisdiction and VAT-status records, delivery/API download logs, and payment-rail settlement evidence. B2C licences to non-resident consumers are outside Omani VAT scope when effectively used outside Oman; licences consumed in Oman attract 5% VAT.

  • Customs export declaration (Oman Customs Al-Bayan / Single Administrative Document) for any physical export
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing goods leaving Oman
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice matching invoiced digital-export proceeds
  • Digital export evidence pack: licensing contract, customer jurisdiction and VAT-status attestation, delivery/API logs, and Stripe/PayPal settlement reports

Oman is implementing phased controlled e-invoicing under the Tax Authority E-Invoicing Regulation. VAT-registered businesses must issue structured electronic tax invoices (XML/JSON) through certified e-invoicing software or the TMS portal, preserve OTA-issued invoice sequence numbers and digital stamps, and provide near-real-time fiscal data via API for designated cohorts (B2G and large-taxpayer phase commencing 2025, broader B2B/B2C phases thereafter). Until full phase-in, all taxable supplies require OTA-compliant e-invoices or paper tax invoices produced in an electronic VAT-compliant system; non-compliance attracts administrative fines.

OTA may reopen returns for five years (extended where fraud or evasion is shown), claw back incorrectly zero-rated VAT with statutory late-payment charges, impose fines up to 100% of evaded tax or up to OMR 100,000 for false or forged documents, deregister the SPC VAT number, suspend refund rights, and refer deliberate falsification for criminal prosecution; directors and shareholders face joint personal liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman operates under a pegged exchange rate regime with the Omani Rial (OMR) fixed to the U.S. dollar. The OMR is fully convertible for current-account transactions, including cross-border payments for the commercial stock photography and AI-licensing business. Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity contribution into an Omani SPC and outbound profit repatriation, are permitted for 100% foreign-owned entities, provided the underlying funds are routed through a licensed Omani bank and evidenced by audited financial statements, board resolutions, and corporate registration documents. Profit remittance does not require periodic government approval, but the paying bank will require tax clearance certificates and compliance with anti-money-laundering verification of the ultimate beneficial owner. Dividends and royalty payments are subject to relevant withholding tax obligations administered by the Oman Tax Authority; banks treat settlement of such taxes as a precondition for outward remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose a statutory surrender period or a mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. An Omani SPC may retain export earnings in foreign currencies in a CBO-approved foreign-currency account with a licensed commercial bank. There is no requirement to convert receipts into OMR within a fixed timeline. However, as a practical banking requirement, the exporter must maintain a complete audit trail of commercial invoices, platform statements, payment gateway settlement reports, and underlying license contracts supporting each cross-border receipt. Because the target business is digital stock photography and generative visual assets, all export proceeds should be matched against ISIC 7420 / NAICS 541922 service descriptions to avoid mischaracterization as IP sale proceeds, which could trigger enhanced AML review and potential tax differencing.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), established under the Banking Law as the sole monetary and foreign exchange authority for Oman.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low acceptability as a primary treasury rail. Unlicensed offshore digital EMI providers (e-money institutions) and third-party payment platforms cannot hold OMR-denominated settlement accounts or provide a fully compliant local clearing route absent a CBO license. Stripe, PayPal, and similar gateways may be used only as the downstream payment collection layer for digital content licensing; the upstream liquidity must ultimately settle into the Omani SPC's licensed domestic bank account via regulated SWIFT or local rails. The Omani bank will treat offshore EMI funds as high-risk unless the SPC can produce the merchant agreement, settlement statements, and evidence that no Omani consumer-facing e-money service is being provided by the EMI without CBO authorization. Overall legal viability: limited to payment collection, never as the primary legal account for statutory corporate and tax obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing is via a single direct SWIFT MT103 from the ultimate beneficial owner's overseas corporate or personal bank account to the Omani SPC's bank account in USD or OMR. The remittance must contain an explicit purpose code such as FDI equity capital injection; the beneficiary bank will require the share subscription agreement, the certificate of incorporation, the Invest Easy extract, board resolution, and an anti-money-laundering beneficial-ownership declaration. The Omani bank will likely ask the sender to answer a pre-funding questionnaire when the amount exceeds USD 50,000. It is critical to avoid using an unlicensed money exchanger, an OTC crypto service, or any third-party payout aggregator for the capital injection. Any funds first received into PayPal/Stripe and then routed into the Oman account will be re-categorised as trade receipts, not share capital, which can distort the statutory balance sheet and erode the tax shield available for the SPC.Outbound clearing rails for the stock photography and AI assets marketplace should be segmented. For high-value license payouts above USD 5,000, use direct SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary bank details, an underlying license or royalty agreement, and invoice description aligned with ISIC 7420. For recurring small-value royalty settlements from stock agencies, a monthly aggregated MT103 with a platform reconciliation report is preferred to reduce transaction costs. Commercial letters of credit are technically available but commercially unsuitable for low-value digital content licensing due to documentary presentation costs; LCs should be reserved only for high-value, physically deliverable visual asset archives or corporate content-work-for-hire contracts. For OMR-denominated domestic disbursements, use local credit transfer through the Omani Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) operated by CBO, or the chequing system for small amounts. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required for aggregate controlled/related-party transactions exceeding OMR 1,000,000 in a fiscal year; a consolidated Master File is additionally required where the ultimate parent’s group revenue exceeds OMR 50,000,000. A simplified TP disclosure annexure is mandatory for all SPCs with non-resident shareholders even below the formal documentation threshold.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not operate a UAE-style ESR filing regime, but its Commercial Companies Law, Income Tax Law GAAR and the Oman Tax Authority anti-avoidance practice require the SPC to maintain physical office premises, Omani management decision-making, adequate staff/contractors, and a real profit-generating function. A stock-photo SPC whose only Omani footprint is a post-office box while all algorithm/asset management is performed outside Oman is exposed to recharacterisation as a shell/conduit, denial of treaty benefits, and assessment of Omani-source royalty-like income in the hands of the beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imposition of up to 200% of the tax evaded as an administrative penalty, criminal fine up to OMR 25,000, and imprisonment up to 3 years for willful evasion; the tax authority may additionally publish the SPC's name and suspend all commercial licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated Omani manager are jointly and severally liable for the SPC's tax debt where false returns were signed with knowledge; Oman courts may impose a travel ban under the civil execution law, a debarment directive from acting as director, and restitution orders against personal bank accounts for funds diverted to foreign parents.
Shell Company and Artificial Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: OTA can disregard the SPC under general anti-abuse rules and assess 15% tax on gross receipts without deductions, impose a 50% penalty for gross negligence, and refer the company to the Commercial Registry for striking-off or license revocation.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for conduct tantamount to misuse of legal personality; the non-resident ultimate shareholder can be held personally liable for unpaid tax and penalties, and directors may be ordered to refund the amounts withdrawn while the SPC remained insolvent.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Oman AML Law (RD 30/2016, as amended by RD 15/2021) imposes fines up to OMR 100,000, asset confiscation, and imprisonment up to 7 years; the Central Bank of Oman may withdraw the SPC's payment gateway/SWIFT access and blacklist the entity from any future licence.

Director personal liability: Each director, compliance officer, and the beneficial owner can be criminally prosecuted for failure to file Suspicious Activity Reports; the Oman Public Prosecutor may impose a precautionary travel ban and freeze personal funds pending criminal proceedings.
