Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Single Person Company under RD 18/2019; however, the declared share capital must be adequate for the software development object, fully subscribed, and deposited and frozen in an Omani bank account (escrow / capital deposit) opened in the SPC's name prior to application; a bank Certificate of Deposit evidencing the credited amount is mandatory for foreign-owned SPC incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense and Ambulance Authority (Royal Oman Police) fire-safety certification for the office premises, covering fire-detection alarm systems, portable extinguishers, emergency evacuation signage, and assembly points; structural load inspection evidenced by the landlord's building compliance certificate; workplace occupancy certificate from Muscat Municipality; and compliance with Ministry of Labour occupational safety and health requirements for workstations, ergonomics, electrical safety, and emergency preparedness.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Single Person Company under RD 18/2019; however, the declared share capital must be adequate for the software development object, fully subscribed, and deposited and frozen in an Omani bank account (escrow / capital deposit) opened in the SPC's name prior to application; a bank Certificate of Deposit evidencing the credited amount is mandatory for foreign-owned SPC incorporation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (Secretariat General of Taxation), Ministry of Finance; official national e-Portal: https://taxoman.gov.om (Tax Portal e-Services).

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent entity first incorporates the Single Person Company (SPC) as a 100% foreign-owned Omani limited liability company with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MCIIP) through the Commercial Registry and the Invest in Oman platform. After issuance of the Commercial Registration Certificate and approved constitutional documents (Memorandum and Articles of Association), the SPC must register with the Oman Tax Authority through the Tax Portal e-Services system to obtain its Tax Identification Number (TIN) and Tax Card. The procedure requires submission of the commercial registration, shareholder and director identification, certified constitutional documents, registered lease/address proof, and power of attorney. VAT registration is made separately through the same Tax Portal when the SPC meets or expects to meet the taxable supply threshold, or voluntarily from the OMR 500,000 threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign parent entity first incorporates the Single Person Company (SPC) as a 100% foreign-owned Omani limited liability company with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MCIIP) through the Commercial Registry and the Invest in Oman platform. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Oman has no check-the-box or entity classification election regime. The SPC is automatically treated as an entity-level taxpayer for both corporate income tax and VAT purposes. There is no pass-through election available for an Omani SPC. No hybrid mismatch or branch-equivalent elections exist under Omani income tax law; cross-border arrangements remain subject to OECD hybrid mismatch analysis and general anti-avoidance rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 1,000,000 in annual taxable supplies (mandatory registration); OMR 500,000 in annual taxable supplies (voluntary registration). Non-resident taxable persons making taxable supplies in Oman may be required to register regardless of turnover under the Tax Authority's determination.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate is available on (i) physical goods exported from Oman, evidenced by a customs export declaration and actual removal from Oman; and (ii) services supplied to a non-resident customer where the supply is directly connected to export goods or where the benefit, use, and effective consumption occur entirely outside Oman. For an Offshore Development Center, zero-rating applies to software engineering, development, maintenance, and cloud-facilitation services supplied to a foreign customer whose business establishment is outside Oman and who has no nexus or collection obligation in Oman. The ODC must prove the non-resident status of the customer, the offshore place of use, absence of any local beneficiary, and real economic delivery outside Oman through contracts, service acceptance, server usage logs, IP ownership, and evidence of foreign-currency remittance into Oman.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, and/or Airway Bill, in each case where tangible media or goods accompany the software export, showing actual departure from Oman
  • Signed and dated offshore service agreement, statement of work, and master services agreement naming the non-resident customer, delivery location, and outside-Oman place of benefit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an Omani licensed bank, or equivalent SWIFT/MT103 proof, evidencing full foreign-currency payment for the zero-rated services received in Oman within 180 days
  • VAT zero-rated tax invoice issued in accordance with the VAT Law, showing the SPC VAT registration number, customer VAT/GST number if registered, zero-rating basis, sequential invoice number, and place-of-supply statement
  • Supporting technical and economic substance evidence, including server access logs, code repository location, remote deployment records, customer acceptance certificates, or similar proof demonstrating that the software services were provided from Oman for consumption exclusively outside Oman

As of the current implementation timetable, there is no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping requirement for B2B software export services in Oman. The Oman Tax Authority has announced an e-invoicing transition roadmap, but the ODC is not currently required to interface with a central clearance platform. The SPC must nevertheless issue sequential VAT invoices through approved systems or the Tax Portal, display its VAT Registration Number and TIN, and retain electronic records for at least five years. No real-time API validation or mandatory digital stamp is presently imposed on exported IT services.

The Oman Tax Authority conducts risk-based audits and cross-matches VAT returns and zero-rating claims with customs filings, banking remittance records, contracts, tax invoices, and customer residency data. Any export zero-rating claim that fails documentary substantiation is reclassified as a taxable domestic supply, resulting in assessment of 5% VAT on the full invoice value, disallowance of related input VAT through clawback, and statutory late-payment penalties and administrative fines. Knowingly fraudulent zero-rating or false export declarations constitute VAT/tax evasion and attract criminal penalties, including imprisonment of up to five years and fines of up to OMR 1,000,000, as well as cancellation or suspension of the SPC's Tax Card, commercial registration deregistration, and reassessment of all previously refunded export VAT within the applicable statutory limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current account transactions under Oman's observance of IMF Article VIII; no prior CBO approval is required for trade in goods, services, and profit remittances related to licensed economic activities. Capital account transactions for non-resident 100% foreign-owned Single Person Companies (SPCs) are permitted under Oman's liberal foreign investment regime, but all inflows and outflows of capital, shareholder loans, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds must be routed through an Authorised Dealer (commercial bank). Remittance of dividends and branch/SPC profits is allowed provided the SPC holds a valid Oman Tax Registration Number (TIN), has filed audited financial statements, and obtains a tax clearance certificate for accumulated taxes at the time of remittance if the amount crosses the Oman Tax Authority's reporting threshold. The OMR is pegged to the US Dollar at a fixed parity of 1 USD = 0.3845 OMR; market convertibility risk is therefore minimal, but standard anti-money laundering (AML) and Know Your Customer (KYC) checks will apply to every cross-border transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds or a mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters under an Offshore Development Center (ODC) licence may retain software-export service revenues in foreign currency (e.g., USD, EUR) in a Foreign Currency Account or immediately convert to OMR without CBO approval. For goods exports, the CBO and commercial banks generally expect repatriation of the full invoice value within 180 days from the date of shipment; for services exports, typical bank policy is to acknowledge a 90-day collection cycle unless the underlying contract provides otherwise. Authorised Dealers must submit to CBO reports for all export proceeds exceeding OMR 20,000 per transaction, and an exporter that keeps proceeds abroad for more than the bank-approved term must document the commercial justification to avoid a report-based AML trigger.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury bank; Medium as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail. Wise B2B and Payoneer are not licensed by the Central Bank of Oman and do not hold a local banking or Payment Service Provider (PSP) licence under Oman's Payment and Settlement System Law; therefore, they cannot provide an OMR-denominated IBAN or serve as the principal account for a domestic SPC. Foreign fintech EMIs may be used for low-value external vendor payments or for receiving settlement from international freelance platforms, but the ODC must maintain a resident bank account with an Omani Authorised Dealer to satisfy capital injection, payroll, and tax compliance obligations. Any use of a foreign EMI as a flow-through for business revenue is legally constrained because it falls outside CBO-regulated financial institutions and may be treated as operating an unlicensed payment business if the activity is conducted from Oman. The recommended compliant structure is to use Wise/Payoneer solely for ancillary expense routing with contractual notification to the Omani tax advisor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection is executed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for second-leg transmission) from the foreign parent's bank to the SPC's Capital Escrow Account at an Omani Authorised Dealer. The remitter must state the correct purpose codes recognised by CBO: for direct foreign equity injection, use purpose code 'INV' (Direct Investment Equity) or the bank's internal code 'M1000' for inward capital transfers; for shareholder loans, use purpose code 'LOAN' (Intercompany Lending) with a certified copy of the loan agreement. The receiving Omani bank will screen the transfer to confirm the source of funds through the remitting institution's AML assurance; amounts above OMR 20,000 trigger an automatic reporting to CBO's Foreign Exchange Transaction Reporting System. If the SPC's paid-up capital is used to purchase fixed assets or fund operating expenses, the capital must first be credited to the Capital Escrow Account and then reclassified to the Operational Account upon submission of a board resolution and a certificate from the licensed auditor. To avoid delays, the parent should use its primary banker and send a pre-advice to the Omani relationship manager with the beneficiary tax identification number and bank branch code.For ODC service exports to overseas clients, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 with the BPM6 balance-of-payments purpose code for telecommunication, computer, and information services (code 223). Monthly salaries of local ODC engineers are paid via the bank's domestic bulk payment file in OMR conversion from USD, supported by an employee payroll register; no capital import approval is needed for salary remittances. For equipment purchases or licence acquisitions, both SWIFT MT103 and an Irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit issued under UCP600 by the Omani bank are permissible; the LC rail is used when the vendor requires documentary protection and is subject to the bank's trade finance facility and collateral. All outbound trade disbursements above OMR 10,000 must be supported by a signed contract, detailed invoice, and, for intra-group service fees, a transfer pricing report demonstrating arm's length pricing, because Oman's Tax Law (Royal Decree 28/2009, as amended) may disallow deductions for excessive cross-border payments. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 500,000 aggregate controlled transactions or intercompany cost allocations (operational Oman benchmark; documentation required when the Oman Tax Authority requests it under the OECD-aligned rule and when related-party transactions exceed normal commercial levels).

Economic substance scope: Oman does not have a dedicated Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime. However, the Oman Tax Authority applies place-of-effective-management, permanent establishment and general anti-abuse doctrines. The ODC must maintain local physical office, qualified staff, substantive client contracts, board minutes and Omani bank accounts. A paper-only or sham ODC will be re-characterized as a foreign parent's PE or an abusive shell, resulting in loss of CIT/treaty benefits and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Assessment of all underpaid corporate income tax plus 2% monthly late interest, penalty up to 100% of evaded tax, criminal prosecution, fines up to OMR 50,000 and imprisonment for up to three years depending on gravity.

Director personal liability: Directors, signatories and resident managers who knowingly participate in false filings are personally and jointly liable for the evaded tax, exposed to travel bans, asset attachment and criminal imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced for fraud.
Sham Offshore Entity / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of the ODC as a shell or foreign PE, disallowance of expenses, upward assessment of 15% CIT on deemed Omani-source profits, denial of treaty relief and fines up to OMR 50,000 plus penalties.

Director personal liability: A director who arrays a sham arrangement to evade tax faces personal restitution, travel bans, disqualification from board service in Oman, and imprisonment if the Omani court finds fraudulent abuse of the SPC form.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment by Oman Tax Authority; additional 15% CIT on the upward adjustment, late interest at 2% monthly, penalty up to 100% of additional tax and mandatory correction of the tax return.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve unsubstantiated intercompany pricing without a compliant transfer pricing study are personally liable for the resulting tax adjustment if they signed the return or directed the transaction; personal fines and travel restrictions may be imposed.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Filing Violation

Statutory sanction: Omani AML Law and CBO rules impose administrative fines of OMR 5,000-100,000 per infringement, possible license suspension/dissolution for the SPC, and criminal penalties including imprisonment for concealment of criminal property.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers are personally liable for false beneficial ownership declarations and willfully obscured KYC files; sanctions include civil restitution, freezing orders, travel bans, and director disqualification.
