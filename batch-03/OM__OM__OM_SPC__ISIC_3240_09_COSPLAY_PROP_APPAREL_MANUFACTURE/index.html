Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Secretariat General of Taxation (SGT) of the Ministry of Finance of the Sultanate of Oman; electronic filing and registration portal: Oman Tax Portal (https://taxoman.gov.om). Oman Customs (Directorate General of Customs under the Royal Oman Police / Asyad Group) operates the national Al-Bayan export declaration and customs clearance environment.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SPC at the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) -- name reservation, constitutional documents and Commercial Registration (CR) certificate; 100% foreign ownership is lawful and the SPC has no statutory minimum paid-in capital requirement. Step 2: Register the SPC as an employer with the Social Protection Fund (SPF) to obtain the employer registration number before hiring staff. Step 3: Apply for a Tax Registration Number (TRN) on the Oman Tax Portal after CR issuance; the application must include the CR certificate, articles of association or deed of incorporation, registered office lease, passport and national ID copies of the owner and authorised signatories, board or owner resolution appointing the tax representative, and a power of attorney if a licensed tax agent is engaged. Step 4: The SGT verifies the applicant and issues the CIT TRN; the VAT registration is assessed simultaneously for mandatory registration when annual taxable turnover exceeds OMR 38,500 or voluntary registration when turnover exceeds OMR 19,250; non-resident suppliers with an Oman VAT nexus must register regardless of threshold. Step 5: Open the corporate bank account after TRN issuance (AML/KYC document package: CR, TRN/VAT certificate, owner passport, source of funds declaration) and complete the Oman Chamber of Commerce and Industry (OCCI) membership registration.

The first OMR 30,000 of annual taxable income is exempt, and taxable income above OMR 30,000 is charged to CIT at 15%. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation -- Under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 28/2009, as amended) and applicable procedure laws, the Omani SPC is a resident juridical person taxed in its own name on its worldwide income from all sources; an SPC is not a transparent entity and cannot elect pass-through treatment. The first OMR 30,000 of annual taxable income is exempt, and taxable income above OMR 30,000 is charged to CIT at 15%. A foreign-owned SPC is fully within the territorial scope of Omani taxation and is not a tax-exempt zone entity.

Available election forms

Omani tax law does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections; the SPC is statutorily fixed as an Entity_Level_Taxation taxpayer. Available elections that do exist include: (i) election of a non-calendar accounting year for CIT purposes subject to SGT approval; (ii) voluntary VAT registration election where taxable turnover exceeds OMR 19,250 but remains below the OMR 38,500 mandatory threshold; (iii) VAT Group election permitting related persons to register with a single representative member for one consolidated VAT account; and (iv) the annual installment and simplified accounting methods for small taxpayers where the SGT approves the simplified mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration is triggered when the annual taxable turnover of the SPC exceeds OMR 38,500 (approximately USD 100,000); the threshold is measured over a rolling 12-month period. Voluntary VAT registration becomes available once taxable turnover exceeds OMR 19,250 (approximately USD 50,000). A non-resident person who makes taxable supplies in Oman is required to register for VAT irrespective of the threshold where the person has a place of establishment in Oman or makes remote/electronic supplies to non-registered persons in Oman.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the Omani VAT regime when the SPC exports physical goods (licensed theatrical costume robes, cosplay apparel and synthetic wigs) from Oman to a final destination outside the GCC, provided the goods depart within 90 days of the supply and the exporter holds the contractual, customs and remittance evidence. Zero-rating also applies to supplies of goods to a VAT-registered customer in another GCC state if the customer furnishes a valid VAT identification number and the goods are transported directly from Oman to that state in accordance with intra-GCC transport rules. Services supplied to a non-resident recipient may be zero-rated where the effective use of the benefit is entirely outside Oman and no part of the service is performed or enjoyed in Oman. Where output supplies are zero-rated, the SPC may credit input VAT incurred on related purchases and may claim a refund of any remaining input VAT credit through the periodic VAT return and the Oman Tax Portal refund process.

  • Original Customs Export Declaration issued through Oman Customs Al-Bayan system, stamped with the exit port code, transport mode and date of departure
  • Original Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing shipment from an Omani seaport/airport to the final destination outside the GCC
  • Commercial invoice in English/Arabic describing the cosplay garments, prop apparel and synthetic wigs with quantities, unit values and Incoterms
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (or preferential origin certificate if a FTA claim is made)
  • Signed sales contract or purchase order issued by the non-GCC overseas buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Omani bank confirming receipt of foreign currency export proceeds within the Central Bank of Oman repatriation window

As of the reference date, Oman has not implemented mandatory real-time fiscal clearance of tax invoices before issuance. The SGT has announced a phased national e-invoicing mandate for B2B and B2G transactions, with the initial cohorts consisting of large taxpayers and government contractors; the future framework will require structured XML invoice data submission through approved APIs and the affixing of a digital stamp / QR code for authenticity. Until the mandate enters into force, the SPC must comply with the current VAT invoicing rules: issue tax invoices containing all particulars under RDL 121/2020 and the Executive Regulations, express amounts in Omani Rial, retain invoices and credit notes for ten years, and integrate compatible accounting/fiscal software that will connect to the SGT sandbox when the electronic clearance mandate is activated.

The SGT, Oman Customs and the Central Bank of Oman cross-verify zero-rating claims against Al-Bayan customs records, cargo manifests, bank remittance advices and the exporter's VAT returns. Failure to provide the customs export declaration, a compliant transport document or the inward remittance certificate within the statutory timelines, or any evidence of circular trade, fictitious export or forged transport documentation, triggers a retrospective reversal of the zero-rating. The SGT will assess VAT at 5% on the full export consideration, charge late settlement interest at the CBO prescribed rate, impose administrative delay penalties and can levy tax evasion fines that may reach 200% of the tax evaded under the Unified Tax Procedures Law and VAT law. In especially serious cases, criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment for up to three years, confiscation of goods, suspension of the exporter's TRN and customs export privileges, and clawback of all refunds previously paid, together with interest and recovery proceedings against the SPC and its licensed tax agent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman maintains an open capital and current account regime with no general exchange controls, and the Omani Rial is pegged to the US Dollar at a fixed mid-rate of OMR 1 = USD 2.6008. Full convertibility applies for trade payments, dividends, service fees, and capital repatriation by foreign-owned entities, subject only to standard anti-money laundering, sanctions screening, and tax-clearance documentation. Profit remittance and liquidation proceeds are legally transferable through licensed commercial banks; practical hurdles are primarily documentary, requiring audited financial statements, board shareholder resolutions, and tax status certificates rather than quantitative convertibility restrictions. There are no separate tiered official, parallel, or black-market rates applicable to licensed banking channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for surrender of export proceeds in foreign currency. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts offshore or credit them to foreign-currency accounts maintained with Omani banks, provided the underlying export shipment is supported by customs declarations, commercial invoices, and bank records that satisfy CBO anti-money laundering traceability expectations. No compulsory repatriation or conversion obligation exists for Omani exporters, but all export-related transactions routed through resident banks must carry accurate purpose codes and substantiating trade documentation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) acting as the national monetary authority and the designated Foreign Exchange Administration under the Oman Exchange Circulars and the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000 as amended)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium for offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs). Oman requires any entity performing payment account or electronic money services to be licensed by the CBO under the Payment Systems Law and associated CBO regulations; offshore EMIs unlicensed in Oman cannot lawfully open settlement accounts, hold OMR balances, or provide domestic payment services. An offshore EMI may only support the Oman SPC through outward/inward funding using its own correspondent banking channels if the SPC maintains a primary operational relationship with a CBO-licensed commercial bank. Legal constraints include sanctions screening, data-localization expectations for resident payment records, and the absence of CBO reciprocity with most non-EMEA EMI charters. As a practical matter, the entity's target payment rails (Stripe and Shopify Payments) will require integration through the Omani bank account or an approved third-party acquiring arrangement, not through an unlicensed digital EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injection from the foreign shareholder must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the beneficial owner's verified bank account to the Oman SPC's operational or capital collection account at a CBO-licensed bank. The payment should carry the standard balance-of-payments purpose code for foreign direct investment (loan or equity contribution), commonly referenced as Oman BOPS code '5100' or the bank-designated FDI equity code, and the remitter's bank should use a clear reference such as 'OM_SPC share capital contribution' to avoid AML ambiguity. Supporting documents for the bank file include the notarized shareholders' resolution, proof of non-resident ownership, updated CR/Chamber of Commerce registration, and the capital allocation schedule. Lower-risk alternative funding is an internal current-account loan accompanied by a board-approved loan agreement and OECD-compliant interest terms, routed over the same SWIFT rail with purpose code 'trade finance other' or 'intercompany financing' as accepted by the CBO reporting framework.The principal clearing rail for trade settlements is SWIFT MT103 for direct wire disbursements to suppliers, logistics providers, and licensors. For larger or confirmed transactions, a commercial letter of credit (MT700) issued by the Omani bank in favor of the overseas beneficiary provides payment guarantee and documentary compliance, especially for bulk fabric and synthetic fiber import from Asian suppliers. The SPC may also use MT202 for interbank transfers and domestic transfer rails for OMR settlement. All outbound payments must be matched against invoices, packing lists, customs documents, and purchase orders, and where the payment is to a third-party agent, the bank will require a written commercial agency agreement and authorization from the beneficiary. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 500,000 (approx. USD 1.3 million) aggregate related-party transactions per fiscal year; also applicable when the taxpayer is in a controlled transaction with associated enterprises and meets the threshold for Master File (OMR 3 million) and Local File (OMR 500,000) under Oman TP Guidelines (Royal Decree 90/2021 implementing BEPS 1.0).

Economic substance scope: Oman’s Commercial Companies Law and the unified tax reporting framework require economic substance in Oman for resident entities claiming tax residency benefits, reinvestment exceptions, or treaty benefits. The SPC must demonstrate physical presence (office/facility), qualified personnel with decision-making authority, and local expenditure proportionate to its income-generating activities in manufacturing cosplay costumes, props, and wigs. The Economic Substance Regulations analogous to GCC frameworks do not apply with the same automated ESR filings, but the OTA can apply substance-over-form, beneficial ownership, and GAAR rules to disregard contracts or reclassify income where substance is lacking; failure to establish substance may trigger denial of treaty relief, recharacterisation of payments, or phantom taxation of untaxed related-party income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: A penalty of 100% of the tax understated plus late interest of 0.1% per day, and criminal fines of up to OMR 50,000; imprisonment for up to 5 years under Royal Decree 28/2009 articles 43–46 for fraudulent tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and sole shareholders can be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; travel bans may be imposed for tax debts exceeding OMR 5,000; personal criminal imprisonment applies for intentional evasion even if the company is a separate legal entity.
Shell Company and Substance Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of income, denial of treaty benefits, disallowance of deductions, a 100% adjustment penalty on the incremental taxable income; possible cancellation or suspension of the Commercial Registration (CR).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed under joint liability provisions for the resulting corporate tax shortfall; travel restrictions under Oman’s debt recovery and tax enforcement law; no direct imprisonment without criminal conviction for a separate fraud offence.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment to the taxable income, a 25%–100% adjustment penalty, late interest, and up to OMR 1,000–5,000 administrative fines for failure to file or substantiate documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certify false TP documentation can be subject to directors’ liability under the Companies Law for the corporation’s tax liabilities; criminal prosecution for document forgery may lead to imprisonment.
AML and Undeclared Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: AML fines under Decree 30/2016 of up to OMR 100,000 per violation, freezing of bank accounts, and confiscation of illicitly derived assets; civil liability for unreported proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face criminal liability for AML offences regardless of corporate veil: imprisonment of 1–7 years for money laundering, orders for asset repatriation, and travel bans during criminal investigation.
Late Filing and Non-Compliance (Procedural)

Statutory sanction: Registration penalty of OMR 100; late filing penalty of OMR 100 plus OMR 500 for continued default; daily surcharge of 0.1% on unpaid tax; possible strike-off of the CR and closure of payment gateway accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held strictly liable for unpaid administrative penalties where they failed to act; travel ban and bank garnishment are enforceable against personal assets of the director for the company’s default under Oman’s Tax Collection Law if the director is a guarantor or controlling person.
