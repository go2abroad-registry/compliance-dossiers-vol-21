Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Single Person Company under the Oman Commercial Companies Law. The issued capital is declared in the AOA and is stated in the commercial registration file. For 100% foreign-owned SPC, the capital attributable to the foreign shareholder must be traceable through an Omani licensed bank when MOCIIP or the bank requires a capital availability certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Royal Oman Police Directorate General of Civil Defense and Ambulance clearance for the warehouse suite covering exits, extinguishers, emergency lighting and rack stability; Muscat Municipality occupancy certification; and, if the SPC employs more than ten workers, an occupational health and safety assessment under the Ministry of Labour.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Single Person Company under the Oman Commercial Companies Law. The issued capital is declared in the AOA and is stated in the commercial registration file. For 100% foreign-owned SPC, the capital attributable to the foreign shareholder must be traceable through an Omani licensed bank when MOCIIP or the bank requires a capital availability certificate.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), Taxoman e-services portal at https://taxoman.gov.om. Export goods clearance is administered by the Royal Oman Police - Directorate General of Customs via the Bayan/ASYCUDA World system.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the SPC with MOCIIP (Invest Easy) as a single-shareholder foreign-owned LLC; obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) and the Oman Chamber of Commerce certificate. 2) Register on the OTA Taxoman portal for CIT; file the standard corporate registration form within 3 months of commencement using the CR, SPC deed, proof of registered office, and a POA for a resident tax agent (required when no resident director is in place); OTA issues the Tax Card/TIN. 3) File the VAT registration within 30 days after the month in which taxable turnover reaches or is expected to reach OMR 38,500 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration starts at OMR 19,250; a non-resident person making taxable supplies in Oman must register irrespective of turnover and appoint a VAT representative. 4) Activate e-filing credentials and link the Bayan/ASYCUDA customs identifier for export declarations. 5) Keep records for 10 years and maintain the OTA monthly/quarterly e-filing schedule.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — 1) Incorporate the SPC with MOCIIP (Invest Easy) as a single-shareholder foreign-owned LLC; obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) and the Oman Chamber of Commerce certificate. 2) Register on the OTA Taxoman portal for CIT; file the standard corporate registration form within 3 months of commencement using the CR, SPC deed, proof of registered office, and a POA for a resident tax agent (required when no resident director is in place); OTA issues the Tax Card/TIN. 3) File the VAT registration within 30 days after the month in which taxable turnover reaches or is expected to reach OMR 38,500 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration starts at OMR 19,250; a non-resident person making taxable supplies in Oman must register irrespective of turnover and appoint a VAT representative. 4) Activate e-filing credentials and link the Bayan/ASYCUDA customs identifier for export declarations. 5) Keep records for 10 years and maintain the OTA monthly/quarterly e-filing schedule.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Oman has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election; the SPC is mandatorily an entity-level resident taxpayer and cannot be treated as a pass-through or branch for Omani tax. Statutory elections available: (i) VAT Tax Group election (common-control related persons established in Oman); (ii) SME tax relief (0% on first OMR 100,000 taxable income for up to 3 years for qualifying micro/small firms); (iii) participation exemption claim for qualifying foreign shareholdings; (iv) foreign tax credit offset election for foreign-branch/PE profits; and (v) treaty-based PE and relief claims under Oman's DTAs. Oman has not enacted domestic ATAD-2 hybrid mismatch rules, so classification follows the Omani legal-form statute. The 2025 OECD Pillar Two domestic top-up tax register applies only to MNEs above EUR 750m consolidated turnover; the MRO e-commerce SPC is outside that scope unless part of such a group.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 38,500 mandatory threshold (actual or projected taxable supplies over rolling 12 months); voluntary registration at OMR 19,250; non-resident suppliers with Omani VAT nexus register with no threshold via a VAT representative.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Articles 38-39 of the Oman VAT Law for: (i) export of MRO industrial goods from Oman to a destination outside the GCC (or to another GCC state with subsequent removal from the GCC), provided physical export occurs within 90 days of the tax invoice date and the SPC retains proof of removal; (ii) services directly related to the exported goods (freight, international transport). The MRO e-commerce transaction must be a genuine sale to a non-resident buyer with risk transfer (EXW/FOB/CIF acceptable) and payment traceable by SWIFT MT103 or credit-card acquiring statement. No prior OTA approval is needed, but the exporter must hold the complete evidence file before treating the output as 0%. Exports to Omani free zones remain subject to the free-zone customs regime; removal from a free zone outside Oman can be zero-rated with bond records.

  • Oman Customs Export Declaration (Bayan/ASYCUDA Single Administrative Document) bearing the declaration number, HS code of the MRO items, quantity and customs stamp of the exit port
  • Original Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing loading at an Omani port (Salalah/Sohar/Duqm/Muscat) and discharge outside the GCC
  • VAT tax invoice raised at 0% including the OTA VAT registration number, buyer name/address, item-level HS codes and consideration in the invoiced currency
  • Certificate of Origin issued by the Oman Chamber of Commerce (where required by the destination customs authority)
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT MT103 bank credit advice or card-acquiring settlement report showing receipt of foreign-currency proceeds for the specific export consignment
  • Transport ancillary proof: container interchange receipt, gate pass, port warehouse entry, and the export packing list

As of the current Omani legislative cycle, no mandatory real-time tax authority clearance or digital stamping (Saudi-Fatoorah style) is in force. OTA has announced a phased national e-invoicing strategy; once activated, the platform will require API-based submission and digital stamping of tax invoices. At present, taxpayers must issue Arabic-language tax invoices (English optional alongside) containing the statutory data fields, transmit periodic VAT returns electronically through Taxoman only, and retain e-records for 10 years; QR-code and clearance-API requirements are not yet compulsory.

The OTA reviews the 90-day physical-export window, customs source match, and the 180-day payment-trace benchmark. Failure of the evidence chain reclassifies the sale as standard-rated, causing a 5% VAT assessment plus a late-payment penalty of 1% per 30 days (capped at 100%) and administrative fines of OMR 100-10,000. For deliberate false invoicing or forged export declarations, OTA imposes a tax-evasion penalty equal to the tax evaded (total exposure 200% including the VAT) and the case is referred for criminal prosecution with up to 3 years' imprisonment. The OTA audit look-back period is five years and ten years for fraud. A false Bayan also triggers Oman Customs Law seizure/personal penalties and, where funds are moved, Anti-Money-Laundering Law exposure. Note: Omani law contains no hard FX surrender or statutory repatriation deadline; the 180-day field is the OTA administrative benchmark for matching export proceeds to the VAT period; an open invoice beyond 180 days without documentation is treated as unsubstantiated zero-rating and clawed back.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions under the CBO regulatory framework. There are no advance exchange-control approvals for bona fide trade settlements, profit remittance, or capital repatriation by a foreign-owned Omani Single Person Company (SPC). Profit remittance restrictions are minimal: the bank requires audited financial statements confirming distributable profits and a valid board/shareholder resolution; Oman assesses no dividend withholding tax on outbound dividends, and no separate remittance tax exists. The only material hurdle is operational: transactions above OMR 10,000 must be reported to the CBO for statistical and AML/CFT purposes, and all payments must have a specific economic-purpose narrative.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose mandatory surrender of export proceeds. There is no statutory conversion timeline and no minimum conversion ratio. Export receipts may be credited directly in the original foreign currency to the SPC's bank account, or converted to OMR at the prevailing bank rate. Upon inward credit of export proceeds, the bank must receive the underlying sales invoice, customs export certificate, and transport document; once these are verified, funds are freely available without any CBO restriction.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) - Directorate General for Banking Supervision and AML/CFT, administered jointly for foreign-exchange purposes with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion under the Omani Foreign Capital Investment Law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW to MEDIUM. Legal status: offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) cannot be used as the primary treasury settlement venue for an Oman-resident SPC. The CBO Payment Services Provider regime requires any entity carrying out money or payment services from, into, or within Oman to be licensed by the CBO. An unlicensed offshore EMI handling settlement for an Omani company exposes both parties to administrative penalties and can trigger AML/CFT red flags. Offshore EMIs may be used only as supplementary card-acquiring gateways for international B2B e-commerce collection, provided that the EMI settlement flows are automatically swept to a CBO-regulated tier-1 bank account on a daily basis, and that the EMI is connected through a CBO-licensed acquirer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires a SWIFT MT103 wire from the non-resident shareholder through a correspondent bank to the Omani SPC's capital escrow account at a tier-1 local bank. The Payment Narrative field must clearly state 'Foreign Direct Investment - Equity Injection into Omani SPC [OM_SPC]' and reference the underlying shareholder resolution. For amounts above OMR 10,000, the receiving bank will report the inward transfer to the CBO and may request the Commercial Registration certificate, the constitutional documents, and an explanation of the economic purpose. Where the entity has not yet been finally registered, funds are locked in an escrow account and released only upon submission of the final Commercial Registration, the shareholder's subscription letter, and an auditor's certificate confirming the share capital was received.Four compliant clearing rails are recommended for an MRO supplies e-commerce business: (i) for low-value catalogue purchases - international credit-card settlement through a CBO-licensed acquiring bank using an appropriate merchant category code (MCC 5199 or similar MRO hardware code); (ii) for direct commercial invoices - SWIFT MT103 payments with the invoice number, bill of lading or airway bill number, and Oman Customs Bayan reference in the remittance information; (iii) for large or high-credit-risk supplier orders - an Irrevocable Confirmed Commercial Letter of Credit issued through a tier-1 bank using SWIFT MT700, with transport, packing, and quality certificates attached to the documentary presentation; (iv) for recurring regional warehouse replenishment - ISO 20022 pain.001 payment files via SWIFT Corporate Access, accompanied by the system-generated inventory replenishment report. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% on adjusted taxable income exceeding OMR 100,000; 3% on the first OMR 100,000

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Oman does not impose withholding tax on dividend distributions made by a resident SPC/LLC to a non-resident shareholder; ordinary profit distributions are outside the domestic WHT framework.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return must be submitted within 4 calendar months following the end of the fiscal year. For a 31 December year-end, the deadline is 30 April of the following calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where related-party transactions in a tax year exceed OMR 75,000 in aggregate, or where a single related-party transaction exceeds OMR 30,000. Master File obligations arise for multinational groups whose consolidated group revenue is OMR 100,000,000 or more (the same threshold triggers Country-by-Country Reporting). Taxpayers below these thresholds must nevertheless maintain contemporaneous arm's-length evidence in the filing working papers.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not operate a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing regime. Instead, RD 41/2021 embeds a General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) permitting the SGT to disregard arrangements lacking genuine commercial purpose, applies an OECD-aligned Permanent Establishment test (place of effective management, authority to conclude contracts, permanent local premises), and requires the taxpayer to evidence substantive local board/management decision-making, staff, inventory, warehousing, invoicing, and distribution activity. A paper SPC conducting e-commerce with no local office, warehouse, or operational control is vulnerable to deemed-income assessments, denial of treaty benefits, and recast distributions to the foreign owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Evasion penalties under RD 41/2021: fine equal to 100% of the evaded tax plus an administrative fine of not less than OMR 5,000 and not more than OMR 50,000, monthly late-payment fines of 1% on the arrears, and exclusion from voluntary-disclosure settlement programs in aggravated cases; asset seizure and criminal prosecution follow.

Director personal liability: The single-owner director or managing officer who directed or authorized the evasion is jointly assessed for the company's tax debts, may be personally fined, and in aggravated cases faces imprisonment of up to 3 years. The SGT may issue a travel ban against the director until the outstanding liability is settled and may attach personal bank assets where the corporate form was used as a sham.
Shell Company / Sham E-Commerce Conduit

Statutory sanction: The SGT may disregard the corporate persona for tax purposes, reattribute the entire profit to the foreign shareholder, deny treaty and trade benefits, apply the full 15% rate without the 3% bracket where the entity is treated as transparent absent commercial substance, and impose false-declaration fines. Administrative fines of OMR 100 per violation and reassessment with monthly surcharges continue until compliance is evidenced.

Director personal liability: Limited liability is pierced under the Omani Commercial Companies Law (RD 18/2019) where the corporate form is abused; the director and the foreign shareholder become personally liable for the company's tax debts. Personal travel bans, director disqualification, and seizure of personal assets are enforceable remedies used by the SGT against non-compliant shell directors.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The SGT may make a primary transfer pricing adjustment using TNMM, CUP, or Profit Split and recompute taxable income; an understatement penalty of 25% to 100% of the additional tax applies where negligence or intentional arrangement is found, plus 1% monthly fines on the adjusted amount. Documentation failure itself carries administrative penalties and adverse inference shifting the burden of proof to the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: A director who signs a return knowing the transfer prices are non-compliant is personally exposed to recovery of the additional tax and fines; the SGT may freeze personal assets and impose a travel ban if the company cannot satisfy the adjusted liability. Criminal liability arises where the pricing structure is used to conceal fraudulent diversion of corporate assets.
AML & Sanctions Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Omani AML Law (RD 30/2016), fines of up to OMR 10,000 per violation, confiscation of the proceeds of crime, suspension or revocation of the e-commerce license, and freezing of corporate bank accounts by the regulatory authority; the financial institution may also terminate the merchant card-processing facility.

Director personal liability: Senior officers and the compliance-designated director face criminal prosecution, imprisonment of up to 7 years for laundering predicate proceeds, and personal monetary fines; the corporate veil is pierced where the SPC functioned as a conduit for illicit funds. Directors are also exposed to private civil actions for complicity and to Cross-border travel restrictions under AML enforcement measures.
