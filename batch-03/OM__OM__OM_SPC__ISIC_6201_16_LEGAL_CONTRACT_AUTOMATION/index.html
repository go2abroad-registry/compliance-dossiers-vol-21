Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) — الهيئة العامة للضرائب, successor to the Secretariat General of Taxation under the Ministry of Finance; operates the national Emtax electronic portal (https://taxoman.gov.om). The OTA administers the Corporate Income Tax (CIT) under Royal Decree 28/2009 as amended, the Value Added Tax (VAT) under Royal Decree 28/2019 and Executive Regulations issued by Ministerial Decision No. 169/2020, and enforces the GCC Unified VAT Agreement ratified by Royal Decree 121/2016. There are no sub-national tax administrations or municipal business taxes applicable to the LegalTech SaaS SPC.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the Single Person Company (SPC) with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) under Royal Decree 18/2019 (Commercial Companies Law), obtain Commercial Registration (CR), operated by an Omani-registered office; foreign non-resident ownership up to 100% is permitted. 2) Register the SPC on the Emtax portal immediately upon CR issuance to obtain the CIT Tax Card / Income Tax Identification Number; a foreign-owned SPC does not obtain a separate PE rate but is taxed as a resident juridical entity. 3) Complete VAT registration on the same Emtax platform to obtain the VAT Registration Number; a resident taxable person registering mandatory when the value of annual taxable supplies, including zero-rated exports, exceeds OMR 500,000 (≈ USD 1,298,000); voluntary registration is available above OMR 38,000 (≈ USD 98,600); any non-resident seller of digital/electronic services to unregistered Omani consumers is obliged to register without a threshold before the first supply. 4) Submit notarized and legalized constitutional documents, foreign parent company incorporation certificate, CR extract, office lease and bank statement for the OTA Know-Your-Customer review; the entire CIT/VAT registration cycle is ordinarily completed within 2 to 3 weeks through the Emtax digital workflow. 5) File PT-return notifications in the first accounting period and withhold taxes (10% standard withholding on qualifying service payments to foreign related counterparts) as applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Omani SPC is a juridically separate, limited-liability taxable person; no fiscal transparency or pass-through status is available. Applicable CIT headline treatment: 15% tax on taxable income up to OMR 100,000 and 25% on the excess; the preferential 7% rate applies only to qualifying manufacturing, basic activities, or small-enterprise taxpayers and is not available to ISIC 6201 LegalTech SaaS operations. Foreign sole ownership does not alter entity-level taxation, and cross-border software/digital exports are assessed through the SPC as an Omani resident company. Dividends repatriated to a non-resident shareholder are not subject to additional Oman income tax withholding unless arising from a foreign-sourced income structure, but payments of royalties, software license fees, and technical service remuneration to non-resident counterparties may attract withholding tax at 10%.

Available election forms

Oman does not provide a US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election. Available statutory elections under Omani tax law include: (i) CIT tax group election — a resident parent holding at least 95% of one or more resident subsidiaries may apply to the OTA to be treated as a single tax group for CIT assessment; (ii) VAT voluntary registration election where annual taxable supplies exceed OMR 38,000 but remain below OMR 500,000; (iii) election of fiscal year (calendar year or a defined 12-month accounting year) for CIT return purposes with OTA acceptance; and (iv) streamlined transfer-pricing contemporaneous documentation election for small/medium enterprises. No pass-through partnership, transparent trust, or disregarded entity election exists for an SPC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 500,000 (≈ USD 1,298,000) annual taxable supplies, including zero-rated export supplies but excluding exempt supplies; Voluntary registration: OMR 38,000 (≈ USD 98,600) annual taxable supplies; Non-resident supplier special rule: a non-resident selling electronic, digital, or telecommunications services to a non-registered consumer in Oman must register for Omani VAT irrespective of the threshold before the first supply, while B2B digital service supplies to an Omani registered business are subject to reverse charge in the hands of the Omani recipient.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available to the Omani SPC where the statutory export conditions are strictly evidenced: (a) goods physically exported outside Oman within 90 days of supply evidenced by a valid customs export declaration; or (b) services supplied to a non-resident person with no Omani establishment and wholly used/consumed outside Oman, provided the stated use-and-enjoyment condition is outside the Sultanate; this covers cross-border SaaS subscriptions, document automation, and e-signature authentication fees where the customer resides abroad, the service consumption is demonstrably offshore, and the recipient is not registered for Omani VAT. For B2B supply to a registered Omani subscriber, the SPC must not charge VAT (reverse charge applies at 5% to the Omani customer); for B2C supply to an unregistered Omani consumer, the SPC must charge 5% Omani VAT. Services directly related to exported goods or international transport also qualify for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (single window / Bayan electronic customs clearance evidence)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or equivalent internationally recognized transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation evidencing receipt of export consideration in the SPC's Omani bank account
  • Executed service contract, order log, and technology-based usage evidence (IP geolocation records, server access logs, timestamped e-signature audit certificates) substantiating offshore consumption for SaaS/e-signature export claims

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based clearance engine, or government digital stamping is operative in Oman as of 2025. The OTA requires every registered supplier to issue a valid VAT tax invoice, in paper or electronic form, in good order and within the statutory invoice issuance window under the Executive Regulations; records, returns, and supporting documents are filed and retained through the Emtax portal. Large taxpayers may transmit bulk return data in structured format, and the OTA is progressively developing an e-invoicing clearance framework, but no mandatory clearance date has been published.

The OTA may reassess and claw back input VAT recoveries or zero-rated treatment for up to five years (unlimited in cases of fraud or deliberate evasion). Where zero-rating documentation is absent or false, the full supply is assessed to 5% VAT plus additional tax, administrative fines ranging from OMR 50 to OMR 10,000 per violation, and an evasion penalty generally calculated at 100% to 200% of the tax evaded; criminal referral may result in imprisonment of up to three years and a fine of up to OMR 10,000. Refund claims found false are rejected or withdrawn with interest-style additions, and future refunds are automatically set off against the assessment until the clawback is settled.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; the Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current-account transactions and, in practice, for capital-account transactions, with no licensing of individual current transfers. The currency is pegged to the US dollar (CBO published mid-rate: USD 1 = OMR 0.3845; OMR 1 = USD 2.6008), eliminating USD/OMR volatility but leaving EUR, GBP, AED, and INR cross-currency exposure for SaaS receipts. All resident FX transactions must be executed through CBO-licensed authorized dealers (commercial banks); OMR is not deliverable offshore, therefore inbound foreign currency must be converted onshore. A 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company may freely receive share capital and remit after-tax profits and dividends, subject to bank verification, anti-money-laundering documentation, the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2019), and OTA tax clearance where applicable. The principal administrative friction is documentary (source-of-funds, invoice, and purpose codes), not exchange-control approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not require mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds, and permits exporters to retain foreign currency in Foreign Currency Accounts (FCAs) at licensed banks, with no forced ratio. However, under the CBO Exchange Control Notification (No. 1/2000, as amended), the full value of exported goods or services must be repatriated to the exporter's account through an authorized bank within the contractual settlement period (generally up to 180 days), supported by the export declaration (for goods) or service contract/invoice (for SaaS and e-signature services), and by the applicable BPM6 purpose code. For cloud and legal-tech services, the bank will record the inflow as 'Export of Services' (BPM6 service code 5.4/5.6) and report to the CBO for balance-of-payments statistics; the SPC may then retain, swap, or convert funds at its discretion.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), established under the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000, as amended by Royal Decree 39/2015); CBO is the sole monetary, currency-issuance, exchange-control, and bank-supervisory authority. The Oman Tax Authority (OTA) co-administers remittance clearance for profit/dividend outflows under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 28/2009 as amended). The Capital Market Authority (CMA) regulates any securities-linked derivative activity by licensed banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. The CBO does not recognize EU/UK/Monetary-Authority EMI licenses as passportable into Oman; any payment institution servicing Omani residents must itself hold a CBO license under the AML/CFT Law (Royal Decree 30/2016) and the CBO e-payment/fintech licensing framework. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) cannot lawfully open OMR settlement accounts for the SPC, act as its principal disbursement bank, or be treated as an authorized dealer for CBO purposes. Permissible workaround: use a regulated overseas acquirer (Stripe's banking partners) purely as a card-collection node in USD/EUR, hold the settlement float temporarily in a licensed foreign bank account, and repatriate the balance via SWIFT MT103 to the SPC's authorized Omani bank within the bank's usual reporting cycle (typically monthly). The SPC must retain a full audit trail of acquirer statements, invoices, and BPM6 purpose codes, and must not route OMR-denominated vendor payments through the EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Since OMR is not deliverable offshore, the foreign shareholder must wire USD or EUR to the SPC's Capital Escrow (Capital Deposit) Account at the chosen Omani bank using the bank's overseas correspondent (e.g., Bank Muscat's USD Nostro at JPMorgan Chase New York, or HSBC Bank Oman's USD Nostro at HSBC New York), rather than sending OMR directly. The SWIFT MT103 must include the remittance information 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION – SPC [name] – Commercial Registration application reference – MoCIIP/FDI purpose code 3.1.1 (Equity Capital – Direct Investment) – board resolution [date]'; the bank credits the capital escrow and issues the Capital Deposit Certificate required by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) for issuance of the Commercial Registration. Upon CR issuance, the escrow account converts into the SPC's operational current account (OMR and USD sub-accounts), and the bank records the inward direct investment with the CBO for balance-of-payments statistics. Conversion to OMR occurs onshore at the pegged market rate plus the bank's FX spread; the shareholder may also elect to maintain a USD sub-account for UCITS and hedging purposes.The standard clearing rail for trade settlements is SWIFT MT103 (with MT202 for cover) for SaaS licensing fees, e-signature certification royalties, cloud-hosting fees, legal/professional services, and vendor payments, using BPM6 service purpose codes (IT services 5.4; professional/legal 5.6; royalties/licensing 6.7) and referencing the underlying invoice and contract in field 70. For physical procurement (e.g., trusted-certificate hardware, servers, peripherals), the SPC may use a commercial letter of credit (sight or usance, governed by UCP 600) or documentary collection where counterparties are low-risk. Profit and dividend remittances to the 100% foreign parent require presentation to the bank of the OTA tax clearance certificate, audited financial statements where applicable, a board resolution declaring distribution, and evidence that the statutory reserve (10% of annual profits until reaching one-third of share capital under the Oman Commercial Companies Law) has been allocated; withholding taxes, if any, must be settled under the Income Tax Law. All outbound transfers are subject to CBO wire-transfer data rules (full originator and beneficiary information for transactions above the CBO/NFTC reporting thresholds) and to SWIFT gpi tracking for auditability.Onshore deliverable outright FX forwards (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/AED, and USD/OMR) with CBO-licensed banks, typically up to 12 months tenor, rollable, and requiring an underlying commercial exposure such as contracted SaaS receivables or vendor payables.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) converting USD/EUR settlement receipts into OMR or servicing foreign-currency term borrowing, executed under ISDA 2002 Master Agreement plus Credit Support Annex (CSA) with the Omani bank.FX swaps and tom-next swaps for short-term liquidity alignment between incoming Stripe settlement float and monthly OMR payroll, VAT, and vendor payment obligations.Plain-vanilla FX options (European call/put) permitted where the premium is economically linked to identified invoice collections and the bank holds a CBO derivative authorization; embedded option termination provisions are required.Interest-rate and currency basis swaps added if the SPC raises USD term financing from an international lender, ensuring parity of interest and principal flows in USD/OMR; all transactions must be reported to the CBO for derivatives statistics and are subject to the bank's suitability and know-your-client policies.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. The CBO does not recognize EU/UK/Monetary-Authority EMI licenses as passportable into Oman; any payment institution servicing Omani residents must itself hold a CBO license under the AML/CFT Law (Royal Decree 30/2016) and the CBO e-payment/fintech licensing framework. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) cannot lawfully open OMR settlement accounts for the SPC, act as its principal disbursement bank, or be treated as an authorized dealer for CBO purposes. Permissible workaround: use a regulated overseas acquirer (Stripe's banking partners) purely as a card-collection node in USD/EUR, hold the settlement float temporarily in a licensed foreign bank account, and repatriate the balance via SWIFT MT103 to the SPC's authorized Omani bank within the bank's usual reporting cycle (typically monthly). The SPC must retain a full audit trail of acquirer statements, invoices, and BPM6 purpose codes, and must not route OMR-denominated vendor payments through the EMI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File (and supporting Master File elements) is required when aggregate related-party transactions exceed OMR 1,000,000 in a tax year; multinational group consolidated turnover above the prescribed OMR 160,000,000 threshold triggers more extensive Master File and Country-by-Country reporting obligations.

Economic substance scope: Oman's tax framework applies substance-over-form, beneficial ownership, and anti-avoidance principles under the Income Tax Law. A legal-contract-automation SPC is not subject to a sector-specific ESR filing regime, but it must maintain actual premises, employees, board decisions, and commercial contracts in Oman. Artificial shell structures, nominee directors without authority, or circular licensing arrangements may be recharacterized, denied treaty benefits, and subjected to severe penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Recovery of underpaid tax, administrative penalties up to the amount of the evaded tax, daily late-payment surcharges capped at 25%, and criminal prosecution punishable by imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Omani courts may pierce the corporate veil where directors signed false returns or orchestrated sham transactions; such directors face joint restitution, asset freezing, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the entity for tax purposes, denial of treaty benefits, reassessment of Oman-source profits at 15%, penalties for incomplete documentation, and administrative strike-off or Commercial Registration cancellation.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and beneficial owners may be jointly assessed for unpaid tax and face market exclusion and travel bans if they knowingly participated in a sham corporate arrangement.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment, secondary adjustments or deemed dividend treatment, late-payment charges, and penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's length structures may be personally liable for the resulting tax deficit; failure to prepare and retain Local File and Master File shifts the burden of proof and triggers penalty exposure.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: AML Law penalties, asset forfeiture, bank account closure, revocation of commercial licence, and criminal prosecution by the Public Prosecution in coordination with the National Centre for Financial Information.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face direct criminal liability for assisting laundering, failing to maintain beneficial ownership records, or knowingly concealing the controller of the SPC; travel bans and imprisonment may be imposed.
