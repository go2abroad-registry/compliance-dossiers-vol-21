Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is mandated under CCL 2019 for a Single Person Company. The shareholder determines the share capital in the Memorandum of Association. Any declared capital must be deposited in an escrowed bank account with an Oman-licensed bank and evidenced by a bank deposit certificate; the funds must remain restricted until the Commercial Registration Certificate is issued and the capital is released for lawful business purposes.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Oman Civil Defence and Ambulance Authority (CDAA); structural load inspection certificate from the Muscat Municipality Engineering Department; occupational health clearance and workplace registration with the Ministry of Labour; approved fire evacuation plan, emergency lighting, and temperature alarm/backup power systems.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Secretariat General for Taxation (SGT), Sultanate of Oman; e-services portal: taxation.gov.om

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned entities (including an SPC/LLC) must register with the Secretariat General for Taxation (SGT) within 30 days of commercial registration (Commercial Register, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion). The application for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) is submitted via the SGT e-portal (taxation.gov.om) or through a licensed tax agent, along with the Commercial Registration certificate, Articles of Association (certified Arabic translation), board resolution appointing the resident manager, and power-of-attorney for the tax agent. All documents must be authenticated by the Royal Oman Police (ROP) and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs where required. For SPC structures, no minimum capital requirement applies and 100% foreign ownership is permitted; the entity is deemed a juridical person taxable on its worldwide income unless the scope is confined to Omani-source income. Upon completion, SGT issues a TIN and VAT ID as applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Oman's tax code does not provide a check-the-box election comparable to the U.S. regime. Tax grouping is available for wholly-owned resident companies under certain conditions (single economic unit), enabling consolidated filing for income tax. Hybrid mismatch rules are incorporated under the Unified Corporate Tax Law and are reflected in anti-abuse provisions (e.g., PAYAS/withholding alignment). No separate pass-through election is available for an SPC; it is always taxed at entity level under the Omani Corporate Income Tax Law (promulgated by Royal Decree 116/2024, effective for tax periods beginning on or after 1 January 2025, replacing the previous SPT law; until such new law's effective date, the existing Law on Income Tax (Royal Decree 28/2009) applies).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration threshold: OMR 500,000 (annual tax-sensitive supplies). Voluntary registration threshold: OMR 50,000 (annual tax-sensitive supplies). A foreign entity making no taxable supplies in Oman but requiring a VAT refund may register as a 'non-resident taxpayer' under specific refund conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export of goods requires (i) the goods are physically exported from Oman within 90 days of the sale invoice (or within an extended period allowed by SGT), (ii) the recipient is a non-resident of Oman, (iii) the exporter retains proof of export and payment, and (iv) the supply is made under a valid VAT invoice showing 0% rate. For refrigerated warehousing services (cold storage), zero-rating may apply only where the services are directly linked to the export of goods (e.g., handling and storage in confidence of export), provided the service is supplied to a non-resident and is directly attributable to the exported goods; pure domestic cold-storage services are standard-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayaan / Single Window export declaration conforming to GCC requirements)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill for air freight) showing Oman as port of loading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank proof of foreign currency receipt from the same non-resident buyer)
  • Original commercial invoice and VAT invoice (0%)
  • Packing list and certificate of origin

Oman has adopted a phased mandatory e-invoicing framework (Law on Electronic Transactions, Royal Decree 69/2008; VAT Executive Regulations). As of 2025, the SGT is progressively mandating real-time fiscal data clearance for selected taxpayer segments; APIs and digital stamping (MofTab model) are mandatory for registered businesses in those segments. For the remaining taxpayers, electronic invoicing (XML/JSON format) is required for B2B transactions, with a continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoice clearance obligation being expanded sector-by-sector. Cold-storage operators (ISIC 5210) are considered standard-rate taxpayers; they must issue compliant e-invoices through SGT-approved fiscal devices or a certified API connection, and must store/transmit VAT records in real time once the CTC mandate is rolled out to their segment.

False/fraudulent zero-rating attracts retrospective VAT assessment at 5% on the supply value, an increased tax liability up to 100% of the tax differential, a fixed penalty ranging from OMR 5,000 to OMR 50,000 per incidence under the VAT Law, and in serious cases criminal prosecution and/or customs penalties. Under the SGT audit framework, the six-year record retention period applies after the end of the tax year; failure to substantiate the export within a prescribed deadline results in VAT, interest (at the prevailing SGT rate), and penalties. Furthermore, the supply of 'offshore' services to a non-resident requires documentary proof that the service was genuinely performed outside Oman (contract, bank statements, and operational records) to avoid recharacterisation as a taxable local supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions, with no mandatory approval for profit remittance by a 100% foreign-owned SPC. Profit repatriation is subject to documentary compliance: audited financial statements, tax clearance from the Omani Tax Authority, and the annual financial reporting requirements of the Commercial Companies Law. The CBO applies a unified BPM6-compliant reporting framework, and transfers exceeding certain reporting thresholds trigger enhanced review solely for AML/CFT verification, not convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose a statutory export proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in OMR or any freely convertible foreign currency in a licensed Omani bank account. The CBO requires exporters to route proceeds through an Omani licensed bank, and banks apply a documentary reconciliation process matching the shipping records to the inward remittance, but there is no compulsory conversion or central bank-mediated surrender.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) is the sovereign monetary and foreign exchange regulator, supervising all licensed banks, money changers and clearing systems under the Financial Institutions Law and the Foreign Exchange Regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs or cross-border fintech platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Currencycloud) cannot act as the primary transactional bank for an Omani SPC under CBO rules. All resident corporate cash management and cross-border payments must settle through a fully licensed bank in Oman. Offshore EMIs retain limited viability as supplementary collection channels for low-value receivables, but the governing legal constraint is CBO's requirement that the SPC maintain at least one clearing account with a domestically licensed bank. Overall feasibility rating: Low to Medium, acceptable only as a secondary ancillary collection rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI share capital from the foreign parent should be routed via SWIFT MT103 with a BPM6-compliant purpose category reflecting 'Foreign Direct Investment – Equity Capital' (the CBO's unified ITRS designation). The receiving Omani bank will require the Commercial Registration certificate, the SPC constitution, a board resolution authorizing the capital increase or initial subscription, and the parent bank's remittance reference. For any credit above the CBO reporting threshold, the receiving bank completes a balance-of-payments declaration and the SPC obtains a dated credit advice confirming that the funds are recorded as share capital; that advice is subsequently filed with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion and the Omani Tax Authority for capital registry and tax-capital matching purposes.Trade settlements for imported refrigeration and pharmaceutical logistics equipment are executed through the licensed Omani bank's treasury desk using SWIFT MT103 for standard open-account payments or commercial letters of credit (LC) for high-value and deferred-delivery contracts. The SPC must provide a signed commercial contract, pro-forma or final invoice, and where applicable a bill of lading or airway bill. Domestic disbursements use the Oman Automated Clearing House (ACH) / Direct Debit system; cross-border payments require sanctions screening and CBO-consistent purpose-code declaration. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 500,000 aggregate annual related-party controlled transactions (or OMR 300,000 for any single controlled transaction) triggering mandatory Local File preparation; Master File obligation applies where the consolidated group turnover exceeds OMR 10 million; Country-by-Country reporting thresholds apply separately for groups above OMR 50 million consolidated turnover.

Economic substance scope: Oman's Economic Substance Regulations (RD 25/2020) require resident SPCs engaged in 'relevant activities' to demonstrate sufficient economic presence in Oman. Refrigerated cold storage warehouse operations likely fall within 'distribution and service centre' activity. The SPC must maintain a physical cold-storage facility, employ a sufficient number of qualified full-time refrigeration/warehouse staff, incur adequate local management expenditure, and perform core income-generating activities within Oman. Failure to comply exposes the entity to a penalty of up to OMR 300,000 and potential freezing of the tax residency certificate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Underreporting of Cold-Chain Revenue

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 200% of the evaded tax, additional late-payment surcharge of up to 25%, publication of taxpayer name, and criminal prosecution with imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false returns are jointly, severally, and personally liable for the unpaid tax plus penalties; the OTA can seek civil restoration, apply travel bans, attach personal assets, and refer the matter for criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Artificial Avoidance of Economic Substance and PE

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation of all profit allocations to the offshore principal, denial of treaty benefits, penalty of 50% of the underpaid tax for a serious transfer pricing breach, and removal from the Omani Commercial Register.

Director personal liability: The shareholder/manager is jointly liable for the re-allocated tax under tax avoidance and piercing-the-veil principles; an asset freeze, exit ban, and disqualification from serving as an Oman-registered director may be imposed.
AML and Sanctions Breach in Cross-Border Settlement

Statutory sanction: Fine up to OMR 200,000 under Oman's AML Law, confiscation of the underlying proceeds, revocation of the refrigerated storage licence, and cancellation of the tax clearance certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face criminal liability of up to seven years' imprisonment, personal confiscation orders, and a ten-year prohibition from operating any regulated business in Oman.
