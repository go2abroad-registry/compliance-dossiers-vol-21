Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available in Oman's domestic tax law. Income tax law applies at the entity level on Omani-source income. No hybrid mismatch election is available for indirect tax purposes; transfer pricing and general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) apply without elective regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 38,500 (annual taxable supplies); all non-resident persons must register for VAT if they make taxable supplies in Oman without a fixed place of establishment, regardless of the threshold (Article 7 of VAT Law).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated when the goods are exported from Oman to a place outside Oman within 90 days of the date of supply and the export is evidenced by approved customs documentation. Related transport, handling, and freight services directly linked to goods exported are also zero-rated. Dental clear aligner sheets exported to foreign orthodontic labs qualify, provided the title of goods passes to the buyer abroad and departure from Oman is proven.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayān / Single Administrative Document equivalent approved by Royal Oman Police Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (outward shipment)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FX repatriation evidence showing payment for the exported goods)
  • Sales Invoice / Commercial Invoice
  • Certificate of Origin (if required by the importing country)

Oman has not yet implemented a longitudinal near-real-time e-invoicing clearance mandate as of 2025. General VAT invoice requirements are mandatory: sequential numbering, date, supplier and recipient VAT IDs, description, quantity, tax-exclusive amount, tax rate and amount. Non-resident suppliers must issue invoices in accordance with the VAT Law; there is no API-based clearance or digital stamping requirement. However, taxpayers must retain electronic invoices for tax audit purposes and the OTA may require bulk e-invoice supply via agreed data exchange.

Unsupported or fictitious export zero-rating triggers VAT claw-back at 5% on the supply, plus additional tax of 15% of the tax due (minimum OMR 500) for false documentation; recovery of input VAT incorrectly claimed; administrative fines up to OMR 10,000 per violation under the VAT Law; and criminal liability under the Tax Law and Customs Law for forged export documentation, including imprisonment and confiscation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible under CBO exchange-control practice and is pegged to the US dollar at a fixed official rate of 1 OMR = 2.6004 USD, with standard buying and selling margins applied by licensed banks. The State of Oman does not maintain separate capital-account and current-account convertibility regimes; both direct investment flows from 100% foreign-owned entities and trade-related payments are generally permitted without exchange-control approval. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation for an OM_SPC are lawful provided that the remittance is supported by an audited or certified financial statement, evidence of withholding-tax settlement or clearance with the Oman Tax Authority, and normal bank-level source-of-wealth documentation. The principal practical hurdles are documentary, not regulatory: banks must verify the beneficial ownership of the foreign shareholder, confirm that the underlying export and manufacturing activity is genuine, and comply with CBO's AML sanctions screening, economic-sanctions screening, and correspondent-banking counterparty policies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose a statutory export-proceeds surrender ratio or mandatory conversion of export receipts into OMR. All export proceeds must, however, be repatriated to an account held with a CBO-licensed bank in Oman rather than being retained indefinitely at an offshore financial institution. As a standard banking and trade-finance benchmark, export proceeds are expected to be collected and credited within six months from the date of shipment unless a longer contractual payment term is justified by the underlying commercial agreement and accepted by the bank. There is no requirement to convert those proceeds into OMR, and the OM_SPC may maintain a USD-denominated operational account for trade settlements. The company should nevertheless declare each export sale in its audited financial statements and in the annual economic-subsidiary returns to the Oman Tax Authority, with supporting customs declarations and commercial invoices retained for fiscal verification.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), acting as the Sultanate of Oman's sole monetary, banking, and foreign-exchange regulatory authority under the Banking Law and the Exchange Control Rules; commercial company registration and foreign-investment approvals are administered by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP), while anti-money laundering supervision is coordinated by the National Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Financing of Terrorism Committee (NAMLCFTC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a sole or primary treasury account, Medium feasibility as an ancillary payment-acceptance rail. The CBO does not issue a stand-alone electronic-money or EMI licence that would allow an offshore digital EMI to act as the principal depository for the OM_SPC's OMR or USD trade flows. An offshore EMI can only be used, if at all, for card acceptance or low-value collection through licensed payment providers approved under Oman's National Payment Systems Framework. All cross-border settlement, export-proceeds repatriation, and trade disbursements should be routed through an account at a CBO-licensed commercial bank. Legal constraints include CBO know-your-customer rules requiring account title consistency with the Commercial Register of Oman, sanctions screening against CBO/GCC/OFAC/UN lists, and prohibitions on using unlicensed payment intermediaries for clearing of export proceeds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The sole shareholder's initial equity contribution to the OM_SPC should be made by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 via a correspondent bank in the United States or a major GCC clearing centre, remitted to the SPC's capital escrow account at the onboarding Omani bank. The remitter must state the purpose code applicable to foreign-direct-equity investment, such as 'C' for capital contribution or the bank's designated FDI/disinvestment code, and must ensure the beneficiary account name is exactly the name shown on the MoCIIP Commercial Register. The bank will treat the funds as fully cleared only after sanctions screening, UBO verification, and a completion of the source-of-funds questionnaire. If the injection is intended to be used immediately as paid-up capital, the SPC should sign a corporate resolution stating that the amount is equity, not a loan, to avoid accidental classification as shareholder debt for tax and thin-capitalisation purposes. After the initial high-touch capital flow, normal inbound working-capital and intercompany loan ingresses may be settled by MT103 to the operating account, subject to annual approval in the corporate tax and transfer-pricing file. Inbound card-settlement flows from Stripe, where used for direct-to-consumer distribution, are aggregated by the payment provider and settled via SWIFT to the Omani account with merchant-of-record and payout reporting concise enough to satisfy CBO AML requirements. The schema also permits the use of the SPC's Stripe-connected account solely as an ancillary payment-collection conduit; all proceeds must be swept to the CBO-licensed bank account, never held offshore indefinitely.Trade settlements for raw thermopolymer sheets, automated trimming machinery, spare parts, packaging, and freight should be executed using SWIFT MT103 for normal supplier invoices and MT202 COV when a correspondent or second-line bank is used to cover the beneficiary's nostro account. For larger or contractually required transactions, the OM_SPC should use an irrevocable documentary credit issued subject to UCP600, payable at sight or by deferred payment, with standard documents being commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or air waybill, certificate of origin, insurance certificate, and any required inspection certificate. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed no later than four months after the end of the Omani tax period, unless a written extension is granted by OTA. A calendar-year taxpayer is therefore required to file by 30 April. The tax must be paid by the filing deadline; late payment accrues interest.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 1,000,000 in aggregate related-party transactions per tax year triggers the expectation of contemporaneous Local File documentation for the Omani SPC. Master File documentation is expected where the foreign parent group exceeds the OECD BEPS Action 13 reporting threshold (consolidated group revenue above EUR 750 million) or where OTA specifically requests group-level information.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute equivalent to certain other GCC states. However, the Omani Income Tax Law, permanent establishment concepts, general anti-avoidance principles, the Commercial Companies Law, and the beneficial ownership compliance framework require the SPC to have genuine substance in Oman: a physical registered office, active manufacturing facilities, employees, board and management decisions taken in Oman, independent bank accounts, and contracts executed and administered locally. An SPC operating as a paper entity or bare nominee of the foreign parent will be vulnerable to income reattribution, treaty denial, TP adjustment, and regulatory sanction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: OTA will assess the additional tax, impose the statutory late-payment interest and a penalty calculated on the evaded tax, and may enforce collection by attachment of assets. Criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment and/or a substantial financial fine in addition to the recovery of all unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: A director or sole shareholder who signs a false return, directs accounting fraud, or knowingly conceals revenue can be personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax through piercing of the SPC corporate veil. Courts may order civil restitution, asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment in cases of intentional tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: OTA may upwardly adjust the Omani SPC's taxable income to the arm's length level, assess CIT at 15% on the adjustment, impose interest and fixed monetary penalties for failure to disclose related-party transactions, and disallow deductions that lack documentary proof.

Director personal liability: If the controlling shareholder extracts the SPC's profits through inflated outbound payments and leaves the entity unable to pay its Omani tax liabilities, the director or owner may be held personally liable for the resulting tax debt. Personal asset confiscation and travel restrictions may follow where the arrangement is found to be an abuse of the corporate form.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: OTA may disregard the legal existence of the SPC, allocate the income to the foreign principal, deny treaty benefits, assess tax and penalties on the principal, and refer the matter to the Commercial Registry for striking off or sanctions under the Commercial Companies Law.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate form is used as a facade, courts may lift the corporate veil and impose civil liability on the sole shareholder and directors. The individuals involved may face disqualification from serving as directors, travel bans, administrative fines, and in serious cases criminal liability under AML and company law.
AML, Sanctions, and Payment Rails Misuse

Statutory sanction: Oman's Anti-Money Laundering Law and the implementing AML/CFT decisions impose fines, confiscation of funds, suspension or revocation of the commercial license, and imprisonment for serious violations. The low-risk classification of the dental aligner activity does not exempt the SPC from customer due diligence, sanctions screening, and record-keeping.

Director personal liability: Directors and the compliance officer of the SPC can be personally fined and imprisoned for failure to report suspicious transactions, permitting the business to be used for laundering proceeds, or knowingly transacting with sanctioned persons. Directors may also face civil restitution and travel bans in connection with AML proceedings.
