Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on Single Person Companies under the 2019 Companies Law; the share capital must be fully subscribed, stated in the Articles of Association, and evidenced by a bank capital certificate. No escrow account is legally mandated for a non-public SPC; the sole shareholder must deposit the stated capital into the company account before or immediately upon registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (Public Authority for Taxation, Al-Hay'ah al-'Ammah li al-Dara'ib), operating under the Ministry of Finance; e-services and electronic filing portal: https://taxp.gov.om. Customs and excise administration is exercised through the Royal Oman Police Directorate General of Customs using the 'Bayan' single-window electronic customs system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain MOCIIP Commercial Registration (CR) for the foreign-owned SPC (100% foreign ownership permitted under the Foreign Capital Investment Law, RD 50/2019). Step 2: Apply for the corporate Tax Card/TIN on the Tax Authority portal within 60 days of CR/licence issuance (Income Tax Law, RD 28/2009), filing CR, MOA, passport copies, warehouse/customs licence and premises documents. Step 3: Register separately for VAT once taxable supplies and imports exceed OMR 38,500 over any 12-month period, or when 12-month turnover is expected to exceed the threshold within the next 30 days; voluntary VAT registration is available above OMR 19,250; imports are counted towards the threshold, making registration mandatory for a bonded wine importer. Step 4: Obtain the Excise Tax registration and the Royal Oman Police (ROP) bonded warehouse/ alcohol control licence through Bayan before handling duty-suspended wine and spirits.

Non-resident shareholders are not taxed on the SPC's trading profits and are taxed only on Oman-source income (dividends subject to WHT). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The foreign-owned Omani SPC is a resident juridical person taxable on worldwide income on a standalone basis; no pass-through or transparent treatment exists. Non-resident shareholders are not taxed on the SPC's trading profits and are taxed only on Oman-source income (dividends subject to WHT). The bonded warehouse does not confer a Tax_Exempt_Zone status; Customs duty suspension only postpones VAT/excise, it does not exempt income tax.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election regime is available in Oman. Limited statutory choices: (i) voluntary VAT registration below the mandatory threshold; (ii) application for a special input-VAT allocation/pro-rata method for mixed taxable/exempt supplies (VAT Executive Regulations); (iii) the bonded warehouse operator's election to operate all wine/spirit movements under a duty-suspension regime backed by a customs security bond; (iv) no group taxation or consolidated filing election is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration: OMR 38,500 of taxable supplies/receipts in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration: OMR 19,250. Imports of excisable products count toward the threshold, so a bonded wine/spirits importer must register from the first import. Excise Tax registration and bonded-warehouse permit are required from the first litre handled with no turnover threshold; release for local consumption triggers 100% excise plus 5% VAT. The ROP alcohol trade licence applies independently of tax thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Wine/spirits supplied from an Omani licensed bonded warehouse qualify for 0% VAT when the goods physically leave Oman (or are transferred to a registered taxable recipient in another VAT-implementing GCC state) within 90 days of supply, under customs and excise duty suspension, with the bonded warehouse operator acting as exporter of record. Conditions: ROP alcohol export permit secured; Bayan export declaration completed; goods never released into local free circulation; export invoice issued as zero-rated; consideration received in an Omani bank account (repatriation); and full documentary evidence retained. Domestic release of wine/spirits is always taxable at 5% VAT plus 100% excise.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan electronic declaration with customs release reference)
  • Bill of Lading (clean ocean B/L or air waybill, shipped on board)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of receipt of export proceeds in OMR or convertible foreign currency)
  • Commercial export invoice and packing list
  • Bonded warehouse release order and duty-suspension removal record
  • Royal Oman Police (Alcohol Control) export permit and excise movement endorsement

Oman has not yet implemented a real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API stamping or digital tax-stamp mandate for wine/spirits. VAT invoices may be issued electronically with the prescribed particulars and must be retained for 10 years. The operative digital fiscal clearance is the Bayan customs single window: every import, bonded transfer, inventory adjustment, excise release and export must be pre-cleared in Bayan, with bonded stock records reconciled electronically against the Tax Card and VAT file.

Claims are heavily document-driven. Failure to export within 90 days of supply converts the zero-rated supply into a taxable supply with 5% VAT due plus monthly late-payment increases; deliberate or careless misstatement triggers a clawback penalty of up to 200% of the VAT evaded, excise duty reinstatement at 100% on missing quantities, fines up to OMR 10,000 per record-keeping violation, suspension or cancellation of the bonded warehouse and ROP alcohol licences, and criminal liability including imprisonment up to three years for fraudulent export declarations. Bayan export records, remittance certificates and stock reconciliations must be retained for 10 years to withstand retrospective audit adjustment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible on the current account and pegged to the USD at a fixed official rate of 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD. Capital account transactions are generally unrestricted for foreign direct investment and profit remittances, but certain cross-border transfers, portfolio investments, and outward capital movements may require CBO approval or submission of supporting documentation under Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and sanctions screening. Profit remittance from a licensed SPC requires audited financial statements, tax clearance from the Oman Tax Authority, and a board resolution authorizing dividend distribution. No mandatory prior license is required for trade-related payments, provided the underlying transaction is commercially genuine.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose any statutory surrender requirement on export proceeds. The CBO does not mandate conversion of foreign currency receipts into OMR or require repatriation within a specified timeline. However, all export proceeds must be received through licensed banks in Oman, be traced to the underlying goods/services, and be supported by customs declarations, invoices, and bills of lading to satisfy AML and anti-money-laundering financing (AML/CFT) due diligence requirements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) acting as the monetary authority and regulator of banking, foreign exchange, and payment systems; exchange control powers derived from the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000) and CBO Circulars.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Foreign Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not recognized as licensed deposit-taking or payment institutions under Omani law. The CBO exclusively licenses banks, exchange houses, and licensed payment service providers. An SPC engaged in bonded trade must route cross-border payments and FX through a licensed commercial bank operating in Oman. Offshore EMIs may be used only as ancillary invoicing or aggregation tools provided funds are never held or settled without a licensed Omani bank intermediary, which is institutionally impractical and legally risky.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions must be wired via SWIFT MT103 or internationally accepted correspondent banking methods to the SPC's Omani bank operating account. The wire instruction should clearly state 'Equity Capital Contribution to [OM_SPC Legal Name]' with the SPC's Commercial Registration Number (CR) and CBO-approved business category. No separate CBO approval is required for an equity injection into a non-restricted trading company, but the remitting bank and the receiving Omani bank must satisfy all Know-Your-Transaction (KYT) and sanctions screening. The bank may require the underlying SPC registration, board/shareholder resolution, and a source-of-funds declaration for amounts exceeding AML threshold levels. For the bonded wine/spirits business, the receiving bank will verify the licences (MOCI commercial licence, municipal alcohol licence, customs bonded warehouse permission) and may condition the account activation on submission of these licences.Trade payments for wholesale purchase of aged Scotch and Bordeaux wines and for bonded warehouse settlements will be cleared primarily via SWIFT MT103, MT202 (for correspondent bank-to-bank transfers) and Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700/MT707) mechanisms. Domestic bonded warehouse transfers to and from Customs Bonded Zones are settled via local Hundi/Credit Transfer through the Omani Electronic Funds Transfer System (OCIFS, operated by CBO) or through bank-to-bank internal transfers. For cross-border wine/spirit purchases, the preferred route is an L/C at sight or a Collection with documents (DP/DA) to minimize trade-based money laundering concerns. Outbound payments must be matched against the import licence, customs entry numbers, pro-forma invoices, and L/C terms; the bank will also require tax invoices and a statement of purpose. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File is triggered when aggregate cross-border connected-party transactions exceed OMR 1,000,000 in a tax year; Master File is required where group consolidated turnover exceeds OMR 30,000,000; contemporaneous arm's length documentation is expected for all related-party dealings.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not currently apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations code similar to the UAE; however, Omani tax practice requires genuine local management, operational premises, payroll, and business activity. A bonded trading SPC without licensed warehouse capacity, staff, or local trading decisions can be treated as a shell, denied treaty access, and have income re-attributed to its foreign shareholder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 100%-300% of undercharged corporate tax, daily late-payment surcharges, criminal conviction sanctioning imprisonment up to 5 years, and fines up to OMR 10,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax; civil restitution, travel bans, exit visa blocks, and disqualification from managing licensed trade apply.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, re-characterization of the entity as a shell, add-back of management fees, and imposition of 15% income tax on deemed profits plus administrative penalties.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing may expose directors to reimbursement of customs/excise duties and Omani tax arrears; travel bans and restrictions on new commercial licenses are imposed until settlement.
AML / Unverified Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Penalties under Royal Decree 30/2016 (as amended): fines up to OMR 20,000 per breach, bank account freezing, confiscation of bonded inventory, and possible licence revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers face independent imprisonment of up to 7 years, personal restitution orders, and a registered travel ban against leaving Oman.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Upward taxable income adjustment, penalty of 10%-35% of the tax deficiency, and default interest at 1% per month on the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's length pricing are exposed to civil proceedings and may be personally responsible for any tax shortfall after corporate dissolution or liquidation.
