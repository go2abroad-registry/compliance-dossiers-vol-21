Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (Al-Hay'ah Al-Aamah lil-Dara'ib), official e-services portal: https://taxoman.gov.om

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Single Person Company must first obtain its Commercial Registration from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion, then register for corporate income tax through the Oman Tax Authority's e-services portal to receive the Oman Tax Identification Number. For VAT purposes, the entity must file a VAT registration application through the same portal upon exceeding or expecting the statutory registration threshold; the OTA issues a Tax Registration Number and VAT registration certificate. Non-resident entities with Omani-sourced taxable activity but no local established place of business may require limited registration or a tax representative as directed by the OTA.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Single Person Company must first obtain its Commercial Registration from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion, then register for corporate income tax through the Oman Tax Authority's e-services portal to receive the Oman Tax Identification Number. For VAT purposes, the entity must file a VAT registration application through the same portal upon exceeding or expecting the statutory registration threshold; the OTA issues a Tax Registration Number and VAT registration certificate. Non-resident entities with Omani-sourced taxable activity but no local established place of business may require limited registration or a tax representative as directed by the OTA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists under Omani tax law. An SPC is per se a taxable juridical entity, notwithstanding single-shareholder ownership. Oman does not provide hybrid mismatch elections; the SPC is taxed at entity level on its taxable income. Certain procedural elections are available with OTA approval, such as adoption of a non-calendar financial year and grouping/consolidation only where expressly permitted by the Income Tax Law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration applies when taxable supplies and imports exceed OMR 500,000 in the preceding 12 months or are expected to exceed OMR 500,000 within the next 30 days; voluntary registration is available from OMR 250,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for goods physically exported from Oman to a destination outside Oman where the export is supported by official customs and commercial evidence. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a recipient outside Oman and the place of supply is outside Oman; services directly related to exported goods or provided to non-resident recipients for use wholly outside Oman may also qualify. The taxpayer must satisfy the OTA that the recipient has no use or benefit of the service inside Oman and that consideration has actually been received from abroad.

  • Oman Customs Export Declaration (Bayān clearance record)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill demonstrating physical export
  • Signed commercial invoice and underlying M&A advisory agreement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing foreign currency receipt in Oman

Oman's VAT regime mandates tax invoices in a prescribed electronic format for VAT-registered persons. The Oman Tax Authority has introduced a phased national e-invoicing and fiscal clearance framework: in-scope taxpayers must transmit invoice data through OTA-approved Application Programming Interfaces before issue, obtain electronic clearance, and embed a digital stamp or QR code on the final invoice. Taxpayers must retain structured invoice records in the OTA portal and comply with published API, encryption, and digital signature specifications. Real-time clearance is mandatory for the covered taxpayer segments once their phased compliance date has taken effect.

The Oman Tax Authority may audit export zero-rating claims retrospectively. Failure to produce valid export documentation within the statutory period results in denial of the zero rating, reassessment of output VAT at 5%, recovery of associated input VAT credit, administrative penalties, and, in fraudulent cases, criminal prosecution and imprisonment. Late-payment fines and interest apply from the original supply date, and deliberate misstatements may attract enhanced penalties, including up to triple the tax amount in serious evasion cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman is an International Monetary Fund Article VIII member and maintains a fixed exchange-rate regime: 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD (1 USD = 0.3845 OMR). Current-account transactions (cross-border M&A success fees, service fees, royalties, dividends and profit remittances) are freely convertible and remittable without CBO exchange-control approval, subject only to bank-level due diligence and tax compliance documentation. Capital-account transactions, including the foreign shareholder's equity capital subscription into an Omani Single Person Company (SPC), are also permitted without a general CBO licence; however, the bank must classify the inbound remittance under BPM6 direct-investment equity codes for its BOP return and must verify source-of-funds. Profit remittance hurdles are practical rather than regulatory: the company must file its corporate income tax return (Income Tax Law, Royal Decree 28/2009), the shareholder must pass the bank's enhanced due diligence, and a formal tax clearance certificate is legally required at liquidation or cessation, while banks commonly require proof of filing before releasing dividends. Thin-capitalisation and source-of-wealth documentation may be requested for large capital injections.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory mandatory surrender, no minimum conversion ratio and no fixed repatriation window for export-of-goods or export-of-services proceeds. A cross-border M&A advisory boutique earning success fees in USD or EUR may lawfully retain those receipts in foreign-currency accounts at a licensed Omani bank, convert them at the commercial interbank rate, or keep them offshore, provided the revenue is disclosed. The operative obligations are: (i) all service-export receipts must be captured in the company's books and tax return at the CBO mid-point rate on the revenue-recognition date; (ii) banks must record the transaction in their CBO BOP reporting system under the services category; and (iii) a complete customer-due-diligence and contract trail (engagement letter, invoice, closing evidence) must be retained for AML audit. There is no 30-day, 60-day or 100% surrender rule analogous to jurisdictions with centralised export-proceeds regimes.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO, البنك المركزي العُماني), established under the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000 as amended), is the sole monetary, banking and foreign-exchange supervisory authority in Oman. The CBO administers currency issuance, the Omani Rial (OMR) peg, balance-of-payments statistics, licensed commercial banks, exchange houses and the AML/CFT supervisory framework (Royal Decree 30/2016). The Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) regulates foreign investment and company registration, but all cross-border payment approval, FX intermediation, correspondent banking and sanctions compliance rest with the CBO and the licensed banks it supervises.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability for offshore digital E-Money Institutions (EMIs). The CBO does not recognise unlicensed foreign EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Airwallex or similar) as lawful deposit-taking or payment-settlement institutions in Oman. An offshore EMI cannot maintain an OMR account, access the CBO interbank clearing infrastructure, issue the bank capital-deposit certificate needed for commercial registration, or provide the auditable SWIFT trail required for the company's CIT filing and dividend repatriation. Using an offshore EMI as the primary treasury rail for M&A success-fee receipts would trigger enhanced AML review, create a shadow-payment record, and materially impair the company's ability to obtain a local tax clearance certificate. The correct architecture is a licensed Omani Tier-1 bank as the settlement backbone, with an offshore EMI permitted only as an ancillary expense-card conduit fully disclosed to the principal bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Source-of-capital opacity for the initial share-capital injection, including third-party funding or undisclosed lending that the bank cannot reconcile to the SPC's declared capital.

Root cause: Source-of-capital opacity for the initial share-capital injection, including third-party funding or undisclosed lending that the bank cannot reconcile to the SPC's declared capital.

Defense evidence: Signed source-of-wealth declaration per ultimate beneficial owner; six months of bank statements from the remitting shareholder account; accountant-certified net-worth schedule; executed share-subscription resolution; and an undertaking that the CBO and the Oman Financial Intelligence Unit will see the remittance record. If the capital is funded by a family office or trust, include the trust deed or family-office constitutional documents and a licensed fiduciary's attestation.
Beneficial-ownership chain opacity where the 100% foreign shareholder is itself a holding company, family office or partnership, causing the bank to issue a look-through request that stalls onboarding.

Root cause: Beneficial-ownership chain opacity where the 100% foreign shareholder is itself a holding company, family office or partnership, causing the bank to issue a look-through request that stalls onboarding.

Defense evidence: Complete ownership chain from the Omani SPC up to the natural persons; registry extracts or certificates of incumbency for each intermediate entity; passports and address proofs of the ultimate owners; signed CBO beneficial-ownership register undertaking; and a formal group organogram. The boutique must also provide its own internal beneficial-ownership register covering any external beneficiaries who receive fees through the account.
Unsubstantiated cross-border success-fee revenue, where large irregular advisory fee receipts are flagged as potential professional-services money laundering or disguised capital movements.

Root cause: Unsubstantiated cross-border success-fee revenue, where large irregular advisory fee receipts are flagged as potential professional-services money laundering or disguised capital movements.

Defense evidence: Signed client engagement letters with scoped mandates and milestone fee schedules; redacted deal-closing evidence (share purchase agreement headline terms, completion certificate, board minutes); tax invoices with VAT or CIT treatment; and a time-and-activities log substantiating the advisory service. A complete deal file should be kept available for a three-day ad-hoc review if the bank's transaction-monitoring engine flags a payout.
Politically exposed persons (PEP) and sanctions exposure arising from M&A target portfolios, buyers or sellers in high-risk jurisdictions or state-controlled entities.

Root cause: Politically exposed persons (PEP) and sanctions exposure arising from M&A target portfolios, buyers or sellers in high-risk jurisdictions or state-controlled entities.

Defense evidence: A named-entity sanctions-screening log covering UN, OFAC, EU and UK/HMT watchlists for every counterparty and target in each transaction; PEP due-diligence questionnaires for any state-controlled or public-sector-linked client; and a written external-counsel sanctions opinion confirming the underlying transaction does not involve restricted jurisdictions, blocked persons or prohibited activities. The boutique should maintain this log as a living document shared with the bank at account review cycles.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% on taxable profits of resident companies; no separate federal or municipal surcharge in Oman.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends paid by an Omani SPC to a non-resident shareholder; treaty may reduce to 0% for qualifying 10% or 25% shareholders or where the recipient is a treaty-resident beneficial owner.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory minimum monetary threshold is published under the Oman Income Tax Law; contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Study is strongly recommended for any cross-border related-party transaction exceeding OMR 100,000 per annum, and the Tax Authority may request Local File or master file on audit irrespective of transaction size.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not administer separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) equivalent to U.A.E. or Bahrain; however, the Commercial Companies Law and the general anti-abuse rule require the SPC to maintain actual decision-making in Oman, local employees or service premises, and a genuine commercial purpose; a foreign-owned SPC conducting only intra-group M&A advisory services without local staff or real operations will be re-characterised as a sham and assessed on deemed income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 200% of underreported tax plus administrative fine up to OMR 5,000 per offence; Tax Authority may suspend licences and publicly disclose the taxpayer identity after final assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who signed or authorised the false return are jointly and severally liable for the short amount, penalties and late payment interest; criminal courts may impose imprisonment for up to 3 years and a travel ban until full restitution.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Commercial Registry deregistration, forced dissolution under the Commercial Companies Law, reallocation of revenue as Oman-source income subject to 15% CIT plus 0.75% monthly default interest; licence revoked for violations of the investment law.

Director personal liability: The shareholder/director is denied limited liability due to abuse of corporate form; courts may order personal restitution of all fees received, a 5-year travel ban, freezing of personal assets, and a separate criminal fine under the Companies Law.
AML / Money Laundering in M&A Advisory

Statutory sanction: Under the Oman Anti-Money Laundering Law (Royal Decree 30/2010, as amended), licensed advisory firm may incur fines up to OMR 500,000; the commercial licence may be suspended and the professional practice permit cancelled.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and actual operator directors carry personal criminal liability where they consented to or negligently omitted AML checks; punishment includes personal fine up to OMR 25,000, imprisonment up to 7 years, and debarment from managing regulated commercial activities across GCC countries.
