Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Single Person Company under Royal Decree 18/2019; the founder determines capital in the Memorandum of Association. Under Royal Decree 50/2019, a 100% foreign-owned SPC may be formed without any minimum capital condition. Where the founder elects to subscribe stated capital, such capital must be deposited in a bank account in the Sultanate prior to registration and evidenced by a capital deposit certificate; no escrow account is statutorily mandated for wholesale trading activities under ISIC 4662.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), the successor authority to the General Secretariat of Taxation of the Sultanate of Oman's Ministry of Finance, competent for income tax, VAT, and related tax administration under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 28/2009) and the VAT Law (Royal Decree 121/2016). Electronic registration and filing portal: https://www.ota.gov.om (OTA e-Services / VAT Platform – VATP); customs electronic single window: Bayan System administered by the Royal Oman Police – Customs.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) at the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate. Step 2: Obtain Chamber of Commerce membership/investor credentials and lease/office instruments evidencing fixed establishment in Muscat. Step 3: MOCIIP electronically notifies the OTA of incorporation and the OTA automatically issues the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for income tax purposes; a separate VAT registration must be applied for on the VATP portal. Step 4: File VAT registration online (mandatory within 30 days of exceeding OMR 38,500 of taxable supplies; voluntary registration where supplies exceed OMR 19,250), submitting the CR certificate, constitutional documents, authorized signatory/power of attorney, passport copies of the single shareholder/manager, and a local bank account statement. Step 5: Register as an exporter/importer in the Bayan customs environment to obtain the Customs Registration Number and activate the company's export/import code.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box, fiscal-transparency election, or hybrid-mismatch regime exists for an Omani SPC; the SPC is mandatorily treated as a taxable juridical person. Available elections: (i) consolidated income tax filing for Omani-resident group members with at least 85% common ownership; (ii) election to align the SPC's accounting period with the calendar/tax year upon OTA approval; (iii) depreciation and cost-recovery method elections (including accelerated depreciation on eligible capital assets); (iv) election to carry forward tax losses for up to five years (no carry-back); and (v) election to claim foreign tax credits or double-taxation relief on foreign-source income in accordance with Oman's tax treaties and the Income Tax Law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration: OMR 38,500 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months (excluding capital assets whose cost exceeds OMR 38,500 each); voluntary VAT registration: OMR 19,250 of taxable supplies or expenses in the preceding 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available to the SPC on (i) exports of battery-grade lithium carbonate powder (H.S. 2836.99) physically dispatched by the Omani seller for exit from Oman to the GCC external market within 90 days from the date of supply under the applicable customs export declaration; (ii) transport, freight, and handling services directly linked to the exported goods; and (iii) offshore cross-border procurement services supplied by the SPC where the recipient is outside the GCC and the effective use and benefit of the supply occur outside Oman. Evidence must include proof of physical export, transfer of risks and rewards under Incoterms 2020, commercial documentation, and actual receipt of foreign currency consideration; failure to substantiate export within 90 days recharacterizes the supply as taxable at 5%.

  • Customs Export Declaration evidenced by the Bayan certificate of export with AHTN/H.S. code 2836.99 and verified customs officer stamping
  • VAT-compliant Commercial Invoice (Arabic label permissible) describing battery-grade lithium carbonate, quantity, purity, and unit price
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill with proof of physical exit from Oman within 90 days of the tax invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and SWIFT MT103/credit advice evidencing receipt of export consideration in foreign currency
  • Packing List and Third-Party Assay/Certificate of Analysis confirming battery-grade specification
  • Letter of Credit or Export Sales Contract incorporating Incoterms 2020 delivery terms and risk-passes evidence
  • Certificate of Origin (GCC/electronic form) and carrier's proof of discharge at destination

No real-time fiscal e-invoicing or API clearance mandate is presently operative in Oman; taxpayers must issue VAT-compliant tax invoices through controlled accounting systems, file periodic VAT returns on the OTA VATP portal, and preserve e-invoice repositories for ten years. Customs clearance (import and export) is mandatory through the Bayan Single Window, requiring electronic declarations with AHTN/Harmonized System codes, approved electronic stamping of customs documents, and reconciliation of the declared customs value with the VAT return and trade finance documentation. The OTA has published a roadmap toward structured e-invoicing but has not yet activated a statutory clearance API.

The OTA and Customs jointly audit zero-rating claims; where the physical export or FX repatriation is not demonstrated within the statutory 90-day export window and the 180-day repatriation benchmark, the supply is retrospectively recharacterized as a taxable supply, 5% VAT is clawed back with late-payment interest at 1.5% per month (minimum OMR 25, uncapped in practice), and compliance fines of up to OMR 5,000 per violation apply. Deliberate submission of false export declarations or fraudulent VAT refund claims attracts fines up to OMR 25,000 and imprisonment of up to five years under the VAT Law, while false customs declarations expose the SPC to pecuniary penalties of up to three times the customs value and confiscation; income-tax penalties of up to 100% of the evaded tax can further be imposed on connected sham procurement arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Rial Omani (OMR) is fully convertible on the current account and substantially convertible on the capital account. Oman observes IMF Article VIII discipline, so no general exchange-control licence is required for a foreign-owned Single Person Company to receive overseas share capital. Profit remittances, dividend distribution, and intra-group debt-service payments are lawful once audited financial statements, OTA tax registration, and Commercial Companies Law compliance are evidenced. Capital-account transactions must be routed through a CBO-licensed commercial bank, and any material foreign-currency borrowing from a non-resident lender is subject to CBO external-borrowing notification thresholds. The OMR is pegged at approximately 0.3845 per US dollar, making USD-denominated battery-metals procurement operationally stable and eliminating systematic convertibility risk for the treasury model.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not mandate a fixed conversion ratio for export receipts. Under CBO exchange-control practice, exporters and trading intermediaries must repatriate export proceeds to an Omani licensed bank within 180 days of shipment; 100% of proceeds may be retained in OMR or a foreign-currency account. Because the SPC operates as a cross-border buyer and potential re-seller of battery-grade lithium carbonate, the surrender obligation is triggered only upon physical re-export out of Oman. The bank will require the Export Declaration, underlying LC or invoice, and logistics documentation on each outbound cargo movement. Pure inbound procurement payments are not captured by the surrender regime.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), Banking Supervision and Exchange Control Directorate; supplementary oversight for capital markets and finance-company licensing is vested in the Capital Market Authority (CMA), with Oman Tax Authority (OTA) tax-card issuance and Oman Chamber of Commerce and Industry (OCCI) attestation functions as ancillary compliance dependencies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. The Central Bank of Oman does not license foreign fintech EMIs to operate OMR-denominated current accounts for Omani legal entities, and only CBO-licensed banks may clear OMR. A selected offshore EMI may provide a restricted USD virtual-IBAN wallet solely as a clearing adjunct, provided it maintains a correspondent relationship with a licensed Omani bank and does not touch the primary trade settlement flow. The SPC should therefore use EMIs only for low-value pre-onboarding verification exercises and route all material lithium carbonate trade payments through Bank Muscat, NBO, or HSBC Oman treasury desks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital is injected via SWIFT MT103 credit from the overseas parent through its correspondent bank into the SPC's capital escrow account at a CBO-licensed Omani bank. The purpose narrative must read 'Foreign Direct Investment – Share Capital Subscription' and be cross-referenced to the SPC's Commercial Registration, OTA tax card, and the single-member capital resolution. No prior CBO authorisation is required for equity capital; however, any convertible shareholder loan or intragroup debt facility exceeding the regulatory de-minimis threshold must be disclosed under CBO external-borrowing notification. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File obligation is triggered where the aggregate value of controlled transactions with related parties exceeds OMR 3,000,000 in a tax year; Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds OMR 50,000,000; CbC reporting notification applies to groups with consolidated revenue above the OECD threshold of OMR 350,000,000. Even below thresholds, the taxpayer must submit the OTA-TP102 disclosure if any controlled transaction exists and must maintain sufficiently precise contemporaneous pricing evidence.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime, but the Income Tax Law and the Oman Tax Authority apply economic substance as a substantive rule. A foreign-owned SPC is resident and taxable in Oman only if it has genuine place of effective management, fixed premises, personnel performing procurement/trading decisions, and business activity not merely a paper record. Shell entities using Omani residence to book profits, circumvent foreign CFC rules, or abuse DTA claims are subject to GAAR recharacterization, PE attribution, denial of treaty benefits, cancellation of commercial registration, and criminal anti-money-laundering exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Recomputed tax at 15%, plus monthly late-payment surcharge of 1%; additional penalty up to 100% of the evaded tax; administrative fines up to OMR 10,000; criminal prosecution before the Omani courts with imprisonment for up to three years; tax authority freezing of bank accounts and attachment of corporate assets.

Director personal liability: The SPC manager/director who signs a false return is personally liable for the evaded tax, penalties, and court costs when the corporate form is used to conceal fraud; travel bans may be issued by the Public Prosecutor; directors may be sentenced to imprisonment and be required to pay civil restitution to the Oman Tax Authority.
Transfer Pricing and Arm's Length Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Oman Tax Authority TP adjustment that increases taxable income at 15%; disallowance of excessive deductions; administrative penalty of OMR 1,000 to OMR 5,000 for missing documentation; monthly late-payment surcharge on additional tax. If the TP adjustment is connected to an artificial arrangement, GAAR penalties and reconsideration of the SPC's residence may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's length intercompany contracts and sign the CIT return containing TP schedules are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax and penalties if the documentation is knowingly false; they may face direct assessment on the basis of void transactions and abuse of the SPC vehicle.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the SPC as fiscally transparent or as a sham; reallocation of profits to the jurisdiction where actual decision making occurs; 15% Omani tax with penalties for under-reporting; denial of DTA benefits for lack of beneficial ownership; commercial registration strike-off by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry, and Investment Promotion; director disqualification orders.

Director personal liability: The foreign controlling director and the SPC manager may be personally assessed when the corporate veil is pierced for sham or undercapitalization; they may face travel bans while the tax case is pending, disqualification as officers, and personal liability for unpaid corporate tax plus sanctions costs if funds were diverted abroad.
Anti-Money Laundering, Sanctions and Trade-Based Value Shifting

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Oman AML Law (Royal Decree 30/2016 as amended by Royal Decree 60/2021): corporate fines up to OMR 50,000 and cancellation of commercial registration; freezing orders issued by the Central Bank of Oman and ROP; trade sanction enforcement under UNSC resolutions; goods seizure; criminal fines of OMR 5,000 to OMR 20,000 and imprisonment up to three years for responsible persons.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners with actual knowledge or willful blindness are criminally liable; they can be fined personally, imprisoned, banned from travel, and have their personal assets frozen or forfeited, notwithstanding the SPC's limited liability, where the SPC is used as a conduit for money laundering or trade-based financial crime.
