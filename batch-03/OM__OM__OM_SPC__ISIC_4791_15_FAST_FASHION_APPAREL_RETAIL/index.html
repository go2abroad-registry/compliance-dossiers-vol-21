Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Authority of the Sultanate of Oman (الهيئة العامة للضرائب; formerly the Secretariat General of Taxation), Muscat; official self-service taxpayer portal: https://taxoman.om. Customs export clearance is handled by the Royal Oman Police – Directorate General of Customs through the Bayan single window. A foreign-owned Oman Single Person Company (SPC) obtains both its income tax file and VAT registration through the Tax Authority portal; the same TRN is used for e-invoicing and export substantiation.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SPC under the Commercial Companies Law via MoCIIP (Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion) through the Invest Easy platform; obtain a Commercial Registration bearing ISIC 4791/NAICS 458110 e-commerce fast-fashion activity; 100% foreign ownership is permitted under the Foreign Investment Law (RD 50/2019, amended by RD 108/2020) and no minimum capital is required. Step 2: Complete the SPC register and appoint a resident general manager and registered address in Oman. Step 3: Register for corporate income tax with the Tax Authority upon commencement of business; the Authority issues the Tax File Number/TRN and activates the tax record linked to the Commercial Registration. Step 4: Register for VAT under Royal Decree 121/2020 where taxable supplies or deductible input purchases in the preceding 12 months reach OMR 38,500, or are expected to reach that amount in the following 12 months; voluntary registration is allowed from OMR 19,250; a non-resident supplier of e-commerce goods to Omani consumers must register without a threshold. Step 5: Obtain/activate the Bayan customs registration number and, if applicable, a bonded warehouse or foreign-trade-zone authorization. Step 6: Open the Oman bank account and link it to the TRN; activate Tax Authority e-invoicing credentials; retain all statutory records for at least 5 years.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The SPC is a separate juridical person and is taxed at entity level under the Income Tax Law (RD 28/2009 as amended by RD 14/2023): corporate income tax applies at 15% on taxable profits above OMR 30,000 (small business profits up to OMR 30,000 are effectively exempt subject to conditions). No pass-through or partnership transparency exists; profits are not attributed to the foreign shareholder for Omani income tax at the entity stage. The SPC is also a separate taxable person for the 5% VAT; it is subject to worldwide-income taxation as an Omani resident company, while Omani-source income of a non-resident shareholder is generally outside the SPC's pass-through profile and dealt with at shareholder level under the applicable double tax treaty.

Available election forms

Oman does not operate a US-style check-the-box or any elective classification regime; tax classification is fixed by the legal form under the Commercial Companies Law and Income Tax Law. Available statutory elections include: (i) voluntary VAT registration at the OMR 19,250 threshold; (ii) application-based VAT grouping/related-party consolidation under the VAT Law where the SPC controls or is controlled by another Omani taxable person, allowing single-taxpayer treatment; (iii) election to recover input VAT incurred before VAT registration on capital and pre-operating expenses under the VAT Executive Regulations; (iv) election to defer import VAT through customs warehousing or the Bayan customs procedure for goods imported for re-export; (v) election to issue consolidated self-billed/export tax invoices for marketplace and BNPL fulfillment arrangements where the transactional chain qualifies under the Executive Regulations. Hybrid mismatch elections are not provided for in Omani domestic law; the SPC should therefore rely on treaty and GCC VAT-framework mapping to prevent unintended double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 38,500 of taxable supplies or deductible input expenses in the preceding 12 months, or an expectation of exceeding OMR 38,500 in the following 12 months. Voluntary registration: OMR 19,250. Non-resident suppliers of digital and e-commerce services to Omani consumers have no threshold and must register from the first taxable supply. Registration must be completed within 30 days of crossing the mandatory threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a 0% VAT rate applies where (i) the Oman SPC is the supplier and exporter of the fast-fashion goods; (ii) the goods are physically removed from Oman to a destination outside the GCC implementing states; (iii) removal occurs within 90 days from the date of supply; (iv) the customer is a non-resident located outside the GCC; and (v) the SPC holds a complete export file (customs export declaration, transport document, foreign exchange remittance evidence). Freight, packing, handling and insurance directly connected to the export are also zero-rated. Supplies consigned to another GCC/VAT-implementing state are zero-rated only where the goods are transported to that state and VAT is accounted for there under the intra-GCC movement rules; otherwise they are taxable in Oman. Services: services supplied to a non-resident recipient are zero-rated where the economic benefit is enjoyed outside Oman under the place-of-supply rules, including cross-border marketplace, logistics and marketing services for the fast-fashion trade; services physically used in Oman remain taxable at 5%. E-commerce fulfillment via a marketplace requires the SPC to retain marketplace consignment and transport records to support the zero-rated claim.

  • Customs export declaration (Bayan) validated by the Royal Oman Police – Directorate General of Customs, referencing the SPC TRN, HS classification of the apparel/footwear and the export destination;
  • Original or authenticated transport document: negotiable Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the Oman SPC as shipper/exporter and the non-resident buyer or consignee as final consignee;
  • Inward remittance certificate issued by a licensed Omani bank evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in convertible foreign currency within the CBO 180-day repatriation deadline;
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and certificate of origin (GCC or preferential RoO where claimed) showing contract terms, quantities, unit prices and total consideration identical to the customs declaration;
  • Zero-rated VAT export tax invoice containing the SPC VAT registration number, the Article 48 zero-rating reason code, ship date and all statutory VAT invoice fields.

Oman is implementing a phased mandatory e-invoicing framework under Tax Authority invoicing decisions of 2022–2023 implementing VAT Law Articles 55–56. Phase 1 large taxpayers (annual taxable supplies above OMR 10 million) were onboarded to the centralized e-invoice integration and clearance framework from January 2024; other taxpayers are being rolled out during 2024–2025. The mandate requires: (a) generation of structured, machine-readable electronic tax invoices in the Authority-approved XML/JSON schema; (b) transmission of B2B e-invoice data to the Tax Authority platform in near real time before or at the time of supply; (c) a digital stamp/QR code on B2C receipts, including retail checkout receipts issued through Shopify Payments and Klarna BNPL checkout; (d) Arabic/English language content and full VAT fields; (e) 5-year electronic archiving of the invoice database; and (f) integration between the e-commerce platform, payment gateway and the Taxoman-compliant invoicing API. Failure to obtain the Authority verification ID after the taxpayer's integration phase begins triggers administrative penalties and potential disallowance of input VAT credits.

Export zero-rating is a high-risk verification area for the Oman Tax Authority. The SPC must retain the complete export dossier for at least 5 years from the end of the VAT period to which it relates. Where the documentation bundle is incomplete, internally inconsistent or fabricated, the Tax Authority will reverse the zero-rated treatment, assess 5% VAT on the gross consideration, claw back input VAT previously credited on the exported stock and add late-payment penalties and administrative fines of up to OMR 25,000 per violation under VAT Law Article 80. Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under VAT Law Article 83, attracting a penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded, potential imprisonment of up to 5 years, publication of the taxpayer's name and a 5-year retrospective assessment window. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days triggers Central Bank of Oman exchange-control procedures and, in practice, the denial of the inward remittance certificate relied upon for zero-rating; sustained non-compliance leads to suspension of the tax clearance certificate, which in turn blocks renewal of the Commercial Registration and continued use of the Bayan customs account.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial is externally convertible and strictly pegged to the U.S. Dollar at OMR 1 = USD 2.60028. Both current and capital account transactions are in principle freely convertible for resident and non-resident entities, subject only to standard CBO reporting and AML/KYC due diligence. For the foreign-owned SPC, profit remittance to the 100% non-resident shareholder is not subject to separate CBO exchange-control approval or dividend withholding tax; however, the bank will require proof of settlement of Oman corporate income tax liability (15% standard rate) and a tax compliance clearance in practice. Capital repatriation on liquidation or share-transfer remains unrestricted although bank-level source-of-funds verification and the corporate registry clearance certificate must be produced.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no fixed mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in freely convertible foreign currency in a CBO-licensed authorized-dealer account. For balance-of-payments and AML purposes, banks require the export invoice, customs export declaration, and transport document upon crediting, and commercial practice expects repatriation within 90-120 days. For the fast-fashion SPC the same documentary discipline applies to import settlements: payment must be supported by supplier invoices, purchase orders, and the relevant customs or logistics documentation to avoid transaction-without-economic-substance flags.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), the Sultanate of Oman's statutory monetary, banking, and exchange-control authority established under the Banking Law. The CBO issues binding Foreign Exchange and Payments circulars, licenses Authorized Dealers, and supervises the clearing and settlement of all foreign-currency transactions conducted within the Sultanate.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-Medium with material legal constraints. The CBO does not currently operate a standalone Electronic Money Institution licensing lane; non-bank cross-border payment providers must obtain separate authorization and face elevated regulatory capital and operational restrictions. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex or Currencycloud may be engaged only as a supplementary low-value disbursement channel under a bank-sponsored arrangement and cannot serve as the primary clearing rail. For this fast-fashion SPC, the principal settlement ledger must reside with a CBO-licensed commercial bank while Shopify Payments and Klarna BNPL remain integrated as digital merchant-of-record gateways rather than treasury substitutes.

For this fast-fashion SPC, the principal settlement ledger must reside with a CBO-licensed commercial bank while Shopify Payments and Klarna BNPL remain integrated as digital merchant-of-record gateways rather than treasury substitutes.
