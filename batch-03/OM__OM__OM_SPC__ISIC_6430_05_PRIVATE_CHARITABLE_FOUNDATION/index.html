Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Omani law does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Single Person Company. The subscribed capital must be stated in the Memorandum of Association and paid within the period fixed by the founding shareholder. No escrow deposit is required for the standard SPC, but the shareholder must show lawful source of funds and maintain capital consistent with the charitable endowment and grant obligations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Secretariat General of Taxation, Ministry of Finance, Sultanate of Oman; electronic portal: O-Tax (https://otax.taxoman.gov.om)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a Single Person Company in Oman must first obtain Commercial Registration from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion, then apply for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) via the O-Tax portal. For VAT purposes, a separate VAT registration is required if taxable supplies exceed the compulsory threshold. The application must include constitutive documents, Commercial Registration certificate, lease agreement, and valid passport copies of owner. Registration is typically completed within 5-10 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities establishing a Single Person Company in Oman must first obtain Commercial Registration from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion, then apply for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) via the O-Tax portal. For VAT purposes, a separate VAT registration is required if taxable supplies exceed the compulsory threshold. The application must include constitutive documents, Commercial Registration certificate, lease agreement, and valid passport copies of owner. Registration is typically completed within 5-10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime available. Oman's tax law recognises SPC as a juridical person subject to corporate income tax (15% standard rate under Royal Decree No. 9/2017, as amended). For VAT, the SPC may elect to register voluntarily if below threshold, subject to timing restrictions. There are no hybrid-mismatch elections; however, thin capitalisation and transfer-pricing documentation requirements align with OECD minimum standards.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 38,500 (annual taxable supplies and imports threshold for mandatory registration); voluntary registration permitted at OMR 19,250

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods exported outside Oman and certain international services (e.g., transportation, direct services related to exported goods) as specified under Article 81 of the Executive Regulations of the VAT Law. The exporter must be the VAT-registered supplier, retain proof of export, and evidence that the goods physically left Oman within 90 days of supply or invoice date. For offshore services, evidence must demonstrate the recipient is outside Oman and that the benefit is effectively consumed outside Oman.

  • Customs Export Declaration (single administrative document)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank statement evidencing foreign currency receipt from export)

Oman VAT Law (Royal Decree No. 121/2020) mandates e-invoicing for B2B transactions, but full real-time clearance is not yet universally deployed. Taxpayers with taxable supplies exceeding OMR 1 million must submit VAT returns electronically via the O-Tax portal. The Tax Authority is phasing a Mutanabi-driven unified invoicing schema; digital stamping and real-time fiscal clearance are expected to be compulsory for all VAT-registered taxpayers by 2026, with API integration via the O-Tax developer portal.

False or fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger repayment of VAT plus a penalty of up to 100% of the unpaid tax, with administrative fines ranging from OMR 500 to OMR 50,000 depending on severity. In cases of deliberate misstatement, the Tax Authority may impose criminal prosecution under Oman's Tax Evasion Law (Royal Decree No. 90/2021), including imprisonment up to 3 years. Retrospective clawback period is 5 years from the tax period in which the claim was filed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current-account transactions under the CBO's liberalised foreign-exchange framework; the currency is pegged to the US dollar at a central rate of 1 OMR = USD 2.6008, and the official exchange regime is anchored to the US dollar with no band for market variability. Capital-account convertibility is formally free, but capital inflows and outflows are monitored through the CBO's statistical reporting system: inbound endowment contributions and outbound grant disbursements are classified respectively as 'other capital transfers' or 'other current transfers' and require a signed endowment deed plus a bank declaration for the CBO's banking statistics module. Profit remittance hurdles for a foreign-owned SPC are limited to (i) production of a valid tax-clearance certificate covering corporate tax and the 10% dividend withholding tax (subject to relief under Oman's tax treaties), (ii) evidence that the funds have been subjected to the foundation's internal audit process, and (iii) compliance with the CBO's 'transaction purpose' disclosure requirement for amounts above OMR 10,000. No exchange-control licence, no prior CBO approval and no profit-repatriation quota apply to the charitable foundation's grant-making operations; the foundation is not a licensed bank and is therefore not subject to prudential FX position limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: none. Oman does not impose any mandatory surrender, conversion or repatriation timeline on export proceeds, and export receipts may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts held with licensed banks. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% - the OMR/USD peg makes conversion unnecessary and the CBO does not require businesses to convert export earnings. However, the CBO's reporting framework requires a licensed bank to capture all receipts above OMR 10,000 in the daily Foreign Exchange Statistics system and to flag any receipt inconsistent with the foundation's stated charitable purpose; the foundation must therefore maintain a documentary trail for each receipt to avoid misclassification as a commercial trade receivable.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), acting under the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000), the Foreign Exchange Operations Regulations and the Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism Law (Royal Decree 30/2016), with the Oman Tax Authority jointly responsible for issuing the tax-clearance certificate required for outward profit or dividend remittances by wholly foreign-owned Omani companies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM for cross-border treasury use; offshore electronic-money institutions (EMIs) may serve only as a supplementary rail for small grants and expenses, not as the primary corridor. Legal constraints: the CBO does not recognise an 'EMI' licence category for offshore digital banks; a foreign EMI may not open OMR- or USD-denominated correspondent accounts in Oman and may not hold the foundation's endowment corpus for more than 30 calendar days. Where a foreign EMI is used for inbound donations or outbound scientific grants, the foundation must (i) sweep all funds received into a licensed onshore bank within five business days, (ii) ensure the EMI is registered on the CBO's list of 'foreign payment service providers allowed to settle through a national corridor bank', and (iii) observe substance-limits, so that the EMI cannot receive any donor contribution whose instrument restricts funds to Oman's territory. The recommended structure is a dual-rail system: licensed banks for endowment custody and cross-border clearing, supported by an approved EMI for micropayments below USD 10,000 with an automatic settlement sweep to the onshore account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Protocol: the endowment is settled by an SWIFT MT103 or MT202 message from a credible donor bank to the foundation's account at Bank Muscat or HSBC Oman. The payment must carry the beneficiary account number and the narrative 'Charitable endowment contribution for scientific research grants'; Oman does not operate a numeric purpose-code system, but the bank's back office will encode the transaction in the CBO's batch statistics as 'inward remittance - donation' for capital or current classification. The CBO reporting threshold is OMR 10,000 per transaction, and a same-day exception report is filed for any larger credit. The receipt is first held in the Capital Escrow Account; release to the operational treasury account requires a matching endowment deed, a board resolution and a source-of-funds confirmation. For a foreign sovereign grant or for funds originating from an international foundation, the bank may require a supplementary endowment verification certificate confirming that the contributor is not a shell entity; the standard completion time for the first inbound settlement is T+1 with intraday verification by the bank's compliance desk.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 outbound wire for international grant disbursements to university or research-institution beneficiaries, supported by a grant-identification reference inserted in the Remittance Information field (tag 70) and by a paper remittance instruction. Domestic grants to Omani-resident beneficiaries are cleared intraday through the Oman Real-Time Gross Settlement (ORTGS) system, and interbank instructions are free of charge. The MT103 message must be accompanied by the executed grant agreement; otherwise the bank will route the payment through enhanced sign-off and may apply a 72-hour hold for additional verification. Commercial letters of credit are unsuitable for ISIC 6430 grant payments because grants are unconditional transfers rather than trade settlements; a commercial LC should be opened only where the foundation purchases scientific equipment directly and names the university as the documentary beneficiary. For markets outside the GCC, the settlement corridor must be a CBO-approved correspondent bank such as Bank Muscat's New York corridor or HSBC's London corridor, ensuring optionality of USD or EUR settlement, and all USD payments to potentially sanctioned jurisdictions must first pass the bank's OFAC filter and the CBO's list of prohibited counterparties.Deliverable FX forwards (OMR/USD) with tenors from one to twelve months, booked through a CBO-licensed onshore bank to fix the rial value of a known foreign-currency grant inflow or outflow.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) between OMR and USD, EUR or GBP legs concluded with a licensed local bank, matching the endowment corpus tenure to a scientific grant liability and confirmed under an ISDA or approved local master agreement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted only in the offshore interbank market for currency pairs that lack onshore OMR liquidity, for example INR or PHP grant obligations; booking an NDF through a CBO-licensed bank is permissible only on a back-to-back fully hedged basis and is prohibited as a speculative standalone position.Purchased vanilla FX options (premium-paid call and put structures, with no option-writing) to cap disbursement costs on multi-currency grant commitments, provided the option is entered with a level-1 credit institution and registered in the foundation's treasury risk policy; exotic structures, leveraged hedging and option-selling are not permissible for a charitable endowment treasury.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory when the Omani taxpayer's annual related-party turnover or transaction value exceeds OMR 10 million; Master File mandatory when the worldwide group consolidated revenue exceeds OMR 100 million; Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) is relevant if the ultimate parent group revenue exceeds OMR 2.5 billion or the foreign parent's country requires joint filing.

Economic substance scope: Oman's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to every Omani-resident legal entity, including a Single Person Company structured as a private charitable foundation. The foundation must maintain real commercial substance in Oman: a strategic board or trustee committee meeting onshore, a documented grant-making and investment policy, adequate local staff and premises, and annual expenditure commensurate with the value of assets managed. Passive asset-holding without charitable activity is treated as an artificial shell arrangement and can invalidate the non-profit exemption. An annual ESR filing is required through the OTA portal, with supporting substance evidence retained for at least 6 years.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Exemption Claim

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Income Tax Law (RD 28/2009 as amended): a fine equal to 100% of the tax evaded, plus an administrative penalty of 25% of the under-declared tax, and imprisonment of up to three years upon referral to the Public Prosecution. OTA also imposes late-payment interest at 2% per month and may make an estimated assessment.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the SPC is used as a sham charitable vehicle; directors and the owning shareholder become jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of unpaid tax, face asset freezes, travel bans, and disqualification from serving as directors in Oman for up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Artificial Asset Holding

Statutory sanction: OTA applies a deemed taxable income equal to 15% of gross assets if no audited records are available, imposes a 40% penalty on the resulting tax difference, revokes the charitable license, and levies ESR penalties of up to OMR 50,000. The Ministry of Commercial Investments may order the compulsory liquidation of the SPC.

Director personal liability: Omani company law allows unrestricted piercing of the veil when the company is a fraud or façade; the shareholder and directors are personally liable for the foundation's unpaid taxes, restitution of misappropriated endowment assets, and are subject to travel bans and up to five years' imprisonment for abuse of identity.
AML / Terrorist Financing through Charitable Endowment

Statutory sanction: Under the Omani AML Law (RD 30/2016 as amended), the financial and administrative penalties range from OMR 50,000 to OMR 1,000,000, plus confiscation of the funded property and cancellation of the entity's commercial and charitable licenses. Criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment of up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Directors and trustees who knowingly facilitated illicit transfers face joint criminal liability, personal asset forfeiture, travel bans, and imprisonment. The Central Bank of Oman may also issue a freeze order against personal bank accounts of the controlling shareholders.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Related-Party Manipulation

Statutory sanction: OTA adjusts the taxable income to arm's length and imposes a 30% penalty on the transfer-pricing adjustment, plus interest at 2% per month and up to 100% of the tax as a severe-due penalty. Falsifying TP records is a criminal offence under the Income Tax Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incomplete or false TP disclosure schedules are personally liable for the adjusted tax difference; the corporate veil may be pierced to recover penalties from personal assets, accompanied by travel bans and a custodial sentence of up to three years for submitting false official documents.
