Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Omani Single Person Company under RD 18/2019. The sole shareholder must allocate capital sufficient for the stated objects; funds must be remitted through a licensed Omani bank. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; the OMR is fully convertible on current account. The capital account is de facto open: 100% foreign ownership is permitted for Single Person Companies (SPCs) under the Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2019) and Commercial Companies Law (Royal Decree 18/2019), with no minimum capital requirement. No CBO pre-approval is required for equity injections, shareholder loans, dividends, royalties, interest, or repatriation of liquidation proceeds. The OMR is pegged at 1 USD = OMR 0.3845. Practical profit-remittance hurdles are operational and tax-driven rather than exchange-control-driven: (i) 10% withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents unless reduced by treaty; (ii) requirement for audited financial statements showing distributable reserves; (iii) settled tax position and tax clearance from the Oman Tax Authority; (iv) bank documentation evidencing the underlying commercial purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory OMR conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may lawfully retain export proceeds in OMR or in permitted foreign currency accounts at licensed onshore banks. As a prudential/BOP reporting matter, CBO and banks expect export proceeds to be credited to Oman within the agreed commercial terms (bank policy default: 180 days from shipment, extendable to 360 days with documented justification). For ISIC 3250 titanium implant export, the receiving bank must obtain the Bayan export declaration, commercial invoice, ocean/air waybill, and the OFDA medical-device registration reference at the time of crediting. Non-repatriation beyond 12 months without reasonable cause will trigger enhanced AML/TF review and may result in downgrade of trade facilities, but is not a criminal exchange-control offence.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), operating under the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000) and the Foreign Exchange Business Law (Royal Decree 38/2000). CBO exclusively licenses commercial banks and money exchange houses, administers the Omani Rial (OMR), supervises the OMARG real-time gross settlement system, and enforces AML/CFT obligations under Royal Decree 30/2016 and CBO Circular BM1110.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

LOW as a primary OMR treasury rail; MODERATE only as an auxiliary offshore foreign-currency facility. Legal constraint: CBO Foreign Exchange Business Law (RD 38/2000) requires any entity providing money exchange or remittance services in or from Oman to hold a CBO licence. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Mercury, Payoneer) are not CBO-licensed, cannot hold OMR IBANs, cannot clear through OMARG or the Muzn retail payment system, and cannot execute Bayan customs payments, VAT/tax payments, or commercial LC/shipping document settlements. Omani banks will normally refuse to credit third-party payments routed via unlicensed EMIs unless full supporting trade and FX documentation is provided. Recommended structure: maintain all statutory, OMR, LC, and trade accounts at a licensed Omani bank; use an offshore EMI only for residual foreign-supplier payments below ancillary thresholds, with full reconciliation back to the local account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder/parent to the SPC's account at a licensed Omani bank. Remittance instruction must include: beneficiary SPC legal name and MoCIIP Commercial Registration (CR) number; beneficiary bank SWIFT BIC; beneficiary IBAN under the CBO National IBAN Format (OM + check digits + bank code + account number); and remittance purpose code BOP-521 / BPM6 Direct Investment-Equity ("Capital Injection for OM_SPC"). Receiving bank will require the SPC's onboarding file, CR certificate, constitutional documents, and confirmation that the Commercial Companies Law (RD 18/2019) single-person structure is valid. No CBO prior approval is needed; however, the bank must submit a Foreign Exchange Transaction report for CBO balance-of-payments statistics. Although no statutory escrow account is mandated for an SPC (unlike SAOG share issues), a designated Capital Escrow Account at the bank is recommended to evidence segregation of subscribed capital for the MoCIIP capital attestation and auditor's capital certificate. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 30,000,000 consolidated group revenue triggers the Master File requirement; an aggregate controlled transaction value exceeding OMR 5,000,000 in a single tax year triggers the Local File requirement. All taxpayers with any cross-border related-party transactions are expected to maintain a contemporaneous summary TP disclosure, even below these thresholds, and the Oman Tax Authority may request full documentation where there are indicators of non-arm's length pricing.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not apply a formal Economic Substance Regulation modeled on UAE Cabinet Resolution No. 31/2020, but the Income Tax Law and the anti-avoidance doctrine require that an Omani SPC maintain real business substance in Oman. For a titanium orthopedic implant manufacturer, the SPC must lease or own industrial premises, retain full-time technical production staff, conduct electron beam 3D melting and sterilization operations from Oman, maintain board minutes and strategic decision files in Oman, and operate the SWIFT/letter of credit collection infrastructure with commercial substance. A non-resident parent cannot strip taxable manufacturing profits through passive invoice arrangements where the core value creation is executed in Oman.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Imposition of additional assessed tax, penalties up to 25-100% of the evaded tax, monetary fines of up to OMR 20,000, and imprisonment under the Oman Income Tax Law and Penal Code. Tax clearance may be revoked, halting export registrations.

Director personal liability: A director who signs a false return may be personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties if the corporate veil is pierced; Omani courts and the Tax Authority may, upon criminal conviction, issue travel bans to all directors, freeze personal assets in Oman, and impose imprisonment of up to three years in serious cases.
Withholding Tax and Repatriation Violation

Statutory sanction: The taxpayer is held liable for the uncollected withholding tax as a penalty in addition to the original tax liability; super-added fines and a monthly late-payment charge of 1% apply. The Oman Tax Authority may block renewal of the SPC's tax clearance and deny deduction of the gross payment.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Omani SPC are named as statutory withholding agents; personal liability attaches to the officers who caused the payment if the SPC is unable to settle, and senior managers may face fines and travel restrictions pending collection.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: The Oman Tax Authority may re-determine taxable income at arm's length, disallow excessive intercompany payments, impose a 25% adjustment penalty, and charge a monthly 1% late-payment surcharge on any resulting tax deficiency. Taxpayers may also be required to file amended returns and revise subsequent pricing.

Director personal liability: Directors and liaison officers who approved non-arm's length invoices can be held civilly liable for the deficiency if they have signed a certificate declaring the intercompany prices correct; an unresolved assessment may trigger an order preventing departure from the Sultanate until the tax is settled.
Shell Company / Artificial Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Oman Tax Authority may disregard the entity as a sham, implicate the foreign parent directly, and assess tax on the ultimate beneficial owner; the SPC may lose deductions, tax clearance, and the right to use the SPC legal form, and the Registrar may strike it from the Commercial Register.

Director personal liability: Directors and the single foreign shareholder face piercing of the corporate veil, recovery of all unpaid taxes from personal assets, cancellation of visas and residency, a travel ban, and in aggravated cases a term of imprisonment for facilitating an entity used to defeat taxation or money laundering controls.
Money Laundering and Suspected Illicit Payment Flows

Statutory sanction: Oman's AML Law imposes independent sanctions of fines up to OMR 100,000 or up to seven years of imprisonment for each money laundering offense; the Financial Services Authority and the Oman Tax Authority may freeze the bank account of the SPC and seize assets derived from the offense; the SPC may be terminated and permanently disqualified from medical device export licensing.

Director personal liability: The single foreign shareholder and every signed director face joint and several criminal liability; authorities may impose a travel ban and an asset freeze against personal property and shares. If the SPC is considered a façade, the corporate veil will be pierced, and directors will be personally liable for restitution to victims and for all tax and penalties related to the concealed revenues.
