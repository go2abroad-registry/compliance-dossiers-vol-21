Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), Al Khuwair, Muscat; official electronic services portal 'Tax Pulse' (https://taxpulse.om).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain Commercial Registration (CR) for the Single Person Company (SPC) from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) and the Omani commercial register. Step 2: Enroll in the OTA 'Tax Pulse' portal and complete the electronic tax-registration application using the CR number, the SPC's constitutional documents, and the shareholder's natural-person identification data. Step 3: Submit the VAT registration application within 30 days of the month-end in which taxable supplies exceed OMR 38,000 (mandatory registration) or elect registration once taxable supplies reach OMR 19,000 (voluntary registration); a foreign-owned SPC without a fixed place of residence in Oman must appoint a tax representative resident in Oman for VAT registration and filing. Step 4: Upon approval, the OTA issues the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN / VAT ID) and the VAT registration certificate. Step 5: The SPC simultaneously registers for corporate income tax purposes under Royal Decree No. 28/2023 (new Oman Income Tax Law), opening a periodic annual CIT compliance account in Tax Pulse.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Oman does not operate an OECD-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections and claims include: (i) voluntary VAT registration at the OMR 19,000 threshold; (ii) VAT tax-group / fiscal unity application under Sultani Decree No. 121/2020 (Oman VAT Law) for related persons, subject to OTA approval; (iii) statutory small-business relief / zero-rate band claim under Royal Decree No. 28/2023 for resident enterprises with taxable income within the prescribed minimum threshold; and (iv) choice between the standard quarterly VAT return cycle and any permitted alternative return period on grounds of administrative efficiency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration when the value of taxable supplies over any 12-month rolling period exceeds OMR 38,000 (approximately USD 98,700); voluntary registration is available when taxable supplies exceed OMR 19,000 (approximately USD 49,350).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to: (i) supplies of goods physically exported from Oman, conditional on the goods leaving Omani territory within the statutory period and being supported by a customs declaration; and (ii) certain offshore services, including digital video-chat matching and platform subscription services, supplied to a recipient located outside Oman, provided the recipient has no place of residence or fixed establishment in Oman, the services are not effectively used or enjoyed in Oman, and the SPC maintains contemporaneous documentary evidence of the non-resident status of the counterparty, subscriber geolocation data, payment-origin records, and platform-level sales reports.

  • Oman Customs Export Declaration (electronic Bayan)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or equivalent bank statement confirming receipt of foreign-currency consideration from the non-resident counterparty
  • Non-resident status certificate, subscriber/end-user geolocation logs, and platform sales reports

Oman is executing a phased electronic-invoicing mandate under OTA e-invoicing decisions applicable from the 2024/2025 tax-compliance cohorts. VAT-registered persons must issue machine-readable electronic tax invoices through OTA-integrated solutions, transmit invoice datasets to the Tax Pulse clearance environment via secure APIs, and incorporate the OTA-issued QR/digital-stamp element on every tax invoice for Omani supplies. The SPC's digital sales through Apple IAP and Paymentwall remain subject to the same invoice-data and record-keeping obligations for Omani end-user transactions, and the OTA requires real-time or near-real-time clearance for taxpayers included in the mandatory e-invoicing wave.

The OTA may require an exporter to reverse any zero-rated treatment and re-assess VAT at the standard rate if the substantiation bundle is incomplete, internally inconsistent, or not produced within the statutory demand period. Fraudulent zero-rating facilitation is treated as tax evasion under the Oman VAT Law and Executive Regulations, subjecting the SPC to refund clawback, late-payment penalties, administrative fines per the statutory contravention schedule, and, for material or repeated offences, criminal-court prosecution including imprisonment of the natural persons responsible.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Sultanate of Oman maintains a fully open capital and current account with no centralised exchange-control licensing regime for convertible currencies. The OMR is freely convertible and trading is unrestricted for all current-account transactions, including cross-border digital-service receipts, app-store settlement flows, operational expenses, staff salaries and vendor payments. Capital-account convertibility is equally permissive: a 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) may receive equity injections, shareholder loans, trade finance and related-party funding without prior CBO approval under the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2019). Profit remittance is unrestricted; dividends and repatriated capital may be transferred abroad once corporate tax returns are filed and the annual audited financial statements are approved, and no special repatriation licence, concession or exchange-control permit is required. Practical frictions are limited to bank-level source-of-funds due diligence, the proper documentation of accrued profits, and possible cross-border withholding-tax exposure under the Oman Tax Law (Royal Decree 28/2009 as amended) on non-resident royalty, technical-service or other business-contract payments. Non-resident investors face no currency-convertibility premium and no restrictions on holding, transferring or liquidating OMR-denominated assets.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no fixed timeline for repatriation or conversion of foreign-currency receipts, and no CBO directive requiring exporters or service exporters to exchange export proceeds into OMR. Cross-border digital-service revenues, including app-store remittances and aggregated payment-platform settlements, may therefore be credited to and retained indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts with CBO-licensed banks. Conversion into OMR is discretionary and executed at prevailing market rates only when needed for local operating expenditure. The SPC remains obliged to record all such receipts in its accounting records, reflect them in audited financial statements, and provide accurate balance-of-payments data to the receiving bank and the National Centre for Statistics and Information (NCSI) for statistical reporting, but no surrender, liquidation or central-bank approval obligations attach to export earnings.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), established under the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000), is the sole monetary and exchange-control authority in the Sultanate of Oman, acting in coordination with the National Centre for Financial Information (NCFI) as the Omani Financial Intelligence Unit and the Oman Tax Authority for fiscal enforcement. The CBO licenses all commercial banks, payment service providers, exchange houses and finance companies; administers the Omani Rial (OMR) exchange-rate system, which is the fixed peg of 1 USD = 0.3845 OMR (1 OMR = USD 2.6008); operates national payment, clearing and settlement systems; and issues binding AML/CFT and foreign-exchange circulars to licensed financial institutions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The equity capital injection must be executed by SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's home-country bank to the SPC's capital verification account maintained with the nominated Omani tier-1 bank. The wire must identify the purpose code for direct investment equity capital in accordance with the IMF Balance of Payments Manual and CBO statistical reporting (category 'Direct investment: equity capital', typically reported under BOPS code DI.1); the remitter name and account must exactly correspond to the sole shareholder recorded in the Commercial Register; and the beneficiary must be the SPC's legal name and CR number. The receiving bank applies standard KYC and sanctions checks, credits the funds to the temporary capital account, and releases them to the operational current account after verifying the paid-up capital is not below any minimum stated in the Articles of Association, the CR certificate is current, and the shareholder resolution authorising the transfer is archived. No prior CBO exchange-control approval is necessary, but both the sending and receiving banks will issue a Foreign Inward Remittance Advice that must be retained with the MT103 terminal confirmation, the purpose-code statement and the FX conversion ticket for CBO, auditor and Oman Tax Authority inspection.Outbound treasury disbursements will be executed primarily through: (1) SWIFT MT103 international wires for all contractual payments (cloud infrastructure, content-moderation services, marketing agencies, bandwidth and CDN fees, intercompany loans, dividends, and royalty streams) carrying precise BOP purpose codes, with Swift GPI tracking for high-value same-day confirmation; (2) UCP600-compliant commercial and standby letters of credit issued by the Omani relationship bank for any physical procurement of hardware or equipment, with the LC advised through the beneficiary's bank and documentary compliance handled under standard UCP rules; (3) CBO-operated local clearing and RTGS mechanisms for OMR-denominated payments to Oman-resident vendors, payroll, taxes and utilities; and (4) treasury-sweep structures that consolidate daily Apple IAP and Paymentwall settlement batches arriving in USD/EUR, reconcile them against revenue reports, and rebalance OMR, USD and EUR working-capital ledgers to optimise net position and hedge costs. Paymentwall's payout rails may credit USD or EUR into the SPC's foreign-currency account before conversion; Apple settlement is received on a periodic net-settlement schedule from Apple's treasury entities; the SPC treasurer must maintain a two-signatory authorisation matrix and end-of-day liquidity report to avoid unplanned overdrafts.Deliverable OMR/USD FX forward contracts (tenors up to 12 months) offered by CBO-licensed commercial banks; although the OMR/USD peg at 1 USD = 0.3845 OMR lowers structural risk, forwards fix the exact bank margin and OMR amount for scheduled dividend and tax payments.Cross-currency FX forwards and vanilla swaps between USD, EUR and GBP to hedge app-store proceeds and European vendor invoices when net currency exposure exceeds the treasury tolerance band.USD-denominated time deposits and multi-currency operating ledgers serving as a natural liquidity hedge, matching foreign-currency assets against forecast foreign-currency liabilities.OTC currency options and longer-dated cross-currency swaps (above 12 months) for planned capex or acquisition flows, subject to the bank's derivatives eligibility, the corporate treasury policy and the SPC's internal board delegation limits.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on OMR are not locally permissible because the OMR is deliverable against USD under the fixed peg; if residual offshore NDF protection is commercially requested, it may only be executed with non-resident professional counterparties and is discouraged by the CBO; the SPC's treasury policy assumes no NDF reliance.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Medium feasibility. Offshore digital EMI and banking-as-a-service platforms (Stripe, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Payoneer, Revolut Business and similar) are not authorised by the CBO to open OMR-denominated accounts, hold client deposits in Oman, act as a local settlement agent, or function as the SPC's primary treasury relationship bank. Such platforms may be used only as auxiliary collection and disbursement rails for foreign end-user microtransactions, provided all ultimate treasury flows are routed through a CBO-licensed commercial bank or a locally licensed Payment Service Provider under the CBO's payment-system regulatory framework. All statutory corporate matters — registered capital verification, tax and Zakat? (no zakat applies to commercial companies) — tax payments, statutory audit confirmation letters, FX conversion, dividends and capital repatriation — must pass exclusively through the licensed commercial banking channel. A local PSP licence may be considered later if the SPC wishes to accept Omani card transactions directly; meanwhile, Apple IAP and Paymentwall remain the compliant external monetisation rails, with settlement into the Omani bank account via SWIFT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and a transfer pricing disclosure form are mandatory where aggregate related-party transactions in a tax year exceed OMR 1,000,000; a Master File is required for members of multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding OMR 100,000,000. Transactions below the threshold must still comply with the arm's-length principle and be documented upon request.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations code comparable to the UAE regime. However, the Oman Income Tax Law and Tax Procedures Law require genuine local substance for tax residence, treaty access, and anti-avoidance purposes. A 100% foreign-owned SPC operating a video-match app must evidence premises, local management, employees, and substantive decision-making in Oman; otherwise the Oman Tax Authority may recharacterise the SPC as a sham, attribute the digital activity to a permanent establishment of the foreign parent, or disregard the corporate form.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to 100% of the tax evaded, additional delay surcharges, criminal prosecution, confiscation of assets, and imprisonment of up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties if the company is dissolved without settlement; courts may issue travel bans, freezing orders, asset attachment, and custodial sentences against culpable directors
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Upward adjustment of taxable income, denial of deductions, imposition of additional CIT at 3%/15%, late-payment interest at monthly default rates, and TP-specific penalties under the Oman Tax Procedures Law

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for additional tax and interest where the pricing arrangement is found to be a sham or intended to strip profits out of Oman; personal travel restrictions and asset attachment can follow
Shell Company and Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the SPC's separate existence, assessment of the foreign parent on an Omani permanent establishment basis, cancellation of tax registration, and deregistration of the Commercial Registration by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion

Director personal liability: Nominee and executive directors face civil restitution for concealed foreign profits, administrative disqualification, travel bans, and potential criminal liability for issuing false declarations of substance
AML and Sanctions Breach in Digital Payment Flows

Statutory sanction: Penalties under Oman's Anti-Money Laundering Law (Royal Decree 30/2016 as amended) including fines up to OMR 100,000, freezing of assets, suspension or revocation of the Commercial Registration, and imprisonment of up to 7 years

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal criminal responsibility for AML failures; the court may impose travel bans, confiscation of personal assets, disqualification from corporate directorships, and custodial sentences in cases of wilful blindness or complicity
