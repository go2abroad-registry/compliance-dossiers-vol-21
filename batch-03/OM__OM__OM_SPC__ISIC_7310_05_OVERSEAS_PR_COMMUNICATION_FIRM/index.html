Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) – Companies Registration Department; electronically through the 'Invest in Oman' / 'Estilaam' or MoCIIP e-Services portal.

Governing statute

Oman Commercial Companies Law (Royal Decree No. 18/2019, as amended), Articles 4 (forms of company), 9 (SPC incorporation), 13 (minimum capital), 15 (registration), 108-114 (single person company provisions); Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree No. 50/2019).

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association for a Single Person Company (SPC), filed with the Company Registration Certificate Application Form (Form No. 1 under the Executive Regulations).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SPC unless the company's activity is regulated by a sectoral authority; capital may be fixed by shareholder resolution but must be fully paid and deposited in a designated Oman bank account, evidenced by a bank certificate (capital verification letter) if the shareholder elects to demonstrate solvency. No escrow account required as a general rule; if applicable under sector-specific regulations, the escrow or paid-up capital deposit must remain blocked until official registration is completed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and civil defense certification: Civil Defense and Ambulance Authority (CDAA) – General Directorate of Civil Defense 'Fire Safety Certificate' for the office premises, requiring a fire safety layout plan, portable fire extinguishers, emergency exits, smoke detectors, and alarm systems conforming to Omani fire code NFPA standards. Structural load inspection: not required for fit-out only, but the landlord must confirm structural load capacity if placing heavy equipment. Occupational clearance: the Ministry of Labour (formerly played under Ministry of Manpower) requires workshop/office premises to meet minimum workplace standards – adequate ventilation, lighting, sanitation, first aid kit, and a valid labor clearance for foreign employees through the electronic 'Musaned' or similar system if staff housing is provided. A signed 'workplace health and safety declaration' should be retained in the personnel file along with CDAA certification.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SPC unless the company's activity is regulated by a sectoral authority; capital may be fixed by shareholder resolution but must be fully paid and deposited in a designated Oman bank account, evidenced by a bank certificate (capital verification letter) if the shareholder elects to demonstrate solvency. No escrow account required as a general rule; if applicable under sector-specific regulations, the escrow or paid-up capital deposit must remain blocked until official registration is completed.

RESOLVED (1) that the subscriber, as sole shareholder, approves the incorporation of the Company as a Single Person Company under the Oman Commercial Companies Law and adopts the Memorandum and Articles of Association as filed; (2) that the registered office of the Company shall be situated at the address provided to MoCIIP; (3) that the subscriber, acting as the sole shareholder, subscribes for and takes the entire issued share capital of the Company; (4) that the Company's first directors and officers, namely [Name] as Chairman/Director, [Name] as Managing Director, and [Name] as Corporate Secretary, are appointed effective immediately; (5) that the Managing Director is authorized to execute key corporate banking and operational mandates; (6) that this resolution is effective as of the date of incorporation.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable – this is a non-industrial, office-based media/PR service firm; where physical premises are used, local municipality licensing (Muscat Municipality / Dhofar Municipality) and the Public Authority for Special Economic Zones and Free Zones (OPAZ) may issue a commercial premises license.

Category: Not applicable (no industrial operating license). For office premises, a 'Business Premises License' (Ijazat Masarif / Ijaza Tijari) is issued by the relevant municipality; if operating within a free zone, a 'Free Zone Trading License' from OPAZ applies.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority ('OTA'; the General Tax Authority), successor to the former Secretariat General for Taxation under the Ministry of Finance; all registrations, returns, tax-card issuance and e-payments are processed through the Tax Authority Electronic Services (TAES) portal at https://taxoman.gov.om.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned SPC in mainland Oman: (1) incorporate with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion and secure the Commercial Registration certificate plus municipal and labor clearances (no minimum capital applies); (2) file online registration in TAES attaching the CR, Arabic notarized constitutional documents, resolutions appointing the financial manager, tax representative and OTA-licensed auditor, passport or Iqama of the authorized signatory, registered-office proof and opening balance sheet; (3) obtain the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and Tax Card, which are prerequisites for VAT registration, customs clearance and banking facilities; (4) complete separate VAT registration on the TAES portal when actual or expected annual taxable supplies exceed OMR 38,000 (voluntary registration at OMR 19,000); (5) file the annual corporate income tax return with audited financial statements within four months after the tax year-end.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the SPC is a separate juridical taxpayer under Royal Decree 28/2009 (Income Tax Law); no pass-through eligibility; CIT is imposed at 15% on taxable income sourced in Oman under territorial sourcing rules; not a Tax_Exempt_Zone vehicle; foreign shareholders are not subject to dividend withholding tax on distributions from the SPC. — For a foreign-owned SPC in mainland Oman: (1) incorporate with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion and secure the Commercial Registration certificate plus municipal and labor clearances (no minimum capital applies); (2) file online registration in TAES attaching the CR, Arabic notarized constitutional documents, resolutions appointing the financial manager, tax representative and OTA-licensed auditor, passport or Iqama of the authorized signatory, registered-office proof and opening balance sheet; (3) obtain the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and Tax Card, which are prerequisites for VAT registration, customs clearance and banking facilities; (4) complete separate VAT registration on the TAES portal when actual or expected annual taxable supplies exceed OMR 38,000 (voluntary registration at OMR 19,000); (5) file the annual corporate income tax return with audited financial statements within four months after the tax year-end.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SPC is a separate juridical taxpayer under Royal Decree 28/2009 (Income Tax Law); no pass-through eligibility; CIT is imposed at 15% on taxable income sourced in Oman under territorial sourcing rules; not a Tax_Exempt_Zone vehicle; foreign shareholders are not subject to dividend withholding tax on distributions from the SPC.

Available election forms

No check-the-box, pass-through or hybrid-mismatch elections exist under Omani law; the SPC is mandatorily entity-taxed. Available statutory elections and regimes: (i) small-business income-tax exemption for annual gross income up to OMR 100,000 subject to registration and conditions; (ii) voluntary VAT registration at the OMR 19,000 threshold; (iii) OTA-approved non-calendar fiscal-year election; (iv) joint-venture income allocation to a nominated participant where the JV itself is not separately taxable; and (v) treaty-based withholding-tax relief on cross-border retainers, claimed through a Tax Residence Certificate — there is no unilateral CFC election.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and broad capital-account convertibility. The Omani Rial is pegged at 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD (1 USD = 0.3845 OMR) and is freely deliverable onshore for trade, services, dividend remittances and capital repatriation with no CBO prior-approval requirement, quota, conversion budget or foreign-exchange licence for ordinary corporate flows. A foreign-owned SPC may hold unrestricted OMR, USD, EUR and GBP accounts and remit profits free of dividend withholding tax under the Omani Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 9/2017, CIT rate 15%) provided the entity holds a valid tax clearance from the Secretariat General of Taxation, audited financial statements and the standard bank remittance documentation. Cash transactions above the CBO reporting thresholds are subject to STR/CTR surveillance, but electronic cross-border funds movement is unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation deadline or mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds or service-export receivables. A PR/communications SPC may retain EUR, USD and GBP collections indefinitely in foreign-currency operating accounts at a CBO-licensed bank. The only binding duties are (i) routing receipts through a licensed Omani dealing bank for balance-of-payments and AML/CFT reporting, and (ii) the bank's internal monitoring of export-receivable overdues beyond 180 days. No percentage of proceeds must be converted into OMR; any tax obligations, if triggered, are settled by electronic transfer rather than by forced conversion.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), the Sultanate of Oman sole monetary authority and licensing regulator for banks, exchange houses, fintech payment-service providers and authorised foreign-exchange dealers. CBO operates under the Banking Law promulgated by Royal Decree 38/2020 (replacing Royal Decree 114/2000), the Payment Systems Law (Royal Decree 18/2015) and the AML/CFT regime anchored in Royal Decree 30/2016; it manages the OMR/USD peg, reserves, domestic clearing rails and the financial-intelligence interface with the National Center for Financial Information.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility as a principal treasury rail; medium feasibility as an ancillary receivable-collection channel. The CBO requires any entity holding Omani-domiciled funds or clearing OMR to hold a CBO banking licence or an authorised payment-service-provider licence under Royal Decree 38/2020 and the CBO PSP framework. Unlicensed offshore digital EMIs and neobanks (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, multi-currency platforms) cannot lawfully provide OMR clearing or custody for an Omani SPC, and CBO does not extend extraterritorial recognition to their licences. The CBO Regulatory Sandbox exists but does not waive licensing for standard cross-border EMIs. Recommended architecture: a domestic CBO-licensed bank as the single treasury system of record for OMR and high-value FX, with a supplementary foreign EMI used solely to collect small international retainer invoices in USD/EUR/GBP and swept daily or weekly into the Omani bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Third-party funding of retainers: retainer invoices paid by the client's parent, holding entity or affiliate instead of the direct contract counterparty, with funds arriving from a third-country bank.

Root cause: Third-party funding of retainers: retainer invoices paid by the client's parent, holding entity or affiliate instead of the direct contract counterparty, with funds arriving from a third-country bank.

Defense evidence: Full contract set (master retainer agreement plus statements of work) incorporating a valid payment-assignment or pay-on-behalf clause; corporate resolutions of both the Omani SPC and the paying entity authorising the cross-payment; source-of-funds documentation (latest audited accounts, tax-residence certificate, bank reference of the payer); written declaration of the commercial rationale; and confirmation that no undisclosed overlap exists in beneficial ownership.
High-velocity outward payment pattern: near-same-day disbursement of retainer receipts to media agencies, influencers and advertising exchanges in the UAE, UK and US without a matching invoice trail, or unexplained overdraft usage inconsistent with a low-risk PR business model.

Root cause: High-velocity outward payment pattern: near-same-day disbursement of retainer receipts to media agencies, influencers and advertising exchanges in the UAE, UK and US without a matching invoice trail, or unexplained overdraft usage inconsistent with a low-risk PR business model.

Defense evidence: Twelve-month treasury cash-flow forecast; detailed media buy schedule with insertion orders; publisher invoices and agency fee breakdowns; monthly campaign reconciliation pack; evidence that every counterparty has been screened against CBO, OFAC, EU, UN and HM Treasury consolidated sanctions lists; and, where requested, an external auditor's agreed-upon-procedures report on treasury movements.
Sanctions and embargo exposure: the account selects or funds foreign media outlets, state broadcasters or advertising exchanges located in or controlled from Iran, Russia, Syria, North Korea or other comprehensively sanctioned jurisdictions, triggering a CBO AML-law red flag even when the services are legal in Oman.

Root cause: Sanctions and embargo exposure: the account selects or funds foreign media outlets, state broadcasters or advertising exchanges located in or controlled from Iran, Russia, Syria, North Korea or other comprehensively sanctioned jurisdictions, triggering a CBO AML-law red flag even when the services are legal in Oman.

Defense evidence: Automated sanctions-screening result log applying the 50% ownership rule across all applicable lists; contractual warranty clauses requiring every vendor and sub-vendor to certify non-dealing with sanctioned persons; a sanctions-compliance policy signed by the compliance officer; SPC undertaking letter to the bank; and a documented 48-hour escalation protocol to the relationship manager and the bank's Money Laundering Reporting Officer.
Beneficial-ownership opacity: the sole shareholder is a low-disclosure foreign holding company (for example a BVI, Seychelles or certain UAE free-zone entity) with layered ownership and no substantive staff or audited operations.

Root cause: Beneficial-ownership opacity: the sole shareholder is a low-disclosure foreign holding company (for example a BVI, Seychelles or certain UAE free-zone entity) with layered ownership and no substantive staff or audited operations.

Defense evidence: Notarised Beneficial-Owner registry extract filed with MoCIIP and the CBO-accessible BO register; unbroken organisational chart down to the final natural persons holding 10% or more; apostilled certificates of incumbency and registry extracts; passport copies and proof of residential address for all UBOs; signed undertaking to notify the bank of any UBO change within 15 days; and disclosure of any nominee arrangements.
