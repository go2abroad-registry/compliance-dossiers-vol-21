Governing statute

Corporate: Royal Decree No. 18/2019 (Companies Law), Single Person Company chapter at Articles 240 et seq.; Commercial Register Law, Royal Decree No. 3/1974; Foreign Capital Investment Law, Royal Decree No. 50/2019 (100% non-Omani ownership permitted); implementing ministerial decisions of the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP). Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum paid-up capital of OMR 5,000 (approximately USD 13,000) is required for an Oman Single Person Company under Royal Decree No. 18/2019 and its implementing ministerial decisions. The full capital must be evidenced by a bank certificate from a licensed Omani bank before commercial registration is completed; no separate notarized escrow release structure is required. Because the licensed scope is payment services, the Central Bank of Oman imposes a higher regulatory capital floor for PSP license classes (commonly OMR 150,000 to OMR 500,000 depending on license category), which must be maintained as unencumbered locked deposits with a licensed local bank for the duration of the license.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the Civil Defence and Ambulance Authority (CDAA) after on-site inspection of fire alarms, portable extinguishers, emergency exits, and gas-suppression systems covering network/server cabinets; structural loading and electrical safety certification from a licensed civil and electrical engineer; occupational safety and health compliance audit under the Labour Law (Royal Decree No. 35/2003) covering first aid, CCTV, access control, ESD flooring, and emergency lighting; final municipality signboard permit attached to the operating license.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) — central e-Services Portal: https://taxoman.gov.om (integrated taxpayer register and VAT/e-invoice portal)

E-government filing portal

Foreign shareholders must first incorporate the SPC with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) through the Invest Easy portal to obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) number. The OTA then issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon online income-tax registration of the SPC within 30 days of commencement of activity. VAT registration is completed through the same OTA e-Services portal: mandatory when annual taxable supplies exceed OMR 500,000; voluntary at OMR 100,000 of supplies or expenses. A non-resident PSP with no Omani fixed establishment but making taxable supplies to non-taxable persons in Oman must register for VAT through an OTA-recognized tax representative regardless of turnover.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the foreign-owned SPC is a resident juridical person subject to Oman corporate income tax at entity level; no transparent or pass-through treatment is available; resident SPCs are generally taxable on worldwide income, while non-resident entities are taxed on Oman-source income. — Foreign shareholders must first incorporate the SPC with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) through the Invest Easy portal to obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the foreign-owned SPC is a resident juridical person subject to Oman corporate income tax at entity level; no transparent or pass-through treatment is available; resident SPCs are generally taxable on worldwide income, while non-resident entities are taxed on Oman-source income.

Available election forms

No check-the-box, tax-transparent, group consolidation, or hybrid mismatch election exists under Oman law; the SPC cannot elect pass-through treatment. The only available statutory election relevant to indirect taxation is voluntary VAT registration below the OMR 500,000 mandatory threshold when the OMR 100,000 supplies/expenses condition is met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 500,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month look-back period (mandatory registration); OMR 100,000 of taxable supplies or expenses (voluntary registration); no threshold for non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies to non-taxable persons in Oman. Registration must be completed within 30 days after the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (i) goods physically exported outside Oman and outside the GCC implementing states; and (ii) exported services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not present in Oman and has no fixed establishment in Oman, provided the place of supply is outside Oman or the service is directly connected to exported goods. For a cross-border payment gateway, fee-based acquiring, clearing, settlement, and card-processing services supplied to foreign merchants or foreign card-issuing institutions qualify as export services if the benefit is entirely outside Oman and the consideration is received in convertible foreign currency. Services rendered to Omani VAT-registered customers remain domestic taxable supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Bayan / OTA-Customs linked export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill where airfreight is used) showing movement out of Oman
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a licensed Omani bank evidencing export consideration receipt

Oman Tax Authority has phased in the national 'Mithaq' e-invoicing and continuous transaction control regime requiring all VAT-registered businesses to issue structured electronic invoices and credit notes through OTA-certified systems. Invoice records must be submitted to the OTA clearing platform in near-real-time via a machine-to-machine API using authenticated JSON payloads with qualified electronic signatures. Every tax invoice must pass OTA validation and receive a unique digital stamp/UUID, and the invoice must display a scannable QR code. PSPs must preserve ledger-level transaction data, fee/tax-representative details, and zero-rating evidence in the mandatory e-archive for audit access.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims are treated as tax evasion. The OTA may retrospectively assess the under-declared 5% VAT, disallow input VAT recovery, and claw back refunds already paid. The standard discovery window is 5 years, extended to 10 years where intentional evasion or abuse is established. Late-payment penalties accrue on the assessed tax, and administrative fines are imposed per contravention under the OTA penalty schedule. Criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment and/or substantial monetary fines, and the PSP may be suspended from export zero-rating treatment and removed from the trusted exporter register. Documentary evidence (including FX receipt confirmation) must be retained for at least 10 years to support future audits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman operates a credible open capital and current account convertibility regime, reinforced by the OMR/USD fixed exchange rate peg ($1.00 = OMR 0.3845). Current account payments for goods and services are freely convertible via licensed banks without prior CBO approval. Profit repatriation and capital outflows are likewise permitted for licensed foreign-owned entities, subject only to documentary substance, tax clearance, and the standard balance-of-payments reporting to the CBO. The Omani rial is fully convertible for current account transactions, and capital transaction convertibility is available for investment-related flows provided the underlying SPC is licensed and compliant with the CBO's rules and the Commercial Companies Law, which permits 100% foreign ownership. There is no multiple-currency practice or discriminatory exchange rate treatment. Transactions with sanctioned or prohibited jurisdictions, however, are not permitted and will be blocked pursuant to CBO circulars and the National Sanctions framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender obligation or conversion ratio exists for export proceeds in Oman. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign currency or convert to OMR as commercially appropriate. For balance-of-payments data and statistical compliance, the CBO requires that all export invoices and customs documents be routed and reported through a licensed commercial bank. In practice, export receipts must be repatriated into the exporter's Omani bank account within the bank's defined trade cycle (typically 3 to 6 months from the bill of lading date). The statutory conversion ratio is 0% mandatory surrender; where a bank has applied a specific timeline due to image reporting, any overdues must be explained and supported by shipping or collection evidence.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) – acting as the national monetary authority, banking and payments system regulator, and the competent authority for foreign exchange administration and capital flow reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to selective viability. An offshore digital EMI is not acceptable as the primary regulated settlement bank or clearing rail for an Oman-licensed PSP engaged in merchant acquiring and payment gateway activities. CBO's Legal Framework for Payment Services and national electronic payment systems rules require any entity that issues e-money or provides payment services to Omani residents to secure a CBO approval or a PSP license. An offshore EMI may nominally act as a technical service provider, but such an arrangement does not waive the requirement for the SPC to hold a local corporate bank account and settle through a CBO-regulated commercial bank. The practical rating is 'Low' for dependence on an offshore EMI for OMR clearing; it is 'conditional and medium' only when the EMI is used as an internet payment facilitator with an Omani-licensed acquiring bank as the mandated entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital funding must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for inter-bank transfers to the SPC's capital escrow account at an Oman-licensed bank. The market-standard purpose code for direct investment injection is 'G300' under the CBO's BPM6 classification, and the originator bank must use a valid IBAN (OM48 fully populated) to avoid rejection. The incoming payment must be accompanied by a written capital contribution letter, the SPC's Unified Economic Register ID and the Committee's investment approval (if applicable), and a copy of the shareholders' resolution. The bank will verify the remitter's identity and source of funds, and any inbound credit must pass the sanctions screening and CBO's post-settlement reporting before the funds are available for cross-border investing or general operational use.Outbound trade settlement to global merchants and service providers is executed via the bank's SWIFT infrastructure using MT103 customer credit transfers for non-documentary transactions. For commercial trade-based flows, documentary letters of credit (LC) issued in OMR or USD (UCP 600) are respected and cleared through the corresponding confirmed bank network. For operational OMR liquidity at the local level, the bank uses the Electronic Clearing System of the Omani Pay, the national automated clearing house, for low-value paperless payments and direct credit transfers. Where a settlement requires high-speed clearing, the bank may use correspondent U.S. banks utilising Fedwire and CHIPS as the final leg of the payment to and from U.S.-domiciled gateway processors.Outright OMR/USD foreign-exchange forwards (deliverable onshore up to 12 months subject to CBO permitted booking)Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for regional currencies (e.g., INR, PKR, AED) where an onshore deliverable forward is not operableCross-currency swaps (principal and interest exchange in OMR against USD or other fully convertible currencies) executed through a licensed Omani bank under CBO risk-recognition guidelines

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding under the Unified Tax Law (Royal Decree 8/2024) on dividend distributions by an Omani SPC to non-resident shareholders. The withholding exemption applies where the recipient is a foreign company or governmental body holding at least 5% of the share capital, and dividends distributed under the previous Income Tax Law regime were not subject to Oman dividend withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File is triggered where annual cross-border related party transactions exceed OMR 500,000; Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds OMR 10 million, per OTA Transfer Pricing Guidelines and Decision 23/2021.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR), permanent establishment attribution principles, and beneficial ownership tests under the Unified Tax Law require the SPC to maintain genuine economic substance. A cross-border payment gateway is expected to hold the Central Bank of Oman licence, maintain local management, employ operational staff, host board meetings in Oman, and keep core payment-settlement and treasury functions onshore; deficient substance invites recharacterisation of income and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of up to 200% of the evaded tax, plus monthly surcharges; under the Unified Tax Law, criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment of up to 10 years, forfeiture of tax benefits, suspension of the SPC's CR, and revocation of the payment service licence.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors, and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax; the OTA may impose a travel ban, freeze personal bank accounts, and refer the case to the public prosecutor leading to civil restitution and imprisonment.
Shell Company and Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The OTA may disregard the corporate vehicle, recharacterise intercompany service fees as taxable dividends, deny treaty relief, and impose penalties; the Commercial Registration may be struck off and the Central Bank of Oman licence revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders face personal civil restitution for unpaid tax, disqualification from serving as directors for up to 5 years, travel restrictions, and criminal liability if the sham arrangement was knowingly used to evade tax.
Unauthorised PSP Operations and AML Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Oman AML Law provides for imprisonment of up to 5 years, fines of up to OMR 100,000, confiscation of criminally derived proceeds, and administrative penalties by the Central Bank of Oman of up to OMR 50,000 per breach; clearing and settlement privileges are suspended pending remediation.

Director personal liability: Senior management, compliance officers, and directors are individually criminally liable; the court may impose personal fines, bar individuals from board service, seize personal assets, and restrict travel while investigations are pending.
Unpaid Withholding Tax on Cross-Border Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The unpaid withholding tax becomes fully recoverable from the SPC, together with a 1% monthly surcharge capped at 25% of the tax due; the OTA will refuse tax clearance certificates and the SPC may lose its payment institution licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders act as joint guarantors of corporate tax obligations; the OTA may obtain a travel ban and enforce execution against personal assets where corporate assets are insufficient.
