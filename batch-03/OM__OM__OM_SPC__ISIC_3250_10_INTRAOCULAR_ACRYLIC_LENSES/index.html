Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to Single Person Companies under RD 18/2019; issued capital is fixed in the Memorandum of Association and must be fully subscribed by the sole shareholder, with non-resident ownership permitted at 100%. No statutory escrow account requirement exists; cash and in-kind contributions must be supported by bank evidence or a certified valuation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and Life Safety Certificate from the Royal Oman Police - Civil Defense and Ambulance Department (CDAA) following inspection of the cleanroom, HVAC, and extraction systems; structural load inspection certificate by a licensed structural engineer for CNC lathes, autoclaves, and surface-finishing equipment; occupational safety and health clearance from the Ministry of Labor under Ministerial Decision No. 286/2008 covering PPE programs, emergency evacuation plans, hazard communication, and industrial hygiene monitoring; final building occupation permit from the responsible Baladiyah.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), Ministry of Finance, Sultanate of Oman; electronic portal 'Taxman' at https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC), the Oman Tax Authority requires registration under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 9/2017) and the VAT Law (Royal Decree 121/2015). Procedure: (1) Obtain Commercial Registration from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion; (2) within three months of Commercial Registration, submit the CR certificate, memorandum/articles of association, owner's passport and residency documentation, and local address to OTA via the Taxman portal; (3) receive the Omani Tax Card and, separately, a VAT registration number where the entity's taxable turnover exceeds OMR 38,500 in the preceding 12 months or is expected to exceed that threshold in the next 12 months; (4) if not yet meeting the threshold, voluntary VAT registration may be applied for with an OMR 19,250 threshold; (5) non-resident foreign entities making taxable supplies in Oman must register for VAT without any threshold and may be required to appoint a tax representative resident in Oman.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (a single-person company is a separate legal taxable person, subject to Oman Corporate Income Tax on its worldwide taxable profits; no look-through or pass-through treatment is available; Oman operates a territorial-source system for non-resident entities, while resident Omani companies are taxed on global income subject to foreign tax credit relief conventions) — For a foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC), the Oman Tax Authority requires registration under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 9/2017) and the VAT Law (Royal Decree 121/2015). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a single-person company is a separate legal taxable person, subject to Oman Corporate Income Tax on its worldwide taxable profits; no look-through or pass-through treatment is available; Oman operates a territorial-source system for non-resident entities, while resident Omani companies are taxed on global income subject to foreign tax credit relief conventions)

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch elections are available under Omani law; entity classification follows the Commercial Companies Law (Royal Decree 18/2019). Statutory election mechanisms include: (a) VAT group registration election for related resident persons under Article 18 of the VAT Law; (b) election to apply the small and medium enterprise preferential tax threshold/rate where applicable under the Income Tax Law; (c) election by a non-resident entity to register a VAT agent or representative; (d) election to withhold/account for VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism in specified intra-GCC and import scenarios.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 38,500 (mandatory registration limit) / OMR 19,250 (voluntary registration threshold) based on annual taxable supplies or reverse-charge imports in a 12-month look-back/forward period; non-resident persons have no registration threshold and must register for every taxable supply made in Oman

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% VAT rate applies to exports of goods where the goods are physically removed from Oman to a destination outside the implementing GCC VAT states within the time frame permitted by law (generally within 90 days of supply), provided the supplier retains the official customs export declaration and transport documents; the goods must not be subject to further processing or use in Oman before export. For services, zero-rating applies when the beneficiary is a non-resident person not resident in Oman, the recipient receives the service as a business outside Oman, and the actual use/benefit of the service occurs entirely outside Oman; for intraocular acrylic lens manufacturing, exported medical devices to foreign distributors or hospitals fall within the services-goods export zero-rating category when title and risk pass to the non-Omani buyer and documentary proof is retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (sealed by Royal Oman Police / Customs)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill for air freight)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank SWIFT credit confirmation showing the export proceeds were remitted into an Omani bank account)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin
  • Stock/Inventory Movement Record showing removal from Oman within the statutory period

Oman currently does not apply a mandatory continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance system with real-time digital fiscal stamping or API pre-clearance; however, all registered taxpayers must keep electronic issuance records of tax invoices and submit VAT returns via the Taxman e-portal. The Oman Tax Authority has publicly announced a phased implementation of a national e-invoicing platform (electronic fiscal recording/clearance) for resident registrants; targeted industries and large taxpayers are expected to enter the mandatory phase through published transitional orders. At present, valid VAT invoices must contain the OTA-issued VAT registration number, supplier and customer details, description and value, VAT rate, and VAT amount; export invoices require the customs export declaration reference.

The Oman Tax Authority may verify, audit, and reject any zero-rating claim that is not supported by complete customs, transport, and remittance evidence. If the Authority determines that the export conditions were not satisfied or the documents were false, it will assess VAT at the standard 5% rate on the full supply consideration, disallow the corresponding input tax credit, and claw back any refunded VAT. Administrative penalties include a 15% fixed penalty for incorrect declarations or failure to meet documentation requirements, an additional penalty of 1% per month on unpaid VAT from the due date until full settlement, and, for deliberate evasion or forged documents, fines of up to OMR 50,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years under the Oman VAT Law and its Executive Regulations. The Authority also imposes 'bad credit' restrictions on the autonomous customs declaration system, blocking future export declarations until all disputed amounts are settled.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current account transactions under the IMF Article VIII obligations; Oman maintains a soft peg to the US dollar at an official fixed rate of 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment inflows, are generally liberalized for 100% foreign-owned entities, but profit remittance and liquidation proceeds require underlying audited financial statements, tax clearance from the Oman Tax Authority, and a valid Central Bank of Oman (CBO) licensed bank attestation. The CBO imposes no general surrender requirement on capital account inflows; however, current account receipts such as export proceeds are fully surrenderable to the domestic banking system under the Foreign Exchange Law and CBO Circular No. 29 of 1992, as amended, and must be routed through a licensed Omani bank. Practical hurdles include marginal transaction-level documentation for settlement of AED/USD against INR or CNY clearing, and enhanced scrutiny of medical-device sector payments due to the dual-use and licensed product nature; advance import payments for ophthalmology machinery require a proforma invoice and registered purchase order.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBO regulations and the Omani Exchange Circular 31/2011, exporters must repatriate and surrender foreign exchange receipts into a licensed Omani commercial bank within 180 calendar days from the date of shipment or export proceeds realization, whichever is earlier. The statutory mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts is effectively 100% in respect of proceeds denominated in foreign currency, converted into OMR at the applicable market rate, unless a specific exemption is obtained from the CBO for holding a foreign-currency working capital account. For intraocular lens exports invoiced under DDP or CIF terms to Gulf and Indian Ocean buyers, the surrender computation is based on the full invoice value net of expressly permitted deductible expenses (insurance, freight, and third-party commission up to 5%). The exporter must submit the Export Form (customs declaration) and the CBO T-2 category reporting through the bank within 7 working days of receipt.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for non-resident/licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs). The CBO does not maintain a fintech EMI licensing framework for non-resident institutions; only locally licensed payment service providers under the Oman E-Money Regulation (CBO Circular BM 1191/2019) may offer multi-currency wallet or cross-border payment services. Offshore digital EMIs cannot acquire a direct clearing membership in the Oman Real-Time Gross Settlement (OMRGT) system or access the Export Proceeds Surrender regime; their use is limited to ancillary collections, subject to maximum transaction size and ultimate settlement through a licensed Omani bank. Physical presence and formal correspondent banking relations are mandatory for medical-device export proceeds; use of unregulated offshore EMI platforms is likely to be blocked by Omani clearing banks as a correspondent due-diligence red flag.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections for the 100% foreign-owned SPC must be remitted through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 as direct FX conversion into Omani Rial, routed only to the licensed commercial bank holding the Capital Escrow Account. The payment must cite economic purpose code 'P014' (equity capital for direct investment) under the CBO/BMA statistical reporting taxonomy; supporting documentation for credit must include: notarized shareholders' resolution, CBO Foreign Investment Form, commercial registration certificate of the SPC, MOCI investment approval (where applicable), and a letter from the legal counsel confirming the transaction is not subject to anti-money-laundering reporting. Incoming capital should remain in the escrow account until the original revocable/irrevocable credit instruments and the underlying purchase order for the high-precision lathe and foldable hydrophobic lens production lines are submitted; the bank will require a declaration that the first tranche is not for treasury arbitrage or unreported trade debt. For construction and equipment-equity injection, the Omani bank will charge a nominal 0.05% to 0.15% conversion spread, and the account holder must provide timely tax clearance if the injection is made subsequent to the issuance of tax registration number.The primary clearing rails for outbound trade disbursements are 1) SWIFT MT103/MT103 (personal and commercial remittance) for direct transfers to suppliers and foreign distributors; 2) SWIFT MT700/MT710 and MT202 for back-to-back trade settlements through a confirmed commercial Letter of Credit; 3) settlement via the Omani Real-Time Gross Settlement (OMRGT) for domestic OMR legs and the UAE dirham clearing for regional shipments is only permitted through correspondent bank relationships of the Omani bank. The outbound disbursement package must include a signed invoice, packing list, customs export declaration from the Royal Oman Police Customs (ROP), an end-user/intended-use certificate for intraocular lens shipments under HS 9001.30.00, and a quality certificate for the foldable hydrophobic acrylic materials. For payments to suppliers in China, the Omani correspondent bank may reject MT103 references to 'P2P' or 'cryptocurrency' denominations, so every payment narrative must exclusively contain the contract number, invoice reference, HS code, and delivery terms; onshore SWIFT-fee absorption should be minimized and transaction purpose code 'S102' (payment for exports) should be used.Fixed-dated forward contracts with licensed Omani commercial banks or the CBO-approved dealer, subject to underlying commercial exposure (non-financial transactions only)Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) where the OMR is the settlement currency for hedging against USD and INR receivablesForeign exchange currency swaps between OMR and USD for short-term liquidity and working capital managementFX options purchased (not sold) for hedging export proceeds from the Iraqi/USD denominated distribution contractsCross-currency swaps with a licensed international bank operating within the Central Bank of Oman's regulatory perimeter for long-term capital expenditure financingInterest-free or cost-of-carry amortizing swaps referencing a fixed USD/OMR parity to minimize convertibility risk on multi-year commercial contracts

Annual corporate income tax (CIT) return with audited financial statements filed on the OTA e-filing portal Within four months of the tax year end (by 30 April for a 31 December year end); same deadline applies to loss-making filings $0 USD OMR 100 fixed penalty for late submission plus 1% monthly surcharge on unpaid CIT; persistent non-filing over three tax periods triggers enforced best-judgment assessment and criminal enforcement.
Quarterly withholding tax (WHT) declarations and remittances for cross-border royalty, management fee, interest and technical service payments Within 30 days of each calendar quarter end; final quarter return due by 30 January of the following year $0 USD OMR 100 per late return plus 1% monthly interest on unremitted WHT; payer remains liable for the full 10% WHT on non-resident payments and may lose tax deductibility.
Annual confirmation filing of sole-shareholder assembly resolution approving financial statements and declaration of continued beneficial ownership structure Within one month of the sole-shareholder resolution approving the annual financial statements and unconditionally before 30 June of the succeeding calendar year $260 USD OMR 200 late-recording penalty; unresolved non-filing is recorded as a corporate non-compliance flag and can block CR renewal, banking facility renewals and future import activities.
