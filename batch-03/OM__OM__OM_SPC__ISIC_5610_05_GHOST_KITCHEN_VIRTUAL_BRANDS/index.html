Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) – Sultanate of Oman; e-Services Tax Portal: taxoman.om

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Omani Single Person Company obtains its Tax Registration Number (TRN) by submitting an electronic application through the Oman Tax Authority e-Services Portal (taxoman.om) with the certified Commercial Registration certificate, constitutional documents of the SPC, power of attorney of the registered legal representative, certified passport copies of all owners/directors, and the required authorization. OTA issues the TRN and tax card after verification. VAT registration must be applied for separately via the same portal if the mandatory registration threshold is met. A foreign entity without a local fixed establishment is not generally registrable for Omani TIN unless deriving Oman-source taxable income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election or partnership pass-through election is available under Omani tax law. An SPC is mandatorily treated as an independent taxable juridical person liable to Omani corporate income tax. There is no tax group consolidation / fiscal unity and no hybrid mismatch election mechanism under current Omani tax legislation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 38,500 annual taxable supplies or imports in the previous 12 months; Voluntary registration: OMR 19,250 annual taxable supplies or expenses. Registration is also mandatory where there is an intention to conduct economic activity with annual taxable turnover exceeding the compulsory threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported to destinations outside the GCC are zero-rated for Omani VAT provided the goods leave Oman within 90 days of the time of supply and the supplier retains proof of export approved by Royal Oman Police Customs. Services supplied to a non-resident customer are zero-rated where the services are effectively used and enjoyed outside Oman and are not connected to immovable property in Oman. Transportation services in international traffic, directly related freight and logistics services, and supplies to free-zone exporters also qualify under executive regulations.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan e-declaration accepted by Royal Oman Police Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export outside Oman/GCC
  • Commercial invoice in the name of the overseas buyer with clear terms of delivery
  • Packing list and transport document showing outgoing movement from Oman
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit statement proving receipt of foreign-currency consideration
  • Signed export-claim declaration / supplementary VAT export certificate (per OTA guidance)

Oman does not currently require real-time fiscal clearance of invoices. VAT-registered businesses must issue VAT tax invoices in Arabic and English containing the OTA-mandated particulars. The Oman Tax Authority is gradually implementing an electronic invoicing framework; once fully phased in, B2B/B2G invoice clearance via OTA APIs and digital stamping will become mandatory. Taxpayers should monitor OTA technical specifications for e-invoicing and ensure ERP readiness for future fiscal API integration.

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating claims trigger retrospective VAT assessment at 5%, late payment interest, and administrative penalties under the Oman VAT Law. Tax evasion penalties may reach 100% of the tax evaded, and criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment for up to 3 years. The OTA has a statutory audit lookback of 5 years; suppliers may lose their zero-rating benefit, be disqualified from future VAT refunds, and have their VAT registration revoked for falsified export evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction, meaning the Omani Rial is freely usable for current international payments without exchange restrictions. The Rial is pegged by the Central Bank of Oman to the US dollar, supporting de facto full convertibility for profit remittances, trade settlements, and repatriation of paid-up capital. There is no legal distinction between current-account and capital-account convertibility quotas; however, equity injections must be routed through licensed banks with appropriate Board resolutions and Foreign Capital Investment evidence. Outward remittance of dividends requires a tax clearance certificate from the Oman Tax Authority, audited financial statements, and confirmation that all associated taxes and social insurance obligations have been settled.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose a statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement or a mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be credited to a foreign-currency account with a licensed Omani bank and used freely for trade-related disbursements or converted to OMR at the prevailing market rate if operational liquidity is required. The CBO and banks nevertheless require complete export documentation (commercial invoice, bill of lading, customs export declaration, and VAT zero-rating evidence) and expect proceeds to be received and reconciled within 360 days to satisfy anti-money-laundering and trade-based money-laundering controls. Contractual or financing arrangements may create a conversion obligation, in which case conversion must occur through the same authorised dealer and be reported under the applicable CBO statistical mandate.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), acting as the monetary authority and exchange-control supervisor; day-to-day FX licensing and reporting are administered through authorised dealers and licensed commercial banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary banking rail. Oman does not issue electronic-money-institution (EMI) licences comparable to the EU or UK regimes, and CBO-regulated banks are the only legally recognised deposit-taking and FX clearing counterparties for OMR-denominated activity. Offshore digital EMIs and virtual-account providers may be used only as ancillary collection or disbursement vehicles provided that no OMR cash balance is held outside an Omani authorised bank. The CBO expects a resident bank account for tax payments, payroll, and supplier settlements; relying solely on an offshore EMI is a regulatory and reputational red flag and should be avoided.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inward remittance uses SWIFT MT103 from the registered non-resident shareholder to a CBO-licensed bank, credited initially to a Temporary Capital Suspense Account and released to the operating account only after source-of-funds verification and OCR/trade-license validation. The wire must identify the sender as the legal shareholder, use a capital-investment purpose code such as 'Direct Investment in Oman - Equity Capital' or 'K1.1.0', and be accompanied by the board capital-call resolution, share allotment register, Ministry of Commerce endorsement where applicable, and the bank's onboarding letters. Transfers from unrelated third parties, crypto custodians, or mixed-purpose accounts will be rejected. The funds must be broken-down in the bank's inward remittance statement and reflected in the share capital within the statutory incorporation window. Funds should not be artificially split into amounts below OMR equivalent USD 10,000, as structuring alerts will trigger Savings Bank fraud suspicion and enhanced due diligence.Domestic disbursements for payroll, rent, and local suppliers are cleared through CBO-supervised electronic payment systems, principally the Oman Electronic Payment Network (OEPN) and SWIFT-compatible local interbank rails. Cross-border trade settlements are executed through SWIFT MT103 single-payment messages or MT202 covers for correspondent bank applications; documentary import settlements use irrevocable, confirmed commercial letters of credit raised in SWIFT MT700 format under UCP 600 and ISBP 745. For payouts to DoorDash and UberEats, the company should use platform-preferred clearing rails: local MarketPlace payout collection via card/Visa Direct, Payoneer, or a dedicated CBO-licensed bank settlement account, while supplier invoices for kitchen supplies, packaging, and food ingredients are paid by MT103 with quotation references in the narrative. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% for taxable income exceeding OMR 100,000; 3% on the first OMR 100,000 of taxable income (Royal Decree 53/2023)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividend and profit remittances paid by the Omani SPC to its non-resident shareholder under the withholding framework of Royal Decree 53/2023; no withholding applies where the distribution is made to a tax-resident Omani shareholder and the distributed profits have already been subject to entity-level taxation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 1,000,000 in aggregate related-party transactions per tax year triggers mandatory Local File and Master File documentation, together with the annual transfer pricing disclosure schedule attached to Form CIT-100; the Oman Tax Authority may require transfer pricing documentation below this threshold on a risk-based audit or when the taxpayer has significant cross-border payments to low-tax jurisdictions.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute comparable with the UAE, but the general anti-abuse, substance-over-form and treaty entitlement provisions of Royal Decree 53/2023, together with the Commercial Companies Law and beneficial ownership registration rules, require the SPC to demonstrate real commercial activity, qualified local staff, physical commercial kitchen premises, board minutes, local procurement and dispatch decision-making; a shell SPC used merely to route virtual brand profits is re-characterized as a sham arrangement and the income is taxed directly in the hands of the foreign owner through a deemed permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Under-Declaration of Platform Revenue

Statutory sanction: Additional tax equal to the underpaid amount, an administrative fine of up to OMR 100,000, daily delay surcharges, suspension of the tax clearance certificate, seizure of bank accounts and commercial assets for collection, and criminal prosecution of the responsible natural persons.

Director personal liability: The local manager and the foreign beneficial owner who knowingly sign false returns become jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax; Omani courts may issue travel bans, freeze personal assets, order civil restitution to the state, and impose imprisonment of up to five years.
Shell-Company Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the corporate vehicle, re-characterization of income as attributable to the foreign owner's permanent establishment, denial of the 3% band with the full 15% rate applied to the entire taxable income, and reopening of the previous five tax years with corrective assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors forfeit limited-liability protection for tax debts; personal restitution orders, director disqualification, travel bans and asset freezing are applied where artificial management fees or dividends are extracted to strip the SPC of taxable capacity.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to OMR 500,000 against the entity and up to OMR 50,000 per individual officer, revocation of the SPC's commercial license, confiscation of laundered proceeds, and mandatory disgorgement of unlawful gains.

Director personal liability: The compliance officer and directors face joint civil restitution, freezing of personal bank accounts, placement on the Central Bank of Oman blacklist, travel bans, and imprisonment of up to ten years where the SPC is used as an instrument of money laundering.
Wrongful Withholding and Cross-Border Payment Leakage

Statutory sanction: The SPC is held primarily liable for the unwithheld tax amount, monthly delay surcharges, and an administrative fine of up to OMR 10,000; the non-resident recipient is denied treaty protection and the entire remittance is restated as a taxable distribution.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized or signed the gross remittance are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding tax; enforcement may be directed at personal assets where the SPC has already distributed its reserves.
