Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), operating the Masar electronic tax portal (ota.gov.om)

E-government filing portal

For a Single Person Company (SPC), obtain Commercial Registration from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP). Thereafter, register with the Oman Tax Authority through the Masar portal to obtain a Tax Registration Number (TRN). For VAT, register separately under the VAT Law when turnover exceeds the mandatory threshold; the TRN serves as the VAT ID. Non-resident owners may use a local tax representative for registration and compliance filings.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a Single Person Company (SPC), obtain Commercial Registration from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP). Thereafter, register with the Oman Tax Authority through the Masar portal to obtain a Tax Registration Number (TRN). For VAT, register separately under the VAT Law when turnover exceeds the mandatory threshold; the TRN serves as the VAT ID. Non-resident owners may use a local tax representative for registration and compliance filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available. An SPC is taxed as an independent juridical person under the Oman Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 28/2009, as amended). No hybrid mismatch election is permitted. VAT registration is optional for taxpayers below the voluntary threshold (OMR 19,250 annual taxable supplies) and mandatory above the compulsory threshold; group VAT registration is available under specific conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 38,500 annual taxable supplies; voluntary registration: OMR 19,250 annual taxable supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods or services qualifies for 0% VAT if the supply is exported outside the GCC, provided the goods are removed from Oman within 90 days of the date of supply (or such extended period approved by the Oman Tax Authority). For services, the recipient must be outside the GCC and the benefit of the service must be received outside Oman. The taxpayer must retain documentary proof of export and international payment evidence. The zero rate does not apply to supplies to GCC residents unless the GCC-wide movement provisions are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayani) / Single Administrative Document
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial invoice with zero-rating declaration
  • Proof of departure / export certificate from Oman Customs
  • Bank transfer or Inward Remittance Certificate from an Omani bank

The Oman Tax Authority has not yet enforced a mandatory real-time e-invoicing clearance regime. Taxpayers must issue VAT invoices in a prescribed electronic or paper format and retain records via the Masar platform. A phased e-invoicing framework (Bayan) has been announced, but as of the current reporting period, no API clearance or digital stamping is mandatory for standard B2B/B2C supplies. Export zero-rated supplies must be supported by customs documentation and bank transfer evidence.

The Oman Tax Authority may conduct post-export audits to verify the physical export, the foreign receipt of payment, and the absence of buyer-side fraud. If the zero rating is improperly claimed (e.g., failure to export within 90 days, missing documentation, or artificial circumvention), the authority retrospectively claws back the VAT, and the taxpayer is liable for the full 5% tax, plus late penalties (monthly penalty of 1% on unpaid tax, up to 12 months), additional fines up to 100% of the tax amount, and possible criminal penalties for deliberate evasion under the VAT Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including profit remittance and dividends, for foreign-owned Single Person Companies. Capital account transactions are liberalised; inward direct investment requires registration under the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2002) and CBO balance-of-payments reporting. There is no prior CBO approval requirement for bona fide corporate remittances or service fees. Profit transfers require audited financial statements, tax clearance from the Oman Tax Authority, and repatriation in the original currency or OMR. No dual exchange rate, no surrender requirement, and no differentiated treatment for non-residents exists. The fixed peg of OMR 1 = USD 2.6008 provides FX stability, but banks will perform enhanced due diligence on medical data and software-related cross-border flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain receipts in foreign currency accounts at CBO-licensed banks. Banks must report export transactions exceeding OMR 10,000 to the CBO for balance-of-payments statistical purposes. Prudent treasury practice for the EMR SaaS entity is to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days of invoice due date to align with documentary trade standards and to avoid bank-induced unflagged outward remittance scrutiny. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 0%; conversion to OMR occurs only when the trade owner opts to settle service obligations in local currency.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) established under the Central Bank of Oman Law, Royal Decree 114/2000

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility as principal treasury rail. Oman does not license offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs); the CBO mandates that all in-country payment and liquidity services be provided by CBO-licensed banks. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Wise Business) cannot open OMR-denominated accounts or onboard the SPC without a locally licensed bank sponsor. Such EMIs may only serve as ancillary disbursement channels for non-OMR transactions outside Oman, and only via their partner bank SWIFT infrastructure. For regulatory compliance and liquidity control, the primary bank relationship must remain with a Tier 1 Oman domestic bank. Legal constraints include CBO Circular BM 1059 on payment services and the requirement for all sanctioned-money flows to be intermediated through a locally supervised entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial equity injection into the Omani SPC, use SWIFT MT103/MT202 COV to the Capital Escrow Account at Bank Muscat, HSBC Oman, or National Bank of Oman. The wire must carry the purpose code 'FDI' in the payment narrative, reference the CBO Foreign Investment Declaration number, and include the note 'SPC equity contribution' to satisfy anti-money-laundering screening. Beneficiary bank forms should specify the SPC's Commercial Registration and the capital account number. The Omani Single Person Company law does not mandate minimum capital, but banks ordinarily require an initial funding deposit of between OMR 1,000 and OMR 2,500 to activate the account. All inbound funds must trace their origin to identified shareholder bank accounts; any high-risk jurisdiction funding will trigger enhanced due diligence and request for a historical trail.Service subscription disbursements for the EMR SaaS platform are cleared through SWIFT MT103 transfers against commercial invoices. For physical medical/EHR infrastructure rollouts, use commercial letters of credit issued through the CBO-licensed bank under UCP 600, with SWIFT MT700/MT707 for issuance and amendment. OMR-denominated local settlement occurs via the Oman Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) platform; international USD settlement is effected through New York correspondent banking lines under SWIFT CT-systems. Banks require a standard FX conversion form (e.g., Form 0254) for service payments above OMR 10,000, accompanied by the underlying invoice, service contract, and proof of tax registration.
