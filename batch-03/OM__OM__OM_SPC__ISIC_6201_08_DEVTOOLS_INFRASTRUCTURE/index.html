Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) — Sultanate of Oman, official portal: https://taxoman.gov.om

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned SPC obtains automatic corporate tax registration upon issuance of the Commercial Registration (CR) at the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP); the SPC must then file an online application with OTA for a Tax Card (TIN) within 30 days. VAT registration is mandatory when projected 12-month taxable turnover exceeds OMR 38,500, and is filed separately via the same portal, resulting in a single VAT registration number.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated when the goods are physically exported from Oman outside the GCC within 90 days of supply and documentary proof of export is maintained. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident customer and the benefit of the service is received wholly outside Oman, subject to OTA substantiation and anti-avoidance conditions.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Oman is implementing a mandatory national e-invoicing regime through OTA technical circulars. Under the clearance model, VAT invoices must be transmitted in real time via an OTA-accredited API, digitally stamped with a unique identifier, and archived in an approved e-archive. The rollout is phased by taxpayer revenue categories, with high-revenue taxpayers first and all VAT-registered businesses expected to be within scope by 2027.

OTA audit procedures require full traceability of the exported goods and inbound foreign currency. Erroneous or fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger immediate VAT clawback plus statutory interest, administrative fines up to 100% of tax evaded under the Tax Procedures Law, and criminal sanctions for deliberate evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible on both current and capital account under Oman's liberal foreign exchange regime as codified in the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2019) and the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000). No prior CBO approval is required for current account transactions, and profit repatriation by foreign-owned entities is unrestricted provided the SPC is in good standing and taxes are settled. Capital account operations (e.g., liquidation proceeds, share capital reductions) are largely unrestricted, though banks may require a tax clearance certificate and audited financials. The OMR is pegged to the USD at 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD, eliminating convertibility risk; practical hurdles are limited to documentary compliance and anti-money laundering (AML) verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman has no mandatory export proceeds surrender regime. There is no statutory timeline or forced conversion ratio requiring the SPC to convert export receipts into OMR. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign currency accounts (up to 100%) with licensed banks, subject to normal AML reporting thresholds. The SPC may choose to hold USD, EUR, or OMR based on operational needs. However, all inbound transfers above OMR 20,000 must be reported to the CBO via the bank's electronic system for statistical purposes (Circular BM/1084/2018).

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not licensed by the Central Bank of Oman for direct market entry. The CBO does not recognize EMI charters from the UK, EU, or other jurisdictions, and the legal framework (Banking Law and Payment Card Issuance Regulations) restricts deposit-taking and payment initiation to licensed Omani banks and locally domiciled payment service providers. The SPC may use a licensed EMI solely for outbound vendor payments or as a secondary receivables collection tool, but that does not replace the statutory obligation to maintain a domestic Omani bank account for tax filings and capital contributions. Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury rail; Medium only as a supplementary corridor for low-value, non-critical cloud services subscriptions, provided the EMI's KYC/AML posture is acceptable to Omani auditors and the CBO's foreign account reporting rules are satisfied.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity and capital infusions must be wired from the parent company's corporate bank account in the home jurisdiction using SWIFT MT103. The sender must include the following purpose code in the payment narrative: 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION – DEVT INFRA' (reference Omani SPC name and CR number). For amounts above USD 100,000, the receiving bank requires a copy of the MCA certificate, board resolution approving the injection, and a share allotment letter. The CBO's balance-of-payments reporting will classify the transfer as foreign direct investment (FDI); no prior CBO approval is needed, but the bank will verify the SPC's CR and tax card. If the injection is structured as a shareholder loan, use the purpose code 'INTERCOMPANY LOAN – SH TF' and attach a signed loan agreement with interest rate and maturity to prevent recharacterization as equity for bank reporting.Clearing rails for Operational Imported Services (cloud infrastructure, API monitoring, DevOps tooling) are primarily via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the Omani SPC's operational account to the vendor's bank account. The SPC must use the correct IMF BOP purpose code (e.g., 'Telecommunications, computer, and information services' – code 222) and ensure the remittance advice corresponds to an underlying invoice. For large cross-border purchases of hardware (servers, network equipment), a Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) with a confirmed irrevocable L/C at sight is permissible, but given the low-risk nature of environmental infrastructure, an MT103 with invoice escrow or credit terms is standard. The Omani bank will apply a foreign remittance fee of OMR 5–15 per transfer and will require the invoice, bill of lading (if goods), and a service agreement for any periodic disbursement over OMR 30,000.Vanilla FX forwards (USD/OMR and OMR/INR, up to 12 months) with a licensed Omani bank, requiring a documented underlying trade exposure (invoice or purchase order).FX swaps (USD to OMR and reverse) for managing short-term liquidity in dual-currency accounts.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) only for OMR-denominated borrowing to convert to USD-linked liabilities, subject to CBO prudential limits.NDFs (Non-Deliverable Forwards) are not permitted for the OMR due to the fixed peg; banks may offer the OMR as the deliverable leg in a USD/OMR forward, but no NDF settlement in OMR is available offshore.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% on taxable income exceeding OMR 30,000; nil band up to OMR 30,000

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Oman generally does not impose dividend withholding tax on distributions made by an Omani SPC to a non-resident shareholder, provided the dividend is paid from post-tax profits, is not a disguised distribution, and the shareholder has no additional Omani-source income through a PE.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 4 months after the end of the tax year. For a calendar-year taxpayer the filing deadline is 30 April of the following year. Payment of any balance due accompanies the return; quarterly provisional payments are required only where OTA has issued an advance assessment or the taxpayer's estimated liability exceeds statutory thresholds.

Knowingly filing false declarations places the SPC and directors within the scope of tax evasion penalties, including additional fines of up to 100% of the evaded tax and criminal prosecution.
Box 4 — Related-Party Transactions and Transfer Pricing Declaration Provide aggregate values of related-party revenue and expenditure, intercompany royalty payments, management fees, and intra-group service charges, together with adopted transfer pricing methodology. The foreign owner must attach a transfer pricing disclosure and contemporaneous documentation if related-party turnover exceeds the statutory threshold; identical documentation must be retained on file in Oman for audit inspection. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation and submission of the OTP TP disclosure form are required where aggregate related-party revenue or expenditure exceeds OMR 1,000,000 in a tax year; a Master File under OECD/G20 BEPS guidance is expected where the foreign parent group has consolidated revenue above OMR 100,000,000 or where cross-border related-party transactions are material to the Omani SPC.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not operate a separate Emirates-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the Income Tax Law's general anti-avoidance and substance-over-form provisions achieve an equivalent standard. OTA may disregard artificial transactions and can deny treaty, free-zone, or CIT benefits where the SPC lacks genuine office premises, locally controlled board decisions, employees with operative authority, and real commercial activity. A foreign-owned SPC created solely as a billing shell for DevOps/cloud sales without substantive management in Oman is treated as a tax-avoidance vehicle and fully taxable at 15%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Omission of Income and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: OTA redetermines taxable income on a robust basis and issues a revised assessment for full 15% CIT. Late payment surcharges accrue at 1% per month. Evasion penalties add up to 100% of the under-assessed tax, and criminal proceedings may lead to imprisonment of directors and a fine of up to OMR 10,000.

Director personal liability: Where the SPC is shown to be a façade for the foreign owner's business, the corporate veil is pierced and the director and effective shareholder are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, penalties, and audit costs. Courts may also impose travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment up to three years.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Unsubstantiated Intercompany Charges

Statutory sanction: OTA makes an arm's length adjustment, increases taxable income, assesses the 15% CIT differential, and imposes a monthly late-payment charge. False TP documentation is treated as an evasion-related administrative offence, separately punishable by fines and loss of deduction for unsubstantiated transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or sign fraudulent transfer pricing documents can be personally penalised for preparing false records. OTA may seek restitution for undeclared tax from the SPC and from any director beneficially diverting funds; travel restrictions may be imposed while the investigation is pending.
Shell Company and Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: OTA revokes any tax exemption or treaty benefit, applies 15% CIT to all taxable income from the commencement of the sham structure, and assesses penalties and late payment surcharges. The Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion may also strike the company off the Commercial Register for conducting no genuine business activity.

Director personal liability: Directors and promoters of a sham SPC are exposed to personal civil liability for tax debts where assets and income have been mixed with shareholder assets. Omani courts have authority to issue travel bans, disqualify directors from serving on other boards, and require restitution of tax, penalties, and government costs.
AML, Beneficial Ownership and UBO Disclosure Breach

Statutory sanction: Violation of Oman's AML Law and commercial registry UBO obligations attracts heavy administrative fines, confiscation of funds, and deregistration of the SPC. In aggravated cases, directors face criminal fines of up to OMR 100,000 and imprisonment up to seven years for money-laundering-linked activities.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are individually liable for failure to maintain robust beneficial ownership registers. Courts may impose personal fines, travel bans, asset freezes, and custodial sentences if the SPC is used to launder proceeds of fraudulent subscription contracts or to conceal hidden owners.
