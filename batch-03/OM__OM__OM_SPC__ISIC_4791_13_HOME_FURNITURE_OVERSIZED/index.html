Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) - Official portal at taxoman.gov.om; taxpayer e-services accessible through the OTA online services platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) must obtain an Omani Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT ID through the OTA online registration. The SPC is a resident juridical entity under Omani law; accordingly, submission of the commercial registration certificate, constitutional documents, proof of registered address, ownership declarations, and board/director identification is required. Registration is typically processed within 5 business days and a VAT certificate is issued electronically. For non-resident digital sellers operating without a local establishment, appointment of a resident tax representative may be required by the OTA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration threshold: OMR 500,000 of annual taxable supplies for resident persons; voluntary registration threshold: OMR 250,000; non-resident suppliers with Oman nexus are required to register from the first OMR of taxable supply (zero threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods physically removed from Oman to a destination outside the GCC is zero-rated where the exporter retains evidence of export within 90 days of supply. Transportation services related to exported goods and certain ancillary services supplied directly to the exported goods are also zero-rated. The overseas customer must be outside Oman/GCC and risk and title must pass to the buyer before export completion.

  • Customs Export Declaration (single administrative document) issued by Oman Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing shipment to a non-GCC destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement showing foreign exchange receipt for the exported goods
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Purchase order or contract with the non-GCC customer

The Oman Tax Authority has commenced a phased e-invoicing mandate. VAT-invoicing compliant with OTA technical specifications is required; accredited billing systems must support API-based transmission, digital stamping, and real-time clearance once the e-invoicing modules become operational for e-commerce registrants. Foreign sellers are expected to integrate with OTA-approved fiscal intermediaries for cross-border transaction clearance.

Export zero-rating is subject to audit by the OTA. If supporting documentation is found inaccurate, incomplete, or fraudulent, the zero-rated supply is recharacterized as taxable at 5% VAT, interest/surcharges accrue from the original due date, and administrative penalties of up to OMR 25,000 may be imposed. Serious evasion may trigger criminal proceedings with fines up to OMR 100,000 and imprisonment under Oman VAT legislation, in addition to clawback of refunds previously granted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current account transactions, consistent with Oman's Article VIII obligations under the IMF Articles of Agreement. Capital account convertibility is selectively open: direct foreign investment, including 100% foreign-owned SPCs, enjoys liberal entry, and capital repatriation is generally permitted. Profit and dividend remittances by non-resident shareholders are not subject to ad hoc exchange control licences, but require bank-level compliance with the SPC's audited financial statements, tax clearance where applicable, and the filing of a Foreign Capital Investment certificate if the SPC was registered under the Foreign Capital Investment Law. Hurdles are largely prudential, not regulatory: the receiving Omani bank must verify the underlying transaction validity, source of funds, beneficial ownership, and the absence of bribery or sanctions flags. Counterparty delays may arise from incomplete shareholder KYC, absence of registered address physical verification, or mismatched commercial invoice documentation. No currency blockages exist for genuine operational income or capital injections, but transfers to sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions will be scrutinised.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose any mandatory surrender of export proceeds into OMR and there is no statutory conversion ratio. CBO exchange control requires that all export proceeds derived from Omani exports be repatriated to an Omani-based authorised bank account within 180 days from the date of shipment. Exporter accounts can retain realised receipts in OMR or in foreign currency without conversion, but must be able to produce shipping documents, customs declarations, and bank credit advices to evidence the inflow. Extension beyond the 180-day window requires a justified application to CBO through the account-holding commercial bank, normally granted for open account trade with extended payment terms. In practice, the online furniture retail exporter should direct all cross-border payment rails so that settlement of international sales is credited to its OMR or USD account at a licensed Omani bank within the timeline.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Stripe, Payoneer, Wise, Affirm BNPL partners) are not licensed as banks or payment service providers by the Central Bank of Oman. Their viability is Medium as auxiliary payment collection and disbursement rails for low-risk e-commerce proceeds, but not as a primary treasury settlement account. Legal constraints include the prohibition on holding client funds in Oman without a CBO licence, the need for all OMR-denominated payments to pass through an authorised Omani bank, and point-of-sale or online acquiring that must involve a licensed local acquiring bank or an intrabank cross-border arrangement. The SPC may integrate these EMIs solely as customer payment channels; funds must be swept regularly into the tier-1 Omani bank account. EMIs cannot issue settlement accounts in OMR unless they obtain CBO authorisation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the foreign parent must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from your parent's bank to the SPC's CBO-licensed bank account. The MT103 beneficiary account name must exactly match the SPC Commercial Registration, and the payment narration must state 'Capital Subscription / Equity Contribution' together with the SPC's CR number. The transferring bank should include the purpose code for direct investment; there is no mandatory Omani purpose code system, but using 'General Capital Transfers / Investment' is advisable. The CBO-designated bank will verify the share subscription agreement, board approval, and if applicable the Foreign Capital Investment certificate. Transfers above OMR 20,000 may attract enhanced due diligence. Do not split an equity injection into multiple small wire transfers to avoid reporting; banks and CBO view splintered payments as a red flag. For a 100% foreign-owned SPC, the share capital is not statutorily determined, but a disclosed capital figure in the Commercial Registration must be funded consistently.Trade payments to suppliers and freight forwarders for oversized furniture purchases should be cleared through SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments, or SWIFT MT700 documentary letters of credit where a more secure settlement is required for high-value bulk shipments. The outbound remittance instructions must match exactly the EU or beneficiary bank BIC/SWIFT code and include the underlying commercial invoice number, contract reference, and shipment date. For payments exceeding USD 10,000, banks require supporting trade documents: commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, certificate of origin, and packing list. Omani banks are transaction-wary on payments to third-country intermediaries; direct payment to the actual seller is preferred. For cross-border ACH or local clearing, no meaningful OMAN-specific rail exists beyond CBO real-time gross settlement (RTGS) for OMR and SWIFT for foreign currencies. Trade finance products such as supplier advance and promissory note discounting are available at tier-1 banks but should be reserved for high-value inventory purchases.Onshore OMR/USD forward contracts through an authorised Omani dealer bank, used to lock the pegged rate and mitigate any revaluation spread exposureCross-currency swaps (USD/OMR, USD/INR, USD/EUR) allowing the SPC to match future trade settlement cash flows without speculative open positionsOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) can be used by the parent, but because OMR/USD is pegged, NDFs are generally redundant and may raise heightened compliance scrutiny; Omani banks tend not to offer them locally

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

7.5% marginal rate on taxable income exceeding OMR 1,000,000; lower brackets: 0% up to OMR 30,000, 3% on OMR 30,001–OMR 350,000, 5% on OMR 350,001–OMR 1,000,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Oman does not impose dividend withholding tax on distributions by an Omani SPC to its non-resident shareholder(s). Profits distributed from already-taxed corporate income bear no additional Oman source tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual CIT return must be submitted within six months after the end of the financial year. For a 31 December year-end, the statutory deadline is 30 June of the following year. Taxable entities are also liable to pay any assessed tax by the same deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where the Oman SPC has annual gross revenue exceeding OMR 10 million and related-party transactions exceed OMR 500,000 per tax year. Master File is mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds OMR 50 million. The Oman Tax Authority may still request transfer pricing documentation for lower-risk taxpayers through risk-based assessment notices.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime to all commercial licenses; however, the OTA enforces substantive-residence requirements and anti-avoidance principles under the Income Tax Law, transfer-pricing rules, and tax treaty provisions. A foreign-owned SPC engaged in oversized furniture e-commerce must demonstrate genuine Oman-based management, local operational premises, sufficient employees, real logistical supervision, and board-level decisions in Oman. Failure to maintain economic substance creates risk of denial of Omani tax residence, recharacterisation of the company, and loss of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Under-declaration of e-commerce revenue

Statutory sanction: Assessment of omitted tax, late surcharge of 1% per month, administrative penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud, with fines and/or imprisonment under Oman tax and Penal Code provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors and the foreign sole shareholder who knowingly cause false reporting can be jointly assessed for the SPC's tax debts. Oman enforcement measures include attachment of personal assets, travel bans, and imprisonment upon criminal conviction.
Shell Company — Artificial corporate structure and lack of Oman substance

Statutory sanction: The OTA may disregard the corporate form, reattribute taxable income to the true foreign owner, deny the SPC's Omani resident status, impose deemed-profit assessments and applicable withholding taxes, and refer the entity for administrative strike-off or dissolution.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate veil is pierced, the foreign shareholder and nominee director may be personally liable for corporate debts and taxes, subject to civil restitution, travel bans, seizure of assets, and criminal prosecution under the Commercial Companies Law and UBO disclosure rules.
Transfer Pricing — Non-arm's-length profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Oman Tax Authority may impose a primary transfer pricing adjustment, deny deductions for excessive related-party payments, reclassify excess payments as deemed dividends, assess additional CIT, and impose administrative fines for non-production of transfer pricing documentation, with late-payment surcharges continuing until settlement.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate declarations or approve sham intercompany contracts can be held jointly and severally liable for the additional tax, and face director disqualification, travel bans, and restrained bank accounts pending full restitution.
AML and Payment-Routing Infringements

Statutory sanction: Oman's AML law provides for freezing and confiscation of proceeds, closure of commercial registration, administrative fines, and criminal sanctions including imprisonment for money laundering where online retail proceeds are intentionally concealed or cycled.

Director personal liability: The single foreign shareholder and local manager are exposed to personal seizure orders, travel restrictions, and imprisonment if they knowingly operate revolving payment rails to launder funds or conceal the true economic benefit of the furniture e-commerce venture.
