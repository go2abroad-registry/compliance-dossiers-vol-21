Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Oman Companies Law stipulates no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Single Person Company; the sole member must state the subscribed share capital in the Memorandum and fully pay such capital into the SPC's Omani bank account before commencement of business. Escrow account is not statutorily mandated; a notarial certificate of capital availability is recommended for foreign-owned SPCs.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense and Ambulance Authority (CDAA) Fire Safety Clearance Certificate for the registered office and storage area; structural load inspection certificate by a licensed civil engineer confirming floor loading capacity for surveying instruments and test equipment; municipal occupational health clearance and workplace environment certificate confirming ventilation, sanitation, and emergency evacuation compliance.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) | Online Portal: https://tms.ota.gov.om and https://tax.om

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Single Person Companies (SPCs) must file an application for Corporate Tax ID and VAT ID (where applicable) through the OTA's Tax Management System (TMS). The SPC's legal representative or licensed tax agent must create an online user account, submit the company's Commercial Registration (CR) certificate, memorandum and articles of association, registered office proof, valid Oman residence/visa of the sole shareholder (if any), and any existing foreign tax identification number. Post-submission, OTA reviews, requests any missing documentation, and issues the SPC's unified Tax Identification Number (TIN). VAT registration is a separate but integrated process in TMS, requiring projected taxable supplies assessment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box elective pass-through regime exists under Oman tax law. SPCs are treated as corporate taxpayers at the entity level. No hybrid mismatch or fiscal unity elections are available. Election mechanisms are limited to voluntary VAT registration (if threshold not met), mandatory VAT registration when the threshold is exceeded, and small-business tax exemption election under the Corporate Income Tax Law for annual revenues up to OMR 100,000 (subject to conditions).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration: OMR 50,000 (taxable supplies and imports over the preceding 12 months); voluntary VAT registration: OMR 17,500 (taxable supplies and imports over the preceding 12 months)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to the export of goods outside the GCC/VAT Area, provided the exporter retains evidence of physical removal. For services, zero-rating may apply when the recipient is a non-resident person, the service directly benefits the non-resident, and the service is not directly related to land located in Oman or any imported/exported goods in a manner that derogates from international services treatment. For a marine insurance surveyor, zero-rating is available only if the service is performed for a non-resident insurer or insured party, the service is directly linked to goods being shipped from Oman or to an overseas overseas risk, and no local consumption in Oman occurs. If the survey service relates to a marine casualty inside Omani territorial waters or to a local vessel’s operation, standard 5% VAT likely applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (adapted for services: duly signed Service Export Contract and relevant insurance policy schedules)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (where physical goods involved)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Credit Advice evidencing the non-resident payment in foreign currency

Oman has not yet implemented a fully mandatory real-time e-invoicing/clearance system. As of the current stage, taxpayers must issue VAT-compliant tax invoices under Article 77 of the VAT Law, including the VAT registration number, invoice serial number, and all statutory elements. The OTA is preparing a gradual e-invoicing rollout; continuous digital stamping and API-based real-time clearance are not yet statutory requirements. However, taxpayers must retain all invoicing data in structured digital format for OTA audits.

Unauthorized zero-rating or fraudulent export claims are subject to VAT Law penalties up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus administrative fines, interest, and criminal liability for willful fraud. Retrospective clawback (assessment) may extend over the statutory limitation period (generally five years, extended to ten years for non-filing or material intentional default). The OTA aggressively audits zero-rated export transactions, requiring full documentary proof of the non-resident recipient and the actual currency cross-border payment trail. Incomplete or forged documentation triggers immediate penalty assessment, seizure of bank accounts, and cancellation of VAT registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial is fully convertible for current account transactions under IMF Article VIII, with no foreign exchange license requirement for trade settlements, dividends, or interest remittances. Capital account transactions are also liberalized for foreign investors, but banks must verify the underlying economic substance and obtain supporting contracts, board resolutions, audited financials, and tax clearance certificates. Profit remittance for a 100% foreign-owned SPC is permitted through the domestic banking system without CBO approval, provided the company has filed Oman Tax Authority returns and satisfied Commercial Companies Law escrow or creditor-protection requirements applicable to the specific distribution. No overnight foreign currency position restrictions apply to corporates in Oman, but all inward and outward transfers must pass through an authorized Omani bank to preserve statistical reporting and AML controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose a mandatory physical export proceeds surrender regime for marine surveyor service fees or general private-sector exports; exporters are not required to convert export proceeds into OMR within a fixed statutory timeline, nor are they required to surrender a fixed percentage of foreign currency receipts to the Central Bank. However, under CBO banking supervision, export receipts derived from marine claims inspection services must be credited to the SPC's resident OMR/FX account and be traceable through SWIFT or equivalent payment rails. Documentary evidence such as the signed survey report, insurance claim settlement document, commercial invoice, and effect of a bank credit advice must be retained for five years to support anti-money laundering, tax, and balance-of-payments reporting requirements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), acting as the national monetary and foreign exchange authority; the CBO administers the Oman Rial (OMR) under an IMF Article VIII open-economy framework, in coordination with the Ministry of Economy for government-flagged oil revenue flows and the Capital Market Authority for securities-related remittance reporting.

Economic substance scope: Oman applies substance requirements as a gate for treaty benefits, free-zone tax exemptions, and strategic-project incentives; the SPC must demonstrate management and control in Oman through locally executed survey contracts, presence of qualified marine surveyor staff in Oman, physically conducted hull and cargo inspections, board meetings and commercial decision-making held in Oman, and local bank accounts; the Tax Authority may disregard preferential treatment and recharacterise income where the entity is a letterbox company or conducts no core income-generating activity; marine insurance survey revenue is classified as an insurable-services activity, requiring renewal of substantive local operations evidence annually

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under Royal Decree 54/2023: fine from OMR 5,000 up to OMR 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment for a term not exceeding 5 years; full recovery of evaded tax plus 1% monthly late-payment surcharge capped at 25% of the unpaid tax; forfeiture of business records and seizure of survey equipment used in the offence

Director personal liability: Directors and the authorised manager are jointly and severally liable for evaded tax and penal fines when they authorised the concealment; Omani courts may impose asset freezes, attachment of personal bank accounts and real estate, travel bans, and an exit-ban entry on the GCC Civil Identity system; a criminal conviction disqualifies the director from holding any Omani trade licence and triggers removal from the Commercial Register
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment increasing chargeable income plus 1% monthly tax surcharge up to 25% of the recomputed tax; administrative fine up to OMR 5,000 for failure to file contemporaneous documentation; denial of deductions for undocumented cross-border payments; risk of double taxation if the taxpayer fails to pursue a Mutual Agreement Procedure

Director personal liability: The director who signs the TP declaration is personally liable for intentionally false statements; penalties may be recovered from personal assets, the director is subject to a 5-year disqualification from management positions in Oman, and a travel ban may be imposed pending payment of the TP adjustment tax
Shell Company and Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: The Tax Authority may disregard the SPC as a tax resident, deny expense deductions, recharacterise the entire marine survey income to the foreign beneficial owner, impose 10% unrelieved withholding tax, and levy a fine up to OMR 100,000; the ISIC 5222 marine survey licence may be revoked and the Commercial Registration suspended

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: shareholders and directors become personally liable for unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and interest; courts may order deportation of foreign directors, attachment of Omani and GCC asset holdings under mutual enforcement agreements, and criminal fraud charges for signing false substance declarations carry imprisonment of up to 5 years
AML and Payment Rails Abuse

Statutory sanction: Oman AML Law (Royal Decree 30/2016, as amended): fine up to OMR 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment not exceeding 7 years; freezing and confiscation of the survey fee proceeds; suspension of the SPC's access to Omani banking channels and electronic payment initiation; revocation of the professional licence upon conviction

Director personal liability: Directors and the appointed compliance officer face personal criminal liability, bank account freezes, travel bans, disqualification from managing any Omani trade licence, and extradition risk under GCC security protocols if illicit funds were moved through Wise or foreign underwriter correspondent accounts
