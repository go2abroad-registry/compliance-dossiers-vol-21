Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) — Directorate General of Commerce, Commercial Registration Department and Investment Services Center (Invest Easy one-stop-shop), Muscat, Sultanate of Oman.

Governing statute

Oman Commercial Companies Law (Sultani Decree No. 18/2019), Articles 63–72 and 96–106 governing the Single Person Company (SPC), Articles 130–132 governing managers, together with the Executive Regulations issued via MoCIIP Decision No. 168/2019; Foreign Capital Investment Law (Sultani Decree No. 50/2019), Articles 3 and 5 permitting 100% foreign ownership of an SPC; Commercial Registration Law (Sultani Decree No. 1/2016), Articles 2–8; and the Investment Services Center licensing rules administered by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Commercial Registration (Form CR-01) accompanied by the notarized Articles of Association / Memorandum of Association of the SPC executed in Arabic, filed electronically and recorded in the Commercial Register; evidenced by the Commercial Registration (CR) Certificate and official CR extract.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a foreign-owned SPC under Royal Decree No. 18/2019 and Royal Decree No. 50/2019 unless a sectoral regulator imposes a higher floor; the sole member must subscribe the full share capital stated in the Memorandum of Association, deposit it into a temporary capital escrow account at a licensed Omani bank, and submit a bank-issued Capital Confirmation Certificate with the CR application; in-kind contributions require a registered expert valuation and must be fully delivered before CR issuance.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and Life Safety certificate issued by the Royal Oman Police — Directorate General of Civil Defence and Ambulance after inspection of fire extinguishers, fork-lift charging areas, emergency egress, and segregation of fertilizer stock from ignition sources; structural load inspection of the warehouse slab and racking systems certified by an engineering office licensed by the Ministry of Housing and Urban Planning; and occupational safety clearance from the Ministry of Labour under the OSH regime of Ministerial Decision No. 286/2014, requiring a written safety plan for dust control, manual handling of 50 kg fertilizer bags, fork-lift operations, and emergency procedures.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a foreign-owned SPC under Royal Decree No. 18/2019 and Royal Decree No. 50/2019 unless a sectoral regulator imposes a higher floor; the sole member must subscribe the full share capital stated in the Memorandum of Association, deposit it into a temporary capital escrow account at a licensed Omani bank, and submit a bank-issued Capital Confirmation Certificate with the CR application; in-kind contributions require a registered expert valuation and must be fully delivered before CR issuance.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), the Sultanate of Oman national tax administration reporting to the Ministry of Finance; primary e-services portal: taxoman.gov.om (TIN/TRN application, VAT and CIT registration/filing, e-invoicing clearance, refund submission); customs and export border control are separately processed through the Royal Oman Police Customs single-window Bayan platform.

E-government filing portal

For a 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC): (1) incorporate with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) via the Invest Easy portal to obtain the Commercial Registration (CR); (2) obtain membership of the Oman Chamber of Commerce and Industry (OCCI); (3) within 30 days of CR issuance, file the TRN (statutory Tax Registration Number) application on taxoman.gov.om using the certificate of incorporation, constitutional papers, Power of Attorney of the registered auditor or tax representative, physical Oman address evidence (lease or title) and passport copies of the shareholder and authorized manager (no minimum paid-up capital is legally required); (4) complete the VAT e-registration on the same OTA e-services account as soon as the mandatory threshold (OMR 38,500 annualized taxable supplies) is exceeded or anticipatable within the next 30 days; (5) open the local corporate bank account after CR (AIRO/AML procedure) and, if staff are engaged, register with the Public Authority for Social Insurance (PASI); (6) retain the OTA digital credentials and TIN to operate the e-invoicing and export-refund modules.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — an Omani SPC is a juridical person and is itself the taxpayer under the Corporate Income Tax (CIT) Law, Royal Decree No. 37/2024; as an Oman-incorporated resident company it is subject to the flat 15% CIT rate on worldwide taxable income (with per-country foreign tax credit) and no pass-through or fiscal transparency to the single foreign shareholder; the territorial scope is residence-based for the SPC (worldwide basis) and source-based for non-residents, while an SPC physically established in a recognized economic zone would enjoy an alternative zone incentive rate (not applicable to the standard onshore OM jurisdiction in this dossier). — For a 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC): (1) incorporate with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) via the Invest Easy portal to obtain the Commercial Registration (CR); (2) obtain membership of the Oman Chamber of Commerce and Industry (OCCI); (3) within 30 days of CR issuance, file the TRN (statutory Tax Registration Number) application on taxoman.gov.om using the certificate of incorporation, constitutional papers, Power of Attorney of the registered auditor or tax representative, physical Oman address evidence (lease or title) and passport copies of the shareholder and authorized manager (no minimum paid-up capital is legally required); (4) complete the VAT e-registration on the same OTA e-services account as soon as the mandatory threshold (OMR 38,500 annualized taxable supplies) is exceeded or anticipatable within the next 30 days; (5) open the local corporate bank account after CR (AIRO/AML procedure) and, if staff are engaged, register with the Public Authority for Social Insurance (PASI); (6) retain the OTA digital credentials and TIN to operate the e-invoicing and export-refund modules.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Omani SPC is a juridical person and is itself the taxpayer under the Corporate Income Tax (CIT) Law, Royal Decree No. 37/2024; as an Oman-incorporated resident company it is subject to the flat 15% CIT rate on worldwide taxable income (with per-country foreign tax credit) and no pass-through or fiscal transparency to the single foreign shareholder; the territorial scope is residence-based for the SPC (worldwide basis) and source-based for non-residents, while an SPC physically established in a recognized economic zone would enjoy an alternative zone incentive rate (not applicable to the standard onshore OM jurisdiction in this dossier).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Omani CIT law; the SPC tax classification is statutory. Available elections include: (i) tax group election — a resident company that holds 90% or more of the share capital and voting control of another resident company may jointly elect group taxation (irrevocable for five tax periods, requiring consolidated tax computation and joint liability); (ii) small-business relief election for Omani-licensed taxpayers with gross Omani income not exceeding OMR 100,000, exempting the first OMR 50,000 of taxable income; (iii) election of the cash accounting basis for qualifying small businesses instead of accrual accounting; (iv) fiscal-year-end alignment election subject to OTA approval; and (v) characterization elections under the CIT anti-hybrid/mismatch rules to align foreign hybrid entities, branches and PE profit attribution with BEPS Action 2 outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration for a resident SPC: OMR 38,500 (approximately USD 100,000) of cumulative taxable supplies and taxable imports in the preceding 12 months or reasonably expected in the next 30 days; voluntary registration is available from OMR 19,250 (approximately USD 50,000); non-residents making taxable supplies into Oman without a local establishment are required to register under the VAT non-resident registration rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The SPC may zero-rate an export supply of prilled urea or potassium chloride where (i) the goods are physically dispatched from an Omani port (e.g., Sohar, Salalah or Duqm) or border point to a destination outside the GCC VAT area; (ii) title and risk pass to a non-resident buyer while the goods are in transit; (iii) the goods leave Oman within 90 days of the date of supply (the OTA may accept a 90-day extension, giving a 180-day maximum export window); and (iv) the SPC retains full documentary proof of export, exit clearance and foreign-currency remittance. Zero-rating also covers ancillary export logistics services (international freight, loading/stevedoring, marine insurance and export customs clearance) and qualifying offshore services supplied to a non-resident customer with effective use and benefit wholly outside the GCC VAT area.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan single-window filing with port exit validation/stamp)
  • Bill of Lading (Master/House B/L or sea waybill) or air waybill evidencing physical dispatch from Oman to a non-GCC destination
  • Commercial invoice, proforma contract and packing/weight list describing ISIC 4669 goods (prilled urea and KCl), Incoterms and the transfer of risk to the non-resident buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT MT103 issued by the Omani paying bank evidencing receipt of the full export consideration in foreign currency
  • Carrier certificate of shipment and customs transit records confirming exit from Omani territory (and GCC territory if routed through UAE/GCC corridors)
  • Any applicable free zone/customs warehouse exit authorization, export permit, or letter of credit documentation evidencing the underlying commodity sale

Oman operates a phased e-invoicing mandate announced by the OTA for 2025-2026: the first compliance cohort covers taxable persons with annual turnover above OMR 1,000,000, the second cohort covers turnover above OMR 100,000, and the final tranche covers all remaining VAT registrants. The regime applies a hybrid clearance and delegated-stamping model — B2B invoices are transmitted in near-real-time from certified ERP/POS systems to the OTA e-invoicing engine through REST/JSON APIs, cryptographically validated and stamped with an OTA digital token and QR code, and returned to the issuer; B2C receipts are digitally signed before issuance. Unstamped or uncleared invoices are deemed not issued for VAT input-credit purposes and subject the SPC to OMR 1,000 to OMR 10,000 per-document violation fines.

If within the 180-day export-window the SPC fails to procure and retain the Bayan export declaration, bill of lading and inward remittance certificate, the OTA retroactively re-characterizes the zero-rated supply as taxable at 5%, assesses the VAT with a late-payment charge of OMR 100 or 1% per month (whichever is greater), and applies a violation fine of up to OMR 50,000 per deficient invoice or record. A fraudulent export zero-rating claim (forged Bayan documentation, circular exports, or false IRC/SWIFT records) is treated as VAT evasion under Royal Decree No. 121/2021: the OTA may claw back 100% of any refund paid, impose a penalty of three times the tax evaded, apply criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to five years, and suspend or revoke the SPC's TIN and e-invoicing clearance.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for all current-account transactions, including payments for physical fertilizer imports, freight, insurance, and associated trading services. Capital-account transactions are broadly liberalized for foreign-owned entities under the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2019), allowing 100% foreign ownership of an SPC. Profit and dividend remittance by a non-resident shareholder is lawful and unrestricted provided the SPC holds an issuance from the Oman Tax Authority confirming that all corporate tax and registration liabilities are settled, the remittance is supported by audited financial statements, and the transfer is effected through a licensed commercial bank using a standard outward remittance purpose code. There are no exchange controls requiring prior CBO approval for routine trade-related settlements; however, banks must maintain documentation supporting the underlying transaction for regulatory and anti-money-laundering compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose mandatory surrender or statutory conversion ratios on export receipts. An Omani exporter may retain export proceeds in a foreign-currency account with a licensed bank or convert them to OMR at the freely prevailing market rate. The CBO expects all export charges, including proceeds from urea and potash trade, to be repatriated to a licensed bank account within 180 days of the shipment date unless an extended documentary collection period is approved by the financing bank. Failure to repatriate or evidence proceeds within this timeline may trigger CBO balance-of-payments reporting and banking prudential review, though no forced conversion into OMR is prescribed.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), acting under the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000) and the Foreign Exchange Circulars issued by CBO in its capacity as the country's sole monetary and foreign exchange administrator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore fintech EMIs and digital-only money institutions are not recognized by the CBO as regulated settlement or clearing counterparties for OMR-denominated accounts or for receipt of capital injection proceeds. Use of an offshore EMI as a collection account is permissible only as a discretionary treasury adjunct for foreign-currency inflows outside Oman, but never as the primary payment rail for trade settlements, tax filings, or capital transfers into Oman. A foreign EMI must hold a payment-services licence recognized in Oman or operate through a locally incorporated commercial bank partner; otherwise the SPC remains exposed to CBO anti-money laundering and exchange-control violations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be remitted by wire transfer, SWIFT MT103, to the SPC's capital escrow account at an Oman-licensed bank using the purpose code 'equity participation in a resident enterprise' (e.g., BPM-FDI code 521) or the equivalent bank-specific inward remittance code. The remitter's bank should include a clear narrative referencing 'Payment against Share Capital Contribution to [SPC name]' alongside the SPC's Commercial Registry Number and CBO Tax Registration Number. The receiving bank will not credit the operational account until the capital escrow requirement is verified against the notarized articles of association and the Commercial Registration certificate issued by MOCIIP. Any later utilization from the escrow account requires the bank to receive a board resolution and an undertaking that the funds will solely finance working capital and trade payments for the licensed wholesale fertilizer business.All trade disbursements for urea and potash purchases or freight settlement shall be executed via SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) or SWIFT MT202 (bank-to-bank cover) and, for high-value sea cargo settlements, through documentary Letters of Credit (LC) confirmed or unconfirmed at the SPC's option. The outbound remittance voucher must include a valid purpose code for payment of imported goods or transportation services, accompanied by the proforma invoice, contract, bill of lading, inspection certificate, and freight invoice. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% on taxable income exceeding OMR 100,000; 3% on the first OMR 100,000 of taxable income (Royal Decree 9/2023, Income Tax Law).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — dividends and profit distributions by an Omani SPC to a non-resident shareholder are not subject to Omani dividend withholding tax, and no secondary remittance tax is applied on outbound distributions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where aggregate controlled (related-party) transactions in a tax year equal or exceed OMR 300,000; Master File: mandatory where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds OMR 50 million; a full Transfer Pricing Disclosure Form is required for any controlled transaction regardless of size, with penalties for omission.

Economic substance scope: Oman's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply where the SPC carries on a 'distribution and service centre' activity, which includes wholesale commodity trading serving group or third-party markets. The SPC must be managed and directed in Oman (local management decisions, meeting records), maintain adequate employees, local premises, and core income-generating activities in Oman, and file the annual ESR return/report with the relevant authority. Failure to satisfy the substance test or to file the ESR report exposes the SPC to a fine of up to OMR 10,000 per year, and repeat violations may lead to suspension or strike-off from the commercial register.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: A penalty equal to 100% of the evaded tax (with a statutory minimum), plus imprisonment for up to 3 years and personal cost orders; the OTA may re-open assessments for up to 10 years and impose daily coercive fines until records are produced; the tax claim ranks as a preferential debt in liquidation.

Director personal liability: Any director, SPC manager, or authorized signatory who directed or participated in the fraud is personally and jointly liable for the company's unpaid tax, penalties, and interest; the OTA may issue travel bans and passport seizure, freeze personal bank accounts, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution leading to imprisonment.
Shell Company / False Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Failure to satisfy the ESR substance test or to file the ESR report triggers fines of up to OMR 10,000 per year; the Ministry can suspend or strike off the CR, and the OTA can estimate profits by deemed assessment with a 15% tax on an uplifted deemed income base; bank accounts are typically frozen under AML de-risking.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell SPC face personal civil liability for debts of the company under veil-piercing theories, director disqualification orders, and potential personal assessment where assets were stripped; combined with travel bans, the practical effect is inability to leave or re-enter the jurisdiction until all liabilities are settled.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The OTA may disregard the taxpayer's pricing, substitute its own arm's-length value (often a higher benchmark OMR per tonne), and impose an upward adjustment on taxable income; an additional penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax is applied; documentation penalties apply separately (up to OMR 2,000 per return).

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false TP declarations are exposed to derivative claims from co-shareholders, joint liability for the additional tax and penalty where group pricing was directed by them, and potential disqualification from serving as directors of any Omani entity.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering (Medium-Risk Sector)

Statutory sanction: Under Oman's AML Law (Royal Decree 30/2016), penalties for money laundering include imprisonment of up to 10 years and fines of up to OMR 100,000, plus confiscation of the laundered assets and the underlying cargo; trade sanctions violations are handled by the National Committee with asset freezes and referral to the Public Prosecutor.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who know of or negligently facilitate trade-based laundering are personally criminally liable; the authorities may impose travel bans, freeze personal and corporate accounts, and prosecute the board as co-conspirators; directors' assets used to launder criminal proceeds are seizure targets.
