Governing statute

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Sultanate of Oman Tax Authority (OTA); official e-services portal: https://taxoman.gov.om

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Single Person Company (SPC) with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion and obtain the Commercial Registration certificate. Step 2: Register with the Oman Tax Authority through the Tax e-Services portal (taxoman.gov.om) by submitting the Commercial Registration, Articles of Association, passport or Civil ID of the sole foreign shareholder/manager, registered office lease evidence, bank account details, and board-appointment documentation. Step 3: Receive the Oman Tax Identification Number (TIN) and Tax Card. Step 4: Separately file VAT registration through the same OTA portal once the statutory turnover threshold is met or voluntarily elected; obtain a distinct VAT registration number. A foreign-owned SPC is not required to maintain a physical Omani premises for tax ID issuance beyond an acceptable registered address and local representative/agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The SPC is a non-transparent Omani juridical person subject to Oman income tax at entity level on worldwide income; no pass-through or fiscal transparency regime is available.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election is available under Oman law. The SPC may elect to form a VAT tax group with related Omani residents for VAT purposes if control and place-of-establishment conditions are satisfied; consolidated corporate income tax filing is permitted only for wholly-owned Oman-incorporated subsidiaries under restrictive group relief conditions, and such elections are binding for the relevant tax period.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration applies when annual taxable supplies equal or exceed OMR 500,000; voluntary registration is available from OMR 250,000. Imports of goods into Oman attract VAT regardless of the registration threshold. Non-established digital/software service suppliers making taxable supplies to Omani consumers must register and charge VAT if the annual turnover threshold is exceeded; otherwise the Omani recipient accounts for VAT under reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies to: (i) goods physically exported from Oman outside GCC territory, with export shipment completed within the statutory window and supported by transport/export evidence; (ii) services directly and exclusively related to goods exported outside Oman; (iii) services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside Oman at the time of supply and where the service is not consumed or used in Oman; and (iv) international transport of goods or passengers and ancillary services. The supplier must retain contemporaneous commercial, customs, transport, payment, and destination evidence before the zero rate may be applied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Oman Customs Bayan single-window declaration)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / carrier-receipted transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT/FX repatriation advice
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Certificate of origin and any applicable export permit

Oman does not currently operate a mandatory real-time e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping system for private-sector VAT invoices. Taxpayers must issue VAT invoices containing all statutory fields, retain full invoice records, and file VAT returns through the Tax e-Services portal. No API-based fiscal clearance mandate or electronic-stamping requirement is presently in force, although the OTA has announced phased preparatory work toward future e-invoicing standardization.

On audit, the Oman Tax Authority may disallow the 0% rate, reassess VAT at the standard 5%, and issue late-payment penalties and interest. Fraudulent zero-rating or false export documentation can trigger statutory evasion penalties up to triple the assessed tax, criminal fines, imprisonment under the VAT Law, personal liability of the SPC licensee/member, immediate clawback of any refunded VAT, and interest recalculated from the original refund date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman is an IMF Article VIII member and maintains a de facto open capital account. The Omani Rial (OMR) is pegged to the US dollar at 1 USD = 0.3845 OMR. Current account transactions, including software licence fees and remote support subscriptions, are fully convertible without CBO prior approval. Capital account operations are equally liberalised for foreign-owned SPCs; however, banks enforce verification of source of funds, commercial registration, and corporate approvals. Profit remittance abroad is permitted with no profit or dividend surrender requirement, but requires no outstanding tax clearance from the Omani Tax Authority and completion of normal bank KYC diligence. No discriminatory restrictions exist on non-resident ownership, and no aggregate foreign ownership cap applies under the Foreign Investment Law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose a statutory surrender obligation or a mandatory conversion ratio on export receipts. Export proceeds must be collected through both onshore and offshore clearing, routed through a licensed bank in Oman, and credited to the SPC's operational account. CBO regulations require all export/income invoices to be supported by customs documentation, service contracts, or a tax invoice, and the funds must be repatriated within a commercially reasonable period consistent with normal payment terms, typically 90 days, but no statutorily fixed surrender timeline exists. No percentage of foreign currency must be sold to the CBO.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), Foreign Exchange Department

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-Low viability. CBO does not grant standalone Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licences to offshore digital wallets; thus Stripe and Paddle can be used only as inward merchant collection channels backed by a licensed Oman bank. An offshore EMI cannot disburse OMR, cannot clear Omani RTGS, and cannot operate as a treasury settlement account. To remain compliant, the SPC must maintain a primary CBO-supervised bank account and use the EMI rails strictly as a customer billing portal.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (headline statutory rate under Royal Decree 28/2023; 3% preferential rate applies only to taxpayers with annual gross revenue not exceeding OMR 100,000; the first OMR 10,000 of taxable income is effectively exempt for most corporate taxpayers)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding tax on gross dividends paid by an Omani resident SPC to a non-resident shareholder, deducted at source at the time of distribution under Royal Decree 28/2023; distributions to resident natural persons are subject to final individual income tax rather than an additional SPC-level withholding

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 500,000 annual aggregate related-party controlled transactions triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File and Master File preparation under Ministerial Decision 57/2020; groups with consolidated group revenue of OMR 75 million or more must further file a Country-by-Country report; transactions below the threshold require at least a documented economic and pricing memo in the SPC's tax file

Economic substance scope: Oman does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to the UAE, but Royal Decree 28/2023 codifies an economic-substance and anti-artificial-arrangement doctrine: the SPC must demonstrate genuine commercial activity in Oman, including local remote-support staff, a physical monitoring/telemetry center, local decision-making, and Omani books and records; treaty claims, the 3% preferential band, and tax residency all depend on the place of effective management being in Oman; the OTA can re-characterize a shell arrangement and deny the intended tax benefit

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Understatement

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years and/or fines of up to OMR 25,000 at the court's discretion; the OTA simultaneously raises a civil assessment at 15% with a 3% monthly late-payment surcharge capped at 50%, denies all related deductions, and may freeze bank accounts pending resolution

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the SPC acts as the sole shareholder's alter ego; directors and the manager become jointly and severally liable for unpaid CIT; the OTA issues travel bans for outstanding tax debts above OMR 10,000 and may attach personal assets in evasion cases
Shell Company and Artificial Arrangement / General Anti-Abuse Rule

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of the structure and reallocation of income by the OTA; immediate loss of treaty relief and of the 3% band; additional tax at 15% plus a 3% monthly surcharge; administrative penalties between OMR 5,000 and OMR 20,000 for false representation; commercial strike-off and CR cancellation of the shell

Director personal liability: Limited liability is denied where the SPC is a formal cover; civil courts allow piercing against the sole shareholder/director for unremitted withholding tax, unpaid CIT, and contractual liabilities arising from the artificial arrangement; a trading ban or travel ban may be imposed during investigation
AML and Beneficial Ownership Disclosure Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed administrative fine of up to OMR 10,000 for beneficial-ownership non-disclosure; AML violations escalate to fines of up to OMR 50,000, revocation of tax clearance, and confiscation of proceeds; severe cases are prosecuted as criminal money laundering carrying imprisonment of 2 to 7 years

Director personal liability: Directors, the compliance officer, and the natural-person controller are personally liable for failure to prevent money laundering; asset freezes, travel bans, criminal fines, and disqualification from managing companies apply at the individual level even where the SPC itself is the registered entity
Transfer Pricing Documentation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The OTA substitutes an arm's-length result, raising the taxable base at 15% and withdrawing the 3% band; penalty interest of 3% per month applies on the additional tax; documentation deficiency fines reach OMR 20,000; the taxpayer is placed on an elevated audit watchlist

Director personal liability: No personal liability arises where documentation is robust and contemporaneous; however, deliberate absence of or falsified documentation constitutes gross negligence, making the SPC director personally liable for the secondary penalty and potentially disqualifying the individual from board positions for up to 5 years
Withholding Agent Failure on Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent becomes personally obligated to pay the shortfall to the OTA, plus a penalty of up to 100% of the unwithheld amount; a 3% monthly surcharge runs on the outstanding WHT; the SPC's tax clearance certificate is withheld, blocking all related commercial renewals and owner repatriations

Director personal liability: The SPC manager and sole director are treated as joint debtors for the WHT as the withholding representative; civil enforcement is available against personal bank accounts; travel bans are issued for outstanding sums; intentional non-deduction triggers criminal prosecution and personal civil restitution
