Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Single Person Company under Royal Decree No. 18/2019; the registered capital is fixed by the sole member in the constitutive contract. If declared capital is adopted, the full subscription amount must be deposited into an Oman-licensed bank escrow or current account opened in the company's name, and the bank deposit certificate must be attached to the Commercial Registry filing before issuance of the final registration certificate.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office must satisfy Royal Oman Police Civil Defence requirements, including fire extinguishers, emergency exit signage, smoke alarms, and a Civil Defence fire safety certificate; structural safety is confirmed through the landlord's building completion certificate referenced in the lease, and occupational safety clearance for the office workplace must be maintained under Ministry of Labour workplace rules.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), the national revenue authority for corporate income tax, withholding tax, and VAT; primary e-service portal: https://taxoman.gov.om (including the OTA Tax Management System and VAT e-registration/returns interfaces). Incorporated SPCs are additionally registered with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) as the commercial-registry prerequisite.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Omani SPC must, in order: (1) incorporate under the Oman Companies Law and obtain the MoCIIP Commercial Registration (CR) certificate and SPC constitutive documents; (2) register for corporate income tax with the OTA through the e-Services portal (directly or via a licensed tax agent), submitting the CR, certified Arabic/English Articles, passport copies of the sole shareholder and manager, registered-address/lease evidence, bank account details, anticipated activity description, and an email/EGS account for OTA correspondence; (3) receive the OTA Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the annual Tax Card/Certificate of Registration before commencing activity; (4) notify the OTA within the statutory period of any change to shareholder, manager, address, or scope of activity; and (5) complete VAT registration separately and only when annual taxable supplies reach OMR 500,000 (mandatory) or decide to register voluntarily at OMR 50,000. A complete application is usually processed within 15 business days; late or incorrect registration exposes the SPC to fixed penalties and prevents issuance of valid VAT invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Omani SPC must, in order: (1) incorporate under the Oman Companies Law and obtain the MoCIIP Commercial Registration (CR) certificate and SPC constitutive documents; (2) register for corporate income tax with the OTA through the e-Services portal (directly or via a licensed tax agent), submitting the CR, certified Arabic/English Articles, passport copies of the sole shareholder and manager, registered-address/lease evidence, bank account details, anticipated activity description, and an email/EGS account for OTA correspondence; (3) receive the OTA Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the annual Tax Card/Certificate of Registration before commencing activity; (4) notify the OTA within the statutory period of any change to shareholder, manager, address, or scope of activity; and (5) complete VAT registration separately and only when annual taxable supplies reach OMR 500,000 (mandatory) or decide to register voluntarily at OMR 50,000. A complete application is usually processed within 15 business days; late or incorrect registration exposes the SPC to fixed penalties and prevents issuance of valid VAT invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Oman has no 'check-the-box' or pass-through election; a Single Person Company (SPC) is a resident juridical person taxed at entity level on worldwide taxable income under the Corporate Income Tax Law (Royal Decree No. 9/2017). Available statutory mechanisms include: the tax group/fiscal-unity election for resident companies under common ownership (85% or more direct/indirect) that file as a single taxable group with OTA approval; the election of the SPC's accounting year and consistent depreciation/capital-allowance pooling methods; the election to compute taxable income on an accrual basis; and the carry-forward of tax losses for a maximum of 5 years within the statutory continuity-of-activity conditions. Oman has adopted the OECD Multilateral Instrument, but domestic law does not provide a hybrid-mismatch or hybrid-entity election; cross-border hybrid arrangements are instead constrained by the general anti-avoidance rule and treaty-limitation-of-benefits principles.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 500,000 of annual taxable supplies and imports in any consecutive 12-month period (mandatory registration); OMR 50,000 of annual taxable supplies (voluntary registration threshold); a non-resident supplier making taxable supplies in Oman may be required to register without regard to the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of goods physically removed from Oman to a place outside the implementing GCC States and to services supplied to a non-resident recipient established outside Oman/GCC where the place of supply and the place of benefit are outside Oman. For the ESG climate/carbon audit and offset-brokerage activity, eligibility requires: (i) a written contract with a non-resident client identifying the services and foreign place of use; (ii) delivery of the audit report/carbon-assurance deliverables and broker confirmations to the foreign recipient; (iii) the fees are invoiced to the foreign entity and settled from abroad in foreign currency; and (iv) the services are not performed or consumed in relation to immovable property or goods located in Oman. Fee-based carbon-accounting/audit services are zero-rated; however, carbon-offset brokerage margins can be classified as exempt financial services under the VAT law, in which case input VAT is non-recoverable. The SPC should separate audit and brokerage engagements contractually and obtain a formal OTA ruling where the brokerage element is material. All zero-rated transactions must nevertheless be disclosed in the VAT return with the applicable export evidence retained for not less than 5 years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Oman Customs electronic declaration output) evidencing the physical export of goods
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or multimodal transport document evidencing movement from Oman to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Omani bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the export consideration in foreign currency
  • Signed export contract, commercial invoice, and detailed scope-of-work/services agreement
  • Foreign recipient tax existence certificate / VAT registration certificate issued outside the GCC implementing States
  • Deliverable evidence: ESG/carbon audit report, assurance statement, and offset-broker transaction confirmations addressed to the foreign client

The OTA is implementing a phased mandatory e-invoicing and fiscal-data-clearance mandate for VAT-registered businesses. In-scope taxpayers must generate structured e-invoices and credit notes in the OTA-specified format (XML/UBL), apply a digital signature/unique identifier per invoice, and transmit datasets to the OTA in near real-time via secured APIs for validation/clearance; once a phase is effective, only digitally cleared invoices entitle the customer to input VAT deduction. Phase I applies to large/priority taxpayers, with subsequent phases covering smaller VAT registrants. Taxpayers not yet in the active phase must issue statutory VAT invoices containing all Arabic/English mandatory fields, retain machine-readable records, and reconcile export zero-rated sales to OTA audit queries.

The OTA may reopen a VAT period for audit within 5 years. Export zero-rating is accepted only if the complete customs-clearance, transport, contract, and foreign-currency receipt documentation is on file; any missing or inconsistent document recharacterizes the supply as standard-rated, triggering 5% output VAT, disallowance of associated input VAT, and administrative penalties of 25%-50% of the understated tax plus monthly late-payment interest at 1%. Fraudulent export claims (false customs declarations, backdated or fictitious invoices, sham remittances, shell foreign recipients, or collusive offset schemes) attract aggravated penalties up to 200% of the evaded tax, fines up to OMR 100,000, imprisonment of responsible persons, suspension/withdrawal of VAT registration, loss of e-invoicing clearance privileges, and public blacklisting by the OTA.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. The CBO maintains a de jure currency peg to the US dollar at 1 USD = 0.3845 OMR, with a quoted trading band of +/-0.0147 OMR per USD. No exchange control license is required for inward FDI equity subscriptions, current international trade settlements, debt servicing, or profit repatriation. The only substantive hurdle is documentary and tax clearance: a foreign-owned Single Person Company must be in full compliance with Oman Corporate Income Tax (tax clearance certificate issued by the Oman Tax Authority), and payments routed through licensed banks must include underlying commercial or contractual documentation for AML purposes. There are no general restrictions on converting OMR into USD or transferring dividends abroad, but banks may enforce de minimis anti-money-laundering limits on cash-based remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirements on export proceeds. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign-currency export receipts in duly licensed bank accounts within Oman or offshore accounts opened with CBO-approved banks. The Export Proceeds Retention regime permits direct crediting to corporate accounts, with no due-date conversion obligation. However, all export receipts must be traceable to commercial invoices and subject to CBO reporting thresholds for balance-of-payments statistical compilation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Offshore digital EMIs and virtual-account providers are not recognized as licensed banks in Oman under the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000) and the CBO's Payment Service Provider Regulation. Such providers cannot accept deposits, open current accounts, or provide local clearing access in OMR. Cross-border monetary processing through an offshore EMI may be allowed only as a payment gateway for ancillary fees, but it cannot serve as the principal treasury rail for equity capital, trade disbursements, or payroll. Any licensed fintech activity requires a CBO Payment Service Provider licence or a banking agent arrangement with a tier-1 local commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward direct investment equity subscriptions must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent bank directly to the SPC's OMR or foreign-currency account at a CBO-licensed commercial bank. The payment instruction should reference the SPC's Commercial Registration number, the legal basis (share capital contribution under the Oman Companies Law), and the statement 'Direct investment – equity subscription' in the remittance information. Oman does not maintain a mandatory national purpose-code list, but the receiving bank will map the remittance to the CBO's balance-of-payments direct-investment classification, typically 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital'. For any capital injection above bank internal thresholds, the bank will require a board resolution approving the capital call, the SPC's CR and Articles, and a copy of the FDI notification filed with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (if applicable).Outbound payments for trade settlements are executed via SWIFT MT103 (telegraphic transfer) for open account transactions and via SWIFT MT700 (documentary letter of credit) for LC-based imports or exports. All outward wires must be supported by underlying invoices, purchase orders, and relevant customs/import evidence where available. Payments in USD and EUR clear through correspondent banking networks (primarily US and European respondent banks); OMR-denominated domestic disbursements clear through the CBO's RTGS (Oman Real Time Gross Settlement) system. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return and settlement are due no later than the statutorily prescribed filing date, in practice by 30 April following the fiscal year-end for calendar-year taxpayers unless the Oman Tax Authority grants a formal extension. The fiscal year is normally 1 January to 31 December; a different fiscal year may be adopted only with prior approval and must be consistently applied.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Oman Local File and Master File obligations are triggered when aggregate related-party controlled transactions exceed OMR 1,000,000 in a tax year; Master File and Country-by-Country Report obligations arise when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds OMR 75,000,000. The Oman Tax Authority may lower these thresholds on notice or by audit determination.

Economic substance scope: Oman has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to UAE Cabinet Resolution 31/2019, but the Income Tax Law's general anti-abuse rules, substance-over-form doctrine, and the OECD BEPS minimum standards effectively require an SPC to maintain real board decision-making, active management, personnel, business facilities, and risk-carrying functions in Oman. Carbon offset brokerage models that rely on offshore intermediaries without local commercial substance risk Lease-Tax rejection of treaty benefits and denial of expense deductions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Additional tax equal to the tax evaded, administrative fines up to 25% of underpaid tax, monthly late-payment surcharges, and criminal prosecution under the Oman Income Tax Law and Penal Code. Conviction may result in imprisonment of up to three years and fines not less than OMR 1,000 and not more than OMR 50,000, with mandatory disqualification from government contracts.

Director personal liability: Where fraud is proved, the court may pierce the SPC's corporate veil and the sole shareholder/director becomes jointly and severally liable for all unpaid tax; travel restriction orders, asset seizure and confiscation of unlawful gains apply.
Transfer Pricing and Artificial Shell Arrangements

Statutory sanction: The Oman Tax Authority may reprice the controlled transaction to arm's length, disallow excessive deductions, tax the SPC on 15% of revised taxable income, impose penalties up to 25% of the underpaid tax, and charge late-payment interest. A deeming may also create a permanent establishment of the foreign parent in Oman.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's length charges and stripped-profit arrangements may be joined as collection defendants when the SPC is found to be an artificial conduit; court-ordered restitution, directors' disqualification, travel bans and, in cases of fraudulent enrichment, imprisonment up to five years may be imposed.
AML, Beneficial Ownership and Shell Company Misuse

Statutory sanction: Under Oman AML Law (Royal Decree 30/2016) and the Commercial Companies Law obligations, sanctions include monetary penalties of up to OMR 50,000, revocation of the Commercial Registration licence, confiscation of funds linked to illegal offsets, and criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to five years for money laundering or financing of prohibited activities.

Director personal liability: The single shareholder, managers and compliance officers face personal fines and imprisonment. Beneficial owners using the SPC to conceal carbon assets risk civil asset forfeiture; directors may be personally ordered to reverse dividends or payments made without substance.
Repatriation and Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: The Omani payer SPC is liable for the un-withheld 10% tax, interest, and penalties up to 25% of the amount; the Tax Authority may additionally treat the payment as non-deductible. Intentional misclassification is criminally sanctioned as tax evasion with fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve unlawful payments and foreign shareholders receiving tainted distributions can be held personally liable for withholding deficiencies; asset seizure, bank account freezes, and travel bans are available enforcement tools.
ESG / Carbon Certification Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines and licence revocation under Omani standards bodies and anti-fraud laws; any tax deduction derived from a sham carbon purchase is disallowed, and penalties plus interest are imposed on the recomputed tax liability; criminal fraud sanctions apply where the misrepresentation causes investor loss.

Director personal liability: The director who signs the audit opinion or trades fraudulent offsets bears personal civil liability to affected counterparties. Oman courts may award compensation, impose travel bans, and refer the matter for fraud prosecution with potential imprisonment.
