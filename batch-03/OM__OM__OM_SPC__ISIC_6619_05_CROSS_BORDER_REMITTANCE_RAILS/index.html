Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for SPCs under Royal Decree 18/2019; the sole shareholder must subscribe capital sufficient for the company's stated objects and deposit it into an Omani bank account. The bank's capital deposit certificate is filed at incorporation, and the funds remain escrowed/unavailable for withdrawal until the Commercial Registration is issued. For a cross-border remittance agency, the Central Bank of Oman's separate paid-up capital floor is assessed at the Stage 3 licensing step.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Secretariat General of Taxation (SGT) of the Ministry of Finance, Sultanate of Oman — the successor to the Oman Tax Authority and the sole national competent authority for Corporate Income Tax, Value Added Tax, Excise and related fiscal procedures; official e-services and electronic filing are administered through the Oman Tax e-Portal (public and taxpayer access at https://taxoman.gov.om with authenticated portal links published by the SGT).

E-government filing portal

A 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC / single-member LLC) obtains its statutory TIN and VAT ID as follows: (1) Incorporate before the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) through the national e-Invest platform and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate; the SPC must also obtain the Central Bank of Oman (CBO) Money Exchange / Cross-Border Remittance Service License as a precondition to lawful operation. (2) File corporate income tax registration on the SGT e-Tax portal within three months from commencement of business, using Form CIT-1 with the CR, constitutional deed, manager appointment resolution, CBO license, and opening financial statements; the SGT issues a permanent Tax Identification Number (TIN) electronically. (3) For VAT, apply within 30 days of the end of the month in which taxable supplies reach or are expected to reach OMR 500,000 in any 12-month period, using Form VAT-1 with turnover schedules and supporting evidence of intended zero-rated supplies; a separate VAT registration ID is issued under the same TIN. (4) Complete ancillary registrations: Public Authority for Social Insurance (PASI) registration for employees, municipal licence renewal, and appointment of a resident representative and a licensed tax agent/practitioner for all SGT filings and service of process. Non-resident branch structures register on the same portal as non-resident taxpayers and file solely on Oman-source income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Omani SPC is a resident juridical person taxable at entity level; no pass-through or transparency applies. The SPC is subject to Corporate Income Tax at a flat 15% on worldwide income, with a foreign tax credit available for foreign-source income; dividend distributions to a foreign corporate shareholder attract 10% withholding tax unless reduced by an applicable double tax treaty; non-resident persons and branches are within the territorial scope of Oman tax only in respect of Oman-source income.

Available election forms

Oman does not operate any check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch classification election; tax classification is determined by the statutory legal form under the Commercial Companies Law and the Income Tax Law. Available elections and mechanisms include: (a) tax-group or consolidated filing election for Oman-resident companies in which the SPC holds at least 75% of the share capital; (b) carryforward election for tax losses up to five years subject to continuity-of-business conditions; (c) advance pricing agreement (APA) requests with the SGT for transfer pricing certainty; (d) VAT input-tax recovery method election between the standard pro-rata method and sector-specific special methods for mixed exempt/taxable remittance supply chains; and (e) discretionary election to apply treaty relief on cross-border dividend, interest and royalty flows.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 500,000 of taxable supplies and taxable imports (net of exempt supplies) in any rolling 12-month period, or expected to exceed that amount in the next 12 months. Voluntary registration: OMR 187,500. Anti-fragmentation rules aggregate related natural and legal persons as a single taxable person; persons making exclusively exempt supplies may apply for a special registration exception.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAT Law and Executive Regulations, zero-rating applies to: (i) exports of goods physically leaving Oman within statutory time limits under a valid customs export declaration, with title transferred to a non-resident; (ii) international transport of goods and passengers and directly related services (including loading, transhipment and handling performed in Oman); and (iii) services supplied to a non-resident customer where the place of supply is outside Oman under the cross-border place-of-supply rules and the service is not considered supplied in the Sultanate. A remittance SPC's core fee income — money-transfer commissions, FX margins, SWIFT, clearing and cash-pickup agent charges — constitutes an exempt financial service, not a zero-rated export, and therefore blocks input VAT recovery on attributable costs. Zero-rating relief may be asserted only for a separately contracted, genuinely non-financial agency or infrastructure service (for example correspondent-bank processing or system management) supplied to a non-resident principal for use and enjoyment wholly outside Oman; such a position must be supported by contemporaneous written contracts, AML/KYC records, SWIFT/correspondent counterparty evidence and ideally advance SGT confirmation.

  • Omani Customs Electronic Export Declaration (Bayan) bearing the SGT VAT reference and customs goods classification entries
  • Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill / Air Waybill or Combined Transport Document evidencing physical international carriage of the goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103/MT202 and MT950 account records proving actual collection of export proceeds through an authorized Omani bank
  • Commercial Export Invoice, packing list, insurance certificate where applicable, and GCC Certificate of Origin
  • For service exports: executed correspondent/agency agreement with the non-resident principal, CBO money exchange or remittance licence, operational service logs, KYC/AML records, place-of-performance evidence, and the cleared VAT e-invoice in XML/QR format
  • General-ledger reconciliation and accounts-receivable ageing schedule demonstrating booking of proceeds to the SPC and repatriation within the statutory deadline

SGT has announced and is phasing in a mandatory e-invoicing and real-time fiscal clearance framework, beginning with high-revenue VAT filers and extending thereafter to all VAT-registered persons. The mandate requires issuance of structured electronic tax invoices and credit notes in the prescribed national XML/JSON data model, real-time or near-real-time API transmission to the SGT national clearance and validation node, assignment of a unique invoice number, application of a digital stamp/QR code before issuance to the customer, and secure archival of cleared e-invoices; only cleared electronic invoices are admissible to support input VAT credits. Until the binding go-live of the clearing platform, compliant VAT-format tax invoices must be generated by VAT-capable billing software and retained electronically.

Zero-rating and refund claims are subject to SGT risk-based post-clearance audit with an extended limitation period in fraud cases. Deficient, inconsistent or unwarranted documentation results in immediate VAT reassessment at 5% on the gross export billing, denial or clawback of the refund, statutory late-payment interest, administrative penalties under the Unified Tax Violations and Procedures Law (RD 61/2021) of up to 200% of the tax evaded, suspension of e-invoicing clearance privileges, and public publication of the violation. Where false, backdated or forged customs or FX documents are used to obtain a zero-rating benefit, the conduct constitutes tax evasion and criminal fraud: imprisonment for up to three years and fines of up to OMR 50,000 may be imposed, together with CBO/CFSA anti-money-laundering sanctions and revocation of the remittance licence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction with no exchange-control restrictions on current international transactions. The Omani Rial is freely convertible through CBO-licensed banks and exchange houses under a fixed USD peg at approximately 1 USD = 0.3845 OMR. Profit remittances, dividends, loan repayments, and capital repatriation by a foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) are lawful without CBO pre-approval, although banks apply standard CDD, AML/CFT, tax clearance, and documentation checks. Capital-account restrictions are limited to sectoral licensing rather than currency convertibility; all transactions must be routed through licensed financial institutions for sanctions screening and statistical reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rule applies in Oman. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or converted at the exporter's discretion; CBO imposes no mandatory conversion ratio into OMR and no statutory repatriation timeline for export proceeds. The only mandatory obligations are accurate commercial documentation, reporting of inward/outward remittances through licensed banks, and compliance with AML/CFT and sanctions obligations before settlement.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Standalone offshore digital EMI feasibility: Low. A foreign EMI without a CBO banking or money-exchange/payment-service licence cannot join Oman's clearing rails or accept customer funds locally. Legal viability becomes Medium only through locally incorporated Oman SPC structures using a licensed domestic bank or exchange house under a banking-as-a-service or white-label arrangement; the domestic entity must hold the CBO licence, maintain an in-country physical presence, appoint an MLRO, and execute full AML/CFT controls. Remote-only offshore EMIs are effectively barred from direct market access.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital for the Oman SPC should be routed through SWIFT MT103 with field 70 referencing 'Equity Subscription — OM SPC Commercial Register No.'; large correspondent payments may additionally use MT202 COV for interbank cover. Funds must land in a CBO-licensed bank in a dedicated capital escrow/current account opened by the SPC, with the Commercial Register (CR) number and trade licence cited. The receiving bank will perform CDD, verify the sole shareholder's resolution and capital allocation schedule, confirm source of funds, and report the credit to the CBO's statistical and AML monitoring frameworks. No third-party or unlicensed account may receive the capital injection.Outbound settlement uses SWIFT MT103 for direct commercial payments when no trade finance instrument is required; irrevocable documentary LCs are issued/amended through MT700/MT707 under UCP600; correspondent reimbursement is executed through MT202/MT202 COV. Local OMR payouts are cleared through CBO-operated electronic clearing and RTGS equivalents, while cross-border USD/INR/PKR/BDT corridors are settled via correspondent nostros pre-screened for CBO and OFAC sanctions. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 30,000: mandatory transfer-pricing declaration and contemporaneous controlled-transaction schedule to be submitted with the annual tax return. OMR 3,000,000: mandatory detailed Local File for aggregate related-party controlled transactions. OMR 30,000,000: group consolidated revenue threshold triggering preparation of a Master File.

Economic substance scope: Oman has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations law equivalent to the UAE. Nevertheless, the OTA applies place-of-effective-management, benefit-of-treaty anti-abuse, and GAAR principles under Royal Decree 9/2017. The licensed remittance agency is separately required by the Central Bank of Oman (CBO) to maintain physical office premises, operational personnel, senior management presence in Oman, board/management decision-making in Oman, and full AML/CFT substance. Non-compliance enables the OTA to recharacterize income, deny DTA benefits, and impose fines under the CBO license conditions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution before the Oman courts; fine up to OMR 100,000 under the Income Tax Law Article 42; confiscation of proceeds; recovery of evaded tax plus a penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded; cancellation of the tax clearance certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors and the SPC manager may be held jointly and severally liable for evaded tax and penalties. A criminal conviction can result in imprisonment of up to five years, asset freezing, and a travel ban against the responsible officers.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Cancellation of the commercial registration and CBO license; re-characterization of the Oman SPC as a permanent establishment of the foreign parent; 15% corporate income tax on deemed profit share; denial of treaty benefits; revocation of the payment-service license.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally assessed for unpaid taxes under veil-piercing rules; senior officers face administrative disqualification, travel bans, and criminal liability for operating an unlicensed remittance business under the Oman Financial Services Law.
AML/CFT Deficiency in Remittance Rails

Statutory sanction: CBO enforcement action with fines up to OMR 1,000,000; confiscation of illicitly transferred funds; suspension or revocation of the remittance license; criminal prosecution under the Oman Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Financing of Terrorism Law (Royal Decree 30/2016).

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal liability for money-laundering facilitation. Sanctions include imprisonment of up to ten years, substantial personal fines, asset freezing, and long-term travel bans.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration and Improper Cross-Border Fee Stripping

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment to the arm's-length amount; additional corporate tax at 15%; late-payment charge of 2% per month; penalty up to 100% of the under-declared tax; rejection of the TP disclosure and imposition of an OTA best-of-judgment assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false TP documentation can be held personally liable for the additional tax and functional audit penalties. In aggravated cases, criminal fraud charges apply and can lead to imprisonment and director disqualification.
Failure to File / Chronic Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Increased fixed penalties, daily surcharges, a 2% monthly late-payment levy capped at 60% of the tax due, forced collection, seizure of bank accounts, revocation of tax clearance, and publication of the defaulting taxpayer's name by the OTA.

Director personal liability: Where the failure is knowingly caused, directors may be personally liable for the corporate tax debts and face a civil recovery order, travel restrictions, and disqualification from serving as a director or manager in Oman.
