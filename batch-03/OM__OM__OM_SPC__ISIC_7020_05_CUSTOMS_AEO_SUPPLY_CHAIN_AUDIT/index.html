Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), Muscat, Sultanate of Oman; statutory administrator of Corporate Income Tax (CIT) under Royal Decree 28/2009, as amended, and of VAT under Royal Decree 121/2020; e-filing and registration portal: taxoman.gov.om (OTA e-Services). Customs competence for export clearance rests with the Royal Oman Police – Customs Directorate.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) through the InvestEasy portal and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate; 2) Within the statutory 90-day window from CR issuance or commencement of activity, submit the CIT registration application to OTA via taxoman.gov.om, attaching the CR, constitutional documents (SIA/Articles), lease/address proof, and valid foreign ownership documentation; 3) Upon approval, obtain the Tax Identification Number (TIN/TRN) and the OTA e-services electronic profile; 4) Register separately for VAT if actual or expected taxable supplies exceed OMR 38,500 (mandatory) or OMR 19,250 (voluntary) over any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers performing taxable supplies in Oman must register through an OTA-approved tax representative unless the recipient's reverse-charge mechanism applies; 5) Open an Omani bank account and file bank account declaration details with OTA; 6) For AEO-related business scope, apply separately for Customs registration and Authorized Economic Operator certification with the Royal Oman Police – Customs, and obtain the fiscal digital-signature credentials required for electronic declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the Omani SPC is a legal tax resident of Oman by virtue of incorporation, subject to CIT at the entity level on worldwide income, with no pass-through or transparent treatment available for foreign shareholders; standard CIT rate is 15% on taxable income above OMR 100,000 and 3% on taxable income up to OMR 100,000; foreign permanent establishment income of an Omani resident company may be exempt from Omani CIT where the foreign-source income has been subject to tax at a rate of at least 15% in the foreign jurisdiction; Omani taxation is unitary at the national level with no sub-national corporate or municipal income taxes; a standard Muscat-registered SPC is not located in a designated free-zone or special economic zone, and therefore does not benefit from the CIT holidays, customs duty suspension, or VAT deferral regimes available in Salalah Free Zone, Sohar Free Zone, Duqm SEZ, or Al Mazunah FEZ.

Available election forms

No check-the-box entity classification election or separate hybrid-mismatch entity regime exists under Omani tax law; the SPC is compulsorily within the corporate income tax net. Statutory elections available to an eligible SPC include: (i) voluntary VAT registration where taxable turnover is between OMR 19,250 and OMR 38,500; (ii) depreciation method election for CIT purposes (straight-line or written-down value) applied consistently to fixed assets; (iii) unilateral foreign tax credit election in lieu of the foreign permanent establishment income exemption for highly-taxed foreign-source income; (iv) election to carry forward tax losses for up to five years; and (v) election for cash-basis accounting where OTA qualifying small-taxpayer criteria are met. Oman has not yet enacted comprehensive OECD Pillar Two or CFC legislation; hybrid mismatch effects are therefore limited to the domestic general anti-avoidance rule and transfer pricing provisions under the CIT law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration threshold: OMR 38,500 (approximately USD 100,000) of cumulative taxable supplies or taxable expenses subject to reverse charge over any rolling 12-month period; Voluntary registration threshold: OMR 19,250 (approximately USD 50,000); non-resident suppliers carrying out taxable activities in Oman have no de minimis exemption and must register (or be registered through a tax representative) unless the reverse-charge mechanism shifts the obligation to the Omani recipient. No separate registration threshold applies to the reverse-charge importation of services, which is triggered regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to: (a) direct export of goods from Oman to a destination outside the GCC/economic territory, provided the goods physically depart Oman within the statutory period (generally within 90 days of the supply date or within an extended period approved by OTA), and the supplier is the legal consignor stated in the customs export declaration; (b) services directly and exclusively related to exported goods or to the export process, including pre-export inspection, packing, handling, freight, loading, and commission services up to the point of departure; (c) international transport of goods or passengers from Oman to a destination outside Oman and ancillary services directly connected to such transport; (d) supplies (including auditing and certification services) supplied to a non-resident where the place of supply is deemed outside Oman under the B2B place-of-supply rules; and (e) supplies of goods to licensed free-zone businesses for direct or onward export where the free-zone operator holds the goods in bond. For the AEO supply-chain audit business, zero-rating eligibility attaches to certification and audit services supplied to a non-resident client whose audit findings relate to foreign trade chains, provided the services are not effectively used or enjoyed in Oman and the supplier retains evidence of the recipient's non-resident status.

  • Oman Customs Export Declaration (Export Bayan / Single Customs Declaration) bearing the exit validation stamp or electronic customs clearance code
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / International Road Consignment Note (CMR) or multimodal transport document evidencing physical departure from Oman
  • Commercial Invoice and underlying purchase order/contract of the non-resident consignee, showing consistent quantities, unit prices, incoterms, and final destination
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT credit confirmation / credited export proceeds statement) proving receipt of payment in an Omani bank account
  • Packing list, certificate of origin, and, where applicable, inspection certificate, pre-shipment survey report, or AEO security-audit evidence file linked to the customs declaration

Oman is implementing a national e-invoicing and real-time clearance mandate under OTA digital governance: (i) taxable persons above the phased-in threshold must issue invoices through OTA-accredited ERP/e-invoicing software that exposes structured standard-format invoice data (XML/UBL-compatible) via API; (ii) the OTA platform performs real-time fiscal clearance for larger taxpayers, assigning a unique digital fiscal code to each invoice before the invoice is legally valid for input tax credit; (iii) a digital stamp/QR-compatible visual code must appear on the customer copy of every cleared e-invoice and e-receipt; (iv) phased rollout is being sequenced by revenue/turnover bands beginning with large taxpayers from the 2025 mandate window, with a hard cut-off for newly registered taxpayers; (v) standalone offline invoices, hand-written credit notes, and exception-based manual procedures are limited to statutorily defined retail emergencies and failover contingencies; (vi) e-invoicing data retention and archival must align with the 10-year VAT recordkeeping horizon, and access to the cleared fiscal archive must be granted to OTA and Customs upon request, which is especially relevant for AEO-certified audit providers whose deliverables are themselves fiscal evidence.

OTA can audit export zero-rating claims within a general 5-year assessment window, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or willful misstatement, and may retrospectively assess VAT at the 5% rate on improperly zero-rated supplies. Clawback consequences include: (i) assessment of VAT plus late-payment additional tax (generally 1% per month on the unpaid VAT) and a fixed late-payment penalty; (ii) rejection of input VAT recovery on costs attributable to the failed export; (iii) retrospective clawback of any previously granted VAT refund, plus interest; (iv) customs penalties and suspension or revocation of AEO status and Customs release privileges; (v) criminal prosecution for tax evasion in cases of forged export documentation or artificial circling of goods, punishable by imprisonment of up to three years and/or fines of OMR 5,000 to OMR 50,000 per offense; and (vi) for licensed audit professionals, disciplinary action and debarment under the Oman Association of Accountants and Auditors professional rules. Failure to effect foreign-exchange repatriation within the 180-day deadline raises a rebuttable presumption that the export consideration was not genuinely received, triggering full clawback of the zero-rating treatment plus administrative penalties. All export-substantiation files must be retained for at least ten years in line with the VAT recordkeeping requirement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman maintains a fully liberalised exchange and payments regime under the IMF Article VIII status. The Omani Rial (OMR) is pegged to the US Dollar at a central rate of USD 1 = OMR 0.3845 (band ±0.15%). Current account and capital account transactions are generally freely convertible through licensed banks. Profit remittance by a wholly foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) is permitted without prior CBO approval, subject to (i) compliance with Oman Tax Authority withholding regulations (10% dividend withholding unless reduced by tax treaty), and (ii) submission of standard supporting documents by the remitting bank, including audited accounting records or management statements evidencing distributable profits, a board resolution, and a tax absence or clearance certificate for the relevant fiscal period. The CBO reserves powers to request transaction-level data, but imposes no restrictions on conversion for bona fide service-fee and investment returns.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose mandatory export proceeds surrender or a fixed conversion ratio to OMR. Export receipts may be credited directly to a foreign-currency account held with a CBO-licensed bank. The parent obligation under CBO supervision is that all export proceeds must be received into the Omani banking system in a timely manner consistent with the underlying commercial invoice and full repatriation within 180 days of the shipment or service-completion date, unless an extension is obtained due to documentary, commodity, or settlement defaults. No statutory conversion threshold applies. Banks are required to file Customer Foreign Exchange Transaction Reports (CFXTR) with the CBO for any transfer of USD 10,000 or OMR equivalent and above, as part of balance-of-payments and AML/CFT reporting.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, and Currencycloud are not licensed to provide local currency settlement or to open CBO-regulated accounts inside Oman. They may be used only as auxiliary cross-border collection/disbursement channels for non-Oman counterparties; funds must still be ultimately cleared through a CBO-licensed bank for onshore corporate obligations. A locally registered EMI requires a payment-service-provider license from the CBO, which is rarely granted to wholly foreign digital-first firms. For the Omani SPC, an offshore EMI should not be treated as a primary liquidity rail because it creates FX and regulatory settlement gaps for OMR-denominated tax payments, licence renewals, and supplier obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inward equity capital will be routed via SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.003 credit transfer from the foreign parent’s bank, denominated in USD, EUR, GBP, or OMR. The remittance instruction must unmistakably state the Oman SPC’s Commercial Registration number; purpose code: '3.3.1.1 – Direct Investment: Equity Capital' under BOP/IMF direct-investment classification; field 57A/57D: beneficiary bank and branch (Bank Muscat or NBO); field 59: Omani SPC name and physical address; field 70: direct reference 'FDI CONTRIBUTION / OM_SPC ISIC 7020'. On acceptance, the receiving bank executes KYC verification by matching SWIFT originator with the company’s final-UBO records. Additional supporting package for the transfer: Board Resolution of the foreign shareholder authorising the capital injection; notarised copy of the Foreign Capital Investment Law (FCIL) registration; Equity capital deposit certificate from the SPC’s auditor; and, when the sum exceeds OMR 100,000, a CBO corporate foreign-currency declaration. Legal qualification of the SPC's capital recording at the Commercial Registration is performed after the funds land in a capital escrow/object account. No CBO prior approval is required, but the bank must provide a Capital Diversion Form to internally tag the funds as FDIC.Outbound payment rails for the SPC’s supplier payments, subcontract services, licensing, and other trade settlements will be executed through SWIFT MT103/ISO 20022 under CLEARING_HOUSE: SWIFT. For suppliers within Oman, payments use the CBO-operated National Payment System (Miyar) with IBAN-to-IBAN transfer and OCS (Oman Clearing System) via infocash. For cross-border goods-linked disbursements, the preferred instruments are: (a) direct MT103 for regular service payments, (b) sight Commercial Letter of Credit for goods involving warehouse audit equipment or supply-chain technology purchases, and (c) documentary collections when the beneficiary is a vetted partner under high-frequency low-ticket trade. The bank will require the SPC to provide an underlying invoice, transport/insurance/commercial document (if LC), tax invoice for VAT or OMR excise, and condition-of-use declaration. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, and Currencycloud are not licensed to provide local currency settlement or to open CBO-regulated accounts inside Oman. They may be used only as auxiliary cross-border collection/disbursement channels for non-Oman counterparties; funds must still be ultimately cleared through a CBO-licensed bank for onshore corporate obligations. A locally registered EMI requires a payment-service-provider license from the CBO, which is rarely granted to wholly foreign digital-first firms. For the Omani SPC, an offshore EMI should not be treated as a primary liquidity rail because it creates FX and regulatory settlement gaps for OMR-denominated tax payments, licence renewals, and supplier obligations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% for standard corporate taxable income under Royal Decree 9/2017 Article 12; a reduced 3% rate applies only to qualifying small businesses with taxable income not exceeding OMR 100,000, which a 100% foreign-owned SPC does not ordinarily satisfy.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory WHT on dividends paid by an Omani SPC to a non-resident shareholder under Royal Decree 9/2017 Article 64; no WHT applies to dividends paid to resident Omani companies or individuals.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No formal Master/Local File threshold is published for SME taxpayers; a contemporaneous TP documentation file is expected where annual aggregate related-party transactions exceed OMR 100,000. CbCR obligations apply only to MNE groups with consolidated revenue above approximately OMR 375 million, and a TP-1 disclosure is filed with every CIT return.

Economic substance scope: Oman has not enacted a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations law; however, the GAAR in the Income Tax Law, the beneficial ownership clauses in DTAs, and anti-conduit principles permit the Tax Authority to disregard transactions or entities lacking commercial substance. The SPC must demonstrate real presence in Oman through staff payroll, office lease, client contracts, board decisions, and bank accounts maintained in Oman.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to OMR 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years under RD 9/2017; additional liabilities include evaded tax at 15%, a 3% monthly late-payment surcharge up to 100%, and a 25% incorrect-return penalty on the differential.

Director personal liability: An Omani court may pierce the veil where books are destroyed or funds diverted; directors are jointly liable for restitution of the evaded tax, asset forfeiture, travel bans, and a criminal record that prevents future AEO and Customs audit work.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under the GAAR: disallowance of management fees, taxation at 15% on the recharacterized profit, a 25% penalty on the adjusted tax, and denial of treaty benefits and foreign tax credits.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal assessment where the SPC is used as a mere facade; the court may issue travel bans and attach personal real estate; involvement of false government documents triggers criminal imprisonment.
Withholding Tax Failure on Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: The unpaid WHT becomes immediately collectible; a penalty of 25% of the undeducted amount applies, plus 3% monthly surcharge for each month of delay; repeated violations trigger public naming and licence disqualification by the Oman Tax Authority.

Director personal liability: Directors are treated as responsible withholding agents; civil execution may proceed against personal bank accounts for unremitted WHT, and travel bans and board-level debarment may follow.
AML, Sanctions and AEO/Customs Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under the Oman AML Law (RD 30/2016 as amended) and the Customs Law: fines up to OMR 100,000, confiscation of audit fees, imprisonment, summary revocation of the client's AEO status by the Royal Oman Police Customs, and professional deregistration of the SPC.

Director personal liability: Individual directors face criminal prosecution, disqualification from company management, asset freezing under FIU orders, and cross-border arrest warrants; the corporate veil is pierced for knowing participation in laundering the proceeds of customs fraud.
