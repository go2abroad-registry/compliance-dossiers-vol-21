Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Omani Single Person Company; the declared capital must be fully subscribed by the sole shareholder and must be available in a licensed Omani bank account or supported by a licensed auditor's valuation for in-kind contributions. No mandatory escrow account is required for ordinary SPC incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: A Fire and Life Safety Certificate must be obtained from the Civil Defence and Ambulance Authority before the municipal commercial license is issued. The registered office must pass the CDAA inspection for fire exits, extinguishers, emergency lighting, and signage. A licensed structural engineer must verify floor-loading if the office contains a heavy vault or secure records strongroom. Electrical safety inspection and occupational workplace clearance are required for the admitted office area; the annual renewal of the operating license is conditioned on continuing compliance.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Omani Single Person Company; the declared capital must be fully subscribed by the sole shareholder and must be available in a licensed Omani bank account or supported by a licensed auditor's valuation for in-kind contributions. No mandatory escrow account is required for ordinary SPC incorporation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), established by Royal Decree 39/2023 as the successor to the Secretariat General of Taxation (Ministry of Finance). Electronic access: eTAX/Tax Management System portal at taxoman.gov.om; separate Mawakib e-invoicing platform for VAT clearance.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Single Person Company (SPC) with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) and obtain Commercial Registration (CR), a special fiduciary/trust-license, and register the Discretionary Asset Protection Trust (DAPT) in the Ministry of Justice Fiduciaries Register under Royal Decree 9/2017. Step 2: File for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) with OTA through eTAX within 30 days of CR issuance/commencement of activity, submitting the CR certificate, memorandum and articles of association, trustee license, board resolution, authorized signatory passports, place-of-business declaration, and prior-period financial statements (if any). Step 3: Register the SPC for VAT upon OMR 38,500 taxable turnover (or voluntarily at OMR 19,250), and obtain the Capital Market Authority/Central Bank of Oman license where the SPC acts as regulated fund/trust administrator.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the foreign-owned SPC is a resident corporate taxpayer under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 28/2009, as amended), taxed at the standard 15% rate on Omani-sourced and certain residence-based income; a preferential 3% SME band applies to taxable income up to OMR 100,000 for qualifying small enterprises. The DAPT itself defaults to non-transparent accumulation taxation at the fiduciary level under Article 33 of the Income Tax Law and the fiduciary-contracts framework unless a transparency election is approved; free-zone tax exemption does not arise automatically for this activity.

Available election forms

(1) Fiduciary transparency election: the trustee may elect, subject to OTA approval and conditions in the Income Tax Law Executive Regulations, to treat the irrevocable discretionary trust as fiscally transparent so that trust income is charged in the hands of settlor or discretionary beneficiaries. (2) SME preferential-rate election: eligible law-income SPCs may access the 3% band on taxable income up to OMR 100,000. (3) VAT grouping election under Article 15 of the VAT Law (RD 121/2016) for jointly-controlled resident SPC/trust structures. (4) Tax grouping of 100%-owned Omani companies under Article 41(bis). No U.S.-style check-the-box regime exists under Omani law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 38,500 annual taxable supplies (mandatory); OMR 19,250 (voluntary); measured on a 12-month look-back basis

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated where the goods physically leave Oman and the other GCC states within 90 days from the date of supply, evidenced by electronic Customs Export Declaration (Bayan) release. Services: trust administration, fiduciary custody and asset-management services exported to a non-resident customer established outside the GCC may be zero-rated either (i) as a deemed export of an otherwise-exempt financial service where the benefit is economically consumed outside Oman, or (ii) under the general B2B place-of-supply rule to a non-GCC recipient; the supplier must hold a complete evidential file, including proof of the recipient's non-resident status and actual economic enjoyment outside the GCC.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan/Mersal electronic customs record)
  • Bill of Lading or Original Airway Bill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate confirming receipt of foreign exchange through a licensed Omani bank
  • Commercial Invoice, packing list, insurance certificate and purchase order
  • Executed trust/fiduciary service agreement and beneficiary non-residency certification

Mawakib e-invoicing is mandatory for B2G transactions from April 2024, with phased B2B/B2C rollout thereafter. Oman applies a real-time clearance model: invoices must be transmitted electronically to OTA via secured API/CSID digital certificate before issuance, validated, and returned with a unique identifier and digital stamp (QR code); non-cleared invoices are legally invalid for VAT deduction purposes.

Export substantiation files must be retained for 5 years under VAT rules and 10 years under the Income Tax Law. Incorrect zero-rating triggers output tax adjustment with default interest of 1% per month on unpaid tax; fraudulent export zero-rating or false documentation constitutes tax evasion punishable by a fine of OMR 1,000-25,000 and/or imprisonment, with repeat offenses doubling the maximum, in addition to OTA assessment of 100% of the evaded tax and retrospective clawback of all zero-rated benefits. Income Tax Law anti-avoidance provisions (Article 15(g)) further permit OTA to disregard artificial fiduciary structures and recharacterize transactions to restore the tax position.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account purposes and is pegged at USD 1 = OMR 0.3845 with a narrow intervention band of approximately ±0.0010. No prior CBO approval is required for profit remittances, shareholder distributions, fiduciary fees, or trust settlements, provided the bank receives standard documentary support. Capital-account openness permits 100% non-resident ownership of the SPC without exchange rationing, profit-blocking mechanisms, or mandatory conversion of foreign currency; only bank-level sanctions screening and balance-of-payments statistical reporting apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. As a predicate for trade-finance compliance, CBO-regulated banks require that export proceeds be credited to the exporter's account in Oman within 180 days of shipment, substantiated by invoice, contract, bill of lading, and customs declaration. For the discretionary asset-protection trust SPC, fiduciary fee income and capital distributions received from abroad must be credited to the operational trust account with full source-of-funds identification but are not subject to any surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO). Foreign-exchange oversight is exercised under the Foreign Exchange Business Law (Royal Decree No. 13/2003) and the Banking Law (Royal Decree No. 114/2000), with CBO Exchange Control and the CBO Financial Intelligence Unit jointly enforcing sanctions, AML/CFT, and statistical reporting obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Currenxie, Mercury) may not hold OMR balances or provide settlement accounts in Oman without a CBO license under the Payment Service Provider Regulations issued by the Central Bank of Oman. An offshore EMI may be used only as a downstream USD payout corridor for non-Oman beneficiary distributions, while the core treasury liquidity, escrow, and OMR-clearing legs must remain with CBO-licensed banks or licensed money-exchange houses. Onboarding the SPC directly with an EMI for trust-account purposes is not legally viable in Oman.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from the non-resident shareholder are wired via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the SPC's escrow or operating account at Bank Muscat or National Bank of Oman. The remittance must reference the exact SPC name and Commercial Registration number, with narration 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION – SPC OM' and the shareholder's own bank account as the sole originating account to avoid third-party payment prohibitions. The receiving bank files a CBO foreign-exchange transaction return under the balance-of-payments category 'FDI Equity – Share Capital', supported by the share-subscription resolution, inward remittance application, AML declaration, and source-of-funds evidence.Trust settlements, beneficiary distributions, fiduciary fees, and asset-purchase payments are cleared by SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wires and through OmanNet/RTGS for intra-Oman OMR legs. Trade settlement for tangible or financial assets may use commercial letters of credit under UCP600 with conforming transport and insurance documents presented through the CBO-licensed issuing bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% flat corporate income tax (applicable to tax years commencing on or after 1 January 2023); no progressive brackets for Omani-resident Single Person Companies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends paid by an Omani SPC to any non-resident shareholder; no additional branch remittance tax if the SPC is a resident LLC.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return must be submitted within four calendar months after the end of the SPC's fiscal year; the Tax Authority may grant a single extension for a further period if application is made before the original deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 2,000,000 of annual aggregate related-party transaction value triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) and a TP information form filed with the Tax return. A Master File is required for an MNE group with consolidated revenue exceeding OMR 30,000,000; country-by-country reporting is expected when the group exceeds OMR 100,000,000 consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations code like the UAE, but the Income Tax Law and judicial anti-avoidance doctrines require that a corporate trustee SPC demonstrates real substance: physical office, employees, risk management, board meetings in Oman, and independent decision-making. A shell SPC that merely holds the title of trustee will be disregarded; its income may be re-attributed to the settlor or foreign beneficiary, triggering 15% CIT and WHT penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to double the amount of evaded tax, imprisonment up to three years, and compulsory restitution of the tax plus 25% administrative penalty and monthly late-payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors and resident manager are jointly liable for the unlawful acts; the Oman Tax Authority can obtain a travel ban and asset freeze, and criminal conviction automatically disqualifies the individual from managing any Omani company.
Shell Company / Sham Trust

Statutory sanction: Commercial registration cancellation, forced strike-off, and tax authority re-characterization of the SPC's income as the income of the foreign settlor/beneficiary, leading to 15% CIT and 10% WHT plus penalties.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced; directors personally liable for unpaid taxes and restitution to the trust estate; court-imposed bans on leaving Oman are routinely issued.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment reallocating profit to Oman, additional penalty equal to 15% of the corrected tax difference, and an additional fine up to OMR 50,000 for failure to produce documentation.

Director personal liability: CFO/director who signed false TP documents may be liable for the resultant tax shortfall; in aggravated cases, criminal liability for fraudulent misrepresentation.
Anti-Money Laundering / Licensed Activity Violation

Statutory sanction: Fine up to OMR 200,000, confiscation of trust assets, revocation of license, and imprisonment up to seven years under Oman's AML Law and Central Bank instructions.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners are personally liable for AML penalties; passport surrender and international asset seizure are immediate consequences.
Withholding Tax / Dividend Distribution Failure

Statutory sanction: Liability for the un-withheld tax, a further administrative fine of 100–150% of the unpaid WHT, plus monthly late-payment supplements; settlement required before any renewal of commercial license.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the uncollected WHT; personal bank accounts may be seized where funds were diverted abroad.
