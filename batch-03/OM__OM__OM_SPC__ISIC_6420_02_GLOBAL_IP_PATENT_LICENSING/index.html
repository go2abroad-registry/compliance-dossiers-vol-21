Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an SPC in Oman unless the company undertakes a regulated activity; the declared capital must be fully subscribed and deposited into a bank account in Oman or evidenced by a capital deposit certificate. No mandatory escrow account is required, but in-kind contributions are subject to valuation and a sworn auditor's confirmation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) — the Sultanate's national revenue administration for corporate income tax, VAT, and withholding taxes; official e-services portal: https://taxes.gov.om.

E-government filing portal

For a 100% foreign-owned SPC: (1) incorporate before the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate; (2) open an Omani bank account; (3) file income-tax registration with the OTA portal within the statutory grace period after commencement of business, attaching the CR, memorandum/articles of association, registered-office/lease evidence, shareholder resolution, and opening balance sheet — OTA issues the unified corporate TIN; (4) apply separately for VAT registration when annual taxable supplies exceed OMR 500,000 (mandatory) or elect voluntary registration at OMR 250,000; (5) appoint a tax agent-with-power-of-attorney and an Omani-licensed auditor; (6) register on the Oman Data platform (omanidata.gov.om) for authenticated electronic official correspondence. Non-resident licensor entities without an Oman SPC are not granted an Omani TIN and instead enter the Omani tax net through withholding/reverse-charge mechanics.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a 100% foreign-owned SPC: (1) incorporate before the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate; (2) open an Omani bank account; (3) file income-tax registration with the OTA portal within the statutory grace period after commencement of business, attaching the CR, memorandum/articles of association, registered-office/lease evidence, shareholder resolution, and opening balance sheet — OTA issues the unified corporate TIN; (4) apply separately for VAT registration when annual taxable supplies exceed OMR 500,000 (mandatory) or elect voluntary registration at OMR 250,000; (5) appoint a tax agent-with-power-of-attorney and an Omani-licensed auditor; (6) register on the Oman Data platform (omanidata.gov.om) for authenticated electronic official correspondence. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box, pass-through, or hybrid mismatch election exists under the Oman Income Tax Law (RD 28/2009, as amended). The SPC is mandatorily a tax-resident corporate taxpayer; entity classification follows the Commercial Registration and place-of-effective-management rules. Limited statutorily available elections: (a) accounting reference date and functional currency (with OTA approval); (b) cash or accrual accounting method (accrual mandatory above OMR 500,000 turnover); (c) foreign tax credit relief for creditable foreign taxes on foreign-source income; (d) VAT tax group election for related Omani entities; (e) GCC VAT relief for intra-GCC cross-border supplies. None of these allows hybrid or pass-through characterisation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 500,000 annual taxable supplies; voluntary registration: OMR 250,000; non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Oman must register without any threshold. For the resident SPC, royalties received from Omani licensees count as taxable turnover for threshold measurement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) applies to: (a) goods exported outside the GCC; (b) services directly connected with exported goods; and (c) services supplied to non-resident, non-GCC recipients who are not registered or registerable for Omani VAT where the service is not intrinsically connected to Oman real estate and is effectively consumed outside Oman. For the SPC's patent/trademark licensing, royalty income qualifies under (c) when: the license/sub-license is executed with a foreign non-GCC licensee; the licensed IP is exploited exclusively outside Oman; the licensee has no Oman VAT registration nexus; and the SPC holds contemporaneous evidence of non-Oman use. Royalties for IP exploited inside Oman are standard-rated at 5%, and the SPC must apply reverse-charge VAT when paying a non-registered non-resident licensor for IP used in Oman.

  • Oman Customs electronic export declaration (Bayan/Customs single-window) evidencing exit of any goods from Oman/GCC territory
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or road consignment note naming the SPC as exporter
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a licensed Omani bank evidencing foreign-currency receipt from the offshore licensee
  • Zero-rating evidence file: executed patent/trademark licensing agreement, VAT tax invoice, end-use declaration by the licensee, and proof of non-Oman use of the licensed IP

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance API, or digital stamping regime is currently gazetted in Oman. VAT taxpayers must issue statutory VAT invoices in Arabic or English, retain records electronically for 10 years, and file VAT returns electronically via the OTA portal (monthly for annual taxable supplies above OMR 1,000,000; quarterly otherwise). The OTA has signaled a phased e-invoicing mandate; until enacted, voluntary issuance through the Oman Data platform remains optional.

Unsubstantiated zero-rating claims expose the SPC to recharacterisation of the supply as taxable at 5% from the supply date, recovery of the VAT understated together with delay penalties, denial of input-VAT recovery and refunds, administrative penalties per violation and per return, and criminal prosecution in cases of forged export or licensing records, carrying potential imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines up to OMR 25,000. Retrospective clawbacks and extended assessment periods apply where fraud is established; directors and the sole shareholder may be held jointly and severally liable if the SPC is dissolved without settling assessed VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman is an IMF Article VIII member with a liberal currency regime. The Omani Rial (OMR) is pegged to the US Dollar at approximately USD 1 : OMR 0.3845. Current-account payments and transfers, including patent and trademark royalties, license fees, and profit remittances, are freely convertible and remissible through CBO-licensed authorised dealers without quantitative limits. Capital-account transactions, including the original non-resident shareholder equity injection into the Omani Single Person Company (SPC), are not subjected to general foreign-exchange or surrender controls but must comply with Oman's foreign-ownership, anti-money-laundering, and inward-FDI registration rules. Dividend and IP royalty remittance by the SPC to its foreign parent is permitted after the Omani Corporate Income Tax and any applicable withholding tax are paid or effectively assured through tax clearance documentation. There are no material current-account convertibility obstacles, but any cross-border remittance is subject to sanctions screening, source-of-funds checks, and CBO-mandated Balance of Payments reporting by the designated commercial bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not operate a statutory export-proceeds surrender regime. There is no fixed statutory timeline for repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio into OMR. Exporters and recipients of cross-border receipts, including an Omani IP Holding SPC that sub-licenses patents and trademarks worldwide, are entitled to retain foreign currency in permissible external accounts. The CBO nonetheless requires authorised dealers to report export and service receipts under the sixth edition of the IMF Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) for statistical purposes. For prudential reporting, a licensed local bank may ask the SPC to provide underlying licensing contracts, invoices, and CBO-compliant declarations confirming that royalty receipts constitute genuine current-account receivables rather than disguised capital transfers. Consequently, the effective statutory obligation is zero percent surrender with a nil mandatory conversion ratio, subject only to record-keeping and tax disclosure duties.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), acting alongside the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) with respect to FDI registration and incorporation of a Single Person Company under the Omani Commercial Companies Law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Oman does not currently maintain a defined banking-license category for pure-play offshore virtual banks or electronic-money institutions (EMIs) operating without a local branch or CBO license. An offshore digital EMI cannot legally sustain the primary treasury function for an Omani SPC because it does not provide OMR-denominated domestic clearing, access to CBO settlement schemes, or a compliant in-country corporate account. Such an EMI may be used only as an ancillary foreign-currency collection tool if the underlying Omani corporate account remains the primary transaction rail, and only if the EMI is expressly approved by CBO for inbound/outbound remittances. Legal constraints include the Oman Banking Law, CBO AML/CFT circulars, and the prohibition on unlicensed payment services targeted at residents. The recommended route is to open with a CBO-licensed tier-1 commercial bank and, if needed, supplement with a regional financial institution that holds a valid cross-border payment license.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign parent should remit paid-in capital through a compliant SWIFT-compliant wire or MT103 using the SPC's capital escrow/operational account held with a CBO-licensed tier-1 bank. The wire must identify the beneficiary as 'OM_SPC' and include a beneficiary reference identifying the shareholder and the purpose of payment. The purpose code or remittance narrative should read 'Equity injection into an Oman Single Person Company for global IP licensing, ISIC 6420, under the Oman Commercial Companies Law.' Supporting verification must include a board resolution from the parent-company authorising the transfer, a signed share subscription agreement or capital contribution statement, the official Commercial Registration Certificate of the SPC, the CBO FDI notification or inward capital registration confirmation, and evidence of the ultimate beneficial owner's source of funds. For OMR settlement, the authorised dealer will automatically convert the inward USD at the established OMR/USD peg rate; the SPC should instruct whether the account is to be maintained in USD or OMR. For large injections exceeding the bank's internal threshold, a physical copy of the notarised constitutional documents and a concise business-plan package will be required to clear the bank's sanctions and AML filters.Outbound trade and royalty disbursements should be executed through the SPC's primary operational account using SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers. For recurring patent and trademark royalty payments to non-resident licensors, the disbursement instruction must reference the license agreement, applicable tax withholding rate, and the Oman tax clearance certificate if required. For large-scale trade or capital-equipment purchases tied to IP monetisation, a Commercial Letter of Credit issued under ICC UCP600 may be used as the underlying trade-disbursement rail, with SWIFT MT700 issued by the Omani issuing bank and SWIFT MT756 for reimbursement. Interbank cover or nostro funding between the Omani lender and the correspondent bank should occur through SWIFT MT202 COVER. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Oman does not impose withholding tax on dividend distributions made by an Omani tax-resident company to its non-resident shareholders. After corporate income tax has been paid, profits may be repatriated as dividends without additional Omani withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months after the end of the tax year, being the calendar year or another 12-month accounting period approved by the Oman Tax Authority. For a calendar year-end SPC, the statutory deadline is 31 March of the following year. The payment of any final tax falls due on the same date as the return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No separate gazetted Local File threshold is publicly codified under Oman tax law. In practice, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation should be maintained when aggregate cross-border related-party gross revenue or payments exceed OMR 1,000,000 in a tax year, and the Oman Tax Authority may request a transfer pricing documentation package for any material related-party transaction. Taxpayers with substantial consolidated group revenue should also prepare Master File information on request.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) filing regime modeled on UAE-style ESR. However, the Oman Income Tax Law applies substance-over-form principles, anti-avoidance rules and transfer pricing provisions that require an SPC to demonstrate genuine commercial substance. For a global IP licensing entity, the Omani SPC must have functional and operational substance in Oman: physical office, resident employees, strategic board control, active license contracts, independent decision-making and adequate DEMPE (development, enhancement, maintenance, protection and exploitation) functions. A pure mailbox or conduit SPC can be disregarded or recharacterized, and treaty benefits may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of tax, additional tax up to 100% of the underpaid liability, administrative fines, interest and late-payment surcharges, plus criminal prosecution. Corporate fines and confiscation of assets may be imposed; the offence carries imprisonment for the responsible individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the submission of a fraudulent income tax return are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and may face travel bans, asset freezes, disqualification from board positions and imprisonment under Omani tax and penal law.
Artificial IP Holding Company / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: The Oman Tax Authority may disregard the artificial structure, attribute the income to the shareholder or related entities, deny treaty benefits and apply transfer pricing adjustments to the full arm's length amount, with additional tax and penalties. The Commercial Companies Law may also treat the company as not carrying on a lawful business and order its dissolution.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and controlling foreign shareholders can be held personally liable for amounts assessed if they operated the entity as an agent of the foreign parent without an independent commercial rationale; travel bans and criminal sanctions for aiding tax evasion are possible.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing of Royalties

Statutory sanction: The Oman Tax Authority may adjust the SPC's taxable income to reflect an arm's length result, disallow non-arm's length deductions, impose understatement penalties, monthly late surcharges and recover additional corporate income tax. Deliberate mispricing is treated as tax evasion with aggravated penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve related-party arrangements without appropriate transfer pricing analysis may be held jointly liable for the additional tax if the company is insolvent, and persons involved in manipulating transfer prices may face criminal fines and imprisonment as participants in tax evasion.
Failure to Withhold Tax on Non-Resident Payments

Statutory sanction: The SPC becomes personally liable for the full amount that should have been withheld, together with late-payment surcharges and administrative penalties. Interest and penalties are calculated from the date the withholding tax became due until the date of payment.

Director personal liability: The responsible director is personally liable if the company fails to deduct and remit withholding tax and the tax authority cannot collect from the company. The director may also be prohibited from leaving Oman until the tax debt is settled.
Money Laundering and AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Seizure of property, fines, suspension or revocation of commercial registration, closure of bank accounts and criminal prosecution. Natural persons convicted of money laundering may be sentenced to imprisonment; legal entities face substantial fines and confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate money laundering lose the protection of limited liability, are jointly liable to the company and creditors, and face personal criminal liability, travel prohibitions and imprisonment.
Late Filing and Non-Payment of Tax

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties for late filing, monthly late-payment surcharges, enforced collection proceedings, attachment of bank balances, seizure of movable and immovable assets, and suspension of the taxpayer's tax card and transactions with government authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the company's tax debts if they allow the company to trade recklessly or distribute assets to shareholders while tax liabilities remain unpaid; travel bans and execution orders can be issued in collection proceedings.
