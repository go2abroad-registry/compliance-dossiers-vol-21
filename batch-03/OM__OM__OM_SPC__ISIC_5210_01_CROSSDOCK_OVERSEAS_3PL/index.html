Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for SPCs under the current Companies Law (RD 18/2019), which abolished the former OMR 500,000 minimum applicable under RD 112/91. The sole shareholder must fully subscribe the stated capital in an amount proportionate to the warehousing/3PL activity (market standard for logistics SPCs: OMR 20,000–50,000 ≈ USD 52,000–130,000). No escrow account or upfront deposit is statutorily mandated for non-regulated sectors; banks generally require an initial capital deposit or evidence of availability (OMR 5,000–10,000) when opening corporate accounts.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Clearance Certificate issued by the Civil Defense and Ambulance Authority (CDAA) verifying compliance with fire-load limits, emergency exit configuration, extinguisher/hydrant placement, alarm and smoke-detection systems, and loading-dock safety. In parallel, the Ministry of Labour must issue the workplace clearance under the Labour Law (RD 35/2003) confirming racking structural-load safety, machine guarding, personal protective equipment, ventilation, first-aid provision, and accident-reporting procedures for warehouse and cross-dock staff.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for SPCs under the current Companies Law (RD 18/2019), which abolished the former OMR 500,000 minimum applicable under RD 112/91. The sole shareholder must fully subscribe the stated capital in an amount proportionate to the warehousing/3PL activity (market standard for logistics SPCs: OMR 20,000–50,000 ≈ USD 52,000–130,000). No escrow account or upfront deposit is statutorily mandated for non-regulated sectors; banks generally require an initial capital deposit or evidence of availability (OMR 5,000–10,000) when opening corporate accounts.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), Ministry of Finance; e-services portal at https://taxoman.gov.om

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the Single Person Company (SPC) with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP), the company must apply for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and Value Added Tax (VAT) Registration Number via the OTA e-services portal. The application must include the MoCIIP commercial registration certificate, memorandum and articles of association, certified true copies of the shareholder's passport and civil ID, board resolution appointing an authorized tax representative, and evidence of the registered office in Oman. The OTA will issue the TIN and, when the VAT registration threshold is exceeded, the VAT Registration Number. CIT registration must occur within 30 days from the date of incorporation or commencement of business; VAT registration must be filed within 30 days from the date the applicable annual taxable supply threshold is exceeded.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box, pass-through, or hybrid-mismatch election is available in Oman. An Omani Single Person Company is mandatorily regarded as an independent resident juridical entity for income tax purposes. VAT grouping is available on an elective basis for associated persons established in Oman; CIT consolidated return filing is not permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 500,000 (mandatory) on annual taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; OMR 250,000 (voluntary) threshold enables optional VAT registration, subject to OTA approval.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero percent VAT applies where goods are physically exported from Oman to a destination outside Oman, including to another GCC state if the recipient is a taxable resident, provided the export is effected within 90 days from the date of the supply. The supplier must hold a valid Customs Bayaan evidencing export, transport documentation showing the supplier as exporter, and proof that control of the goods passes to the non-resident recipient. For services, zero-rating applies to services provided to a non-resident recipient who is outside Oman, international transport services, and services performed directly in relation to exported goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayaan)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial Invoice in the name of the overseas buyer
  • Container Tracking or Vessel/Aircraft Manifest extract
  • Certificate of Origin where claimed
  • Signed delivery confirmation or POD from the overseas warehouse

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate is currently operative. The Oman Tax Authority has published a phased mandatory e-invoicing roadmap expected to commence in 2025, requiring OTA-approved solution-provider APIs for B2B and B2C invoices. Until then, issuance of electronic tax invoices in auditable XML or PDF formats without pre-clearance is accepted, provided the tax invoice content and numbering meet the VAT Executive Regulations.

The OTA and Royal Oman Police Customs may jointly scrutinize export proof for up to 5 years. If a zero-rated export is found not to have physically left Oman, or the export proceeds are not repatriated or are circularly funded, the VAT becomes immediately due at 5%, input credit is reversed, and refunds already paid are clawed back. An evasion penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded, late payment charges up to 2% per month, and criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to OMR 100,000 may apply for fraudulent or forged export documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is freely convertible for current account transactions and has no substantive capital account exchange controls. The currency operates under a conventional peg to the Special Drawing Rights (SDR), with an official fixed rate of USD 2.6008 per OMR. Foreign investors, including wholly foreign-owned Single Person Companies (SPCs), may remit profits and dividends abroad provided the SPC has filed its corporate tax returns, settled all tax liabilities, and presented the statutory tax clearance certificate from the Oman Tax Authority. Capital account inflows generally do not require prior CBO approval unless they involve borrowing from overseas financial institutions of more than OMR 10 million or the issuance of securities in Oman, both of which fall under CBO's foreign borrowing approval regime. No exchange control permission is required for the initial equity injection or the subsequent capitalization of the SPC. Repatriation of disposal proceeds, liquidation surpluses, and loan repayments is permitted upon submission of dissolution documents and tax clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose any statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in nostro accounts, convert them freely in the onshore interbank market at commercial rates, or use them to settle genuine trade obligations. There is no time limit for bringing export proceeds into Oman, although for VAT purposes exporters must retain bank receipts to evidence actual receipt of consideration. The Marrakech Treaty on trade facilitation and the GCC common external tariff framework do not alter this position; the CBO requires only lawful evidence of the underlying export transaction for purposes of anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing (AML/CFT) record-keeping.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) and the Exchange Control Directorate of the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) for foreign investment dossiers

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility is strictly Medium-Low for unlicensed offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs). Oman has no statutory license for stand-alone digital EMIs that operate without a banking charter or a licensed money services business (MSB) registration from the CBO. An offshore EMI such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, or Wise cannot accept deposits in OMR from Oman-resident importers/exporters unless it operates through a permitted agent, partner bank, or obtains a CBO-licensed MSB status, which is available only to Oman-incorporated entities with substantive local presence. Pragmatically, cross-border payment platforms are viable only as SKY sourcing solutions direct from the SPC's OMR settlement account into the sales platform's offshore acquiring bank; funds are then routed through SWIFT or the platform's own liquidity network. Use of any unlicensed digital wallet to receive payments from Omani consumers would breach the CBO's Payment System Oversight and AML/CFT directives and expose the SPC to regulatory sanctions.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-border e-commerce marketplace payouts originating from platforms such as Amazon, Noon, Shopify, and other global payment aggregators without a clear source-of-funds chain

Root cause: Cross-border e-commerce marketplace payouts originating from platforms such as Amazon, Noon, Shopify, and other global payment aggregators without a clear source-of-funds chain

Defense evidence: Detailed platform settlement reports (listing order-level transactions, fees, refunds), supplier contracts, warehouse cost invoices, route-mapped trade invoices, logistics bills of lading, customs declarations, treasury reconciliation spreadsheets, and a written explanation of each marketplace's payout cadence. Include the platform's terms of service confirming the seller is the SPC and a statement from the fully licensed account provider (Payoneer/Airwallex) showing the underlying sales ledger.
High expected transaction velocity relative to books of record, particularly sudden spikes in cross-border receipts from multiple jurisdictions

Root cause: High expected transaction velocity relative to books of record, particularly sudden spikes in cross-border receipts from multiple jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Trailing 12-month management accounts, monthly inventory purchase orders from Chinese suppliers, warehouse-pick-and-pack reports, freight forwarding invoices, and evidence that the SPC maintains meaningful local logistics infrastructure (leased warehouse, employees, intellectual property). The bank will require a business narrative and, if needed, the e-commerce platform's store statistics showing sales volume.
Ultimate beneficial ownership opacity due to a foreign-looking single shareholder domiciled in a free-trade zone or through a nominee holding structure

Root cause: Ultimate beneficial ownership opacity due to a foreign-looking single shareholder domiciled in a free-trade zone or through a nominee holding structure

Defense evidence: A certified ownership chart from the SPC's registered agent, audited financial statements of the beneficial owner if available, proof of the ultimate natural person's identity and residence (passport, utility bill), and a declaration of no legal sanctions exposure. Where the shareholder is itself a company, the entity must be registered in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction and the board resolution authorizing the SPC's bank account opening must expressly identify the natural person who controls the group.
Standing instruction payments to sanctioned-risk trade corridors (e.g., suppliers in East Asia, marine insurance to brokers with GCC exposure, or payments routed via international financial centers)

Root cause: Standing instruction payments to sanctioned-risk trade corridors (e.g., suppliers in East Asia, marine insurance to brokers with GCC exposure, or payments routed via international financial centers)

Defense evidence: Supplier due diligence file, UN Sanctions List and OFAC screening results, professional export control questionnaire, and a stable business rationale for the corridor. Include the commercial pro-forma invoice, transport orders, and any indemnity or compliance certificate given by the counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial equity injection must be wired by the foreign shareholder from its own bank account to the SPC's newly opened capital escrow account at a tier-1 Omani bank. The remittance must be denominated in USD or EUR using a SWIFT MT103 message with, if applicable, the BIC OMANMUSXXXX for Bank Muscat or the correct BIC of the chosen bank. An IBAN is not mandatory for SWIFT transfers into Oman, but the bank's local account number and branch code are essential. The purpose code should be reported as 'FDIEQ' or an equivalent BOP code denoting equity subscription; the shareholder's identity on the wire, the SPC's name (exact commercial name) and the reference to the capital subscription annexture must be included. The bank will require the notarized Articles of Association, the SPC's initial capital deposit certificate, the commercial registration, and a Board Resolution / sole shareholder resolution confirming the amount, the subscribing shareholder, and the capital scheduled contribution. Verification by the bank includes matching the SWIFT remitter name against the ownership registry and requiring the payment to be free of any custodial or nominee marker. Subsequent DTA or subordinated loan injections require purpose code 'LOAN' or 'DEBT' and, where applicable, a formal loan agreement under CBO's non-resident borrowing rules.Trade payments to suppliers and overseas warehouses are cleared through SWIFT MT103 for direct transfers, documentary credit (Commercial Letter of Credit) under UCP600 for bulk purchase financing, and the local ACH (Omani Real-time Gross Settlement System, known as the OmanNet or RTGS) for any OMR obligations. For supplier payments in USD, the SPC may instruct its local bank to execute a spot conversion OMR to USD and initiate an MT103 to the beneficiary's bank. For procurement from China and Southeast Asia, banks commonly require submission of the commercial invoice, bill of lading, and a letter of instructions confirming the goods are imported into Oman or into a third-country warehouse. For payments to affiliated overseas warehouses, the SPC should use purpose code 'GCC' or 'TRADE' and provide a detailed invoiced description, the warehouse lease agreement, and a full transfer pricing file.
