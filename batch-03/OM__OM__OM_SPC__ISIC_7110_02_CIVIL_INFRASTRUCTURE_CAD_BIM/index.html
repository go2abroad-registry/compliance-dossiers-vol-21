Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SPC under Oman law; the capital amount is fixed in the Memorandum of Association and must be remitted to a licensed Omani commercial bank. For foreign-owned SPCs, the bank issues a capital deposit certificate, and the capital remains credited to the SPC's account and may not be withdrawn to shareholders except on lawful distributions or dissolution.

Funding & unlock path: If a physical office is leased, a fire safety clearance and basic civil defense inspection are required from the Civil Defense and Ambulance Authority (CDAA); electrical and structural load certificates for an office fit-out are required by the municipality. For home-office or virtual-office arrangements, no occupational clearance is triggered beyond ordinary office fire-safety compliance.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SPC under Oman law; the capital amount is fixed in the Memorandum of Association and must be remitted to a licensed Omani commercial bank. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) — unified national tax authority administering Corporate Income Tax (CIT), VAT, and withholding; official e-services portal: taxes.gov.om (tax payer login via OTA user account; registration also accessible through InvestEasy Oman for FDI).

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) must first obtain its Commercial Registration (CR) from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion. Then: (1) create a taxpayer profile on taxes.gov.om using the CR number, constitutional deed, and proof of Omani bank account; (2) receive a single 10-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) valid for CIT, VAT, and withholding; (3) file CIT registration immediately upon commencement of business (no separate license needed for offshore CAD/BIM drafting; a simplified economic substance declaration may apply); (4) register for VAT within 30 days of the date taxable supplies exceed the mandatory threshold; (5) appoint the SPC manager and (if applicable) an OTA-approved tax agent for the electronic filing of returns. The entire registration is paperless and grants access to the VAT e-portal for export zero-rating declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist in Omani law. Available statutory elections: (a) Small Business Relief under the CIT Law — taxable income up to OMR 100,000 is taxed at 0% (the excess is taxed at 15%); (b) VAT voluntary registration election where annual taxable supplies exceed OMR 19,250 but remain below the OMR 38,500 mandatory threshold; (c) VAT tax group registration election for related Omani companies with OTA prior approval; (d) irrevocable election to claim permanent establishment treatment for the SPC's foreign branch, subject to OTA's substance assessment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 38,500 (annual taxable supplies); voluntary registration: OMR 19,250; non-resident suppliers without Oman establishment must register for reverse-charge VAT on Oman B2B supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (a) direct export of goods from Oman to a destination outside the GCC, and (b) export of services supplied to a non-resident recipient who has no place of establishment in Oman, provided the recipient is outside Oman at the time of supply, receives the service abroad, and the service is not directly connected with immovable property situated in Oman. CAD/BIM civil infrastructure drafting, structural modelling, and construction document production supplied electronically to a foreign client qualify as zero-rated exported services when the contract, digital delivery record, and beneficial-use test demonstrate the recipient's foreign location.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan) with OTA-approved export customs site certificate
  • Tax invoice bearing the exact zero-rating wording, the SPC's VAT registration number, and the foreign client's name
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or secure digital delivery certificate for cloud-based BIM files)
  • Signed service contract (including scope, deliverables, and Incoterms)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT advice proving receipt of export proceeds
  • Foreign client's non-residency evidence (overseas VAT/TIN certificate or commercial register extract)

OTA has issued an e-invoicing framework and technical specifications with phased implementation. The current mandate for B2B supplies requires an electronic XML invoice integrated with OTA-approved billing software, plus a QR-signed PDF presentation; real-time fiscal clearance is not yet mandatory (expected from late 2025 for Large Taxpayers). Until full clearance, invoices issued under the SPC's Oman VAT number, containing all Article 47 statutory particulars, constitute the fiscal proof. Export zero-rated supplies must be flagged with the zero-rating code and matched to Customs Bayan documentation in the VAT return electronic file.

Export zero-rating claims are automatically flag-reviewed by OTA risk engines that cross-match Customs Bayan, foreign exchange inflows, and contract value. Failure to substantiate the export within audit requires the SPC to account for 5% output VAT on the invoice value, plus a deficiency penalty of up to 100% of the tax assessed and default interest at 14% per annum. Deliberate falsification of export evidence constitutes tax evasion under the VAT Law and the Penal Code, carrying fines up to OMR 10,000 and imprisonment of up to three years; the SPC manager and any signatory engineer are jointly liable, and retrospective clawback covers a six-year statutory audit window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible and freely tradable under IMF Article VIII, with factor market stability anchored by the hard peg of 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD in place since January 1986. Oman maintains complete current-account convertibility; there are no discriminatory exchange restrictions, prior approvals, or surrender requirements for service-related receipts. The capital account is likewise liberalized for foreign direct investment: a 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) may receive equity injections and repatriate dividends and liquidation proceeds without CBO policy authorization, provided the transactions are routed through licensed Omani banks and evidencing documentation (audited profit statements, tax clearance certificates, and the SPC's valid Commercial Registration) is presented to the executing bank. Practically, profit remittance requires the SPC to file statutory financial statements with the Omani Tax Authority, secure a no-objection/tax clearance for the distribution, and settle any applicable withholding tax (generally 0% on dividends under the Oman Corporate Tax Law, subject to the shareholder being a non-resident with no Oman permanent establishment). Main operational hurdles include: (i) all wire transfers must be cleared via a CBO-licensed bank; (ii) the CBO Balance-of-Payments statistical declaration must be filed for each cross-border flow; (iii) bank-side AML review may demand source-of-funds for the injection; and (iv) any deviation from the scheduled business model described in the initial KYC file could trigger enhanced due diligence. Overall convertibility and profit-remittance risk is assessed as LOW.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender mechanism; the SPC may lawfully retain invoiced proceeds in USD, EUR, or OMR within Oman and is free to convert to OMR at market-determined rates. However, CBO regulations expect all documented commercial revenue derived from services exported (including offshore engineering/BIM deliverables) to be repatriated to the SPC's Omani bank accounts within a reasonable commercial settlement cycle, generally interpreted as within 90 days from the invoice due date. The SPC must record each inbound soft-copy payment against the specific purchase order / master service agreement so that the CBO's electronic Balance-of-Payments reporting and the bank's Transaction Monitoring Unit can reconcile cash flows to the declared business profile. No minimum OMR conversion ratio is prescribed, and holding of foreign currency in the SPC's multi-currency current account is permitted; however, accumulation of unrepatriated offshore balances over a prolonged period may trigger a bank-level review of the entity's economic substance in Oman.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) — exercising sole authority over banking supervision, monetary policy, and foreign exchange administration under the Oman Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000), the Foreign Exchange Policy issued by the CBO, and the Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Financing of Terrorism Law (Royal Decree 30/2016, as amended).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM (auxiliary only). Wise (formerly TransferWise) is operationally usable in Oman as a regulated remittance corridor for low-value inbound and outbound transfers: it supports OMR receipt into the SPC's local account via Oman's local clearing rails and can execute cross-border payments to the SPC's foreign BIM subcontractors and software vendors. Wise is NOT a CBO-licensed bank and therefore cannot serve as the SPC's primary statutory treasury account, escrow account, or the sole cash-management platform; all material cross-border receipts and all capital movements must first land in an account at a CBO-licensed commercial bank. Other offshore digital EMIs (Revolut Business, Mercury, Airwallex, Payoneer) have no licensing or physical presence in Oman, and Omani corporate account-opening regulations effectively prohibit a foreign-owned SPC from routing its operating revenue through any non-CBO-licensed institution. Recommended architecture: use the licensed Omani bank as the legal and financial settlement spine; use Wise only for sub-threshold (under OMR 5,000 per transaction) operational disbursements with full invoice references retained for audit and CBO reporting.

Defense evidence: Obtain and present: (i) the capital category registration in the Invest Easy / MoCIIP investment file; (ii) the SPC board resolution authorizing equity capital contribution; (iii) a parent-company capital call letter referencing the registered capital amount; (iv) a source-of-funds letter from the parent's home bank with two years of statements evidencing earnings; and (v) a capital escrow instruction designating the exact account at Bank Muscat or HSBC Oman to which the MT103 wire will be directed.
Elevated transaction velocity or large round-trip flows in the first 90 days of account opening, including transfers to offshore personal accounts

Root cause: Elevated transaction velocity or large round-trip flows in the first 90 days of account opening, including transfers to offshore personal accounts

Defense evidence: Adopt and submit a board-approved treasury policy confirming: (i) every outbound payment must be tied to an executed sub-contractor invoice or payroll record; (ii) personal-account transfers are strictly prohibited; (iii) vendor payments above OMR 10,000 require a second-approver mandate; and (iv) all counterparties undergo a simplified due-diligence check for sanctions and PEP status. Present the policy at onboarding, and reconcile monthly with a bank-statement mapping report.
Jurisdictional risk flag arising from the UBO's nationality or tax residence on a FATF watch-list or sanctions-adjacent registry

Root cause: Jurisdictional risk flag arising from the UBO's nationality or tax residence on a FATF watch-list or sanctions-adjacent registry

Defense evidence: Commission an independent sanctions and negative-news screening report (Refinitiv / Oracle / Moody's) showing clean results; provide proof of current permanent residence in a non-flagged jurisdiction; present the UBO's employment or business-owner track record in the engineering sector; and submit a signed rectification letter explaining the country linkage, supported by a real commercial nexus to GCC infrastructure projects.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI equity injections must be wired via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202/COVER for intermediate correspondent routing) from the parent entity's corporate bank to the designated Capital Escrow Account at Bank Muscat or HSBC Oman, ensuring the remittance information (field 70) reads: 'FDI CAPITAL INJECTION — OM_SPC — COMMERCIAL REGISTRATION [CR NUMBER] — ISIC 7110 ENGINEERING/BIM SERVICES — INVEST EASY CAPITAL REF [REF]'. Where the remitting bank applies formal ISO 20022 purpose classification, use the OECD/ECB BOP-code equivalent for 'Direct Investment — Equity Capital' (e.g., P0007 / N512 under the SWIFT category set, or the home-jurisdiction equivalent) to avoid misclassification as ordinary trade payments. The SPC must present the MoCIIP capital settlement certificate and the bank's credit advice for the CBO Balance-of-Payments filing within two business days. For injections above OMR 250,000, the receiving bank will require the end-use undertaking, a source-of-wealth memorandum, and the parent's audited statements; funds must be sourced strictly from the named parent entity — third-party funding without a notarized subordination/deed of contribution is rejected.Operational liquidity outflows (subcontractor fees, BIM software subscriptions, cloud infrastructure, payroll, rent) clear via SWIFT MT103 for foreign-currency payments and via Oman's real-time gross settlement (RTGS/OmanNet) for local OMR transfers. Recommended routing tiers: (i) sub-threshold payments under OMR 10,000 — executed via the bank's corporate internet-banking API (single file ISO 20022 pain.001) with invoice imprints; (ii) medium-value payments of OMR 10,000–100,000 — routed through the bank's cash-management desk with pre-vetted beneficiary files to expedite MT103 release; (iii) large-value settlements above OMR 100,000 for procurement of mission-critical infrastructure or where the counterparty demands guaranteed settlement — use an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (LC at sight or 60 days tenor) issued under the SPC's trade line, covering deliverables described as 'Building Information Modeling (BIM) software licenses, structural drafting services, and cloud computing infrastructure', with all transport docs replaced by delivery and acceptance certificates given the digital nature of the supply. Same-day clearing is available within the GCC via the GCC-SCGS and regional correspondent links; UK/EU/US payments typically settle T+2 via the banks' correspondent corridors. Wise may be used strictly as a supplementary low-value rail for payments below OMR 5,000, provided the same beneficiary due-diligence and invoice-reference retention requirements are applied.Deliverable Outright Forwards (USD/OMR, EUR/OMR, GBP/OMR) transacted with CBO-licensed commercial banks only; these constitute the standard hedging vehicle given the OMR/USD hard peg and are available for commitment-driven exposures arising from BIM project contracts denominated in EUR, GBP, or USD.FX Swaps (spot-forward combination) for short-cycle treasury mismatches, such as aligning USD receipts into OMR payroll funding dates, entered under an ISDA Master Agreement or the bank's standard FX master.Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS) between USD/OMR and EUR for the tenor of any structured project-finance liabilities linked to GCC civil-infrastructure delivery, conditional on hedge-strategy approval by the SPC's internal treasury risk committee and periodic CBO/BOP reporting.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) — permissible only where the notional is matched to an underlying borrowing or investment asset; speculative interest-rate positions are prohibited by standard CBO policy.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) in OMR — NOT permissible and commercially unnecessary because the OMR/USD is officially pegged and a fully deliverable OMR forward market operates within the licensed banking system; any attempted NDF booking on OMR would be rejected by the CBO as outside the sanctioned product scope.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM (auxiliary only). Wise (formerly TransferWise) is operationally usable in Oman as a regulated remittance corridor for low-value inbound and outbound transfers: it supports OMR receipt into the SPC's local account via Oman's local clearing rails and can execute cross-border payments to the SPC's foreign BIM subcontractors and software vendors. Wise is NOT a CBO-licensed bank and therefore cannot serve as the SPC's primary statutory treasury account, escrow account, or the sole cash-management platform; all material cross-border receipts and all capital movements must first land in an account at a CBO-licensed commercial bank. Other offshore digital EMIs (Revolut Business, Mercury, Airwallex, Payoneer) have no licensing or physical presence in Oman, and Omani corporate account-opening regulations effectively prohibit a foreign-owned SPC from routing its operating revenue through any non-CBO-licensed institution. Recommended architecture: use the licensed Omani bank as the legal and financial settlement spine; use Wise only for sub-threshold (under OMR 5,000 per transaction) operational disbursements with full invoice references retained for audit and CBO reporting.
