Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) — Companies Registry, Muscat, Sultanate of Oman, acting through the Invest Easy one-stop electronic portal, with notarial authentication by a licensed Notary Public of the Ministry of Justice and Legal Affairs.

Governing statute

Commercial Companies Law of the Sultanate of Oman (Royal Decree No. 18/2019, as amended by Royal Decree No. 53/2022 and Royal Decree No. 27/2024); Single Person Company provisions under Book Two, Chapter 4, Articles 79–96; general incorporation, capital and registered office formalities under Articles 6–16; governance and officer appointment rules under Articles 19–28.

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum and Articles of Association (MoA/AoA) in Arabic, executed before a licensed Notary Public and registered electronically through the MOCIIP Invest Easy platform, culminating in the issuance of the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate and the obligatory publication notice.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 19 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an SPC under RD 18/2019; however, the declared capital must be adequate for the intended in-flight catering business. The sole shareholder must remit the declared capital and obtain a capital credit/escrow confirmation from a commercial bank licensed by the Central Bank of Oman; the funds are released into the company's operational account upon issuance of the CR. The Oman Airports concession may additionally require a performance guarantee.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense General Directorate (Royal Oman Police) fire-safety certification covering cooking lines, extraction ducts, sprinkler/foam suppression systems, and staff evacuation plans; structural load and fixity inspection for mezzanine and ramp-interface structures; workplace occupational-safety clearance under Ministerial Decision 286/2014 covering electrical safety, food hygiene, and lifting equipment; and pre-employment food-handler health certificates.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an SPC under RD 18/2019; however, the declared capital must be adequate for the intended in-flight catering business. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Authority of the Sultanate of Oman (successor to the Secretariat General of Taxation, Ministry of Finance; autonomous authority under Royal Decree establishing the Tax Authority). E-Services and e-filing portal: https://www.tax.gov.om (single online tax account covering Corporate Income Tax, VAT, and tax notifications). Customs authority: Directorate General of Customs, Royal Oman Police, operating the national single-window system 'Bayân' for export/import declarations.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the 100% foreign-owned SPC with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate; no statutory minimum capital is required. 2) Register the CR with the Tax Authority within 30 days of commencing taxable activity to obtain the Tax Registration Number (TRN) via the online tax account. 3) Apply for VAT registration in the same portal where annual taxable supplies reach the mandatory threshold (OMR 500,000) or elect voluntary registration at OMR 187,500; VAT effective date coincides with the CR date or the first taxable supply date. 4) For airport bonded in-flight catering, additionally secure from the Oman Civil Aviation Authority and the airport operator (Oman Airports Management Company) the bonded-kitchen and ramp-delivery authorisation evidencing the dedicated export distribution channel; this letter is exhibited to Customs and the Tax Authority for export zero-rating substantiation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: The SPC is a deemed-resident juridical person under the Oman Income Tax Law (RD 28/2009 as amended) and is taxed at 15% on Oman-source taxable income at entity level (unified rate established by RD 9/2024, which also abolished the 3% municipal tax surcharge). The SPC has no fiscal transparency; retained profits are not taxed in the hands of the sole shareholder; Territorial_Scope: only Oman-source income is within charge, while foreign-source income remains outside the tax base. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The SPC is a deemed-resident juridical person under the Oman Income Tax Law (RD 28/2009 as amended) and is taxed at 15% on Oman-source taxable income at entity level (unified rate established by RD 9/2024, which also abolished the 3% municipal tax surcharge). The SPC has no fiscal transparency; retained profits are not taxed in the hands of the sole shareholder; Territorial_Scope: only Oman-source income is within charge, while foreign-source income remains outside the tax base. The in-flight catering revenue is Oman-source when the production, bonded storage, and ramp delivery occur in Oman, notwithstanding the foreign airline counterparty.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available; entity classification is fixed by the Commercial Companies Law (RD 18/2019). Available statutory mechanisms: (i) VAT group registration under Art. 6 of the Oman VAT Law (RD 121/2020) allowing related persons to file under a single VAT account; (ii) voluntary VAT registration election once taxable supplies exceed OMR 187,500; (iii) choice of financial year with written notification to the Tax Authority; (iv) no hybrid mismatch election is recognised in Omani domestic law. The SPC cannot elect pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 500,000 annual taxable supplies (look-forward 12-month test); voluntary registration: OMR 187,500 (VAT Law RD 121/2020 and implementing regulations). For the foreign-owned SPC, all catering supplies physically delivered to aircraft in Oman count toward the threshold in both Omani riyals and in-kind consideration, including international airline contract value apportioned to Omani legs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Art. 29 of RD 121/2020 applies to: (i) goods physically exported from Oman - including meals produced in the bonded in-flight kitchen, transferred to customs-bonded aircraft stores, and loaded onto aircraft that depart on an international sector; and (ii) services directly related to the exported goods or to international transport where the foreign airline has no establishment in Oman. The zero-rate is sustainable only if the flight log proves an international sector; any domestic (Oman-national or GCC-intra) sector requires apportionment, and the domestic portion is taxable at 5%. Input VAT on raw food, packaging, and kitchen operating costs is fully creditable against zero-rated output, and excess input VAT is refundable through the VAT return mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed in the Bayân single-window platform, showing the bonded-store release and the aircraft-specific customs transfer/export approval evidencing physical departure from Oman
  • Air Waybill or aircraft catering ramp manifest with the airline signature, flight number, and international sector departure stamp, together with the daily kitchen despatch log matching quantities to the flight rotation schedule
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank statement evidencing receipt of the export invoice value in foreign currency from the airline within the prescribed repatriation window

The Oman Tax Authority has introduced a phased e-invoicing mandate based on the CEN-EN 16931 interoperability standard (PEPPOL-style XML); obliged taxpayers issue electronic VAT invoices via Tax-Authority-approved service providers. As of the current regime, Oman has not implemented real-time fiscal clearance or per-invoice digital stamping (unlike Saudi Arabia's ZATCA model). VAT invoices, credit notes, and debit notes must nonetheless be issued in the prescribed e-format with QR code once the taxpayer's rollout wave is reached. For bonded airline catering in the interim, the Tax Authority accepts a consolidated e-invoice matched with the daily aircraft despatch manifest and final export declaration; standard VAT returns must be filed electronically in the tax portal.

The Tax Authority audits airline-catering exporters with an integrated data match against Bayân departure records, airline ramp manifests, and bank remittances. Failure to substantiate physical export and proceeds repatriation triggers: (i) full clawback of the zero rating with VAT assessed at 5%; (ii) late-payment penalty of 1% per month capped at 50% of the tax due; (iii) incorrect-return penalties of OMR 100 per month up to OMR 1,000; and (iv) for fraudulent zero-rating or forgery of customs/remittance evidence, tax evasion sanctions under the VAT Law: a fine of not less than OMR 10,000 and not more than OMR 250,000 and imprisonment of one to five years, with liability extending to the SPC director or responsible officer; the Tax Authority may also administratively suspend the eligible exporter status and freeze refunds until the clawback is fully settled.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman operates a conventional open-market peg with OMR 1 = USD 2.6008 (USD 1 = OMR 0.3845), with bank buying/selling rates published by the CBO around the base rate. Current-account convertibility is unrestricted: dividends, profit remittances, interest, management fees, and liquidation proceeds may be transferred abroad without CBO licensing, subject to documented proof, audited financial statements, board resolution, and tax-compliance verification at the Oman Tax Authority. Capital-account transactions are also liberal under the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2019) and the Commercial Companies Law, which permit 100% foreign ownership of an Omani Single Person Company; inbound equity, shareholder loans, and intercompany funding require no exchange-control approval, but banks must classify each flow under IMF BPM6 purpose codes (e.g., D3 direct-investment equity) and complete source-of-funds CDD. No profit-reinvestment quota, convertibility tax, or residency-based remittance cap applies to the airline-catering SPC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Oman has no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no deadline for repatriating or landing foreign-currency export receipts. The catering SPC may receive aviation-contract revenues in OMR, USD, EUR, or GBP and hold them in FCY current accounts at a licensed Omani bank without conversion. Only statistical reporting through the servicing bank (EDF/BOP reporting) is required; exporters are free to use FCY balances directly for settlement of imported raw-food materials, duty, and logistics.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), Ruwi, Muscat, Sultanate of Oman. The CBO is the sole monetary, banking, FX-policy, and payment-system regulator; it manages the OMR/USD peg, licenses all commercial banks, and operates the national clearing, electronic, and real-time settlement infrastructure. The Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) administers company registration through the Invest Easy portal, while the Oman Tax Authority registers the SPC for income tax and issues tax-clearance certifications relevant to profit transfers.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% flat corporate income tax rate under Royal Decree 28/2023 on the Income Tax Law, effective for tax years commencing on or after 1 January 2024; the predecessor RD 47/2015 applied a 0% band on the first OMR 30,000 of taxable income and 15% above.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Oman does not impose any withholding tax on dividend distributions by an Omani company to a non-resident shareholder. Dividends may be remitted freely without prior OTA clearance or reduced-rate application.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed within 4 months after the end of the fiscal year (e.g., 30 April for a 31 December year-end). The OTA may grant a discretionary extension of up to 2 months on application; the final balance of tax is payable on or before the filing deadline.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Tax Identification Number (TIN) and Commercial Registration (CR) number of the SPC. Enter the OTA-issued TIN exactly as registered; the beneficial foreign shareholder must be disclosed in the shareholding schedule. The non-resident shareholder does not sign Box 1; the resident manager signs under power of attorney. Late filing attracts an administrative fine of OMR 1,000 plus a monthly surcharge of 1% of the unpaid tax for each month or part-month of delay, capped at 24 months; a second reminder triggers an estimated assessment and an additional penalty of 10% of the assessed amount; wilful non-filing is referred to the Public Prosecution and may result in imprisonment of up to 3 years and a fine of up to OMR 10,000; failure to maintain accounting records carries a fine of OMR 500 to OMR 1,000.
Box 2 Fiscal year start and end dates (must correspond to the approved accounting period). A newly incorporated SPC must file its first return for the period from licence commencement to its approved year-end; a non-calendar year-end requires prior OTA approval. Late filing attracts an administrative fine of OMR 1,000 plus a monthly surcharge of 1% of the unpaid tax for each month or part-month of delay, capped at 24 months; a second reminder triggers an estimated assessment and an additional penalty of 10% of the assessed amount; wilful non-filing is referred to the Public Prosecution and may result in imprisonment of up to 3 years and a fine of up to OMR 10,000; failure to maintain accounting records carries a fine of OMR 500 to OMR 1,000.
Box 3 Gross revenue from the sale of in-flight meals, airline catering services, and ancillary ramp/logistics services. All gross revenue attributable to services physically performed in Oman is taxable, including sales to non-resident airlines delivered at Muscat or Salalah airports; revenue booked offshore must be re-attributed to the Omani kitchen. Late filing attracts an administrative fine of OMR 1,000 plus a monthly surcharge of 1% of the unpaid tax for each month or part-month of delay, capped at 24 months; a second reminder triggers an estimated assessment and an additional penalty of 10% of the assessed amount; wilful non-filing is referred to the Public Prosecution and may result in imprisonment of up to 3 years and a fine of up to OMR 10,000; failure to maintain accounting records carries a fine of OMR 500 to OMR 1,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 1,000,000 (aggregate annual cross-border related-party revenue and expenses); above this threshold the SPC must prepare and retain a contemporaneous Local File and Master File aligned with OECD TP guidelines. Below the threshold, a simplified Transfer Pricing Disclosure Schedule (Form TP-1) is still mandatory for every controlled transaction; Oman does not apply a general de minimis exemption, and the arm's length standard applies whenever the taxpayer enters into a related-party contract.

Economic substance scope: Oman has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation statute comparable to the UAE regime, but the Income Tax Law RD 28/2023 embeds substance-based anti-avoidance and controlled foreign company rules. The bonded inflight catering kitchen must prove genuine economic activity in Oman: licensed premises inside the airport customs zone, owned kitchen equipment and cold-chain vehicles, full-time production and quality staff, daily meal production logs, and contract-signing authority exercised in-country. If the OTA determines that the SPC is a shell used solely to shift profit abroad, it may disregard the SPC's separate personality, re-attribute its revenue to the foreign parent, deny treaty benefits and revoke the airport catering licence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine equal to 100% of the tax evaded; additional penalty of up to 3 times the evaded amount; interest of 1% per month on outstanding tax; administrative name-and-shame publication by the OTA; criminal prosecution by the Public Prosecution with imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to OMR 50,000.

Director personal liability: The resident manager or director who signs the false return is jointly and severally liable for the principal tax, penalties and interest; personal bank accounts and shares may be seized; a court-ordered travel ban prevents exit from Oman; signatories face imprisonment of 6 months to 5 years.
Shell Company and Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: OTA disregards the SPC's separate legal personality, attributes the full meal-production income to the foreign shareholder, disallows the SPC's cost deductions, recharacterises intra-group flows as non-deductible distributions, and imposes late-payment penalties and interest; the Public Authority for Civil Aviation and customs suspend the bonded-kitchen and ramp-delivery licences.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the sham structure may be ordered by the Commercial Court to make civil restitution of the full tax avoided plus damages; personal travel bans are imposed where the tax debt exceeds OMR 20,000; directors found to have signed false licence declarations risk criminal liability under the Commercial Companies Law.
AML/CFT and International Sanctions

Statutory sanction: AML Law (RD 30/2016): fines of up to OMR 100,000 per violation; confiscation of the proceeds of crime; cancellation of the Commercial Registration; daily additional fines until compliance; referral to the National Centre for Financial Information and the Public Prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise transactions with sanctioned counterparties are criminally liable with imprisonment of up to 7 years; personal assets are frozen pending investigation; courts may ban the director from managing any company in Oman for a period of up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's length mark-up; penalty of 25% of the additional tax assessed; disallowance of interest exceeding the 3:1 debt-to-equity ratio; monthly interest of 1% on the adjusted tax; the OTA Large Taxpayer Office opens an industry-wide audit of the catering group.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the transfer pricing penalty where they failed to exercise due diligence in preparing the Form TP-1 and intercompany agreements; if the documentation is proven forged, directors face criminal proceedings and a travel ban; conviction may carry imprisonment of up to 3 years.
