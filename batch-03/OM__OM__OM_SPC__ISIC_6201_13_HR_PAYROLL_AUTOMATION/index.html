Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Omani law; the SPC shall state its subscribed capital in OMR in the Articles of Association; no notarized escrow or capital deposit receipt is required for payroll software service activities, provided that the sole shareholder can evidence lawful source of funds for the paid-up share capital if requested by the Registry or a licensed commercial bank.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense and Ambulance Authority (CDAA) fire safety certificate for the office premises; structural load assessment for the floor, particularly where server racks and heavy IT infrastructure will be installed; completion of an Occupational Health and Safety check under Oman's Labor Law; provision of fire extinguishers, emergency exit signage, and an annual fire alarm test record.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Omani law; the SPC shall state its subscribed capital in OMR in the Articles of Association; no notarized escrow or capital deposit receipt is required for payroll software service activities, provided that the sole shareholder can evidence lawful source of funds for the paid-up share capital if requested by the Registry or a licensed commercial bank.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), official name: Al-Hai'ah al-'Ammah li al-Dara'ib; established by Royal Decree No. 25/2020 as successor to the Secretariat General of Taxation; unified national electronic tax services portal: https://www.tax.gov.om (covering corporate income tax, VAT, customs-linked checks and taxpayer ledger services).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: incorporate the foreign-owned SPC at the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) through the Invest Easy single-window portal and obtain the Commercial Registration certificate. Step 2: file the tax registration application with the OTA within 30 days of Commercial Registration issuance, submitting the CR certificate, constitutional documents, proof of registered office, appointment of a resident legal representative, and opening financial statements via the OTA e-Services portal. Step 3: obtain the Tax Registration Number (TRN), which is the single statutory identifier for all Omani tax obligations. Step 4: separately apply for VAT registration within 30 days of ceasing to meet the VAT threshold test, using the same TRN-led taxpayer account. Non-resident digital services suppliers making B2C supplies in Oman must register for VAT on a threshold-free basis.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Omani SPC is a resident juridical person under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree No. 28/2009, as amended); corporate income tax is imposed at the entity level on worldwide income at the standard rate of 15%; no pass-through, fiscal transparency, or territorial-ring-fenced classification is available to a 100% foreign-owned SPC; foreign branch profits are exempt only where taxed abroad at a comparable statutory rate under the statutory foreign-tax relief mechanism.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or entity-classification election exists in Oman; the SPC is mandatorily classified as a taxable corporation. Elective mechanisms available: (i) voluntary VAT registration at OMR 100,000 taxable supplies or OMR 50,000 taxable expenses; (ii) election to file consolidated income tax returns for qualifying related juridical persons; (iii) explicit written election to apply group loss-relief within an approved group structure; hybrid-mismatch, CFC look-through, and similar anti-deferral elections are not codified in Omani tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration for Omani-established businesses: OMR 500,000 of annual taxable supplies and imports (tested on a rolling 12-month prospective and retrospective basis); voluntary registration: OMR 100,000 of annual taxable supplies or OMR 50,000 of annual taxable expenses; non-resident suppliers of taxable goods or services to non-registered consumers in Oman must register regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports are zero-rated where the goods are physically removed from Oman and leave the Sultanate within 90 days of the date of supply. Services exports, including cross-border payroll SaaS and HR-staff-compliance digital services, are zero-rated where (i) the recipient is a non-resident who is outside Oman at the time of supply and is not registered for Omani VAT; (ii) the effective use and enjoyment of the service occurs outside Oman; (iii) the service is not directly linked to Omani immovable property or to goods situated in Oman at the time of supply. Ancillary transport services and services physically performed abroad also qualify. Complete contemporaneous commercial evidence of export and of the recipient's non-resident status must be retained.

  • Customs export declaration validated through the Oman Customs Bayan single-window system
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international consignment note evidencing removal from Oman within 90 days
  • Original tax invoice and commercial invoice cross-referencing the customs declaration
  • Packing list, weight certificate, and certificate of origin (where claimed)
  • Loading certificate or port/airport terminal receipt endorsed by the carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign bank credit advice evidencing receipt of proceeds within 180 days
  • Recipient's foreign VAT registration certificate or foreign tax residency certificate proving non-resident status

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-mandated clearance, or digital stamping regime is currently enforced. VAT-registered suppliers must issue valid tax invoices, simplified invoices, and credit or debit notes in Arabic (or bilingually with Arabic), showing the TRN and all mandated invoice content, and retain them for the statutory retention period. The OTA has published an e-invoicing roadmap toward structured electronic invoicing; taxpayers should configure ERP systems to emit standardised electronic tax invoice formats in anticipation of the phased mandate.

The OTA cross-matches VAT refund files with the Bayan customs export declarations and bank FX credit evidence. Failure to prove physical export within 90 days or FX repatriation within 180 days triggers clawback of the previously recovered input VAT, reassessment of output VAT at the full 5% rate, and a late-payment fine of 25% of the assessed VAT. Fraudulent or intentionally false export zero-rating or refund claims expose the SPC to an extended 10-year VAT reassessment window (versus the ordinary 5-year limitation), evasion fines up to OMR 50,000 (doubled for repeat violations), imprisonment of the responsible persons for up to 5 years, revocation of the OTA good-standing status, and placement of the entity in the high-risk compliance registry with intensified customs and VAT controls.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman maintains a quasi-fixed exchange rate regime with the Omani Rial (OMR) pegged to the US Dollar (USD) at approximately 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD. The current account is largely convertible for trade and service transactions, subject to standard documentary compliance and anti-money laundering (AML / CFT) verification. Capital account transactions, including direct investment, profit repatriation, and dividend remittances, are permitted for 100% foreign-owned Omani Single Person Companies (SPCs), but remittances must be executed through a licensed bank with appropriate underlying documentation, including audited financials, board or shareholder resolutions, tax clearance certificates, and confirmation of settlement of any CBO or regulatory obligations. There are no general exchange controls restricting the transfer of profits or dividends, but banks apply enhanced scrutiny to ensure the transaction aligns with the declared corporate purpose and the Omani Commercial Companies Law. Certain strategic sectors remain subject to foreign ownership limitations under the Oman Foreign Capital Investment Law, though ISIC 6201 software and payroll automation does not fall within restricted sectors. Profit remittance may be temporarily delayed if the SPC has outstanding liabilities to government agencies or if the beneficial ownership structure is not fully disclosed as per CBO anti-money laundering directives.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman applies no statutory mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the Central Bank of Oman. Exporters may retain foreign currency revenues in their operational or export proceeds accounts maintained with licensed banks in Oman or abroad, subject to CBO reporting thresholds. Export receipts for software services and subscriptions (non-physical exports) must be evidenced by commercial invoices, service contracts, and SWIFT payment records. Banks may require conversion of received hard currency into OMR when settlement is needed for local operating expenses or tax liabilities, but there is no fixed percentage or timeline imposed by the CBO. The tax authority, however, requires repatriation to be matched within the corporate financial statements for transfer pricing and permanent establishment compliance. For payment service providers and payroll SaaS platforms, cross-border inbound payments must be tagged with the correct purpose code (e.g., ISIC 6201 software services, royalties, or SaaS subscriptions) to avoid delays and elevated AML review.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital payment institutions and electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed by the Central Bank of Oman and do not have direct access to the local real-time gross settlement (RTGS) system or OMR clearing infrastructure. Foreign EMIs may be used only as auxiliary collection channels for cross-border non-OMR funding, but the principal treasury clearing and settlement must be executed through a licensed Omani bank or a licensed payment service provider (PSP) registered with CBO under the Oman Payment Services Law and CBO e-Payment regulations. Feasibility rating: Low for replacing domestic banking rails; Medium as a supplementary cross-border collection tool if the EMI holds a separate license in a reputable jurisdiction and can pass AML/KYC checks with the Omani correspondent bank. The SPC must ensure that all inbound client funds are consolidated through a licensed Omani corporate account and settlement must comply with the CBO's outsourcing and cloud banking guidelines.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a 100% non-resident owned Omani SPC, the shareholder must inject equity or shareholder loan through a licensed commercial bank wire transfer using SWIFT MT103 with strict purpose code mapping. Recommended purpose codes for capital injection: 'Capital contribution in cash' (World Bank / BPM6 code 'C3 Equity shares in corporations'), or 'Loan financing for working capital' for shareholder loans. The remitting bank's beneficiary field must indicate the Omani SPC's exact legal name, commercial registration number, and account number at the receiving Omani bank. Supporting documentation must include the shareholder resolution, updated CR, the SPC's constitution, and a letter from the local bank compliance officer confirming the capital is part of the registered capital or subordinated cross-border loan. Funds must be received in OMR or convertible major currencies (USD, EUR, GBP) and must not be fragmented into amounts below 3,000 OMR to circumvent the currency declaration regime. For subscription-based SaaS income, inbound wires should use the IMF BPM6 Services code 'Telecommunications, computer, and information services (code 4.2)' or appropriate 'SaaS license / software subscription' purpose code. All inbound transfers are subject to CBO reporting if they exceed the threshold of OMR 10,000 per transaction; the bank will file the required statistical declaration on behalf of the SPC.Outbound treasury disbursements for payroll, supplier, and licensing payments shall be routed through the domestic RTGS / clearing system for OMR payments and through SWIFT for cross-border foreign currency remittances. For non-OMR cross-border vendor payments, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) with a fully populated structured remittance field containing the invoice number, tax ID, and the international bank account number (IBAN). For larger trade settlements exceeding OMR 2 million, the SPC may issue Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) or Documentary Collections through the Omani bank, with LC confirmation by top-tier international correspondent banks. Since ISIC 6201 is a software supplier and not a physical commodity trader, LC usage should be limited to software implementation contracts with milestone-based escrow arrangements. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% on taxable income exceeding OMR 30,000

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% standard withholding tax on dividends distributed by an Omani company to a non-resident shareholder, unless reduced under a Double Taxation Agreement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 500,000 aggregate annual controlled transactions between the Omani SPC and non-resident related parties triggers the mandatory preparation of a formal transfer pricing documentation file (Local File / Master File equivalent).

Economic substance scope: The Oman Economic Substance Regulations require any licence holder or company performing relevant activities to maintain substantial economic presence in Oman: local office premises, proper staffing, board decision-making, adequate capital and operational expenditure in Oman. A payroll software SPC performing cloud/SaaS services, treasury or IP functions for a foreign group must evidence real management and commercial activity in Oman. A paper-only or booking company without Omani substance will be denied treaty benefits, may have its corporate facade disregarded, and can trigger severe penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of up to OMR 25,000 plus additional tax equal to 100% of the evaded tax; criminal referral with imprisonment of up to three years and confiscation of the proceeds of the offence.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors who sign or direct the false return can be personally and jointly liable for the evaded tax; courts may issue travel bans, asset-freezing orders, and criminal custodial sentences in egregious cases.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, disallowance of expenses attributable to the sham structure, recharacterisation or allocation of income, monetary penalties under the Income Tax Law, and potential cancellation of the Commercial Registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and designated shareholders of the SPC can be held personally accountable for the underlying tax, and may be subject to temporary travel bans and personal financial liability where the company is treated as a facade.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment increasing Oman taxable income to the arm's length result; late payment interest of 12% per annum; penalties of up to 10% of the adjusted amount; and secondary withholding tax on deemed dividend distributions.

Director personal liability: Chief executive and chief financial officer who certify the transfer-pricing disclosures can incur joint liability for adjustments and penalties; an unresolved audit may lead to personal insolvency and travel restrictions.
AML / Cash-Flow Integrity Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Oman's Anti-Money Laundering Law, sanctions include significant fines, freezing orders, confiscation of funds, and revocation of licences; the company is classified as Medium-Risk Enhanced, triggering enhanced due diligence and periodic compliance reviews.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners can be criminally liable with imprisonment of up to seven years, personal fines and travel bans where the business was knowingly used to launder payroll proceeds.
