Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), Oman; OTA succeeds the Secretariat General for Taxation within the Ministry of Finance. E-filing and tax registration are performed through the unified tax portal 'taxoman.gov.om' with identity authentication via the 'Mypass' government ID system. Customs export control is administered by the Royal Oman Police - Directorate General of Customs through the 'Bayan' single-window system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) through MoCIIP's 'Invest Easy' portal and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR). Step 2 - CIT registration: File the Income Tax Registration via the OTA portal within 30 days of CR issuance, attaching the CR, executed MOA/AOA, passport copies of the non-resident shareholder, registered-office lease, and a notarized local tax agent authorization; the OTA issues the Tax Card / Tax Identification Number. Step 3 - VAT registration: If taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period equal or exceed OMR 38,500 (or with voluntary election at OMR 19,250), file the VAT registration application in the OTA VAT module, declaring activities, expected supplies, and opening balance sheet; a 15-digit VAT Registration Number is then issued. Step 4: Obtain ancillary identifiers - Oman Chamber of Commerce and Industry (OCIC) membership, PASI (Social Protection Fund) registration, municipal license, and a customs 'Bayan' registrant code - prior to any export shipment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Step 1: Incorporate the 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) through MoCIIP's 'Invest Easy' portal and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR). Step 2 - CIT registration: File the Income Tax Registration via the OTA portal within 30 days of CR issuance, attaching the CR, executed MOA/AOA, passport copies of the non-resident shareholder, registered-office lease, and a notarized local tax agent authorization; the OTA issues the Tax Card / Tax Identification Number. Step 3 - VAT registration: If taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period equal or exceed OMR 38,500 (or with voluntary election at OMR 19,250), file the VAT registration application in the OTA VAT module, declaring activities, expected supplies, and opening balance sheet; a 15-digit VAT Registration Number is then issued. Step 4: Obtain ancillary identifiers - Oman Chamber of Commerce and Industry (OCIC) membership, PASI (Social Protection Fund) registration, municipal license, and a customs 'Bayan' registrant code - prior to any export shipment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Oman is a unitary civil-law tax jurisdiction without US-style check-the-box elections. Available statutory elections include: (1) voluntary VAT registration at OMR 19,250; (2) VAT Tax Group election for related resident entities (Art. 15, RDL 121/2020); (3) reduced 3% SME CIT rate election for taxable profits up to OMR 100,000 (Art. 42, Income Tax Law, RDL 28/2009 as amended by RDL 59/2023); (4) OTA-approved non-calendar tax year for CIT; (5) transfer-pricing Advance Pricing Agreement application; (6) OECD Pillar Two 15% global minimum top-up tax registration/filing duties for MNE groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750 million.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 38,500 (mandatory registration for taxable supplies in a rolling 12-month period); OMR 19,250 (voluntary registration threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Art. 32 VAT Law and Art. 41 Executive Regulation No. 341/2021 zero-rate: (a) goods physically exported from Oman to a destination outside the GCC Implementing States (or to another GCC state for consumption there), provided the goods leave Oman within 90 days of the supply date; (b) transport and ancillary services directly related to such export; (c) specified services supplied to non-resident recipients not effectively consumed in Oman. The supplier must hold documentary proof of export and must apply the zero rate only when the export evidence exists at the time the VAT return is filed. Dental CAD/CAM zirconia blocks supplied to foreign dental laboratories and milling centers qualify under (a) if title and risk pass outside Oman, the goods are cleared through the 'Bayan' export system, and the customer is outside the GCC Implementing States.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Oman Customs 'Bayan' export clearance showing declaration number, date, port of exit, and HS code)
  • Original Bill of Lading (sea) / Air Waybill (air) / CMR consignment note (land exports within GCC)
  • Commercial invoice and matching VAT tax invoice (ISIC 3250 dental consumables description, HS code, quantity, unit price, and Incoterms)
  • Packing list and container/cargo manifest (total gross weight, number of packages, port of discharge)
  • Carrier or terminal loading receipt proving physical removal from Oman (vessel/aircraft name, ETD, loading date)
  • Signed purchase order or international supply agreement with the foreign dental laboratory/distributor
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 (TT payment) evidencing receipt of foreign-currency consideration from the overseas buyer

No compulsory real-time fiscal clearance or digital-stamping e-invoicing regime is currently in effect in Oman. OTA requires every taxable supply to be evidenced by a VAT tax invoice containing the full statutory data set (sequential number, invoice date, supply date, seller and buyer VAT registration numbers, description, quantity, unit price, 5% VAT amount, and total), retained in original form for 6 years. The OTA has announced a phased national e-invoicing mandate (2024-2027) with API-based submission and integration with the 'Bayan' customs platform; until the mandate enters into force, B2B/B2G electronic invoices are acceptable but not obligatory, and no digital stamping obligation applies.

OTA risk-assurance systems automatically screen zero-rated exports using HS-code analytics, counterparty tax-residency checks, related-party pricing flags, and refund-size thresholds. If the export documentation bundle is incomplete or inconsistent, the OTA rejects the zero rate and reassesses 5% VAT, adding a late-payment penalty and administrative fines under RDL 121/2020; deliberate documentary falsification or sham export arrangements constitute tax evasion (Art. 63 VAT Law) carrying imprisonment of up to 5 years and substantial monetary fines, with clawback of any refunded amounts and possible suspension of VAT registration. Oman maintains no statutory FX repatriation deadline (Rial Omani is fully convertible); consequently, the numeric field is set to zero. In audit practice, the OTA expects export proceeds to be received in normal trade terms, generally within 180 days of export clearance; longer collection cycles will trigger deepened genuineness testing and potential recharacterization as a domestic taxable supply. All export evidence, VAT records, and CIT books must be retained for at least 6 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible on the current account, with Oman accepting Article VIII obligations of the IMF. Capital account transactions, including inbound foreign direct investment and repatriation of capital, are generally liberalized but may require post-incorporation notification, registration, or an investment license from MCIIP. Profit and dividend remittances are permitted for foreign-owned SPCs once audited financial statements and tax clearance from the Oman Tax Authority are obtained; however, banks apply strict anti-money laundering (AML) screening and must be satisfied with ultimate beneficial ownership documentation. No exchange control approval is required for current account payments, including trade settlements and service fees, subject to compliance with CBO-issued circulars and the Commercial Companies Law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose a mandatory statutory surrender requirement on export proceeds or an obligatory conversion ratio to OMR. Export receipts denominated in foreign currency may be retained in foreign currency accounts with licensed Omani banks. Nevertheless, exporters must ensure that title documents and bills of lading are presented to their bank to support the underlying trade through normal banking channels, and the proceeds from dental zirconia block shipments should be settled within standard commercial terms through SWIFT or correspondent banking, with any unexplained delays or routing discrepancies subject to AML reporting. The Oman Tax Authority has not issued specific deeming provisions for unrepatriated export receipts, but treasury policy should confirm receipts are booked in the Omani SPC's books and reflected in audited accounts.

Central bank supervision: The Central Bank of Oman (CBO) is the primary regulatory authority for banking, foreign exchange, and payment systems, together with the Capital Market Authority (CMA) and the Oman Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MCIIP) for foreign investment approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) accounts are not recognized by the Central Bank of Oman as a substitute for a regulated Omani bank account. A foreign-owned SPC must maintain its primary operating and capital accounts with an Omani-licensed commercial bank because invoicing, tax registration, export proceeds collection, and FX transactions require a local banking relationship. CBO-licensed payment fintechs may provide ancillary payment initiation, card issuance, or digital onboarding services, but full treasury clearing must be through SWIFT or correspondent banking channels with a licensed Omani bank. Feasibility rating: Low as a standalone liquidity rail; possible Medium as an auxiliary e-commerce collection tool only after primary bank account activation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the foreign parent or shareholder must be routed through a SWIFT MT103 transfer to the SPC's operational account at an Omani bank, with the payment reference clearly describing 'equity injection–foreign direct investment' and the SPC's commercial registration number. The remitter should use appropriate cross-border payment purpose codes consistent with the IMF Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6), typically equity capital that is recorded under direct investment; Omani banks may require completion of a Capital and Money Market Transaction Form or a Foreign Investment Registration Form. The bank will verify the CR certificate, the MOICI/SOCPA investment license (if applicable), and the capital allocation in the constitutive documents. To avoid AML delay, the client must provide the bank's compliance team with the board resolution, signed subscription letter, passport copies of the investor, and proof of source of funds; no cash or third-party funding is permitted for equity injection.Outbound trade settlements for import of raw materials or distribution of finished dental zirconia blocks should be executed via SWIFT MT103 wire payments for payments under open account and documentary collections, or through irrevocable commercial letters of credit (L/C) for large value or recurring shipments. For import L/Cs, the Omani bank requires a proforma invoice, a valid import license, and customs code declaration; for export outbound payments to distributors, the bank will assess the sales contract, invoice, and transport documents. Trade disbursement timing should align with CBO rules that require banks to report foreign transfers and to screen all SWIFT messages against sanctioned entities. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 1,000,000 aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions in a tax year triggers the obligation to prepare and submit a Local File to the Oman Tax Authority. MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of at least OMR 375,000,000 are also required to prepare a Master File and a Country-by-Country Report in accordance with the OECD action 13 framework.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations statute for manufacturing companies; however, the Income Tax Law and Commercial Companies Law contain anti-abuse and substance-over-form doctrines requiring every resident company to maintain real management, employees, premises, and operational assets in Oman. The physical manufacturing of dental zirconia blocks using industrial plant, floor staff, quality laboratories, and local inventory in Oman constitutes substantial economic substance. The Tax Authority may disregard artificial interposition of shell arrangements or contracts whose sole purpose is to reduce taxable profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Falsification of Books

Statutory sanction: Under Royal Decree 28/2009 as amended, tax evasion is punishable with a penalty equal to 100% of the evaded tax, additionally a fine of not less than OMR 1,000 and imprisonment for a term not exceeding 3 years; evaded tax and penalties are collected by compulsory enforcement against the company assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved or participated in falsification are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, fines, and statutory surcharges. Oman courts have ordered travel bans, passport seizure, and freezing of personal bank accounts of directors where the company was used as an instrument of fiscal fraud; the corporate veil is pierced under the Commercial Companies Law.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Tax Authority may adjust the transaction to an arm's length price, impose additional CIT at 15% on the underreported profit, charge interest of 1% per month on the additional tax, and levy a separate administrative penalty up to 50% of the additional tax determined. Where documentation is deliberately missing, the penalty may increase to 100% of the adjustment.

Director personal liability: A director who signs a transfer pricing declaration knowing it to be untrue is personally liable for the additional tax without relief from article 48 of the Companies Law. The director may be disqualified from holding directorships in Oman and can be subject to a travel ban during tax collection proceedings.
Shell Company / Sham Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Tax Authority may disregard the corporate form and reattribute the entire revenue to the controlling foreign entity, assess CIT at 15% on deemed Omani-source profit, cancel the Commercial Registration, and order the liquidation of the SPC. The Ministry of Commerce can also impose administrative fines for conducting commercial activity without genuine establishment.

Director personal liability: Oman courts treat lack of substance as fraudulent abuse of the company form; directors and the beneficial owner are jointly and severally liable for the company debts including tax assessments. The director's personal assets may be attached, a travel ban can be issued until the deemed tax is settled, and the foreign principal can be interdicted from future Omani trade.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Breach in Wire/TT Payment Streams

Statutory sanction: Under the Omani Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism Law, as amended by Royal Decree 7/2021, intentional AML violations carry a fine up to OMR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years; funds and assets from the relevant transactions are confiscated. Additionally, the company loses customs and industrial licences for dental medical device exports.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated compliance officer are criminally liable if they knew or wilfully ignored the source of funds. Oman law permits asset freezing, passport seizure, and travel bans. Personal criminal liability extends to imprisonment of the responsible director, and the company's banking facilities may be terminated by the regulator.
