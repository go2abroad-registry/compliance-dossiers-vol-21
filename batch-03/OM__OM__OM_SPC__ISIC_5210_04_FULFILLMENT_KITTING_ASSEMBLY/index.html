Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) - Official national tax authority; e-services portal accessible at https://tms.tax.gov.om, principal website https://tax.gov.om

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) must first be incorporated as a limited liability entity under the Oman Commercial Companies Law. After the commercial registration certificate and Constitution are issued by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion, the SPC must register with the Oman Tax Authority through its online TMS portal (https://tms.tax.gov.om). The online application requires the commercial registration certificate, the SPC's Memorandum/Constitution, passport copies and address evidence for all shareholders/directors, and details of the registered office in Oman. Upon approval, the SPC is issued a Tax Identification Number (TIN). A separate VAT ID is automatically assigned or additionally applied for on the same portal if the SPC's taxable supplies in Oman exceed or are expected to exceed the statutory VAT registration threshold, or if it voluntarily registers. Non-resident foreign suppliers without PE activity in Oman may be required to register for VAT in limited categories, but the standard Oman SPC is a domestic taxable person.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or elective pass-through regime exists under Omani tax law; a foreign-owned SPC is mandatorily taxed as a corporate entity under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 28/2009). The only relevant statutory election is a tax group election for wholly-owned or at-least-75%-owned Omani subsidiaries, allowing consolidated taxable profits submission with OTA approval. No hybrid mismatch election is available under domestic Omani law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration threshold: OMR 38,500 of annual taxable supplies or taxable expenses (for resident persons); voluntary registration threshold: OMR 19,250; non-residents making no exempt supplies in Oman are required to register without threshold when they make a taxable supply subject to reverse charge or import VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT-registered Oman SPC may apply the 0% VAT rate on the export of tangible movable goods to a customer located outside the GCC (or, in limited B2B cases, to GCC customers meeting verification conditions), provided the goods leave Oman under customs supervision and the supplier retains specified export proof. Export-related services and services directly related to export goods may also be zero-rated if the services are supplied to a non-taxable person outside Oman or the services are on-exported, subject to the statutory conditions and supporting documentation. The kitting, packaging and assembly activities of the SPC under ISIC 5210, even when performed in an Oman logistics/re-export zone, are not zero-rated as a category; zero-rating applies only at the point of approved export supply of goods and qualifying ancillary services. The standard rate of 5% applies to local kitting, B2B handling and fulfillment service fees unless those services qualify for export zero-rating under the VAT Law and Executive Regulations.

As of the current stage, Oman does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime for electronic invoices. However, OTA mandates electronic filing of VAT returns and supporting schedules via the TMS e-services portal, and taxpayers must issue valid VAT invoices and retain copies and transactional XML/EDI records for five years for audit purposes. OTA has announced staged rollout of an e-invoicing framework, so future clearance mandates are to be monitored.

OTA scrutinizes zero-rated export claims on a transactional basis. In the event of fraudulent, incorrect, or improperly substantiated zero-rating, the VAT input credits previously relieved against zero-rated output are retrospectively clawed back, and the SPC becomes liable to a 5% VAT assessment on the full export supply. Late compliance and evasion charges are imposed in addition, including financial penalties under Royal Decree 121/2020 and its Executive Regulations (typically ranging from OMR 250 to OMR 1,000 for filing violations, plus up to 200% of the tax amount for tax evasion). In egregious cases, criminal fines and imprisonment may be applied, and the SPC's tax payment certificate or import/export privileges may be suspended by OTA and the Royal Oman Police Customs authorities until restitution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible. Since 1986, the exchange rate has been pegged to the US Dollar at a fixed central rate of 1.000 OMR = 2.6008 USD. Oman has accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF, and in principle there are no restrictions on current-account payments, direct investment, profit repatriation, dividend remittance, or capital transfers. In practice, all such foreign-exchange transactions must be routed through a CBO-licensed Authorized Dealer Bank and must satisfy transaction-level documentary requirements; banks will require the corporate commercial registration (CR) number, tax registration, audited/management financials, board or shareholder resolutions, and tax clearance where applicable. There are no quantitative controls, remittance fees, withholding requirements, central-bank conversion, or surrender impositions on the Omani Rial for ordinary commercial purposes, although large or unusual transfers are subject to AML/KYC and sanction screens and CBO statistical reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation in Oman to surrender export proceeds to the CBO or to any local bank, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies to foreign-currency export receipts. An Omani exporter, including the foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC), may lawfully retain export earnings abroad or convert them at a licensed dealer bank as commercial discretion permits. However, the CBO's cross-border payments reporting regime, including the ITRS-style inward/outward transfer forms and the Anti-Money Laundering Law, requires the receiving bank and the exporter to identify, substantiate, and archive: the sales contract, invoice, bill of lading/airway bill, delivery evidence, customs export declaration, and trade payments documentation. Export receipts must pass through a licensed Omani financial institution for local recordation if they are to be later remitted as dividend or capital distribution; a minimum retention period of five years is recommended to satisfy bank, CBO, and Oman Tax Authority audit requirements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), operating principally under the Banking Law (Royal Decree No. 114/2000), is the sole currency, monetary, banking, and foreign-exchange regulatory authority in Oman. It licenses Authorized Dealer Banks, administers the Sultanate's exchange control and cross-border balance-of-payments reporting, and supervises anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing obligations for banks, exchange houses, and payment service providers. Certain listed derivatives and securities-market matters may also fall within the remit of the Capital Market Authority (CMA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise Business, Payoneer) have only conditional viability as a supplementary cross-border collection or disbursement rail. They are not a substitute for a licensed Omani bank account because these EMIs are not regulated by the CBO and cannot lawfully accept deposits or conduct money-exchange business in Oman without a CBO license. In practice, an offshore EMI may be used to receive payments from foreign marketplace customers in USD, EUR, GBP or AED and issue transfer instructions to suppliers, but the final OMR liquidity position must reside within an Omani licensed bank; the EMI must be onboarded as an inbound remittance originator and will be treated as a third-party payment institution for AML screening. Feasibility rating: Medium-Low as a primary treasury bank; Medium-High as a supplemental third-party logistics receivables collector, provided the underlying SPC retains the mandatory local bank ledger and CBO reporting pathway.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital flows must be routed through a CBO-licensed Authorized Dealer Bank via SWIFT MT103 or equivalent high-value payment message. The remittance instruction must include: (i) the SPC's full legal name and Commercial Registration (CR) number; (ii) the Tax Identification Number in Oman; (iii) a purpose description strictly aligned with equity capital or shareholder loan, e.g., 'Paid-up share capital in Omani SPC' or 'Conversion of shareholder loan to equity'; (iv) the bank account number and account name in the receiving Omani bank; and (v) the underlying source account held in the name of the foreign shareholder. The receiving bank will require CBO-required reporting forms (inward transfer and Balance-of-Payments/I-TRS codes), often categorized as inward direct investment capital, and will validate that the sender matches the registered shareholder. To avoid delays, the SPC should upload the notarized corporate registration, share register, shareholder resolution, and capital injection letter through the bank's onboarding portal before the wire is sent. After receipt, the funds should be designated as a capital reserve or used to fund the capital escrow attestation, and the statutory capital confirmation and tax books must record the injection before any operational use.Outbound trade payments for raw materials, consumables, subcontract services, and logistics in the fulfillment/kitting business should settle through: (1) SWIFT MT103 for standard supplier invoices, remittance instructions, and low-value/medium-volume settlements; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700, MT707, MT720) through an Omani issuing bank when a supplier requires documentary security or when the import amount is above a bank-tolerated threshold; (3) SWIFT gpi tracked payments for time-critical disbursements; (4) regional clearing for UAE and GCC transactions through the corresponding Gulf payment/clearing channels at the banks' discretion; (5) trade loans or trusted buyer financing facilities against shipping documents for high-value raw material purchases. Each outward payment must be supported by: the supplier invoice, purchase order, HS6 classification and national tariff code, customs company status from Oman Customs, certificate of origin, bill of lading/airway bill where applicable, and a sanctions screen confirming that the beneficiary and intermediary banks are not blocked. Tax withholding certificates under the Oman Tax Law must be preserved where the payment attracts withholding tax on foreign services; no statutory withholding applies to ordinary goods purchases unless the bank requires a withholding certificate for services contracts.Plain-vanilla FX forward contracts between OMR and USD through a CBO licensed Authorized Dealer bank, subject to an underlying commercial transaction or proven exposureFX swap contracts (OMR/USD, OMR/AED and OMR/EUR) for short-term liquidity smoothing between the SPC's trade creditors and pooling accountsCross-currency interest rate swaps (Omani Rial against USD/EUR/GCC currencies) to hedge medium- and long-term capital-asset and lease financingNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in illiquid counterparty currencies where a local Omani bank has correspondent arrangement or offshore parent support, only when underlying trade or debt exposure can be documentedCBO-mandated delivery-based forwards and plain-vanilla options permitted by the bank's risk-tolerance policy; exotic FX derivatives are prohibited unless specifically pre-cleared by the Central Bank of Oman

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise Business, Payoneer) have only conditional viability as a supplementary cross-border collection or disbursement rail. They are not a substitute for a licensed Omani bank account because these EMIs are not regulated by the CBO and cannot lawfully accept deposits or conduct money-exchange business in Oman without a CBO license. In practice, an offshore EMI may be used to receive payments from foreign marketplace customers in USD, EUR, GBP or AED and issue transfer instructions to suppliers, but the final OMR liquidity position must reside within an Omani licensed bank; the EMI must be onboarded as an inbound remittance originator and will be treated as a third-party payment institution for AML screening. Feasibility rating: Medium-Low as a primary treasury bank; Medium-High as a supplemental third-party logistics receivables collector, provided the underlying SPC retains the mandatory local bank ledger and CBO reporting pathway.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% on taxable income exceeding OMR 100,000; first OMR 100,000 taxed at 3% (progressive band per Royal Decree 24/2023); single national-level tax, no sub-federal corporate levies

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Oman imposes no withholding tax on dividends; profit distributions from the SPC to a 100% foreign sole shareholder are tax-free at source and require no OTA clearance certificate

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory when the Omani entity's annual turnover exceeds OMR 3 million; Master File mandatory when the worldwide group's consolidated turnover exceeds OMR 50 million; Country-by-Country Report when the ultimate parent group's consolidated turnover equals or exceeds the OECD-equivalent threshold (approx. OMR 315 million / EUR 750 million); entities below OMR 3 million file a simplified TP disclosure

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) under Royal Decree 74/2020 are applicable: third-party fulfillment, kitting and custom assembly constitute a 'Distribution and Service Centre' relevant activity. The SPC must demonstrate (i) direction and management exercised in Oman through local board meetings and records, (ii) adequate physical premises, qualified employees and operating expenditure in Oman, and (iii) core income-generating activities (order receipt, kitting, quality control, inventory management, dispatch) performed in Oman. An annual ESR notification/report must be filed with the competent authority; absence of substance risks recharacterization as a shell company and loss of tax benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Assessment on an estimated basis, penalty equal to 100% of the evaded tax (effectively doubling the liability), 1% monthly late-payment interest, and criminal referral to the Public Prosecution; monetary fines up to OMR 50,000 for fraudulent declarations

Director personal liability: Directors and the foreign sole shareholder acting as shadow manager face criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years; the OTA can obtain travel bans, freeze and attach personal assets, and hold directors jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the tax shortfall
Shell Company / ESR Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ESR administrative penalties up to OMR 20,000, mandatory disclosure orders, upward tax assessments at 15% plus monthly interest and penalties, and suspension or revocation of the Commercial Registration by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion

Director personal liability: Where the shell arrangement defrauds the Tax Authority or creditors, directors are personally liable for unpaid taxes and liquidation shortfalls; travel bans may be imposed during assessment disputes, and commercial licence revocation triggers personal civil restitution obligations
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The OTA makes a discretionary TP adjustment, levies 15% corporate tax on the uplifted amount, charges 1% monthly interest, and imposes documentation-omission fines of up to OMR 10,000 per document package; repeat violations trigger escalated fines and audit referral

Director personal liability: Directors who sign intercompany agreements unsupported by TP analyses are jointly liable for the additional tax and penalties; deliberate TP manipulation is prosecuted as tax evasion with imprisonment up to 5 years and travel bans pending resolution
AML / Sanctions / Structuring via Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Offences under the Omani Anti-Money Laundering Law (RD 30/2016): imprisonment up to 7 years and/or fines from OMR 5,000 to OMR 50,000, confiscation of proceeds, revocation of the commercial licence, and permanent debarment from bank and fintech payment networks

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is routinely pierced in AML prosecutions: directors, the beneficial owner and the designated compliance officer face personal criminal conviction, civil restitution of the laundered amounts, seizure of personal bank accounts, and court-imposed travel bans pending trial
