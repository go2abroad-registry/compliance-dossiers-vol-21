Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), established as an autonomous body under Royal Decree 52/2023, succeeding the Secretariat General of Taxation at the Ministry of Finance. National e-services portal: https://taxoman.gov.om/ (e-Tax Portal). Customs enforcement and export declarations are handled through the Royal Oman Police Customs Directorate via the 'Bayan' single-window system. Corporate income tax and VAT share the same OTA digital registration infrastructure.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Single Person Company (SPC) with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) and activity license; 100% foreign ownership is permitted, no statutory minimum share capital applies, and a licensed local office and resident manager are required. Step 2: Register with OTA for corporate income tax via the e-Tax Portal using the CR number, filing the attested Memorandum/Articles of Association, ownership chart, board/shareholder resolutions, registered office lease, resident manager appointment, financial year-end election and opening balance sheet; registration is required before commencement of activity or within 30 days of CR issuance. Step 3: Apply concurrently for VAT registration where taxable supplies, including zero-rated export supplies, are expected to exceed OMR 38,500 in any 12-month period (voluntary registration threshold: OMR 19,250); the application must be submitted within 30 days from the end of the month in which the threshold was exceeded. Step 4: OTA issues a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) covering CIT and VAT, activates the taxpayer's e-invoicing profile and VAT returns, and registers the SPC's bank accounts; the foreign sole shareholder does not require a separate Omani TIN unless it itself creates a permanent establishment in Oman.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Omani SPC is a resident juridical person and a separate taxpayer from its sole shareholder. Corporate income tax is imposed on worldwide taxable income of the resident company; taxable income up to OMR 100,000 is effectively exempt, and taxable income exceeding OMR 100,000 is taxed at 15% under Royal Decree 28/2009 as amended by Royal Decree 9/2017. A branch of a foreign company is taxed only on Oman-source income. Oman applies no pass-through or check-the-box regime. Dividends distributed by the SPC to a foreign shareholder are not subject to Omani withholding tax under current law. If the SPC were licensed within a Special Economic Zone (e.g., Duqm, Sohar, Salalah), a separate zone-based exemption or 0% regime may Apply only to income derived from zone-licensed activities, but the standard national classification remains entity-level taxation.

Available election forms

Oman has no US-style check-the-box election and no elective hybrid mismatch classification. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are controlled by statutory denial rules and the OECD-aligned transfer pricing framework. Available elections and options include: (i) annual accounting period: with OTA approval, the SPC may elect a fiscal year different from the calendar year, consistent with its audited financial statements; (ii) small business relief: taxable income not exceeding OMR 100,000 is exempt, but the claim must be documented in the CIT return; (iii) VAT grouping: related resident juridical persons may jointly apply to OTA under VAT Law Article 12 to be treated as a single taxable person, eliminating intra-group VAT charges; (iv) filing frequency: an exporter may elect monthly VAT returns to accelerate input VAT recovery; and (v) transfer pricing: no routine election is available, but the taxpayer may request an advance pricing arrangement (APA) or mutual agreement procedure (MAP) to secure arm's-length treatment for heparin fractionation tolling fees and royalty payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration: OMR 38,500 (approximately USD 100,000) of taxable supplies, including zero-rated export supplies, in the preceding 12 months or expected in the next 12 months (look-back and look-forward tests). Voluntary registration threshold: OMR 19,250 (approximately USD 50,000). Non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Oman or imports subject to reverse charge must register without threshold. An export-only SPC is eligible for mandatory registration once the zero-rated export supply value exceeds the threshold, or may voluntarily register to recover input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to the export of heparin sodium API finished goods where: (i) the goods are physically exported from Oman to a destination outside Oman and outside the GCC Implementing States; (ii) the export is effected by the Omani supplier, either through its own transport arrangement or through a licensed carrier/forwarder acting on the supplier's contractual responsibility; (iii) the goods leave Oman within 90 days from the date of supply, unless OTA approves a longer period; and (iv) the supplier holds contemporaneous proof of export comprising the customs export declaration, international transport document and bank evidence of foreign proceeds. Related international transport services, freight forwarding, handling and inspection services directly connected to the exported goods are also zero-rated. The OTA verifies zero-rating through electronic matching of VAT return codes, e-invoice data and Bayan customs export records. Refund of input VAT attributable to zero-rated supplies is recoverable through periodic VAT returns, with excess credits refundable by OTA; export-only businesses typically file monthly to accelerate refunds.

  • Customs Export Declaration generated in the Royal Oman Police Customs 'Bayan' single-window system, showing the export declaration number, commodity tariff code (HS 3001.90 for heparin), shipment weight, exit port and customs exit validation
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued to the order of the Omani exporting SPC evidencing physical export and destination outside Oman
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for the export consignment referencing the zero-rated VAT e-invoice number and the specific export shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 credit advice from a licensed Omani bank confirming receipt of export sale proceeds from the foreign buyer

Phased e-invoicing mandate under OTA Decision No. 57/2023 and subsequent implementing circulars. VAT-registered suppliers must issue structured electronic invoices in XML/JSON format using OTA-approved software, including a unique UUID, issuer digital signature or cryptographic stamp, and a machine-readable QR code. Invoices must be transmitted to the OTA e-invoicing platform for validation and clearance in real time or near-real time (within 24 hours of issuance); the OTA returns a clearing status and validation code. Large VAT taxpayers (annual turnover at or above OMR 5 million) are in the first mandatory implementation wave, with medium and small taxpayers onboarded in later phases. The OTA platform integrates with the Bayan customs system, enabling automatic matching of export declarations, e-invoices and VAT returns. Summarized tax invoices are not permitted for standard B2B export supplies, and taxpayers must retain the structured machine-readable invoice files for 8 years.

OTA audits export zero-rating claims against the Bayan customs declaration, transport documentation, e-invoice validation codes and bank inflows. If the export evidence is missing, inconsistent or fraudulent, the zero rate is retrospectively denied, output VAT at 5% is assessed, input VAT credits are clawed back, and statutory per-week delay penalties apply on the unpaid VAT. Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating documentation constitutes tax evasion under VAT Law RD 121/2020, punishable by imprisonment of up to 3 years and fines of up to OMR 50,000, in addition to administrative penalties, public naming by OTA, suspension of the taxpayer's e-invoicing registration, travel restrictions and extension of the assessment limitation period. Voluntary disclosure made before the commencement of an OTA audit can substantially reduce penalties. VAT and CIT records supporting export zero-rating must be retained for 8 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible on both current and capital account; the CBO administers a fixed exchange-rate regime preserving OMR 1 = USD 2.6008 (selling) and USD 1 = OMR 0.3845 (buying), with no central-bank prior-approval gate for capital inflows into a 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company. Capital-account convertibility extends to equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds, conditioned only on registration of the capital with the Commercial Registry and on filing the foreign-investment statistical declaration through a CBO-licensed bank. Profit remittance is unrestricted after audited financial statements are filed and corporate tax obligations are satisfied under the Income Tax Law (latest consolidation by Royal Decree 50/2023); Oman applies a 0% withholding tax on dividends to non-resident shareholders, while interest, royalties, and certain services remain within the 10% withholding-tax net. No exchange-control surcharge, forced lending rule, or staged conversion schedule applies to an active pharmaceutical extraction business such as ISIC 3250 heparin sodium fractionation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory surrender ratio and no mandatory central-bank conversion on export receipts; proceeds may be retained in OMR, USD, EUR, or other freely convertible currencies in CBO-authorised accounts. Nevertheless, banking-compliance rules under the AML/CFT Law (Royal Decree 30/2016) require an exporting SPC to repatriate full proceeds through a CBO-licensed bank within 90 days of the bill-of-lading date, to file the customs export declaration and a foreign-exchange worksheet for each heparin sodium consignment, and to maintain a complete trade-cycle dossier (sale contract, commercial invoice, quality certificate, packing list, and transport documents). Netting or cross-invoice offsetting of export proceeds outside a licensed bank is prohibited; irrevocable documentary-credit proceeds routed directly from the issuing bank are the preferred and fully compliant compliance path.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as the primary treasury rail; High viability only as an ancillary collection/payment aggregator. The CBO does not license offshore electronic-money institutions, and the National Payment Systems Law (Royal Decree 29/2021) confines cross-border clearing membership to CBO-licensed banks and sanctioned payment-service providers. A foreign fintech platform may therefore be used for convenience invoicing or low-value supplier disbursements, but it cannot hold export proceeds, issue OMR IBANs, evidence the export-repatriation rule, or support commercial LC operations. For a Medium_Risk_Enhanced pharmaceutical API entity, reliance on an offshore EMI as the main account would trigger adverse correspondent-bank scrutiny and is not recommended.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability as the primary treasury rail; High viability only as an ancillary collection/payment aggregator. The CBO does not license offshore electronic-money institutions, and the National Payment Systems Law (Royal Decree 29/2021) confines cross-border clearing membership to CBO-licensed banks and sanctioned payment-service providers. A foreign fintech platform may therefore be used for convenience invoicing or low-value supplier disbursements, but it cannot hold export proceeds, issue OMR IBANs, evidence the export-repatriation rule, or support commercial LC operations. For a Medium_Risk_Enhanced pharmaceutical API entity, reliance on an offshore EMI as the main account would trigger adverse correspondent-bank scrutiny and is not recommended.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Oman does not prescribe a public Local File/Master File de minimis threshold. In practice, the Oman Tax Authority expects arm's length pricing documentation for all controlled transactions. As a safe harbor governance threshold, maintain a contemporaneous Local File where aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed OMR 500,000 in a tax period; prepare a Master File where the multinational group has consolidated turnover exceeding OMR 20,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Oman has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain other GCC states. However, the Oman Income Tax Law and tax audit practice require real local substance: either registered office or local place of business, active decision-making in Oman, appropriate employees, operational premises, and bank accounts. A foreign-owned SPC with only nominee directors, no staff, and no operational control over heparin fractionation risks being characterized as a shell company, triggering denial of tax exemptions/treaty benefits, tax residence recharacterization, and anti-abuse assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Revenue Suppression

Statutory sanction: Confiscation of evaded tax, additional assessment for six years, automatic surcharges, administrative fines up to OMR 10,000, and criminal prosecution under the Oman Income Tax Law and Penal Code; involvement of the Oman Tax Authority and Public Prosecution can lead to prison terms up to three years.

Director personal liability: Directors and the sole shareholder of the SPC face personal civil restitution for unjust enrichment, asset freezes, travel bans, disqualification from managing Omani companies, and imprisonment.
Artificial Shell SPC / Lack of Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: Rejection of the SPC as the taxable operator; reattribution of the full sales margin to the foreign principal; withdrawal of import/export manufacturing licence; dissolution under the Commercial Companies Law; and penalty of up to OMR 5,000 plus reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false annual filings or permit the shell arrangement can be personally assessed for outstanding corporate tax, prohibited from re-entry into Oman, and made liable to compensate the Treasury through asset confiscation.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation of Raw Material Flows

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment by the Oman Tax Authority, additional taxable income assessed at 15%, late-payment surcharges, fines up to OMR 10,000, and possible criminal referral for false tax declarations; comparable OECD TP penalties may also apply if deliberately concealed.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved contrived intercompany pricing are jointly liable for resulting tax and penalties; repeated wilful misconduct may trigger personal criminal liability and repatriation restrictions.
AML / Money Laundering in Pharmaceutical Payments

Statutory sanction: Asset freezing, seizure of the transaction value, administrative fines, and imprisonment under the Oman AML Law; registration with the National Committee for Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Risks and the CBO Financial Intelligence Unit. Penalties can include up to ten years' imprisonment and confiscation of the SPC's assets.

Director personal liability: The sole shareholder/director of the SPC is subject to personal criminal indictment, personal asset forfeiture, travel ban, and debit of the SPC's obligations from personal assets where the corporate veil has been used to launder proceeds.
Mischaracterization of IP Royalties as Management Fees

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax re-levied at 10% on the true royalty character, denial of the expense deduction, additional penalties up to OMR 10,000, and criminal sanction for false documentation; the gross-up liability falls on the SPC.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax and penalties if they knowingly signed or caused the mischaracterized agreements; professional license suspension and disqualification from board roles may follow.
Persistent Non-Filing and Evasion of Assessment

Statutory sanction: Best-of-judgment assessment by the Tax Authority, mandatory fine of at least OMR 500 plus daily escalation, surcharge of 10% to 25% of unpaid tax, revocation of tax clearance/certificate, suspension of import/export activity, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for the tax debt where non-filing was intentional, plus imprisonment and a travel ban; the Commercial Register may prevent incorporation/management of any new Omani entity within the individual's name.
