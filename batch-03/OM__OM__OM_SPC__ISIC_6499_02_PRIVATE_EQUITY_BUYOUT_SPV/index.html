Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Single Person Company; the Memorandum of Association fixes the authorized and issued capital, which must be paid into the company's bank account upon incorporation. No escrow account is statutorily required, but source-of-funds evidence may be requested by AML compliance for foreign-owned investments.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (General Authority for Taxation) under the Ministry of Finance; e-services portal: https://taxes.gov.om

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Single Person Company must first obtain a Commercial Registration Certificate (CRC) from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) via the InvestEasy platform. Thereafter, the entity must apply for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) through the Tax Authority's online portal by submitting the notarized Articles of Association (Single Person Company contract), CRC, board/shareholder resolution, passport copies and residency documents of the sole shareholder and manager, proof of registered Omani address, and a power of attorney for the local tax agent if applicable. Registration for VAT is a separate obligation triggered upon exceeding the statutory threshold; both TIN and VAT registration are administered through the same unified taxpayer portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SPC is a separate juridical person subject to Omani corporate income tax at the headline rate on its worldwide income; no fiscal-transparency or look-through treatment is available, and exports/services are within the territorial scope of Omani income tax law. Tax-exempt zone relief may apply only if the entity operates inside an approved Free Zone (e.g., Duqm, Sohar, Salalah) with an investment certification; the present private-equity SPV domiciled in the general territory is ordinarily fully subject to entity-level taxation.

Available election forms

No check-the-box, partnership-passthrough, or hybrid-mismatch elections exist under Omani tax law. A Single Person Company is mandatorily taxed as an entity. Loss carry-forward of five years is permitted without a statutory election; group consolidation or group relief is not available. An SPC may elect to be registered for VAT voluntarily from OMR 19,250 of annual supplies even when the mandatory threshold is not met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 38,500 (approx. USD 100,000) of taxable annual supplies or expenses; voluntary registration: OMR 19,250 (approx. USD 50,000). A non-resident supplier is required to register for accountable-person status if it makes taxable supplies to non-VAT-registered customers in Oman, with the SPC as an Omani tax resident registering through the standard threshold test on its Omani taxable turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Oman VAT where the supplier exports movable goods out of the Sultanate within 90 days of the date of supply, provided the goods leave Oman in the same condition and the export is evidenced by customs documents. Services can be zero-rated when the recipient is a non-resident who belongs in a foreign country and the services are not directly related to Omani real estate or movable goods in Oman, or where the services are ancillary to an exported good (Article 46 of the VAT Law). The private-equity SPV's investment-holding activities are not VATable, but any management, advisory or fee arrangements exported to foreign funds must meet the evidentiary standard and the 'belonging' analysis of the recipient to claim 0%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayann) bearing the clearance stamp of the Royal Oman Police – Directorate General of Customs and the unique customs reference number
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or International Consignment Note evidencing physical movement from Oman to the export destination within 90 days
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales/contract documentation identifying the exporter, foreign buyer, and goods description corresponding to the customs declaration
  • Bank advice or SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation evidencing inward remittance of export proceeds to an Oman-licensed bank account
  • Packing list, certificate of origin (where required), and insurance certificates supporting the shipment's bona fides

Oman is progressively deploying the 'Mersal' (مرسل) national e-invoicing platform developed by the Tax Authority; as of the current implementation phase, taxpayers are not yet subject to mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping of every invoice. Once fully phased in, supplies from registered persons will require validation of e-invoice XML files through certified service providers or the Mersal API, with QR-code and structured digital signature requirements for B2B and B2G transactions.

The Tax Authority applies strict documentary scrutiny to zero-rating claims, requiring reconciliations between customs export records, VAT return entries, and bank foreign-currency receipts. Where an exporter fails to produce compliant export evidence or the goods do not physically leave Oman within 90 days, output tax at 5% is immediately assessable, and the input VAT previously recovered is clawed back with statutory late-payment penalties. Fraudulent or false zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under Articles 58-59 of the VAT Law, exposing the SPC and its responsible officers to administrative fines of up to 150% of the evaded tax, disqualification from voluntary disclosure, and criminal prosecution with imprisonment of up to three years and/or fines of up to OMR 10,000.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman is an IMF Article VIII member with full current-account convertibility for OMR. Capital-account transactions for a 100% foreign-owned Single Person Company are liberal, requiring only registration with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP). Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted once annual tax clearance (General Authority for Taxes) is obtained, no CBO prior approval is required, and banks will perform standard underlying documentation checks. The OMR is pegged to the USD at 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD, creating predictable transaction exposure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender or conversion requirement for export proceeds in Oman. Exporters may maintain foreign-currency accounts with licensed banks. However, for LBO SPV financing where export-related revenues are expected, the bank shall require the original contract, commercial invoice, and bill of lading to validate the underlying trade for source-of-funds and purpose-of-payment checks.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), acting under the Banking Law and the Foreign Exchange Regulations; CBO also supervises the national payments system and anti-money-laundering e-AML platform (MIAZAN).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for the SPC as a primary banking channel. An offshore EMI (e.g., UK, EU, UAE-based) cannot provide a fully regulated OMR account in Oman without a CBO-licensed payment service provider arrangement. EMIs may be used only for outbound side-settlement after clearing OMR funds into a UAE FBO account? Nevertheless, for compliance with Oman’s source-of-funds rules, all inbound PE equity capital must land in a CBO-licensed bank account. Offshore EMIs are not recognized for tax registration or Commercial Registry requirements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends paid by an Omani SPC to a non-resident shareholder. DTA relief may reduce the rate to 0%, 5%, or 10% depending on the applicable treaty and the shareholder's ownership percentage.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the close of the fiscal year (31 December for a calendar-year SPC); an extension of up to six months may be applied for through the e-Tax portal before the original deadline.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box A1 Taxpayer identification: legal name, tax registration number, commercial registry number, registered address, legal form, and capitalization. Enter the Oman Tax Registration Number (TRN) exactly as issued; for a foreign-owned SPC, also state the ultimate foreign shareholder's name and residence country for treaty purposes. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 500,000 aggregate controlled transactions per tax year; a Local File and Master File are mandatory once this threshold is exceeded. Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of OMR 75 million or more.

Economic substance scope: Oman's Economic Substance Regulations apply to geographically mobile activities; a pure equity-holding buyout SPV is subject to a notification requirement and must maintain real management, a registered office, and adequate staff in Oman. If the SPV engages in intra-group financing, leasing, or intellectual property holding, it must pass the core income-generating activity test. Failure to satisfy substance requirements may result in denial of treaty benefits, disallowance of deductions, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Fine of not less than OMR 5,000 and up to OMR 100,000, imprisonment for up to three years, additional tax equal to the evaded amount, and administrative surcharges. The Tax Authority may re-assess the SPC under a best-of-information basis and revoke tax clearance certificates.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or cause the SPC to engage in sham transactions may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and civil restitution. The Commercial Companies Law allows veil-piercing where the SPC is used for fraud; offending directors may also face travel bans and criminal imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Denial or clawback of treaty relief, disallowance of intercompany deductions, fines under the Economic Substance Regulations of up to OMR 50,000, and possible deregistration of the commercial license.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders can be made personally liable for additional tax, penalties, and restitution where the corporate form masks the true asset owner. Asset freezes, travel restrictions, and disqualification orders may be issued by the competent court.
AML / Financial Crime / Undisclosed UBO

Statutory sanction: Under the Oman Anti-Money Laundering Law, penalties include imprisonment up to seven years, fines up to OMR 50,000, confiscation of the proceeds of crime, freezing of bank accounts, and cancellation of the entity's tax clearance certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors and the AML compliance officer face personal criminal prosecution for failure to file suspicious transaction reports, civil liability to creditors and regulators, asset freezes, travel bans, and disqualification; the corporate veil will be disregarded where fraud or willful misconduct is established.
