Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Secretariat General of Taxation (SGT), Ministry of Finance, Sultanate of Oman (formerly the Oman Tax Authority); official e-services portal: https://taxes.gov.om.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: obtain Commercial Registration (CR) from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) via the national Invest in Oman / MoCIIP e-services single window; 100% foreign ownership is permitted for a Single Person Company under the Companies Law. Step 2: apply through the SGT/OTA e-services portal (taxes.gov.om) for the unified Tax Registration Number (TRN), attaching the CR, certified MOA/AOA, registered-address proof (lease/title deed), shareholder and director identification, and required licensing documents. Step 3: complete CIT and VAT registration activation and obtain the VAT registration certificate; statutory deadlines run from commencement of commercial activity (generally within 90 days). The TRN is the single identifier used for CIT returns, VAT returns, customs declarations and employer withholding.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the Omani-resident SPC is taxed as a juridical person on worldwide income under the Unified Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 59/2023, applicable to tax periods beginning on or after 1 January 2025); headline rate 9% on taxable income exceeding OMR 100,000 and 0% on the first OMR 100,000; special regimes apply to hydrocarbon (55%) and mining (35%) activities. No pass-through treatment is available to shareholders; 10% final withholding generally applies to dividends, interest, royalties and management fees paid by the SPC to non-residents, subject to any applicable tax treaty relief. — Step 1: obtain Commercial Registration (CR) from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) via the national Invest in Oman / MoCIIP e-services single window; 100% foreign ownership is permitted for a Single Person Company under the Companies Law. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Omani-resident SPC is taxed as a juridical person on worldwide income under the Unified Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 59/2023, applicable to tax periods beginning on or after 1 January 2025); headline rate 9% on taxable income exceeding OMR 100,000 and 0% on the first OMR 100,000; special regimes apply to hydrocarbon (55%) and mining (35%) activities. No pass-through treatment is available to shareholders; 10% final withholding generally applies to dividends, interest, royalties and management fees paid by the SPC to non-residents, subject to any applicable tax treaty relief.

Available election forms

Oman does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch pass-through election. Available statutory elections and applications include: (i) tax-group election to file a consolidated CIT return with directly 100%-owned Omani subsidiaries; (ii) small-taxpayer simplification for gross revenue up to OMR 100,000; (iii) advance pricing agreement (APA) application with the OTA for the franchise royalty and syrup supply chain transfer-pricing margin; (iv) foreign tax credit election for foreign-source income; and (v) VAT elections for voluntary registration at the OMR 19,250 threshold, input-tax apportionment and tax-period selection.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: annual taxable supplies exceeding OMR 38,500 (approximately USD 100,000); voluntary registration: taxable supplies exceeding OMR 19,250. Non-resident suppliers of digital or B2C services in Oman must register regardless of the local threshold, and imported B2B services are subject to reverse-charge VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for 0% VAT if they physically leave Oman within 90 days of the supply date and the supplier retains customs and transport evidence. Export of services, including franchise licensing, royalties, and syrup/IP licences supplied to an offshore franchisee, is zero-rated when the recipient is established outside Oman and the service is not connected with Omani goods or real property (place-of-supply and effective-use-and-enjoyment tests). The SPC may recover attributable input VAT and file periodic VAT refund claims for recurring excess export credits.

  • Oman Customs Export Declaration (Bayan single-window manifest)
  • Certified commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment to the foreign franchisee
  • Signed export/supply contract and, where applicable, certificate of origin
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT payment evidence proving receipt of export proceeds

Phased national e-invoicing mandate under the OTA e-invoicing framework: VAT registrants above prescribed turnover must integrate OTA-approved billing software with the OTA fiscal platform via API, submit XML/UBL invoices for near-real-time validation (clearance), and issue invoices carrying an OTA-generated QR code and digital stamp. Only cleared e-invoices support the recipient's input-VAT deduction. The roll-out is staggered by taxpayer turnover segment, with all registrants required to be on the cleared e-invoicing channel within the published OTA compliance window.

The OTA and Oman Customs cross-match Bayan export declarations with VAT zero-rating schedules and bank remittance records. Goods failing the 90-day physical-export test are recharacterised as taxable supplies at 5% VAT, with input-VAT clawback, late-payment interest and administrative fines. Fraudulent export zero-rating, including false invoices, forged bills of lading and circular remittances, triggers penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded, criminal prosecution or imprisonment under tax offence provisions, loss of export privileges, and commercial-registration sanctions. The OTA's clawback assessment window is generally four years, extended to ten years in cases of fraud or wilful default.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is officially pegged to the USD at a fixed rate of 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD. Oman's exchange control regime is liberal for current account transactions, including dividend and royalty remittances, which are freely transferable after tax settlement and audited financial statements are submitted to the licensed bank. Capital account inflows and outflows, including foreign direct investment equity and shareholder loans, are permitted without prior CBO exchange-control approval for qualifying foreign-owned SPCs, provided funds arrive through licensed banks and are credited to the proper accounts with a purpose code. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal; a fully owned foreign SPC may remit net profits, branch profits, or liquidation proceeds, subject to CBO anti-money-laundering verification, submission of the annual audited accounts, tax clearance (if applicable under Oman Corporate Income Tax Law), and the absence of any commercial or administrative freeze. The rial's convertibility is effectively full for approved current transactions, but for hedging and large capital outflows counterparties are expected to transact within the USD peg corridor and via CBO-regulated swap facilities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose mandatory export proceeds surrender or a statutory conversion ratio on export receipts. Exporters may retain receipts in OMR or in a permitted foreign currency (commonly USD) in a CBO-licensed bank account. For the boba tea franchise supply chain, export proceeds from the sale of syrups and additives to overseas franchisees must be received via a licensed Omani bank within a commercially reasonable period (typically 180 days from shipment), supported by the underlying commercial invoice, bill of lading, and customs export declaration. There is no obligation to forcibly convert USD to OMR; however, OMR-denominated disbursements may require a spot conversion at prevailing market rates within the pegged band.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) in coordination with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion for FDI-related approvals

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low — Offshore digital electronic money institutions and non-bank EMIs cannot obtain CBO e-money licenses without a material in-country presence, and CBO regulations stipulate that payment services and e-money issuance are restricted to licensed Omani payment service providers and banks. The SPC should rely on CBO-regulated Omani banks for cross-border clearing, SWIFT, and FX. Global fintech corridors may only be used as a supplementary collection channel for low-value franchisee POS payments if the EMI has a valid CBO pass-through or is operating via a licensed Omani payment aggregator; otherwise, corporate treasury must execute all high-value royalty flows through SWIFT-enabled commercial banks.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Royalty income from a network of international franchisees with mixed beneficial-owner jurisdictions

Root cause: Royalty income from a network of international franchisees with mixed beneficial-owner jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Franchise agreements, royalty schedules, due diligence files for each franchisee (UBO identification, sanction screening, licensed food-safety permits), periodic audited royalty statements, invoices and payment evidence demonstrating the commercial substance of each cross-border transaction, and a group AML policy with risk-rating methodology.
Presence of UAE- or GCC-based intermediaries in the capital or trade chain

Root cause: Presence of UAE- or GCC-based intermediaries in the capital or trade chain

Defense evidence: Letters of explanation, commercial agreements showing a genuine supply-chain and distribution relationship, bank statements of the intermediary, certificates of incorporation, passport copies of UBOs, and legal and tax invoices that match the payment flow exactly.
High-volume inbound USD wires from multiple countries to a newly opened Omani SPC account

Root cause: High-volume inbound USD wires from multiple countries to a newly opened Omani SPC account

Defense evidence: Customer Risk Assessment, source-of-funds declarations, underlying franchisee master-license and operations agreements, CPO and trade documents, historical bank statements from an existing operating entity, and a treasury policy that segregates royalty income from supply-chain disbursements to eliminate commingling.
UBO or signatory is a politically exposed person or resident of a high-risk sanctions jurisdiction

Root cause: UBO or signatory is a politically exposed person or resident of a high-risk sanctions jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Independent PEP screening reports, detailed source-of-wealth and source-of-funds dossier, notarized and apostilled legal certificates, bank reference letters from prior reputable banks, and clear evidence of zero nexus to sanctioned jurisdictions or persons.
Large lump-sum capital injection (share capital and shareholder loan) routed through an intermediary

Root cause: Large lump-sum capital injection (share capital and shareholder loan) routed through an intermediary

Defense evidence: Legalized share subscription agreement, certified CR of the SPC, CBO-approved capital verification statement, board resolution approving the capital injection, overseas tax clearance certificates, entity registry extracts of the parent company, and signed SWIFT MT103 documentation with a precise purpose code.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols for the initial capital and shareholder loans must be executed via SWIFT MT103 from the parent entity's bank to the SPC's capital escrow account at a CBO-regulated Omani bank. The incoming wire must specify purpose code 'Share Capital Injection – FDI' or 'Subordinated Loan – Operational Funding' as applicable, include the SPC's Commercial Registration number and Omani bank account IBAN, and be accompanied by a signed capital contribution certificate and board resolution. CBO will automatically treat the credit as FDI; the SPC must file the capital injection with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion and the National Centre for Statistics and Information if the amount exceeds the Omani FDI threshold. The bank will perform a standard FX conversion to OMR only if the account is OMR-denominated; otherwise, USD and OMR clearing are separated. For any amount above OMR equivalent of USD 10,000,000, the bank may require a copy of the CBO or Ministry approval if the transaction falls under a restricted GATS Mode-3 category, though this franchise activity is a liberalized sector.Trade settlements for syrup shipments to international franchisees and payments to third-party logistics and raw material suppliers shall be cleared via SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account trade, or through irrevocable commercial Letters of Credit (LC) at sight for high-value consignments. For LC issuance, the SPC must apply to its Omani bank with the pro-forma invoice, transport documents, packing list, certificate of origin, and ISIC 5610-related franchise supply agreement. Once invoices are verified and confirmed, the negotiating bank pays the beneficiary using the clearing system of the corresponding currency (Fedwire/CHIPS for USD, SEPA/TARGET2 for EUR, or the local GCC RTGS used for OMR). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 1,000,000 - aggregate annual controlled transactions triggering the mandatory preparation and filing/support of Local File and Master File with the Oman Tax Authority, in accordance with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines

Economic substance scope: Oman's Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) cover enumerated activities such as banking, insurance, fund management, shipping, finance/leasing, and holding-company activities; boba tea franchise licensing and syrup supply are not listed ESR activities. However, the OTA's General Anti-Abuse Rules and DTA limitation-of-benefits clauses require the SPC to demonstrate real premises, qualified staff, board decision-making in Oman, and genuine commercial risk for its royalty income. A shell SPC with no substance risks treaty denial, GAAR recharacterization, and penalty exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Assessment of underpaid tax plus 1% monthly late-payment surcharge; evasion fines of OMR 5,000–10,000 or up to 200% of the evaded tax in aggravated OTA determinations; imprisonment of 6 months to 3 years for responsible persons.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who approved false returns assume joint and several civil liability for the evaded tax; courts may impose travel bans, passport surrender, disqualification as manager, and imprisonment.
Shell Company and Sham Substance Abatement

Statutory sanction: GAAR denial of conduit status: income is re-attributed to the Omani SPC, controlled-transaction deductions are disallowed, and an administrative penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax assessment is imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who stripped or dissipated corporate assets to frustrate the assessment are personally liable for the SPC's unpaid tax under the piercing-the-corporate-veil doctrine; travel bans and asset-freezing orders are routinely issued.
Withholding Tax Default on Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: The SPC as withholding agent is liable for the unpaid WHT, penalties of 100% of the amount not withheld, monthly late-payment surcharge, and potential criminal prosecution for misuse of treaty relief.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally accountable for WHT debts under the Oman Companies Law joint-liability provision; courts may issue individual travel bans until the WHT and related damages are settled.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Breach via Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under Oman's AML Law (Royal Decree 30/2016 as amended): fines up to OMR 50,000, confiscation of assets, freezing orders, imprisonment up to 10 years for money-laundering offences, and revocation of the commercial licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are criminally liable for failing to file STRs/SARs with the Oman Financial Intelligence Unit; personal assets, pensions, and board positions are subject to confiscation and automatic travel bans.
