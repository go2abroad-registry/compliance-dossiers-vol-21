Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Omani law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SPC; the nominal capital is stated in the Memorandum of Association and must be subscribed and paid in cash into a corporate bank account at a licensed Omani bank. No escrow account is statutorily mandated for an SPC.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Civil Defence and Ambulance Authority (CDAA) verifying alarm, suppression, egress, and separation of flammable storage; structural load inspection certificate by a licensed Omani structural engineer for printing equipment; workplace occupational health and safety clearance including machine guarding, ventilation, lighting, emergency exits, and employee safety training records.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) — Sultanate of Oman; e-Services Portal: https://www.taxoman.gov.om; company incorporation portal: Invest Oman / Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP).

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate SPC via MOCIIP commercial registration; obtain Commercial Registration (CR) and CR-linked provisional TIN. (2) Register with Oman Chamber of Commerce and Industry where required. (3) Submit tax-registration application to OTA through the Tax Management System (TMS) e-portal with CR extract, memorandum, power of attorney, shareholder/beneficial-owner identification, and statutory address. (4) Obtain permanent TIN and NID for Corporate Income Tax, plus VAT registration where taxable supplies reach OMR 38,500 mandatory threshold or OMR 19,250 voluntary threshold. Foreign-owned SPCs are eligible on equal footing; 100% foreign ownership is permitted without a local sponsor.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Oman-incorporated SPC is a separate taxable juridical person taxed on worldwide income (Corporate Income Tax Law, Royal Decree 51/2023; 15% headline rate). Pass-through treatment is unavailable; qualifying shareholding dividends may be exempt, and non-resident shareholders are taxed only on Oman-source income.

Available election forms

Oman does not operate a check-the-box election; classification follows legal form. Available elections: voluntary VAT registration; adoption of a non-calendar accounting year with OTA approval; election to file as a tax group by related resident companies subject to conditions and OTA pre-approval; small-business cash-basis/accrual accounting election per VAT rules; and strategic-investment tax-incentive agreements under Investment and CIT laws. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized; BEPS Action 2 treatment is not elective in Oman.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

OMR 38,500 mandatory threshold / OMR 19,250 voluntary threshold, based on taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months or reasonably expected in the next 12 months; registration includes zero-rated export supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported outside the GCC implementing states are zero-rated if exported within 90 days from the date of supply and supported by customs evidence. Exports to GCC states follow cross-border rules requiring destination-state registration evidence. Services to non-resident recipients, including offshore printing/design services, are zero-rated if not effectively connected to Oman, supplied to a recipient outside Oman, and the consideration is not chargeable to a person in Oman; the SPC must hold contemporaneous contractual evidence and bank/FX remittance proof.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Oman Trade Single Window/Bayan)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing departure from Oman
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT credit advice evidencing foreign-currency proceeds

OTA is rolling out the national 'Muwafaq' e-invoicing framework with phased mandatory compliance by taxpayer segment. Mandated taxpayers must transmit and clear B2B/B2C invoices through OTA API in near-real time, embed QR codes and digital signatures, and archive structured invoice data (XML/UBL). Until a taxpayer is in a mandatory phase, approved e-invoicing files/fiscal devices and standard invoice data rules apply; export invoices retain the same integrity and traceability requirements to support 0% VAT claims.

Zero-rating claims are subject to OTA audit testing of the full evidence chain; failure to evidence export departure within the 90-day rule or FX proceeds within the typical 180-day inward remittance window retrospectively triggers 0% entitlement loss, assessed VAT at 5%, administrative penalties of up to 100% of understated tax, and possible criminal liability including fines and/or imprisonment for fraudulent refund claims; false invoices can extend recovery to related-party transactions and trigger VAT registration revocation and customs-duty clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial is fully convertible and operates under a fixed peg of 1 OMR = 2.60028 USD. Oman maintains liberalized current-account and capital-account transactions for licensed corporate entities; no standing exchange-control approval is required for foreign direct investment, trade payments, dividend remittances, or capital repatriation. Convertibility is effectively assured through licensed Omani banks, subject to AML/CFT verifications, sanctions screening, and underlying transaction documentation. Profit remittance is permitted after tax clearance and standard banking due diligence; withholding tax and transfer-pricing documentation apply as per Oman Income Tax Law and OECD BEPS alignment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Oman. Export receipts may be retained in OMR or foreign currency in corporate accounts with CBO-licensed banks. Banks are required to verify the economic substance of inward remittances, conduct sanctions screening, and report large or unusual cross-border transactions to the CBO/Financial Intelligence Unit under the Oman AML/CFT Law and CBO regulations.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) – Banking Supervision Department, Foreign Exchange Department, and Financial Intelligence Unit coordination; Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) for investment registration and 100% foreign ownership permissions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low: Offshore digital banking institutions and electronic money institutions are not CBO-licensed deposit takers and cannot serve as the primary corporate treasury or clearing account for an Omani Single Person Company. Stripe and Authorize.Net may be used only as online card acceptance aggregators, with final settlement routed through an Omani acquiring bank or an inbound/outbound licensed correspondent arrangement. No standalone offshore EMI liquidity clearing is permissible for an Oman-incorporated entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection should be executed through SWIFT MT103 from the overseas shareholder's licensed bank account to the SPC's OMR or USD corporate account at a CBO-licensed commercial bank. The remittance must reference 'equity contribution / share capital injection' and include the share subscription agreement and MoCIIP registration details to permit the receiving bank to classify the funds correctly for balance-of-payments reporting. No minimum capital is statutorily required for the SPC, but any imported capital should be recorded in the bank's electronic exchange reporting system to preserve clean profit-repatriation and dividend-remittance evidence. Cash infusions, third-party payments unrelated to the commercial agreement, and ambiguous payment narratives are red flags and should be avoided.Outbound trade disbursements for purchasing card stock, security holographic cold foils, inks, and print machinery should use SWIFT MT103 direct transfers for low-value/supplier payments and documentary commercial letters of credit under UCP600 for larger imports. Payments must include clear commercial invoices, packing lists, and customs declarations; the issuing bank will require country-of-origin documentation and sanctions compliance screening. Correspondent settlement will occur via the bank's USD correspondent network, typically through New York clearing channels such as CHIPS/Fedwire, or via GBP/EUR TARGET2 equivalents. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File trigger: aggregate related-party transactions exceeding OMR 500,000 per tax year. Master File trigger: group consolidated revenue exceeding OMR 50,000,000. Country-by-Country Reporting trigger: group consolidated revenue exceeding OMR 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Oman does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute like the UAE. However, the Income Tax Law's place of effective management (POEM) test and the general anti-avoidance rules require the SPC to demonstrate commercial substance in Oman. A foreign-owned SPC engaged in trading card publishing must maintain actual local decision-making, physical or operational presence, sufficient technical staff (e.g., production supervision, quality control of holographic foil printing), and real risk exposure. Without this, the SPC risks income recharacterization, denial of tax treaty benefits, or reclassification as a tax-transparent conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years and/or a fine up to OMR 10,000; additional tax equal to 25% of the under-assessed amount plus 1% monthly late-payment surcharge; cancellation of commercial registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and the SPC owner may be jointly and severally liable if they signed false returns or knowingly procured the evasion. Travel bans and seizure of personal assets may be ordered. Tax evasion conviction can trigger disqualification as director for up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of intercompany deductions, recharacterization of income under general anti-avoidance provisions, 15% tax on recharacterized income (or up to 25% on deemed taxable base), financial penalties up to OMR 10,000, and possible forced strike-off.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate tax up to the amount of distributed capital under veil-piercing provisions if the company is a mere facade. Oman courts may impose a personal capacity fine and a travel ban pending tax settlement.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of OMR 3,000 for each missing TP documentation set; tax adjustment to apply an arm's length price, with the additional tax subjected to 1% monthly late-payment surcharge; potential double taxation where the corresponding TP adjustment is disallowed to the counterparty.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed TP declarations knowing them to be false may be jointly liable for the additional tax and penalties, and may be imprisoned up to 3 years under the tax penalty code.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The withholding tax is recoverable from the SPC as payer. Penalty of the amount not withheld plus 10% monthly late-payment surcharge, and a fine up to OMR 5,000; the SPC faces denial of deduction for the underlying expenditure if WHT is unpaid.

Director personal liability: Board members are jointly and severally liable for the uncollected withholding tax when they authorized the non-compliant remittance. Executive officers in Oman may receive an individual fine and a personal liability letter from the Oman Tax Authority.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Omani AML Law fines up to OMR 20,000 for companies, suspension of business, and potential criminal confiscation of trafficking proceeds; payment processors (Stripe, Authorize.Net) may terminate access and report to the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors and the compliance officer can be personally prosecuted under the Oman Anti-Money Laundering Law; penalties include imprisonment for up to 7 years and personal fine of OMR 10,000; repeat violations carry mandatory director disqualification and travel bans.
