Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, an operating unit of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE); the Registrar of Companies is the statutory decision-maker for all incorporations.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), particularly sections 12-19 (Incorporation), 20-25 (Names), 31 (Constitution), 155-160 (Directors), 161-166 (Registered Office & Address for Service), and Schedule 2 (Default Constitution Rules); coregulated by the Financial Reporting Act 2013 for financial reporting obligations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NZCN1 — Application for Registration of a Company, lodged electronically through the New Zealand Companies Office Online Register (companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the Companies Act 1993 for a company limited by shares, and no escrow account requirement applies. The incorporated company must issue at least one share, of any nominal value or of no par value, and the subscriber/shareholder may subscribe for shares at a nominal price; a paid-up capital declaration is not a precondition to registration.

Funding & unlock path: No fire safety certificate or structural load inspection is mandated for an e-commerce administrative office or online store operating in a standard commercial office lease; the premises must, nevertheless, comply with the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 requiring a safe workplace, first-aid facilities, and an emergency evacuation plan; if the business operates only as a home-based online vendor, no WorkSafe notification is necessary unless hazardous substances are stored, and the business must carry out a simple self-assessment for ergonomic and electrical safety.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the Companies Act 1993 for a company limited by shares, and no escrow account requirement applies. The incorporated company must issue at least one share, of any nominal value or of no par value, and the subscriber/shareholder may subscribe for shares at a nominal price; a paid-up capital declaration is not a precondition to registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake), the national tax authority of New Zealand, administered under the Tax Administration Act 1994; principal taxpayer portal: myIR at https://myir.ird.govt.nz; integrated with the New Zealand Companies Office register and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) registry.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: The CK_IC International Company is a non-resident, limited-liability offshore entity that is neither incorporated nor managed and controlled in New Zealand; it derives no New Zealand-source income and maintains no New Zealand fixed establishment or GST taxable activity. Its offshore income from online merchandising of fashion jewellery, watches and accessories remains outside the New Zealand income tax and GST net. For comparison, a New Zealand-resident company is subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide income at the 28% corporate rate; a non-resident company carrying on business through a New Zealand branch is taxed only on New Zealand-source income at 28%; and eligible closely-held companies may elect pass-through look-through company treatment. The Tax_Exempt_Zone classification is therefore accurate solely because the International Company avoids all New Zealand taxing nexus.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box elective classification regime. Statutory election mechanisms under the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 include: (1) the look-through company (LTC) election under subpart HB, under which an eligible close company may elect flow-through treatment so that income, expenses and tax credits are attributed to shareholders; (2) the closed qualifying company (QC) regime with grandfathered transitional elections for existing companies; (3) the portfolio investment entity (PIE) election for investment companies taxed at the 28% top individual rate; (4) GST group registration and GST branch registration elections; (5) the simplified GST registration election available to non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value imported goods; (6) controlled foreign company (CFC) elections, including exemption elections for active foreign business income and de minimis interests; and (7) the hybrid and branch mismatch re-characterisation rules in subpart FH implementing BEPS Action 2, which operate to deny deductions where double non-taxation arises. A tax-exempt offshore International Company would not make a New Zealand LTC or PIE election, as doing so would create New Zealand tax presence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month rolling period (section 51(1) of the GST Act 1985); for non-resident suppliers of remote services (digital services) to New Zealand consumers, the compulsory registration threshold is NZD 60,000 of such supplies per 12-month period (section 51(1B)); for non-resident suppliers, electronic marketplaces and re-deliverers of low-value imported goods (each consignment valued at or below NZD 1,000) to New Zealand consumers, the threshold is likewise NZD 60,000 per 12-month period under sections 8B to 8D and section 51 of the GST Act 1985; voluntary registration is available below these thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods qualifies for 0% GST zero-rating as an export under section 11(1)(a) of the GST Act 1985 where the supplier or the supplier's agent exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply, or within an extended period approved by the Commissioner of Inland Revenue. Services are zero-rated under section 11(1)(c) where the recipient is a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed, subject to express exceptions for services connected with land or goods situated in New Zealand and for certain relationship-of-person services. For the online jewellery, watches and fashion trade, zero-rating is sustainable only if physical export from New Zealand is verified by customs documentation, the sale is made to a genuine offshore customer at arm's length, and the invoice is settled in foreign currency or equivalent value. Digital services supplied to consumers present in New Zealand are taxable at 15% GST under the remote-services regime and are not eligible for zero-rating.

Inland Revenue subjects export zero-rating claims to documentary and economic-substance review. Failure to satisfy the export documentation bundle results in reassessment of the supply at the standard 15% GST rate, with retrospective clawback of input tax credits, assessment of GST payable, and imposition of use-of-money interest (UOMI). The Commissioner may also amend assessments beyond the ordinary four-year limitation period where fraud, evasion or wilful misrepresentation is established. Exporters with repeated compliance failures may be subjected to heightened review, pre-approval conditions, or suspension of zero-rating privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible across both current and capital account transactions. There are no statutory exchange controls, no restrictions on the conversion of NZD to foreign currency, and no prohibitions on repatriation of profits, dividends, loan repayments, or invested capital. Non-resident investors and corporate entities may freely remit funds in and out of New Zealand, subject only to (i) compliance with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act), which imposes due diligence, record-keeping, and reporting obligations on banks, (ii) economic sanctions administered by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade (MFAT), and (iii) taxation, including non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends, interest, and royalties, and general income tax on any New Zealand-source income if the International Company is deemed to have a fixed establishment or is managed and controlled in New Zealand. Profit remittance by a non-resident-owned entity to shareholders is lawful and unrestricted, but dividends remitted to non-residents are subject to NRWT at the applicable treaty rate or the default 30% (or 15% for fully imputed dividends) unless reduced under a double tax agreement. For a Cook Islands International Company (or similar tax-exempt offshore structure) administered from New Zealand, tax residency and permanent establishment risk must be managed to preserve the tax-exempt character; a mere bank account and treasury function in New Zealand does not itself create New Zealand tax residency, but treasury personnel exercising management and control in New Zealand would do so. There are no currency surrender, mandatory conversion, or balance-of-payments restrictions that would obstruct multi-currency treasury operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand imposes no statutory obligation on exporters to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or any government authority, nor any mandatory percentage conversion of export receipts into NZD. Exporters and e-commerce operators may lawfully hold export proceeds in foreign currency accounts, convert them to NZD at commercial rates, or use them directly to pay overseas suppliers and operating expenses. The only obligations pertain to (i) record-keeping under the AML/CFT Act, including transaction records and wire transfer information for payments above NZD 1,000, (ii) reporting of large cash transactions (NZD 10,000 or more) or suspicious transactions to the FIU (NZ Police Financial Intelligence Unit), and (iii) ensuring that settlement amounts received from payment aggregators such as Stripe and PayPal are accurately reconciliated and disclosed for goods and services tax (GST) and income tax purposes if any New Zealand tax nexus exists. The absence of a surrender regime enhances treasury flexibility: the operating entity may designate a portion of foreign currency receipts to an offshore settlement account and route the remainder to a NZD functional account without exchange control approvals.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), acting as the central bank and prudential regulator, together with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for conduct supervision and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) for AML/CFT registration and supervision of non-bank financial institutions. Exchange controls formerly administered under the Exchange Control Act 1953 were abolished in 1984; New Zealand has not re-imposed exchange controls and maintains full observance of its IMF Article VIII obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium for offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs). A fully online, offshore EMI (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, or a European e-money institution) can be engaged as a treasury overlay, but it cannot be the sole banking infrastructure. Offshore EMIs are not registered as banks in New Zealand and cannot take deposits from the New Zealand public under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. They operate under an FMA-issued financial advice/derivatives issuer license or as a money remitter registered on the DIA AML/CFT register if they provide money remittance services into New Zealand. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million for cross-border related-party transactions in a single income year (aggregate threshold triggering contemporaneous documentation expectations for large taxpayers); taxpayers with transactions below this threshold must still document on a best-efforts basis if they engage in high-risk arrangements.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply the Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations as a standalone regime; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007), the financial arrangements rules, and the business continuity test for losses require real economic presence, business premises, employees, and substantive activity in New Zealand for an entity to claim treaty benefits, branch attribution, or loss carryforward. A shell holding company without commercial substance in New Zealand may have its transactions recharacterised and be denied treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, evasion penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall may be imposed, with a minimum 75% penalty for evasion or fraud. Criminal conviction may result in fines up to NZD 50,000 for an individual and NZD 250,000 for a company, plus imprisonment for up to 5 years under the Crimes Act 1961 (fraud offences).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for tax shortfalls if they caused or participated in serious tax evasion under the 'knowledge offence' provisions (TAA s 149A). Civil restitution orders, asset freezing, travel bans (for unpaid tax debt above NZD 50,000), and director disqualification orders may be imposed. Criminal imprisonment applies to individual directors who knowingly defraud the Commissioner; corporate veil piercing is available where the company is a facade or agent of the directors.
Improper Use of Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may recharacterise the arrangement under the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1) and impose a 40% shortfall penalty for abusive tax positions (TAA s 141D). Denial of treaty benefits and DTA relief may result in full New Zealand taxation at 28% on attributed profits plus NRWT on distributions. Fines for document falsification or false declarations up to NZD 50,000; additional shortfall penalties up to 100% where taxpayer took a 'dominant purpose' arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for the company's tax if the company is liquidated with unpaid tax and they failed to comply with statutory obligations under the Companies Act 1993 (reckless trading, insolvent transaction provisions). Orders for disqualification under the Companies Act (up to 5 years) and personal liability for tax debts under the 'duty to prevent' evasion provisions are enforceable, including bankruptcy petitions for unpaid amounts above NZD 1,000.
Anti-Money Laundering and Payment Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to NZD 2.5 million for a company for each breach of the AML/CFT Act; criminal penalties up to NZD 300,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years for money laundering (Crimes Act 1961 ss 243–245). Fines for reporting breaches up to NZD 200,000 per failure; supervised entities may lose their registration as AML reporting entities.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and directors face personal criminal liability where they knowingly participated in, or were wilfully blind to, money laundering. Travel bans can be issued under the Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters Act where proceeds of corruption are identified, and UN Security Council targeted financial sanctions lists apply. Directors may also be disqualified for up to 5 years and are exposed to civil liability for assisting in breaches of AML obligations.
Cross-Border Transfer Pricing and Permanent Establishment Exposure

Statutory sanction: IRD may apply transfer-pricing adjustments under s GC 6–GC 14 and impose shortfall penalties of 20% for unacceptable transfer pricing if no 'reasonable basis' was documented, or 40% for gross carelessness and 100% for fraud. Interest under use-of-money rates applies. Any resulting additional tax liability is assessable plus NRWT on deemed dividends if profits are recharacterised as distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed false transfer-pricing documentation may be liable under the Tax Administration Act knowledge offences (s 143A) with fines up to NZD 150,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years. The veil may be pierced for unpaid tax if interposed entities are sham trust arrangements; personal liability also arises through the 'breach of duty' provisions for directors of an insolvent taxpayer.
