Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), Wellington; online registry: New Zealand Companies Register (www.companiesoffice.govt.nz), accessible with RealMe authenticated login for electronic filings.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No 105), ss 11–16 (incorporation procedure), s 18 (name requirements), ss 22–23 (directors), s 37 (no authorised capital), ss 58 and 88 (share allotments and certificates), ss 153–157 (board resolutions), ss 184–186 (registered office and address for service), s 200 (financial reporting requirements), and Schedule 1 (default constitution); Financial Reporting Act 2013 ss 35–38 (exempt small/overseas company relief); Income Tax Act 2007 ss 82 and 140 (foreign-sourced income rules); Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 s 8B (remote services). The ‘International Company’ (ELF_CUSTOM_CK_IC) is implemented as a standard New Zealand private company limited by shares, classified internally for offshore trust protection; no separate statutory ‘International Company’ vehicle exists in New Zealand.”

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NZCN1 – Application for Registration of a Company (filed under s 13 of the Companies Act 1993), supported by Form NZCN1A (Consent to Act as Director/Shareholder) for each appointee.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital requirement. The Companies Act 1993 s 37 abolished authorised share capital, so a company may issue a single ordinary share at NZD 1.00 (or any nominal value the board determines). No statutory escrow account, capital deposit, or paid-up capital affidavit is required for registration. For this structure, the issued share capital is one fully-paid ordinary share held by the offshore trust owner.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable to cloud-hosted online operations. If a leased physical office in New Zealand is later established, the following clearances become mandatory: (i) a Building Warrant of Fitness (BWOF) from the territorial authority under the Building Act 2004; (ii) a fire evacuation scheme approved by Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017; (iii) an employer health and safety policy and worker engagement plan under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including first-aid provision and notifiable event reporting under ss 24–26. No heavy structural load inspection is required for ordinary office occupancy.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital requirement. The Companies Act 1993 s 37 abolished authorised share capital, so a company may issue a single ordinary share at NZD 1.00 (or any nominal value the board determines). No statutory escrow account, capital deposit, or paid-up capital affidavit is required for registration. For this structure, the issued share capital is one fully-paid ordinary share held by the offshore trust owner.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand — official portal: myIR (https://myir.ird.govt.nz); business registry: New Zealand Companies Office (https://companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident International Company (CK_IC) with no fixed establishment in New Zealand: (1) Obtain a certificate of incorporation and extract from the Companies Office Register; (2) File Form IR596 'Application for an IRD number for a non-resident company' accompanied by certified passport copies of directors, proof of registered office, and evidence of foreign tax residence; (3) If the entity will make supplies of remote services (e.g., webnovel digital content) to New Zealand consumers, file a separate GST registration application via myIR using Form IR742; (4) The IRD issues an 8–9 digit IRD number within 5–10 working days. An NZBN (New Zealand Business Number) is automatically allocated to all registered entities and is used for e-invoicing and government interactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The CK_IC is a non-resident incorporated International Company that is outside the New Zealand income tax net on its foreign-sourced income. New Zealand operates a territorial tax system for non-residents: the entity is taxable only on New Zealand-sourced income under section BD 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, and then only if it carries on a business through a fixed establishment (permanent establishment). Non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) or Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) may apply to NZ-source interest or dividends. No New Zealand-source income is derived from the operation of the webnovel subscription platform where content is exported and consumed wholly offshore.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not have a check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections relevant to the CK_IC include: (i) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB; (ii) Consolidated group election under subpart FM; (iii) Branch equivalent income election under section DN 1 (for foreign branches); (iv) Imputation credit account election under section OB 7 (only if the entity becomes NZ tax-resident); (v) GST group election under section 55 of the GST Act 1985; (vi) Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) election under section 86I of the Stamp and Cheque Duties Act 1971 for offshore lending. Hybrid and branch mismatch rules (enacted under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018, subpart FE) apply mandatorily with no elective opt-out, neutralizing deductions where a hybrid instrument or entity is used.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies within any 12-month period, calculated by reference to section 51 GST Act; a non-resident supplier of 'remote services' (which includes webnovel subscriptions and interactive reading apps supplied via Apple IAP or Google Play Billing) must register if its supplies to New Zealand-resident consumers exceed NZD 60,000 per annum under the remote services regime (sections 54B–54D GST Act). Registration is also possible on a voluntary basis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under sections 11 and 11A of the GST Act 1985: (a) goods physically exported from New Zealand within 28 days of supply; (b) services supplied to a non-resident recipient and not performed in connection with New Zealand land; (c) remote digital services delivered electronically to a non-resident recipient and consumed wholly overseas, subject to the provider being satisfied the recipient is a non-resident (using two independent indicators such as billing address, IP address, payment gateway country, or mobile country code); (d) a zero-rating election under section 11A where the supply is made to a GST-registered non-resident business. For the CK_IC's webnovel subscription platform, zero-rating applies to subscriptions purchased by customers outside New Zealand and consumed offshore; any in-app purchases (virtual coins, premium chapters) provided to an NZ-resident consumer are standard-rated at 15% unless the consumer is GST-registered.

  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Electronic Sea Waybill (where physical goods are exported with digital content or promotional materials)

New Zealand imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory pre-approval API requirement. E-invoicing is voluntary and aligns with the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 standard through the New Zealand e-Invoicing Network, facilitated by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE). Inland Revenue accepts electronically transmitted records; however, the CK_IC must preserve transaction-level records (section 75 GST Act) sufficient to verify both output tax and zero-rating claims. For app-store-derived income, Apple IAP and Google Play Billing transaction reports are recognized as acceptable supporting records, but the entity must retain merchant records for at least 7 years.

Inland Revenue's 'Export GST' compliance programme conducts targeted audits of zero-rated export claims. Failure to substantiate the export of goods or the offshore nature of the digital service triggers a retrospective clawback of the zero-rating (i.e., output tax becomes payable at 15%) together with use-of-money interest under section 120A of the Tax Administration Act 1994 (TAA). Penalties under the TAA escalate by culpability: 20% shortfall penalty for failing to take reasonable care; 40% for gross carelessness; 50% for taking an unacceptable tax position (where the position is not 'more likely than not' to be correct); 50% for an abusive tax position; and 100% for evasion or an arrangement with a dominant purpose of tax avoidance. Voluntary disclosure to the IRD before audit reduces evasion penalties by up to 75% and other shortfall penalties by up to 40% (sections 141D–141J). Directors or controlling shareholders may be held personally liable for the company's GST shortfalls under section 135 TAA in cases of serious non-compliance, and fraudulent zero-rating claims may lead to criminal prosecution under section 143A TAA, carrying a maximum sentence of 5 years' imprisonment and fines of up to NZD 50,000 per offence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the Bretton Woods Agreements Act 1958 and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely floating and internationally tradable; there are no exchange controls, no registration requirements for foreign direct investment inflows or outbound profit repatriation beyond standard tax and anti-money laundering documentation, and no restriction on dividend distributions, royalty payments, or loan repayments to non-residents other than applicable withholding tax and thin capitalization compliance under the Income Tax Act 2007.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists in New Zealand. Export receipts and all foreign currency earnings may be retained in full in offshore or onshore accounts, converted freely at market rates, or used directly for cross-border settlements; no mandatory conversion timeline or ratio applies. Entities must only satisfy ordinary reporting obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) regime and the Tax Administration Act 1994 for financial transaction record keeping.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may provide limited NZD IBAN-equivalent and SWIFT rails, but full corporate banking functionality requires registration as a Financial Service Provider under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and may trigger AML/CFT obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. New Zealand does not issue standalone 'EMI licences' in the European sense; non-bank service providers operating payment and settlement functions must comply with the NZ Clearing and Settlement Systems Act 2013 if accessing local clearing infrastructure. Pragmatic routing is therefore a hybrid structure: a Tier-1 NZ bank account as the primary settlement anchor with an offshore EMI used solely for app store payouts and small-value cross-border disbursements, not as the principal treasury vehicle.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is via SWIFT MT103 international funds transfer with the stated payment narrative describing equity injection or shareholder loan. The injector's remitting bank must complete standard FATCA and CRS originator due diligence; the NZ receiving bank will apply a 'capital contribution' or 'intercompany loan' transaction narrative, verify that the beneficiary account is the dedicated escrow account, and may request the executed board resolution and source-of-funds evidence. Use of NZD clearing through the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) applies where the counterparty bank is locally clearing; otherwise same-day bilateral Nostro settlement occurs via SWIFT. For amounts exceeding NZD 25,000, the payer must comply with the FATF-style wire transfer record-keeping and include originator identity; no exchange control approval is needed from the Reserve Bank of New Zealand.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct operational disbursements to foreign authors, translators, marketing agencies, and content licensors; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) via the NZ issuing bank's trade finance desk for larger fixed-asset or content-library acquisitions; (3) batch SEPA-equivalent NZD and major currency sweeps through the bank's e-channel for recurring small-value royalty payments; and (4) SWIFT MT202 for interbank treasury cover. Apple App Store and Google Play Billing proceeds are remitted through their respective payment gateways to the designated settlement account, then periodically swept to the primary operational account. All outbound payments to foreign beneficiaries must transmit full originator and beneficiary legal names, addresses, and purpose codes to satisfy the AML/CFT cross-border electronic funds transfer supervision regime.Deliverable FX forward contracts (plain vanilla, up to 12 months tenor) governed by the New Zealand Financial Markets Association (NZFMA) FX Master Agreement or ISDA 2002 Master AgreementFX cross-currency swaps (NZD vs USD/EUR/AUD) for long-term subscription royalty and licensing liability hedgingFX options (European call and put, OTC) for contingent treasury risk management when platform revenue fluctuates with app store seasonalityNZD/USD deliverable FX swaps for efficient funding of fortnightly author royalty settlementsCross-currency interest rate swaps only in conjunction with matched intercompany debt structures; non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally unnecessary and unavailable for NZD onshore hedging because the NZD is fully convertible and a deep deliverable FX market exists
