Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Companies, New Zealand Companies Office, part of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE); operational fililings are made through the Companies Office Online Portal located at Auckland and Wellington, accessible via www.companiesoffice.govt.nz.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), particularly ss 5-6 (definitions and company formation), ss 10-16 (incorporation), ss 20-32 (constitution and governance), ss 36-50 (share capital), s 128 (registered office), ss 152-161 (directors), and ss 360-368 (registration and remedial powers of the Registrar). Secondary legislation: the Companies (Share Capital) Regulations 2020 and the Companies (Postal and Electronic Communications) Regulations 1988. Incorporation is effected electronically under the Companies Act 1993 and the Electronic Transactions Act 2002 through the New Zealand Companies Office.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Incorporation (online form on the Companies Office 'Incorporate Now' portal; historically also issued as Form N1), together with a constitution (optional but recommended), signed consents of directors and shareholders to act, and the required statement of registered office address and shareholding information.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up share capital or escrow account requirement under New Zealand law. A company may be incorporated with one share of no par value, provided there is at least one shareholder and one director resident in New Zealand for the purpose of registered office service of documents. As a practical matter, the initial capital may be set at NZD 1.00 fully paid or a nominal issued share capital, with subsequent funding by subscription, shareholder loans, or retained earnings. No lodging of capital in an escrow account is required.

Funding & unlock path: Under the Building Act 2004, the catering kitchen must obtain a Building Consent and a Code Compliance Certificate (or, for new use of an existing building, a building warrant of fitness) covering structural, sanitary, fire, and mechanical ventilation compliance. The facility must secure a Fire Safety Evacuation Plan approved by Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ), including commercial kitchen fire suppression systems, grease-duct fire protection, extinguishers, and clear evacuation signage. Under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, a Workplace Health and Safety audit must be completed, and, where hazardous substances (e.g. ammonia cooling systems, industrial cleaning agents, propane cylinders) are present, an additional 'Hazardous Substances and New Organisms (HSNO) Test Certificate' from WorkSafe New Zealand is mandatory. For personnel accessing secure airside areas of an airport, background checks, identity verification and a Civil Aviation Authority security clearance are required, along with annual defensive driving and aviation safety training, as an occupational clearance for ramp delivery and apron access.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up share capital or escrow account requirement under New Zealand law. A company may be incorporated with one share of no par value, provided there is at least one shareholder and one director resident in New Zealand for the purpose of registered office service of documents. As a practical matter, the initial capital may be set at NZD 1.00 fully paid or a nominal issued share capital, with subsequent funding by subscription, shareholder loans, or retained earnings. No lodging of capital in an escrow account is required.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake), Wellington; online portal 'myIR' at www.ird.govt.nz; corporate registration via New Zealand Companies Office (www.companiesoffice.govt.nz) and New Zealand Customs Service (www.customs.govt.nz) for bonded/CCA approvals.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with Territorial_Sourcing for non-residents; a New Zealand-incorporated IC is resident and taxed on worldwide income at 28% corporate rate, while a non-resident (overseas-registered) IC is taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income; the customs-bonded airport kitchen functions as a Tax_Exempt_Zone for indirect tax purposes (GST zero-rating on 'stores' supplied to international flights and duty/GST suspension on imported inputs under the Customs and Excise Act 2018).

Available election forms

No general check-the-box pass-through election exists for companies in New Zealand. The IC may elect 'Look-Through Company' status (LTC) under the Taxation (Annual Rates for 2010-11, Measures No.3) Act 2010 if it has ≤5 owners, all individuals/eligible trusts and meets the 80% test, or use the 'Qualifying Company' regime only for grandfathered cases. Hybrid mismatch elections can be made under the OECD hybrid mismatch rules (Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007) to elect ordering of rules for MNE structures; GST grouping election under GST Act 1985 ss 55-55B; GST zero-rating of land purchase election (s 11(1)(mb)); no de minimis VAT regrouping in NZ. For meals supplied as ship/stores, election to zero-rate requires the supplier to hold written airline purchase orders confirming the international-flight 'stores' end-use.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months (mandatory GST registration; voluntary registration available; a non-resident IC supplying zero-rated stores to international aircraft may register even with lower turnover to claim input VAT refunds).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under GST Act 1985 s 11(1)(h)/(i) (stores for craft/vessel engaged in international carriage). Meals, beverages, and bonded-inflight food must: (a) be delivered into the aircraft from the CCA kitchen by the IC; (b) be consumed in connection with an international leg (outbound or inbound where the airline is non-resident); (c) be supported by airline purchase orders and flight schedules; and (d) be entered on the Customs electronic manifest/export declaration. Offshore services (e.g., menu R&D charged to the foreign airline head office) may be zero-rated under s 11(2)(c) if supplied to a non-resident who is outside NZ when the services are performed. The IC can also claim refunds of input GST on raw materials via its standard GST return; no statutory FX repatriation deadline applies in NZ law - the 180-day benchmark reflects the airline's standard payment terms and prudential evidence-retention cutoff, and any delayed settlement beyond arm's length may trigger transfer pricing adjustments under s GC 6 Income Tax Act 2007.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusWeb/Electronic Cargo Transhipment/Customs Export Entry lodged via the Customs Electronic System and the CCA operator's air waybill/ramp delivery manifest)
  • Bonded Catering transfer records (Stock Status Report of the CCA; duty/GST suspension store records; airline signature of the in-flight catering delivery docket / ULD seal record)
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rated tax invoice issued per s 24 GST Act 1985 showing 'zero-rated' and the airline's GST status
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank statement evidencing receipt of payment from the foreign airline within the 180-day contractual benchmark
  • Flight departure documentation (aircraft movement report, flight number/leg confirmation, CTA log) proving the goods left New Zealand on an international service

New Zealand has no government-issued fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or real-time tax invoice clearance mandate. E-invoicing is voluntary and uses the Peppol network (TRA, Te Tari Taake requires B2G e-invoices on Peppol from 2025 for public sector contracts); the catering company must issue a valid tax invoice with GST number and, for zero-rated stores, the invoice must state 'zero-rated' (s 24 GST Act) and be archived with the Customs export documentation; Customs SW (CusWeb) JMS is used for export entries, with the CCA operator lodging export declarations in the Customs Electronic System (CES) for each inflight delivery.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible. New Zealand has no exchange controls on capital or current account transactions; therefore, non-resident profits and dividends may be repatriated freely, subject to ordinary tax compliance and AML/CFT vetting by the receiving bank. No central bank approval is required for any currency conversion, but companies must transact through an authorised NZ-registered bank or registered financial service provider.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand law imposes no mandatory surrender or compulsory repatriation of export proceeds. Exporters may invoice in foreign currency and retain funds overseas, provided that for New Zealand tax purposes those earnings are declared. Banks handling export settlements require underlying documents (airway bill, commercial invoice, CERS export declaration) and may reject transactions if the remittance does not match the declared trade value.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) – the integrated financial regulator and central bank, responsible for monetary policy, banking supervision, and the oversight of the NZ dollar wholesale settlement system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility for offshore digital EMIs as a primary banking relationship. Offshore EMIs are not licensed banks in New Zealand and cannot access domestic clearing and settlement systems (ESAS). They may act only as secondary payment corridors for expense disbursements and foreign-exchange float, but legal constraints require them to hold an NZ Financial Service Provider (FSP) licence if onboarding NZ-resident customers. The ultimate AML/CFT responsibility remains with the operating company. Hence, no operational treasury concentration should be warehoused with an EMI.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Certified copy of the trust deed or IC register; waiver or consent from the protector; tax identification number of the settlor/beneficiary; source-of-wealth declaration; bank references from the original jurisdiction; signed declaration confirming compliance with NZ AML/CFT rules.

Defense evidence: Valid company registration certificate; registered office/local agent agreement; letters of intent from NZ-based aviation catering buyers; lease or service agreements evidencing operational substance; employment contracts of managerial staff; payroll and office utility documentation; tax opinion letter confirming eligibility and reporting.
Unexpectedly large inbound capital injection relative to initial revenue projection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan payments should be routed via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'EQUI' (equity injection) or 'LOAN' (intercompany lending), and the reference field must quote the share allotment resolution or loan agreement. Prior to the wire, the account officer must receive: (i) certified board resolution; (ii) updated share registry; (iii) source-of-funds statement; (iv) signed KYC questionnaire; (v) tax declaration (IRD number, if applicable). Use the bank's dedicated treasury onboarding team and nominate an account signatory. Annual Statutory Financial Statements Filing (conditional on audit/filing triggers) Within 5 months after annual balance date (standard 31 March balance date); filing due within 10 working days after the financial statements are completed and signed. $0 USD Directors incur personal liability for default; statutory offence fines up to NZD 50,000 under the Financial Reporting Act 2013, daily penalty for continuing breach, and Registrar capacity to place the company in default leading to deregistration.
Ultimate Beneficial Ownership Annual Confirmation Same 20-working-day window as the Company Annual Return; any qualifying share transfer or control change must be entered in the UBO register within 10 working days of the relevant event. $0 USD False or misleading UBO statements are a criminal offence carrying fines and imprisonment exposure for officers; persistent non-maintenance of the UBO register compounds default status and supports strike-off.
