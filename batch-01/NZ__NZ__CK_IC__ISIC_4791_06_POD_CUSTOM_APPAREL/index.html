Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, an operating arm of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), Wellington, New Zealand.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (NZ), particularly ss 10 (incorporation), 12 (application for registration), 13 (constitution), 87-93 (share register, issue of shares, minimum shareholding), 152-160 (director requirements), 186-189 (registered office and address for service), and 199A-199A (director limitation provisions); Companies (Registration) Regulations 1994; Financial Reporting Act 2013; Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (for AML/CFT compliance).

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NZCN1 - Application for Registration of a Company, filed electronically via the New Zealand Companies Office online register (companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 16 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. Section 92 of the Companies Act 1993 requires at least one issued share, which may be issued fully paid for cash, property, or services. There is no escrow account, capital deposit, or bank attestation requirement; non-resident ownership up to 100% is permitted without prior Overseas Investment Office consent for non-sensitive retail activities.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: compliance with the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017 and the Building Act 2004, including a means-of-escape certificate for commercial premises, fire extinguisher and smoke detection equipment, and a Building Warrant of Fitness (BWOF) if a Compliance Schedule is active for the premises. Structural clearance: occupancy must comply with the building code, with a current Code Compliance Certificate or Certificate of Acceptance issued by the territorial authority. Occupational clearance: WorkSafe New Zealand health and safety due diligence under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including a documented worker engagement policy, first aid facilities, electrical safety inspection, and a COVID-safe or general emergency response plan displayed to employees.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital. Section 92 of the Companies Act 1993 requires at least one issued share, which may be issued fully paid for cash, property, or services. There is no escrow account, capital deposit, or bank attestation requirement; non-resident ownership up to 100% is permitted without prior Overseas Investment Office consent for non-sensitive retail activities.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Te Tari Taake; principal portal: myIR Secure Online Services (www.ird.govt.nz). Export clearance and customs administration: New Zealand Customs Service via the Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident International Company (IC) obtains its New Zealand IRD number (tax file number) by filing the non-individual IRD number application (form IR596) through the myIR portal or by using the combined Business Registration service at business.govt.nz, which simultaneously issues a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) and registers the entity for GST. GST registration is completed on the same application or on form IR742; registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period and is effective from the date of application or the date liability arises. Non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value goods to New Zealand consumers may register under the offshore supplier GST rules without requiring a New Zealand physical presence or bank account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — non-resident limited-liability International Company (IC) with no New Zealand permanent establishment; taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income under the territorial rules of the Income Tax Act 2007, while offshore conduct of the POD retail business remains outside the New Zealand income tax base.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not recognise a US-style check-the-box election. The statutory elections relevant to an IC include the tax consolidation election for groups of resident companies (Subpart FM, Income Tax Act 2007), the look-through company election for close-held companies (Subpart HB), and the GST grouping election for associated suppliers (section 55, Goods and Services Tax Act 1986). The hybrid mismatch rules (Part 4B, Income Tax Act 2007) apply mandatorily with no elective opt-out, and there is no election to alter the entity-level taxation classification of a limited company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (section 51, Goods and Services Tax Act 1986); non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value goods are subject to the same NZD 60,000 offshore registration threshold, with voluntary registration available to all suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods, including POD custom apparel, are eligible for the 0% export rate under section 11(1)(a), Goods and Services Tax Act 1986 where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply and retains documented evidence of export. Services are zero-rated where supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of performance, subject to exceptions for services directly connected with land or goods situated in New Zealand. POD goods drop-shipped from an offshore fulfilment centre to the overseas customer are outside the scope of New Zealand GST rather than zero-rated, and no GST refund arises on foreign production inputs. Input tax refunds for zero-rated exports are recoverable through the normal GST return mechanism, subject to Inland Revenue set-off and verification; inward remittance evidence is typically benchmarked to 180 days from export.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

New Zealand imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime. E-invoicing operates on a voluntary Peppol framework. GST returns (form GST101) are filed electronically through myIR on one-month, two-month, or six-month periods depending on turnover, and export declarations must be lodged electronically with the New Zealand Customs Service via the Trade Single Window.

The Inland Revenue may reassess and claw back the 15% GST together with use-of-money interest where export zero-rating is not substantiated or the goods are not exported within the statutory 28-day window. Shortfall penalties under Part IX of the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion; late payment penalties accrue at 1% at the due date, 4% if unpaid after seven days, and 1% monthly thereafter. Fraudulent export documentation also attracts criminal liability, reassessment time bars are extended or removed for evasion, and Inland Revenue may disclose significant shortfalls under the tax debt information disclosure rules.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has fully accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; the New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible on both the current and capital accounts. There are no exchange-control approvals, licensing gates, or prior-authorisation mechanisms for inward remittances, capital injections, dividend/profit repatriation, loan servicing, or liquidation distributions. For the non-resident-owned International Company (IC) structure with tax-exempt characterization, profit remittance faces no regulatory hurdle; the only practical frictions are bank-level documentary verification and standard outbound withholding tax obligations: non-resident dividend withholding tax (NRWT) at 15% on dividends, and 2% Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) on interest paid by an approved issuer to non-associated offshore lenders. Capital account convertibility is unrestricted for equity, shareholder loans, and intercompany debt restructuring, subject only to the Overseas Investment Act 2005 for sensitive land or strategic assets, which does not apply to a POD retail treasury operation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio (0% mandatory conversion), and no repatriation timeline for export proceeds or cross-border e-commerce receivables. Exporters and POD operators may freely retain foreign currency in licensed bank accounts or offshore EMI wallets, convert at market spot rates, or deploy funds directly for offshore supplier settlement without central bank permission. The statutory timeline for repatriation is N/A.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ / Te Pūtea Matua), operating under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 as the central bank, sole currency-issuing authority, and prudential supervisor of registered banks. New Zealand maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control authority; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) supervises market conduct and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) serves as the principal AML/CFT supervisor for non-bank financial institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH as a complementary settlement and treasury-sweeping layer, subject to two material legal constraints. First, under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013, an unlicensed offshore digital EMI is not a registered bank in New Zealand and cannot hold client funds as a deposit-taker; neither funds held offshore nor balances held with NZ-licensed non-bank EMIs benefit from any New Zealand depositor-compensation or FSCS-equivalent scheme. Second, offshore EMIs create segregation, creditor-risk, and audit-lineage issues if used as primary operating accounts. Accordingly, NZ-registered EMIs that are DIA-supervised and remittance-registered under the AML/CFT Act 2009 (e.g., Wise Payments NZ Ltd, Airwallex New Zealand) may be used as fast inbound/outbound rails for supplier payouts and marketplace settlements, but all statutory capital escrow balances and majority operating liquidity must be consolidated into a Tier 1 domestic bank account with the legal-name-matching account title.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: SWIFT MT103 transmission from the investor/trust bank into the IC's verified NZD or USD account at the onboarding Tier 1 bank. SWIFT field 50K must show the named remitter consistent with share-subscription or loan documentation; field 59 must contain the IC's exact registered legal name, NZ bank BIC, and local account number. Purpose codes: use 'EQUITY' / 'LOAN' descriptors or NZ-bank-specific codes (e.g., 101 Direct Investment Equity Contribution; 102 Intercompany Shareholder Loan). Verification stack: (i) board resolution approving issuance/allotment; (ii) Companies Office return of allotment; (iii) tax-residence and compliance certificate; (iv) source-of-funds evidence; (v) trust deed and trustee resolution where the investor is a trust. The RBNZ cross-border transaction report is generated automatically by the receiving bank; no prior authorisation is required. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Cook Islands statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold; the Cook Islands has not enacted OECD-aligned transfer pricing rules for international companies. However, related party transactions must be arms length in substance to avoid challenge by tax authorities in New Zealand on behalf of the ultimate beneficial owner.

Economic substance scope: The Cook Islands Economic Substance (International Companies) Regulations 2020 apply. An IC is required to demonstrate substantial economic activity in the Cook Islands relative to geographically mobile activities, including distribution and service centre functions inherent in a POD custom apparel business. The IC must maintain premises, qualified staff, and core income-generating activities within the Cook Islands to avoid classification as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

New Zealand Tax Evasion / Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax avoided; criminal conviction under s.143A Tax Administration Act 1994; fine up to NZ$250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years on conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the filing of false tax returns are personally liable under s.143B; civil restitution includes clawback of diverted profits plus interest; tax debt may trigger a border alert preventing travel overseas; disqualification under the Companies Act.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of US$20,000 per relevant activity under Cook Islands Economic Substance Regulations; discretionary daily penalty of up to US$5,000 for continued non-compliance; strike-off of the company; reporting to overseas tax authorities under automatic exchange of information.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally required to make a just and equitable contribution to costs of enforcement; a travel ban may be imposed for repeated non-compliance with substance information notices; foreign directors may be extradited for ancillary offences.
AML / CTF and Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Under the Cook Islands AML/CFT Act 2019, penalties for body corporates up to NZ$1,000,000 per offence and imprisonment of controlling officers up to 14 years; freezing orders may be issued; the company may be deregistered by FSDA.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for implementing AML procedures; civil asset forfeiture applies to the proceeds of money laundering; criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment and disqualification from managing any company in New Zealand or the Cook Islands.
