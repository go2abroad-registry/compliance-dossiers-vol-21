Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under New Zealand law. Shares may be issued for nominal or non-cash consideration as determined by the board, and no escrow deposit or public capital maintenance account is required.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under New Zealand law. Shares may be issued for nominal or non-cash consideration as determined by the board, and no escrow deposit or public capital maintenance account is required.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake), New Zealand Government — myIR digital portal (https://www.ird.govt.nz); corporate registry: New Zealand Companies Office (https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

The foreign International Company with no New Zealand fixed establishment applies to Inland Revenue for an IRD number (tax file number) via the myIR non-resident application or the relevant paper registration form, submitting constitutional documents, director and ultimate beneficial owner declarations, and a valid address for service (including an approved electronic address). Where annual taxable supplies to New Zealand (including remote SaaS services) will exceed NZD 60,000, the entity files a separate GST registration application through myIR; the IRD number itself becomes the GST registration number. Registration may be made in advance of exceeding the threshold (voluntary registration) once a taxable activity exists. IRD verifies identity and ownership information under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 before issuing the IRD/GST number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — Non-resident International Company without a New Zealand permanent establishment: chargeable only on New Zealand-source income; foreign-source income and capital gains are outside New Zealand income tax jurisdiction; GST liability attaches only upon NZ-connected taxable supplies once the NZD 60,000 registration threshold is met.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election. Statutory elections relevant to this entity: (i) GST grouping election under s 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 (if the entity later establishes related NZ entities); (ii) consolidated group election under subpart IG of the Income Tax Act 2007 for corporates with 66% or greater common ownership; (iii) election to zero-rate B2B remote services under s 11(1)(mb) of the GST Act upon receipt of the NZ recipient's GST registration number and certification; (iv) simplified non-resident supplier registration with six-monthly GST return filing and payment using the GST Act offshore supplier provisions; (v) hybrid mismatch determinations and joint elections under subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 (Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 — aggregate value of taxable supplies (including remote services supplied to New Zealand consumers) in any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services must register when this threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is permitted; imported goods remain subject to GST through the Customs border system, with the low-value goods regime requiring offshore suppliers of goods valued at or below NZD 1,000 to collect GST at the point of remote sale regardless of supplier registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is governed by s 11 of the GST Act 1985. Goods: supply is zero-rated where the exporter removes the goods from New Zealand (or supplies them to a nominated carrier for export) within 28 days of the time of supply and retains transport evidence. Services — applicable rules for the PropTech SaaS platform: (i) B2B remote services supplied to a GST-registered New Zealand recipient for the purpose of the recipient's taxable activity are zero-rated under s 11(1)(mb), provided the supplier holds the recipient's GST registration number and the recipient certifies the business purpose; (ii) remote services supplied to a non-resident recipient located outside New Zealand at the time of supply are zero-rated under s 11(1)(m), provided the services are not supplied through a New Zealand fixed establishment; (iii) services physically performed entirely outside New Zealand qualify under s 11(1)(m) irrespective of recipient location. The platform must evidence the recipient's non-resident status, the offshore delivery of SaaS functionality, and the foreign-currency payment trail to sustain zero-rating and any resulting refund claims; while New Zealand law does not prescribe a fixed inward-remittance deadline, the 180-day evidentiary window is applied to preserve the export payment trail.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration / Intended Export), lodged electronically with the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window, including the UCR, HS tariff classification, and certified value and quantity data
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (master and house, as applicable) showing the consignor, consignee, port of discharge, shipping marks, and date of departure, accompanied by the commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward remittance evidence: SWIFT MT103 credit advice or New Zealand bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign-currency settlement from the overseas buyer (functional equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate), together with the executed SaaS agreement, service delivery logs, and the recipient's non-resident or GST-registered status declaration

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, invoice pre-approval, or digital stamping regime exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary under the PEPPOL interoperability framework (Government Digital Service e-invoicing standards from 2022). Tax invoices must contain the content prescribed by s 24 of the GST Act 1985 (supplier name and GST number, invoice date, description, consideration, and customer name for B2B supplies); buyer-created tax invoices are available for certain B2B supplies. GST returns must be filed electronically via myIR or IRD-certified accounting software using IRD's electronic filing APIs; no invoice-level API clearance is mandated, and non-resident remote-service suppliers may account for GST on a simplified basis with foreign-currency conversion.

Inland Revenue may reassess and claw back refunds of zero-rated GST within 4 years from the end of the assessment period (s 108 of the Tax Administration Act 1994), with the limitation period extended indefinitely for fraud or evasion and extended for abusive tax positions or notable omissions. Shortfall penalties scale by culpability: 20% of the tax shortfall for an unacceptable tax position (s 141C), 100% for an abusive tax position (s 141B), and 150% for evasion or a similar act (s 141A); use-of-money interest accrues daily on the amount of any over-refunded GST from the date of payment. Fraudulent export zero-rating may be reversed where goods are not exported within the statutory 28-day window or where a remote-service supply is recharacterised as made in New Zealand. Criminal prosecution under s 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994 for evasion or related offences subjects a person to fines up to NZD 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years per offence, with parallel liability under s 143B for persons who aid, incite, or abet such conduct. Additional consequences include cancellation of simplified non-resident GST registration, freezing of refunds under Part 9 of the TAA, and director disqualification under the Companies Act 1993.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised current and capital account. There are no general exchange-control restrictions on capital inflows, capital outflows, profit repatriation, dividend distribution, or liquidation proceeds remittance. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible and trades in a deep onshore and offshore market. Convertibility is subject only to standard banking due diligence, tax withholding on outbound dividends and interest, and AMLCFT screening. No central bank approval is required for foreign direct investment, equity capital injection, or intercompany loans exceeding any prescribed threshold, although an Overseas Investment Act screening may apply to certain sensitive real property transactions — not engaged by a pure SaaS and property-management software fee model.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand has no statutory requirement for exporters to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or to mandatorily convert foreign currency receipts into NZD. Exporters, including software-as-a-service licensors, are free to hold export proceeds in foreign currency accounts with New Zealand authorised banks or to convert them into NZD. The only practical requirement, if funds are received through a New Zealand bank, is proper remittance coding for statistical balance-of-payments reporting and the bank's verification of the commercial underlying transaction.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand – Te Pūtea Matua, acting jointly with the Financial Markets Authority and the Financial Intelligence Unit under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AMLCFT Act).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility with strict licensing constraints. New Zealand does not have a standalone e-money institution (EMI) licence; non-bank payment providers must operate under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, the AMLCFT Act, and, if they take funds from the public, the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013. Offshore EMI partners such as Wise, Stripe Treasury, Airwallex, or Currencycloud may be used as payment gateways for collection of subscription fees, but they cannot serve as the primary treasury account provider for a New Zealand-registered International Company without a New Zealand banking relationship. Legal constraints include mandatory registration as a financial service provider under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 when soliciting New Zealand customers, and the practical restriction that foreign EMIs generally are not admitted to domestic clearing and settlement systems (ESAS/NZCDC).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an International Company under the New Zealand pipeline, inbound equity capital should be effected by SWIFT MT103 from the overseas parent or subscriber to the New Zealand bank account, using remittance narrative such as 'Share subscription — New Zealand International Company', 'Paid-in capital', or 'Intercompany capital contribution'. The bank should be provided with a board resolution authorising the issuance of shares, a subscription deed or share register extract, and a funds-origin statement. New Zealand banks route incoming foreign currency wires into a segregated inward-draft account, automatically convert to NZD on settlement, and post to the corporate operational account. There is no Reserve Bank approval requirement; however, the bank must complete a standard inward remittance surveillance survey and preserve documentation under AMLCFT Part 2 record-keeping obligations. The final treasury blueprint should designate the beneficial owner of the NZD account as the International Company, with wire purpose code reflecting 'A0008 — Services receipts' if the injection represents prepayment of services revenue, or 'A1001 — Equity capital' for pure capital injection, depending on the bank's classification taxonomy.Trade settlement for operating and trade-related outflows (e.g., cloud infrastructure, subprocessors, legal fees, marketing expenses) should be executed through SWIFT MT103/credit transfers using SWIFT gpi for end-to-end tracking. Major New Zealand banks settle high-value NZD obligations through the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) operated by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand, and route international leg through correspondent banks in USD, EUR, or AUD. For documentary trade transactions (e.g., LC-backed procurement of hardware, software licensing), the bank may issue an irrevocable commercial letter of credit subject to UCP600 using SWIFT MT700 series, together with accepted bills under a banker's acceptance.
