Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Under the Income Tax Act 2007, New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box system; the following statutory elections and classifications are available: (i) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB for closely held companies, allowing tax-transparent treatment; (ii) Portfolio Investment Entity (PIE) election under subparts HL and HM, including listed PIEs that provide REIT-equivalent pooled real estate tax settings, with a 28% final tax at entity level and no further investor tax for qualifying investors; (iii) source-based territorial limitation, whereby non-resident companies are taxable only on New Zealand-sourced income and may otherwise be exempt; (iv) hybrid and branch mismatch rules under Part FH, superseding tax avoidance arrangements and requiring disclosure where mismatch outcomes arise; (v) GST branch registration election for an overseas company to register its New Zealand branch as a separate GST group member. The Tax Exempt Zone designation in the underlying metadata is respected only to the extent the entity has no New Zealand-sourced income and qualifies under an explicit exemption; it does not override the 28% corporate tax rate on New Zealand-sourced real estate income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies per 12-month period (compulsory); non-resident suppliers of 'remote services' (including digital, legal, financial, and certain advisory services supplied to New Zealand-resident consumers) are also required to register when New Zealand-resident consumer supplies exceed NZD 60,000 per 12-month period; voluntary registration is available to non-residents carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand, subject to the remoteness and land-connection exceptions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% GST applies when the supplier exports the goods within 28 days of supply, supplies goods for removal to the Chatham Islands or for use in certain approved overseas projects, and retains the statutory export evidence. Services: 0% GST applies to services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed, provided the services are not supplied to a non-resident while in New Zealand, are not 'connected with real property in New Zealand', and are not physically performed in New Zealand (subject to statutory exceptions for remote services under s 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985). For a New Zealand real estate investment trust, rental, leasing, and property management income connected to New Zealand land is standard-rated (or exempt for residential accommodation) and cannot be zero-rated; only genuinely offshore treasury, administrative, or technical advisory functions performed outside New Zealand for non-resident counterparties, and not connected with New Zealand real property, may qualify for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (lodged via New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export within the statutory period
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT/MT103 evidence confirming receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency

No real-time fiscal clearance, prior authorisation, or digital stamping regime applies in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates on the Peppol interoperability network; from 1 July 2022, all public-sector agencies are obligated to receive Peppol-compliant e-invoices, so suppliers to government should maintain e-invoicing capability. GST-registered persons must issue or provide tax invoices (standard tax invoice, buyer-created tax invoice, or credit note) within 28 days of a supplier's request, and must retain sufficient contemporaneous records (including export shipping documents, bank remittance records, contracts, and board resolutions) to substantiate zero-rated and exported supplies.

Export zero-rating is a priority audit area for the Commissioner of Inland Revenue. Where the supporting documentation bundle is incomplete, inconsistent, or fraudulent, the zero-rating is reversed and output GST at 15% is assessed on the supply, together with use-of-money interest. Shortfall penalties apply under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or fraudulent intent. Knowingly lodging false GST returns or falsifying export documents constitutes a criminal offence under s 143A, carrying imprisonment and/or fines. Overpaid GST refunds, including those generated through circular foreign-exchange flows or sham export arrangements, may be recovered under ss 75 and 113 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, and repeat offenders may be excluded from GST refund schemes and subjected to Commissioner-initiated audit and information-holding notices.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is an independently floating currency with fully open capital and current account convertibility. New Zealand has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations on current international payments, and all statutory exchange controls were abolished following the repeal of the Exchange Control Act 1953 in the mid-1980s. There are no conversion quotas, no exchange-control licensing requirements, and no prior central-bank approval for cross-border capital injections, dividend distributions, or repatriation of property-sale proceeds. For a Real Estate Investment Trust structured as a tax-exempt-zone International Company, the only material monetization frictions are tax-related (non-resident withholding tax on distributions and foreign investment fund classification for non-resident owners) and regulatory (Overseas Investment Act consent for non-resident acquisition of New Zealand land), neither of which restricts currency convertibility or wire clearing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable under New Zealand law: the jurisdiction maintains no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio into NZD. Export and any other foreign-currency receipts may be retained, converted, or disbursed at the account holder's absolute discretion. For the REIT, core operating income comprises NZD-denominated domestic rental receipts collected via local clearing rails; where incidental foreign-currency proceeds arise (e.g., disposal of an offshore property holding), they may be held in a multi-currency account at the Tier-1 bank or converted through the bank's FX desk without any notification to the Reserve Bank. The sole compliance duty is documentary: the REIT must maintain a source-of-funds and foreign-currency treasury ledger to satisfy AML/CFT monitoring expectations for credits at or above NZD 10,000 equivalent.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) as the apex monetary, prudential, and financial-stability authority; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) supervises market conduct and AML/CFT compliance for financial institutions; and the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) separately administers the Overseas Investment Act 2005 screening applicable to non-resident acquisitions of sensitive New Zealand land. All foreign-exchange dealing and liquidity clearing are performed by registered banks under the Reserve Bank's prudential supervision framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium as an ancillary rail; Low as a primary settlement venue. Offshore digital EMI platforms (UK FCA-registered payment institutions or EU PSD2 e-money institutions) are operationally feasible for low-value, recurring dividend wires to international unitholders and for minor foreign-currency conversions, but they cannot substitute for a Tier-1 New Zealand commercial bank. Legal and practical constraints: (i) EMIs lack access to the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS), so they cannot effect real-time NZD clearing; (ii) they are not registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act and their custodial sub-structure introduces unregulated counterparty risk in a REIT context; (iii) mortgage and mezzanine lenders will not recognize EMI balance screens for drawdown conditions; and (iv) the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 supervisor requirements and the trust deed restrict where rental income may be held. The treasury blueprint mandates that all rental concentration, capital escrow, and reserve accounts remain at a Tier-1 NZ bank, with EMI accounts used only as a displacement mechanism for outbound distributions under an executed counterparty-risk opinion.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed as SWIFT MT103 credits in NZD, USD, or AUD to the REIT's designated Tier-1 operational or capital-escrow account. The remitter must complete the SWIFT purpose-code field with 'EQUI' for equity subscription proceeds, 'CPIC' for cash capital contributions, or 'LOAN' for intercompany shareholder advances, and must reference the entity legal name plus the IRD and GST numbers in the narrative. Where the injection funds a New Zealand land acquisition, the OIO consent or exemption certificate (if the acquirer is an overseas person under the Overseas Investment Act 2005) must be in force and lodged with the receiving bank before settlement to avoid a transaction-monitoring escalation. Verification protocol: (i) any single credit at or above NZD 10,000 equivalent is subject to source-of-funds review; (ii) same-day aggregate credits above NZD 50,000 equivalent are conditionally cleared against a pre-approved funding matrix signed by the REIT manager; (iii) the bank will not require any Reserve Bank conversion license or surrender approval because NZD is fully convertible; and (iv) the complete MT103 chain, purpose codes, and funds-destination letters must be archived for at least five years to satisfy AML/CFT record-keeping obligations.The primary cross-border disbursement rail is SWIFT MT103 for dividend distributions, escrow releases, and overseas trade payments, using purpose codes 'DIV' for distributions, 'OTHR' for general trade settlement, or 'GOOD' for merchandise-related payments, as applicable. Domestic NZD clearing, including property completion settlements and tenant rebates, is executed through the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS), New Zealand's real-time gross settlement infrastructure, or through Direct Debit / ACH origination for recurring pre-authorized trust distributions. Where the REIT acquires property from a vendor's solicitor, the compliant bridge is an ESAS RTGS transfer from the REIT escrow account to the solicitor's trust account; a Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600) is an acceptable alternative where the counterparty requires trade-finance security. Dividend disbursement protocol for international unitholders: compute the NRWT withholding at the applicable statutory rate or the reduced rate prescribed by the relevant double-tax agreement; execute a single professional FX conversion on the aggregate distribution amount rather than per-beneficiary conversions; batch the payments into one MT103 file with a detailed beneficiary schedule; and archive the payment file and bank confirmation for the IRD dividend reconciliation and year-end audit.Deliverable FX forward contracts on the NZD/USD or NZD/AUD pairs with maximum tenor of twelve months, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a registered New Zealand bank counterparty, limited to hedging forecast rental income conversions, dividend distributions, or property-sale receivables.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts referencing the NZD, documented under an NDF Annex or the ISDA 1998 FX and Currency Option Definitions, permitted only where a deliverable forward market is inaccessible to a counterparty and the NDF is tied to an underlying real exposure of the REIT; speculative or thinly documented NDFs are prohibited by the treasury policy.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) between NZD and USD or AUD to convert the REIT's floating-rate mortgage liabilities into fixed-rate foreign-currency obligations, executed under ISDA 2002 with a Credit Support Annex and subject to daily mark-to-market collateralization.European-style FX vanilla options on NZD pairs used to hedge the acquisition price of an offshore asset or the repatriation of sale proceeds, with premiums expensed in the period and a strict no-speculative-overlay rule.New Zealand dollar interest rate swaps referencing the Official Cash Rate (OCR) overnight index and 90-day bank-bill rates for managing the REIT's floating debt portfolio, subject to the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 derivatives licensing exemptions and the trustee's investment-statement authority.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium as an ancillary rail; Low as a primary settlement venue. Offshore digital EMI platforms (UK FCA-registered payment institutions or EU PSD2 e-money institutions) are operationally feasible for low-value, recurring dividend wires to international unitholders and for minor foreign-currency conversions, but they cannot substitute for a Tier-1 New Zealand commercial bank. Legal and practical constraints: (i) EMIs lack access to the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS), so they cannot effect real-time NZD clearing; (ii) they are not registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act and their custodial sub-structure introduces unregulated counterparty risk in a REIT context; (iii) mortgage and mezzanine lenders will not recognize EMI balance screens for drawdown conditions; and (iv) the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 supervisor requirements and the trust deed restrict where rental income may be held. The treasury blueprint mandates that all rental concentration, capital escrow, and reserve accounts remain at a Tier-1 NZ bank, with EMI accounts used only as a displacement mechanism for outbound distributions under an executed counterparty-risk opinion.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) rate on dividends paid by a New Zealand-resident company to a non-resident shareholder is 30%. Under New Zealand's double tax agreements, the treaty rate is commonly reduced to 15% for portfolio dividends and to 5% where the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 10% to 25% of the voting power, depending on the applicable treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand transfer pricing documentation thresholds are NZD-denominated: a Master File and Local File must be prepared and retained when the New Zealand taxpayer's cross-border related-party transactions in a category exceed NZD 10,000,000 per income year and the multinational group's global consolidated turnover exceeds NZD 100,000,000. Country-by-Country Reporting is separately required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue of at least NZD 1,000,000,000. Below those thresholds, taxpayers must still maintain sufficient records substantiating arm's length pricing under the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain named Economic Substance Regulations of the type found in zero-tax offshore centres. Instead, the Income Tax Act 2007, incl. the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1, applies a substance-over-form doctrine. An International Company established in an offshore tax-exempt zone must demonstrate genuine economic substance in its residence jurisdiction and cannot rely on a mere mailbox, nominee directors, or a single corporate service provider. If central management and control, board decision-making, real estate management, financing, or asset supervision is exercised in New Zealand, the entity is treated as a New Zealand tax resident and is taxed on worldwide income. Furthermore, the presence of a fixed place of business, or a dependent agent in New Zealand acting for the IC, constitutes a permanent establishment and requires New Zealand corporate tax compliance on the REIT income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Dishonest Filing

Statutory sanction: A tax shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the evaded tax, late payment penalties of 1% per month, and use-of-money interest. Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994 may result in conviction, substantial fines, and imprisonment for up to five years; court-ordered reparation and confiscation of proceeds may also be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, approve, or participate in evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. The corporate veil is pierced by fraudulent conduct, allowing Inland Revenue to pursue personal assets. Directors may be subject to travel bans, asset freezing orders, and criminal imprisonment; disqualification as directors under the Companies Act 1993 may follow a conviction.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance / GAAR

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may disregard the shell entity and reconstruct the arrangements under section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007. The income is reallocated to the person or entity with real control and economic ownership, assessed at 28% plus shortfall penalties (100% for an abusive tax position or 150% for evasion), and interest. The IC may also be denied DTA benefits under the principal-purpose test of the relevant treaty.

Director personal liability: Directors and promoters of the shell arrangement face personal assessments as tax representatives, civil penalties, disqualification, and personal liability for company debts where the company was used for fraudulent trading; travel restrictions may be imposed and personal assets may be reached through civil recovery.
Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism (AML/CTF) Failure

Statutory sanction: Regulatory action under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009: civil penalty orders, infringement notices, compliance remedies, and suspension or cancellation of the reporting entity's registration. Money laundering offences under the Crimes Act 1961 can result in imprisonment of up to seven years; criminal proceeds are subject to forfeiture under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Directors, senior managers, and compliance officers may be held personally liable for failing to establish or maintain an adequate AML/CTF programme. Knowing involvement in money laundering triggers personal criminal liability, imprisonment, travel bans, and confiscation of personal property; the corporate veil does not shield a director who controls the transaction.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax and Profit Repatriation Evasion

Statutory sanction: The payer is personally liable for the NRWT that should have been withheld. A failure to withhold cannot be cured by later voluntary payment by the recipient. Late payment penalties, monthly incremental penalties, interest, and shortfall penalties of up to 100% apply. Criminal charges may be brought for deliberate avoidance of withholding tax obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise or accept non-withholding are personally liable for the unwithheld tax under the withholding tax regime. Civil recovery may enforce against the director's personal bank accounts and assets; travel bans and prosecution may follow; the corporate entity's separate legal personality will be disregarded where there has been fraud or sham.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner of Inland Revenue will adjust the transfer price to an arm's length standard under the Income Tax Act 2007, increasing taxable income and issuing amended assessments. Shortfall penalties may range from 20% for lack of reasonable care to 40% for an unacceptable tax position, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion-related conduct. Use-of-money interest accrues from the date the additional tax should have been paid.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign transfer pricing documentation knowing it to be false, or who direct pricing policies with the dominant purpose of tax avoidance, may be personally liable for penalties and interest. Inland Revenue can seek director penalty notices and pursue restitution where withholding tax, GST, or PAYE obligations are used as part of the profit-shifting arrangement. Directors who are merely wilfully blind are not protected by the corporate veil and may be prosecuted for aiding and abetting tax evasion.
