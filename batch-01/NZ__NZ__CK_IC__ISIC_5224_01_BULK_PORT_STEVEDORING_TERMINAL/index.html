Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand – central portal 'myIR' administered at www.ird.govt.nz; the New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS) manages border and export declaration systems with integrated reporting to the IRD for GST purposes.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand must first obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) via the New Zealand Companies Office and then apply to the IRD for an IRD number (8-9 digit unique tax identifier). For GST purposes, the entity files Form GST 1 (Registration of Person for GST) or the non-resident variant GST 2, providing the registered office address, principal place of business, expected turnover, and evidence of foreign incorporation. The IRD issues the IRD number and GST registration certificate within 5-10 business days; registration is retroactive to the date the taxable activity commenced or the date the entity first exceeded the NZD 60,000 threshold. If the entity is a non-resident company not incorporated in New Zealand, it must also appoint a local agent for service of process (under section 336 of the Companies Act 1993) and disclose the ultimate holding company structure for BEPS and controlled foreign company (CFC) reporting purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope with entity-level taxation for resident companies; the subject 'International Company' (CK_IC) is classified internally as Tax_Exempt_Zone only in respect of foreign-sourced income of a non-resident company. Under the Income Tax Act 2007, a non-resident company is taxable only on income that has a source in New Zealand; income derived wholly overseas is exempt. However, stevedoring services physically performed at New Zealand marine docks (bulk cargo loading/unloading) have a New Zealand source (the services are performed in New Zealand and are directly connected with land and goods situated in New Zealand at the time of supply), hence that income is within the New Zealand tax net. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope with entity-level taxation for resident companies; the subject 'International Company' (CK_IC) is classified internally as Tax_Exempt_Zone only in respect of foreign-sourced income of a non-resident company. Under the Income Tax Act 2007, a non-resident company is taxable only on income that has a source in New Zealand; income derived wholly overseas is exempt. However, stevedoring services physically performed at New Zealand marine docks (bulk cargo loading/unloading) have a New Zealand source (the services are performed in New Zealand and are directly connected with land and goods situated in New Zealand at the time of supply), hence that income is within the New Zealand tax net. The entity is therefore a New Zealand taxable branch of a non-resident company, subject to New Zealand corporate income tax at the standard rate of 28% on its New Zealand-source business profits, and to GST on its taxable supplies.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' classification regime. Elections available include: (i) under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018, the Commissioner of Inland Revenue may adjust treatment of hybrid or reverse-hybrid entities; (ii) a non-resident company may elect to apply the Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) regime (2% levy on interest paid to non-residents) instead of non-resident withholding tax; (iii) a non-resident company can elect for a branch equivalent tax account (BETA) or for the use of the branch equivalent method; (iv) for GST, division 60 of the GST Act 1985 permits a non-resident to elect to appoint a New Zealand-resident agent for GST compliance; and (v) hybrid mismatch elections under the Tax Administration Act 1994 must be disclosed using IRD form IR858 to neutralise deduction/no-inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (for both resident and non-resident persons); once exceeded, mandatory registration is required within 21 days. For non-residents supplying 'remote services' (electronically supplied services, telecommunications, digital services) to New Zealand-based consumers, an additional registration threshold of NZD 60,000 of remote-service supplies applies. Because the stevedoring terminal supplies services physically performed in New Zealand, the standard threshold applies, and the entity must register and charge GST at 15% on all supplies, unless zero-rating applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under section 11 of the GSTA applies to: (a) goods physically exported from New Zealand within 28 days of supply, evidenced by customs documentation; (b) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and who does not use the services in New Zealand, except where the services are supplied in connection with land in New Zealand, or with goods in New Zealand at the time of supply; (c) international transport, insurance, and vessel-refueling services. For the bulk port stevedoring terminal, the core stevedoring service (vessel loading/unloading at a marine dock) is performed in New Zealand and is directly in connection with New Zealand land and with cargo physically present in New Zealand; consequently it does not qualify for zero-rating as a service to a non-resident and must be taxed at 15%, regardless of the invoicing currency. Only ancillary services provided to a non-resident outside New Zealand (e.g., offshore freight forwarding, dispatch documentation services, or fees for services supplied remotely to the ship owner while the vessel is outside New Zealand waters) can be zero-rated. The entity may claim input tax credits on all GST-bearing costs, and any excess credits are refundable by the IRD within 21 days of the return being filed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Cargo Movement Reference / CUSR) as filed with the New Zealand Customs Service and the Integrated Cargo System (ICS) export clearance confirmation
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill evidencing the export of the cargo from a New Zealand port, with the vessel name, voyage number, port of loading, and port of discharge
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or Foreign Exchange Contract confirmation from a registered New Zealand bank or NZX-regulated foreign exchange dealer evidencing receipt of the foreign currency settlement within the prescribed repatriation window
  • Port Terminal Service Order and Vessel Operations Report (including stevedoring time sheets and tally clerk records) demonstrating that the supply was physically performed and linked to the export cargo, and the final GST return and tax invoice set if the supply is claimed as zero-rated

New Zealand operates a voluntary electronic invoicing regime (no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance). E-invoicing follows the Peppol framework and is available via certified service providers; no digital stamping or pre-clearance tax authority approval is required. GST returns must be filed electronically through myIR (mandatory for GST-registered persons with annual turnover exceeding NZD 30,000) on a monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly basis. The IRD performs automated data matching with bank transactions and NZCS border data. A 'buyer-created tax invoice' mechanism exists under section 24D of the GSTA for port terminal operators, subject to prior IRD approval, and all invoices must contain the tax invoice statement 'Includes GST'. Digital asset record keeping is acceptable, and e-invoicing via Peppol is presumed compliant.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to intensive IRD audit, including cross-referencing of NZCS export declarations, port operator records, shipping lines' manifests, and bank inflows. If the IRD determines that a supply was incorrectly zero-rated (e.g., services connected with New Zealand land or goods, or evidence of export is insufficient), the taxpayer is liable for the GST shortfall plus use-of-money interest from the original taxable period. Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, an unacceptable tax position attracts a shortfall penalty of 20% of the tax shortfall; gross carelessness increases the penalty to 40%, and intentional disregard or abuse of the zero-rating rules attracts a 150% penalty. Additionally, promoting or facilitating fraudulent zero-rating schemes carries a criminal penalty of up to NZD 50,000 for a first offence and up to NZD 100,000 for subsequent offences, and imprisonment for up to 10 years for knowingly providing false export documentation under the Crimes Act 1961. Where the wrongful zero-rating involves cross-border reimbursement or a related-party supply, the IRD may apply the general anti-avoidance provision (section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007) to reconstruct the transaction and disallow all associated input credits, with retrospective adjustment across up to four years (and longer in cases of fraud or absolute evasion).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand Dollar (NZD) is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange rate regime. There are no exchange controls on current account transactions (goods, services, dividends, interest, royalties) or capital account movements. Non-resident investors may freely repatriate dividends, branch profits, loan repayments, and disposed proceeds, subject only to reporting and AML/CFT documentation. Capital inflows for port terminal stevedoring operations are not subject to central bank approval; however, investments in 'sensitive' land or significant business assets may require Overseas Investment Office consent. The International Company (Tax-Exempt Zone) will be treated as a non-resident for CRS/FATCA classification, and all capital account remittances must be supported by legal ownership and source-of-funds documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters of stevedoring and cargo-handling services may retain USD and other foreign currency receipts in resident foreign currency accounts or offshore accounts at their discretion. Cross-border transactions above NZD 10,000 equivalent must be reported to the transacting bank for Reserve Bank Balance of Payments statistics, and banks are required to comply with screening under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act). Invoicing of port services in USD with settlement to the NZ operational account is lawful; conversion to NZD is a commercial decision of the IC.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) acting as prudential supervisor and administrator of the Exchange Settlement Account System; Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for wholesale market conduct; Overseas Investment Office (Toitū Te Whenua) for inward foreign direct investment screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low as a primary operating bank; Medium only as a supplemental payment corridor. New Zealand does not maintain an electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime; offshore digital EMIs cannot provide NZD deposit liabilities, credit, or direct access to the domestic BECS/SWIFT clearing system. An offshore EMI may operate only under Financial Service Provider (FSP) registration and a bespoke AML/CFT compliance programme, with settlement held through a NZ incorporated bank. Such arrangements do not satisfy the commercial substance and domestic payment requirements of port concession agreements, nor do they provide committed FX lines or documentary trade services.
