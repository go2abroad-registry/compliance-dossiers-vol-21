Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue, Te Tari Taake) — official online portal: myIR at www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (non-resident company) seeking a New Zealand IRD number must: (1) determine whether it is required to file NZ tax returns because it carries on a taxable activity or derives New Zealand-sourced income; (2) complete the applicable IRD number application form, typically Form IRD 596 'Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application', and submit it together with certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitution, register of directors and shareholders, and evidence of the registered office in the entity's home jurisdiction; (3) if the entity creates a physical or economic nexus requiring NZ GST registration, simultaneously apply for GST registration (Form GST 1A or online via myIR) by providing the IRD number, principal place of business, anticipated turnover, and business activity description; (4) obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) if required for digital B2B transactions, noting that the IRD number remains the statutory tax identification; (5) where no taxable nexus exists, a foreign entity may nevertheless obtain a non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) registration or a New Zealand tax file number for refund filing purposes; all applications are reviewed by Inland Revenue, which issues the IRD number in writing.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign entity (non-resident company) seeking a New Zealand IRD number must: (1) determine whether it is required to file NZ tax returns because it carries on a taxable activity or derives New Zealand-sourced income; (2) complete the applicable IRD number application form, typically Form IRD 596 'Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application', and submit it together with certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitution, register of directors and shareholders, and evidence of the registered office in the entity's home jurisdiction; (3) if the entity creates a physical or economic nexus requiring NZ GST registration, simultaneously apply for GST registration (Form GST 1A or online via myIR) by providing the IRD number, principal place of business, anticipated turnover, and business activity description; (4) obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) if required for digital B2B transactions, noting that the IRD number remains the statutory tax identification; (5) where no taxable nexus exists, a foreign entity may nevertheless obtain a non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) registration or a New Zealand tax file number for refund filing purposes; all applications are reviewed by Inland Revenue, which issues the IRD number in writing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity classification election. The relevant statutory elections affecting cross-border entities include: (a) GST grouping election under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, allowing eligible companies to register as a GST group; (b) GST cash versus accrual (invoice) accounting basis election; (c) election under section 18 of the Income Tax Act 2007 for the use of a particular foreign currency for tax calculation; (d) disclosure-based hybrid mismatch elections/compulsory adjustments under sections FH 1-FH 8 of the Income Tax Act 2007 to neutralise hybrid mismatch outcomes; and (e) election to be taxed as a New Zealand resident solely for double tax relief purposes is not generally available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (economic turnover threshold over any 12-month period for goods and services supplied in New Zealand; for offshore supplies of remote services, supplies to non-residents are generally not subject to NZ GST and do not count toward the registration threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating applies to exports of goods where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply and the goods are sold or leased to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand when the contract is made. Services are zero-rated when the services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not directly connected with New Zealand real property or goods requiring performance in New Zealand. In addition, 3D animation and visual-effects services supplied for offshore clients to be used/lived outside New Zealand qualify for zero-rating if the non-resident customer is the recipient and is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, and the supplier has documentary evidence of the non-resident's identity, the offshore payment source, and contractual nexus. In all cases, the supplier must obtain and retain credible evidence of export and offshore delivery/payment to justify 0% GST treatment.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

There is no real-time fiscal clearance or electronic tax-invoice stamping mandate in New Zealand. GST returns are filed electronically via myIR or certified software (e-invoicing via the Peppol network is voluntary). Taxation authorities may impose a requirement to provide digital records in a prescribed format upon audit, but there is no central government clearance of individual invoices.

Inland Revenue audits export zero-rating claims closely, examining customs declarations, bills of lading, ocean or airway waybills, contracts, invoices, payment methods, and foreign-exchange settlement evidence. If zero-rating is incorrectly claimed, the taxpayer is liable to repay the un-output tax (15% GST) plus use-of-money interest from the date the GST would have been payable. Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, shortfall penalties range from 20% (failure to take reasonable care) and 40% (gross carelessness) up to 150% for evasion or knowingly providing false information. Criminal prosecution may result in fines and imprisonment. Retrospective reassessment may extend four years from the annual GST return period, with no time bar where fraudulent intent or obstruction is established. Refunds to non-resident suppliers are also scrutinised against anti-money-laundering and outward-remittance evidence requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely floating and fully convertible across both current-account and capital-account transactions. No surrender, approval or repatriation hurdles apply to proceeds of services exports, royalties, equity injections, shareholder loans or dividend remittances. For the tax-exempt International Company structure, profit remittance is unimpeded from an FX-control perspective, with the only practical frictions arising from New Zealand withholding taxes (NRWT at 15% on dividends with treaty reductions to 0-5% for substantial shareholdings; NRWT at 15% on royalties with treaty reductions to 5-10%; NRWT at 15% on interest cross-border payments, which is effectively 2% under the Approved Issuer Levy regime), thin-capitalisation safe-harbour limitations on deductible shareholder debt (60% debt-to-asset ratio for foreign-owned groups), and standard CRS/FATCA reporting obligations. The entity must, however, maintain contemporaneous substance and beneficial-ownership documentation to evidence commercial reality and to preserve treaty access.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. A commercial 3D animation and VFX production company may invoice in NZD or any foreign currency, retain 100% of export proceeds in domestic or foreign-currency accounts, and hold funds offshore without any statutory repatriation deadline. Reporting obligations are limited to statistical returns under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS), which are filed by the receiving bank and applied to reportable cross-border settlements, and to income recognition on an accruals basis for New Zealand income tax filings. No exchange-control permission, conversion quota or minimum financing tenor applies to export receipts or the timing of their conversion.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), supervising all registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) regulates licensed derivatives issuers and fund managers under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, and the Department of Internal Affairs supervises non-bank financial institutions under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. New Zealand operates no dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration or exchange-control licensing regime; the Overseas Investment Act 2005, administered by the Overseas Investment Office of Toitū Te Whenua Land Information New Zealand, is the only statutory gateway for foreign equity or asset acquisitions involving sensitive land or significant business assets.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility as a secondary payment rail, subject to material constraints. Wise B2B operates in New Zealand as a licensed money remitter supervised by the Department of Internal Affairs under the AML/CFT Act 2009 and maintains client funds segregated from its own balance sheet, but it is not a registered bank under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand framework and cannot provide settlement balances, credit facilities, letter-of-credit issuance, or capital-escrow custody. Offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut Business, Airwallex and OFX are similarly viable for low-value USD, EUR, GBP and AUD vendor payouts and subcontractor settlements below NZD 100,000 per transaction; however, they are not substitutable for a licensed New Zealand bank account where tax clearance, Overseas Investment Act compliance, government procurement eligibility, or statutory capital verification is required. The recommended treasury architecture is to maintain the primary clearing and escrow relationship with a Tier 1 licensed New Zealand bank and to use Wise B2B exclusively as an accelerated disbursement rail for approved low-value international vendor payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the initial equity injection: (i) the shareholder effects an SWIFT MT103 remittance from its external bank to the New Zealand licensed Tier 1 bank quoting purpose code G029 (capital contributions to the capital of corporations) or the bank's domestic capital-inward classification; (ii) the receiving bank records the credit under the Reserve Bank ITRS statistical framework as a capital-account financial inflow; (iii) the company presents the board resolution authorising the share issue, the share subscription agreement with a fair-value valuation, the certificate of incorporation and certificate of good standing, and the register of shareholdings; (iv) funds are lodged in the Capital Escrow Account pending final UBO verification and issuance of the share certificate; (v) where the capitalisation would trigger the Overseas Investment Act 2005 thresholds relating to sensitive land or significant business assets, clearance is obtained from the Overseas Investment Office prior to completion, though a cash-only equity injection into an ordinary trading International Company is generally exempt; and (vi) following completion, funds may be migrated to the Operational Account for working capital. No Reserve Bank approval is required, and there is no conversion quota, minimum tenor or foreign-sourced-funding ratio applicable.SWIFT MT103 is the primary clearing rail for B2B trade settlements, including rendering farm costs, overseas VFX subcontractors, software licences, talent payroll and vendor payments, with enrichment of creditor identifiers and ISO 20022 purpose codes such as T900 (intellectual property and licence fees), R003 (trade-related payments) and U007 (business travel) to sustain straight-through processing and AML transaction monitoring. Payments above the trade ceiling, payments in exotic currencies, and payments requiring next-day value cross-border must be executed through the licensed bank's global payments network under a pre-deal FX confirmation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory for each NZ company when aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZ$1 million in an income year; Master File: required when the multinational group's worldwide revenue exceeds NZ$100 million AND the NZ entity's covered related-party transactions exceed NZ$1 million; Country-by-Country Report obligation arises at NZ$1 billion group consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no statutory Economic Substance Regulations in the UAE sense; substance is nonetheless tested through: (i) corporate tax residence under s YD 2 (incorporation, centre of management), (ii) the general anti-avoidance rule s BG 1, (iii) Part 4A arm's length transfer pricing rules, and (iv) thin-capitalisation rules (60% debt-to-asset / 110% world-wide ratio) for foreign-controlled NZ companies; a paper 'International Company' with no employees, premises or production activity in NZ will be re-characterised, its 'tax-exempt' status overridden, and its NZ-source animation revenue assessed at 28% with penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / deliberate under-reporting

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty up to 150% of the tax shortfall for evasion or acting to defeat the Revenue (s 141D TAA); misdemeanour/felony prosecution under s 143B: fine of up to NZ$50,000 (individual) or NZ$250,000 (company) and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; confiscation of proceeds under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved false accounts face personal prosecution as principals under s 143B, disqualification as company directors, civil recovery orders over personal assets, and travel bans while IRD criminal proceedings are pending; the corporate veil is pierced where the company is a façade for the director's tax fraud.
Transfer pricing mispricing / disguised profit stripping

Statutory sanction: IRD recomputes the consideration at arm's length and assesses the resulting NZ tax at 28% plus UOMI and late payment penalties; shortfall penalties of 50% apply for an abusive tax position and 150% where the TP arrangement amounts to evasion; documentation penalty applies for failure to keep the NZ$1-million-threshold Local File.

Director personal liability: Directors who set or approved the non-arm's length scheme are liable for penalties as parties to the arrangement (ss 141E and 141EB); a director who is a 'promoter of a tax avoidance scheme' faces a NZ$100,000 penalty; under TAA s 150, an officer of a company that cannot pay its tax may be personally assessed for the unpaid tax if the officer was knowingly a party to the avoidance.
Shell-company substance abuse / sham 'tax-exempt zone' status

Statutory sanction: The tax advantage is denied: IRD applies s BG 1 GAAR and the 'void for tax purposes' doctrine; the entity is treated as a NZ tax resident (s YD 2) or as the taxable PE of the offshore entity; assessments at 28% on all NZ-source income plus shortfall penalties up to 150%; presenters/promoters of the tax-avoidance structure incur separate penalty of NZ$500,000 (companies) / NZ$100,000 (individuals) under s 141EB; the entity is also exposed to deregistration under the Companies Act 1993 for sham arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors or shadow directors of the shell company are jointly and severally liable for unpaid company tax under statutory director-assessment grounds; where assets are transferred offshore to defeat IRD, personal bankruptcy or liquidation procedures may be invoked; foreign directors face travel bans and extradition assistance where evasion charges are laid.
Failed withholding, NRWT evasion and AML/CFT exposure on cross-border payment rails

Statutory sanction: Unpaid NRWT is recovered from the company with LPP (1% + UOMI) and a 150% evasion penalty; failure to deduct NRWT leaves the payer personally assessed under s 31 TAA; money laundering offences attract criminal liability (up to 7 years' imprisonment under the Crimes Act 1961); AML/CFT civil penalty orders for a reporting entity can reach NZ$5 million; tax clearance restrictions and seizure of payment assets apply on conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the company's failure to withhold NRWT where the company is insolvent; participation in laundering the company's tainted animation revenue exposes directors to prosecution as principals, personal criminal forfeiture of bank balances/real estate under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009, and five-year bans from managing NZ companies without Court leave.
