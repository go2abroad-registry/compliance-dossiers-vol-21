Competent corporate registry

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Companies Act 1993; the company may be incorporated with zero issued share capital. No escrow account or capital deposit is required by the Companies Office for a New Zealand International Company. Consideration for issued shares may be satisfied in cash, by shareholder loan offset, or by services in kind; the terms of issue and the value of consideration must be recorded in a board resolution and contemporaneous financial records to satisfy the solvency test in s 4 of the Act.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certification: the warehouse and office premises must obtain a Building Warrant of Fitness (Form 12) issued by the building owner under the Building Act 2004 and the Building (New Zealand Building Code) Regulations 1992; the warrant must include the annual Fire Safety Certificate issued by a qualified IQP (Independent Qualified Person) confirming the sprinkler system, fire hydrants, and fire alarm monitoring are compliant with the Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) Act 2017; a Fire Evacuation Scheme must be approved by FENZ where more than 10 persons are present. An annual ‘Form 11A’ building inspection record must be kept. Structural and Load Clearance: a Chartered Professional Engineer (CPEng) must certify the warehouse floor loads and pallet racking seismic restraint in accordance with AS/NZS 1170.0-2002 and NZS 1170.5-2004; the racking installation must be undertaken by a certified racking engineering contractor and a certificate of seismic compliance for a 5000-pallet high-bay configuration must be on file. Occupational and Workplace Clearance: WorkSafe New Zealand registration under the Health and Safety at Work at Work Act 2015 is automatic on commencement of business, but the Company must prepare a Site Specific Safety Plan (SSSP) that includes: (i) a controlled-temperature pharmaceutical storage zone validated at 15–25°C (or 2–8°C for refrigerated clinical logistics) with continuous data logging and an alarm response plan; (ii) a quarantine area for temperature excursion stock; (iii) forklift operator certificates with a valid F-endorsed licence issued by a WorkSafe-accredited tertiary provider; (iv) manual handling and fit-to-work medical clearances for warehouse operatives; (v) spill containment and PPE protocols for pharmaceutical spillage; and (vi) a health and safety induction log and emergency response team certification. A separate ‘worker engagement and participation’ system must be documented, and hazardous substances must be segregated in a dedicated HSNO-approved caged area with a secondary containment racetrack.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Available elections under New Zealand law: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 for a closely-held New Zealand-resident International Company, rendering it fiscally transparent; (2) GST group registration election under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 to consolidate related NZ entities; (3) consolidated group election for wholly-owned corporate groups under subpart FG of the Income Tax Act 2007; (4) hybrid mismatch ordering elections under the BEPS hybrid rules (Part FH of the Income Tax Act 2007); and (5) for the Tax_Exempt_Zone IC profile, a non-resident company may rely on the territorial attribution rules so that foreign-source income is excluded from New Zealand corporate tax, provided it elects or confirms that it has no fixed establishment in New Zealand and conducts no New Zealand-source taxable supplies beyond permitted thresholds. New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (mandatory registration); voluntary registration is available to non-resident businesses carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand; non-resident suppliers of remote/digital services must register on a NZD 60,000 global-supplies basis when supplying B2C remote services to New Zealand consumers

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% GST) applies to: (a) goods physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier, where the goods are exported within the required timeframe and the supplier retains evidence of export (GST Act 1985 section 11(1)(a)); (b) services supplied to a non-resident where the services are not physically performed in New Zealand and are directly in connection with goods situated outside New Zealand at the time of supply (section 11A); (c) international freight, transport and insurance services in respect of exported goods, including GDP pharma logistics, where the freight forwarder demonstrates that title, control and risk in the pharmaceutical cargo pass to the overseas consignee and that the cargo physically departs New Zealand; and (d) supplies to non-residents that are expressly zero-rated by Gazette exemption under the GST Act. Eligibility for the CK_IC requires the entity to hold a valid zero-rating certificate, verify the non-resident status of the recipient, and maintain a complete audit trail of export logistics, including time-stamped departure evidence; pure domestic storage or handling of pharma goods inside New Zealand for a New Zealand principal is taxable at 15%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (NZ Customs electronic export entry filed through the Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign currency bank confirmation evidencing offshore payment
  • Commercial invoice and signed GST zero-rating certificate (section 11(1) statement)
  • Non-resident recipient/supplier declaration confirming offshore status and taxable activity
  • GDP temperature-monitored transport/reconciliation log and pharma cargo integrity certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate exists under New Zealand law. GST invoices must satisfy section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and be issued within 28 days of the supply. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Peppol network under the New Zealand Peppol Authority. GST returns are filed electronically via myIR on a two-month or six-month filing cycle; Inland Revenue performs data matching and risk-based audit selection, but there is no API-based pre-clearance stamping, digital watermark, or invoice-by-invoice fiscal approval mechanism applicable to B2B exports.

Inland Revenue may reassess incorrectly zero-rated supplies within four years of the assessment date, extended to seven years for gross carelessness or evasion, and with no time limit where no return has been filed; clawback of GST at 15% plus use-of-money interest applies. Civil shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 escalate from 20% (lack of reasonable care or unacceptable tax position) to 100% (abusive tax position) and 150% (evasion). Fraudulent export zero-rating documentation exposes the CK_IC to criminal liability under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994, including fines up to NZD 50,000 per conviction for the company and imprisonment for responsible individuals, together with NZ Customs enforcement under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, including seizure of cargo, suspension of export privileges, and imposition of penalties for false export declarations as to destination, value, or pharmaceutical cargo status. Although New Zealand law does not prescribe a 180-day inward remittance statute, banking and transfer pricing practice treats remittance within 180 days as the documentary benchmark; failure to repatriate consideration within that window may trigger substantive review and reversal of the zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a free-floating currency regime with full current and capital account convertibility. There is no exchange-control legislation restricting the inflow or outflow of foreign currency, and the New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely tradable on global markets. Profit remittances and capital repatriations are subject to no quantitative restrictions; the only statutory filters are the Overseas Investment Act 2005 (which screens foreign acquisitions of sensitive land and significant business assets, generally NZD 200 million or above for treaty partners) and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (which governs customer due diligence at the banking intermediary level). A non-resident Cook Islands International Company (Tax_Exempt_Zone classification) faces no convertibility hurdle for dividend, interest, or equity capital flows, and the New Zealand income tax system does not impose a branch-remittance barrier on non-resident treasury accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement under New Zealand law to surrender export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for bringing export receipts onshore. Exporters may retain foreign currency earnings in offshore bank accounts indefinitely, provided those earnings are properly declared for income tax and covered under the Controlled Foreign Company and transfer-pricing rules where applicable. For this GDP pharma freight structure, freight forwarding fees and logistics reimbursements from non-resident clients may be credited directly to the IC's offshore or onshore NZD foreign-currency account without Reserve Bank reporting, although NZ banks will apply domestic International Transactions Reporting (ITR) thresholds (typically NZD 10,000 per transaction) for balance-of-payments statistics.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Cross-border financial technology EMIs (Electronic Money Institutions) such as Airwallex (registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register), Wise NZ, and OFX provide viable ancillary clearing capacity for low-value freight disbursements and supplier payments. Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for operational transactions, LOW for escrow or capital injection vehicles. Legal constraint: no EMI may hold a New Zealand banking licence; they are non-deposit-taking financial service providers supervised by the FMA under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and are required to meet AML-CFT obligations. Because the underlying business is GDP pharma freight (Low_Risk_Standard AML tier), an EMI may be used for routine disbursement rails, but the primary settlement ledger must be held with a licensed New Zealand registered bank to ensure MT103 settlement finality and to satisfy customs and pharmacovigilance audit trails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial capital injection from the parent or beneficial owner to the NZ operating treasury account must be executed exclusively via SWIFT MT103 with full remitter detail. New Zealand banks require a purpose-of-payment code under the International Transactions Reporting (ITR) framework: use code 'FDI – Equity Capital' (inward direct investment into a resident enterprise) if the IC is registered as an NZ branch; if the capital is held offshore and used to fund a New Zealand trust, use code 'Trust Increase in Liability' or the specific FMA-approved narrative. The compliant protocol is: (i) the remittance must originate from a bank account in the name of the declarant shareholder, never from a third-party aggregator; (ii) the reference field must cite the IC's unique identifier and the words 'Capital Escrow – GDP Freight Validation', (iii) supporting board resolution, subscription agreement, and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's no-objection implicit posture are transmitted to the receiving bank in advance; (iv) amounts above NZD 100,000 per single credit may require the bank to file a Reportable Transaction summary and source-of-funds confirmation, though no prior exchange-control approval is required; (v) for currency conversion, the NZD/USD spot trade must be booked through the receiving bank to create a single audit trail.Outbound disbursements for GDP pharma freight—covering airfreight, cold-chain storage, customs duties, and inland cartage—are cleared through four rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for international supplier payments (airline cargo carriers, overseas pharma forwarders), with a strict 24-hour execution window and a standing instruction to append the pharma product NDC or freight-airway number in payment narrative; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) for high-value imports of temperature-controlled packaging materials where the logistics provider is not yet a trusted counterparty; (3) NZ BECS (Bulk Electronic Clearing System) for domestic NZD vendor payments, executed as Direct ACH (Direct Credit) with the four-digit PHARM-GDP reconciliation reference; and (4) same-day High-Value Clearing System (HVCS) for urgent settlements above NZD 500,000 to customs brokers or cargo terminal operators. All outbound payments must be matched against an approved purchase order and a goods-receipt confirmation generated by the GDP manual; the treasury policy disallows unearmarked 'general sweep' transfers, and every MT103 must carry a BIC-validated beneficiary account.Deliverable OTC Forward (Plain Vanilla NZD/USD, NZD/AUD, NZD/EUR) – permitted for balancing the receivable-denominated treaty of sale when the pharma freight client is invoiced in USD while the airlines bill in NZD; must be transacted on ISDA or NZ-documentation master agreement and fully covered by underlying exposureWindow Forward Contracts – permitted to hedge a portfolio of uncertain inbound remittances arising from third-party logistics quarterly reconciliationsFX Swap (Swap Against Spot) – permitted for temporary bridging of NZD liquidity into USD for weekend warehouse duty payments, closed within one monthCross-Currency Swap – permitted for a single-medium-term USD liability of the IC's foreign-currency bank loan, if any, with interest and principal exchanged via two residual spot conversionsVanilla Exchange-Traded and OTC Options (Zebra and Call/Put structures) – permissible only for hedging recognised freight tender bids, and subject to FMA derivative issuance rules if the IC becomes a regular issuer; an IC using options purely for hedging trade lines under ISDA 2002 with a NZ registered bank is exempt from FMA licensingNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) generally NOT required for NZD because the NZD is freely deliverable; prohibited as speculative positional hedging under the corporate treasury mandate unless expressly approved by the board and booked with a bank licensed in New Zealand

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Cross-border financial technology EMIs (Electronic Money Institutions) such as Airwallex (registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register), Wise NZ, and OFX provide viable ancillary clearing capacity for low-value freight disbursements and supplier payments. Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for operational transactions, LOW for escrow or capital injection vehicles. Legal constraint: no EMI may hold a New Zealand banking licence; they are non-deposit-taking financial service providers supervised by the FMA under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and are required to meet AML-CFT obligations. Because the underlying business is GDP pharma freight (Low_Risk_Standard AML tier), an EMI may be used for routine disbursement rails, but the primary settlement ledger must be held with a licensed New Zealand registered bank to ensure MT103 settlement finality and to satisfy customs and pharmacovigilance audit trails.
