Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, a business unit of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE); operates the electronic Companies Register at www.companiesoffice.govt.nz.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No 105): ss 5-15 (registration and incorporation), s 16 (constitution), s 21 (name requirements), s 22 (restrictions on name), s 24 (address for service), s 25 (registered office), ss 32-46 (share capital and issue of shares; no par value rule), ss 122-126 (powers of board), s 128 (director duty of care, diligence, and skill), ss 131-138 (good faith and related party duties), ss 151-158 (appointment and qualification of directors; residence requirement at s 152), ss 189-200 (shareholder and board meetings), and Schedule 1 (default rules for companies without a constitution). Also refer to the Financial Reporting Act 2013 and the Companies (Fees) Regulations 2016 (online incorporation fee NZD 100).

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NZCN (electronically filed), Application for Registration of a New Zealand Company, incorporating the memorandum of incorporation, consents of directors and shareholders, and the constitution (if any); the Registrar issues the Certificate of Incorporation under s 17(2) of the Companies Act 1993.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is required by the Companies Act 1993. A company may be registered with no issued shares or with shares issued without par value; paid-up capital may be nil or a nominal amount (e.g., NZD 0.01 per share). There is no statutory escrow account or blocked capital account requirement in New Zealand. Share consideration must be determined by the board in compliance with ss 44-45 of the Companies Act 1993 and may take the form of cash, property, or past services.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake), New Zealand Government; online portal: myIR (myir.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned entity physically retailing goods in New Zealand must obtain a New Zealand IRD number (the combined income tax/GST identifier) by submitting Form IR742 (Company Registration for Non-Resident Companies) or by online application through myIR with certified constitutional documents and proof of foreign registration. Once the IRD number is issued, GST registration is compulsory within 21 days from the end of any month in which taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a rolling 12-month period; voluntary GST registration is available immediately from the commencement of taxable activity, enabling input credit recovery for establishment costs.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-owned entity physically retailing goods in New Zealand must obtain a New Zealand IRD number (the combined income tax/GST identifier) by submitting Form IR742 (Company Registration for Non-Resident Companies) or by online application through myIR with certified constitutional documents and proof of foreign registration. Once the IRD number is issued, GST registration is compulsory within 21 days from the end of any month in which taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a rolling 12-month period; voluntary GST registration is available immediately from the commencement of taxable activity, enabling input credit recovery for establishment costs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box income tax election is available to a Tax_Exempt_Zone International Company under New Zealand domestic law; the entity may elect voluntary GST registration, elect a one-month, two-month, or six-month GST filing frequency, and may elect the invoices, payments, or hybrid GST accounting basis. If the entity were converted to a New Zealand resident company, a Look-Through Company election (Income Tax Act 2007, ss 48A-48G) would permit member-level pass-through taxation; Qualifying Company elections are closed to new electors.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period; non-resident and tax-exempt entities are equally subject to the compulsory threshold and may register voluntarily below that threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST-registered supermarket may apply the 0% export rate only to supplies of goods that are physically exported by the supplier within 28 days from the time of supply and for which the supplier retains evidence of exportation acceptable to the IRD. Offshore services, remote services, and goods supplied in connection with overseas installation or border-crossing logistics may also qualify. Domestic point-of-sale retail supplies to consumers are standard-rated at 15% and cannot be zero-rated.

  • New Zealand Customs Export Declaration (Tradelink/Integrated Cargo System electronic entry) evidencing outward processing
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the goods departed New Zealand within 28 days of supply
  • Inward remittance certificate or matching bank/foreign exchange payment evidence recording full overseas settlement
  • Commercial invoice and packing list reconciled to the export declaration and GST return

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing operates on a voluntary Peppol-compliant framework administered by the New Zealand Peppol Authority. GST returns and invoice data are transmitted to the IRD periodically through the myIR portal or via approved EDI/API gateway integrations, not through continuous fiscal clearance. There is no requirement to obtain a prior government stamp or cryptographic token on individual invoices.

IRD and New Zealand Customs jointly audit export zero-rating claims; unsupported or fraudulent claims trigger GST shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the unpaid tax (Tax Administration Act 1994 s 141), use-of-money interest, compulsory clawback of input credits previously allowed on export stock, and potential criminal prosecution under s 143A with imprisonment up to 5 years in cases of evasion. Customs penalties under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 apply separately for false or incomplete export declarations.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions under a market-determined floating exchange rate regime. There are no restrictions on profit remittances, dividend distributions, interest payments, or repayment of shareholder loans to non-residents. Non-residents may freely acquire, hold, and dispose of NZD and may transfer funds in and out of New Zealand without CBI-style exchange control approval. The only practical hurdles are standard anti-money-laundering (AML/CFT) checks, tax withholding obligations under the Income Tax Act 2007, and the filing obligations applicable to ongoing foreign direct investment in certain sensitive sectors.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion window or ratio. Exporters may retain receipts in original foreign currencies, convert them at arm's-length commercial rates, or hold them in designated foreign-currency accounts at New Zealand registered banks for future trade settlements. Financial institutions are required to report balance-of-payments statistical data to the Reserve Bank in aggregate when transaction thresholds are exceeded, using IMF Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) classifier codes, but this creates no timing or conversion obligation. Any perceived surrender-like condition would arise only from bank-specific facility covenants, not from the national regulatory framework.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), acting as the central bank and prudential regulator under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989. The Overseas Investment Office (OIO) administers the Overseas Investment Act 2005 for screening of specific significant land and business assets, but ordinary treasury flows require no RBNZ or OIO prior approval. Foreign exchange policy operates within a free-market framework; there is no standalone foreign exchange control statute actively imposing exchange restrictions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium for full treasury-grade clearing; Medium for ancillary payment aggregation. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) are generally treated as payment service providers, not banks, under New Zealand law. An offshore EMI that does not hold a New Zealand financial service provider (FSP) registration and a relevant AML/CFT registration cannot lawfully offer money-transfer or e-money services in New Zealand. Even an FSP-registered EMI cannot provide the same treasury settlement certainty as a licensed registered bank under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989 and Deposit Taker Act 2022 regime. To access direct NZD clearing, an offshore EMI must establish a local corporate presence, obtain FSP registration, appoint a New Zealand agent, and maintain a settlement account with a Tier 1 licensed bank; this effectively reintroduces a correspondent-banking layer and materially undermines the cost or friction advantages sought through an offshore EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital injection from a non-resident shareholder should be routed as a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer in NZD or a freely convertible major currency, with the transaction purpose indicated as 'Direct Investment / Equity Contribution' under standard BPM6 classification (code for direct investment equity capital). No foreign-exchange approval certificate is required from the Reserve Bank. The receiving Tier 1 bank will conduct standard incoming-wire due diligence and will expect the transfer narrative to identify the New Zealand entity, the shareholder, and the nature of the contribution, for example 'Capital injection into CK_IC for working capital purposes.' For amounts above NZD 100,000, the bank typically requires a board resolution of the New Zealand entity, the signed subscription or contribution agreement, and, where relevant, the overseas parent's share register. If the capital is conveyed in foreign currency, the bank will automatically convert it at a repo rate? The bank's prevailing market rate or allow the entity to hold it in a foreign-currency account. All capital must be reflected in the company's equity records; if in any doubt, the bank may request an accountant's letter confirming equity treatment as opposed to a shareholder loan, which has different tax and thin-capitalisation consequences.Outbound payments to overseas suppliers of grocery goods and condiments are settled primarily through the SWIFT network. Clean straight payments use SWIFT MT103 messages, with clearing in NZD operating over the Reserve Bank's real-time gross settlement system (the Exchange Settlement Account System, ESAS) where accounts are credited final and irrevocable. For foreign-currency payments, the correspondent leg settles through the relevant offshore clearing system (e.g., Fedwire or CHIPS in the United States, Target2 in Europe). For documentary trade, irrevocable letters of credit are issued via SWIFT MT700, accompanied by the sales contract, pro-forma invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, phytosanitary certificate, and any required export/import licence documentation. Bank collection services using SWIFT MT400/MT202 are available but are generally inferior to LC arrangements for retaining documentary discipline. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% on unimputed dividends; 15% on fully imputed dividends paid to non-residents. Under New Zealand's network of double taxation agreements, the rate is commonly reduced to 15% (5% where the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 10% of the voting capital).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a standard 31 March balance date, the IR4 return must be filed by 7 July of the same calendar year. Terminal tax (the balance of income tax after provisional tax and credits) is due by 7 April of the following income year. Non-standard balance dates require filing by the 7th day of the 4th month following the balance date, with corresponding provisional tax dates adjusted accordingly. Use-of-money interest runs from the terminal tax date on any underpaid balance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand has no statutory Local File/Master File threshold in domestic legislation; however, Inland Revenue explicitly requires taxpayers to prepare and retain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZ$10 million per income year. Mandatory country-by-country reporting and Master File obligations arise for New Zealand resident entities of MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million or more.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not administer an offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, because it is an onshore substantive tax jurisdiction with no general tax-exempt international-company status. Instead, the statutory tests for tax residence (incorporation, head office, centre of management, and director control) combined with the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007) require genuine commercial activity, physical presence, staff, inventory, local decision-making, and real accounting substance. A grocery retail hub is expected to maintain an actual trading store in New Zealand; a bare nominee or conduit arrangement lacks substance and may be disregarded with penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Returns

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994 ss 143A-143B carries imprisonment of up to 5 years and/or a fine of up to NZ$50,000 for individuals; a company is liable to a fine of up to NZ$200,000. Civil shortfall penalties escalate from 20% (unacceptable tax position) to 40% (gross carelessness) and 150% (evasion), plus use-of-money interest on the entire unpaid balance.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly prepare or authorise false returns are personally liable to prosecution. The IRD director penalty regime (Part 9A of the Tax Administration Act 1994) renders directors personally liable for the company's unpaid GST, PAYE, and other withholding taxes when the company has not remitted them. Courts may issue arrest warrants, impose travel bans, and make directors personally liable for civil restitution in fraud-based insolvency.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner of Inland Revenue has authority under section GC 8 of the Income Tax Act 2007 to adjust the consideration to an arm's length amount. Adjustments trigger additional tax, shortfall penalties of 40% for gross carelessness or 150% for evasion, and use-of-money interest. Failure to produce contemporaneous TP documentation in response to an IRD notice also attracts a specific penalty and shifts the burden of proof to the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to ensure adequate transfer pricing documentation or who approve knowingly inflated intercompany payments are exposed to civil penalties and personal liability. Where the resulting shortfall is linked to unpaid withholding taxes or GST, the director penalty provisions apply directly, and egregious cases may support director prosecution and travel restrictions pending criminal inquiry.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule under section BG 1 voids the tax advantage of such arrangements. The company may he reassessed as a New Zealand-resident company on its worldwide income, with shortfall penalties of up to 100% and strike-off under the Companies Act 1993 for failure to carry on business. Directors can be disqualified for up to 5 years for abusing the corporate form.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil liability for reckless trading under section 135 of the Companies Act 1993 and fraudulent trading under section 380, which carries personal liability for all company debts, fines, and up to 5 years imprisonment. Courts may order asset freezes, passport surrender, and travel bans to secure the directors' availability for examination or prosecution.
AML/CFT Deficiencies and Money-Laundering Exposure

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a reporting entity that fails to conduct customer due diligence, maintain an AML/CFT programme, or report suspicious transactions is liable to fines of up to NZ$2 million for companies and NZ$300,000 for individuals per contravention. Offences related to laundering criminal proceeds under the Crimes Act 1961 carry imprisonment of up to 7 years, and assets derived from crime are subject to forfeiture under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.

Director personal liability: If the grocery hub is used as a cash-clearing vehicle, directors and beneficial owners may be prosecuted as principals or accessories to money laundering, facing imprisonment, confiscation of personal assets, and international travel bans via Interpol red notices. Directors are also obliged to file suspicious transaction reports; failure to do so gives rise to personal criminal liability.
Failure to Withhold NRWT on Cross-Border Distributions

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is personally liable for the full amount of NRWT that should have been deducted (Tax Administration Act 1994 s 31A). Late payment penalties and use-of-money interest apply from the due date. Failure to withhold with intent to evade creates criminal exposure identical to tax evasion under ss 143A-143B.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise the distribution without confirming NRWT compliance are personally liable for the uncollected tax under the director penalty regime. In deliberate cases, directors face prosecution for aiding the foreign shareholder's tax evasion, with consequent fines, imprisonment, and travel bans pending extradition proceedings.
