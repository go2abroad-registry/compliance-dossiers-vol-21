Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Tāke) of New Zealand; administrative portal: myIR (www.ird.govt.nz); legislative authority under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident International Company (IC) obtains a New Zealand IRD number by filing Form IR742 (Application for an IRD number – Non-Resident Individual or Company) with the Inland Revenue Department, either directly or through an approved New Zealand tax agent. Supplementary GST registration under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 is effected separately via Form IR356 or online through myIR when the IC carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand or makes supplies of remote services to New Zealand-resident consumers. No statutory licence, minimum capital, or local physical presence is required for registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone: For New Zealand tax purposes, the International Company is default-classified as a non-resident company subject to entity-level taxation only on New Zealand-sourced income (notably non-resident withholding tax on NZ interest, dividends, and royalties). All foreign-sourced business income derived by the consumer-panel survey platform falls outside New Zealand's income tax net under the territorial sourcing rules, provided the company is not deemed tax-resident in New Zealand under the dual-residency tie-breaker test and does not have a New Zealand fixed establishment. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: For New Zealand tax purposes, the International Company is default-classified as a non-resident company subject to entity-level taxation only on New Zealand-sourced income (notably non-resident withholding tax on NZ interest, dividends, and royalties). All foreign-sourced business income derived by the consumer-panel survey platform falls outside New Zealand's income tax net under the territorial sourcing rules, provided the company is not deemed tax-resident in New Zealand under the dual-residency tie-breaker test and does not have a New Zealand fixed establishment. The classification therefore operates as an offshore tax-exempt zone in respect of the platform's worldwide non-NZ income.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' entity election. The relevant statutory election mechanisms are: (i) GST grouping election under section 55 of the GST Act 1985 for related entities; (ii) election of GST accounting basis (payments/cash basis vs. invoice basis) and filing frequency (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly); (iii) election to register the IC as a New Zealand foreign trust pursuant to the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 where the trust-structured IC has a non-resident settlor, thereby exempting non-NZ-source income; and (iv) hybrid-mismatch elective alignment or self-correction adjustments to neutralise deductible-payment mismatches across cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of cumulative taxable supplies in any 12-month period; registration is compulsory once the threshold is exceeded and voluntary below it for persons carrying on a taxable activity. Non-resident suppliers of remote/digital services to New Zealand-resident consumers are also required to register under the offshore remote-services GST regime once the same NZD 60,000 threshold is met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available to the consumer-panel survey platform under section 11 of the GST Act 1985 where: (i) goods are physically exported from New Zealand within 28 days of supply; (ii) services are supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed and is not in New Zealand when the services are received; or (iii) services are supplied directly to a non-resident business in connection with that business's tax assessment, tax collection, or tax payment obligations (deemed zero-rated B2B supply). Cloud-based market-research and consumer-polling services provided to offshore clients qualify as zero-rated supplies of services when the recipient is outside New Zealand and the services are not performed in connection with land or goods situated in New Zealand. The zero-rating claim must be supported by contemporaneous evidence of the non-resident recipient, the offshore supply nexus, and the receipt of consideration in foreign currency.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Export Entry filed through the New Zealand Trade Single Window or equivalent electronic declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of goods from New Zealand, where goods are exported
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency from the offshore client
  • Signed commercial invoice and underlying service contract identifying the non-resident recipient and the offshore market-research service
  • Recipient non-residency certificate or comparable proof of offshore status for zero-rated services

New Zealand imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no API tax-stamping requirement, and no mandatory electronic invoicing for private-sector suppliers. E-invoicing is optional and operates on the Peppol network under the New Zealand e-Invoicing scheme administered by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment; central government agencies are required to receive e-invoices, but suppliers may issue paper or PDF invoices. GST compliance is audited through periodic returns filed in myIR, and the Inland Revenue Department may require SAF-T data extraction as part of an audit.

The Inland Revenue Department scrutinises zero-rating claims through document-matching and risk-rating algorithms. An unjustified zero-rating claim triggers a reassessment with GST clawback plus interest from the due date of the return. Penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 scale by culpability: 20% shortfall penalty for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for a tax-avoidance arrangement, and 150% for evasion or an abusive tax position. Misdeclaration in connection with income tax carries an additional 43% accuracy penalty. Although New Zealand does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline, the 180-day benchmark is used by IRD as a reasonable period for receipt of export proceeds; overdue repatriation will be treated as evidence that the export supply is not genuine, leading to cancellation of zero-rating, reversal of input credits, and clawback of refunds. Criminal conviction for export-fraud can result in a fine of up to NZD 250,000 and/or five years' imprisonment per offence, with no time limitation for fraudulent evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. New Zealand operates an open capital account with no exchange-control licensing, no prior approval for profit repatriation, and no limits on dividend distribution to non-resident shareholders, subject only to normal tax withholding obligations (non-resident withholding tax) and AML/CFT scrutiny. Direct inward investment above statutory thresholds may require consent under the Overseas Investment Act 2008, but this is an ownership-control filter, not a currency convertibility restriction. The International Company structure is tax-exempt in its formation jurisdiction; no New Zealand thin capitalization or exchange-control impediments apply to the clearing rails described.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export receipts, no mandatory surrender of foreign currency to the Reserve Bank, and no minimum conversion ratio. The International Company may retain earnings in offshore foreign-currency accounts and repatriate or settle trade obligations freely via any registered bank or authorised non-bank clearing provider.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High, with structural limits. Cross-border digital EMI/fintech platforms such as Wise and Airwallex can be onboarded as client accounts and payment-disbursement rails, but they are not a regulated bank substitute in New Zealand. Wise holds a New Zealand FSPR licence; Airwallex holds an FSPR registration and operates through partnerships to provide NZD and FX clearing. Neither platform provides the regulated status of a locally incorporated registered bank, and the International Company must still maintain at least one operational banking relationship for escrow, collateral, and large-value settlements. New Zealand does not prohibit offshore EMIs from servicing non-resident International Companies, but the EMI must comply with New Zealand AML/CFT and the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 when carrying on financial services in or from New Zealand.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loans into the New Zealand banking platform should be routed via SWIFT MT103 with explicit remitter and beneficiary identification, using the receiving bank's transaction-purpose and narrative classification, such as paid-in capital, shareholder loan, service revenue, or intercompany settlement. New Zealand does not mandate regulatory purpose-code approval, but the receiving bank will require a capital-introduction letter, board resolution, invoiced or subscription documentation, and a UBO diagram. The funds should be held first in a segregated Capital Escrow Account, then moved to the Operational Account only after satisfactory KYC, for the purpose of purchasing panel data, paying software-infrastructure costs, and funding operating expenses.Outbound settlements should be split by rail: (a) bank-to-bank B2B supplier payments, software licensing, and market-research vendor payments through SWIFT MT103 over the New Zealand clearing system via the tier-1 bank; (b) high-frequency small-value consumer-panel incentives through Wise or PayPal Mass Payout platforms with aggregate settlement by the treasury to the EMI's master account; (c) commercial letter of credit or SWIFT MT700 only for goods-related procurements, although the survey platform's service-based nature will rarely require LC terms. Wise holds a New Zealand FSPR licence; Airwallex holds an FSPR registration and operates through partnerships to provide NZD and FX clearing. Neither platform provides the regulated status of a locally incorporated registered bank, and the International Company must still maintain at least one operational banking relationship for escrow, collateral, and large-value settlements. New Zealand does not prohibit offshore EMIs from servicing non-resident International Companies, but the EMI must comply with New Zealand AML/CFT and the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 when carrying on financial services in or from New Zealand.
