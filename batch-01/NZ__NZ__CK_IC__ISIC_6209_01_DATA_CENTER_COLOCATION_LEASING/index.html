Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand, the national tax and revenue collection agency, operating the online portal 'myIR' (https://www.ird.govt.nz). For the Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC), the Cook Islands side is administered by the Revenue Management Division (RMD) of the Cook Islands Ministry of Finance and the Financial Supervisory Commission; however, all New Zealand tax registration, compliance, and indirect tax administration is exclusively handled by the IRD through myIR.

E-government filing portal

STEP 2 - Obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) through the NZBN Register (optional for non-residents, but accelerates identity verification). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (the CK_IC is tax-exempt under the Cook Islands International Companies regime, being a non-resident offshore entity), but for New Zealand tax purposes the IC is treated as Entity_Level_Taxation with Territorial_Scope: it is taxed as a separate corporate person at the 28% corporate rate under the Income Tax Act 2007 on New Zealand-source income only, with non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at 15% (or any applicable reduced rate under the limited NZ-Cook Islands arrangement) on dividends, interest, and royalties. The Cook Islands tax-exempt status does not exempt the CK_IC from New Zealand income tax, GST, NRWT, or the PE rules under s YD 4.2 of the Income Tax Act 2007; the New Zealand data center facility constitutes a fixed establishment and therefore a PE for income tax purposes.

Available election forms

New Zealand has NO check-the-box or comparable elective fiscal-transparency regime for companies. Available elections to the CK_IC include: (1) GST group registration election under ss 55-55B Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, allowing the IC and related NZ group entities to be treated as a single GST person; (2) voluntary GST registration election under s 51 before reaching the NZD 60,000 threshold, enabling immediate input tax credits on the data center construction and equipment; (3) GST ratio method / apportionment election under s 10(24C) for mixed-use or partial business use of the facility; (4) hybrid mismatch notice/election under s FH 5 Income Tax Act 2007 to adjust deductions for any cross-border hybrid instruments or reverse hybrid structures; (5) branch equivalent tax regime (BET) elections under s FC 2 for the NZ PE; (6) thin-capitalisation worldwide group debt test election under s FE 16; and (7) application for an IRD determination under s 91AAT for financial arrangement spreads. No election confers pass-through or disregarded-entity treatment; the IC remains a distinct taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in the immediately preceding 12-month period (mandatory registration); no economic nexus threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of 'remote services' (electronic and telecommunications services) to New Zealand residential consumers, with those suppliers requiring registration irrespective of turnover; however, the CK_IC supplies from a fixed establishment in New Zealand and is therefore subject to the standard NZD 60,000 registration test based on its aggregate taxable supplies in New Zealand.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under s 11A(1)(k) Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for services supplied to a person who is not resident in New Zealand and who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, PROVIDED the supply is not supplied directly in connection with (i) land situated in New Zealand or (ii) movable personal property situated in New Zealand (other than movable personal property that is subsequently exported from New Zealand), and is not a service physically performed in New Zealand by an individual present in New Zealand unless the benefit of the service is consumed entirely offshore. Application to CK_IC operations: (A) bare-metal dedicated server hosting, where the hardware is owned by the CK_IC and the offshore customer receives a remote computing service, is treated as a supply of services to a non-resident and is eligible for zero-rating; (B) rack space leasing and true colocation of customer-owned servers is generally TAXABLE at 15% because the supply is directly connected with the customer's movable personal property (the customer's servers) situated in New Zealand at the time of supply, notwithstanding the customer's non-residence; (C) remote hands, patching, and physical maintenance are taxable if performed by personnel in New Zealand; (D) power, cooling, connectivity, and cross-connect pass-through charges follow the characterisation of the principal supply and must be separately identified in the contract; (E) all contracts must contain a clear allocation between taxable and zero-rated components, and contemporaneous evidence of recipient non-residence and place of supply must be retained; failure to substantiate the zero-rating results in full 15% GST liability on reassessment. The CK_IC cannot rely on the 'exported goods' exception unless the customer's servers are physically removed from New Zealand, which must be documented through Customs export procedures.

  • Customs Export Declaration and formal border export entry evidencing removal of customer-owned server equipment from New Zealand, together with bill of lading / air waybill and packing list, where the zero-rating claim relies on the exported movable personal property exception
  • Non-resident recipient evidence pack: foreign tax registration certificate (e.g., Cook Islands RMD registration), overseas registered office certificate, director identification, passport copy, and signed non-resident declaration executed by the customer
  • Executed master service agreement / colocation contract clearly stating the recipient's non-resident status, the place of supply of each service element, and the applicable GST treatment of every charge line
  • Tax invoices issued within 28 days of the time of supply, showing 15% GST on taxable components and an explicit 'zero-rated supply to non-resident - s 11A(1)(k) GST Act 1985' legend on all zero-rated components
  • Inward foreign exchange remittance certificates, SWIFT MT103 payment advices, or bank statements demonstrating payment from an offshore bank account into the CK_IC's New Zealand operating account via Monthly ACH or SWIFT Wire
  • GST working papers and GST 3 return schedules reconciling every zero-rated invoice to the supporting evidence file and the myIR filed return
  • Where applicable, any inwards/outwards movement certificates and proof that the customer's goods were exported within the statutory timeframe of the supply
  • An internal compliance matrix mapping each service category (bare-metal hosting, colocation, remote hands, power/cooling) to its GST treatment with legal basis

No real-time fiscal clearance, no government pre-clearance API, no digital stamping, and no continuous transaction control (CTC) regime in force in New Zealand. The IRD relies on risk-based post-clearance analytics via myIR. E-invoicing is voluntary though interoperable with the Peppol network; tax invoices must be raised within 28 days of the time of supply; GST returns must be filed electronically via myIR (GST 101 / gateway) on the elected cycle (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly). Zero-rated invoices must be legended 'zero-rated supply to a non-resident' and supported by third-party evidence retained for seven years. A simplified registration and filing portal exists for non-resident suppliers of remote services to NZ consumers, but it does not apply to the CK_IC's New Zealand fixed-establishment colocation supplies.

The IRD may reassess GST within 4 years of the annual return, extended to 7 years where the return is fraudulent, wilfully misleading, or omitted (s 108 Tax Administration Act 1994). Incorrect zero-rating exposes the CK_IC to: (1) reinstatement of 15% GST on the full invoice value; (2) late payment penalties of 1% of the unpaid tax on the day after the due date, followed by 1% per month for the first month and 1.5% per month thereafter, capped at 150% of the base tax (s 139A); (3) use-of-money interest at the daily prescribed rate from the original due date until full payment; (4) shortfall penalties for lack of reasonable care (20%), unacceptable tax position (20%), and abusive tax position (100%) under ss 141A-141D; (5) criminal prosecution under s 143A for knowingly providing false GST documentation, with fines up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; (6) promoter penalties up to NZD 10,000 per false invoice; and (7) directors'/shareholders' personal liability under s 149 where the company is stripped of assets to defeat GST creditors. The Cook Islands IC's offshore tax-exempt status provides no protection against New Zealand clawback, penalties, or director liability in respect of GST fraud or negligent zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a freely convertible currency regime under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and the Foreign Exchange Control Regulations 1985. Both current account and capital account transactions are generally unrestricted, with no standing exchange control approval requirements for profit repatriation, dividend remittance, loan servicing, or capital return to non-resident shareholders. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully floatable, and banks may execute spot and forward FX transactions without central bank pre-approval. Practical hurdles to profit remittance are limited to normal tax compliance (non-resident withholding tax, thin capitalisation rules), bank-level anti-money-laundering due diligence, and accurate foreign-exchange reporting under the Statistics New Zealand balance-of-payments framework. No material convertibility risk is present for the data-centre colocation leasing scope.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand does not impose any statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement. There is no mandated timeline for repatriation, no minimum conversion ratio into NZD, and no requirement to liquidate foreign-currency export receipts with the Reserve Bank of New Zealand. Overseas receivables may be retained in foreign currency, repatriated at a time of commercial choice, or used directly for offshore working capital, subject to accounting standards, transfer-pricing rules, and the general anti-avoidance provisions of the Income Tax Act 2007. For customs and balance-of-payments statistical purposes, exporters must accurately declare the value and currency of exported services on the relevant Trade Single Window and Bank of New Zealand reporting forms, but no exchange control approval is triggered.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) have low direct onboarding viability in New Zealand because they are not licensed as registered banks or non-bank deposit takers under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989, and they must comply separately with the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. An offshore EMI may operate only through a locally registered and supervised subsidiary or a licensed New Zealand bank partner; otherwise, accepting customer deposits or offering payment services to New Zealand-resident businesses can constitute unlicensed financial service provision. For this data-centre colocation treasury blueprint, the recommended route is a fully licensed tier-1 domestic bank account supported by a dedicated treasury management system, with an EMI used only as a supplementary low-value collections gateway after obtaining confirmations of its NZ FSP registration and compliance status.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the parent or ultimate beneficial owner should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the NZ operational account with a clear payment purpose code corresponding to an equity subscription or intercompany loan, e.g., 'INRO' or a structured reference such as 'CAP-INJ-DATE-ENTITY'. The remitter must ensure that the beneficial-owner and full legal-name fields are populated consistently with bank KYC records. Where capital is in USD or another foreign currency, the receiving NZ bank will convert it to NZD at the applicable wholesale spot rate on the value date or hold it in a non-NZD operational account if approved. The bank will issue a separate remittance advice for inward foreign transfer and may request underlying board resolutions, certified incorporation documents, and the executed capital subscription agreement. Under New Zealand law, no Reserve Bank approval is required for capital injection; however, the bank must complete identity/beneficial-ownership verification before crediting proceeds. For monthly cross-border collections of colocation lease payments, the preferred inbound rail is SWIFT MT103 on a recurring cycle, with each invoice amount correlated to the lease agreement and the invoice reference embedded in the payment details to facilitate matching and prevent hidden-supplier money laundering typologies.Outbound trade settlements for colocation infrastructure and hardware purchases should be executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 single payments for vendor invoices, with a minimum-value MT103 threshold for material equipment purchases. For high-value, multi-stage procurement of server racks, UPS systems, and cooling infrastructure, a Commercial Letter of Credit is recommended: the issuing NZ bank sends SWIFT MT700 (issue) or MT710 (advice of third-party credit), and the beneficiary presents complying transport, invoice, inspection, and insurance documents under documentary terms. The treasury system should monitor MT300 series confirmations from correspondent banks to ensure value-dating accuracy. For recurring operational costs such as cloud interconnect bandwidth, energy, and facility-management fees, both NZD domestic high-value clearing (payments through the Exchange Settlement Account System) and cross-border SWIFT MT103 are appropriate. Where the vendor accepts card-based payment, the monthly ACH rail is not suitable for international disbursements; instead, a corporate commercial card or a closed-loop e-invoicing floor with settlement through a payment service provider may be used, but any payment to an unrelated non-resident vendor should default to SWIFT MT103 to maintain a full audit trail.Deliverable FX forward contracts (USD/NZD and EUR/NZD) with tenors aligned to monthly lease-invoice cycles and stated capital-repayment schedulesFX swaps for short-term rotation of currency exposures without disturbing underlying treasury exposureCross-currency interest rate swaps for long-term intercompany loan hedges where both legs carry floating ratesNon-deliverable forwards (NDF) for hedging non-NZD currencies where the underlying settlement amount cannot be delivered onshore due to counterparty constraintsVanilla FX options for contingent exposure arising from uncertain monthly trade disbursement timing, subject to bank credit approval and collateral marginFX spot contracts for same-day settlement of operational receipts and disbursements when timing exposure is immaterial

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) have low direct onboarding viability in New Zealand because they are not licensed as registered banks or non-bank deposit takers under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989, and they must comply separately with the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. An offshore EMI may operate only through a locally registered and supervised subsidiary or a licensed New Zealand bank partner; otherwise, accepting customer deposits or offering payment services to New Zealand-resident businesses can constitute unlicensed financial service provision. For this data-centre colocation treasury blueprint, the recommended route is a fully licensed tier-1 domestic bank account supported by a dedicated treasury management system, with an EMI used only as a supplementary low-value collections gateway after obtaining confirmations of its NZ FSP registration and compliance status.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% - New Zealand standard corporate income tax rate under the Income Tax Act 2007, Schedule 1, Part A. The Tax-Exempt Zone International Company (CK_IC) is taxable at 0% on non-New-Zealand-source income; any New Zealand-source branch income derived through a fixed place of business in New Zealand is taxable at the 28% rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% NRWT on unimputed dividends paid by a New Zealand-resident company to non-residents; 15% NRWT on fully imputed dividends (with supplementary dividend mechanism); 33% Resident Withholding Tax (RWT) on dividends paid to New Zealand-resident shareholders. A Tax-Exempt Zone International Company that is non-resident and pays dividends solely from non-New-Zealand-source income is generally outside New Zealand NRWT; however, if the IC is or is deemed to be New Zealand-resident, or the dividend is sourced to New Zealand, NRWT applies at the above rates.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where aggregate cross-border related-party transactions equal or exceed NZD 10 million in an income year. Master File: mandatory where the multinational group has global consolidated revenue of NZD 1 billion or more, or aggregate group related-party transactions of NZD 100 million or more. In addition, IRD expects contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for any related-party transaction with associated enterprises in low-tax jurisdictions of NZD 5 million or more.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a codified Economic Substance Regulation regime; economic substance is instead enforced through tax-residence rules (incorporation versus management and control), the transfer-pricing provisions of Part 4A of the Income Tax Act 2007 aligned with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and BEPS Actions 8-10, and the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG1 and s GA1). However, if the CK_IC is registered as a Cook Islands International Company, the Cook Islands Economic Substance Regulations 2019 apply: any relevant activity, including financing and leasing, requires the entity to be managed and directed in the Cook Islands, maintain adequate employees, premises and expenditure proportionate to the activity, and file an annual Economic Substance Declaration with the Financial Supervisory Commission. Failure breaches attract monetary penalties of up to USD 10,000 plus USD 5,000 per month of continued non-compliance, and a re-registration freeze of up to 3 years for struck-off entities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False or Misleading Returns (Tax Administration Act 1994, ss 143A, 143B, 143D, 148)

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty of 150% of the tax avoided for evasion, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 20% for an unacceptable tax position; criminal prosecution on conviction: fine of up to NZD 50,000 for a company and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years (s 143B TAA); repeat offending attracts fines of up to NZD 100,000; the tax shortfall remains payable with UOMI from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Under the director penalty regime, directors and persons concerned in the management of the company are personally liable for unpaid tax of associated or phoenix companies (s 293B TAA) and for unremitted withholding taxes (s 15 TAA); the corporate veil may be pierced for fraud or reckless trading (Companies Act 1993, ss 135, 300); directors may be disqualified for up to 5 years, subject to border stop orders and travel bans where tax debts exceed NZD 2,000 and the director attempts departure to evade payment, and liable to imprisonment for aiding tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Contemporaneous Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment to the arm's-length profit plus shortfall penalties of 50% of the resulting tax shortfall for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion; UOMI accrues on the underpayment from the original balance date; failure to maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation attracts a civil penalty of up to NZD 100,000 in addition to any shortfall penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false IR4 declarations are exposed to the evasion penalties and criminal liability described above; directors who authorise contrived pricing arrangements can be assessed under the general anti-avoidance rules in their personal capacity as parties to the arrangement; reckless trading liability under s 135 of the Companies Act 1993 may attach where the company continues trading while insolvent and owing tax, with directors personally liable for losses to creditors including the Crown.
