Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. The Companies Act 1993 does not prescribe a minimum paid-up share capital for a limited liability company, and no escrow account or deposit condition is imposed by the New Zealand Companies Office. Shares may be issued partly or fully paid; incorporation proceeds upon lodgment of the Form NZCN, submission of consents, and payment of the prescribed registration fee.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance through a Building Warrant of Fitness (BWoF) issued by the territorial authority, including approved means of escape and fire alarm system certification; structural load inspection for any mezzanine or racking under the Building Act 2004; and an occupational health and safety clearance comprising a site-specific risk assessment, first aid provisions, spill containment kits, and worker training as required by the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Tāke), New Zealand; secure online portal: myIR (myIR.ird.govt.nz) and public gateway ird.govt.nz.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity seeking a New Zealand tax identity must first determine whether it is required to register as a non-resident company. If the entity is incorporated in New Zealand, it must first obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) from the Companies Office, after which an IRD number is automatically issued for tax purposes. For a non-resident company not incorporated in New Zealand, the standard procedure is to file Form IR742 (Application for an IRD Number for a Non-Resident Company) with certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitution, director and shareholder register, and any evidence of a fixed establishment or permanent establishment in New Zealand. The same IRD number is used for income tax, GST (Goods and Services Tax), and PAYE obligations. GST registration is effected separately via myIR by completing the online GST registration application (form IR356 for residents; the non-resident remote-services variant for offshore suppliers of digital services and low-value goods). A non-resident supplier may be required to appoint a local representative or agent where Inland Revenue directs, and must provide bank account details for refund payments. The IRD number issuance is typically completed within 5 to 15 business days of receipt of a complete application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a United States-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election; corporate tax status is fixed by statute under the Income Tax Act 2007. Relevant statutory elections and elective mechanisms available to the International Company include: (i) election to be treated as a Look-Through Company (LTC) where eligibility criteria for closely held companies are satisfied; (ii) election under s CQ 2 for the foreign dividend exemption to apply to certain foreign shareholdings; (iii) controlled foreign company (CFC) elections, including the limited attribution rule election to exclude non-attributable active foreign income and the 'grey list' de minimis election; (iv) hybrid mismatch elections under Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007, including the election to align tax treatment of excess profits or losses under a hybrid financial arrangement; and (v) for the Tax-Exempt-Zone International Company, an elective position that the entity is a non-resident not carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand, thereby remaining outside the New Zealand net of income tax and GST unless statutory thresholds or permanent establishment rules are triggered.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in taxable supplies made in New Zealand during the preceding 12 months or expected to be made in the following 12 months. Non-resident suppliers of remote or digital services and of low-value goods (individual goods valued at NZD 1,000 or less) are required to register for GST if their total supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for: (i) supplies of goods exported from New Zealand by the supplier pursuant to a contract of sale, provided the goods are physically removed from New Zealand by the supplier within 28 days after the date of supply (or such longer period as approved by the Commissioner); (ii) supplies of services performed in New Zealand for a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, excluding services physically performed in New Zealand or services supplied in connection with New Zealand land or moveable personal property situated in New Zealand; and (iii) supplies of low-value goods (each item NZD 1,000 or less) sold through distance-selling channels to consumers outside New Zealand under the non-resident supplier rules. The exporter must retain sufficient evidence of actual export, non-resident recipient status, and foreign-currency settlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Transaction Entry lodged through the New Zealand Trade Single Window or Customs Connect)
  • Bill of Lading (for sea freight) or Air Waybill (for air freight) evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank foreign-currency receipt evidencing settlement of the export consideration
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract or purchase order
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (where applicable)
  • Proof of non-resident status of the recipient for zero-rated services (e.g., certificate of residence, overseas address, and evidence that the recipient is outside New Zealand when services are performed)

New Zealand has no statutory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing approval, or digital stamping mandate applicable to B2B or B2C transactions. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates under the PEPPOL interoperability framework, with central government agencies obliged to receive e-invoices via PEPPOL from April 2022. There is no requirement for API-based pre-clearance of invoices with Inland Revenue; GST returns are filed electronically through myIR, and payments are made via the IRD electronic payment system. Invoice integrity is maintained through normal commercial and record-keeping obligations under the Tax Administration Act 1994, not through a national clearinghouse.

Inland Revenue may reassess any zero-rated supply where the goods are not exported within the statutory time limits, are returned to New Zealand, or where the export documentation is false or misleading. The zero-rating is reversed and GST at 15% becomes payable on the original consideration, with use-of-money interest running from the original due date. Fraudulent or careless zero-rating claims attract civil shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for an unacceptable tax position, 100% for gross carelessness, and 150% for evasion or a tax avoidance arrangement. Late payment penalties accrue at 1% on the first day of default plus 4% on the 8th day and the 8th day of each subsequent month, capped at 10% of the unpaid amount. Criminal prosecution may be commenced for serious fraud, and directors and controlling officers may be held personally liable for penalties and unpaid GST in appropriate cases. Export substantiation records must be retained for at least 7 years, and the statutory reassessment time bar is generally 4 years (extended to 8 years where fraud or evasion is involved).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalized, free-floating exchange-rate regime with unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange-control statutes, no remittance ceilings, and no prior-approval requirements for profit repatriation, dividend distributions, royalty payments, loan servicing, or capital redemption by a non-resident International Company. The NZD floats freely against all major currencies with no managed band. Practical hurdles are limited to standard tax compliance: Non-Resident Withholding Tax on interest and dividends, CRS/FATCA self-certification, and reasonable AML/CFT source-of-funds documentation. Convertibility risk for the CK_IC online cosmetics retail structure is negligible.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement in New Zealand. Export receipts and internet-sale revenues may be retained indefinitely in the original foreign currency, held in offshore accounts, or converted at the company's commercial discretion. No mandatory conversion ratio or statutory time frame applies. Obligations are limited to: (i) accurate foreign-currency bookkeeping and tax treatment under the Income Tax Act 2007, including the foreign-currency timing and translation rules; (ii) standard AML/CFT transaction monitoring under the AMLCFT Act 2009; and (iii) statistical reporting borne by the institutional NZD counterparty, not the corporate client.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the central bank and prudential supervisor established under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and amended by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021. The RBNZ issues the New Zealand dollar, sets the Official Cash Rate, and supervises registered banks. New Zealand maintains no separate foreign-exchange control authority: the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) oversees market conduct, the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) is the AML/CFT supervisor under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) screens inbound investments under the Overseas Investment Act 2005. No currency-control or exchange-licensing regime applies to ordinary trade transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium feasibility. An offshore digital EMI may operate as an auxiliary collection or disbursement rail for low-value e-commerce flows but cannot access the New Zealand clearing infrastructure directly. Under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Disputes Resolution) Act 2008, any provider carrying on financial services in New Zealand must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register and enrol in an approved dispute-resolution scheme; payment remitters must also register as reporting entities under the AMLCFT Act 2009. A purely offshore EMI without New Zealand residence typically requires a local Tier 1 partner bank as settlement agent. The CK_IC treasury should not rely on an EMI for capital escrow, trade settlement, or FX hedging. Recommended use: high-frequency, low-value Shopify Payments and PayPal float consolidation through an EMI-backed multi-currency account, with monthly sweeps to the primary New Zealand Tier 1 account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% (New Zealand flat company income tax rate under the Income Tax Act 2007; applies to resident companies on worldwide income and to non-resident companies on New Zealand-source income).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends paid by a New Zealand resident company is 30%; the rate is reduced to 15% for fully imputed dividends satisfying standard conditions. Under most New Zealand Double Taxation Arrangements, a beneficial owner holding at least 10% of the voting power in the payer can obtain a reduced dividend rate of 5%. Distributions made by the offshore International Company itself to its ultimate shareholders are not New Zealand-source dividends and therefore attract no New Zealand NRWT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand does not codify a separate statutory 'Local File' or 'Master File' filing, but mandatory retention of transfer pricing documentation is required for cross-border related party transactions where a single transaction exceeds NZD 1,000,000; the Annual Disclosure Form is required for large multinational groups with New Zealand turnover equal to or exceeding NZD 100,000,000; Country-by-Country reporting applies to consolidated multinational groups with global revenue equal to or exceeding EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not administer separate Economic Substance Regulations in the Cayman/BVI sense. Substance is instead evaluated through the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, the corporate residence 'centre of management and control' test, permanent establishment attribution rules, and the significant purpose test in section GA 1. An International Company’s claim to a tax-exempt offshore status is rebutted where actual board meetings, key decision-making, digital supply chain management, or material personnel functions are performed in New Zealand.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent income tax filing

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the full tax shortfall plus a 150% shortfall penalty for evasion under the Tax Administration Act 1994; criminal conviction under section 143A may result in a fine up to NZD 250,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years; use-of-money interest continues to accrue from the original due date.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the International Company is a mere façade or sham; directors face civil restitution orders for diverted corporate profits, travel prohibition orders as bail conditions in criminal tax proceedings, and imprisonment of up to 7 years where convicted of tax evasion or bank account fraud.
Shell company and artificial tax-exempt structure

Statutory sanction: Application of the general anti-avoidance rule to invalidate the arrangement; recapture of the royalty deduction at the New Zealand company level; imposition of transfer pricing adjustments under ss GC 6–GC 13 of the Income Tax Act 2007; shortfall penalties of 40% for an arrangement not reasonably arguable, escalating to 150% if a sham is established.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute or certify artificial intercompany agreements may be made jointly and severally liable for the resulting unpaid corporate tax; courts may issue director disqualification orders, asset freezing orders, and travel bans while the directors remain flight risks.
Transfer pricing manipulation on DTC beauty retail margin

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may redetermine the arm's length gross margin, assess the New Zealand entity on the adjusted taxable income at 28%, and impose a 40% transfer pricing shortfall penalty for taxpayers failing to adopt a reasonably arguable position; complete evasion documentation triggers the 150% penalty scale.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structure the pricing to defeat the 28% New Zealand tax base may be held personally liable where the company is left insolvent by the artificial profit strip; insolvency-based claims may be brought by Inland Revenue on behalf of creditors, and criminal travel bans may be imposed during extradition or mutual assistance investigations.
AML and economic substance red flags in digital payment flows

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under the AML/CFT Act 2009 of up to NZD 2,000,000 for a body corporate and up to NZD 300,000 for an individual for serious reporting entity failures; failure to register as an overseas company in New Zealand may trigger daily penalty liability of NZD 10,000 per day; knowingly false director declarations attract criminal penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are exposed to criminal prosecution under the AML/CFT Act for knowingly submitting false beneficial ownership documentation; courts may impose imprisonment of up to 2 years, director prohibition orders of up to 5 years, confiscation of assets derived from concealed revenue, and departure prohibitions in respect of tax or money laundering charges.
