Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office (Registrar of Companies), part of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), Wellington, New Zealand

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand): s. 10 (formation of company), s. 12 (issue of certificate of incorporation), s. 16 (constitution may be filed), ss. 47-49 (registered office), ss. 87-89 (share allotment and share register), s. 128 (no minimum capital requirement), ss. 151-159 (directors), ss. 189-190 (registered office requirements); Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 (only if public offer); Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT obligations for designated financial services).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of a Company (online electronic form NZ1) filed via the Companies Office portal with integrated RealMe identity verification; upon approval the Registrar issues a Certificate of Incorporation.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 16 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital requirement under the Companies Act 1993; a company may be incorporated with issued shares of zero par value and no paid-up capital. No escrow account or capital deposit is required at incorporation. Directors must, however, ensure solvency and that the company will be able to meet its obligations as they fall due.

Funding & unlock path: For the proposed office premises only: (1) Fire Safety — a Fire Evacuation Scheme and/or building fire compliance certificate under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) Act 2017 and the Building Act 2004; where the leased building has a compliance schedule, the tenant must comply with the Building Warrant of Fitness (BWOF) regime and annual building inspections; (2) Structural — the council issue a Certificate of Title / Code Compliance Certificate confirming the premises meet the structural load requirements of the Building Code; (3) Workplace — a general health and safety clearance under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 (WorkSafe NZ), including a documented worker engagement plan, hazard register, and first-aid provisions; and (4) Civil defense — the premises must display the local council civil defence emergency coordination information and participate in an evacuation drill if required by the FENZ evacuation scheme.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital requirement under the Companies Act 1993; a company may be incorporated with issued shares of zero par value and no paid-up capital. No escrow account or capital deposit is required at incorporation. Directors must, however, ensure solvency and that the company will be able to meet its obligations as they fall due.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Tauhake) of New Zealand; operational portal: myIR at www.ird.govt.nz; customs administration for export documentation: New Zealand Customs Service (Te Mana Ārai o Aotearoa).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-incorporated International Company (CK_IC) seeking registration in New Zealand: (1) Determine whether the entity has a sufficient nexus with New Zealand, such as carrying on a taxable activity, having a permanent establishment, supplying goods or services in New Zealand, or incurring employer obligations; (2) Apply for an IRD number using the appropriate registration pathway, normally through the myIR portal or the IR742 application for non-resident companies where the entity is not incorporated under the Companies Act 1993; if the entity establishes a New Zealand branch, register the overseas company with the New Zealand Companies Office and then apply for the IRD number; (3) If the entity makes or intends to make taxable supplies in New Zealand, register for GST through myIR using the IR303 form when total taxable supplies in any 12-month period is NZD 60,000 or more, or when voluntarily registering as a non-resident supplier of remote services; (4) Present the IRD number certificate, certificate of incorporation, and proof of director identity to the New Zealand bank when opening an account supporting bank wire and credit card payment rails; (5) Maintain a New Zealand address for service and appoint a local agent when required; (6) File GST returns in the assigned filing frequency (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly) and maintain the statutory export evidence files for any zero-rated supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Applicable statutory mechanisms include: (a) look-through company (LTC) election under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 for closely held New Zealand companies, not available to a non-resident international company structure; (b) controlled foreign company (CFC) attribution and branch-equivalent elections under Subparts CQ and EX for New Zealand residents with interests in foreign companies; (c) GST grouping election under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1986; (d) hybrid mismatch neutralisation rules under Subpart FH and Subpart GB intended to prevent double non-taxation through entity/hybrid mismatches; (e) unilateral tax exemption or double tax agreement relief for offshore sourced income of non-resident companies where the entity does not have a New Zealand permanent establishment; (f) consumption tax registration elections for non-resident suppliers under the remote services regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (compulsory registration is triggered when total taxable supplies in any 12-month period exceed NZD 60,000; non-resident suppliers of remote services are subject to the same threshold and, where applicable, must register and charge GST on supplies to New Zealand consumers, with reverse-charge or platform-collection obligations for certain B2B and B2C digital supplies)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST is available where the supplier can substantiate the supply under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1986. For goods: the supplier must export the goods outside New Zealand within 28 days of supply (or within an approved longer period) and retain transport documentation. For services, including medical device FDA 510(k) and CE Mark regulatory consulting: the supply must be supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, or the services must relate to intellectual property, know-how, or data supplied to a non-resident recipient that is not registered and not liable to be registered for GST in New Zealand, and the supply must not be directly connected with land or goods situated in New Zealand. Additional eligibility criteria include: the services benefit a taxable activity carried on outside New Zealand by the recipient; the recipient provides a valid overseas business address; the contract contains a zero-rating representation clause; and the supplier holds contemporaneous evidence of payment in foreign currency and the professional nature of the regulatory consulting engagement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export entry lodged with the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/telex confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or invoice-level pre-approval API regime exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates under the New Zealand e-Invoice framework using the Peppol BIS 3.0 standard, with mandatory adoption only for government-agency receivables through accredited Peppol access points. GST returns (IR303) are filed electronically via myIR or certified third-party software. There is no national two-way API clearance obligation for commercial invoices.

Inland Revenue may audit zero-rated export supplies and retrospectively claw back any GST refunded where the documentary evidence is insufficient, the goods are not exported within the statutory time frame, or the services are found to be supplied to or consumed by a person in New Zealand. Reassessments may extend up to four years, and longer where evasion is established. Use-of-money interest runs from the original due date. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply at tiered rates: 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, and up to 100% for evasion or a similar act, while dishonest or fraudulent behaviour may attract additional monetary penalties. Criminal conviction for knowingly zero-rating taxable supplies without lawful entitlement can result in imprisonment of up to five years and a fine not exceeding NZD 50,000. The GST-registered supplier remains jointly and severally liable for the clawback and penalties irrespective of any recovery action taken against the overseas recipient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The NZD is fully convertible on both current and capital account for residents and non-residents. There are no restrictions on the purchase, sale, or transfer of foreign currency, no approval requirements for dividends, management fees, royalties, loan repayments, or sale proceeds, and no differentiated treatment for offshore or tax-exempt entities. The only frictions are (i) the bank's AML/CFT due diligence before executing large transfers, (ii) statistical reporting of international transactions via the bank's International Funds Transfer Instruction (IFTI) when the transaction is over NZD 10,000, and (iii) non-resident withholding tax on any NZ-source dividends or interest. MedTech consulting fees earned from foreign clients are not NZ-source income and can be accumulated offshore or repatriated freely.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand does not impose any export-proceeds surrender obligation. Since the deregulation of the financial system in 1984, no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation quota applies to export receipts or service fees. An exporting or consulting company may keep foreign currency proceeds in overseas accounts, convert at a time of its choosing, or settle forward contracts without regulatory consent. The sole administrative duties are that the bank retain underlying invoices/contracts for at least 7 years, and that any individual cross-border transfer exceeding NZD 10,000 be recorded in the RBNZ's IFTI reporting return.

Central bank supervision: The Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) is the central bank and sole monetary authority in New Zealand; it exercises prudential regulation over all registered banks and administers the International Transaction Reporting System (ITRS). The Financial Markets Authority (FMA) supervises AML/CFT compliance for non-bank financial institutions and financial advisers, while the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) under Land Information New Zealand screens only 'sensitive' land or significant business-asset acquisitions, which are not engaged for an advisory-only International Company. There is no separate foreign-exchange control authority; exchange controls were abolished in 1984.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for inbound multi-currency collection (USD/EUR/GBP) and outbound disbursements, but Low for primary settlement. Global EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, and Tazapay are not registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and may not accept NZD deposits from the public; they operate under the Financial Service Providers Act 2008 (FSPR registration) or hold an Australian AFSL/ACL and provide local-account virtual numbers through partner banks. Legally, the IC remains the sole reporting entity under the AML/CFT Act 2009 for its own transactions even where the EMI provides the rail, and the EMI is entitled to perform its own KYC, sanctions screening, and transaction monitoring at account-opening. For a Low-Risk Advisory entity, an EMI is viable as a secondary collection-and-disbursement channel, but the tier-1 NZ bank must remain the treasury anchor to preserve clean SWIFT BIC/IBAN routing and creditworthiness.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 from the parent company's bank to the IC's operating account at the NZ tier-1 bank. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must cite 'Capital contribution to International Company [IC registration number]' with purpose code 'EQ – Equity Capital' in the bank's IFTI classification so the RBNZ can classify the flow without further inquiry. Supporting documents must be pre-submitted before the wire: (i) board resolution of the IC approving the equity subscription, (ii) certificate of good standing of the IC, (iii) passport copies of the contributing beneficial owner, and (iv) source-of-funds statements from the remitting bank. If the liquidity event is a shareholder loan, use purpose code 'LO – Intercompany Loan' and provide a signed loan agreement with an interest-rate schedule and an intercompany balance sheet. No Overseas Investment Office consent is required for a service company with no sensitive land and business assets below NZD 100 million; the only statutory step is the bank's statistical IFTI return.For service-related disbursements (payments to FDA consultants, EU Notified Bodies, legal counsel, and subcontractors), the standard clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) because it provides a complete audit trail and is accepted by all financial institutions globally. A commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700) is permissible for substantial project-specific purchases but is uncommon for consulting services; a standby LC is preferable for securing performance obligations. Inbound client origination fees from medical-device sponsors are collected by direct card acquiring through the tier-1 bank's merchant facility (Visa/Mastercard clearing settlement into the IC's operational account T+1) or by SWIFT/ACH/SEPA transfer converted into NZD using a spot contract. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% (New Zealand resident corporate tax rate); nil effective rate on foreign-sourced income of a qualifying non-resident International Company in the tax-exempt zone.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% statutory New Zealand non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on non-imputed dividends to non-residents; 15% NRWT for fully imputed dividends; nil for dividends paid from a qualifying International Company out of exempt foreign-sourced income with no New Zealand source connection.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 1,000,000 aggregate per category of cross-border related-party transactions per income year. New Zealand does not prescribe mandatory OECD Master File / Local File filings, but contemporaneous OECD-aligned transfer pricing documentation is expected when the threshold is exceeded, and Inland Revenue may require disclosure through the IR371 International Questionnaire.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime, but applies the general anti-avoidance rule in the Income Tax Act 2007, OECD BEPS substance requirements, and permanent establishment principles. A tax-exempt International Company must be genuinely managed and controlled outside New Zealand and must maintain real economic substance in its jurisdiction of incorporation, including physical office, qualified staff, and substantive board decision-making. If the entity is a pure letter-box used solely to convert New Zealand advisory fees into exempt foreign income, Inland Revenue may treat the business as having a New Zealand PE or reclassify the company as New Zealand tax-resident.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Understatement of New Zealand-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue will reassess the omitted income, charge use-of-money interest, impose a shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax, and prosecute under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Conviction may result in a fine up to NZD 50,000 for a first offence and up to NZD 100,000 for a subsequent offence, in addition to imprisonment for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in evasion may be prosecuted as principals, be jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax shortfall under asset recovery powers, be subject to director disqualification for up to five years, and face bail conditions or court-ordered travel bans pending trial. Restraint orders may be made under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the entity as a New Zealand tax resident or as having a New Zealand permanent establishment, resulting in full taxation of worldwide income at 28%. Shortfall penalties of 20% for an unacceptable tax position or 100% for an abusive tax position may be imposed, and the International Company registration may be cancelled by the Registrar for failure to maintain qualifying exempt-zone status.

Director personal liability: Directors who structure a sham or engage in artificial tax avoidance can be personally assessed under the Companies Act 1993 for losses caused to creditors, subject to freezing orders, disqualified from managing companies, and prevented from leaving New Zealand where criminal proceedings or asset-recovery applications are pending.
AML/CFT Deficiencies on Bank Wire and Credit Card Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, civil penalties may reach NZD 2,000,000 for a body corporate and NZD 400,000 for an individual. Money laundering or terrorist financing conduct may be prosecuted under the Crimes Act 1961, carrying imprisonment for up to seven years, asset forfeiture, and confiscation of consulting fees.

Director personal liability: A director who consents to or knowingly participates in AML/CFT failures is separately liable to civil penalties and criminal prosecution. Courts may impose banning orders preventing the director from managing reporting entities, order restitution of laundered funds, and impose restrictions on international travel pending forfeiture proceedings.
False Regulatory Representation – FDA 510(k) and CE Mark Compliance Fraud

Statutory sanction: Sanctions include enforcement under the New Zealand Fair Trading Act 1986, with fines up to NZD 200,000 for individuals and NZD 600,000 for bodies corporate; FDA civil monetary penalties for false 510(k) submissions under 21 CFR; and penalties for fraudulent CE marking under applicable EU medical device regulation in the destination market. Fraud charges may also be prosecuted under the Crimes Act 1961.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations of conformity or knowingly supervise the issuance of fraudulent certificates face personal criminal liability, consumer-protection disqualification orders, restitution orders to the affected medical device clients, imprisonment, and travel bans where extradition or cross-border investigative proceedings arise.
