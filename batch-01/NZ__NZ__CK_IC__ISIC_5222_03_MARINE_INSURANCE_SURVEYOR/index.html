Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand, operating through the MyIR online portal and the IRD business.govt.nz services; customs and excise matters are administered by the New Zealand Customs Service with GST on imports collected at the border.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity engaging in a taxable activity in New Zealand must register for New Zealand GST and obtain an IRD number by filing the IR596 Application for IRD number for non-resident companies and the GST registration application (IR421) through the Inland Revenue Department. For non-resident companies without a physical New Zealand presence, registration may be completed via an approved tax agent or through the MyIR portal with certified entity documents, proof of foreign tax residency, and a New Zealand appointed agent where required. Companies incorporated in New Zealand receive an IRD number upon incorporation through the New Zealand Companies Office; for foreign-incorporated entities deriving New Zealand-sourced income or making supplies connected with New Zealand, a separate application to IRD is mandatory before commencing taxable activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not maintain a check-the-box classification regime analogous to the United States; however, a non-resident company may be treated as tax resident under the New Zealand domestic rules if central management and control is exercised in New Zealand (with applicable double tax agreement tie-breakers) and may elect to become a wholly-owned group company for GST grouping purposes under Section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Hybrid mismatch rules under Subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 apply to structured arrangements, but there is no elective hybrid entity classification. For qualifying foreign superannuation schemes and certain foreign investment vehicles, specific elective rules may apply under the Income Tax Act 2007.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Registration is compulsory if the supplier makes taxable supplies in New Zealand exceeding NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, or if supplies in the next 12 months are expected to exceed NZD 60,000; non-resident suppliers must register for GST if they supply remote services to New Zealand resident consumers in excess of the threshold, and non-resident businesses are generally required to register for GST on all taxable supplies regardless of turnover when they are carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand, except for non-resident suppliers of low-value cross-border services who may use the simplified registration and return regime without a physical presence threshold of no more than NZD 60,000 in respect of those services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of goods from New Zealand where the supplier has shipped the goods out of New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply (or the supplier holds evidence of the removal from New Zealand), including direct exports, goods exported as part of passenger baggage, and services supplied in relation to exported goods. Offshore supplies of services to non-residents are zero-rated where the services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed and the services are not supplied in connection with land or moveable property in New Zealand, and not supplied to a person in New Zealand for a taxable activity. Additionally, certain services physically performed outside New Zealand and intellectual property rights supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated under Section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, subject to specific anti-avoidance rules. Marine insurance surveying services performed in respect of goods to be exported, or performed outside New Zealand, may qualify if the recipient is a non-resident and not in New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are not supplied in connection with taxable property in New Zealand; vessel hull and cargo surveyors should confirm they are not arranging or facilitating insurance as a separate taxable supply.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Export Entry (Export Declaration) and approved electronic clearance via Trade Single Window (Tradex)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing actual removal of goods from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice issued by the supplier to the overseas customer showing zero-rated supply
  • Certificate of insurance declaration / marine survey report and contract evidencing the supply to the non-resident recipient
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/PayPal/Wise statements evidencing receipt of foreign currency consideration for export sales

New Zealand has no government-mandated real-time e-invoicing clearance or continuous transaction controls for domestic B2B GST; the primary mandate is the requirement to issue a valid tax invoice (in paper or electronic form) within 28 days of a request, meeting prescribed content requirements under Section 24 of the GST Act, and to use the New Zealand Peppol network if supplying government entities that require e-invoicing. The Inland Revenue Department accepts standard e-invoicing formats and has no digital stamping or real-time fiscal API pre-clearance requirement; however, electronic records and invoices must be retained for seven years and be capable of being produced in a readable format on IRD demand. GST returns are filed electronically via MyIR.

Under Section 108 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, the Commissioner of Inland Revenue may adjust zero-rated treatment if the exporter fails to evidence actual exportation within the required timeframe, resulting in an assessment of GST at 15% plus use-of-money interest from the original due date. Knowingly or recklessly providing false or misleading information to claim zero-rating constitutes an offence under the Tax Administration Act 1994, with penalties up to NZD 10,000 for individuals and NZD 50,000 for companies for a first offence, and up to NZD 50,000 for individuals and NZD 100,000 for subsequent offences; in addition, an evasions shortfall penalty of 150% of the tax shortfall may apply. The IRD has a dedicated international and high-wealth audit programme and may exchange information under New Zealand's tax treaties and the Multilateral Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance in Tax Matters; retrospective clawback of input credits and penalties of 20% or 40% for not taking reasonable care or taking an unacceptable tax position apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible under IMF Article VIII obligations. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted for resident and non-resident entities. A Tax-Exempt-Zone International Company may convert NZD or foreign currency without any RBNZ approval, and may repatriate dividends, interest, loan repayments, service fees and liquidation proceeds without an exchange-control licence. The only practical hurdles are fiscal and reporting in nature: non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at the treaty rate of 15% under the NZ–Cook Islands Double Taxation Agreement, thin-capitalisation limits on shareholder debt, CRS/AEOI disclosure of ultimate beneficial owners, and arm's-length transfer-pricing expectations of Inland Revenue. No balance-of-payments approval is required for profit remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender or conversion obligation on export proceeds. The Foreign Exchange Control Regulations were revoked by the Foreign Exchange Control (Revocation) Order 1984, and no timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. A marine insurance surveyor providing vessel-hull and cargo-damage claims inspection services to overseas underwriters may lawfully hold export proceeds indefinitely in a foreign-currency account at a registered New Zealand bank or repatriate them to NZD via the normal interbank market. For statistical coding, the Surveyor should report the underlying service export to Statistics New Zealand if annual receipts exceed the institutional threshold (NZD 1 million), but no exchange-control permission is triggered.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand – Te Pūtea Matua; sole monetary, prudential and financial-system stability authority. There is no separate foreign exchange administration body; exchange controls were fully liberalised in 1984. The Overseas Investment Act 2005 applies only to sensitive land, fishing quota and material assets, not to ordinary FX conversion, corporate treasury operations or service-export proceeds of a marine insurance surveying company.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

CONDITIONAL-MEDIUM. Wise is the designated fintech corridor (target_payment_rails: Underwriter_Wire, Wise). Wise NZ operates as a registered Financial Service Provider under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and is licensed under the NZ/Australian e-money regime, safeguarding client funds with tier-1 custodian banks; however, Wise is not a registered bank under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and cannot provide capital escrow custody or regulated term lending. The legal constraint is that an International Company classified as Tax-Exempt-Zone with non-resident beneficial owners may be declined by Wise's proprietary risk engine if any controller is tax-resident in a FATF greylisted or monitored jurisdiction. Therefore, Wise is legally viable only for low-value operational remittances (maximum NZD 50,000 per transaction and cumulative NZD 250,000 per month); all primary treasury, premium pass-through and claims-settlement flows must be cleared through a locally incorporated registered bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the NZ settlement account are executed through a SWIFT MT103 payment initiated from the shareholder's corporate bank to the beneficiary tier-1 bank's BIC. New Zealand operates a purpose-code-free FX regime; however, the originator must embed ISO 20022/TSF remittance information reading 'Capital injection – share subscription' and the New Zealand Companies Office registration number. The beneficiary bank performs inbound AML and sanctions screening; funds are credited T+0 in NZD via the settlement-account relationship, with real-time availability through the ESAS clearing system. Supporting documentation for the bank's regulatory pack: board resolution, share-allotment form, certificate of compliance, and a signed statement confirming no cash or unregulated-electronic-money rails were used. There is no RBNZ limit on inbound non-resident capital for a marine survey service company, but inflows exceeding NZD 10 million require balance-of-payments statistical coding and may trigger Overseas Investment Act screening if any asset acquired is 'sensitive'.Outbound professional-fee remittances and claims pass-through settlements are cleared over SWIFT MT103 with Global Payments Innovation (gpi) tracking, using the NZ registered bank's correspondent network (USD via CHIPS/Fedwire, GBP via CHAPS, EUR via TARGET2). For underwriters requiring documentary security, a commercial letter of credit (UCP600) may be issued by an NZ registered bank on presentation of the signed survey contract, survey report and commercial invoice; the beneficiary's confirming bank then negotiates against conforming documents. Domestic NZD payments to local insurers or agents settle through the Reserve Bank's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) in real time. Foreign-currency outflows are netted through Nostro accounts with end-of-day reconciliation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold for maintaining a Local File or Master File. However, the IRD Transfer Pricing Guidelines expect contemporaneous documentation for cross-border related-party transactions with an aggregate annual value exceeding NZD 3 million. For Country-by-Country reporting, a multinational group with consolidated revenue of NZD 1.14 billion or more must file a CbC report.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as seen in the GCC, but the general anti-avoidance rule (BG1 ITA 2007) invalidates arrangements lacking commercial substance. For a marine insurance surveying company, maintaining physical presence, qualified staff, and board control in NZ is essential to support tax residency or non-residency claims. Inland Revenue may also apply the delegated profit attribution rules to foreign-owned entities that lack substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion or Deliberate Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994, s 143B – fine up to NZD 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil shortfall penalty up to 150% of the tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false declarations or direct the evasion are personally liable for civil penalties and may face imprisonment; travel bans and disallowance under the Companies Act 1993 can also be imposed.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: IRD may adjust the taxable income and impose a shortfall penalty of 20% or 40% depending on whether the taxpayer failed to take reasonable care or used an unacceptable interpretation. Re-computation may trigger additional NRWT on deemed dividends.

Director personal liability: Directors who were party to the arrangement may be jointly and severally liable when they authorised the incorrect pricing and failed to ensure proper records; legal professional privilege is not available in tax fraud cases.
Failure to Withhold and File Dividend/Interest/Royalty Payments

Statutory sanction: NRWT under-assessment plus use-of-money interest and late payment penalties (1% per month). A punitive shortfall penalty of 100% of the unpaid NRWT may be applied for total absence of reasonable care.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable to the amount of the unremitted NRWT if the company cannot pay and the failure was knowingly allowed (Tax Administration Act 1994, s 136B). Overdue taxes may lead to a travel ban and statutory civil debt remedies.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may disregard the arrangement via the general anti-avoidance rule, re-assessing all income as NZ-source and imposing shortfall penalties (up to 150%). The company may also be struck off the Companies Register, resulting in the loss of the marine insurance surveying statutory licence.

Director personal liability: Directors who permit the company to trade without genuine substance may be liable for wrongful trading under the Companies Act 1993 (s 135); where the company is used as a façade to evade tax, courts may pierce the corporate veil, making directors personally liable for the full tax debt and subject to comparison orders.
AML/CFT and Statutory Licensing Breach

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed operation under the Insurance (Prudential Supervision) Act 2010 attracts a fine of up to NZD 1 million and an injunction to cease trading. Breach of the AML/CFT Act 2009 may result in fines up to NZD 2 million for an individual and NZD 5 million for a company; continual non-compliance can also result in criminal conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly consent or connive in the breach face personal criminal liability under the AML/CFT Act; the court may also impose a management ban of up to 10 years. Travel bans may be issued to prevent forum flight.
