Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, an operational business unit within the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), operating the New Zealand Companies Register and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) registry.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No 105), principally sections 12 (registration), 14 (constitution), 15 (separate legal entity), 17-19 (incorporation procedure), 20-26 (company name), 30-33 (registered office and address for service), 46-59 (share issues), 128-151 (share capital and distributions), 152-195 (directors, duties, and governance), and the Financial Reporting Act 2013 (financial statement and audit obligations).

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NZCO1 / Form 1: Application for Registration of a Company under section 18 of the Companies Act 1993, filed via the online Companies Register portal (https://www.companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 42 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a company limited by shares under the Companies Act 1993 (ss 46, 128). Shares may be issued at a price determined by the subscriber or board, including for nominal consideration (e.g., NZD 1 per share); no government escrow or impoundment account is required for share capital. If the lease transaction requires rental security, pre-payment, or debt-service reserves, the parties may contractually establish an escrow account with a licensed NZ bank, but this is a commercial arrangement, not a statutory condition of incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate and Building Code compliance: the registered office and any ancillary premises must hold an annual Fire Safety Certificate for means of escape, alarms, and extinguishing systems issued under the Building Act 2004 (clauses 3.11-3.13, Building Code) and certified by accredited fire safety engineers. Structural load inspection is not required for ordinary occupancy of a leased office, but any hangar or engine storage structure must have a structural engineer's certificate and a building warrant of fitness. Occupational clearance: the office must comply with the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015; the Company must implement a general risk and workplace management programme under the Health and Safety at Work (General Risk and Workplace Management) Regulations 2016, including hazard registers, first aid, and emergency evacuation plans. No hazardous industrial licence is required for a pure head-office lessor.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a company limited by shares under the Companies Act 1993 (ss 46, 128). Shares may be issued at a price determined by the subscriber or board, including for nominal consideration (e.g., NZD 1 per share); no government escrow or impoundment account is required for share capital. If the lease transaction requires rental security, pre-payment, or debt-service reserves, the parties may contractually establish an escrow account with a licensed NZ bank, but this is a commercial arrangement, not a statutory condition of incorporation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (Inland Revenue Department / Te Tari Taake), Wellington, New Zealand; taxpayer portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz; customs and export documentation are administered separately by the New Zealand Customs Service (Trade Single Window / SWIFT)

E-government filing portal

The Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC) is a non-resident foreign company for New Zealand tax purposes and is not required to obtain an IRD number unless it derives New Zealand-sourced income, has a fixed establishment in New Zealand, or voluntarily registers for GST to claim input credits or to zero-rate cross-border supplies. Procedure: (1) File Form IR742 'Application for IRD Number for a Non-Resident Company' with Inland Revenue; (2) attach certified copies of the Cook Islands Certificate of Incorporation, the constitution/articles of association, the register of directors, the ultimate beneficial ownership declarations under the AML/CFT rules, and the aircraft engine dry lease agreement; (3) simultaneously submit a GST registration application (Form GST1) if net annual taxable turnover exceeds NZD 60,000 or if voluntary registration is sought; (4) upon approval, Inland Revenue issues an 8-9 digit IRD number by letter confirming the registration; (5) a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) may be obtained separately from the Companies Office only if the IC establishes a place of business or registers a branch in New Zealand. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope (with the Cook Islands IC noted as a Tax_Exempt_Zone entity in its domestic jurisdiction; however, under the Income Tax Act 2007 the CK_IC is treated as a non-resident entity-level taxpayer subject to New Zealand income tax only on New Zealand-sourced income. Available election forms

New Zealand has no 'check-the-box' or equivalent entity classification election; entity status is statutory and non-elective. Available elections relevant to a non-resident aviation lessor include: (1) voluntary registration for GST (Section 51 GST Act 1985) to enable zero-rating of export supplies, notwithstanding that the lessor is non-resident; (2) GST group registration election under Section 55 of the GST Act 1985 if a fixed establishment and closely-related entities exist in New Zealand; (3) election to be treated as having a taxable activity in New Zealand solely for the purpose of the reverse charge rules on imported services; (4) for NZ-resident counterparties, hybrid mismatch rules under Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007 apply and cannot be elected away; the CK_IC should obtain a binding product ruling from Inland Revenue in respect of the zero-rating of engine leases and the absence of a New Zealand permanent establishment

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (annual turnover from taxable supplies made in New Zealand over any 12-month period); NZD 130,000 for non-profit bodies; non-resident suppliers of 'remote services' or intangibles are subject to the same NZD 60,000 threshold on their New Zealand-resident customers; for the CK_IC, cross-border dry leases of aircraft engines located outside New Zealand are generally outside the threshold because the supply is zero-rated export supply, but voluntary registration is commonly adopted to maintain clean invoicing and refund of GST on any New Zealand-sourced expenses (e.g., storage, insurance, legal)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Section 11(1)(b) of the GST Act 1985, services (including a dry lease of an aircraft engine) are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not supplied directly in connection with land in New Zealand, not physically performed in New Zealand in circumstances where the recipient is in New Zealand, and not domestic transport or telecommunications services; additionally, Section 11(1)(a) zero-rates exported goods where the engine is physically removed from New Zealand within 28 days of supply. For the CK_IC structure, the dry lease is zero-rated when: (i) the lessee is a non-resident airline or operator with no fixed establishment in New Zealand; (ii) the engine is physically located and operated wholly outside New Zealand during the lease term; (iii) the contract is a true dry lease (no crew, no operating services performed in New Zealand); and (iv) the lessors' New Zealand presence does not constitute a fixed establishment. If the engine is ever imported temporarily into New Zealand for heavy maintenance or storage, the zero-rating may be lost and import GST charged, unless the temporary import bond regime applies.

  • New Zealand Customs Export Entry (Export Declaration) filed through the Trade Single Window / SWIFT portal, where the engine physically departs New Zealand
  • Original or certified clean Bill of Lading / Master Air Waybill evidencing consignment of the aircraft engine to a foreign lessee outside New Zealand
  • Commercial Invoice / GST Tax Invoice annotated 'Zero-Rated (0% GST)' and bearing the CK_IC's New Zealand GST registration number
  • Aircraft Engine Dry Lease Agreement certifying the engine serial number, lessee's offshore address, lease term, and covenant that the engine will not be operated in New Zealand airspace
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a registered New Zealand bank evidencing receipt of lease rental proceeds in foreign currency from an overseas payer
  • Engine handover and redelivery certificates, maintenance logbooks, and GPS tracking reports demonstrating continuous physical location of the engine outside New Zealand

No real-time fiscal clearance, no API e-invoicing clearance, and no digital stamping regime exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 network (National e-Invoicing Solution); in any event, a valid GST tax invoice must contain: the words 'Tax Invoice', the supplier's name, address and GST number, the recipient's name, a sequential invoice number, invoice date, description and quantity of the supply, consideration, and the GST content. For zero-rated export supplies, the invoice must be endorsed with the statement 'Zero-Rated (0% GST)' and the underlying export evidence retained. New Zealand Customs Service operates a real-time export declaration platform (Trade Single Window) for physical exports, separate from GST filing via myIR.

The zero-rating of aircraft engine lease income is subject to audit scrutiny by Inland Revenue and the New Zealand Customs Service. If the CK_IC cannot substantiate the offshore location of the engine and the non-resident status of the lessee, Inland Revenue may assess GST at 15% on the full lease consideration, effectively clawing back the zero-rating with retrospective effect. Penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 include: shortfall penalties for not taking reasonable care (20% of the GST shortfall), unacceptable tax positions (20% or 40%), abusive tax positions (100%), evasion (150%), and goods and services tax evasion criminal prosecutions; use-of-money interest accrues on unpaid GST from the date the return was due. Additionally, if an engine that was exported under Section 11(1)(a) is subsequently re-imported to New Zealand for domestic use without an exemption, import GST becomes payable at the border under Section 12(2) of the GST Act 1985. For false or fraudulent export documentation, the Export (Customs) Rules and Crimes Act 1961 expose the directors and the CK_IC to fines up to NZD 50,000 per offence plus imprisonment; Inland Revenue also retains the power to cancel GST registration and deny refunds where the leasing activity lacks economic substance in New Zealand.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised foreign exchange regime for both current and capital account transactions. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible, and there are no general exchange control laws restricting the transfer of funds into or out of New Zealand, nor any requirement for prior central bank approval for cross-border payments related to aircraft engine dry-leasing or associated trade settlements. Profit remittances are permitted to non-residents, subject to applicable non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) and, where applicable, approved issuer levy (AIL), but no fungibility restriction applies to the underlying conversion or transfer. The exchange control framework is consistent with the IMF's Article VIII obligations; New Zealand accepts convertibility obligations for current international transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or surrender obligations for export receipts under New Zealand law. Export proceeds derived from dry-leasing income, including lease rentals and related fees invoiced in foreign currencies, may be retained in any designated foreign-currency account or converted to NZD at the exporter's discretion. The absence of export surrender rules also applies to lease payments routed to a New Zealand corporate account from an offshore trustee or lessee, provided the funds are of legitimate origin and comply with the AML/CFT Act 2009 and sanction-screening obligations of the receiving bank.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand / Te Pūtea Matua

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with strict limitations. New Zealand does not operate a general Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime akin to the EU's EMD2. Non-bank fintech providers wishing to hold or transmit client funds must either be incorporated as registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and Banking Prudential Supervision Act 1989, or operate under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 without acquiring a full banking licence. In practice, NZ-registered non-bank payment providers are limited to payment facilitation and cannot offer safeguarded EMI accounts for commercial treasury clients without a bank partner. Offshore digital EMIs may be used as auxiliary payment corridors provided they have valid AML/CFT registrations in their home jurisdictions and enter into back-to-back arrangement with a New Zealand registered bank; however, most domestic tier-1 banks will not treat offshore EMI balances as qualifying operational liquidity for the aircraft engine leasing business. The New Zealand bank account remains the mandatory primary treasury rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed via SWIFT wire (MT103) to the New Zealand corporate operating account or a dedicated capital escrow account designated for the aircraft engine acquisition. Compliant protocol: (1) instruct the remitting bank to include a clear purpose of payment code relevant to the transaction; for payment of engine lease consideration or financing purposes New Zealand banks generally accept the generic code 'INVCAP' or the ISO 20022 structured category 'Investment Capital'; for trade-related capital injection the remitter should use a purpose narrative stating 'inbound capital injection for acquisition of aircraft engine for operational dry-lease', 'equity contribution', or 'shareholder subordinated loan', as applicable. (2) The remitting bank must include the beneficiary's account number, SWIFT/BIC of the New Zealand bank, and the full legal name and address of the International Company; a remittance slip should be delivered electronically to the receiving relationship manager. (3) The NZ bank may request the underlying loan or equity documentation, a certified beneficiary ownership chart, and confirmation that the transaction is not subject to the Overseas Investment Act 2005 when the asset is not 'sensitive land' or a 'significant business asset' exceeding applicable thresholds; for aircraft engine dry-leasing with no New Zealand land interest and a leasehold value below the statutory threshold, a general legal certificate or self-certification is sufficient. (4) The bank will screen the inbound sender against sanctions lists and verify that the source-of-funds file matches the wire amount and stated purpose; any discrepancy triggers a hold. (5) Once cleared, the funds will be credited in the original foreign currency or converted to NZD at market rate. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with strict limitations. New Zealand does not operate a general Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime akin to the EU's EMD2. Non-bank fintech providers wishing to hold or transmit client funds must either be incorporated as registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and Banking Prudential Supervision Act 1989, or operate under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 without acquiring a full banking licence. In practice, NZ-registered non-bank payment providers are limited to payment facilitation and cannot offer safeguarded EMI accounts for commercial treasury clients without a bank partner. Offshore digital EMIs may be used as auxiliary payment corridors provided they have valid AML/CFT registrations in their home jurisdictions and enter into back-to-back arrangement with a New Zealand registered bank; however, most domestic tier-1 banks will not treat offshore EMI balances as qualifying operational liquidity for the aircraft engine leasing business. The New Zealand bank account remains the mandatory primary treasury rail.
