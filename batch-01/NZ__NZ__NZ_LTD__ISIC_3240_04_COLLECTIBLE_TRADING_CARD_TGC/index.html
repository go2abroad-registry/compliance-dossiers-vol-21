Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the Companies Act 1993; a company may be incorporated with fully paid, partly paid, or redeemable shares as determined by the board. No escrow or deposit account requirement exists for ordinary incorporation in New Zealand. For practical bank account opening and commercial credibility, a nominal paid-up capital of NZ$1,000 is commonly subscribed, represented by, for example, 1,000 ordinary shares issued at NZ$1.00 each.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Tauhokohau), New Zealand; online portal myIR at www.ird.govt.nz. Customs border enforcement is separately administered by the New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS) through the Trade Single Window (TSW).

E-government filing portal

A foreign or non-resident company obtains a statutory New Zealand tax file number (IRD number) by filing the 'Non-resident company registration' application (Form IR 596) or by registering online through myIR. Required documentation includes: certified certificate of incorporation and constitution of the foreign company; complete particulars of all directors, shareholders and ultimate beneficial owners; and the appointment of a New Zealand resident agent for service of notices under section 34H of the Tax Administration Act 1994 where the company has no New Zealand place of business. A New Zealand-incorporated Limited Company (NZ_LTD) is automatically issued an IRD number at incorporation through the Companies Office integration with Inland Revenue. GST registration, PAYE registration and fringe benefit tax registration are completed separately through myIR after the IRD number is issued. Standard processing time is 8–10 working days for complete applications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A New Zealand incorporated Limited Company is a standalone taxpayer subject to the New Zealand company income tax rate of 28%. Resident companies are taxed on worldwide income with a full imputation credit account system (dividend imputation); non-resident companies are taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income. Territorial_Scope: tax is imposed on an accrual basis for residents and source basis for non-residents; no territorial exemption regime applies. Tax_Exempt_Zone: none — New Zealand operates no income-tax-free trade zones or general tax exemption zones.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a United States-style check-the-box entity classification election. Available statutory elections include: (i) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under Subpart HG of the Income Tax Act 2007 for closely held companies with five or fewer qualifying owners, converting entity-level taxation to a pass-through model (not available to a widely held NZ_LTD); (ii) election to maintain an imputation credit account (ICA) — mandatory for dividend-paying resident companies, with a statutory election to not maintain an ICA for companies that will pay no imputed dividends; (iii) GST grouping election under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 to treat related companies as a single registered group; (iv) hybrid and branch mismatch elections under Parts GB and FI of the Income Tax Act 2007 (BEPS Action 2), including structured arrangement and deferred tax timing elections; and (v) the MIXER regime election (Subpart HM) permitting offshore portfolio investment entities to align New Zealand and foreign tax outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 — compulsory registration threshold for a person carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand where total taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period. Non-resident businesses supplying 'remote services' (electronically supplied services and certain intangible services received by New Zealand consumers) must register under the offshore supplier registration regime once supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed NZD 60,000 per annum. Registration below the threshold is voluntary where a taxable activity is carried on in New Zealand.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for the 0% GST rate under sections 139–141 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supplier or purchaser exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply and the supplier holds written evidence of export (customs export declaration, shipping documents, or signed certificate of export). Services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply are zero-rated if not directly connected with land in New Zealand or with goods in New Zealand; 'remote services' (including digital product files, holographic design/artwork and IP licensing) supplied to non-resident publishers are zero-rated. The collectible trading card manufacturer may zero-rate physical card exports upon proving physical export within 28 days, and may zero-rate design and pre-press service fees supplied to overseas publishers.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusDec filed through the Trade Single Window of the New Zealand Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (evidencing physical departure of goods from New Zealand)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent bank foreign-currency settlement advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in New Zealand)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-authorisation API mandate exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary for private-sector businesses but mandatory for New Zealand government departments and agencies to receive invoices in the Peppol format; suppliers should adopt Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 via a certified access point for government contracting. All GST returns and payments must be filed electronically through myIR, and GST-R digital submissions through accounting-software APIs are supported. Export declarations are filed with the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window (CusDec), which is not a fiscal clearance mechanism.

Inland Revenue may retrospectively claw back the 0% GST rate and re-assess GST at 15% (with use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties) where the exporter fails to substantiate physical export within 28 days, holds defective documentation, or engages in artificial arrangements. Shortfall penalties escalate from 20% (unacceptable tax position), 40% (prior offshore/export offences), 100% (abusive tax position) to 150% (evasion or fraudulent intent). Intentional GST evasion is a criminal offence under the Tax Administration Act 1994, carrying fines up to NZD 50,000 per offence for individuals and NZD 250,000 for companies, together with imprisonment up to 5 years. False export declarations to the New Zealand Customs Service are separately prosecutable under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 with goods forfeiture, monetary penalties up to NZD 300,000, and debarment from deferred payment arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully freely convertible and New Zealand maintains an open capital account. There is no exchange-control licensing requirement distinguishing current-account payments from capital-account transfers, and no general prior-approval requirement for profit remittances. Dividend remittance by an NZ limited company is subject only to the Companies Act 1993 dividend-solvency test and to Non-resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) at the statutory 30% rate, which is commonly reduced to 15% or lower under an applicable double-tax agreement. Interest payments to non-resident lenders may also attract NRWT, with the Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) regime as a cost-effective alternative in eligible non-associated lending scenarios. The only material cross-border restriction is the Overseas Investment Act 2005 screening regime for foreign investments in sensitive land, fishing quota, or significant business assets above statutory thresholds; a standard collectible trading card publishing and cold-foil printing company is not sensitive by default unless it acquires sensitive land or controls assets over the applicable threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no minimum realisation period for export receipts. Exporters may lawfully retain proceeds in onshore or offshore foreign-currency accounts and may apply those funds directly against foreign supplier invoices without Reserve Bank approval. Banks must nevertheless submit international transaction reporting data for balance-of-payments and statistical purposes, and all cross-border payments remain subject to AML/CFT and sanctions screening. For the proposed trading-card printing and wholesale business, no export licence is required for the finished cards provided they contain no prohibited content, comply with applicable intellectual-property and licensing rules, and are not exported to sanctioned jurisdictions.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ; Te Pūtea Matua) is the central bank and prudential supervisor for registered banks. New Zealand does not maintain a separate foreign exchange administration body; exchange-control functions were historically abolished and are not currently active. Foreign-investment screening is administered by the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) under Land Information New Zealand pursuant to the Overseas Investment Act 2005. The Financial Markets Authority (FMA) supervises non-bank financial service providers for Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) compliance, while the Reserve Bank supervises registered banks for AML/CFT purposes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a secondary payment corridor; Low as a substitute for a Tier-1 New Zealand bank account. An offshore digital EMI (electronic money institution) cannot rely on a home-state passport in New Zealand. It must be registered as a Financial Service Provider under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and be an AML/CFT reporting entity under the AML/CFT Act 2009, or operate solely through a local registered bank as its settlement channel. Non-bank EMIs are not registered banks and cannot accept call or term deposits from the public; therefore they cannot legally provide the deposit-taking, escrow, documentary-letter-of-credit, or RBNZ-supervised treasury services required for capital injection and large trade settlement. The prudent blueprint is to use a licensed non-bank payment provider for fast payouts to overseas distribution partners and to maintain the primary capital, trade, and clearing accounts with a New Zealand registered bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder or parent must effect the equity injection as a SWIFT MT103 transfer from an overseas bank account to the designated NZ_LTD capital-escrow account at a New Zealand registered bank. The payment instruction should carry the purpose classification 'EQUITY CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / SHARE SUBSCRIPTION' and a unique reference such as 'NZ_LTD_CAPITAL_2025_INJECTION'. No Reserve Bank foreign-exchange approval is required. The receiving bank will verify source-of-funds documentation, screen all parties against sanctions and politically exposed person registers, and hold the funds pending completion of bank-side AML/CFT clearance. The company secretary must then file the return of allotment with the New Zealand Companies Office and record the share issue in the company register before funds are released to the operational account. For shareholder loans, the parties should execute a formal loan agreement with commercial interest terms and comply with non-resident interest withholding tax (NRWT), Approved Issuer Levy, and transfer-pricing documentation to prevent re-characterisation as equity.For operational trade settlements, use SWIFT MT103 with GPS tracking (SWIFT gpi) for direct cross-border payments to suppliers, stating the invoice number, purchase-order number, and goods description such as 'printed collectible trading cards with holographic cold-foil finish'. For large capital-equipment purchases, issue an irrevocable documentary letter of credit under UCP600 using a SWIFT MT700 message, with conformity checking of commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, inspection certificates, and certificates of origin before payment. Domestic New Zealand payments should use the local BECS clearing system for payroll, rent, utilities, and domestic VAT/GST obligations. Recurring international royalty and licensing payments should be made by SWIFT MT103 with supporting licence agreements and tax withholding evidence retained in the treasury file.
