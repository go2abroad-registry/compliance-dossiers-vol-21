Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a New Zealand company; the Companies Act 1993 abolishes par value and permits directors to issue shares for such consideration as the board determines (s 47). The corporate trustee must, however, settle the underlying discretionary asset protection trust with a verifiable initial corpus (typically NZ$1,000 or USD 1,000) deposited into a designated fiduciary trust account held at a New Zealand registered bank; no escrow account is prescribed by the Companies Act, but the foreign trust regime under Tax Administration Act 1994 Schedule 26 requires complete record-keeping of settlements and distributions.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: the premises must obtain a Building Warrant of Fitness and compliance schedule where required under ss 100-108A of the Building Act 2004, and an approved fire evacuation scheme under s 66 of the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017 including trial evacuations and signage. Structural load: the territorial authority's building inspectorate issues the certificate of occupancy / code compliance certificate confirming structural adequacy; no additional structural load clearance is required for an office occupancy. Occupational workplace clearance: the company as a Person Conducting a Business or Undertaking (PCBU) must comply with the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including a documented workplace safety policy, hazard identification, first aid and incident reporting procedures, and, where relevant, a certificate of inspection from the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment in respect of the workplace facilities.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a New Zealand company; the Companies Act 1993 abolishes par value and permits directors to issue shares for such consideration as the board determines (s 47). The corporate trustee must, however, settle the underlying discretionary asset protection trust with a verifiable initial corpus (typically NZ$1,000 or USD 1,000) deposited into a designated fiduciary trust account held at a New Zealand registered bank; no escrow account is prescribed by the Companies Act, but the foreign trust regime under Tax Administration Act 1994 Schedule 26 requires complete record-keeping of settlements and distributions.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake), New Zealand; official national tax administration portal at www.ird.govt.nz; secure online services and return filing conducted through the myIR (Inland Revenue digital services) platform; legislative foundation is the Tax Administration Act 1994, the Income Tax Act 2007, and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-sourced discretionary asset protection trust operated through a New Zealand International Company (CK_IC) acting as corporate trustee, the statutory IRD number procurement process is as follows: (1) appoint a New Zealand-resident tax agent and, where applicable, a disclosure agent for the foreign trust; (2) file Form IR596 (Application for IRD number for non-resident individual or company) or the equivalent online application through myIR, submitting certified constitutional documents, the trust deed, and proof of the non-resident settlor's status; (3) register the trust itself as a New Zealand foreign trust with IRD under section 59BA of the Tax Administration Act 1994, which requires lodgement of the annual foreign trust disclosure return (Form IR607) and payment of the NZD 47.50 annual disclosure fee; (4) complete AML/CFT verification for the settlor, beneficiaries, protectors, and the corporate trustee, including certified passports, tax residence certificates, and bank reference letters; (5) provide evidence of the trustee's centre of administration in New Zealand (meeting minutes, local agent appointment, New Zealand office address); and (6) receive the dedicated IRD number for the company and the trust, typically issued within 10 to 20 working days. Non-resident beneficiaries are not required to obtain New Zealand IRD numbers unless they derive assessable New Zealand-source income from the trust.

Non-resident beneficiaries are not required to obtain New Zealand IRD numbers unless they derive assessable New Zealand-source income from the trust.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the International Company and its associated discretionary asset protection trust is classified within the New Zealand foreign trust exemption zone: a trust whose settlor was non-resident at all times since settlement is a foreign trust; foreign-sourced income derived by the trustee is excluded income under section HC 29 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and is therefore not subject to New Zealand income tax; New Zealand-source income remains taxable, generally through final withholding taxes (NRWT/RWT), and the entity is outside the ordinary entity-level corporate tax net for offshore income.

Available election forms

Available statutory elections include: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 for New Zealand-incorporated international companies with five or fewer owners, which recharacterises the entity as fiscally transparent; (2) qualifying company transitional elections for grandfathered close companies; (3) foreign trust registration election under section 59BA of the Tax Administration Act 1994, which elects the trust into the tax-exempt foreign trust regime conditioned on annual IR607 disclosure; (4) election to designate income as beneficiary income versus trustee income, altering the effective rate between 33% trustee rate and beneficiary marginal rates; (5) hybrid mismatch and branch mismatch elections under Subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007, limited to neutralising double non-taxation outcomes; (6) GST zero-rating elections for exported trustee and fiduciary administration services where the recipient is a non-resident located outside New Zealand at the time of supply; and (7) option for a non-active foreign trust to obtain a certificate of exemption from annual disclosure filing where no New Zealand-source income arises and no distributions are made to New Zealand residents.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) made in New Zealand in any 12-month period; a non-resident supplier of remote services (cross-border B2C services) or low-value imported goods to New Zealand consumers is likewise required to register for GST where the same NZD 60,000 threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is available at lower turnover; supplies of fiduciary and trust administration services that are exempt financial services do not count toward the registration threshold unless separately zero-rated as exported services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply and retains sufficient documentary evidence of actual export. Services are zero-rated under section 11A where the services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services do not relate to land or moveable goods in New Zealand and are not supplied directly to an individual present in New Zealand. For the Discretionary Asset Protection Trust, the corporate trustee's fiduciary administration, trust accounting, protector support, and asset-holding services supplied to the offshore settlor, offshore protector, or the foreign trust itself are eligible for zero-rating when the contractual recipient is non-resident and outside New Zealand; the trust's own foreign-source investment income is excluded income for income tax purposes and is not a GST supply. Exported financial services are zero-rated only where they are supplied to a non-resident outside New Zealand and correctly documented; otherwise they remain exempt financial supplies without input tax recovery.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusDEC or approved New Zealand customs entry) evidencing the physical export of any tangible trust assets or goods forming part of the supply chain
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued to the non-resident party proving shipment from New Zealand within the statutory 28-day export period
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103/MT202 confirmation evidencing repatriation of export proceeds or fiduciary management fees into the New Zealand trustee's account
  • Certified executed Trust Deed and signed fiduciary services engagement letter identifying the offshore settlor, protector, and beneficiary class
  • Tax residence certificates of the settlor and principal beneficiaries substantiating non-resident status for foreign trust classification
  • GST zero-rated invoices bearing the recipient's overseas address and the statutory zero-rating statement

  • Trustee meeting minutes and board resolutions confirming the offshore decision-making and service delivery arrangements

New Zealand does not mandate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime for GST. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and implemented through the PEPPOL network; government agencies are progressively able to receive e-Invoices under the New Zealand e-Invoicing for Business programme, but no nationwide mandatory e-invoicing deadline applies to the private sector. All GST returns, registrations, refund claims, and foreign trust disclosure returns are filed electronically through the Inland Revenue myIR portal using IRD-validated return schemas; system-to-system bulk filing through gateway services is permitted for larger taxpayers. There is no requirement for API-based real-time government pre-clearance of individual invoices.

Inland Revenue audit scrutiny of zero-rating claims focuses on documentary proof of physical export for goods and non-resident overseas receipt for services, as well as on foreign trust registration status under section 59BA of the Tax Administration Act 1994. Where zero-rating is claimed without adequate documentation, the supply is recharacterised as a taxable supply at 15% GST, and the IRD may issue amended assessments with retrospective effect; under section 108 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, the general reassessment period is four years, but no time limit applies in cases of fraud, evasion, or gross carelessness. Unpaid GST and income tax attract use-of-money interest from the original due date. Shortfall penalties apply progressively: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% for an abusive tax position. Fraudulent evasion of GST, or intentional failure to file foreign trust disclosure returns, is a criminal offence under sections 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994, exposing the entity and responsible officers to fines up to NZD 50,000 for the first offence, NZD 100,000 for subsequent offences, and imprisonment for up to five years. Additionally, failure to properly register or disclose the foreign trust causes the trust to be treated as a domestic trust for income tax purposes, triggering retrospective taxation of foreign-source income at the 33% trustee rate and denial of the excluded income treatment, along with clawback of any previously refunded GST and loss of the Tax_Exempt_Zone classification.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand applies a full open-account convertibility standard. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible, with no general requirement for exchange-control approval or central-bank clearance. Dividend, interest, trust distribution, and other profit-repatriation flows may be converted into any V7 currency through authorised banks and remitted abroad without reserve-bank consent, subject only to standard AML/CFT, tax-reporting, and foreign-account-information-exchange obligations. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a freely traded hard currency; the absence of surrender, rationing, or multiple-currency practices means that a discretionary asset-protection trust administered in New Zealand faces zero statutory convertibility hurdles. The only material friction arises where a bank classifies the trust as high-risk and imposes enhanced due-diligence (EDD) evidence requirements before processing the conversion or cross-border remittance; such requirements are compliance-based rather than exchange-control based.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists in New Zealand. Exporters are not required to repatriate or convert hard-currency earnings into NZD. Nevertheless, resident providers of fiduciary and trust-administration services must retain full supporting records for every cross-border receipt, classify the transaction under the Reserve Bank's balance-of-payments (BOP) statistical reporting conventions, and report a transaction to the receiving bank if it involves cash or appears to be a material or unusual transfer. For trust-linked service exports (i.e., fiduciary administration fees, trust structuring, and custodial services), the bank will apply a BOP service-purpose code, commonly 'F/XF – Fiduciary funds administration services' or the corresponding standard service industry code, in the electronic transfer instruction to satisfy transaction monitoring and external-sector statistics.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), as the central bank and prudential regulator; in the anti-money-laundering/countering-financing-of-terrorism (AML/CFT) perimeter, the Reserve Bank supervises banks and the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) credits certain non-bank reporting entities; the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE) administers the Companies Office and trusts registration, and the New Zealand Customs Service enforces cross-border cash reporting. New Zealand maintains fully liberalised exchange controls under the IMF Article VIII obligations and does not operate a separate foreign-exchange-administration regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. New Zealand's regulatory perimeter treats non-bank digital EMI-type service providers as financial service providers, but they are not licensed as banks and cannot independently provide full cross-border treasury clearing at bank-graded standards. An offshore digital EMI, unlicensed in New Zealand, may only operate through a locally registered financial-services provider that is an AML/CFT reporting entity and that has complied with the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008. For a high-risk discretionary asset-protection trust (ISIC 6430), most recognised banks will reject or freeze an account funded through an unregulated offshore EMI because the EMI's aggregate-knowledge of source-of-funds and beneficial ownership is rarely acceptable under local 'designated-business-group' diligence. Consequently, the viable path is to maintain the primary treasury rails through a licensed domestic bank and use a New Zealand-registered fintech for ancillary payment facilitation only where the fintech clearly holds an FSP license, maintains a dispute-resolution scheme, and can obtain correspondent-bank AML acceptance.

Defense evidence: A full look-through ownership and control chart naming the settlor, protector, trustee-directors, beneficiaries (actual or describable class), and any person who ultimately owns or controls more than 25% of the trustee company; copies of all identity evidence and source-of-name verification; signature authority and management-accountability resolutions issued by the trustee board.
Settlement assets emanating from an opaque overseas source, third-party wealth, or cryptocurrency conversion

Root cause: Settlement assets emanating from an opaque overseas source, third-party wealth, or cryptocurrency conversion

Defense evidence: Bank or brokerage statements showing the historical original source of funds over at least 12 months, a formal source-of-wealth questionnaire with independent third-party attestation (e.g., accountant or law firm), tax records proving lawful derivation of the settlement amount, a notarized deed of gift or settlement statement, and a sworn declaration confirming no proceeds of unlawful activity.
Cross-border wire received on behalf of beneficiaries resident in jurisdictions perceived as high-money-laundering or sanctions-pressured

Root cause: Cross-border wire received on behalf of beneficiaries resident in jurisdictions perceived as high-money-laundering or sanctions-pressured

Defense evidence: A sanctions-screening report showing no OFAC/EU/FMA/SEC match, a jurisdiction rationale memo explaining legitimate family/administerial nexus, independent legal opinion confirming no tax-evasion or foreign-assisted-tax-treaty abuse, and a binding undertaking to maintain notifiable-transaction reporting to the Financial Intelligence Unit for 5 years.
Trust's tax-exemption classification used to suggest absence of tax-reporting obligations, thus triggering bank suspicion of aggressive tax avoidance

Root cause: Trust's tax-exemption classification used to suggest absence of tax-reporting obligations, thus triggering bank suspicion of aggressive tax avoidance

Defense evidence: A New Zealand tax counsel advice letter or binding ruling confirmation explaining the entity's strict classification as neither resident nor carrying on a taxable New Zealand business except for passive fiduciary services; an economic-substance workbook showing that all services are priced, invoiced, and subject to auditable accounting; a certificate of no outstanding Inland Revenue obligations; and a channeling instruction instructing the bank to apply tax-transparency reporting (e.g., CRS, FATCA).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Because New Zealand has no exchange-control approvals, an inbound capital injection for the discretionary asset-protection trust is routed through a standard international wire transfer (equivalent to SWIFT MT103) to the trust's NZ operating account. The sending overseas bank should include: (a) the exact trustee-company name and account number; (b) a purpose narrative fixed as 'Trust Settlement Contribution – Beneficial Ownership Underlying Settlement Agreement dated [date]'; and (c) a single commercial purpose code recognized by the receiving bank's BOP reporting, typically 'Other investment – liabilities, deposits' or an alternative accepted by the receiving bank's reporting module. The trustee must retain an executed settlement or deed of gift, bank source documents, and an ID check for the remitter where the remitter is the settlor or a related party. The receiving bank will apply standard AML/CFT monitoring, verify the remitter's identity through the correspondent bank or via an attached SWIFT 'COVER' message, and may effect a 1-business-day clearing into the escrow account if all documents are in order. For larger contributions, the bank may require a signed copy of the settlement deed and a board resolution of the trustee company.Outbound trade and fiduciary disbursements from the trust are cleared through direct SWIFT MT103 or MT202 customer transfers for non-documentary remittances, and through documentary commercial letters of credit (LCs) under UCP600 for actual goods-related trade. The settlement method will depend on the nature of the disbursement: (i) for operational costs, beneficiary invoices, legal fees, and trust distributions, a normal SEPA-is-not-applicable SWIFT MT103 transfer is used; (ii) for high-value, protection-linked asset purchases (e.g., agricultural or food-processing assets), a commercial LC opened by the New Zealand bank in favour of the overseas seller provides a safer clearing rail; (iii) for fiduciary distributions to beneficiaries with a valid deed clause, the bank requires a trustee resolution identifying the beneficiary, the distribution formula, and the underlying permitted class, and will process an MT103 with a purpose narrative aligned to the deed; (iv) for same-currency NZD settlements within New Zealand's exchange-clearing system, the bank uses the Reserve Bank's ESAS (Exchange Settlement Account System) or its commercial equivalents. All outbound payments are screened for sanctions and monitored for identifiable commercial rationale.Outright foreign-exchange forwards (plain or deliverable, commonly NZD/USD, NZD/EUR, NZD/GBP)Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing the New Zealand dollar where the underlying exposure is exceptional or where the counterparty bank is domiciled in a jurisdiction without a deliverable-NZD clearing capabilityForeign-exchange swaps (FX swaps) covering matched periods of fiduciary cash-flow exposureCross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) for long-dated asset-protection trust liabilities in foreign currencyPlain-vanilla over-the-counter FX options and collars to hedge disbursements and settlement liquidityCommodity-price hedging through exchange-traded futures if the trust's underlying trade exposure includes agricultural exports (e.g., dairy or horticultural commodity indices)Money-market and deposit-basis hedging entered under an ISDA Master Agreement or local NZX-lineage documentation where bank approval has been granted

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. New Zealand's regulatory perimeter treats non-bank digital EMI-type service providers as financial service providers, but they are not licensed as banks and cannot independently provide full cross-border treasury clearing at bank-graded standards. An offshore digital EMI, unlicensed in New Zealand, may only operate through a locally registered financial-services provider that is an AML/CFT reporting entity and that has complied with the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008. For a high-risk discretionary asset-protection trust (ISIC 6430), most recognised banks will reject or freeze an account funded through an unregulated offshore EMI because the EMI's aggregate-knowledge of source-of-funds and beneficial ownership is rarely acceptable under local 'designated-business-group' diligence. Consequently, the viable path is to maintain the primary treasury rails through a licensed domestic bank and use a New Zealand-registered fintech for ancillary payment facilitation only where the fintech clearly holds an FSP license, maintains a dispute-resolution scheme, and can obtain correspondent-bank AML acceptance.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on gross dividends paid to non-resident shareholders from New Zealand-resident companies. Where the recipient company holds at least 10% of the voting power, treaty rates may apply (commonly 5% under DTA), and portfolio investors may enjoy a 15% reduced rate. Fully imputed dividends may carry imputation credits, but non-resident shareholders generally cannot receive a New Zealand imputation credit offset.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party dealings per income year triggers the mandatory contemporaneous Local File under the New Zealand International Tax regime. A Master File is required when the multinational group meets the OECD criteria and the New Zealand group has cross-border related-party revenue or expenses exceeding NZD 10,000,000. For smaller cross-border transactions, the taxpayer must maintain sufficient documentation to support the arm's length nature of the pricing methodology adopted.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations decree; however, general anti-avoidance rules under section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and the 'arrangement' provisions in Part G apply. For trust structures, Inland Revenue will scrutinise whether the New Zealand trustee exercises genuine discretionary powers, maintains board minutes, operates independent bank accounts, and has adequate staff and office infrastructure. A discretionary asset protection trust that is entirely controlled by the settlor or that lacks commercial substance in New Zealand may be reconstructed as a resident trust, with worldwide income taxed at 33%, and may face shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall. Additionally, foreign trust disclosure requirements mandate the filing of annual financial statements and a foreign trust disclosure form; failure to do so results in the trust being deemed a resident trust for all income years.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: On conviction under the Tax Administration Act 1994, an individual is liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 5 years and a fine not exceeding NZ$50,000; a body corporate is liable to a fine not exceeding NZ$50,000. Inland Revenue may also impose a civil evasion penalty of 150% of the tax shortfall.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who 'aided, abetted, counselled, or procured' the evasion are personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall and penalties. Courts may issue monetary restitution orders, travel bans, and, in egregious cases, custodial sentences for directors found to be the controlling mind of the scheme.
Shell Company / Sham Trust

Statutory sanction: The arrangement is voided under the general anti-avoidance rule; all income is reconstructed as that of the trustee and taxed at the trustee rate of 33% for the relevant years. Shortfall penalties up to 150% of the tax shortfall are imposed, and Inland Revenue may file a civil claim for repayment of any refunds obtained.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate trustee who knowingly sign returns or financial statements that rely on sham arrangements are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall. Travel bans may be imposed pending resolution of civil recovery proceedings, and directors may be prosecuted for obstruction of the Commissioner's enquiry.
AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a body corporate may be fined up to NZ$2,000,000 per breach; individuals may be fined up to NZ$300,000 and imprisoned for up to 2 years. In addition, civil penalties up to NZ$2,000,000 may be levied by the Financial Sanctions Authority or NZ Police.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are personally liable if they directed, sanctioned, or knowingly permitted the AML/CFT breach. They may be disqualified from acting as company directors or trustee-license holders and may face criminal prosecution alongside the corporate entity.
Fraudulent DTA Relief

Statutory sanction: Knowingly providing false information to Inland Revenue constitutes a fraudulent tax offence under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the Crimes Act 1961. Penalties include imprisonment up to 7 years, fines up to NZ$50,000, and immediate disallowance of the treaty rate with full NRWT at the statutory rate (30% dividends, 15% interest/royalties) plus interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or knowingly use a false certificate of residence are personally liable for the under-withheld tax and are exposed to criminal prosecution. Travel bans and asset-freezing orders under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009 may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may make an arm's length determination, adjust taxable income, impose a 40% shortfall penalty for unacceptable tax position and a 100% penalty for taking an abusive tax position, and levy use-of-money interest. The taxpayer may also lose loss carryforward entitlements and face an integrity-based disclosure requirement under the International Financial Reporting schedule.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve transfer pricing policies without obtaining contemporaneous documentation are personally liable for the shortfall attributable to the adjustment. Repeat offenders may face director bans and civil restraint orders preventing them from managing any New Zealand company.
Failure to Disclose Foreign Trust Status

Statutory sanction: The trust is deemed a resident trust under the Income Tax Act 2007, making all worldwide income taxable in New Zealand at 33%. Late filing penalties of NZ$250 plus daily penalties apply, and the trustee may be subject to penalties up to NZ$20,000 for deliberate non-disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate trustee are jointly and severally liable for the trust's worldwide tax liability if the trust is deemed resident. They may be personally barred from acting as trustees pending remediation, and Inland Revenue may seek a personal costs order against directors who failed to ensure filing.
