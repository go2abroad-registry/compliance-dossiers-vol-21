Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up share capital requirement under New Zealand law for an International Company (non-resident owned) limited by shares; shares may be issued for consideration determined by the board, including services, property, or cash. No escrow account rule applies at incorporation. The company must have at least one share and may issue shares without a par value.

Funding & unlock path: If a physical office is leased: obtain a Building Warrant of Fitness / Fire Safety Certificate via the territorial authority (District Council) and the New Zealand Fire and Emergency Act 2017 compliance certificate for passive and active fire systems (EWS, sprinklers, alarms). Provide structural load clearance from a chartered professional engineer for any server rack loads; comply with Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including a written workplace safety policy, first aid kit, and worker engagement; display a NZ Fire Evacuation Plan and obtain a Fire Evacuation Scheme approval from Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) if 10+ employees. For a pure remote SaaS operator, no physical clearance applies, but the company must still maintain a workplace digital safety policy and secure disposal records for hardware.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up share capital requirement under New Zealand law for an International Company (non-resident owned) limited by shares; shares may be issued for consideration determined by the board, including services, property, or cash. No escrow account rule applies at incorporation. The company must have at least one share and may issue shares without a par value.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident foreign company not deriving New Zealand-source income and having no permanent establishment in New Zealand; offshore business profits are exempt from New Zealand income tax; New Zealand-source interest, dividends, and royalties remain subject to non-resident withholding tax).

Available election forms

New Zealand does not administer a US-style check-the-box election. Taxation is determined by legal form and residence under the Income Tax Act 2007. Available elections include: (i) Look-Through Company (LTC) election for qualifying closely held NZ companies; (ii) consolidated group election under Part IC of the Income Tax Act 2007; (iii) imputation election for NZ-resident companies electing to attach imputation credits to dividends; (iv) Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) election for non-resident interest payers to reduce the non-resident withholding tax rate; (v) hybrid mismatch outcomes are not electable and are neutralised automatically under the BEPS hybrid mismatch rules in sections FH 1 to FH 11.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 compulsory registration threshold for taxable supplies made over any 12-month period; non-resident remote-services suppliers registering once New Zealand-resident supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period; voluntary registration permitted where a business intends to undertake taxable activity in New Zealand.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 to: (i) goods exported from New Zealand under a contractual obligation to export, evidenced by transport and customs documents; (ii) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, except services connected with New Zealand land or goods situated in New Zealand; (iii) remote and digital services (including LegalTech SaaS and e-signature authentication platforms) supplied to non-resident business customers where the supplier holds commercial evidence of the customer's non-resident status, such as customer country, IP address, payment card country, and bank location. The supplier must be GST-registered and the supply must not be made to a New Zealand-resident recipient or to a recipient in New Zealand at the time of supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (NZ Customs Service Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Contract or Purchase Order evidencing the export obligation
  • Bank Statement or Inward Foreign Currency Remittance Evidence
  • GST Return (Form GST10) and zero-rated export schedule
  • For services: Non-Resident Customer Declaration and IP address or geolocation evidence

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no API-based invoice approval, and no digital stamping regime. GST invoices must meet the content requirements of section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and be issued within 28 days of the supply. Peppol electronic invoicing is voluntary through accredited service providers. Business records must be retained for seven years.

Shortfall penalties under sections 141A to 141M of the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for failure to exercise reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% for evasion or abusive tax positions. Use-of-money interest applies to late GST and income tax payments, and late filing penalties apply to late GST returns. Zero-rated export supplies are subject to retrospective clawback if the supplier fails to retain and produce the required export evidence on audit, if the goods are re-imported or diverted into New Zealand consumption, or if the non-resident customer is later found to have been in New Zealand at the time of supply. Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion, expose the taxpayer to criminal prosecution, imprisonment, forfeiture of assets, and administrative civil penalties.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full currency convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions. The New Zealand dollar is freely floating with no foreign-exchange control approval required for inbound equity injections, dividend remittances, loan repayments, or trade payments. Profit repatriation is unrestricted from a foreign-exchange perspective; however, non-resident dividend payments are generally subject to New Zealand non-resident withholding tax at a 15% treaty-reduced rate, and any outbound payment must comply with the relevant anti-money laundering and sanctions screening requirements of the transacting bank. No separate central bank approval is required for capital remittance, and there is no restriction on maintaining foreign-currency accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement. There is no mandatory timeline for repatriating export receipts and no mandatory conversion ratio into New Zealand dollars. Exporters and digital-service providers are free to retain funds in foreign-currency accounts or convert through any licensed financial institution. The only relevant obligation is that all banking transactions must be supported by accurate underlying commercial documentation for AML/CFT and tax-compliance purposes, and any suspicious transaction must be reported to the Financial Intelligence Unit under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-Low. New Zealand does not operate a dedicated electronic-money-institution licensing regime comparable to the UK FCA regime. A non-bank entity that accepts deposits or carries on a banking business in New Zealand must be registered as a bank under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021. Offshore digital EMI platforms may be used as payment collection and cross-border disbursement rails only if they are properly licensed in their home jurisdiction and can rely on an exemption or a limited-service arrangement without engaging in prohibited deposit-taking activity in New Zealand. The practical compliance burden is moderate for treasury purposes, but the EMI should not be used as a primary escrow or capital-account custodian. A registered New Zealand financial-services provider holding client funds in trust can be acceptable for SaaS revenue settlement, subject to the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 client-money rules and FSPR registration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan funds should be transferred via SWIFT MT103 from the ultimate parent or subscriber's bank account directly to the New Zealand IC's operational account. The payment message must include a clear remittance information field specifying the purpose, for example: 'Equity injection for treasury operations and working capital of the New Zealand international company.' New Zealand does not impose exchange-control approval or a formal purpose-code regime, but the receiving bank will require supporting documentation for its submission to New Zealand's Financial Intelligence regime. For a legal-tech SaaS entity, no consent is typically required under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 because the activity does not involve sensitive land, fishing quota, or significant business assets as defined by that statute. The bank may nonetheless require an investor declaration and a copy of the Companies Office registration to confirm the beneficial owners for AML/CFT purposes.Cross-border vendor payments and software-royalty outflows should be cleared via SWIFT MT103 with payment details tied to the underlying invoice and commercial contract. For smaller operational disbursements to SaaS marketing vendors and contractors, same-day domestic NZD clearing can be executed via the Payments New Zealand Bulk Electronic Clearing System (BECS) after inward conversion, while USD and multicurrency disbursements should use SWIFT gpi for faster, compliant settlement. For larger cross-border trade or licensing arrangements, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit may be issued by the New Zealand bank through a confirming bank in the beneficiary's jurisdiction, with settlement occurring via SWIFT MT700/MT103 chains. Stripe and credit-card rails are preferred for outward subscription refunds and merchant settlement, but payroll, inter-affiliate loans, and strategic acquisitions should be executed exclusively through the licensed bank treasury channel with full audit trail.Deliverable FX forwards (NZD/USD, EUR/USD, GBP/USD and AUD/USD) available from ANZ, ASB, BNZ, and Westpac treasury desksFX swaps (spot-forward and forward-forward combinations) for short-term liquidity rebalancingCross-currency swaps (CCS) for longer-term balance-sheet hedging of shareholder loans and inter-affiliate fundingEuropean-style over-the-counter FX options and zero-cost collars for revenue protectionNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible at the bank's discretion for non-NZD currencies and for counterparties not able to deliver the underlying, although the NZD is fully deliverable and onshore deliverable hedging is the preferred structural approach

For a standard 31 March balance date, the annual return must be filed by 7 June (paper) or 7 July (myIR). Terminal tax is due 7 April following the income year. Non-standard balance dates require filing by the 7th day of the 7th month after the balance date, subject to any tax agent extension.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 1 million in aggregate cross-border related-party receipts or payments triggers the IR10 International Dealings Schedule in the annual tax return; NZD 680 million consolidated group revenue triggers the Country-by-Country Report and associated master file and local file obligations. IRD may request a complete transfer pricing document under its administrative powers for any transactions, even below the statutory notification threshold.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply Cayman/BVI-style Economic Substance Regulations. Instead, economic substance is tested through tax residence, permanent establishment analysis, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act 2007, s BG 1), treaty beneficial-ownership and limitation-of-benefits clauses, and IRD's anti-conduit principles. A LegalTech SaaS IC must maintain substantive commercial activity in its jurisdiction of residence, including physical or virtual decision-making, personnel, bank accounts, contract execution records, and server or infrastructure ownership, otherwise payments may be recharacterized and treaty relief denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty for evasion or similar act of 150% of the tax shortfall under section 141E of the Tax Administration Act 1994; criminal prosecution under section 143B carrying imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines up to NZD 50,000; full recovery of unpaid tax plus use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or approve fraudulent filings may be prosecuted as principals. The court can order civil restitution for unpaid tax and penalties, impose travel and exit bans, require surrender of passports, disqualify directors, and sentence directors to imprisonment upon conviction.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance and GAAR

Statutory sanction: The arrangement may be voided under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule; IRD may reconstruct income and expenses on a realistic basis; an abusive tax position penalty of 100% of the shortfall applies under section 141D; treaty benefits are denied for lack of beneficial ownership; CFC or attributed-income rules may tax New Zealand resident controllers.

Director personal liability: Directors and trust protectors may be treated as shadow directors. They can be held personally liable for the tax shortfall and penalties, subject to indemnity orders, banned from managing companies, and in serious cases restricted from leaving New Zealand during investigation or prosecution.
Transfer Pricing Misprision and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: IRD may make an arm's-length adjustment under sections GC 13-GC 15 of the Income Tax Act 2007, assess a shortfall penalty of 40% for gross carelessness or 100% for an abusive position, and impose disclosure penalties for the incomplete IR10; use-of-money interest accrues from the original terminal tax date.

Director personal liability: Directors approving fabricated transfer pricing, refusing to produce documentation, or allowing non-arm's-length supply chains to distort profit may be assessed personally as participants in the arrangement. Civil recovery, director disqualification, and exit bans are possible when outstanding tax liabilities are material.
