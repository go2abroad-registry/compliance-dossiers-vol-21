Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Companies Act 1993; the company must issue at least one share. No Government-mandated escrow account or paid-up capital deposit is required at incorporation. For a captive reinsurance SPV, the RBNZ fit-and-proper and prudential standards will require a solvency/holding-capital buffer commensurate with the underwriting risk, generally satisfied by paid-up share capital plus a premium reserve fund held in a dedicated New Zealand bank account or a reinsurance trust.

Funding & unlock path: The SPV must obtain a building warrant of fitness (BWOF) under the Building Act 2004 for any leased or owned office space and a Fire Safety and Evacuation Procedure certificate under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017. WorkSafe clearance for office-based operations is required under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including first-aid equipment, electrical safety inspection, and a written workplace safety statement. If no physical location is established, the board must still adopt an emergency and business-continuity policy and file a nil-employee health and safety registration with WorkSafe, confirming that all operational activities are outsourced to licensed corporate and trustee service providers.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Companies Act 1993; the company must issue at least one share. No Government-mandated escrow account or paid-up capital deposit is required at incorporation. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / IRD) of New Zealand; official e-services portal 'myIR' at ir.govt.nz; company and NZBN registration administered by the New Zealand Companies Office, registry.companiesoffice.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A foreign captive reinsurance entity must (i) obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) through the Companies Office registration or NZBN verification of the offshore International Company; (ii) apply for an IRD number using Form IR596 (non-resident company application) accompanied by certified certificate of incorporation, memorandum/articles, directors' identification and a New Zealand fixed establishment/agent declaration; (iii) register for GST online via myIR (Form GST101) where taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period or where voluntary registration is elected; (iv) for non-resident companies with no NZ taxable activity, an IRD number is not required for shipping-only or offshore passive income, but a myIR account and resident withholding registration must be established if any NZ-sourced withholding tax obligations arise; (v) appointment of a New Zealand resident tax agent is recommended for entity-level IRD correspondence.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident offshore International Company not carrying on a New Zealand taxable activity; a New Zealand-resident captive reinsurance SPV would instead default to Entity_Level_Taxation at 28% under the Income Tax Act 2007) — A foreign captive reinsurance entity must (i) obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) through the Companies Office registration or NZBN verification of the offshore International Company; (ii) apply for an IRD number using Form IR596 (non-resident company application) accompanied by certified certificate of incorporation, memorandum/articles, directors' identification and a New Zealand fixed establishment/agent declaration; (iii) register for GST online via myIR (Form GST101) where taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period or where voluntary registration is elected; (iv) for non-resident companies with no NZ taxable activity, an IRD number is not required for shipping-only or offshore passive income, but a myIR account and resident withholding registration must be established if any NZ-sourced withholding tax obligations arise; (v) appointment of a New Zealand resident tax agent is recommended for entity-level IRD correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident offshore International Company not carrying on a New Zealand taxable activity; a New Zealand-resident captive reinsurance SPV would instead default to Entity_Level_Taxation at 28% under the Income Tax Act 2007)

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (i) look-through company (LTC) election under Subpart YP of the Income Tax Act 2007 for qualifying closely held NZ-resident companies; (ii) no affirmative CFC election — 10%+ NZ-resident shareholding mandates passive attribution under the CFC regime; (iii) GST group registration election under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 allowing related captive insurance group companies to file as a single GST group; (iv) hybrid mismatch elections in Part FM providing for timing/taxable-base alignment adjustments for hybrid financial instruments and hybrid entities; (v) election under section FC 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 to apply the branch equivalent rules for CFCs in certain cases.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period (including remote services supplied to New Zealand consumers); voluntary registration permitted for entities carrying on a taxable activity

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies qualify for 0% GST if: (a) goods are physically exported from New Zealand within 28 days of the supply, or within such extended period as the Commissioner accepts (section 11(1)(a)/(b) GST Act 1985); (b) services supplied to a non-resident customer are performed wholly or partly outside New Zealand and not in connection with land in New Zealand (section 11(2)(a)/(c)); (c) reinsurance premium supplies are zero-rated where the underlying insured risk is located outside New Zealand and the supply is to a non-resident reinsurance party in accordance with section 11(1)(m)/(mb) and the associated 'Risk Insured in New Zealand' determination; (d) the supply is to a GST-registered non-resident recipient who will not use the goods or services in New Zealand. For captive reinsurance, the dominant factor is clear written evidence of the offshore risk location, the non-resident status of the reinsured, and a valid contractual chain demonstrating foreign commercial benefit.

  • Customs Export Entry / Electronic Export Declaration lodged via the New Zealand Customs Trade Single Window (TTSW) or approved service provider
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from New Zealand
  • Inward payment evidence: bank SWIFT confirmation, Remittance Certificate, or Telegraphic Transfer advice showing foreign settlement within the commercial term
  • Reinsurance contract or certificate of participation with the policy schedule identifying the offshore risk location, premium amount, and coverage period
  • Non-resident status certificate (e.g., IR582 or equivalent) or certified tax residency documentation of the reinsured counterparty
  • Insurance broker statements or loss-fund accounts demonstrating that claims and underwriting reserves are held outside New Zealand

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory ROAM/API clearance exists. Mandatory B2G electronic invoicing via the Peppol PINT-IT framework applies since 31 March 2022 for sales invoices to New Zealand government departments. GST-registered suppliers must issue tax invoices within 28 days of supply, must retain a registered-for-GST status, and file GST returns electronically via myIR with a compliance period of one/two/six-monthly (GST101/GST102/GST103) based on turnover. Use of interchange XML or Peppol UBL standard is permitted; no national continuous transaction control (CTC) system.

Under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a false or inadequate zero-rating claim exposes the captive SPV to reassessment at the 15% GST rate on the full premium plus use-of-money interest. Penalties escalate with culpability: 'not reasonably diligent' shortfall penalty at 20% of the tax shortfall; 'unacceptable tax position' at 40%; 'abusive tax position' at 100%; and 'evasion or similar act' at 150% plus prosecution for tax evasion (liable to a fine not exceeding NZD 50,000 for a company, and imprisonment up to 5 years, with confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1991). The Commissioner has a 4-year reassessment window from the tax return date, extended to 7 years where evasion is established, and IRD can issue group/related-party adjustments under transfer pricing (Part FG4 or s GC33) to reallocate premium income to New Zealand at a 28% corporate rate if the risk is economically borne by a New Zealand resident. New Zealand imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline after full liberalisation of foreign exchange controls; the 180-day benchmark is a documented policy proxy for IRD audit acceptance of premium collection, excess of loss and reserve funding flows.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility of the New Zealand dollar. There are no general exchange controls, nor any requirement for prior central bank approval before transferring capital into or out of New Zealand. Profit and dividend remittances may be made in any freely usable currency, and no remittance quota applies. The only legal frictions arise from Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) customer due diligence, New Zealand tax obligations such as Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT), sanctions screening, and the Overseas Investment Act restrictions where a foreign investor is acquiring sensitive land or significant business assets. The captive reinsurance SPV, being a financial-sector entity with a medium-risk banking profile, must maintain complete source-of-funds records, audited financial evidence, and a demonstrable substance file to avoid delays in routine cross-border distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters, captive reinsurers, and multinational treasury centres are free to retain foreign-currency receipts in onshore or offshore accounts and may convert them at market-determined exchange rates without Reserve Bank approval. The absence of surrender rules means that all underwriting premiums, claims recoverables, and investment returns can be held in original currency or hedged as the board directs. Settlement banks nevertheless apply their own AML/CFT thresholds and may request supporting invoices, policies, and reserve schedules for exceptionally large incoming wires.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua); with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) administering the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) regulating foreign direct investment in sensitive New Zealand assets.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as the primary treasury rail. Offshore digital e-money institutions and financial-technology payment providers do not hold banking licences issued by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand and cannot provide protected domestic clearing through New Zealand's exchange settlement system. An offshore EMI may support ancillary receivables, premium collection, or continuous-linked-settlement overlay only if it operates through a New Zealand registered financial service provider and maintains a correspondent or segregation arrangement with an authorised New Zealand bank. The captive reinsurance SPV should treat fintech EMIs as temporary collection points rather than the definitive clearing channel for regulatory capital, claims reserve, and treaty settlement flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections into the captive reinsurance SPV must arrive by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from the parent shareholder's regulated bank account. Field 50K/50F must identify the actual ordering corporate entity; Field 57A must name the New Zealand beneficiary bank and SWIFT BIC; Field 70 should carry an explicit narrative such as 'REINSURANCE CAPITAL INJECTION / PREMIUM FUNDING / POLICY NO ... / RESERVE CONTRIBUTION'. For statutory financial reporting and risk classification, the bank will assign a New Zealand Balance of Payments service purpose code within the insurance, reinsurance, and pension sectors. No Reserve Bank approval is required because capital is freely transferable, but the receiving bank will verify that the ordering customer matches the declared parent entity and will conduct sanctions and fraud screening. Beneficiary names on the bank account title, the incorporation certificate, and the SWIFT field 57A must match exactly; structural mismatches are a common cause of quarantine or return.The principal clearing rail for outbound trade and reinsurance settlements is SWIFT MT103 for direct premium cessions, claims reimbursement, commission payments, and loan repayments. For reinsurance security or statutory collateral in favour of a ceding insurer, a New Zealand bank may issue an MT760 standby letter of credit or an MT700 commercial documentary credit. Domestic NZD legs between New Zealand settlement accounts may be cleared through the Exchange Settlement System operated by the Reserve Bank, while foreign-currency legs settle via the issuing and correspondent banks. Payment instructions should specify charges as 'OUR' on MT103 so that the beneficiary fully receives the reinsurance claim amount without deduction. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium as the primary treasury rail. Offshore digital e-money institutions and financial-technology payment providers do not hold banking licences issued by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand and cannot provide protected domestic clearing through New Zealand's exchange settlement system. An offshore EMI may support ancillary receivables, premium collection, or continuous-linked-settlement overlay only if it operates through a New Zealand registered financial service provider and maintains a correspondent or segregation arrangement with an authorised New Zealand bank. The captive reinsurance SPV should treat fintech EMIs as temporary collection points rather than the definitive clearing channel for regulatory capital, claims reserve, and treaty settlement flows.
