Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) — officially 'Inland Revenue' / 'Te Tari Taake'; statutory digital portal is 'myIR' (https://www.ird.govt.nz), with parallel business identity registration via the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) portal (https://www.nzbn.govt.nz) operated by the Companies Office under the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain an NZBN if the CK_IC International Company is incorporated in New Zealand, carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand, or has a physical place of business in New Zealand; if the company is wholly offshore and has no NZ taxable activity, an NZBN is not a prerequisite. Step 2: Submit Form IR596 (Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application) or, where the company is close-held, Form IR742, to Inland Revenue via a registered New Zealand tax agent, by post, or through the myIR non-resident entity registration channel. The application must include a certified copy of the certificate of incorporation, the constitution/articles of association, the register of directors and shareholders, and a declaration of the entity's tax residence and permanent establishment status. Step 3: Upon issuance of the IRD number, the entity must assess GST registration obligations: if total taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period are expected to exceed NZD 60,000 — including remote services (e.g., automated subtitle transcription, AI voice dubbing) supplied to New Zealand consumers — the company must apply to register for GST in myIR. Step 4: Register for PAYE, FBT, and KiwiSaver only if the IC obtains New Zealand resident employees; for a purely offshore service desk with non-resident contractors, no payroll registration is required. Step 5: Appoint an authorised agent in New Zealand for service of notices where the company has no fixed establishment; this is administratively recommended and does not in itself create a permanent establishment. No minimum capital subscription or local bank account is required for IRD number issuance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The CK_IC (International Company) is classified as a non-resident company under section YD 2 of the Income Tax Act 2007, with central management and control outside New Zealand and no New Zealand permanent establishment. The company is taxable only on New Zealand-sourced income that is effectively connected with a New Zealand taxable activity (sections YD 4, BD 1); all foreign-source trading profits, including revenue from offshore post-production, subtitle transcription, and AI voice-dubbing services delivered to foreign clients, fall entirely outside the New Zealand tax net and are non-assessable and exempt. The Tax_Exempt_Zone status does not automatically confer fiscal transparency; absent a valid Look-Through Company election, the entity is liable at the 28% corporate rate only on any NZ-sourced business income, has no New Zealand capital duty, no wealth tax, no stamp duty, and no branch profit remittance tax. As a pure non-resident with no NZ-sourced income, the effective NZ tax liability is nil.

Available election forms

(1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007: a close-held company (five or fewer ultimate shareholders, no corporate shareholder) may elect to be fiscally transparent, with profits and losses attributed to shareholders — the functional equivalent of a US check-the-box election; the election is effected via form IR861 and requires all shareholders to sign. (2) GST group election under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985: permits a non-resident IC and its New Zealand GST-registered related entities to file a single consolidated GST return. (3) CFC branch-equivalent and de minimis elections under Subparts CQ/EX of the Income Tax Act 2007: available to New Zealand-resident shareholders to mitigate attribution on controlled foreign companies; not relevant to the CK_IC itself but relevant to any NZ-resident parent. (4) Trans-Tasman branch election for Australian-resident companies to be treated as having a New Zealand permanent establishment; not applicable to the Cook Islands-style IC absent an Australian nexus. (5) Hybrid mismatch elections: New Zealand does not provide a discretionary 'check-the-box' for hybrid entities; instead, the hybrid and branch mismatch rules in Part FM (sections FM 1–FM 16) and the investor/group mismatch rules in Subparts FH and FO operate mandatorily to deny deductions or impose withholding tax to neutralise double non-taxation. No special statutory election is required to activate the Tax_Exempt_Zone status; it arises automatically from non-residence and absence of NZ-sourced taxable activity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (annual taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period; the same threshold applies to both resident suppliers and non-resident suppliers of 'remote services' — including digital post-production, automated subtitle transcription, and AI voice-dubbing services — supplied to GST-registered New Zealand businesses and New Zealand consumers; once the threshold is exceeded, registration is compulsory within 21 days; voluntary registration is available to non-residents, but input tax recovery is limited to supplies attributable to New Zealand taxable activities).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under sections 11 and 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, the CK_IC may claim 0% GST on: (a) goods physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier within 28 days of the supply (or within such longer period as the Commissioner allows); (b) services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not supplied to any person in New Zealand in connection with the same supply — this covers automated subtitle transcription, AI voice cloning, and audio dubbing delivered electronically to offshore film studios, streaming platforms, and foreign distributors; (c) 'remote services' (electronically supplied services) received by a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, where the recipient's GST registration is not being used in connection with the supply; and (d) services that are directly connected with movable property that is outside New Zealand when the services are performed. Zero-rating is denied where the service is supplied to a New Zealand resident, where the services relate to land in New Zealand, where the services are performed while the recipient is physically present in New Zealand, or where the supply is part of a deliberate scheme to avoid GST. Evidence of non-residency includes the customer's overseas address, IP address, bank jurisdiction, and contractual warranties.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed electronically via the Trade Single Window / New Zealand Customs Service (required only for physical media, master files on tangible carriers, or any accompanying physical deliverables; for pure digital delivery, a goods export declaration is not applicable and the documented digital delivery trail stands in its place)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (for tangible export of mastered files or physical promotional materials; must be dated within the statutory zero-rating window)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a New Zealand registered bank confirming settlement in foreign currency and the receipt of export proceeds into the IC's offshore or onshore account
  • Zero-rated GST tax invoice and the underlying service agreement/PO with the non-resident client, including the client's overseas jurisdiction of establishment and warranty of non-residence
  • Technical delivery logs, IP geolocation evidence, and email/digital handover records demonstrating that the subtitle and AI-voice deliverables were supplied to, and used by, a recipient outside New Zealand

New Zealand operates a voluntary PAN-NZ e-invoicing framework based on the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 specification (NZ e-Invoicing, supported by MyInvoicing and other PEPPOL access points). There is no real-time fiscal clearance regime, no government pre-clearance API, no mandatory digital stamping, and no continuous transaction controls with Inland Revenue. All registered suppliers, including the CK_IC, must issue a GST tax invoice within 28 days of the time of supply (section 24, GST Act 1985); the invoice must contain the supplier's name, GST number, issue date, description, consideration, and the GST-inclusive/exclusive amounts. Zero-rated export invoices must contain an explicit statement that the supply is zero-rated and the basis for that treatment. E-invoicing becomes mandatory only for suppliers transacting with certain central government agencies; no private-sector mandate currently exists. Inland Revenue performs ex-post data-matching audit analysis rather than transaction-level clearance.

Inland Revenue applies targeted compliance scrutiny to zero-rated export claims by small offshore-facing entities, including verification of the non-resident status of the recipient, the place of supply, and the receipt of foreign currency. Where a zero-rating claim is unsupported or the Commissioner establishes a deliberate arrangement to mischaracterise domestic supplies as exports, the following consequences arise under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the GST Act 1985: (1) reversal of the zero-rating with a default assessment of GST at 15%, plus use-of-money interest from the original due date; (2) shortfall penalties under section 141: 20% for 'lack of reasonable care', 40% for 'unacceptable tax position', and up to 150% for 'tax evasion' or 'obstructive conduct'; (3) an additional 100% 'abusive tax position' penalty may be stacked where the dominant purpose is to defeat the intent of the GST Act; (4) criminal prosecution in the District Court for knowingly filing false GST returns, with fines up to NZD 50,000 for each offence and potential imprisonment under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994; (5) the promoter penalty regime under section 43A can reach promoters of fraudulent export schemes with penalties of up to NZD 100,000; and (6) retrospective clawback of any GST refunds paid, with statutory interest accruing. For the CK_IC, the safest posture is to maintain the full documentation bundle, evidence foreign currency settlement, and comply with the 180-day repatriation benchmark to avoid any inference of unreturned export proceeds or sham exportation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility is the baseline: New Zealand operates a free-floating, market-determined NZD regime with no exchange-control licensing and no distinction between current and capital account approvals. For a non-resident-owned International Company in the Realm of New Zealand, invoicing out of NZD or USD, profit repatriation, intercompany loans, and service-fee remittances may proceed without RBNZ clearance. The operative constraints are non-FX filters: (i) the Overseas Investment Act 2005 applies only to sensitive land or significant business assets, not to post-production AI services; (ii) Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) at 15% applies to outbound dividends unless reduced by an applicable double-tax agreement; and (iii) bank-level AML/CFT documentation of source of funds is mandatory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender rules in New Zealand. Export proceeds from subtitle transcription and AI dubbing services may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency, converted at any time at commercial interbank rates, or offset against import disbursements. No mandatory conversion ratio or time limit exists; the only practical requirement is accurate BOP/transaction purpose codes captured by the collecting bank for statistical reporting.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High as a payment gateway, Medium as a stand-alone liquidity rail. Wise and Stripe are compliant Financial Service Providers (FSP) in New Zealand under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Disputes Resolution) Act 2008 and are eligible for account-to-account payouts, but they are not registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 and therefore cannot hold long-term concentration risk, structured deposits, or provide letters of credit. Legal constraint: all remittances must be linked to identifiable underlying transactions to satisfy AML/CFT custody obligations; they are best used as collection rails with daily sweeps into a tier-1 bank account.
