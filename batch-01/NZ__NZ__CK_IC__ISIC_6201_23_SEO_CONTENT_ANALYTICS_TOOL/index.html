Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by New Zealand law. Shares may be issued at no par value and for such consideration as the Board determines, comprising cash, services, or property; consideration may be recorded as fully or partly paid. No escrow account, capital deposit, or notarised capital verification is required. A statutory declaration of solvency is not required for incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: For operations with a physical leased office: (1) Fire Safety - a Fire Evacuation Scheme must be approved by Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017 where the workforce exceeds the prescribed threshold; (2) Structural Load Clearance - the landlord must hold a Building Warrant of Fitness (BWOF) for the premises under the Building Act 2004, including annual inspection certificates for means of escape, fire alarms, and emergency lighting; (3) Workplace Clearance - the Company must register with WorkSafe NZ if operating within an ACC WorkPlace Safety scheme, appoint a health and safety officer, and implement a written worker-participation system under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015. For a fully remote/virtual operation, no physical building clearance or civil defence consent is required; the Company's sole occupational duty is compliance with general HSWA duties and emergency planning for any home-office or co-working arrangement.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by New Zealand law. Shares may be issued at no par value and for such consideration as the Board determines, comprising cash, services, or property; consideration may be recorded as fully or partly paid. No escrow account, capital deposit, or notarised capital verification is required. A statutory declaration of solvency is not required for incorporation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the CK_IC International Company is a non-resident company associated with a tax-exempt international company regime within the Realm of New Zealand; for New Zealand income tax purposes it is an entity-level, non-resident corporate taxpayer subject only to New Zealand-source income tax (territorial scope) and has no tax liability on foreign-source SEO analytics SaaS revenue unless a New Zealand permanent establishment or an attributable dependent-agent arrangement exists. If the same entity were incorporated as a domestic New Zealand company, the default classification would instead be Entity_Level_Taxation at the 28% corporate rate.

Available election forms

New Zealand has no US-style check-the-box election. Domestic companies are taxed at entity level with an imputation credit account. Election and self-classification mechanisms available under New Zealand law include: (i) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, allowing a qualifying close-held company to be fiscally transparent for income tax; (ii) grandfathered Qualifying Company elections (closed to new applicants); (iii) Portfolio Investment Entity (PIE) election for qualifying managed investment vehicles; (iv) Māori Authority elections for certain collective investment and business structures; and (v) controlled foreign company (CFC) attributable-interest and asset elections for New Zealand-resident shareholders. Hybrid mismatch elections are not discretionary: the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 enacts BEPS Action 2 rules (subpart FH) that automatically deny deductions or require inclusion where a cross-border hybrid instrument, hybrid entity, or imported mismatch would otherwise produce double non-taxation; a Tax_Exempt_Zone IC cannot use its foreign tax exemption to create an unintended New Zealand deduction exclusion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalized and freely floating currency regime with no exchange controls. Both current account and capital account transactions are fully convertible; there is no requirement for central bank approval to convert NZD into foreign currency or to repatriate profits, dividends, loan repayments, or capital. Non-resident investors and offshore entities such as a Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC) may freely remit funds and hold foreign-currency accounts. Profits remitted overseas are subject only to applicable New Zealand non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) or an approved issuer levy (AIL) regime, not FX licensing hurdles. The NZ dollar is a deliverable currency in the global FX market, and there are no surrender restrictions on capital inflows or outflows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters and digital service providers may retain 100% of export proceeds in any currency of their choice, whether held onshore in a New Zealand foreign-currency account or offshore. The only compliance requirements are statistical and AML related: banks reporting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Reporting regime must record transfers of NZD 15,000 or equivalent, and the Reserve Bank collects balance-of-payments data. There is no obligation to sell export earnings to a domestic bank, no compulsory liquidation of FX balances, and no forced repatriation of offshore receivables.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) and the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for market conduct and AML/CFT supervision; the Reserve Bank holds statutory authority over registered banks and payment system stability.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High, subject to New Zealand's financial service provider registration framework. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may provide payment services into New Zealand only if they are registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, and are supervised by the FMA. A full 'banking license' is not required for merchant acquiring or pay-out services, but the EMI must have an approved dispute resolution scheme and be registered with the Department of Internal Affairs for AML/CFT if it engages in the business of providing money or value transfer services. Legal constraints include the requirement to minimize regulatory arbitrage, the inability to take New Zealand deposits without becoming a registered bank or non-bank deposit taker, and the need for an NZ agent or registered office. Using Stripe and Paddle as payment rails is viable, but treasury-grade multi-currency accounts should be held with a tier-1 licensed New Zealand bank to ensure segregated client funds and cleared FX access.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends paid by a New Zealand company to a non-resident, subject to DTA reduction typically to 15% or lower.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a standard 31 March balance date, the IR4 must be filed by 7 July (non-terminal filing) but terminal tax is generally due by 7 April following the end of the income year; the exact return filing deadline is notified by Inland Revenue and may be extended to 31 March of the following year for agents. For non-standard balance dates, the return is due on the 7th day of the second month after the balance date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand transfer pricing documentation is mandatory for taxpayers with related-party cross-border transactions exceeding NZD 1 million per annum; in particular, TP documentation must be prepared and disclosed via IR15 (International Dealing Schedule) if related party transactions exceed NZD 1 million per transaction category. There is no separate local file/master file threshold expressed in NZ law, but TP documentation is required under the Income Tax Act 2007 to substantiate arm's length outcomes.

Economic substance scope: The general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) in the Income Tax Act 2007 applies; additionally, New Zealand's transfer pricing rules require economic substance and real commercial rationale. There is no dedicated ESR statute, but a non-resident International Company with no substance may face challenge as a conduit or sham.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under section 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994: imprisonment up to 5 years and/or a fine up to NZD 50,000. Civil shortfall penalty up to 150% of evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for tax shortfalls as officers involved in evasion, and personal fines or imprisonment may be imposed; the corporate veil may be pierced where the company is an alter ego.
Shell Company / Non-Operating Entity

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the NZ-resident status under tie-breaker rules; application of transfer pricing adjustments; denial of treaty benefits; shortfall penalty up to 40% for lack of reasonable care or 100% for taking an unacceptable tax position.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structured the sham arrangement may be personally liable under the Companies Act for fraudulent conduct; courts may impose compensation and costs.
AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: For entities not financial institutions, no direct AML/CFT penalty unless engaged in financial services; if the token is not financial advice, likely low risk. If deemed financial services, penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 up to NZD 2 million for companies, plus remediation orders.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties up to NZD 400,000 if they knowingly participate in or allow a serious breach of AML/CFT obligations.
Interest Deduction Restrictions / Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of interest deductions to the extent the entity's debt-to-asset ratio exceeds the 60% thin capitalisation threshold; adjustment of taxable income and imposition of shortfall penalty of 20% for unacceptable interpretation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable if they knowingly sign incorrect returns, including potential criminal liability under the Tax Administration Act if false declarations are made.
