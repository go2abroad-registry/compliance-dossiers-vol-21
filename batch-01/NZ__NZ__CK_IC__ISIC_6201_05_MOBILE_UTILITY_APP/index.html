Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under New Zealand law. Shares may be issued with no par value, and the consideration fixed by the board; no escrow account, capital lock-up, or paid-up capital deposit requirement applies.

Funding & unlock path: Compliance is required under the Building Act 2004 for any occupied office premises: a Building Warrant of Fitness (BWOF) must be current where specified systems such as fire alarms, automatic sprinklers, or mechanical ventilation are present. Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) requires an evacuation scheme to be approved and maintained where staff numbers exceed statutory thresholds. WorkSafe New Zealand and the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 mandate hazard identification, worker engagement, welfare facilities, and notifiable event reporting. Local territorial authority consent for building use and structural load inspections must be confirmed by the landlord or tenant before ongoing office operation.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under New Zealand law. Shares may be issued with no par value, and the consideration fixed by the board; no escrow account, capital lock-up, or paid-up capital deposit requirement applies.

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) / Te Tari Taake; secure taxpayer portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz). Supporting registries: New Zealand Companies Office (NZBN for registered companies) and New Zealand Customs Service (export declarations).

E-government filing portal

Confirm non-resident status: under s YC 15 Income Tax Act 2007, a company is New Zealand tax-resident if it is incorporated in New Zealand or if its centre of management (control and management of the business) is exercised in New Zealand. The CK_IC is an overseas-incorporated International Company; provided central management and control remains offshore, it is a non-resident company. 2. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident International Company without a New Zealand permanent establishment and without New Zealand-sourced income; New Zealand income-tax neutrality for offshore App Store revenue; GST exposure limited to New Zealand-consumer remote-service supplies, generally collected by the marketplace operator).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid entity classification election exists in New Zealand. Entity tax treatment is determined by statute under the Income Tax Act 2007 and the CFC/FIF rules; a foreign entity is classified according to its foreign legal status and New Zealand source taxation principles. Statutory elections available to a non-resident company include: (i) voluntary GST registration under s 51(3) GST Act 1986; (ii) GST group registration under s 55 GST Act 1986 (with a New Zealand-resident group member); (iii) functional-currency election for financial arrangements under s DN 2 Income Tax Act 2007, subject to IRD consent; and (iv) hybrid and branch mismatch neutralisation is applied automatically under subpart FH (Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018) without an elective check-the-box mechanism. The look-through company (LTC) election is unavailable because the entity is non-resident and LTC status requires a New Zealand-resident close-held company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (GST registration threshold for any person carrying on a taxable activity in any 12-month period; the same NZD 60,000 threshold applies to offshore suppliers of remote services and low-value goods supplied to New Zealand consumers). Supplies of mobile utility apps to New Zealand consumers through Apple App Store / Google Play are generally treated as made by the marketplace operator, which collects GST at 15%; the underlying non-resident developer may therefore remain below the registration threshold where all sales are effected through such marketplaces.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: s 11 GST Act 1986 zero-rates goods that the supplier exports, provided the goods are exported within 28 days of the time of supply and the supplier holds prescribed export evidence. Services/digital products: s 11A/11B zero-rate services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not supplied in connection with New Zealand land or goods physically present in New Zealand; services performed wholly outside New Zealand are zero-rated. For mobile utility apps sold through Apple App Store / Google Play: the developer's supply to the non-resident marketplace operator is zero-rated as a remote service to a non-resident, while the marketplace operator is deemed to make the supply to New Zealand consumers and must charge 15% GST. To substantiate zero-rating, the developer must retain evidence of the marketplace operator's non-resident status, the app-store distribution agreements, and settlement/payout reports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (lodged via Trade Single Window / New Zealand Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (evidencing physical export within 28 days of supply under s 11(1)(b) GST Act 1986)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advices (evidencing receipt of export proceeds within the recognised repatriation window)
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (only where preferential origin treatment is claimed)
  • Digital-services substantiation pack (app-store settlement reports, marketplace operator contracts, recipient non-residency evidence, and IP ownership records)

New Zealand does not impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-approval, or digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates on the PEPPOL network through certified service providers; invoices must nevertheless satisfy the GST tax-invoice content requirements (s 24 GST Act 1986) and be retained for 7 years. GST returns and payments are filed electronically through myIR; no API-based fiscal-authority pre-clearance is required.

Inland Revenue may reopen GST assessments within the statutory time bar (generally 4 years from the end of the relevant GST period) and claw back any zero-rated refund together with use-of-money interest where export evidence is deficient or the statutory zero-rating criteria are not met. Shortfall penalties apply under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for an unacceptable tax position or failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness (abusive tax position), and 150% for evasion or fraudulent intent. Fraudulent GST evasion also carries criminal liability (fines and/or imprisonment). Exporters must retain all zero-rating supporting records for 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility, having accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) floats freely, and there are no statutory barriers to the repatriation of dividends, capital, loan repayments, or distributed profits by non-resident shareholders of a New Zealand-incorporated International Company. Digital service export revenue, including Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlements, is fully convertible without ministerial consent; the only practical frictions are tax withholding obligations (NRWT/RWT), AML/CFT reporting thresholds, and standard correspondent-bank compliance procedures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligations. There is no mandatory statutory timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no requirement to deposit export receipts into a specified domestic account. Cross-border digital app-store earnings received in USD, EUR, and other foreign currencies may be retained indefinitely in multi-currency operational accounts, segregated into capital escrow sub-accounts, or sold on the open market at prevailing commercial rates without any Reserve Bank notification or licence.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), operating under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989, the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) as conduct regulator; New Zealand maintains a freely floating exchange-rate regime with no centralised exchange-control authority or foreign-investment approval body for standard trade transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low as a primary or sole banking relationship. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex) are not registered banks in New Zealand, are not members of the Reserve Bank's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESB), and cannot provide NZD settlement finality or local clearing. They are viable strictly as auxiliary cross-border payment and FX-conversion corridors for app-store payouts, subject to the core treasury and escrow balances being held with an NZ registered bank. Legal constraints include the absence of deposit-taking authorisation under the Deposit Takers Act 2023 and the application of enhanced due diligence when EMI channel funding originates from jurisdictions without equivalent AML/CFT regimes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital routing utilises the SWIFT MT103 wire protocol from the CK parent's corporate bank to the NZ IC's operational or capital-escrow account, referencing the appropriate Balance of Payments (BOP) purpose code for direct-investment equity injection. The beneficiary NZ bank performs 'name and account' matching against the profile, applies automatic suspicious-transaction reporting for cash-equivalent instruments, and initiates Enhanced Customer Due Diligence (ECDD) for injections exceeding NZD 50,000. The verification dossier must accompany the wire as a scanned payment instruction: share-allotment board resolution, source-of-funds bank statement, certificate of incorporation of the remitter, ultimate beneficial owner declaration, and an import/export or service contract if the capital is linked to future app monetisation. Where the injection exceeds NZD 500,000, banks may require a pre-approval notice and a clearing-certificate from the correspondent bank; funds are credited only after finality is received through the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESB), not on provisional advice.Outbound trade disbursements clear through layered rails: SWIFT MT103 for cross-border supplier wires; Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits subject to UCP600) for hardware procurement and IP-licence acquisitions; and domestic NZD payments via the Reserve Bank's ESB through the clearing member bank. App-store settlement proceeds from Apple IAP and Google Play Billing are received via Apple's and Google's global treasury rails in USD, EUR, or local currencies into the NZ IC's multi-currency account, then pooled and converted into NZD through same-day FX swaps for operating disbursements. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local file documentation required for cross-border related-party transactions exceeding NZ$10 million in total, or NZ$1 million for transactions involving services, interest, royalties, or intangibles. Master file required for multinational groups with annual revenue over NZ$100 million and cross-border related-party transactions above those thresholds. Country-by-Country reporting applies where group revenue exceeds EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate separate Economic Substance Regulations like the UAE or BVI. Instead, the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1, Income Tax Act 2007) and transfer pricing rules (s GC 6) deny tax benefits where an International Company lacks substantive presence and real decision-making in its claimed jurisdiction of residence. For a mobile utility app (ISIC 6201), the IP owner must perform DEMPE functions (development, enhancement, maintenance, protection, exploitation) with actual personnel and decision-making where the IP returns are booked. The BEPS Multilateral Instrument's principal purpose test may also deny treaty benefits under the NZ–Cook Islands DTA if obtaining treaty relief was the primary purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Avoidance

Statutory sanction: GAAR applies: the Commissioner re-characterises the arrangement and assesses the full New Zealand-source income at 28%, plus use-of-money interest. Shortfall penalty: 150% of the tax shortfall for evasion, 40% for unacceptable tax position, 20% for lack of reasonable care. Criminal conviction for evasion can result in imprisonment up to 4 years.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in or directing the evasion can be personally prosecuted under the Tax Administration Act 1994 (fine up to NZ$50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years). They are liable for company debts under the Companies Act 1993 if the company trades recklessly or fraudulently, and may face departure prohibition orders, asset restraint, and personal restitution orders.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The entity may be disregarded as a sham; the New Zealand Inland Revenue can assess the underlying economic activity in New Zealand and deny DTA benefits under the principal purpose test. Penalties: 40% unacceptable tax position or 150% evasion penalty, plus interest and recharacterisation of all distributions as taxable dividends subject to NRWT.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced: directors are personally liable for tax debts, unlawful distributions, and fraudulent trading. The High Court may order repayment of revenue and punitive damages; directors face travel bans, personal bankruptcy, and extradition for serious tax fraud.
AML / Banking Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to NZ$10 million for a body corporate and NZ$200,000 for individuals; criminal liability for money laundering under the Crimes Act 1961 carries imprisonment up to 7 years. Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze accounts and restrain proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are individually liable for breaches of the AML/CFT programme; criminal convictions can lead to imprisonment up to 7 years and fines. Management bans up to 10 years may be imposed, and directors may be personally liable for civil penalties for losses caused to creditors or beneficiaries.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may adjust the taxable profits to arm's length, disallow intercompany deductions, and impose a 20% shortfall penalty for non-documentation (or higher for gross carelessness or evasion). The taxpayer bears the burden of proof without documentation. Penalties can reach 150% of the shortfall.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign returns without reasonable basis face personal penalty risk, including a 100% penalty for gross carelessness under s 141A or 150% for evasion. They may be personally liable for breaches of director duties under the Companies Act 1993, be required to restore misapplied amounts, and be disqualified from management.
