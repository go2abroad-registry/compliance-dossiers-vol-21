Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (Inland Revenue Department; Te Tari Taake) via the myIR secure online portal at https://www.ird.govt.nz; New Zealand Companies Office (New Zealand Business Number regime) for local branch registration; New Zealand Customs Service (Te Mana Arai o Aotearoa / Customs) as the border authority for export clearance and export substantiation.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident offshore International Company has no automatic statutory New Zealand IRD number. The TIN/GST registration obligation arises only upon a defined statutory nexus. Procedure: (1) Confirm nexus, being New Zealand-source income, a fixed place of business or permanent establishment in New Zealand, or a GST taxable activity conducted in or into New Zealand including remote or digital services supplied to New Zealand-resident consumers; (2) If nexus exists, file form IR596 (Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application) or IR742 (Company IRD Number Application), attaching certified certificate of incorporation, certificate of good standing, constitutional documents, board resolution appointing a New Zealand agent or resident contact, and evidence of the registered address; (3) If the entity carries on a GST taxable activity, apply simultaneously for GST registration via the same myIR application using form GST1, which is compulsory when taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period and is available voluntarily below that threshold; (4) If the entity establishes a physical place of business, it must first register the overseas company with the New Zealand Companies Office to obtain an NZBN, then obtain the IRD number and GST number; (5) Where the International Company is administered offshore, has no New Zealand-source income, has no New Zealand permanent establishment, and does not supply remote services to New Zealand consumers above the GST threshold, no New Zealand IRD number or GST registration is required and the entity remains outside the New Zealand tax registration net.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope / Tax_Exempt_Zone — New Zealand imposes entity-level corporate income tax and does not permit pass-through for companies. Taxation is residence-based for New Zealand-resident companies and source-based for non-resident companies. The offshore International Company, being incorporated outside New Zealand, effectively managed and controlled outside New Zealand, and deriving no New Zealand-source income, qualifies as a Tax-Exempt Zone entity with respect to New Zealand income tax, provided no permanent establishment exists in New Zealand.

Available election forms

New Zealand has no US-style check-the-box election. The relevant statutory elections are: (1) deemed-resident election under section YD 4(2) of the Income Tax Act 2007, allowing a non-resident company resident in a double-tax-agreement partner state to elect New Zealand residence for DTA purposes where the treaty tie-breaker would otherwise place residence outside New Zealand; (2) GST grouping election under sections 55 to 57 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, enabling related companies to be treated as a single GST group with one registration; (3) GST accounting-basis elections among the invoice, payments, and hybrid bases under section 20 of the GST Act; (4) GST attribution and adjustment elections for bad debts and debtors; (5) statutory documentation of hybrid entity classification for the purposes of the hybrid mismatch rules in sections FH 1 to FH 14 of the Income Tax Act 2007, which apply by default and are not subject to a general elective override. Pass-through elections such as the look-through company and partnership transparency regimes are not available to an offshore International Company and would be disregarded by Inland Revenue for a non-resident entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies (including goods, services, and remote or digital services) supplied in or into New Zealand in any 12-month period; the same NZD 60,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of remote services supplied to New Zealand-resident consumers where the recipient is not GST-registered; voluntary registration is available below the statutory threshold and, once registered, the supplier must remain registered for a minimum period unless the taxable activity ceases.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11(1) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a supply of goods is zero-rated where the supplier exports the goods before the time of supply or within 28 days after the time of supply and retains sufficient evidence of physical removal from New Zealand. Parcel consolidation, sorting, and direct-injection linehaul services supplied in respect of international export freight are zero-rated as services supplied in connection with the international transport of goods; offshore services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed may be zero-rated under section 11A of the GST Act. The parcel-consolidation hub must obtain and preserve the export documentation bundle detailed below to substantiate each zero-rated transaction, must ensure that goods are physically exported within the 28-day statutory window, and must account for input tax adjustments if exported goods are subsequently returned to New Zealand or diverted for domestic consumption before retail delivery.

  • New Zealand Customs electronic export declaration or export entry (submitted through the NZCS Trade Single Window / Jet Cargo system)
  • Signed ocean bill of lading or master/house air waybill evidencing loading for carriage to the destination country
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT / payment-rails settlement evidence (Airwallex or local clearing FX confirmation) demonstrating repatriation of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list for each consolidated parcel
  • Export consignment manifest and hub consolidation receipts / sortation reports
  • International transport contract and freight-forwarding service agreement
  • IRD zero-rated supply log stating the time of supply, export date, destination port, and the 28-day export compliance date
  • Customs clearance status (export permit / entry release) and, where applicable, the NZ Customs export transaction report (eTR)

New Zealand does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance regime, a continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate, or a digital stamping requirement. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the New Zealand e-Invoicing network based on the Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 standard. The Government New Zealand (GNS) e-invoicing initiative requires central government agencies to accept Peppol-compliant e-invoices, which in practice compels suppliers contracting with government agencies to issue e-invoices through a certified Peppol access point. Inland Revenue accepts electronic tax invoices and retains an optional Gateway API and service-provider interface for mechanical GST return filing and data exchange, but invoice clearance remains ex post and audit-based. Registered persons must issue valid GST tax invoices (including buyer-created tax invoices where the election is made), retain all records in a form accessible to Inland Revenue, and keep records for the statutory 7-year retention period under section 18(2) of the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Inland Revenue and New Zealand Customs jointly audit export zero-rating claims, and the supplier bears the evidentiary burden. Failure to substantiate export within the statutory 7-year record-retention period reverses the zero rating and triggers a GST reassessment at 15% on the full supply value, plus use-of-money interest from the original due date and Part 7 shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for failure to take reasonable care or an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion under section 141E. Retrospective reassessment is permitted for four years, extended to ten years for non-disclosed abusive or unacceptable positions, and unlimited in cases of fraud or evasion. Export refunds are clawed back with interest, fraudulent export zero-rating is prosecuted under sections 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act with fines up to NZD 250,000 for a body corporate and imprisonment for individuals, and the Commissioner may deregister the GST account, publish anti-abuse determinations, and pursue directors for knowing participation in the scheme.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a freely floating currency with full current-account and capital-account convertibility; no exchange control approval or prior authorization is required for trade payments, service fees, loan repayments, dividends, or repatriation of capital to non-residents. The Overseas Investment Act 2005 applies only to foreign acquisitions of sensitive residential or lifestyle land, fishing quota, and significant business assets above statutory thresholds (NZD 100 million for non-treaty investors); it does not restrict the conversion of NZD or the timing or volume of FX flows. Profit and dividend remittance from a New Zealand-paying counterparty to a non-resident payee is permitted without central bank approval, subject to non-resident withholding tax at treaty rates and compliance with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. For a Cook Islands International Company enjoying exempt-tax status, the principal treasury consideration is establishing substantive business purpose in the origin market to avoid re-characterization as a New Zealand tax-resident enterprise.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no export proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory time limit for repatriation of export receipts. Exporters may freely hold foreign currency balances in onshore or offshore bank accounts, and natural hedging through matched currency revenues and costs is expressly permissible. The only reporting duty for direct foreign investment and trade flows is statistical reporting to Statistics New Zealand when quarterly transaction values exceed set thresholds; this is a statistical-only requirement and carries no enforcement of conversion.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) - the prudential regulator and issuer of New Zealand currency; responsible for licensing registered banks and supervising the domestic payments and settlement systems under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for fiduciary-grade functions (treasury consolidation, Capital Escrow), High for operational payment orchestration. Legal constraints: offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex and Wise are not registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and therefore may not take deposits from the public or brand their offerings as bank accounts; they operate in New Zealand as Financial Service Providers under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and must hold AML/CFT compliance registration with the Department of Internal Affairs. Such EMIs can hold client funds in a trust or omnibus account at a prudentially regulated partner bank, and they are suitable for multi-currency collections, local clearing injection (BECS), and SWIFT MT103 outbound instructions; however, for Capital Escrow and long-term treasury reserve purposes, a direct account at a locally incorporated registered bank must be maintained because the deposit-taking prohibition prevents the EMI from providing first-priority safety net protection.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium for fiduciary-grade functions (treasury consolidation, Capital Escrow), High for operational payment orchestration. Legal constraints: offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex and Wise are not registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and therefore may not take deposits from the public or brand their offerings as bank accounts; they operate in New Zealand as Financial Service Providers under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and must hold AML/CFT compliance registration with the Department of Internal Affairs. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory local currency threshold that automatically triggers Local File or Master File. Transfer pricing documentation is required for all cross-border related-party transactions, with penalties applying where documentation is not prepared or is inadequate. Inland Revenue may require contemporaneous documentation for transactions exceeding NZ$1 million per controlled transaction, and for related-party financing arrangements; administrative practice benchmarks the materiality threshold at a transaction value greater than NZ$500,000 per transaction or NZ$2 million in aggregate.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply the OECD Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as such, but applies the substantive economic substance doctrine through the anti-avoidance rules, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) in section BG1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, and the transfer pricing regime. The parcel consolidation hub must have real physical presence, operational staff, and business purpose in New Zealand to support its pricing arrangements, otherwise the arrangement may be void for tax purposes and penalties may apply. The CFC rules also require that income derived through a non-resident company be attributed to New Zealand residents where the company lacks substantive economic activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty of 150% of the tax shortfall for evasion or an intentional act, plus criminal prosecution. On conviction, a fine of up to NZ$50,000 against the company, and imprisonment of up to five years for the responsible officer. Inland Revenue may issue assessment and demand for unpaid tax plus interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally liable for the company's tax shortfalls where they have knowingly signed false returns or directed the evasion. The corporate veil may be pierced. An individual may be prosecuted and face imprisonment of up to five years, together with a fine of up to NZ$50,000. Courts may impose travel bans and order personal restitution of the unpaid tax, interest, and penalties.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Artificial Pricing

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to an arm's-length value plus shortfall penalty of 20% of the resulting tax shortfall for an unacceptable interpretation or 40% for gross carelessness. Inland Revenue may also impose alignment penalties where documentation is not provided within 30 days of a request.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in establishing or maintaining artificial pricing structures may be jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax shortfall. Penalties can extend to personal civil liability and, in egregious fraud cases, prosecution for tax evasion. Travel bans may be imposed pending criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Application of the GAAR under section BG1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, voiding the tax advantage and re-characterizing the arrangement. Tax shortfall penalties of 40% or more apply. Inland Revenue may deregister the company under the Companies Act 1993 for non-compliance. A direction to file corrected returns and pay the resultant tax applies.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors may have personal liability for debts of the company where the company has traded while insolvent or has been used to defeat tax obligations. Under the Companies Act 1993 sections 300-301, the court may order a director to repay or restore company property or money where the director has misapplied or retained company funds. Travel bans may be issued pending investigation.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Failure

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to NZ$2 million for a body corporate for each contravention, plus an additional NZ$200,000 per day for continuing non-compliance. Liability extends to senior managers and beneficial owners. For knowingly laundering funds, criminal conviction carries imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines of up to NZ$300,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally civilly liable for AML/CFT compliance failures where they have directed or acquiesced in the contravention. Individual criminal liability is prescribed under the AML/CFT Act, with imprisonment of up to 2 years for reckless money laundering and up to 7 years for knowing money laundering. Travel bans may be imposed and personal assets may be restrained under forfeiture orders.
