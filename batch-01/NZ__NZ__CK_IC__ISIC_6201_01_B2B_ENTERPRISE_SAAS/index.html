Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Companies, New Zealand Companies Office, Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), National Business Centre, Christchurch; online portal: companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), sections 12 to 16 (incorporation and constitution), sections 38 to 45 (issue of shares and capital), sections 189 to 194 (registered office and records), sections 201 to 206 (directors and resolutions); Companies (Forms) Regulations 1993; New Zealand Business Number Act 2016.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Incorporation of a Company under section 13 of the Companies Act 1993, lodged through the Companies Office online company registration application; the filing also generates the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) and, if requested, IRD number allocation.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 17 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a New Zealand company. The board may determine the issue price and consideration for shares under section 38 of the Companies Act 1993; no escrow account, capital deposit or undertaking is required at incorporation. A nominal issued capital of one New Zealand dollar per ordinary share is customary.

Funding & unlock path: No governmental occupational clearance is required for a low-risk office-based software company. The company must obtain a building warrant of fitness if the leased premises contain specified building systems, comply with the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, and follow Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) guidance on fire evacuation arrangements. If structural alterations are made at the premises, building consent approval from the territorial authority is required.

Paid-in capital rule: There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a New Zealand company. The board may determine the issue price and consideration for shares under section 38 of the Companies Act 1993; no escrow account, capital deposit or undertaking is required at incorporation. A nominal issued capital of one New Zealand dollar per ordinary share is customary.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) / Te Tari Taake of New Zealand; online taxpayer portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident International Company (CK_IC): Step 1 - determine whether the entity has New Zealand-source income, a New Zealand taxable activity, or GST nexus under the remote-services rules; if none, no IRD/GST registration is required. Step 2 - obtain an IRD number by filing the IR 742 entity registration form (or online myIR 'Register a business/entity' application) together with the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered office details, director and ultimate beneficial owner information, and a non-resident declaration. IRD issues a 9-digit IRD number. Step 3 - if the entity carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand, or supplies remote services to New Zealand consumers exceeding NZD 60,000 in 12 months, register for GST by filing the GST 1A application or the dedicated 'Remote services non-resident GST registration' in myIR. Step 4 - a foreign entity with no New Zealand fixed place of business may appoint a local agent or tax representative to manage IRD/myIR correspondence and return filing after registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident International Company without New Zealand central management and control or a permanent establishment; non-New Zealand-source income is outside the New Zealand income tax base; New Zealand-source income, if any, would be subject to entity-level taxation and non-resident withholding taxes as applicable)

Available election forms

New Zealand does not have a US-style 'check-the-box' election. Available statutory classification and tax elections include: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under the Income Tax Act 2007 for eligible close-held New Zealand companies, allowing owner-level taxation; (2) Foreign Investment Fund (FIF) method elections for interests in non-resident entities; (3) hybrid mismatch branch or deemed payment elections relevant where hybrid entities or instruments are present; (4) binding tax ruling applications under section 91C of the Tax Administration Act 1994 to confirm entity classification and GST treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (total taxable supplies in any 12-month period; the same threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of remote services supplied to New Zealand consumers under the GST remote-services rules, with voluntary registration available below the threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies when goods are exported from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply and the supplier has obtained and retains satisfactory proof of export. Services: zero-rating may apply where services, including B2B enterprise SaaS, are supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of performance and is not a New Zealand GST-registered person. Remote services supplied to New Zealand-resident non-GST-registered consumers are subject to 15% GST, not zero-rating. Supplies of remote services to GST-registered New Zealand businesses may be zero-rated where the recipient is a GST-registered person and the supply is acquired for the recipient's taxable activity.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic TSW/CUSDEC data accepted by New Zealand Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (including Master Airwaybill where applicable)
  • Commercial invoice and underlying export contract or SaaS subscription agreement
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (where applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-exchange receipt evidencing payment and repatriation of export proceeds

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or electronic invoicing clearance by the tax authority. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the New Zealand Peppol e-invoicing framework. GST-registered businesses must issue tax invoices containing the statutory particulars prescribed by the GST Act 1985, and may retain digital records. No digital stamping or continuous transaction control mechanism is imposed; IRD uses myIR and post-event risk-based audit systems.

IRD conducts targeted risk-based audits of zero-rated exports and may reclassify inadequately documented exports as standard-rated 15% GST supplies, requiring output tax plus use-of-money interest from the original refund date. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply: 20% for lack of reasonable care or unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or similar intent. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims can also expose the entity to criminal prosecution, fines up to NZD 250,000 per offence for a company, and imprisonment for responsible persons. Documentary proof of physical export for goods and actual supply to an eligible non-resident recipient for services is mandatory and must be retained to support refund claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully and freely convertible. New Zealand maintains no exchange controls, no foreign currency rationing, and no government licensing requirement for current-account or capital-account transactions. Capital-account restrictions are limited to OIO screening for sensitive land and significant business assets (e.g., 25%+ shareholdings or high-value infrastructure), which do not apply to a pure ISIC 6201 cloud software licensing vehicle that holds no land, media, or sensitive quota. Profit and dividend remittance from an NZ-incorporated International Company is legally unrestricted; the only frictions are tax-related: non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) applies to dividends (30% statutory, reduced by double-tax treaties, e.g., 15% to Australia/UK/Germany, 0% in limited fully-imputed scenarios) and royalty outflows are subject to NRWT at 15% (5% under the NZ–US treaty for qualifying software royalties). The entity may hold and operate multi-currency accounts in NZD, USD, EUR, and major APAC currencies; no prior central bank approval is required for conversion at any quantum.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation time-window exists in New Zealand. The Reserve Bank’s balance-of-payments statistical framework (Banking System Monthly Statistics and Standard Credit Reporting) requires licensed banks to categorise cross-border receipts by BPM6 purpose codes, but the underlying SaaS/software-licence revenue (export of cloud services) may be retained in foreign currency indefinitely, netted, or converted into NZD at the exporter’s commercial discretion. For an ISIC 6201 enterprise SaaS supplier, subscription receipts should be coded under EBOPS code 9.2 / services purpose code S42, and there is no obligation to surrender export proceeds to an authorised dealer.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand – Te Pūtea Matua (the central bank and prudential regulator for registered banks, non-bank deposit takers, and insurers under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989; foreign investment screening is administered separately by the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) under the Overseas Investment Act 2005; AML/CFT supervision of banks is co-led by the Reserve Bank, the Financial Markets Authority (FMA), and the Department of Internal Affairs under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for thin-rail treasury operations (collections and vendor disbursements), but MEDIUM-LOW as a replacement for a licensed bank for escrow/holding of statutory capital. Wise Business (NZ) is registered on the FMA’s Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and is supervised for AML/CFT by the FMA; it cannot take deposits or lend, but can hold safeguarded funds and process cross-border SaaS subscription receipts in over 50 currencies. Stripe Billing/Balance is a payment facilitator under Visa/Mastercard rules rather than a licensed NZ bank, settling payouts via an NZ acquiring bank. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not authorised to hold statutory share-capital escrow for an NZ IC, so capital injection must route to a licensed commercial bank; (ii) FX hedging contracts cannot be executed with an unlicensed EMI; and (iii) bank references from EMIs are not accepted by NZClear. The blueprint therefore adopts a hybrid 'Wise + Stripe for commerce rails, Tier-1 bank for fiduciary and hedge rails' architecture.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the NZ-incorporated IC, inbound equity capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank) into a Tier-1 bank-designated Capital Escrow Account. The payment instruction must carry the RBNZ/BPM6 financial-account code F1.1.1 (equity) or the bank’s equivalent 'non-resident equity inward' purpose code; the reference field must state: 'INJECTION OF SHARE CAPITAL, [Company Name], ISIC 6201, pursuant to board resolution [date], subscriber [name], share class/quantity'. A cover letter attaching the shareholder registry, board minutes, and executed subscription agreement is retained by the beneficiary bank. No OIO consent is required because the entity owns no sensitive assets; only standard balance-of-payments statistical capture with monthly RBNZ reporting applies. The bank verifies the ultimate transferor under FATF Recommendation 16; funds arriving from a trust bank account must present the same UBO chain.Outbound supplier and cloud-R&D disbursements use SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) with services purpose codes (S42 for software/cloud, S61 for R&D) and MT202 for correspondent refinancing. Dividend and royalty outflows to shareholders use MT103 with a tax indicator; NRWT is withheld electronically and paid to Inland Revenue under the applicable DTA rate. Commercial letters of credit (Sight L/C and Usance L/C under ICC UCP 600) are available for any physical-goods components, though for a pure SaaS company they are used only where a global reseller contract requires them. All NZD legs settle via NZClear’s ESAS (Exchange Settlement Account System) operated by the Reserve Bank; foreign-currency legs settle via the bank’s correspondent network. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a compliant Cook Islands International Company (statutory exemption under the International Companies Act 2011-12, Cook Islands); 28% for the New Zealand corporate rate if the IC is trapped into New Zealand tax residence by central management and control being exercised in New Zealand

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for a Cook Islands International Company because the IC is statutorily excluded from all Cook Islands withholding taxes; under the New Zealand-Cook Islands DTA, NZ NRWT on a dividend paid to a New Zealand-resident beneficial owner is capped at 15% generally and 5% where the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 10% of the voting power; New Zealand domestic NRWT on dividends is 30% for non-imputed dividends and 15% for fully imputed dividends, but no NZ NRWT arises on a true IC dividend that is sourced outside New Zealand

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand thresholds in NZD: mandatory Local File when cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZ$10 million annually; mandatory Master File when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds NZ$50 million and related-party cross-border transactions exceed NZ$10 million; Country-by-Country Report when group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million (approximately NZ$1.3 billion); the Cook Islands maintains OECD-aligned transfer pricing rules under the Income Tax Act 1997 with no published statutory monetary de minimis for International Companies, so all intercompany SaaS licence fees, R&D cost contributions, distribution margins, and financing arrangements must be substantiated under the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines

Economic substance scope: The Cook Islands applies Economic Substance Requirements under the International Companies Act 2011-12 and the International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations, implementing the OECD/G-20 harmful tax practices framework; relevant activities, including IP holding, distribution and service centres, financing, and pure equity holding, require: (1) economic substance directed and managed in the Cook Islands; (2) adequate local premises, employees, and annual operating expenditure proportionate to the income; and (3) an annual economic substance filing; a B2B enterprise SaaS IP-holder is classified as a high-risk IP company that must demonstrate active decision-making by local directors, substantive local staffing for operational functions, and no bare passive holding of intangibles; failure to satisfy ESR is automatically exchangeable with New Zealand under the OECD exchange-of-information framework

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / New Zealand Residence Trapping

Statutory sanction: New Zealand corporate income tax at 28% on worldwide income; shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 of 20% for gross carelessness, 40% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion; criminal conviction for knowingly evading tax carries a fine of up to NZ$50,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years, together with use-of-money interest, asset restraint orders, and seizure of the SaaS revenue stream

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where NZ management-and-control facts are attributable to directors; directors may be jointly and severally liable for shortfall penalties, unpaid PAYE and GST obligations attach to directors personally, and criminal sanctions extend to disqualification from holding office as a director of any New Zealand or Cook Islands company, departure prohibition orders and travel bans, and imprisonment
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Cook Islands ESR regime: prohibition on transacting business, monetary penalties of up to CI$250,000 for non-compliance, deregistration by the Registrar, and mandatory automatic exchange of information with New Zealand under the Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance in Tax Matters; where New Zealand taxing rights exist, the arrangement is recharacterised as a sham, with reassessment at 28% and evasion penalties

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for ESR penalties when the company defaults on payment; courts may disqualify directors for up to 5 years, impose personal travel bans pending investigation, and order personal restitution to shareholders where the shell structure was used to conceal asset misappropriation
AML/CTF and Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: Cook Islands AML/CFT Act civil fines of up to CI$300,000 per contravention for non-filing and for failure to perform customer due diligence; deliberate money laundering under the Cook Islands Proceeds of Crime Act and the New Zealand Crimes Act carries imprisonment of up to 7 years, freezing and forfeiture of SaaS subscription revenue, and disqualification orders against corporate service providers; suspicious transactions must be reported to the Financial Intelligence Unit

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly or recklessly permits money laundering through the IC is personally indictable for aiding and abetting; personal asset forfeiture, travel bans, and extradition exposure can be imposed; the corporate veil is pierced where the director directed the non-compliant conduct
Transfer Pricing / Intangible Migration Adjustment

Statutory sanction: New Zealand Inland Revenue may recharacterise and adjust the royalty, service fee, or interest base, assess additional income tax at 28%, and impose transfer pricing adjustment penalties under sections FB 2 to FB 8 of the Income Tax Act 2007; shortfall penalties of 20% to 150% of the adjusted tax apply, and non-production of contemporaneous documentation reverses the burden of proof

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct non-arm's-length intercompany agreements can be jointly assessed for civil penalties; egregious deliberate mispricing elevates the conduct to criminal evasion with personal criminal liability; where the company is insolvent at the assessment date, directors face personal liability for the company's shortfall and reimbursement orders
