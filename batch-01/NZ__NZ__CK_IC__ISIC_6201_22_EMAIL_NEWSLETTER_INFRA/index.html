Competent corporate registry

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Companies Act 1993; the company may issue shares fully paid, partly paid, or nil-paid pursuant to section 97. No escrow account, capital deposit, or bank confirmation is required for incorporation of a private international company.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety: any leased office or server-hosting room must hold a Building Warrant of Fitness and a fire evacuation plan approved by Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) pursuant to the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017, including Annual Fire Safety Statements. Structural Load Clearance: if the company installs server racks on-premises, a structural engineer's certification of floor-load capacity must be obtained under section 112 of the Building Act 2004. Occupational Clearance: the company, as a Person Conducting a Business or Undertaking (PCBU) under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, must implement a due diligence framework covering remote-worker ergonomics, electrical safety of computer equipment, first-aid provisions, incident notification to WorkSafe New Zealand, and a worker participation system. No heavy-machinery, explosive, or hazardous-materials licences are required for the email-infrastructure business model.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Companies Act 1993; the company may issue shares fully paid, partly paid, or nil-paid pursuant to section 97. No escrow account, capital deposit, or bank confirmation is required for incorporation of a private international company.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake), New Zealand's national tax authority; primary digital portal is myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz). New Zealand Customs Service (Te Mana Ārai o Aotearoa) separately administers the Customs Export Declaration and import/export controls via the Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

The Cook Islands International Company ('CK_IC') is not incorporated in New Zealand and is assessed as a non-resident foreign company for New Zealand tax purposes. Step 1: determine whether any NZ tax registration is required. An IRD number (the single statutory tax identification number used for income tax, GST, PAYE, and FBT) must be obtained from Inland Revenue before the CK_IC files any New Zealand return, pays NZ-source tax, or registers for GST. Apply using the prescribed IRD registration form for a non-resident company (currently form IR742), submitted with certified copies of the Cook Islands certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director and shareholder registers, a notarised statement confirming that the entity's head office and central management and control are outside New Zealand, and a New Zealand address for service (or the address of a New Zealand tax agent). Step 2: for GST, if the CK_IC supplies 'remote services' (email API and newsletter-hosting infrastructure) to New Zealand-resident private consumers in the ordinary course of business and the value of those supplies exceeds NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, it must apply for GST registration on the same IRD number using the prescribed non-resident GST registration form (currently form GST 1A). If the CK_IC's supplies are made on a B2B basis to NZ GST-registered businesses, the NZ customer self-assesses GST under the reverse charge and the CK_IC is not required to register for GST solely because of those supplies. Where the threshold is not met, the CK_IC may apply for voluntary GST registration at the Commissioner's discretion. No separate tax registration is required at the NZ Companies Office; however, if the foreign company carries on business in New Zealand, it must separately register as an overseas company under the Companies Act 1993.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: The CK_IC has the legal form of a Cook Islands International Company, which is statutorily tax-exempt in its home jurisdiction. For New Zealand income tax purposes, the company is a non-resident entity (not incorporated in New Zealand; head office, central management, and control located outside New Zealand) and is therefore chargeable only on New Zealand-sourced income at the 28% corporate rate under the Income Tax Act 2007. Provided the CK_IC derives no income sourced in New Zealand, it has no New Zealand tax liability and operates within a de facto tax-exempt zone for NZ purposes. It is not eligible for New Zealand pass-through treatment as a look-through company or qualifying company, and it cannot join an NZ imputation or loss-offset group unless it first becomes New Zealand tax-resident.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box election regime. The material statutory elections and mechanisms available in this context are: (i) GST grouping election under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, allowing the CK_IC and any NZ-resident associated companies to file as a single GST group if it has an NZ taxable presence; (ii) the mandatory reverse-charge mechanism for imported remote services under section 8(4B) of the GST Act 1985, under which an NZ GST-registered business recipient self-assesses GST on the CK_IC's services and contemporaneously claims a corresponding input credit, producing a net nil tax outcome; (iii) voluntary GST registration at the Commissioner's discretion under sections 51(3) and 51A of the GST Act 1985 where the NZD 60,000 threshold is not attained; (iv) thin-capitalisation elections under subpart FE of the Income Tax Act 2007, including the election to use the worldwide-group debt/asset ratio in place of the 60% safe-harbour ratio for the NZ operations of non-resident owned groups; and (v) hybrid and branch mismatch adjustments under Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007, which are mandatory and non-elective; if the CK_IC has NZ-source income or NZ-resident investors, full contemporaneous documentation of any imported mismatch or dual-inclusion income must be maintained and disclosed. A look-through company election under Part HA of the Income Tax Act 2007 is available only to NZ-resident close companies and is not available to the CK_IC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000: registration is mandatory when the value of taxable supplies exceeds NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period (section 51 GST Act 1985). For a non-resident supplier of remote services (the CK_IC's email API and newsletter-hosting platform), registration is compulsory when remote services supplied to New Zealand-resident private consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period; supplies made to NZ GST-registered businesses are excluded from the threshold and are instead subject to the reverse charge. Where the threshold is not met, the non-resident supplier may elect to register voluntarily with the approval of Inland Revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: a supply is zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the GST Act 1985 if the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of supply, or supplies them for export on terms consistent with export. For services (the CK_IC's email infrastructure and newsletter-hosting platform): zero-rating applies under section 11(2)(c) of the GST Act 1985 where the services are supplied to a person who is a non-resident and who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, subject to statutory exceptions for services supplied in connection with New Zealand land or goods situated in New Zealand at the time of supply, and for personal services performed in New Zealand. Remote electronic services supplied to a non-resident business customer are zero-rated; where the recipient is an NZ GST-registered business, the CK_IC may zero-rate only if the customer certifies GST registration and the services are acquired for that customer's taxable activity, with the NZ customer then self-assessing the reverse charge. The CK_IC must hold contemporaneous commercial evidence that the recipient of each export supply was a non-resident located outside New Zealand at the time of supply (e.g., foreign customer contracts, jurisdiction data embedded in the email API subscriber records, non-NZ IP geolocation logs, and foreign payment-card details captured in Stripe). GST input credits attributable to the export activity are refundable strictly on production of the same substantive evidence. New Zealand law imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; however, the CK_IC should adopt a 180-day settlement benchmark so that the inward-remittance record is available to substantiate each zero-rated export invoice.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Customs Service via the Trade Single Window / CusDec) - required for physical goods exported from New Zealand; not applicable to pure email-SaaS infrastructure supplies
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing the physical export and the 28-day export window (goods only)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency inward-transfer instruction matching the non-resident buyer's payment to the Stripe Billing payout record
  • Signed B2B service agreement and exported-services invoice identifying the non-resident customer, the jurisdiction of the customer's registered office, and the zero-rating statutory basis (e.g., 'Zero-rated - supply to a non-resident, section 11 GST Act 1985')

No mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping regime exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and follows the Pan-European Public Procurement OnLine (PEPPOL) BIS 3.0 standard, operated through the New Zealand E-Invoicing Framework (Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment, 2020); taxpayers may connect via Inland Revenue's free PEPPOL access point or certified commercial access points. There is no statutory API requirement for invoice clearance, although Inland Revenue may require digital access to the CK_IC's billing platforms (including Stripe Billing and payment-card ledgers) during an audit. GST tax invoices must be issued within 28 days of the tax point, and all transaction records, e-invoices, and underlying API export logs must be retained for 7 years under section 22 of the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Inland Revenue audits export zero-rating claims with heightened scrutiny of digital and SaaS exporters. The CK_IC must demonstrate, on audit, that each zero-rated supply satisfied the section 11 GST Act 1985 criteria; if contemporaneous evidence (export documentation, inward foreign-currency remittance, IP/access logs) is missing or inconsistent, Inland Revenue will re-characterise the supply as taxable at 15% and reassess GST, with use-of-money interest accruing from the original due date under Part 7 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 (TAA). Statutory shortfall penalties under the TAA are then layered: 20% for lack of reasonable care (s 141A); 20% for taking an unacceptable tax position (s 141B); 50% for an abusive tax position (s 141C); and 150% for evasion (s 141D). A temporary shortfall arising from a wash-up is penalised at 40% under s 141EB. Fraudulent export zero-rating (including fake export declarations, circular offshore round-tripping, or falsified non-resident evidence) additionally exposes the CK_IC to criminal prosecution under ss 143A-143B of the TAA, with fines of up to NZD 50,000 for an individual or NZD 250,000 for a body corporate, and imprisonment for up to 5 years. Retrospective reassessments may extend up to 4 years (7 years where evasion is involved), and the enforcement response may include a referral to the Financial Intelligence Unit under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. Since the repeal of the Foreign Exchange Control Regulations in December 1984, no exchange licence, approval, or rationing applies to foreign-currency transactions. The CK IC can receive USD subscription revenue, hold foreign-currency accounts, convert at market rates, remit dividends or profits, repay shareholder loans, and liquidate investment proceeds without restriction or government authorisation. The only frictions are ordinary banking AML/CFT verification, Statistical New Zealand and RBNZ balance-of-payments reporting carried out by the settlement banks, and tax-exposure analysis under New Zealand's CFC and thin-capitalisation rules for any NZ conduit entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion percentage, and no surrender obligation on export-service proceeds. The email-newsletter platform may retain all subscription receipts in USD or any other freely chosen currency, net-settle through the RBNZ ESAS system, and convert internally as liquidity requires. Banks must, however, apply the AML/CFT Act 2009 record-keeping obligations, verify beneficial ownership of the CK IC, and report suspicious transactions and threshold cash movements; no RBNZ foreign-exchange filing or repatriation deadline exists.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) – Te Pūtea Matua; exchange-control responsibilities were formally abolished in 1984, leaving the RBNZ with monetary policy, currency issuance, prudential supervision of trading banks, and oversight of the ESAS settlement system, while the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) supervises AML/CFT conduct of banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: LOW for direct onboarding of the CK IC; MEDIUM only through an NZ-incorporated collection conduit. Wise Business and Airwallex (NZ) Limited are regulated in New Zealand as money service businesses under the Department of Internal Affairs and the Financial Service Providers Register, but their merchant KYC accepts companies incorporated only in their enumerated supported jurisdictions; the Cook Islands is not supported, and a Cook Islands International Company will be declined at the originator-ranking stage. Stripe is the designated collection rail for the target business scope, but Stripe does not localise to the Cook Islands, so the compliant stack is: Stripe_Billing → NZ or US payment-facilitator entity of record → intercompany service and cost-plus agreement → settlement to the CK IC's BNZ or Westpac account. No NZ prohibition blocks use of these EMIs for ancillary payouts, but every instruction remains subject to the AML/CFT Act 2009 and the bank's restriction on pass-through third-party aggregation; the bank will require written confirmation that Stripe is the sole collection agent.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand does not prescribe a hard NZD threshold for Local File preparation in statute; however, taxpayers are expected to prepare contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where related-party cross-border transactions exceed NZ$10 million in aggregate annual value against a benchmark and you must furnish documentation if Inland Revenue issues a notice; for non-resident ICs, a practical minimum threshold of NZ$3 million is used for intra-group services benchmarking

Economic substance scope: New Zealand applies the OECD substantial presence test for tax residence and the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section BG 1 and section GB 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007; explicit Economic Substances Regulations as found in the Cook Islands/offshore financial centres do not operate in New Zealand domestic law, but the IC must demonstrate sufficient commercial substance (real board decisions, genuine SaaS platform revenue, actual staff or contractors and email infrastructure assets) to avoid sham and tax avoidance characterisation

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Tax Avoidance Arrangement)

Statutory sanction: GAAR application: Inland Revenue may cancel the tax benefit, reassess income at 28%, impose shortfall penalties of 100% of the tax shortfall for abusive tax avoidance, plus use-of-money interest; criminal prosecution for evasion can result in a fine up to NZ$50,000 and imprisonment for wilful evasion

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for tax shortfalls and penalties if they signed false returns or were knowingly party to arrangements lacking reasonable care; civil restitution orders and director banning orders under the Companies Act 1993; travel risk noted in serious fraud inquiries and imprisonment up to five years under the Tax Administration Act 1994
Shell Company or Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may disregard the non-resident entity under the OECD BEPS Action 5 and section BG 1, allocating all income to the New Zealand management site; penalty of 100% of shortfall for not taking reasonable care; compulsory deregistration and strike-off under Cook Islands International Companies Act 2021-2022 and NZ anti-avoidance rulings; financial penalties up to NZ$100,000 for directors involved in sham documentation

Director personal liability: Director and beneficial owner may be treated as the deemed New Zealand resident taxpayer and personally assessed on all distributed profits; personal liability for the company's unpaid PAYE and GST where the entity is an arrangement to evade worker taxes; inability to claim treaty benefits due to Limitation of Benefits; possible prosecution for false declarations and up to seven years' imprisonment
AML / CTF and Cross-Border Enforcement

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (NZ) and Cook Islands AML Act 2003: fines up to NZ$200,000 for natural persons and NZ$2 million for bodies corporate for non-compliance with AML/CFT obligations; false UBO disclosure attracts perjury or false-statement penalties and civil pecuniary penalties up to the greater of three times the benefit

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally ordered to pay civil forfeiture of assets where infrastructure profits are traced as proceeds of unlawful activity; travel bans and surrender of passports issued pending financial crime investigation; imprisonment up to seven years for money laundering in connection with tax evasion; personal contribution orders for company penalties under the Financial Markets Authority enforcement framework
