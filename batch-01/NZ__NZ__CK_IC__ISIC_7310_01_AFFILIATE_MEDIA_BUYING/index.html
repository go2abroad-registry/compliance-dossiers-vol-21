Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office (Ngā Tari Kōmihana o Aotearoa), a division of the Ministry of Business, Innovation, and Employment (MBIE).

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), primarily sections 5 (incorporation), 10 (constitution), 19 (certificate of incorporation), 22 (name restrictions), 24 (name reservation), 33-35 (share allotment and capital), 42 (registered office), 88 (share register), 122-123 (registered office and address for service), 124 (first directors), 151 (director consent), and 210 (annual return). Also relevant: Financial Reporting Act 2013, Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008.

Formation / constitutional filings

New Zealand Companies Office — Online Incorporation Application (Registration of a Company) pursuant to the Companies Act 1993, Form 1 (Incorporation of a Company) as embedded in the electronic registry.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under New Zealand law. Shares may be issued as nil-paid, partly paid, or fully paid as determined by the board, and there is no escrow deposit requirement. For an International Company using a New Zealand corporate trustee or nominee structure, the shareholder capital is fixed by the constitution and board resolution; the company must only maintain a registered office in New Zealand and a resident agent. Banking partners may internally require an initial deposit, but that is not a Companies Act requirement.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance is not required for a purely digital company without staffed premises. If the company leases an office: (i) obtain a Fire Safety Certificate / Landlord's Fire Statement under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017; (ii) install and maintain smoke detection and a Means of Escape compliance schedule (Building Act 2004, Code Clause C); (iii) if employees work in premises, develop a Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 worker engagement plan, conduct a hazard risk assessment for office/computer use, and maintain first aid kits. If only using a registered agent's shared office, the agent's certificate covers physical clearance, but the company must document its own health and safety policy.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (IR) – New Zealand's national tax authority; online portal: myIR (https://myir.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity without a physical NZ presence must register for an IRD number (TIN) through the Non-Resident Company Registration process on the Inland Revenue website. The entity must file application IR596 ('Registration of an IRD number for a non-resident company') with certified company incorporation documents, a certificate of good standing, and a New Zealand agent/resident director appointment (unless an exemption applies). Upon approval, the entity receives an IRD number and, if taxable supplies are expected above the GST registration threshold, must simultaneously register for GST via Form GST1.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory election mechanisms include: the Look-Through Company (LTC) election under Subpart HB for qualifying closely held NZ companies; the elective GST margin scheme for land transactions under Subpart 8C of the GST Act 1985; and hybrid mismatch elections/treatment alignments under Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007. A non-resident company can also elect to be treated as a fully-fledged NZ tax resident if central management and control is exercised in New Zealand.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods physically exported by the supplier within 28 days of supply, with the supplier retaining documentary evidence of export. Services are zero-rated under section 11A(1)(k) of the GST Act 1985 when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the services are not physically performed in New Zealand and are not directly connected with land, moveable property, or persons located in New Zealand. Electronically supplied remote services to overseas B2B customers are zero-rated; B2C remote services supplied to New Zealand residents by non-resident suppliers remain subject to GST where the NZ threshold is met.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Trade Single Window entry / Customs CUS830)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or other bank payment/transfer evidence
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales contract
  • Packing list and weight certificate (where applicable)
  • Proof of physical exportation (e.g., carrier receipt, port turnaround evidence)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping mandate applies. E-invoicing is voluntary but supports PEPPOL interoperability; businesses may issue compliant GST tax invoices with prescribed particulars. GST returns and payments are filed via Inland Revenue's myIR or approved gateway services, but there is no pre-clearance requirement for invoices.

Inland Revenue may audit zero-rated export claims and require the exporter to establish, on the balance of probabilities, that the supply was genuinely exported or supplied to a non-resident outside New Zealand. Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under the Tax Administration Act 1994, attracting a shortfall penalty of 150% of the tax shortfall, criminal prosecution, and imprisonment. Civil penalties include: 10% for failure to take reasonable care, 20% for gross carelessness, and 40% for an abusive tax position, plus use-of-money interest. Clawback provisions require the supplier to reverse input tax credits and pay the unremitted GST plus penalties if the zero-rating conditions are retrospectively not satisfied, and the Commissioner may reassess returns within the statutory time bar.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully open current and capital account with no general exchange-control approval gate, no restrictions on foreign-currency accounts, no surrender obligations, and no conversion quotas. A Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC) operating in the Tax_Exempt_Zone may freely hold and remit NZD, USD, AUD, EUR, and GBP. The principal practical remittance hurdle is not exchange control but tax characterisation: if the CK_IC generates New Zealand-source income, dividends, royalties, or interest paid to non-residents may be subject to New Zealand non-resident withholding tax at 15% or a lower applicable treaty rate, and the transaction must be supported by transfer-pricing documentation. Pure offshore affiliate media-buying income retained in the international-company treasury is not subject to New Zealand tax if the company has no New Zealand permanent establishment and does not carry on a taxable New Zealand activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for affiliate media-buying or other service export receipts in New Zealand or the Cook Islands. A CK_IC may retain foreign currency indefinitely in multi-currency accounts. The only compliance margin is AML/CFT and financial-intelligence reporting: beneficiary banks may require underlying publisher contracts, ad-network statements, and invoices to establish the commercial source of each cross-border receipt, and deposits above internal risk thresholds may be subject to enhanced source-of-funds review before they are available for outward disbursement.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), the central bank of New Zealand and the monetary authority for the New Zealand dollar, is the senior bank regulator in the Realm of New Zealand. In the Cook Islands, the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) is the statutory licensing and prudential supervision authority for banks, foreign exchange dealers, trustees, and international companies, while the New Zealand dollar remains the operational settlement currency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium and conditional. Offshore digital EMIs are viable only as auxiliary settlement rails, not as the primary treasury or capital-escrow bank. Wise Business is operationally feasible for NZD, USD, EUR, and GBP disbursements to affiliate networks and publishers. Mercury is generally unavailable to a Cook Islands International Company structure. New Zealand does not operate an EMI licensing regime; non-bank payment providers must register on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register and join an approved dispute-resolution scheme, and any provider serving the Cook Islands entity should hold a relevant FSC money-service or payment-service permission. Legal constraint: an offshore EMI is not a licensed deposit-taker and cannot hold client escrow, cannot provide a New Zealand bank sort code for local BECS settlement, and must never be used as the sole receiving bank for inbound capital injections. The final structure should maintain a FSC-licensed domestic bank account as the treasury core and use EMIs only for high-speed low-value affiliate payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route for inbound capital is a SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's bank to the CK_IC capital-escrow account maintained at ANZ Bank (Cook Islands) Limited or Bank of Cook Islands Limited. Wire narrative must clearly read SHAREHOLDER CAPITAL INJECTION / SHARE SUBSCRIBER CONTRIBUTION, reference the CK_IC registration number, and identify the ultimate transferor. The beneficiary bank should be instructed to classify the payment as foreign direct investment equity capital or other investment loan, as applicable, for New Zealand balance-of-payments and international-transaction reporting. Verification pack: board resolution approving the injection, executed subscription or loan agreement, share register with the issuance entry, source-of-funds evidence, FSC acknowledgement for the IC structure, and certified identities of the transferor and beneficial owner. No prior exchange-control approval is required in New Zealand or for an international company's offshore capital receipts; however, funds may be held for one to three business days pending enhanced due-diligence clearance where the amount exceeds the bank's risk threshold.Affiliate-media and lead-generation trade settlements should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for international publisher payouts, New Zealand BECS for domestic NZ media sellers, SEPA Credit Transfer for European publishers, and US ACH through a permitted partner bank or Wise Business for United States affiliate payees. MT202 cover payments may be used where interbank liquidity routing requires a separate cover message. Commercial letters of credit in MT700 format are not a natural payment rail for digital media arbitrage because there is no physical shipment; a standby letter of credit or bank-issued purchase-order guarantee is preferred only where an advertiser or publisher requires bank protection. All outbound wires must carry a purpose code matching the underlying media-buying service, a reference to the publisher contract, and an invoice or statement of account. Wise Business is operationally feasible for NZD, USD, EUR, and GBP disbursements to affiliate networks and publishers. Mercury is generally unavailable to a Cook Islands International Company structure. New Zealand does not operate an EMI licensing regime; non-bank payment providers must register on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register and join an approved dispute-resolution scheme, and any provider serving the Cook Islands entity should hold a relevant FSC money-service or payment-service permission. Legal constraint: an offshore EMI is not a licensed deposit-taker and cannot hold client escrow, cannot provide a New Zealand bank sort code for local BECS settlement, and must never be used as the sole receiving bank for inbound capital injections. The final structure should maintain a FSC-licensed domestic bank account as the treasury core and use EMIs only for high-speed low-value affiliate payouts.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% NRWT on dividends paid by a New Zealand company to a non-resident company. If fully imputed, the dividend may be exempt (0%) under the imputation credit mechanism. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is mandatory where cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZ$10 million annually. The IR4 must disclose related-party dealings exceeding NZ$1 million. Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of NZ$1.14 billion or more. Cook Islands International Companies are subject to Cook Islands confidentiality and annual return rules, but New Zealand TP thresholds apply to New Zealand-source operations.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007. There are no OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the IRD may recharacterise entities lacking commercial substance. A Cook Islands IC must have real functions, assets, and risks in the Cook Islands; otherwise, New Zealand may attribute its income to a New Zealand resident. The Cook Islands International Companies Act prohibits the carrying on of business within the Cook Islands and requires annual returns. International tax substance doctrines apply in cross-border audits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Under section 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994, imprisonment for up to 5 years for evading tax. Civil shortfall penalties up to 150% of the tax shortfall. Late payment penalties and use-of-money interest apply. Criminal fines may also be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable under section 133 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 for the company's tax debts if they knowingly assisted in evasion. Courts can order civil restitution, asset freezing, travel bans, and disqualification from managing companies. Imprisonment is possible for fraudulent conduct. The corporate veil is pierced for fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may apply the GAAR to reconstruct the arrangement and attribute income to a New Zealand resident. Shortfall penalties of 20% (unacceptable tax position) or 100% (abusive tax position) apply. Interest and late payment penalties follow. In the Cook Islands, carrying on business in the Cook Islands could result in loss of exempt status and penalties under the International Companies Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign tax returns knowing the position is abusive face civil penalties equal to up to 100% of the tax shortfall. They may be personally liable for the company's tax debt under the Companies Act 1993 provisions for reckless trading or fraudulent conduct. Travel bans and asset freezing orders can be issued.
Anti-Money Laundering / AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (NZ), financial institutions and reporting entities face civil penalties up to NZ$2 million per breach and criminal penalties for individuals including imprisonment up to 2 years. The Cook Islands AML/CFT Act 2018 imposes fines and imprisonment for money laundering, and failure to report suspicious transactions carries separate penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be criminally prosecuted if they knowingly authorise money laundering. Penalties include imprisonment, significant fines, travel bans, asset freezes, and forfeiture. Directors may be disqualified and held personally liable for civil penalties.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: No separate TP penalty, but shortfall penalties apply: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for unacceptable position, and 150% for evasion. IRD may adjust income and impose use-of-money interest. Record-keeping failures may attract fines up to NZ$25,000.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for penalties if they sign returns without reasonable care in respect of transfer pricing. In fraudulent circumstances, personal liability for the company's tax shortfall and civil restitution may be imposed.
