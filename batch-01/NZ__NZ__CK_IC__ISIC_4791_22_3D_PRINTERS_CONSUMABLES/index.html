Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: Non-resident International Company not carrying on a taxable presence in New Zealand; exempt from New Zealand income tax on foreign-sourced and offshore trading income; taxable only on New Zealand-sourced income; GST, customs duties, and the low-value goods GST regime apply to physical imports into and supplies delivered within New Zealand.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 neutralise deductions or non-inclusions arising from hybrid entities, hybrid instruments, and reverse hybrids. An offshore International Company may elect, by written notification to Inland Revenue, to be treated as a New Zealand resident company if its central management and control is exercised in New Zealand (Income Tax Act 2007 s YD(2)); otherwise it files as a non-resident company. Closed-held New Zealand resident companies may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under Income Tax Act 2007 ss HB 1–14, but a non-resident International Company is not eligible for LTC. GST registration is mandatory once the statutory threshold is met, with no elective carve-out for non-residents; qualifying persons may elect the provisional tax instalment method and the ratio method for GST apportionment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month rolling period. Non-resident suppliers of remote services must register when supplies to New Zealand residents exceed NZD 60,000. Offshore sellers of low-value goods (each consignment NZD 1,000 or less) selling to New Zealand consumers must register and charge GST at 15% under the offshore seller regime once the same NZD 60,000 threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods qualifies for zero-rating under s 11(1)(a) of the GST Act 1985 if the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply (or within 60 days where the goods are delivered to a purchaser who is not resident in New Zealand and is not in New Zealand at the time of supply) and contemporaneously retains the required evidence of export. Services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply may be zero-rated under s 11A. For online retail of desktop 3D printers, resin, and filaments, zero-rating applies only where the physical goods are despatched to an overseas delivery address via a licensed carrier or courier and where customs export documentation is generated. If goods are delivered to a New Zealand address, used by the supplier in New Zealand, or exported without the statutory evidence, GST must be charged at 15%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (NZ CUS-RAD/Trade Single Window T202 or approved carrier electronic manifest)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing departure of the goods from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank credit advice evidencing payment in foreign currency from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the 3D printers, resin, and filament consumables
  • Proof of delivery in the destination country (overseas customs entry, courier delivery signature, or freight forwarder certificate of export) retained for 7 years under the Tax Administration Act 1994

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital stamping regime exists for New Zealand GST. Inland Revenue operates a voluntary Peppol e-invoicing framework supported by the New Zealand Peppol Authority. GST tax invoices must be issued within 28 days of supply; buyer-created tax invoices are permitted under prescribed conditions in the GST Act 1985. Digital platform operators and payment facilitators (including Shopify and Stripe) are subject to OECD Model Rules information-reporting obligations for platform sellers once the NZD 60,000 platform-assisted supply threshold is met. Inland Revenue conducts automated data-matching of e-commerce sales and bank data, but there is no per-transaction API clearance or pre-approval of invoices.

Inland Revenue may claw back zero-rating and impose shortfall penalties where export evidence is inadequate or the statutory export timeframes are missed. Civil shortfall penalties: lack of reasonable care — 20% of the GST shortfall; unacceptable tax position — 20%; abusive tax position — 50% (increased to 100% where the dominant purpose is tax avoidance, plus an additional 20% for failure to disclose). Use-of-money interest applies on unpaid GST. Criminal liability for knowingly providing false GST returns or evading GST under ss 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994: fines up to NZD 50,000 for individuals and NZD 250,000 for companies and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. Directors and officers may be personally liable for GST shortfall penalties if they are knowingly concerned in the evasion. Zero-rating is retrospectively reversed and GST becomes payable if the goods are not exported within the prescribed 28-day or 60-day window, or if the mandatory documentation bundle is not retained for at least 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a freely convertible major currency with zero exchange control or Central Bank approval requirements on either the current account or the capital account. There is no restriction on inward capital injection, outward supplier payment, profit remittance, dividend distribution, or intra-group debt settlement. The only fiscal impediment to distributions is New Zealand non-resident withholding tax (NRWT): a statutory rate of 30% on dividends if no reduced rate is declared, typically reduced to 15% for portfolio investors and 5% for corporate shareholders holding 10% or more, or to 0% under certain double tax agreements for 80%+ parent-subsidiary chains; interest to non-residents may instead carry a 2% Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) where the borrower is an approved issuer. The CK_IC exempt-zone International Company faces no convertibility hurdle, no prior capital-import licence, and no minimum holding-period requirement for the repatriation of either trade income or subscribed capital

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand has retained no statutory surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export or e-commerce proceeds since the free float of the NZD commenced in March 1985. A cross-border online retailer of desktop 3D printers and resin filaments is legally entitled to hold foreign currency indefinitely in offshore settlement accounts (including Stripe and Shopify payout balances in USD, EUR, or GBP) and to convert any amount into NZD at the time of its own choosing without the involvement of the Reserve Bank. The only compliance interface is the receiving New Zealand bank's statutory reporting to the RBNZ for Balance of Payments and International Transactions Reporting (ITR) purposes on international transfers of NZ$100,000 equivalent or above; that reporting is statistical and does not constitute a surrender, conversion, or approval requirement

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand – Te Pūtea Matua (RBNZ), operating under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) as the co-regulator of the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act) for all New Zealand registered banks and financial service providers

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. New Zealand does not have a statutory electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence category; any non-bank payment service provider operating in or into New Zealand must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, be a member of an approved dispute-resolution scheme, and hold an AML/CFT registration with a compliance officer approved by the Department of Internal Affairs. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer may lawfully be used by the CK_IC to obtain multi-currency local receiving accounts (USD, EUR, GBP) and to execute cross-border conversions, but they cannot supply a New Zealand-resident transactional banking service without a local partnership bank and FSPR registration. The legal constraint for an exempt-zone International Company is that the EMI will require full beneficial-owner identification and a physical registered address, and will typically decline to provide any New Zealand domestic sort-code or electronic transaction account because the entity lacks a verifiable NZ operational nexus; consequently the EMI route is appropriate only as a treasury optimisation layer above a licensed bank, not as a sole account hub

Inward capital for the CK_IC must be transmitted over the SWIFT network using an MT103 message from the subscribing shareholder or lending entity to the beneficiary account held in New Zealand. The remittance narrative must expressly indicate the commercial purpose using recognised ISO 20022 codes where the remitting bank platform permits them: 'EQUI' for equity-securities transaction, 'BLLD' for intercompany loan drawdown, 'CCAP' for a general capital contribution, or 'COMC' for a commitment under the underlying contract. The receiving New Zealand bank performs automatic Balance of Payments and International Transactions Reporting (ITR) capture for every international transaction at or above NZ$100,000 equivalent, and will require the beneficiary to upload a copy of the executed subscription or loan agreement, the board resolution, and the investor's documented source of funds before the credit is made available. Where the funds enter the Capital Escrow Account, the bank places a regulatory hold on the balance pending verification of the share-allotment documentation; once the allotment is confirmed, the funds may be transferred onward to the operating account and subsequently applied to inventory procurement, operating expenses, or permitted FX hedging collateralSupplier payments for 3D-printer units and resin filament inventory are executed through four lawful clearing rails. First, ordinary cross-border trade disbursements are effected via SWIFT MT103 straight-through-processing messages using the SWIFT gpi tracking system, achieving same-day or next-day value from a New Zealand Tier-1 account. Second, for sizeable batch purchases from overseas filament or hardware manufacturers, a commercial letter of credit issued under the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600) provides the supplier with bank-guaranteed payment against presentation of shipping documents (marinetime or air bill of lading, commercial invoice, and packing list). Third, a documentary collection (DP/DA) rail may be used where the supplier requires evidence of goods despatch before settlement without the full expense of a confirming bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. New Zealand does not have a statutory electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence category; any non-bank payment service provider operating in or into New Zealand must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, be a member of an approved dispute-resolution scheme, and hold an AML/CFT registration with a compliance officer approved by the Department of Internal Affairs. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer may lawfully be used by the CK_IC to obtain multi-currency local receiving accounts (USD, EUR, GBP) and to execute cross-border conversions, but they cannot supply a New Zealand-resident transactional banking service without a local partnership bank and FSPR registration. The legal constraint for an exempt-zone International Company is that the EMI will require full beneficial-owner identification and a physical registered address, and will typically decline to provide any New Zealand domestic sort-code or electronic transaction account because the entity lacks a verifiable NZ operational nexus; consequently the EMI route is appropriate only as a treasury optimisation layer above a licensed bank, not as a sole account hub
