Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up share capital exists for a New Zealand company under the Companies Act 1993; shares may be of no par value and may remain partly or fully unpaid at incorporation. There is no escrow, bank guarantee, or capital deposit requirement. All allotments, distributions, and share buy-backs must satisfy the solvency test prescribed in s 4 of the Act, and, where shares are issued for non-cash consideration, a solvency certificate must be filed with the Registrar within 20 working days of the allotment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand law does not provide a US-style check-the-box entity classification election. Eligible closely held companies may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) treatment under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, or transitional Qualifying Company (QC) election under Subpart HA. Hybrid mismatch rules in Subpart FH and controlled foreign company (CFC) attribution rules in Subpart FE require foreign hybrid entity notifications and CFC attributing interest elections to be lodged with the IRD where cross-border structures are involved.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 for domestic supplies of goods and services; for non-resident suppliers of remote/digital services under the offshore supplier registration regime, the same NZD 60,000 annual threshold applies based on taxable supplies to New Zealand consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 applies where goods are exported from New Zealand within 28 days of supply and are not subsequently re-imported, and where services are supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply with the services performed outside New Zealand. For virtual concert metaverse ticketing and digital token services, zero-rating is available if the supply is made to a non-resident recipient outside New Zealand and the benefit of the supply is received outside New Zealand; if the recipient is in New Zealand, the offshore supplier must charge GST at 15% under the remote services reverse-charge regime.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged via the New Zealand Customs Trade Single Window (TSW)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal of exported goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or settlement record from the acquiring bank/payment service provider evidencing FX repatriation and consideration receipt

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government-validated e-invoicing. Voluntary Peppol-compliant e-invoicing is available through certified service providers. GST returns are lodged electronically through myIR on a two-monthly, six-monthly, or annual basis depending on turnover; no real-time tax clearance API is required for issuing invoices.

Exporters claiming zero-rating are subject to IRD audit of the documentary trail, export timing, and foreign exchange repatriation. Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, an abusive tax shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the GST shortfall may apply for evasion or fraudulent zero-rating claims, alongside use-of-money interest and retrospective GST assessments clawing back previously refunded input credits. Knowingly false export claims may also attract fines and prosecution.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely floated and fully convertible; neither the current account nor the capital account is subject to exchange-control licensing, surrender requirements, or differentiated convertibility regimes. Inbound equity, ticket revenue, and intercompany loans may be converted into NZD or any reserve currency without central-bank approval, and dividends, royalty, interest, and management-fee remittances face no FX hurdle. The only material repatriation constraints are fiscal: non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at 30% on non-imputed dividends or 15% on fully imputed dividends (treaty-reduced where a DTA applies), the approved issuer levy (AIL) of 2% on qualifying interest, and royalty withholding at 15% default or 10% treaty rate; these are cleared at the bank via the Inland Revenue IR872 tax-residence certificate and a completed withholding declaration, after which proceeds are credited in freely usable currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline, no minimum repatriation percentage, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export or export-of-services receipts; the former Exchange Control Regulations were revoked and no surrender obligation exists today. Digital-service exporters, including the CK International Company hosting virtual concerts and tokenising tickets, may retain export earnings offshore in any currency or stablecoin, provided that: (i) cross-border wire transfers above NZD 10,000 and physical currency movements above NZD 10,000 are declared or reported by the reporting bank to satisfy AML/CFT and balance-of-payments statistical obligations; (ii) international telegraphic transfers carry an accurate purpose-of-payment classification (e.g., 'Services - Entertainment Services', 'Digital Ticketing Revenue') to support RBNZ statistics; and (iii) the underlying commercial invoices and smart-contract records substantiate the export-of-services nature of the receipts.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the central bank responsible for monetary stability, currency issuance, foreign reserve management, and operation of the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESSA). Foreign exchange administration is exercised under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 rather than through a standalone exchange-control authority. Non-resident investment screening in protected assets is administered by the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) within Toitū Te Whenua Land Information New Zealand (LINZ) under the Overseas Investment Act 2005.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH as a complementary FX-treasury aggregation channel, MEDIUM as a primary settlement venue. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Wise Business, Currencycloud) may lawfully operate through New Zealand FSP registration under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Disputes Resolution) Act 2008, but they are not licensed deposit takers under the Deposit Takers Act 2023 and the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989 regime, cannot accept client deposits, do not access RBNZ settlement finality directly, and must safeguard client funds in segregated trust accounts at an NZ or Australian licensed bank. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are reporting entities under the AML/CFT Act 2009 with transaction-monitoring and sanctions-screening obligations; (ii) USDC flows cannot settle into the NZD banking system without conversion through a licensed virtual asset service provider (VASP); (iii) EMI balances are not covered by the statutory deposit compensation scheme (NZD 100,000 cap under the Deposit Takers Act 2023) which protects only licensed deposit taker customers. Recommended architecture: tier-1 NZ bank as the primary settlement vault, the EMI as a controlled FX/liquidity aggregation node for USD/EUR outbound flows, and a regulated VASP for stablecoin on/off-ramp, all connected through a single treasury management system.

Turnover: A statutory audit is compulsory for large for-profit entities under the Financial Reporting Act 2013, defined as having total assets exceeding NZD 60 million, annual revenue exceeding NZD 30 million, or 50 or more full-time employees in each of the last two consecutive accounting periods. FMC reporting entities and financial institutions regulated under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 are also required to file audited financial statements. Small and medium entities, including a typical virtual event hosting company, are exempt unless shareholders holding at least 5% of the voting rights request an audit.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

New Zealand statutory non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends is 30%; fully imputed dividends may qualify for a 15% NRWT rate. A Tax-Exempt Zone International Company distributing foreign-source exempt income to non-resident shareholders does not incur New Zealand dividend withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border connected-party transactions involving a New Zealand resident entity or permanent establishment require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where the aggregate transaction value exceeds NZD 10 million. Below that threshold, records sufficient to demonstrate arm's-length pricing must still be retained.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a standalone domestic Economic Substance Regulations statute, but Inland Revenue applies the general anti-avoidance rule, controlled foreign company rules, permanent establishment principles and OECD guidance to challenge artificial structures. A CK_IC International Company claiming Tax-Exempt Zone treatment must satisfy applicable economic substance conditions, maintain real local presence, keep accurate records and ensure management and control are not exercised in New Zealand; failure to do so may result in re-characterisation, denial of exemption and NZ 28% tax assessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the evaded tax under the Tax Administration Act 1994, plus use-of-money interest; criminal conviction may attract a fine of up to NZ$50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns or cause suppression of income are jointly and severally liable for assessed shortfalls; travel bans, asset restraint orders and director disqualification may be imposed.
Shell Company / Abusive Use of Tax-Exempt Zone

Statutory sanction: The tax-exempt status may be disregarded; New Zealand-source income is re-attributed to the managing entity and assessed at 28%, with shortfall penalties up to 150%, late payment interest and possible company strike-off for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for assisting in a sham arrangement, restitution of diverted profits, director banning orders and criminal prosecution if the shell is used to defraud revenue authorities.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may adjust the consideration, disallow excessive deductions, impose shortfall penalties of 20%-150% and charge use-of-money interest under transfer pricing rules.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve pricing without contemporaneous economic analysis may incur personal penalties for reckless or knowingly incorrect transfer pricing returns, including liability for the assessed shortfall.
AML / Unverified Digital Payment Wallets

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties for body corporates may reach NZ$2 million per serious breach, with compliance orders, remediation directions and possible criminal liability for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are subject to AML obligations; knowing involvement in laundering proceeds of crime may result in personal criminal prosecution, fines and imprisonment.
Unreported Cryptocurrency and Digital Asset Proceeds

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty of 20% for an unacceptable tax position without reasonable care, escalating to 150% for evasion; unreported digital proceeds are assessed as income with interest accruing until paid.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for civil penalties where the company cannot satisfy assessed tax, and for criminal fines or imprisonment if intentional non-disclosure of digital asset proceeds is proven.
