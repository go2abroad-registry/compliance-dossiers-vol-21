A virtual office or commercial mail-forwarding address can satisfy the New Zealand registered office requirement only if it is a physical street address in New Zealand and not a PO Box. Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand law imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a company; share capital may be nil or fully unpaid. There is no escrow or blocked bank account requirement for incorporation. If shares are issued at a premium, the premium must be disclosed in the register; otherwise, funds are at the directors disposal for the companys lawful purposes.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no physical premises requiring Civil Defence fire certificates or structural inspections; if an office or warehouse is subsequently established, the local council building warrant of fitness and Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) code compliance certificate must be obtained.

Paid-in capital rule: None. New Zealand law imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a company; share capital may be nil or fully unpaid. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake) — national tax administration and collection authority; primary filing and compliance portal is myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/my-ird); customs export clearance is administered separately by the New Zealand Customs Service (Ngā Mana Āhuatanga o Aotearoa) via the Customs Export System and Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

A Cook Islands International Company that derives New Zealand-sourced income, carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand, or is required to register for GST must obtain a New Zealand IRD number. The non-resident company applies on form IR742 ('IRD number application – non-resident companies'), submitted electronically through myIR or lodged by an approved tax agent. Required supporting documents include: certified copy of the Cook Islands Certificate of Incorporation, current Certificate of Good Standing, memorandum and articles of association or equivalent constitutional documents, details of directors/shareholders, and evidence of the NZ nexus (e.g., Shopify store agreements, payment processor accounts, NZ bank account documents, or warehouse/fulfilment contracts). Processing generally takes 8–10 business days. For GST registration specifically, the company must register online via myIR or file form IR356, and the IRD number is assigned to the GST account upon approval.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (a non-resident company is taxed in New Zealand at the 28% corporate rate on New Zealand-sourced income under the Income Tax Act 2007, on a Territorial_Scope basis; the Cook Islands International Company has Tax_Exempt_Zone status in its home jurisdiction, but that status does not exempt the entity from New Zealand income tax or GST where it has a New Zealand sourcing nexus or carries on taxable activity in New Zealand) — A Cook Islands International Company that derives New Zealand-sourced income, carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand, or is required to register for GST must obtain a New Zealand IRD number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a non-resident company is taxed in New Zealand at the 28% corporate rate on New Zealand-sourced income under the Income Tax Act 2007, on a Territorial_Scope basis; the Cook Islands International Company has Tax_Exempt_Zone status in its home jurisdiction, but that status does not exempt the entity from New Zealand income tax or GST where it has a New Zealand sourcing nexus or carries on taxable activity in New Zealand)

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. The relevant statutory elections and mechanisms available to a non-resident e-commerce company include: (1) voluntary GST registration under section 51(3) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when annual taxable supplies are below the NZD 60,000 threshold; (2) GST taxable-period election among monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly return filing cycles under section 15 of the GST Act; (3) GST grouping election for related companies under section 55 of the GST Act; (4) the branch-equivalent election for attributed Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) income under section EX 22 of the Income Tax Act 2007, where applicable; and (5) the general election to file a New Zealand income tax return and claim allowable deductions as a non-resident taxpayer. Hybrid mismatch rules enacted under BEPS (Income Tax Act 2007, sections FH 1–FH 14) apply mandatorily and are not subject to elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (zero-rated and exempt supplies excluded) of total taxable supplies made in New Zealand or to New Zealand consumers in any 12-month period; for non-resident suppliers of remote services and digital goods (including Shopify DTC sales of downloadable products or cross-border services), a special registration obligation under Part 7 of the GST Act triggers at NZD 60,000 of supplies to New Zealand-resident recipients in a 12-month period; the threshold is calculated on a rolling 12-month look-back and prospective test.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods applies under section 11(1)(a) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply (or within such further time as the Commissioner of Inland Revenue allows), and the supplier holds the requisite documentary evidence that the goods have been physically removed from New Zealand. Services supplied by a non-resident or NZ-resident supplier may be zero-rated under section 11(2) if the services are supplied to a non-resident recipient and are performed outside New Zealand, or under other specific zero-rating provisions (e.g., services in connection with land outside New Zealand, international transport, or certain intellectual property supplies to non-residents). For a Shopify DTC brand selling physical consumer goods abroad from New Zealand, zero-rating is available only where the goods leave New Zealand under export through the New Zealand Customs Service and the supplier's accounting records evidence the export sale, shipping, and payment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (full export entry filed with New Zealand Customs Service via Trade Single Window / Customs Export System, including approved exporter code, tariff classification, value, and destination)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of the goods from New Zealand to the overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or supporting bank/payment-provider statement evidencing receipt of payment in foreign currency or via international payment rails (Shopify Payments, PayPal, Stripe) from the overseas customer

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or live tax authority clearance exists for e-invoicing in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Peppol interoperability framework; GST-registered businesses, including non-resident suppliers, must issue valid tax invoices for taxable supplies above NZD 200 inclusive of GST (or NZD 50 for certain supplies), retain equivalent electronic records, and submit periodic GST returns and payments electronically through myIR. Non-resident suppliers registered for GST on remote services may issue simplified tax invoices in electronic form without a New Zealand business number. Inland Revenue does not require API-based clearance of each transaction; however, GST refunds and zero-rated export supplies are routinely subject to automated data matching (including Customs export records, payment processor data, and bank data) and can trigger audit verification before refund release.

Inland Revenue applies strict GST integrity measures to zero-rated export claims. If the goods are not exported within the statutory 28-day period (or extended period), the supply is recharacterized as standard-rated and the supplier must account for GST at 15% on the original consideration, plus use-of-money interest under section 120 of the Tax Administration Act 1994. Where zero-rating is claimed without adequate export documentation, or where false documentation is provided, shortfall penalties apply under sections 141 and 141A–141E of the Tax Administration Act: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100–150% for abusive tax positions or evasion, with criminal prosecution possible under section 143A for willful evasion or knowingly providing false information. Additionally, the Commissioner may cancel or amend GST registration, issue default assessments on a reconstructed basis, deny refunds under section 46 of the GST Act where a refund claim is excessive or fraudulent, apply evidence-preservation requirements, and use data-matching with Customs and payment processors for retrospective clawback of wrongly claimed zero-rating, including recoveries from a Cook Islands International Company by attachment of New Zealand assets or via Mutual Assistance in Tax Recovery under New Zealand's comprehensive double tax agreements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction with fully free current-account and capital-account convertibility. The New Zealand dollar is freely floating and there are no exchange-control approvals required for profit repatriation, dividend remittances, loan repayments, royalty payments, capital injections, or liquidation distributions. The only relevant regulatory gate for foreign investment is the Overseas Investment Act 2005, but an e-commerce DTC International Company with no sensitive New Zealand land or significant business assets will not require Overseas Investment Office consent. No withholding or convertibility surcharge is imposed by the exchange-control regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand does not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender requirement for export receipts. A DTC brand may retain foreign-currency balances in any bank account, settle in any convertible currency, and convert to NZD at the prevailing market rate. Only routine balance-of-payments statistical reporting via Statistics New Zealand or Reserve Bank of New Zealand industry surveys may apply at prescribed transaction thresholds, but this is not a foreign-exchange control filing.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High but operationally limited. Offshore digital EMI providers are not New Zealand licensed banks and cannot legally accept deposits or provide full regulated banking services in New Zealand. They may be used as supplementary payment collection or settlement rails for Shopify, PayPal, and Stripe flows, but they cannot replace a licensed New Zealand bank for capital escrow, statutory corporate treasury management, or SWIFT-cleared trade settlements. Any EMI collection must be layered downstream to a licensed NZ operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-liquidity transfers must be routed using SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the New Zealand operating account. The bank will require a clear payment purpose such as 'Share capital contribution to International Company' or 'Shareholder loan for e-commerce working capital', together with the entity's NZ bank account number and BIC. The inward remittance will be settled in NZD via the Reserve Bank of New Zealand ESAS real-time gross settlement system. No exchange-control approval is needed, but the bank may require a completed source-of-funds pack, a corporate board resolution, and a tax characterization note confirming whether the injection is equity (non-taxable) or debt (subject to New Zealand non-resident withholding tax on interest).Outbound trade payments for inventory, suppliers, and DTC logistics must be cleared via SWIFT MT103 from the licensed New Zealand bank to the beneficiary's correspondent bank. For high-value or letter-of-credit transactions, the bank may issue an irrevocable commercial letter of credit through SWIFT MT700/MT710, with shipping and commercial documents checked against the LC terms. All disbursements must be supported by underlying order contracts, supplier invoices, and shipping records; payments to sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions will be blocked. Mandatory Return Breakdown

For a standard 31 March balance date, the IR4 is due by 7 July of the following income year if filed electronically, or 7 June if filed on paper. For non-standard balance dates, the due date is generally the 7th day of the month following the earlier of the tax return issue date (anniversary rule). The terminal tax payment is due by 7 April following the balance date, or 7 February for non-standard balance dates with a provisional tax top-up. Provisional tax is payable in three instalments (28 August, 15 January, 7 May for 31 March balance date). Inland Revenue may grant extensions for tax agents.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand has no statutory monetary threshold for transfer pricing documentation, but Inland Revenue's Transfer Pricing Guidelines indicate that a Local File is expected for cross-border related-party transactions exceeding NZD 2 million in aggregate value, and a Master File is generally expected for multinational groups with revenue over NZD 60 million. For a DTC brand using Shopify, cross-border payments to related parties (e.g. IP royalties, management fees, advertising recharges) must comply with the arm's length principle. If aggregate covered transactions exceed NZD 10 million, a transfer pricing arrangement may be subject to enhanced scrutiny. Even below thresholds, documentation should be prepared contemporaneously for transactions involving intangibles and hard-to-value services.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a separate 'Economic Substance Regulations' statute like the UAE or Cayman Islands, but economic substance is central to tax avoidance analysis. The general anti-avoidance rule under BG1/Part 5A recharacterises arrangements lacking commercial substance. For a Cook Islands International Company acting as a holding or IP vehicle, New Zealand tax authorities may disregard the entity if it has no real management, employees, or decision-making in the Cook Islands. Also, New Zealand applies the Principal Purpose Test based on the MLI in most tax treaties to deny treaty benefits where one of the principal purposes is to obtain treaty benefits. For NZ domestic purposes, a company must demonstrate real activity to satisfy 'tax avoidance arrangement' tests. For Cook Islands ICs, the Cook Islands International Companies Act 2022 requires a registered agent and registered office in the Cook Islands, but no minimum economic substance requirement; however, Inland Revenue may still attack artificial arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Legal sanction: imprisonment up to 5 years under Tax Administration Act 1994 s 143B; fine up to NZD 50,000 per offence (companies) plus additional daily penalties of NZD 10,000 per month for continuing evasion under s 143A. Civil penalty: 100% of the shortfall as an evasion penalty under s 141D, plus use-of-money interest. Inland Revenue may also garnish bank accounts, seize assets, and issue asset departure orders to prevent removal from NZ.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable if they knowingly participate in or authorise evasion, or allow the company to trade recklessly. The Companies Act 1993 s 300 imposes recovery from directors of company tax liabilities if they act in breach of duty, and s 386 may impose criminal liability on officers. Travel bans under the Tax Administration Act s 44A can be issued against directors to prevent departure while tax debt exists. Imprisonment possible for personal guilt. Directors may be disqualified for up to 10 years.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may recharacterise income under transfer pricing rules (Sections GC1-GC8). Adjustments increase taxable income, shortfall penalties of 20% to 100% may apply, and UOMI accrues. Failure to produce TP documentation on time can lead to penalty of NZD 250 per day. Intentional profit shifting can amount to tax evasion and face criminal prosecution. Additionally, the Pt 5A non-recognition rule may void the arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors who prepare or sign false transfer pricing documentation can be held personally liable for shortfall penalties under s 141 (evasion or abusive tax position). Directors may also be personally liable for the company's tax shortfall if they acted fraudulently or were the directing mind of the mispricing. Civil penalties up to 100% of the tax shortfall, plus potential criminal liability. Travel bans and asset freezing orders are possible.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: New Zealand: Inland Revenue can disregard the entity under the general anti-avoidance rule (BG1/Part 5A), reconstruct the arrangement, and assess tax as if the transaction occurred without the shell. Penalties: 40% gross carelessness or 100% abusive tax position penalty. Directors and shareholders face personal liability under the Companies Act for fraudulent trading. For Cook Islands ICs, failure to appoint a registered agent or register under the IC Act can lead to company strike-off and penalties. AML and beneficial ownership failures impose additional fines (see AML red flag).

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell company are personally liable for fraudulent trading and for debts of the company if it was run for a dishonest purpose. Courts may pierce the corporate veil and order restitution of diverted funds. Director disqualification, travel bans, and asset seizure are available. Imprisonment up to 5 years for tax fraud. In the Cook Islands, directors may be criminally liable for false declarations or for permitting the company to be used for tax evasion.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: New Zealand: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a reporting entity that fails to comply faces civil penalties up to NZD 2 million for a company and NZD 300,000 for individuals per contravention. Criminal penalties: up to 2 years imprisonment for false information and up to 5 years for money laundering (Crimes Act 1961 s 243). Cook Islands: International companies must file beneficial ownership returns with the Financial Supervisory Commission; penalties for non-filing are up to USD 25,000 per month and officers can be imprisoned up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of an AML/CFT reporting entity are personally liable for the entity's AML deficiencies. Under the AML/CFT Act, a director can be sued personally for civil penalty amounts if they knowingly fail to implement a compliance programme. Money laundering conviction carries up to 5 years imprisonment per offence (7 years for aggravated money laundering under s 243A). Directors also face travel bans, asset confiscation, and disqualification.
