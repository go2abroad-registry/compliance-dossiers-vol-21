Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake), Wellington, New Zealand; statutory portal: myIR (www.ird.govt.nz); corporate registry interface: New Zealand Companies Office and New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) register.

E-government filing portal

A qualifying non-resident International Company not locally incorporated must obtain an 8- or 9-digit IRD number (single national identifier used for income tax, GST, and employer obligations). Step-by-step SOP: (1) determine whether the entity has a physical presence, permanent establishment, or NZ-source income requiring registration; (2) complete Form IR742 (Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application) supported by certified incorporation documents, constitutional documents, director register extracts, and a certificate of tax residence from the jurisdiction of incorporation; (3) if the entity carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand or makes supplies to NZ consumers exceeding the GST threshold, register for GST via myIR (same IRD number; no separate VAT/GST identifier exists); (4) where no NZ tax liability arises, apply for a 'non-resident alien' status code to avoid automatic PAYE obligations; (5) obtain the IRD number confirmation letter and file returns electronically through myIR. A locally incorporated NZ subsidiary must first obtain an NZBN through the Companies Office, after which Inland Revenue automatically issues the IRD number.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A qualifying non-resident International Company not locally incorporated must obtain an 8- or 9-digit IRD number (single national identifier used for income tax, GST, and employer obligations). Step-by-step SOP: (1) determine whether the entity has a physical presence, permanent establishment, or NZ-source income requiring registration; (2) complete Form IR742 (Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application) supported by certified incorporation documents, constitutional documents, director register extracts, and a certificate of tax residence from the jurisdiction of incorporation; (3) if the entity carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand or makes supplies to NZ consumers exceeding the GST threshold, register for GST via myIR (same IRD number; no separate VAT/GST identifier exists); (4) where no NZ tax liability arises, apply for a 'non-resident alien' status code to avoid automatic PAYE obligations; (5) obtain the IRD number confirmation letter and file returns electronically through myIR. A locally incorporated NZ subsidiary must first obtain an NZBN through the Companies Office, after which Inland Revenue automatically issues the IRD number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections include: (a) voluntary GST registration under section 51 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for persons carrying on a taxable activity, enabling input tax recovery and zero-rating of offshore supplies; (b) the zero-rating election for services supplied to non-residents under section 11A(1)(k) of the GST Act; (c) GST grouping election under section 55 of the GST Act for related companies with common ownership; (d) the hybrid and branch mismatch rules (Part FH of the Income Tax Act 2007) allowing taxpayers to elect alternative treatment where a mismatch arises from foreign PE throwback rules, provided symmetry of deductions is maintained; (e) for passive foreign equity income, the Fair Dividend Rate (FDR) versus Comparative Value election; and (f) for inbound interest, the election to apply the 2% Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) instead of Non-Resident Withholding Tax on qualifying cross-border debt. For an NZ-resident look-through company, the LTC election under section HB 1 is available but is not applicable to a foreign international company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of actual or expected taxable supplies over any rolling 12-month period; compulsory registration applies once the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold, and non-resident suppliers may register voluntarily to claim input tax credits or to zero-rate exports. Associated-person aggregation rules apply to prevent fragmentation of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For services (including international tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory): zero-rating applies under section 11A(1)(k) of the GST Act where the recipient is a non-resident located outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, the services are not supplied to another person in New Zealand in connection with a taxable activity carried on by that person in New Zealand, and the supplier does not know or have reason to suspect the recipient is in New Zealand when the services are performed. Additionally, services that are physically performed outside New Zealand qualify for zero-rating regardless of the recipient's residence. For goods: zero-rating applies if the supplier exports the goods within 28 days of the time of supply (extended to 60 days for goods exported under applicable customs procedures), the goods are landed outside New Zealand within the statutory timeframes, and documentary evidence of export is retained.

  • New Zealand Customs Electronic Export Declaration lodged through the Trade Single Window (CusMOD), showing HS classification, value, exporter identity, and offshore destination (required for goods exports)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export and foreign landing within the statutory timelines (28/60-day rules)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 bank confirmation from the NZ or offshore paying bank proving receipt of foreign currency from the non-resident buyer
  • Binding engagement contract for the advisory services specifying the non-resident recipient, offshore place of performance, fee structure, and scope of the tax structuring and transfer pricing assignment
  • Certificate of Tax Residency or equivalent official confirmation of the client issued by the client's home tax authority, valid for the year of supply
  • Board resolution or written client confirmation that the services are not consumed in connection with a New Zealand taxable activity, together with client identity and beneficial ownership documentation
  • Supplier's internal work papers evidencing that the services were physically performed outside New Zealand (travel records, time logs, and delivery location evidence)

No real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory national digital stamping, and no statutory pre-clearance API requirement. New Zealand has adopted the PEPPOL network for e-invoicing on a voluntary basis; Inland Revenue encourages e-invoicing through the NZBN but does not mandate it. Tax invoices must satisfy the content requirements of sections 24 and 24B of the GST Act, and buyers, sellers, or the Commissioner may apply for a ruling approving alternative invoicing arrangements. GST returns must be filed electronically via myIR on a monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly cycle, with digital record-keeping for audit purposes.

Inland Revenue conducts targeted audits of GST export zero-rating claims, particularly for professional services exported to related parties. Where zero-rating is incorrectly claimed, the Commissioner assesses the unpaid GST (15%) together with use-of-money interest (currently over 9% per annum, compounding daily). Statutory shortfall penalties apply under section 141 of the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. Retrospective assessments are not time-barred in cases of fraud or misleading conduct; otherwise the four-year adjustment period applies. Criminal prosecution under sections 143 and 144 of the Tax Administration Act can result in fines up to NZD 50,000, and for offshore entities, Inland Revenue may deregister the GST number, impose GST on the full consideration, apply anti-avoidance reconstructions under section 76 of the GST Act, and issue non-resident withholding assessments. Directors and controlling officers face personal liability under the director penalty regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully floating, freely convertible currency with no general exchange-control regime. Both current-account transactions (consultancy fees, royalties, dividends, interest) and capital-account transactions (equity injection, intercompany loans, liquidation distributions) are generally repatriable without central bank approval. Profit remittance is subject only to ordinary New Zealand tax withholding (non-resident withholding tax for interest/dividends/royalties and approved issuer levy where available), AML/CFT source-of-funds verification, and, for certain controlling investments in material New Zealand assets, Overseas Investment Office consent. Non-residents may hold, buy, and sell NZD without restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation or conversion of export receipts. Earned foreign currency may be retained offshore, cleared into an onshore NZD or multi-currency account, or hedged freely, subject only to normal tax declaration and AML/CFT documentary requirements.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), acting under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021; the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) of Toitū Te Whenua Land Information New Zealand exercises complementary screening over foreign acquisitions of significant business assets and sensitive land.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: High as a supplementary payment-clearing rail, Medium as a full treasury custody provider. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) may lawfully execute cross-border SWIFT and open-API payments to and from New Zealand accounts, and may hold segregated client money, but they do not hold a New Zealand banking authorisation and therefore cannot accept New Zealand deposit liabilities, issue licensed lending products, or participate directly in the local high-value settlement system (ESAS). Such providers must register on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register where their services are provided to New Zealand persons, must be licensed/regulated in a recognised home jurisdiction, and should maintain a local correspondent arrangement with an NZ-registered trading bank for NZD clearing.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% statutory offshore exemption for the Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC); New Zealand domestic corporate income tax headline rate of 28% applies only if the IC is treated as resident in New Zealand by incorporation, management and control, or CFC attribution.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% under Cook Islands law for distributions by an International Company to non-resident shareholders; 30% under New Zealand NRWT if the IC is treated as NZ-resident, reduced to 15% for fully imputed dividends and typically 15% (or 5% for a 10%+ voting interest) under applicable DTAs.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 60 days after the anniversary of incorporation; the annual license fee is payable concurrently; fiscal-year-end conventions do not apply to the exempt International Company.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a standard 31 March balance date, the IR4 return is due by 7 July following the income year; approved tax agents obtain an extension to 31 March of the following year; non-standard balance dates are subject to IRD-directed filing dates, generally within six months of the balance date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$10 million aggregate cross-border related-party consideration per income year under Tax Administration Act 1994 s 78C for Master File and Local File preparation; IR453 International Dealings Schedule disclosure threshold of NZ$1 million per related-party transaction category; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: The Cook Islands Economic Substance regime and the OECD substance-over-form doctrine apply to the International Company; core income-generating activities, local premises, local employees, and active board decision-making in the Cook Islands must be demonstrated; a New Zealand-managed and controlled IC remains exposed to NZ GAAR challenge and loss of offshore exemption where the arrangement lacks commercial rationale.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Abusive Tax Avoidance (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1 with reconstruction of the arrangement; shortfall penalties of 20%-40% for abusive avoidance and 100%-150% for evasion under Tax Administration Act 1994 ss 141C-141E; use-of-money interest on the full shortfall; criminal conviction for tax evasion: imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to NZ$50,000.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the IC is an alter ego of the directors; directors and trust protectors are jointly and severally liable for reconstructed tax, penalties, and interest; courts may impose civil restitution orders, asset freezing, border alert travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years for aiding evasion.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Cook Islands monetary penalties of up to NZD 50,000 per contravention; deregistration and strike-off by the Registrar of International Companies; forfeiture of exempt status; in New Zealand, GAAR adjustment reclassifying the income as NZ-source with shortfall penalties up to 150% and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors face disqualification from holding directorships, personal fines for false substance declarations, liability for deregistration and reinstatement costs, and examination orders supported by travel bans where non-compliance is suspected.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Cook Islands AML/CFT administrative fines of up to NZD 250,000 per breach and criminal penalties for unlicensed activity; New Zealand AML/CFT Act 2009 pecuniary penalties up to NZ$2 million for bodies corporate and NZ$300,000 for individuals; confiscation orders under proceeds-of-crime legislation.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and directors face personal criminal liability for wilful contraventions, imprisonment up to 5 years, disqualification as directors, and travel bans in investor-protection proceedings; beneficial owners who conceal control are jointly liable for penalties.
Fraudulent Trading, Insolvency Abuse and Director Veil-Piercing

Statutory sanction: Cook Islands fraudulent trading liability under the International Companies Act with liquidation claw-back and compensation orders; New Zealand Companies Act 1993 s 380 liability of directors for company debts; criminal sanctions of up to 5 years' imprisonment and fines up to NZ$200,000.

Director personal liability: Courts lift the corporate veil and impose unlimited personal liability for debts, equitable compensation, restitution of dissipated assets, travel bans pending liquidation examinations, and custodial sentences; cross-border judgments are enforceable under reciprocal arrangements between the Cook Islands and New Zealand.
