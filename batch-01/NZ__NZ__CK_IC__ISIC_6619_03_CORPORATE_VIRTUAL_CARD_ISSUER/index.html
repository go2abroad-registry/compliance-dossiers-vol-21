Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office (Registrar of Companies), Consumer Protection and Compliance, Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), Level 6, 15 Stout Street, Wellington 6011, New Zealand; online register: www.companiesoffice.govt.nz

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), ss 12 (Registration of company), 14 (Registrar's duty before incorporation), 15 (Certificate of incorporation), 16 (Effect of registration and full capacity), 28-36 (Constitution), 46 (Shares and consideration), 96 (Pre-emptive rights), 180 (Registered office), 150-153 (Directors), and 386-387 (Restoration/removal) — read together with the Companies (Incorporation) Regulations 2013. The 'International Company' (IC) designation is a constitutional and share-structuring overlay on a private limited liability company; it is not a distinct statutory vehicle under New Zealand law. The IC tax envelope relies on non-resident ownership, non-resident taxation of non-New Zealand sourced card-issuance revenue, and the absence of a New Zealand permanent establishment.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form 2 — Application for Incorporation of a New Company submitted electronically through the New Zealand Companies Register; supported by Form 4 — Consent to Act as Director (and Shareholder consent where applicable), a formal Constitution (where adopted), registered-office premises consent, and notarized identity dossiers. The Certificate of Incorporation is issued electronically by the Registrar upon effective registration.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Companies Act 1993; incorporation may proceed with zero or nominal subscribed capital, and no escrow account, capital guarantee, or trust reserve is required. For the IC envelope, one subscriber share of NZ$1 is routinely fully paid, with further funding advanced as non-resident shareholder loan accounts carried at commercial arm's length; the Companies Office confirms no minimum capital threshold for FSP registration of a virtual card issuer, although the FMA-sponsored e-money risk practice recommends maintaining at least NZ$50,000 net tangible equity for prudential credibility.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand; online portal: myIR at https://ird.govt.nz.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated International Company that is not a New Zealand resident must first register with the New Zealand Companies Office as an overseas company (or incorporate locally) and then apply for a New Zealand IRD number on Form IR596 (Company IRD Number Application) via the myIR portal or by post to Inland Revenue, attaching the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, directors' details, and evidence of a New Zealand address for service. The IRD number is issued within 8-10 business days. The same registration form contains a check-box for GST registration, which simultaneously activates the GST account where a taxable activity exists or is intended.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Territorial application: non-resident International Company is taxable in New Zealand only on New Zealand-source income under sections YD 1-YD 4 of the Income Tax Act 2007; offshore virtual-card issuing, interchange, and treasury income is outside the New Zealand tax net, and no New Zealand sub-national income tax is imposed).

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box or deemed-classification system. The income tax treatment is statutory rather than elective. A non-resident International Company is taxed at the corporate rate of 28%, except that effective 'tax-exempt zone' outcomes for offshore income arise automatically under the territorial rules. Election mechanisms available under New Zealand law include: voluntary GST registration under section 51 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; GST grouping election under section 55; GST branch registration election under section 54; and the former 'qualifying company' election and look-through company (LTC) election under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, but LTC status is generally unavailable to a non-resident International Company with non-resident shareholders. Hybrid mismatch rules under Subpart FH (OECD BEPS Action 2) operate automatically and disallow tax advantages from hybrid financial dealings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies made in New Zealand (including zero-rated exported services but excluding exempt financial services) in the current or immediately preceding 12-month period. Compulsory registration applies to any non-resident supplier once this threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is available from the first dollar, with refund entitlement conditioned upon registration being compulsory or the supplier conducting an exporting business.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under sections 11(1)(a)-(e) of the GST Act where goods are physically exported to a destination outside New Zealand within 28 days of supply, supplied as stores for international aircraft or ships, or supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the goods are subsequently exported. Services: zero-rated under section 11A of the GST Act where (a) the services are supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are not supplied under an arrangement with a New Zealand-resident person, or (b) the services are performed directly in connection with goods exported by the supplier, or (c) the services are financial services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed. For the corporate virtual-card issuer, card issuing, transaction processing, interchange, spend-control, and settlement services supplied to non-resident corporate clients (merchants or cardholders) qualify as zero-rated exported financial services provided the recipient is not in New Zealand at the time of supply and the services do not relate to New Zealand land, goods, or a New Zealand resident. Services supplied to New Zealand-resident cardholders or merchants are exempt financial services under section 14(1)(a) of the GST Act, which are neither taxable nor zero-rated and therefore do not create input tax recovery rights.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Export Entry (electronic declaration via Trade Single Window / Class ImpEx) for physical goods movements
  • Bill of Lading / House Air Waybill / Master Air Waybill evidencing export from New Zealand within 28 days of supply
  • Inward Foreign Currency Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 from a registered bank proving payment for the exported supply
  • Signed service agreement or card programme agreement identifying the non-resident customer's jurisdiction and confirming place of consumption
  • Non-residence evidence for each recipient (foreign certificate of incorporation, tax residence certificate, or foreign registry extract)
  • GST invoice issued in accordance with section 24 of the GST Act, explicitly stating 'zero-rated' and citing section 11A for exported services

No real-time fiscal clearance, no pre-clearance API, and no digital stamping regime is imposed by the Inland Revenue Department. e-Invoicing is voluntary and operates on the PEPPOL network under the New Zealand e-Invoicing programme. GST invoices must include the supplier's name, IRD/GST number, NZBN, invoice date, description and quantity of supply, consideration and tax included; invoices must be issued within 28 days of the end of the taxable period. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR on monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly cycles. Transactions are not subject to government fiscal API clearance; however, Mastercard and Visa commercial interchange reporting is contractually mandatory for the card-issuing fintech but does not constitute a tax-clearance mechanism.

The Inland Revenue Department heavily audits zero-rating claims under the Tax Administration Act 1994 (TAA) and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. If zero-rating is improperly claimed, the supply is treated as standard-rated; the IRD will reassess the full 15% GST with retrospective clawback for the entire audit period, plus use-of-money interest (currently 9.33% per annum). Shortfall penalties under section 141 of the TAA escalate from 20% for lack of reasonable care to 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for knowingly obtaining an incorrect tax advantage, and 150% for evasion. Criminal prosecution may be initiated under sections 143A and 143B of the TAA with fines up to NZD 50,000 per offence and imprisonment for up to 5 years for tax evasion or knowingly providing false GST returns. False customs export declarations are separately sanctionable under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, with penalties up to NZD 10,000 per declaration, plus potential deregistration from the approved exporter scheme and publication of the adverse assessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a freely convertible currency regime under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no exchange control laws requiring central bank approval for current account or capital account transactions. Profit remittances, dividends, interest, and capital repatriations are permitted without quantitative restriction, subject only to withholding taxes (RWT/NRWT), the Overseas Investment Act 2023 consent thresholds in cases involving sensitive land or significant business assets, and standard bank anti-money-laundering due diligence. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully fungible across domestic and offshore liquidity pools, and there are no surrender, rationing, or multiple-tier exchange rate mechanisms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand has no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain foreign currency earnings in New Zealand or offshore accounts without obligation to convert into NZD. Inward and outward remittances are subject to monetary reporting thresholds for balance of payments statistical purposes only, and settlement banks generally require a narrative purpose code and supporting commercial documents for large transactions.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), as the central banking authority for registered banks, settlement systems, and foreign exchange clearing infrastructure; the Financial Markets Authority and Department of Internal Affairs serve as co-regulators for AML/CFT supervision of non-bank financial institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Conditional / Restricted. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not eligible for a New Zealand registered bank license without incorporating a local prudentially regulated entity. An offshore EMI may operate as a non-bank payment service provider only through a locally incorporated New Zealand company registered under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and enrolled in an approved dispute resolution scheme, while also satisfying the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act). However, major New Zealand banks generally classify offshore EMIs as high-risk financial institution counterparties and will either decline account opening or require a physical New Zealand office, local independent directors, comprehensive compliance audit statements, and a formal settlement account arrangement with a licensed sponsor bank. End-to-end account opening through a pure digital EMI without local substance is unlikely to succeed.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the New Zealand International Company should be routed through the settlement bank using SWIFT MT103 with the following protocol: (i) remitter field must reference the shareholder name and country of source; (ii) field 70 (Remittance Information) must include the capital purpose code `Supplemental Capital Contribution` and the unique board resolution reference; (iii) field 72 may include authorized signature identifiers; (iv) for USD/EUR allocations, use the bank's correspondent account and confirm the final beneficiary is the regression-validated New Zealand entity account. Supporting documentation must be pre-delivered to the receiving bank: updated Certificate of Incorporation, board resolution approving the capital raise, shareholder subscription agreement, investor source-of-funds declaration, and any Overseas Investment Act 2023 clearance letter if the injection crosses thresholds applicable to non-resident acquisitions. As New Zealand does not maintain exchange control approval, banks routinely process such payments in original currency without buy/sell restriction; however, the bank may impose a temporary hold until UBO verification and purpose-code validation are completed.Outbound trade disbursements are executed through (a) direct cross-border payments via SWIFT MT103 for supplier payments, license fees, and intercompany settlements; (b) commercial letters of credit opened through the sponsoring bank's trade finance platform, using MT700 for issuance and MT760 for guarantee issuance; (c) NZD-denominated domestic payments through the bank's clearing product using the New Zealand Clearing House System (AONZ), with same-day or next-day settlement depending on the clearing batch; and (d) high-value settlements through the Reserve Bank's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) via real-time gross settlement. For virtual card issuer treasury flows, settlement files are accrued in the card scheme's internal settlement ledger and net-settled on T+1 using SWIFT paid-or-received messages, with the final funding leg in NZD via domestic SWIFT or in USD/EUR via the bank's correspondent network. The bank should be notified of the anticipated settlement schedule and permitted to review the scheme rules for mandatory funding buffers.Deliverable spot and outright forward contracts (FXA) in NZD/USD, NZD/EUR, NZD/GBP, and AUD/NZD for core treasury exposuresNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in restricted or non-clearing currency pairs, such as CNH, KRW, INR, and TWD, where required to hedge non-convertible counterparty exposures through a licensed New Zealand dealerCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for minimizing interest and principal cash flow mismatch in borrowing structuresEuropean-style FX options and outright option combinations for downside protection on projected virtual card settlement volumesForeign currency swap and rolling swap arrangements executed under a valid ISDA and New Zealand Financial Markets Conduct Act exemption or dealer licensing arrangement

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Conditional / Restricted. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not eligible for a New Zealand registered bank license without incorporating a local prudentially regulated entity. An offshore EMI may operate as a non-bank payment service provider only through a locally incorporated New Zealand company registered under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and enrolled in an approved dispute resolution scheme, while also satisfying the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act). However, major New Zealand banks generally classify offshore EMIs as high-risk financial institution counterparties and will either decline account opening or require a physical New Zealand office, local independent directors, comprehensive compliance audit statements, and a formal settlement account arrangement with a licensed sponsor bank. End-to-end account opening through a pure digital EMI without local substance is unlikely to succeed.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% (standard New Zealand corporate tax rate for resident companies under Part A of Schedule 1, Income Tax Act 2007)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends is 30% (or 15% for fully imputed dividends paid to non-residents). DTA reductions apply.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand legislation does not prescribe a fixed NZD de minimis; contemporaneous documentation is expected for all attributable cross-border controlled transactions. Mandatory Master File/Local File and Country-by-Country Report thresholds apply to multinational groups with global consolidated turnover exceeding NZ$130 million.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have domestic Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) analogous to offshore centres. Instead, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and source rules require a genuine New Zealand fixed establishment for non-resident business profits, and the AML/CFT Act 2009 mandates substantive presence, NZ-based officers, and on-site compliance systems for payment services.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty up to 300% of the tax shortfall (Tax Administration Act 1994, ss 141E-K); criminal liability under s 143A for evasion with imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to NZ$150,000; unpaid tax, interest and UOMI remain recoverable.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate are jointly and severally liable for the shortfall and may face civil restitution, asset restraint orders, court-imposed travel bans during criminal proceedings, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner can reconstruct the arrangement under the GAAR (s BG1) and apply transfer pricing adjustments. An 'abusive tax position' triggers a 100% shortfall penalty, and failure to provide documents may produce a 20% administratively imposed penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing documentation are exposed to director penalties under the Tax Administration Act, may be disqualified under the Companies Act 1993, and can be the subject of civil recovery proceedings; travel restrictions may be imposed pending tax debt investigations.
AML/CFT and Unlicensed Payment Activity

Statutory sanction: Under the AML/CFT Act 2009, a body corporate faces civil penalties up to NZ$2 million per breach, and individuals up to NZ$200,000; criminal liability for money laundering carries up to 7 years' imprisonment and fines up to NZ$300,000. De-registration from the FSP register and public enforcement notices also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers may be personally prosecuted for knowingly facilitating money laundering, receive civil fines, be made subject to asset forfeiture and restitution orders, be forbidden from leaving New Zealand pending trial, and be disqualified from managing companies.
