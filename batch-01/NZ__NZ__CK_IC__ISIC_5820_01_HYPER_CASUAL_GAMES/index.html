Competent corporate registry

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. A company may allot one or more shares of no par value (Companies Act 1993, s 117); issued share capital may be nominal. There is no capital duty and no escrow account requirement for an ISIC 5820 software-publishing company under New Zealand law. Non-resident ownership is permitted and no bank escrow of capital is mandated before trading commences.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: the subject premises must maintain a Building Warrant of Fitness under the Building Act 2004, including the building's fire safety schedule and compliance inspection by an Independent Qualified Person (IQP), together with means-of-escape compliance under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017. Structural load clearance: the existing building must hold an up-to-date Code Compliance Certificate or Building Warrant of Fitness confirming structural adequacy and life-safety design; for leased offices, reliance on the landlord's current warrant of fitness is acceptable. Occupational clearance: under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, the company as a PCBU must provide a safe office workplace, an emergency plan, first-aid provisions, ergonomic workstations, and electrical-safety checks; no special occupational licence is required for game development and publishing staff.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital. A company may allot one or more shares of no par value (Companies Act 1993, s 117); issued share capital may be nominal. There is no capital duty and no escrow account requirement for an ISIC 5820 software-publishing company under New Zealand law. Non-resident ownership is permitted and no bank escrow of capital is mandated before trading commences.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand; secure online portal 'myIR' at myir.ird.govt.nz, with official guidance at ird.govt.nz.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident International Company (CK_IC) that derives New Zealand-sourced income, carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand, or becomes liable to register for New Zealand GST (including under the remote-services and imported-low-value-goods rules) must obtain a New Zealand IRD number before or at the time of GST registration. The foreign entity applies using Inland Revenue form IR742 (Registration for Non-Resident Companies) or the myIR online non-resident registration channel, submitting certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, the constitution, the register of directors and shareholders, the registered office and agent details, and a statement confirming the absence of any New Zealand fixed establishment. No minimum capital contribution is required, and no New Zealand-resident director or shareholder is required for a tax-exempt offshore International Company. Upon issuance of the IRD number, the entity registers for GST through myIR using the non-resident supplier (remote services) track if its taxable supplies to New Zealand-resident consumers exceed the NZD 60,000 threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the CK_IC is classified as a tax-exempt offshore International Company: it is not a New Zealand tax resident, maintains no New Zealand fixed establishment or permanent establishment, and its non-New-Zealand-sourced revenue (AdMob and AppLovin advertising payouts) is outside the New Zealand income tax net. The entity nevertheless falls within the New Zealand indirect tax (GST) net to the extent it makes taxable supplies of remote services or imported goods to New Zealand-resident consumers and meets the registration threshold. New Zealand-sourced income, if any, would be subject to New Zealand income tax or non-resident withholding tax under ordinary principles.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a United States-style check-the-box entity election. The available statutory elections and designations include: (i) voluntary GST registration under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 before the NZD 60,000 threshold is met; (ii) election to account for GST on the payments basis instead of the invoice basis where the prescribed conditions for non-resident suppliers are satisfied; (iii) the application of the s 11A(1)(k) cross-border B2B zero-rating provision for services supplied to a non-resident GST-registered recipient, supported by the required documentary evidence; (iv) elections and notifications required to neutralise hybrid and branch mismatches under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 (including non-application or consistency elections for a New Zealand branch, offshore controlled entity, or hybrid payer); and (v) for any New Zealand-resident group members, the wholly-owned group election to treat members as a single GST person — generally unavailable to a tax-exempt non-resident IC standing alone.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; for non-resident suppliers of remote services and imported low-value goods to New Zealand-resident consumers, the same NZD 60,000 threshold (measured over 12 months) applies to digital supplies such as mobile game ad monetization and in-app content.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a GST-registered CK_IC may zero-rate: (a) exported goods where the goods are exported within 28 days of supply (s 11(1)(a)); (b) services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply (s 11A(1)(a)-(j)), provided the services are not performed directly in connection with New Zealand land or moveable property; and (c) cross-border B2B services supplied to a non-resident GST-registered business under s 11A(1)(k). For hyper-casual mobile games, programmatic advertising inventory sold to AdMob/AppLovin and digital content supplied to overseas users qualify for zero-rating as services rendered to non-residents outside New Zealand, subject to documentary substantiation. Revenue sourced from New Zealand-resident users is standard-rated at 15% once the entity is GST-registered.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window) — required only where physical goods are exported; not applicable to intangible game downloads or pure digital ad monetization
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill — required for physical export shipments; not applicable to digital-only supplies
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statements evidencing receipt of AdMob and AppLovin foreign-currency payouts
  • Platform payout reconciliation reports (AdMob and AppLovin dashboards) showing the geographic breakdown of users, transaction timestamps, and settlement amounts
  • Signed ad-network and app-store agreements demonstrating the non-resident status of the counterparties and the offshore location of the service recipients

New Zealand imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory digital stamping, and no API-based prior tax-authority approval for individual invoices. Peppol-based e-invoicing is voluntary. GST-registered persons, including non-resident remote-service suppliers, must file GST returns electronically (normally quarterly) through myIR with any net GST remittance due by the 28th day after the end of the taxable period, and must retain electronic records sufficient to establish the residence of New Zealand consumers and the geo-attribute of each digital transaction; no per-invoice pre-clearance is required.

Inland Revenue may reassess GST within four years from the end of the taxable period (extended to seven years where income was omitted or in cases of gross carelessness, and without time limit for fraud, evasion, or wilful misstatement). Incorrectly claimed zero-rating and undocumented export supplies are clawed back at the 15% GST rate plus use-of-money interest at the statutory underpayment rate. Shortfall penalties under ss 141-141E of the Tax Administration Act 1994 escalate from 20% (lack of reasonable care), 40% (unacceptable tax position), 50% (gross carelessness), and 100% (abusive tax position) to 150% (evasion). Criminal liability may arise under ss 143A and 144A of the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, with fines up to NZD 50,000 per offence and imprisonment of up to five years for tax evasion; directors, officers, and tax advisors who facilitate fraudulent export zero-rating may be exposed to the promoter penalty and personal liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates an open capital account with no standalone exchange-control legislation. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Profit and capital repatriation by a non-resident-owned International Company is unrestricted at the currency-conversion level. The only practical hurdles are ordinary tax withholding (non-resident withholding tax or any applicable double-tax-agreement relief), bank-level AML/CFT source-of-funds verification, and bank-mandated balance-of-payments or statistical reporting requirements for large cross-border transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation exists in New Zealand. There is no mandated timeline for conversion, no minimum conversion ratio, and no requirement to deposit export receipts with a designated domestic bank. A New Zealand exporter may retain receivables in foreign currency and convert at its own commercial discretion. Banks may, however, scrutinise unusual conversion patterns or high-risk transaction flows under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and may report suspicious activity to the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand - Te Pūtea Matua (monetary authority and prudential regulator); the Financial Markets Authority (AML/CFT conduct supervision and market discipline); the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) for any residual foreign-investment consent thresholds involving sensitive land, rural land, or fishing quota.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium as a complementary treasury rail, Low as the sole or primary banking relationship. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed as New Zealand registered banks and cannot directly access the Reserve Bank's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) or provide native New Zealand domestic bank account numbers. They may offer multicurrency accounts, card infrastructure, and faster wire routing through a local partner bank or payment aggregator, but New Zealand AML/CFT obligations still require underlying KYC at the participating New Zealand financial institution. A trusted tier-1 New Zealand banking relationship remains necessary for regulated escrow, capital contributions, liquidity management, and final foreign-exchange settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections should be wired to the beneficiary's New Zealand bank account using SWIFT MT103, or SWIFT MT202 COV for bank-to-bank cover when instructed. Field guidance: Field 50K or 50F contains the remitting account of the corporate parent or trust; Field 56A or 57A identifies the intermediary or beneficiary bank in New Zealand; Field 59 identifies the International Company's account with its New Zealand tier-1 bank; Field 70 should include 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION NZ IC' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN NZ IC' as a clear payment reference. New Zealand does not require a central-bank payment purpose code and does not pre-approve inward remittances. For NZD settlement, cleared funds are credited through the Reserve Bank's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS). For verification, the receiving bank may require an inward remittance certificate, a completed corporate authorisation form, and, for loans, an executed loan agreement.
