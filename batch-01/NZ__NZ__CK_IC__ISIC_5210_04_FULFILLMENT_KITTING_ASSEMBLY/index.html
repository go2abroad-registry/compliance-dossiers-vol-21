Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Companies Office, New Zealand Ministry of Business, Innovation, and Employment; incorporated under the Registrar of Companies; online lodgment via the New Zealand Companies Register (www.companiesoffice.govt.nz).

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), principally sections 10 (formation of company), 12 (certificate of incorporation), 15 (name of company), 18 (registered office), 26 (constitution), 33 (contents of constitution), 39 (powers of company), 46 (shares), 48 (shareholding principle), 122 (board of directors), 125 (number of directors), 126 (director qualifications), 128 (appointment of directors), 153 (appointment of secretary), 193 (share certificates), and 201 (company records). Where the entity qualifies as a corporate vehicle of an eligible non-resident owner, the foreign ownership and administrative provisions of the Companies Act 1993 and the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 (exemptions for private offers) apply; no separate authorization is required for non-resident incorporation.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of a Company (Form NZ/CO/001) together with the constitution (if adopted), directors' consent forms, and the Registrar's issued Certificate of Incorporation; electronic lodgment via the Companies Office register.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

New Zealand imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a limited liability company incorporated under the Companies Act 1993. The constitution or shareholders' resolution may authorize share capital denominated in NZD or any freely convertible currency. No escrow account, capital deposit, or bank confirmation is required at incorporation. The first director(s) must confirm in the application that each subscriber has agreed to take the number of shares stated, and consideration may be fully unpaid, paid in cash, or satisfied by property or services as recorded in the board resolution.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety: obtain a Fire Evacuation Scheme approval from Fire and Emergency New Zealand under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017, with a current fire safety compliance warrant for any building fit-out; maintain fire extinguishers, sprinkler systems, an evacuation plan, and an annual fire safety inspection certificate. Structural load: territorial building consent and a Structure Inspection report (or a Building Occupancy Certificate) confirming floor loading capacity suitable for racking and mezzanine storage (generally ≥5 kPa industrial floor; verify design and load path). Occupational clearance: register with WorkSafe New Zealand as a workplace, appoint a health and safety officer, complete an asbestos register if the building predates 2000, conduct a general risk assessment under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, and obtain a sanitation/facilities clearance from the territorial authority confirming adequate toilets, washing, ventilation, and first-aid facilities for the permitted workforce.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Tāke) of New Zealand; primary portal myIR (Secure Online Services) at https://myir.ird.govt.nz, with public guidance published at https://ird.govt.nz. Export/customs evidence is administered by the New Zealand Customs Service (Ngā Ara o te Aro o Aotearoa) via the Trade Single Window (TSW) electronic export lodgement system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Nexus determination: a non-resident International Company (CK_IC) that begins a taxable activity in New Zealand, including operating fulfillment, kitting, or assembly services using inventory located in NZ, must obtain an IRD number and, once turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed the NZ$60,000 GST threshold, register for GST. Step 2 - IRD number application: submit form IR596 (Application for an IRD number for a non-resident company), signed by a director or authorised officer, together with certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, register of directors, share register, and an ultimate beneficial ownership declaration, and appoint an authorised NZ contact or agent for service of notices. Step 3 - GST registration: complete form IR365 (GST registration) or apply through myIR, indicating the monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly filing basis selected under s 19 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; the effective GST registration date is determined by the Commissioner in accordance with s 51, and registration must be made within 21 days of becoming liable. Step 4 - Compliance functionalities: the IRD issues a nine-digit IRD number which also serves as the GST registration number; all GST returns and zero-rating substantiation are filed electronically via myIR or approved gateway software. Step 5 - Branch registration alternative: if the International Company registers as an overseas company on the NZ Companies Register, it is allocated an NZBN; the NZBN is not a substitute for the IRD number and does not alter non-resident tax treatment unless the entity becomes resident under s YD 2 of the Income Tax Act 2007.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - This is the pipeline-level composite classification assigned to the CK_IC structure. In New Zealand statutory terms, the International Company is treated as a non-resident company: it is not incorporated in NZ and has no head office, centre of management, or director control situated in NZ, so it is not a resident company under s YD 2 of the Income Tax Act 2007. As a non-resident, it is taxable in NZ only on NZ-sourced income attributable to a New Zealand permanent establishment, and it is exempt from entity-level NZ income tax on foreign-source income. The Tax_Exempt_Zone designation does not refer to any statutory geographic zone; New Zealand has no general income-tax-free geographic zone. A New Zealand permanent establishment or NZ-sourced income derived from kitting or fulfillment operations would be taxed at the 28% company rate.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not provide a US-style check-the-box election for entity classification. The elections commonly relevant in this structure are: (i) GST grouping election under s 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, allowing the International Company and its NZ-related entities to be treated as a single taxable person for GST; (ii) GST accounting-basis elections under s 19, being invoice basis, payments basis, or hybrid basis, and the annual-filing election where turnover is NZ$500,000 or less; (iii) the Look-Through Company election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 for closely held NZ-resident companies with five or fewer individual shareholders, which is generally unavailable to a non-resident International Company; (iv) legacy Qualifying Company elections for grandfathered companies; (v) statutory rules under the BEPS hybrid mismatch framework in subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007, which disallow deductions on hybrid instruments and structured arrangements without elective override; and (vi) mutual agreement procedures under New Zealand double tax treaties to resolve dual-residency tie-breakers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

New Zealand dollars (NZD/NZ$) 60,000. A person carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand must register for GST when the total value of taxable supplies made or intended to be made in any 12-month period exceeds, or is expected to exceed, NZ$60,000 excluding GST. Registration is also mandatory when the person has commenced a taxable activity and supplies have exceeded the threshold in the past 12 months. A non-resident company may voluntarily register even below the threshold, but once voluntarily registered it is generally required to remain registered for at least two years.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST applies to the following, subject to strict contemporaneous documentary evidence: (1) exported goods under s 11(1) of the GST Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within the prescribed statutory timeframe, generally within 28 days of the time of supply, with extended evidentiary arrangements where goods are exported by a non-resident recipient; (2) goods supplied to a non-resident person who is not in New Zealand at the time of supply and who subsequently exports them, where the supplier holds evidence satisfying the customs and export proof requirements; and (3) certain offshore services supplied to non-residents under s 11A, provided the services do not fall within the statutory exceptions, notably services performed on goods situated in New Zealand at the time of supply, warehousing, kitting, assembly, quality inspection, and services connected with New Zealand land. The International Company's kitting and fulfillment activities in New Zealand are therefore standard-rated at 15% as services connected with NZ-situated goods; the supply of the finished kitted goods can be zero-rated only when the goods physically leave New Zealand and the exporter can produce the customs and transport evidence required by the GST Act. Input GST incurred in New Zealand may be claimed in the same return period; net refunds are paid by Inland Revenue, administratively targeted within 30 days, but are subject to verification of export evidence and may be withheld pending audit.

  • Official New Zealand Customs export declaration, being the electronic export entry accepted through the Trade Single Window (TSW) / Customs Export System
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) naming the New Zealand supplier as shipper or consignor and the non-resident customer as consignee, evidencing physical departure from New Zealand
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank-issued inward remittance advice, including SWIFT MT103 confirmation, evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident customer
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list describing the kitted or assembled goods, quantities, HS codes, and export value
  • Written purchase order or supply contract confirming the non-resident recipient and the contractual terms, including applicable Incoterms 2020 rules
  • Certificate of origin where required by the destination customs regime, and transport insurance certificate evidencing international carriage

New Zealand has no statutory real-time fiscal clearance, no government-run tax invoice clearance gateway, and no digital stamping requirement. E-invoicing is voluntary, implemented via the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 network; since 1 April 2022, New Zealand government agencies are able to receive PEPPOL e-invoices. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR or certified software and must be submitted by the statutory due dates. Tax invoices issued by a registered person must contain the section 24 content prescribed by the GST Act 1985, being supplier identity and GST number, date of issue, description of goods or services, GST-exclusive amount, GST amount, and total; invoices may be electronic or paper and must be retained for seven years. Export documentation must be lodged electronically with the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window; there is no stamping or approved-exporter seal requirement.

Export zero-rating is a priority audit domain for Inland Revenue. Where a taxpayer cannot substantiate physical export or fails to satisfy the section 11 evidentiary rules, zero-rating is reversed and output GST of 15% is assessed, with use-of-money interest applying from the original statutory due date. Shortfall penalties under Part 9 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 are layered: 20% for lack of reasonable care; an additional 20%, or 10% for tenable positions, for an unacceptable tax position; 40% for gross carelessness; 100% for abusive tax avoidance; and 150% for evasion. Where fraudulent export documentation is used, including fabricated customs entries, forged bills of lading, circular foreign-exchange flows, or phantom kitting contracts, criminal prosecution may be brought with fines up to NZ$50,000 for an individual and NZ$100,000 for a company, and/or imprisonment for up to 3 years under the Tax Administration Act 1994; dishonesty offences may also be prosecuted under the Crimes Act 1961. The standard four-year reassessment period is extended in cases of fraud or evasion, and clawback may be assessed against the exporter or, in a GST group context, against an associated person. The New Zealand Customs Service may also seize goods or impose penalties under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 where export declarations are false or misleading.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full convertibility of the New Zealand Dollar (NZD) for both current account and capital account transactions. There are no general exchange control restrictions or approval requirements for foreign exchange operations. Profit remittances and dividends on NZD earnings are freely transferable subject only to standard Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Counter Financing of Terrorism (CFT) checks and documentary verification by the remitting bank. Certain inbound foreign investments may require screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 if they involve significant business assets or sensitive land, but this does not restrict day-to-day currency convertibility. The Reserve Bank does not set conversion ratios or require surrender of export proceeds. Capital outflows are unrestricted, except for addressees on the New Zealand sanctions list and goods falling under the Customs Export Prohibition Orders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender requirement exists. Exporters are permitted to retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency or convert freely into NZD at any domestic commercial bank. Statutory timeline: not applicable (N/A) because the Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not prescribe a remittance window for export receipts. The only compliance obligation is the filing of an Export / Import Transaction Report (EITR) or equivalent documentation with the New Zealand Customs Service where the shipment value exceeds NZD 1,000 or where the bank requires supporting documentation for proceeds below that threshold. The entire export value may remain offshore or be converted, with no enforced conversion or liquidation period.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand – Te Pūtea Matua

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility for cross-border multi-currency settlement, but limited to non-lending payment-wallet services. Airwallex and Wise hold New Zealand Financial Service Provider (FSP) registrations and are AML/CFT reporting entities. They are legally permitted to provide foreign exchange, virtual multi-currency accounts, and global payment routing to international companies. They cannot accept NZD deposits as a licensed bank (unless they hold a registered bank licence under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, which neither does), nor can they issue trade finance instruments such as letters of credit or banker's acceptances. For low-risk standard kitting/fulfilment entities (ISIC 5210/NAICS 493110), Airwallex is a reliable secondary clearing rail, while a Tier 1 commercial bank remains necessary for escrow, overdraft, and settlement balance guarantees.

Compliant inbound capital injection (equity subscription or shareholder loan) is executed over the SWIFT network using MT103 or MT202, with the standard New Zealand balance-of-payments (BOP) purpose code “Direct investment – capital contribution – equity capital” or “Direct investment – capital contribution – loans” respectively. The NZ receiving bank will require a completed New Zealand Inward Foreign Investment form only when the investor's ownership triggers Overseas Investment Act screening (e.g., if the company owns sensitive NZ land or significant business assets). Payment verification: the bank will request a copy of the signed shareholders’ resolution, the constitutional documents, the entry in the New Zealand Companies Office register, and a signed statutory declaration confirming the funds originate from a lawful source. Physical remittance shall be sent from the funder’s bank account (in the same legal name as the beneficial owner of CK_IC) to the designated NZD or USD account at the Tier 1 NZ bank, with the unique beneficiary identity code (e.g., client number of NZ International Company) stated in the message. If using Airwallex as an intermediate rail, the intermediate EMI is restricted to pass-through; funds must ultimately settle at the NZ bank account.Primary clearing rails for trade settlement are SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border wire payments to component suppliers and freight forwarders, and commercial documentary letters of credit (LC) issued by the NZ bank (transferable and confirmed) for high-value purchases. Domestic NZD payments are cleared through the NZ Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) via Direct Debit or ACH, and airwallex/global payment gateways are acceptable for lower-value (<NZD 10,000) disbursements to fulfilment sub-contractors. All outbound remittances above NZD 10,000 require an NZ bank to file a Financial Transaction Report; remittances to restricted jurisdictions require licence screening. Instead, deliverable forwards are the standard hedging tool.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility for cross-border multi-currency settlement, but limited to non-lending payment-wallet services. Airwallex and Wise hold New Zealand Financial Service Provider (FSP) registrations and are AML/CFT reporting entities. They are legally permitted to provide foreign exchange, virtual multi-currency accounts, and global payment routing to international companies. They cannot accept NZD deposits as a licensed bank (unless they hold a registered bank licence under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, which neither does), nor can they issue trade finance instruments such as letters of credit or banker's acceptances. For low-risk standard kitting/fulfilment entities (ISIC 5210/NAICS 493110), Airwallex is a reliable secondary clearing rail, while a Tier 1 commercial bank remains necessary for escrow, overdraft, and settlement balance guarantees.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand transfer pricing documentation thresholds: A Local File is required for taxpayers with related-party transactions exceeding NZD 1 million in aggregate annually (for cross-border related-party dealings); a Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated group turnover of NZD 1 billion or more, and the New Zealand entity must be in a group with a statutory group-wide filing obligation. Transfer pricing documentation must be prepared contemporaneously by the filing date of the income tax return. The minimum related-party transaction threshold for documentation is NZD 1 million for goods and services (aggregate) and NZD 1 million for interest-bearing related-party debt in excess of NZD 1 million. Where no formal documentation is required, the taxpayer must still be able to demonstrate arm's length pricing and apply the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines as incorporated by section GC 13 of the Income Tax Act 2007; transactions below NZD 1 million remain subject to review.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime akin to the UAE; however, the statutory anti-avoidance general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 applies to arrangements whose purpose or effect is tax avoidance, and Inland Revenue may disregard the legal form of a transaction where economic substance differs. A fulfillment/kitting company operating in New Zealand must demonstrate substantive business activity: physical premises, actual employees, management functions, and order-taking/assembly decisions in New Zealand or in the jurisdiction where income is booked. The International Company (CK_IC) catalogued as a 'Tax_Exempt_Zone' entity must nonetheless ensure that any New Zealand permanent establishment does not become a shell/paper operation; the 'economic substance doctrine' is invoked through attribution of profits under Article 7 of the applicable DTA (profits attributable to a permanent establishment) and the transfer pricing rules that require a 'real business purpose' for the booking of offshore profits. New Zealand Inland Revenue applies the 'substance over form' principle in determining whether an offshore International Company has a taxable permanent establishment in New Zealand under section 91 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 and ADDA 1994.
