Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, Registrar of Companies, an agency of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE); online filing exclusively via the Companies Office Connect portal (connect.companiesoffice.govt.nz).

Governing statute

New Zealand Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No 105), sections 10–14 (formation and incorporation), 16–19 (legal capacity and name restrictions), 122–123 (registered office), 129–161 (board governance and officer appointment), 185–201 (share capital and capital maintenance), 215–224 (financial reporting), 378–380 (annual return requirements); supplemented by the Income Tax Act 2007 for non-resident tax classification, the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013, and the Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2006. The 'International Company' (IC) designation is recognized by the Registrar of Companies under the Companies Office international company practice, permitting non-resident incorporation with a New Zealand agent.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NZCN1 — 'Application for Registration as a Company', accompanied by Form NZCN2 (Consent to Act as Shareholder), Form NZCN3 (Consent to Act as Director), Form NZCN4 (Consent to Act as Registered Office Agent), and the optional Company Constitution; all submitted electronically through the Companies Office Connect portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 90 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies under the Companies Act 1993; the International Company may be incorporated with a nominal issued capital of one (1) share of no par value at NZD 1.00 per share subscribed by the non-resident beneficial owner. However, for the Electronic Money Institution (EMI) operating license, the Reserve Bank of New Zealand and the Financial Markets Authority conditionally require a minimum regulatory capital of NZD 500,000 (approximately USD 300,000), which must be fully paid into a segregated escrow account maintained with a New Zealand registered bank under a tripartite escrow agreement, releaseable only upon the satisfaction of all statutory formation and licensing obligations.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ): Fire Safety Certificate and evacuation scheme approval under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017, together with annual fire drill records. Building Act 2004: Building Warrant of Fitness (Form 12A) and annual structural load inspections for commercial premises, including server rack floor-load certification. WorkSafe New Zealand: Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 compliance registration, workplace risk assessment, occupational first aid certificate, electrical safety certification under the Electrical Workers Act 2010, and ergonomic work-station clearance. Civil Defence: registration of the business continuity and emergency response plan with the local Civil Defence and Emergency Management (CDEM) Group, including an earthquake-prone building assessment where applicable.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies under the Companies Act 1993; the International Company may be incorporated with a nominal issued capital of one (1) share of no par value at NZD 1.00 per share subscribed by the non-resident beneficial owner. However, for the Electronic Money Institution (EMI) operating license, the Reserve Bank of New Zealand and the Financial Markets Authority conditionally require a minimum regulatory capital of NZD 500,000 (approximately USD 300,000), which must be fully paid into a segregated escrow account maintained with a New Zealand registered bank under a tripartite escrow agreement, releaseable only upon the satisfaction of all statutory formation and licensing obligations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Tāke), Wellington, New Zealand; self-service portal: myIR (myIR.ird.govt.nz); business registration gateway: Business.govt.nz / NZBN

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a New Zealand IRD number (8- or 9-digit tax file number) before commencing taxable activity. For income tax, non-resident companies apply using form IR596 (Non-resident company registration) or through the myIR portal under the 'Register for an IRD number' workflow; a New Zealand tax agent or registered address for service is required. For Goods and Services Tax (GST), the same foreign entity registers online via myIR or the offshore supplier registration module; the applicant must provide certified company incorporation documents, proof of foreign tax residence (e.g., certificate of residence), and the physical address of the NZ branch or agent. Inland Revenue assigns a single IRD number for all taxes; there is no separate VAT/GST identification number. Processing takes 5-10 business days for paper applications and is generally instantaneous via myIR when identity verification is completed electronically.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box election system. Available statutory elections relevant to a non-resident International Company include: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election for NZ-resident closely held companies, not available to non-resident international companies; (2) Qualifying Company (QC) election, grandfathered for entities that elected before 2011, no longer open; (3) GST grouping election under s55 Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for related entities to file consolidated GST returns; (4) voluntary GST registration election before exceeding the NZD 60,000 threshold; (5) GST annual balance-date adjustment election; (6) zero-rating election for land transactions; (7) hybrid mismatch limitation rules (Income Tax Act 2007 ss FH 1-FH 15) apply automatically to cross-border arrangements, with no elective opt-out; any mismatch-based financing structure executed by the International Company is subject to mandatory adjustment rather than elective treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (taxable turnover in any rolling 12-month period). For non-resident offshore suppliers of remote services (including digital wallet and electronic money services) supplied to NZ consumers, mandatory registration arises when supplies to NZ exceed NZD 60,000 per annum; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold. A related-person aggregation rule applies to determine the NZD 60,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exports is governed by section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Eligibility to apply 0% GST requires: (a) for goods, the goods are exported from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply and the supplier retains evidence of physical export; (b) for services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, the services must be performed outside New Zealand or, for remote/digital services, supplied for consumption outside New Zealand (B2B supplies to non-resident recipients outside NZ qualify for zero-rating even if the service is received remotely); (c) the supply is directly in connection with goods exported from New Zealand; or (d) the supply is a taxable supply to a non-resident that would otherwise be exempt financial services, in which case the 'offshore services' rule zero-rates the fee if the non-resident recipient is outside New Zealand at the time of supply. Electronic money issuance, Virtual IBAN setup fees, and wallet reserve management fees supplied to non-resident corporate clients are zero-rated under the offshore services provisions, provided the non-resident recipient is outside New Zealand and the services are not supplied to an NZ consumer. Standard 15% GST applies to supplies of remote services to New Zealand resident consumers, including platform users in NZ.

  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Airway Bill (air) evidencing physical departure of goods from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-exchange receipt evidencing payment received from the overseas buyer

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping regime exists in New Zealand. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and operates on the Peppol BIS 3.0 standard via accredited Access Points; Inland Revenue does not operate an API-based tax clearance gateway for invoice issuance. Tax invoices may be issued on paper, by PDF, or through Peppol. E-invoicing adoption is promoted by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment, but there is no statutory obligation to obtain Inland Revenue pre-approval, fiscal stamp, or sequential pre-registered invoice numbering before issuing a tax invoice.

Inland Revenue actively audits zero-rated export supplies, remote services, and GST refund claims. Where zero-rating is claimed without substantiating documentation or where an exporter fails to export goods within 28 days, the supply is subject to 15% GST retrospectively, and any input credits previously claimed are clawed back with use-of-money interest under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Civil shortfall penalties apply: 20% for lack of reasonable care; 40% for gross carelessness; 100% for knowingly taking an unacceptable tax position; 150% for evasion. Criminal prosecution for GST evasion under s143A TAA can result in imprisonment and fines up to NZD 50,000. Under s36A of the Tax Administration Act 1994, directors and senior officers may be personally liable for GST debts incurred during their tenure if the company fails to pay. For an International Company, mischaracterisation of financial services as zero-rated offshore services triggers full recovery of GST output tax on all historical supplies plus penalties, and may invalidate the entity's tax-exempt classification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has full current and capital account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no exchange control laws, no prior approval requirements for cross-border capital movements, and no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or liquidation proceeds. Foreign investors may repatriate capital and earnings freely through licensed banks. The only practical remittance hurdles are of a tax nature: non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on interest, dividends, and royalties; approved issuer levy (AIL) options; and thin capitalization rules for foreign-owned entities. No central bank consent or foreign exchange allocation permit is required to convert NZD into any foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand has no statutory export proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no compulsory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters freely retain proceeds in offshore accounts or in New Zealand bank accounts in supported foreign currencies. The only ongoing obligations relate to AML/CFT record-keeping on the origin and destination of funds and Inland Revenue attribution rules for controlled foreign company income where relevant. No percentage of export proceeds must be converted to NZD.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the apex central bank supervising the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS), the high-value clearing system, and the regulatory framework for banks and non-bank deposit takers. Foreign exchange policy is administered through the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, while AML/CFT supervision is shared with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium — restricted but achievable through a licensed bank partnership model. No direct pass-porting regime exists for EU/UK/offshore digital EMI licences into New Zealand; an foreign EMI cannot become a direct participant in the New Zealand retail payment system (Automated Payment Service) or the high-value ESAS clearing system. The commercially viable route is to contract with one or more Tier 1 New Zealand registered banks to act as settlement sponsor and underlying account provider, while the EMI issues virtual IBANs and digital wallets as a registered financial service provider under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008. The entity must also comply with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and register with the FMA and DIA as a reporting entity. The issuance of stored-value facilities and e-money itself is not a prohibited activity in New Zealand but requires full AML/CFT compliance and transparency on reserve safeguarding.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be routed as SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.008 credits to the entity's NZD operational or escrow account at a Tier 1 New Zealand bank, citing an appropriate purpose code such as 'EQUI' for equity capital formation or 'INTC' for intercompany loan funding. The remitter should include the entity's NZ Business Number (NZBN), IRD number, the share subscription agreement, board resolution, and a certificate of inward payment issued by the receiving bank where the commercial bank requires it. All funds must emanate from a banking account held in the name of the beneficial investor or declared financing party; the purpose of payment field must state 'Capital Contribution under Share Subscription' or 'Shareholder Loan under International Company Constitution', never generic terms. There are no exchange surrender obligations and no New Zealand Reserve Bank approvals; verification of source of funds is the bank's principal gatekeeper.Outbound trade disbursements from New Zealand are executed via SWIFT MT103 for open account trade settlements, ISO 20022 pacs.008 for low-value GBP and EUR corridors, SWIFT MT700/MT707 for documentary letters of credit issuance and amendments, and SWIFT MT760 for standby letters of credit guarantees. NZD-denominated interbank payments clear through the Reserve Bank's ESAS high-value settlement system; USD clearings route over CHIPS or Fedwire; EUR clearings settle via TARGET2/SEPA Credit Transfer; GBP clearings settle via CHAPS. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium — restricted but achievable through a licensed bank partnership model. No direct pass-porting regime exists for EU/UK/offshore digital EMI licences into New Zealand; an foreign EMI cannot become a direct participant in the New Zealand retail payment system (Automated Payment Service) or the high-value ESAS clearing system. The commercially viable route is to contract with one or more Tier 1 New Zealand registered banks to act as settlement sponsor and underlying account provider, while the EMI issues virtual IBANs and digital wallets as a registered financial service provider under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008. The entity must also comply with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and register with the FMA and DIA as a reporting entity. The issuance of stored-value facilities and e-money itself is not a prohibited activity in New Zealand but requires full AML/CFT compliance and transparency on reserve safeguarding.
