Competent corporate registry

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand Government; online portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities deriving New Zealand-source income or carrying on taxable activity in New Zealand must register for New Zealand tax purposes. Obtain an IRD number for companies via the IR596 form (Non-Resident Company Registration) or the appropriate entity registration form processed through myIR. For GST, registration is mandatory where the entity carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand and either (a) makes supplies in New Zealand, (b) exceeds NZD 60,000 taxable turnover in a 12-month period, or (c) is a non-resident supplier of remote services to New Zealand resident consumers under the offshore GST rules (registration required irrespective of turnover, with a simplified pay-only registration option). A foreign tax residence certificate, constitutional documents, and director details must be provided. The IRD issues an IRD number (8- or 9-digit) and, if registered, a GST number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: Non-resident foreign company (International Company structure) treated as a non-resident for New Zealand tax purposes; New Zealand source income is subject to territorial taxation, while foreign-sourced income generally remains exempt. Active business income of a CFC may be attributed under controlled foreign company rules; however, an exempt foreign company with no New Zealand permanent establishment and not carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand is generally outside New Zealand income tax except for withholding taxes on New Zealand-source dividends, interest, or royalties. GST registration is required for supplies of remote services to New Zealand consumers, but supplies to non-residents may be zero-rated.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not provide US-style 'check-the-box' classifications. Relevant statutory elections under the Income Tax Act 2007 and Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 include: (a) CFC attribution election (sections EX 20-EX 21), (b) foreign dividend exemption election for non-portfolio foreign investment fund (FIF) interests (section EX 29), (c) GST grouping election (section 55 GST Act), (d) GST registration election for non-resident suppliers of remote services (optional 'pay-only' registration under section 60D), and (e) thin capitalisation ratio elections for non-resident-owned entities (section FE 16). Hybrid mismatch rules under Part FM operate automatically with no elective opt-out except for grandfathered arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 for domestic taxable activity (rolling 12-month period); non-resident suppliers of remote services (including voice streaming and digital services) are required to register and charge GST on supplies to New Zealand resident consumers from 1 December 2019, with no turnover threshold. A simplified 'pay-only' GST registration regime applies to non-resident suppliers of remote services. Marketplace operators (e.g., Apple App Store, Google Play) are deemed suppliers under the GST platform rules, and the underlying vendor may not be separately required to register for GST on sales facilitated through the marketplace.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (a) goods physically exported from New Zealand, (b) services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the services are not supplied directly in connection with land or goods in New Zealand, and (c) remote services (including voice streaming, audio chatroom, and digital platform services) supplied to non-resident consumers or businesses. To zero-rate services, the supplier must hold reasonable evidence of the recipient's non-resident status (e.g., foreign tax registration number, foreign residency certificate, or a declaration). For goods, the supplier must obtain and retain export documentation within 28 days of supply. If the entity is a non-resident supplier of remote services, GST registration is required only for supplies to New Zealand consumers; supplies to non-residents may be zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Trade Single Window or approved export entry)
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill evidencing physical departure of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/FX record evidencing foreign currency receipt
  • Evidence of non-residence for service recipients (GST number, foreign tax residency certificate, or supplier-issued declaration)
  • Exporter Record / Approved Exporter status documentation where applicable
  • Contract or purchase order for offshore services demonstrating supply to non-resident

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API pre-clearance for e-invoicing. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Peppol BIS 3.0 standard. GST-registered businesses with annual turnover of NZD 5 million or more must accept e-invoices via Peppol-capable software when requested. All GST-registered entities must issue a tax invoice within 28 days of supply, retain records for 7 years under the Tax Administration Act 1994, and maintain electronic records that accurately substantiate the nature, value, and zero-rating of exports.

IRD audits zero-rated supplies rigorously. If zero-rating is claimed without adequate substantiation, output tax is reassessed at 15%, plus use-of-money interest from the date GST was incorrectly treated as zero-rated. Shortfall penalties under sections 141-141D TAA apply: not taking reasonable care (20% of tax shortfall), unacceptable tax position (20%), gross carelessness (40%), and fraudulent intent (150%). Prosecution for evasion can result in imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines up to NZD 50,000 for individuals and NZD 200,000 for companies. GST refunds are subject to clawback for up to 7 years, with no time limit in cases of fraud or evasion. Non-resident suppliers failing to register for offshore GST are liable for uncollected GST, penalties, and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalized exchange control regime with complete current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange control laws, licensing requirements, or approval procedures restricting the purchase, sale, holding, lending, or repatriation of any currency by a non-resident company. The New Zealand dollar is a free-floating and fully deliverable currency, and the Cook Islands uses the New Zealand dollar as its official legal tender, eliminating any bilateral currency convertibility friction for the International Company. Dividends, interest, royalties, service fees, loan repayments, capital subscriptions, and app-store settlement proceeds may all be remitted freely without Reserve Bank of New Zealand consent. The only material hurdles are tax and statistical: (i) Non-Resident Withholding Tax of 30% on dividends and royalties paid to non-treaty residents (reduced to 15% under most double tax agreements); no New Zealand-Cook Islands income tax treaty exists, so a dividend routed through an NZ-resident payer to the Cook Islands parent would suffer 30% NRWT unless treaty chain relief is available; (ii) NRWT of 15% on interest (reducible to 2% via the Approved Issuer Levy where the borrowing company is an approved issuer); (iii) New Zealand Goods and Services Tax at 15% on remote services supplied to NZ-resident consumers, while exported digital services are zero-rated where the supplier has no NZ GST registration obligation; and (iv) balance of payments statistical reporting under the RBNZ BOPMON framework, which the clearing bank completes for cross-border transactions aggregating NZD 100,000 or more per reporting month. Given central management and control are maintained outside New Zealand, no permanent establishment exists in New Zealand, and the app revenue is foreign-sourced, the tax-exempt International Company is not subject to New Zealand income tax on its global streaming revenue; the bank will nevertheless require an Inland Revenue non-resident company registration and a tax residence declaration at onboarding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. New Zealand imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no overnight custody requirement for export receipts. The Cook Islands International Company may retain offshore 100% of its Apple IAP and Google Play Billing proceeds in USD or any other freely convertible currency, may hold them in NZD domestic settlement accounts, or may remit them to group treasury centers without any requirement to convert to NZD. The only administrative diligence is the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Balance of Payments monitoring framework: NZ clearing banks are required to capture and report monthly cross-border service and financial transactions of NZD 100,000 and above in the External Assets and Liabilities return (BOPMON EAL-1/EAL-2). This reporting is statistical and not an authorization or exchange-control approval.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), the central bank and prudential regulator for all New Zealand registered banks and non-bank deposit takers. Banking supervision is exercised under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, while AML/CFT oversight of banks and licensed financial service providers is administered by the Financial Markets Authority under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. For the Cook Islands International Company, the home-registry authority is the Financial Supervisory Commission of the Cook Islands, which regulates the registered agent and issues Certificates of Good Standing; however, every FX transaction, treasury operation, and bank account contemplated in this blueprint is executed through New Zealand clearing banks and is therefore subject to RBNZ prudential standards and New Zealand criminal, sanctions, and AML/CFT law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High with documented legal constraints. New Zealand-registered fintech EMIs, most prominently Airwallex and Wise Business, may lawfully onboard a Cook Islands International Company as a multi-currency settlement client, but the service model is materially different from a bank: (i) an EMI is not an RBNZ-registered bank or non-bank deposit taker and does not take deposits; client funds are safeguarded in segregated trust or client-money accounts at a partner tier-1 bank and are not protected under the statutory Deposit Compensation Scheme introduced by the Deposit Takers Act 2022; (ii) the EMI is subject to the AML/CFT Act 2009 and will treat the tax-exempt Cook Islands IC as Medium-Risk Enhanced, requiring the complete beneficial ownership chain, confirmation that there are no nominee shareholders or bearer instruments, and a current Certificate of Good Standing from the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission; (iii) the EMI's own downstream banking partner will independently review the source and purpose of USD settlement flows coming from Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, and may require the IC to demonstrate contractual merchant-of-record status with Apple and Google; (iv) the EMI cannot issue commercial letters of credit, cannot provide deposit products or committed credit lines, and can provide FX only on a matched-principal or agency execution basis. Legal constraint: if the EMI holds NZD balances for more than 90 days or offers account-like features, it must comply with the client money rules under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and, if applicable, the obligations of an authorised financial adviser regime. Recommended structure: maintain the primary capital and settlement account at a tier-1 NZ bank and use the EMI exclusively as an operational corridor for creator payouts, multi-currency float, and low-value mass disbursements.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% statutory headline effective rate for a qualifying Cook Islands International Company on income derived outside the Cook Islands (International Companies Act 2012, s.135); 28% New Zealand corporate income tax rate applies only if the entity inadvertently becomes tax resident in New Zealand through central management and control.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutory rate for dividends paid by a Cook Islands International Company because s.135 of the International Companies Act 2012 exempts the IC and its distributions from all Cook Islands withholding taxes; no dividend WHT applies on repatriations from the CK_IC to its non-resident trust/beneficiaries. New Zealand domestic law nonetheless imposes NRWT at 30% on dividends sourced in NZ, but the exempt IC has no NZ-source dividend base absent an NZ PE or NZ tax residence.
