Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake), New Zealand; official online portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz). Export and customs matters are administered by the New Zealand Customs Service (Te Mana Ārai o Aotearoa) under the Customs and Excise Act 2018.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity with a New Zealand tax nexus (carrying on a taxable activity, deriving New Zealand-source income, or required to register for GST) must apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number by completing form IR742 (Non-Resident Tax Registration) or by registering through myIR where an NZ bank account and registered address are held; the entity must first be validly incorporated or registered as an overseas company on the New Zealand Companies Register and provide certified foreign registration documents, director and resident agent details, and the principal business address; GST registration is then filed separately in myIR using the GST registration application (GSTREG), which issues a GST number linked to the same IRD number; a non-resident may register for GST voluntarily when making taxable supplies, and registration takes effect from the date the taxable activity commences or the date approved by the Commissioner.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - Non-resident International Company (IC) incorporated as a tax-exempt offshore vehicle under Cook Islands international company legislation, as recognised under New Zealand's territorial scope; the entity is treated as having no New Zealand income tax nexus unless it establishes a fixed place of business or permanent establishment in New Zealand; global TikTok creator agency income (creator incubation, multi-channel talent representation, and brand-facilitation commissions) sourced and consummated outside New Zealand is exempt from New Zealand income tax, and New Zealand GST applies only to supplies connected with New Zealand consumption.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; the principal statutory elections are: ordinary company vs Look-Through Company (LTC) status under subpart HA of the Income Tax Act 2007 (with the five-owner and eligibility tests), the CFC attributable CFC income election and active income de minimis exemption under subparts CQ and EX of the Income Tax Act 2007, the GST registration threshold election, the foreign currency accounting elections, and the hybrid mismatch rules in Part FA of the Income Tax Act 2007 which operate to disallow deduction/non-inclusion mismatches and imported mismatches for cross-border structured arrangements involving offshore entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies (excluding GST) in any 12-month period in New Zealand; a non-resident carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand must register once this economic turnover threshold is exceeded and may voluntarily register under section 51 of the GST Act 1985 before reaching the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under sections 11 and 11A of the GST Act 1985: (1) goods physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier or an agent within 28 days of the time of supply (or such extended period as the Commissioner approves); and (2) services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, where the supply is not made to a New Zealand GST-registered recipient for use in a New Zealand taxable activity and is not connected with land or movable property in New Zealand. For a global TikTok MCN agency, creator incubation, talent management, performance tracking, and brand-negotiation services rendered for the benefit of non-resident creators and offshore advertisers qualify as zero-rated offshore services when the contract, service delivery, and consumption all occur outside New Zealand; the supplier must retain adequate documentary evidence proving the offshore character of each supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Combined Export Declaration / Export Entry lodged electronically with the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window prior to departure)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (transport document evidencing physical export of goods from New Zealand and overseas destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange settlement statement (bank statement or Wise B2B payout statement evidencing receipt of cross-border settlement in a New Zealand or foreign bank account and the corresponding foreign exchange conversion)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API transmission, or digital stamping regime exists in New Zealand; B2B e-invoicing is voluntary and interoperable via the Peppol network; GST invoices may be issued electronically provided they satisfy the content requirements in section 24 of the GST Act 1985; export declarations and supporting export data must instead be lodged electronically with the New Zealand Customs Service via the Trade Single Window (TSW), using an approved software system, before goods depart New Zealand.

The Commissioner of Inland Revenue may audit export zero-rating claims retrospectively and, where a zero-rating condition is not substantiated, re-characterise the supply as standard-rated GST at 15%, with use-of-money interest running from the original return date; shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 escalate by culpability: 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness or a hidden arrangement, and 100% to 150% for evasion or an abusive tax position; knowingly lodging false export declarations or fabricating inward remittance evidence may result in criminal prosecution, imprisonment, and full GST clawback extending the assessment period to 7 years (no time limit in cases of fraud or evasion).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand holds IMF Article VIII status; both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible with no residual exchange controls, no central-bank licensing of foreign-exchange payments, and no restrictions on dividend, interest, royalty, or capital repatriation. Practical remittance hurdles are limited to AML/CFT-driven source-of-funds verification, FATCA/CRS self-certification, and, for the CK_IC vehicle, production of a New Zealand-certified non-resident status opinion so that the bank does not erroneously apply NRWT or classify the entity as a New Zealand tax resident. The tax-exempt International Company may hold multi-currency accounts, convert NZD on demand, and repatriate profits without prior regulatory consent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender rules exist in New Zealand. The statutory framework (Exchange Control Act 1978 having been repealed, and no successor regime being enacted) imposes no mandatory conversion ratio, no prescribed settlement timeline, and no requirement to confide export proceeds to the Reserve Bank. A creator-incubation agency may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in offshore or onshore foreign-currency accounts, may invoice and settle in USD, EUR, CNH, or NZD, and may apply those receipts directly to overseas creator-payout obligations. The only obligation is correct balance-of-payments reporting by the receiving bank and ordinary tax documentation where the income is assessable in New Zealand.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), operating as the prudential regulator and apex clearing-system supervisor under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021; foreign-exchange control functions are no longer exercised through a separate exchange-control authority, and transaction-level FX governance is discharged by the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the New Zealand Police Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High for disbursement execution but only Medium as a primary settlement house. Airwallex and Payoneer offer similar B2B payout capabilities; nevertheless, the CK_IC treasury policy must be: (i) the Tier-1 New Zealand bank remains the sole concentration and escrow layer, (ii) Wise B2B serves strictly as creator-payout distribution rail, and (iii) no offshore EMI may open or maintain the capital escrow account or receive platform settlement without passing through the NZ-regulated clearing chain. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital should be injected as equity (preferred) via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's account to the CK_IC's NZD capital escrow account at ANZ, BNZ, or Westpac, referencing 'INVCAP / SHARE CAPITAL CK_IC' and the Companies Office shareholder resolution as remittance instruction; the New Zealand receiving bank will code the receipt as non-resident inward direct investment for balance-of-payments reporting and will release funds only after the corporate KYC pack, share register, and trust-deed disclosure have been verified. No Overseas Investment Act 2005 consent is triggered for a non-sensitive creator-agency business, and no Reserve Bank approval is required. If the injection is instead structured as a shareholder loan, a valid loan agreement, arm's-length interest rate, and thin-capitalization documentation must accompany the wire, otherwise the bank may re-book the credit as paid-in capital and the Inland Revenue Department may recharacterize deductibility. TikTok platform-generated revenue should not be routed as a capital injection; it should flow from TikTok (USD) to Wise B2B, then be converted through the Tier-1 bank's FX desk into NZD only when treasury requires liquidity.Creator settlements and agency disbursements should clear through SWIFT MT103 FFC transfers from the CK_IC's Tier-1 NZD operational account using payment purpose code 'Management Fees / Talent Management Commission', with the beneficiary bank's SWIFT BIC and the creator's tax identifier embedded in the reference field. For high-value regional co-commission arrangements, a Commercial Letter of Credit (usance or sight) or Bank Payment Obligation (BPO) may be issued where TikTok's regional procurement desk requires documentary settlement; each LC must be issued on the entity's board-approved facility with full compliance with UCP600. The TikTok Creator Payout rail aggregates micro-disbursements in USD and converts through Wise B2B, leveraging local-clearing rails (SEPA, Fedwire/ACH, Faster Payments) for last-mile distribution, thereby preserving the NZD operating account as a concentration point rather than a high-velocity transaction trap.
