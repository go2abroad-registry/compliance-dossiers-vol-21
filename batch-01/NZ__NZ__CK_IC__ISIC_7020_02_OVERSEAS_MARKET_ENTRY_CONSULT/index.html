Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office (Te Tari Kamupene o Aotearoa), a division of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), operating the Companies Register and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) register.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), ss 12, 15-16, 26-30, 35, 40, 60-63, 71-73, 74, 83, 87, 90, 92, 94, 97, 99, 101, 110, 114, 122, 123, 128, 129, 153, 154, 156, 158, 161, 162, 189, 190, 214(1)(a), 221, 231, 232 and Part 17 (Overseas Companies); Income Tax Act 2007, ss YA 2, YC 1, YD 2-4; Tax Administration Act 1994, ss 57B and 63E; Financial Reporting Act 2013, ss 25-29.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of a Company (Form 1, Companies Act 1993, First Schedule) filed electronically through the Companies Office online portal, together with the Company Constitution and the Consents to Appointment as Director and Shareholder (Forms 2 and 3 respectively), and the Residential Address Statements and Director Consent and Certification forms.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement under New Zealand law. Shares of a company incorporated under the Companies Act 1993 may be allotted without payment of a par value or premium because New Zealand operates a no-par-value share regime (s 42 and s 43). The company must, however, maintain a registered office in New Zealand at all times (s 186) and have at least one director who is resident in New Zealand or an external director resident in Australia under s 151(1). The company may be wholly owned and controlled by non-residents, and an International Company (IC) structure relying on tax-exempt offshore revenue streams must not derive New Zealand source income.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017, including a compliant fire evacuation scheme where the office occupies a high-occupancy building and annual fire safety inspection certification; structural load inspections and a current Building Warrant of Fitness under the Building Act 2004 issued by the territorial authority; workplace clearance under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 including the WorkSafe general duty of care, registration of the principal place of business with WorkSafe where applicable, completion of an asbestos or hazardous building material assessment for pre-1980 premises, and maintenance of an electrical safety certificate (Regulation 16 of the Electricity (Safety) Regulations 2010) for permanent electrical installations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Tāke); online portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity with no New Zealand tax nexus has no compulsory IRD number obligation. If the International Company carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand, exceeds the GST registration threshold, or voluntarily registers for GST, the non-resident company must first obtain an IRD number. Application is made via myIR or by filing form IR742 (IRD number application for a non-resident company), attaching the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director details, and certified proof of identity. Once the IRD number is issued, GST registration is completed through the myIR 'GST registration' service, selecting a monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly filing cycle. Non-resident suppliers of cross-border remote services may use the simplified offshore GST registration portal. The IRD number serves as the single statutory TIN and GST/VAT identification number; New Zealand does not issue a separate VAT ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand has no check-the-box entity classification election and no general elective pass-through regime. Available statutory elections relevant to this structure include GST grouping under sections 55 to 55C of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, GST branch registration under section 58A, provisional tax payment options, and certain withholding tax and portfolio investment allocation rules. Hybrid mismatch and branch mismatch rules under sections FH 1 to FH 14 of the Income Tax Act 2007 apply automatically and are not elective; taxpayers may however arrange inter-company payments and transfer pricing positions to ensure the rules do not neutralise deductible outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 gross turnover in a 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of cross-border remote services to New Zealand consumers are subject to the same NZD 60,000 threshold and may use simplified registration for offshore suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, zero-rating under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 applies where goods are physically exported by the supplier from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply and the supplier holds the prescribed export documentation. For services, zero-rating under section 11A applies where the services are supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, the services are not directly in connection with land or movable property located in New Zealand, and the supply is not a supply to a New Zealand consumer. For overseas market-entry consultancy supplied by the International Company to non-resident commercial clients, zero-rating is available when the recipient is contractually a non-resident company, the consultancy is performed and delivered offshore or via electronic communications to a recipient located outside New Zealand, the advice is consumed wholly outside New Zealand, and the supplier retains sufficient evidence of non-resident status and offshore usage. Services physically supplied to a person present in New Zealand, or digital services supplied to private consumers in New Zealand, remain subject to 15% GST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (for goods movements)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing foreign currency repatriation
  • Signed service contract identifying the non-resident recipient
  • Non-resident status evidence (overseas tax registration, incorporation certificate, or equivalent)
  • Deliverable and performance records demonstrating offshore consumption

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or mandatory digital stamping system. Electronic invoicing is legally valid and accepted by IRD on a records-retention basis. The government promotes Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 through a voluntary national e-invoicing framework, and central government agencies are required to receive e-invoices via Peppol; however, no general mandate applies to private-sector suppliers. There is no API-based pre-clearance requirement for ordinary GST invoices.

IRD may deny zero-rating and claw back output GST, input credits, and refunds if export documentation is incomplete, the non-resident status of the recipient is not substantiated, or the services are found to be consumed in New Zealand. Shortfall penalties apply at 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion, together with use-of-money interest running from the original GST due date. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims may also subject directors and officers to criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994, including significant fines and imprisonment, with retrospective recovery unlimited by the normal four-year adjustment period in cases of evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full Article VIII currency convertibility. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely floating and fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. There is no exchange-control regime restricting inbound equity, inbound loans, dividend repatriation, royalty remittance, or settlement of service fees. Practical hurdles are non-exchange based: (i) non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) of 15% on dividends and royalties, reduced by applicable double-tax treaties and subject to New Zealand imputation credit rules for dividends; (ii) OIO consent requirements for transactions that involve 'sensitive land' or 'significant business assets' (acquisition of 25% or more ownership / control of an entity with assets over NZD 100 million or turnover over NZD 200 million, or in specified sensitive sectors); and (iii) AML/CFT verification requirements under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. A low-risk consultancies business model involving overseas market-entry advisory fees is treated as ordinary current-account trade in services and faces no convertibility or remittance restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no statutory conversion timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts and foreign-currency earnings may be retained indefinitely in NZD or foreign-currency bank accounts without restriction. The only obligations are statistical and regulatory in nature: banks submit aggregated balance-of-payments reporting on flows exceeding NZD 50,000, and prescribed transaction reporting under the AML/CFT Act applies to wire transfers at or above NZD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) as the central monetary and prudential authority, with the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) within Land Information New Zealand administering capital-account foreign investment screening; there is no separate foreign-exchange control administration and the Reserve Bank does not require exchange-control permits for current or capital account transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High viability as a secondary settlement rail for low-risk consultancy disbursements, subject to licensing constraints. Wise (formerly TransferWise) is registered on New Zealand's Financial Service Providers Register (FSP) and operates through partner banks; Airwallex is likewise FSP-registered in New Zealand. These digital/EMI platforms are reliable for sub-NZD 250,000 cross-border B2B payments and provide competitive FX pricing. They are not licensed New Zealand banks and therefore cannot hold capital escrow balances, issue commercial letters of credit, or provide borrowings-based treasury facilities. Legal constraint: accepting funds or conducting money remittance in New Zealand requires compliance with the AML/CFT Act as a 'financial institution', and taking deposits other than through a licensed bank is prohibited under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. For capital escrow and major trade settlements, tier-1 licensed bank rails remain mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound equity capital is via SWIFT MT103 from the overseas parent or shareholder to the New Zealand entity's tier-1 bank account. The remittance must be classified for balance-of-payments purposes as 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' (BOP component 5200 series), not as a loan, when funding subscription of shares; a capital-escrow contribution should be referenced with the purpose code 'Corporate Treasury – Capital Contribution'. Supporting documents required by the receiving bank: board resolution approving the capital injection; shareholder subscription letter or share allotment certificate; a certificate of incumbency from the jurisdiction of origin; and beneficial-ownership declarations for any natural person holding 25% or more of the entity. Inbound amounts above NZD 50,000 trigger the receiving bank's prescribed transaction verification and potential enhanced due diligence, but require no exchange-control approval from the Reserve Bank of New Zealand unless an OIO consent threshold is met (not applicable to a consultancy with no sensitive land and asset values below OIO thresholds).The primary clearing rail for outbound trade disbursements is SWIFT MT103 for direct cash payments to overseas consultants, vendors, and licensing agents, using standard SWIFT/BIC and IBAN-equivalent beneficiary identifiers and an appropriate purpose code (e.g., 'Business services fees'). For higher-value trade-related settlements with documentary evidence, a commercial letter of credit issued under UCP600 via MT700 is permissible; given the consultancy's services-natured business, a standby letter of credit (MT760) or bank guarantee is more practical to secure performance obligations. Domestic NZD legs within New Zealand clear through the local high-value clearing system (ISO 20022-compliant payments infrastructure), while cross-border FX conversion is executed by the bank on its dealing desk at wholesale rates, with optional same-day or value-tomorrow settlement. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% (standard non-resident withholding tax on dividends paid by a New Zealand company to a non-resident shareholder). A reduced rate of 5% may apply to fully imputed dividends if the company has notified a 5% dividend withholding rate; a 0% DWR is possible for fully imputed dividends paid to a resident of a DTA partner where the shareholding threshold is met. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a standard 31 March balance date, the return must be filed by 7 July of the same calendar year. For non-standard balance dates, the statutory deadline is the 7th day of the second month after the balance date. A tax agent may obtain an extension to a later date under the IRD's extension regime if the taxpayer is not in arrears.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a 31 March balance date, the provisional tax instalments are due on 28 August, 15 January, and 7 May of the following income year. Non-standard balance dates use the 5th day of the month following the corresponding standard instalment date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File equivalent) is mandated by section GC 12 of the Income Tax Act 2007 when: (a) the taxpayer is a 'large taxpayer' (annual gross income of the taxpayer or its group exceeding NZ$30,000,000), and (b) the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZ$1,000,000 in the income year. A Master File may be requested for multinational groups with consolidated revenue over NZ$100,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) framework akin to the BVI or Cayman regimes. However, the Commissioner of Inland Revenue applies a substance-over-form doctrine through: (i) the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007; (ii) the extended definition of 'tax residence' in section YD 4, which looks to incorporation, head office, and centre of management; (iii) permanent establishment and branch profit attribution rules for non-residents; and (iv) the 'artificial arrangement' rule in section GB 1. A shell company lacking real presence and staff in New Zealand is exposed to full NZ residency, PE attribution, and GAAR recharacterisation. If the entity is an offshore International Company resident in the Cook Islands, the Cook Islands ESR 2019 requires economic substance for relevant activities, including consultancy and licensing operations, within that jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Non-Declaration of Income

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994: a fine of up to NZ$50,000 for an individual and NZ$100,000 for a body corporate, and imprisonment for a term not exceeding 5 years. In addition, a shortfall penalty of up to 100% of the resulting tax shortfall for evasion, plus use-of-money interest under section 139A.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, and shadow directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and shortfall penalties under section 149A TAA. Asset preservation orders and travel restrictions may be imposed against directors who attempt to leave New Zealand while tax is outstanding; custodial sentences apply for deliberate evasion.
Transfer Pricing Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: IRD may adjust the consideration to an arm's-length value under Subpart GC. Shortfall penalties under s 141A of the Tax Administration Act: 20% for failing to take reasonable care; 40% for an unacceptable tax position or abusive tax position; up to 100% for evasion. Use-of-money interest accrues on adjusted tax. The adjustment may be complemented by an equivalent compensating adjustment under the applicable DTA.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved a transfer pricing policy lacking commercial substance can be assessed personally for penalties if they were knowingly involved in the tax shortfall. In egregious cases, civil damages in a shareholder or tax authority winding-up action, director disqualification, and an interdiction order preventing removal of funds from New Zealand.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Deemed tax residency under s YD 4 bringing worldwide income into the NZ tax net; application of s BG 1 GAAR to recharacterise the entire arrangement; penalties for failure to file resident returns; and possible deregistration for non-trading under the Companies Act 1993. If the entity is an offshore exempt entity, the Cook Islands Economic Substance Regulations may impose a financial penalty of USD 10,000–100,000 and imprisonment for directors for false declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for company taxes where they knowingly cause the company to use shell status to defeat tax. Civil liability for unpaid taxes, travel bans, and disqualification as a director for up to 5 years under the Companies Act; in serious cases, imprisonment for obtaining tax advantages by fraud.
AML/CFT Violations

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 of up to NZ$2,000,000 for a serious breach by a reporting entity and NZ$500,000 for an individual; suspension or revocation of an MSB licence; criminal prosecution for money laundering under the Crimes Act 1961, punishable by imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who knowingly engage in money laundering or tolerate deferred-dawn reporting obligations face personal criminal liability and imprisonment. They may also be ordered to pay reparations to victims and be subject to asset freezes and international travel bans.
Withholding Tax Mischaracterisation / Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation under s GB 1/GA 1 with full NRWT imposed at 30%; shortfall penalty of 20% for not taking reasonable care, 40% for an unacceptable tax position, or up to 100% if evasion is established; use-of-money interest and late payment penalties apply from the original distribution date.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid NRWT under s 167 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 if the company fails to deduct or pay withholding tax, and they can be prosecuted for a knowing failure to pay tax deductions. Civil restitution to the Tax Commissioner and prohibition from leaving the jurisdiction are available remedies.
