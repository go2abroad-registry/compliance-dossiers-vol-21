Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum issued or paid-up capital applies to a New Zealand company. The subscribers may fix the consideration for shares by agreement and shares may be issued as fully paid without payment in cash. New Zealand law imposes no escrow account, capital deposit, or undertaking obligation for a standard private company.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate and Building Warrant of Fitness (BWOF) issued under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017 and the Building Act 2004, covering automatic smoke detection, refrigeration alarm, and emergency lighting as specified systems; structural load inspection certification by a chartered professional engineer (CPEng) for the racking system and cold room roof load; and a WorkSafe New Zealand workplace clearance certificate under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including validation of ammonia leak detection, enclosed space entry procedures, cold-storage PPE protocols, and an emergency evacuation plan.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake), New Zealand; official portal: www.ird.govt.nz; digital services portal: myIR.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (including the CK_IC International Company) obtains a New Zealand tax identification number (IRD number) through Inland Revenue. The standard procedure is: (1) determine whether the entity has a New Zealand tax nexus, i.e. whether it carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand, derives New Zealand-source income, or is required to register under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; (2) complete the applicable IRD number application form for a non-individual/non-resident entity (principally Form IR742, or the equivalent Inland Revenue registration form for companies); (3) submit certified documentation including the foreign certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents/memorandum and articles, director and ultimate beneficial owner details, foreign tax identification number, registered office address, New Zealand contact or agent details, and any New Zealand business or permanent establishment address; (4) file the application via myIR where an approved agent or tax representative is appointed, or by means of the Inland Revenue entity registration process if no online authentication exists; (5) upon approval, Inland Revenue issues a unique 8- to 9-digit IRD number; (6) if the entity is carrying on a New Zealand taxable activity and the annual turnover threshold is met, or where voluntary registration is beneficial, register separately for GST in myIR; and (7) appoint a New Zealand resident agent for tax purposges if the entity is a non-resident carrying on an assessable activity in New Zealand.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the CK_IC qualifies as a non-resident offshore/international company whose foreign-source income is outside the New Zealand income tax base. It is not automatically exempt from New Zealand income tax on New Zealand-source income. To the extent a permanent establishment, fixed establishment, New Zealand land, or other New Zealand-source income arises, the entity is taxed at entity level at the standard New Zealand corporate rate of 28% unless relieved by the Income Tax Act 2007 or an applicable double tax agreement.

Available election forms

New Zealand has no US-style check-the-box election. Entity classification follows the legal form and residence rules in the Income Tax Act 2007. Statutory elections that may be available are: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election for close-held NZ tax-resident eligible companies, permitting income and losses to flow through to shareholders; this is generally unavailable to a foreign-owned international company with non-resident shareholders; (2) consolidated group tax election for wholly-owned NZ-resident corporate groups; (3) optional GST registration for taxable activities below the registration threshold; and (4) no elective hybrid entity classification — hybrid mismatch outcomes are neutralized compulsorily under the hybrid mismatch rules (subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007) following OECD BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period for a person carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand; non-resident suppliers of remote services (including digital services) and operators of electronic marketplaces must also register when annual taxable supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed NZD 60,000; GST registration is voluntary below the threshold but compulsory once the threshold is met or expected to be met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 (section 11), zero-rating at 0% GST applies to supplies of goods that are genuinely exported from New Zealand by the supplier, or supplied in circumstances where the statutory export conditions are met. For services, zero-rating is available for services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, unless the services are directly connected with New Zealand land or with moveable personal property situated in New Zealand at the time of supply. In the context of a temp-controlled cold-storage business, storage and handling services performed while perishable pharmaceuticals remain physically in New Zealand are generally standard-rated at 15% GST because the services are connected with goods physically in New Zealand at the time of performance. Separate export supplies by the cold-storage operator, where the operator exports the goods and retains supporting export evidence, may qualify for zero-rating; mere foreign ownership of the goods and eventual export by the customer is not, by itself, sufficient to zero-rate the warehousing charge.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged with the New Zealand Customs Service (Trade Single Window/Electronic Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand and discharge at a destination outside New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/ACH payment record evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds from outside New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and signed export supply contract detailing goods, quantities, Incoterms, and overseas buyer
  • Cold-chain temperature monitoring logs and, where applicable, pharmaceutical/health export certificates required by New Zealand authorities

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate. E-invoicing is voluntary and interoperable through the PEPPOL network, with Inland Revenue participating in the e-invoicing framework. GST invoices must still satisfy the substantive content requirements of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, and Inland Revenue may obtain invoice data through myIR, e-invoicing providers, or audit requests. There is no pre-clearance API gate or government stamping requirement for commercial invoices.

Inland Revenue scrutinizes zero-rated export claims using customs records, shipping documents, FX remittance evidence, and supply-chain contracts. If zero-rating is disallowed because the goods were not exported, the supply was standard-rated, or the documentary conditions were not met, the GST clawback is assessed at 15% on the full supply value, plus use-of-money interest from the original due date. Penalties can include: shortfall penalty for not taking reasonable care (20% of the shortfall), gross carelessness (40%), unacceptable tax position (20%), abusive tax position (100%), and evasion (150%). Knowingly providing a false GST return is a criminal offence under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and can result in fines and imprisonment; directors and controlling persons of the International Company may also be held personally liable for tax debts arising from fraudulent or abusive export zero-rating arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. New Zealand does not maintain exchange controls on capital inflows, outflows, profit remittances, dividends, interest, royalties, or loan repayments. Remittance hurdles are limited to standard tax compliance procedures, including non-resident withholding tax on dividends and interest, transfer pricing documentation for intra-group transactions, and AML/CTF source-of-funds verification. The CK_IC International Company, being a non-resident tax-exempt structure, must avoid carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand through a fixed place of business to prevent treaty or domestic-source taxation and to avoid the risk of an unexpected permanent establishment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender, no statutory conversion timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio into NZD. Exporters and service providers may retain foreign currency proceeds in offshore or onshore foreign-currency accounts. For tax purposes, foreign-currency gains and losses on revenue accounts must be recognised and reported to Inland Revenue, and export services of refrigerated storage supplied to non-resident pharmaceutical principals may be zero-rated for GST where the service is supplied to a recipient outside New Zealand or is ancillary to goods located outside New Zealand.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua); there is no standalone foreign exchange control administration in New Zealand. The RBNZ supervises registered banks and payment and settlement systems, while the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) licenses non-bank financial service providers. The AML/CTF Act 2009 is co-supervised by the RBNZ, FMA, and Department of Internal Affairs depending on the reporting entity class.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary bank rail and medium as an ancillary payment channel. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Airwallex, or Payoneer are not registered New Zealand banks and therefore cannot provide local NZD account numbers, domestic clearing access, commercial letter-of-credit issuance, or capital escrow services required for a licensed refrigerated logistics platform. They can be used for small-value multi-currency disbursements after a registered bank account is established, but any EMI operating in New Zealand must hold an FSP licence and comply with the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2014 and AML/CTF Act 2009. The compliance position remains that all statutory capital flows and pharmaceutical trade settlements must be routed through an RBNZ-registered bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 to the CK_IC's New Zealand registered bank account using NZD or USD settlement. The receiving bank requires a fully identified beneficiary account number and BIC, and the payment narrative must clearly state the purpose as 'Equity Contribution – Share Subscription' or 'Direct Investment – Capital Account' together with a board resolution reference. Because New Zealand has no exchange-control approval requirement, the principal compliance gate is the bank's AML/CTF obligation to verify source of funds, source of wealth, and underlying transaction documentation. The bank will map the narrative to an inward direct-investment statistical category for Reserve Bank balance-of-payments reporting. For equity amounts exceeding the Overseas Investment Act 2005 screening threshold, a consent may be required before closing; otherwise the transaction should be structured as a plain equity transfer with no loan characterisation to avoid non-resident interest withholding tax and thin-capitalisation exposure.Cross-border disbursements should be made through SWIFT MT103 for open-account trade settlements, with separate MT202 cover transactions where interbank settlement is required. For documentary trade, the New Zealand bank will issue commercial letters of credit in accordance with UCP 600 via SWIFT MT700, with subsequent amendments by MT707; settlement against compliant documents will occur through MT103/MT202 upon negotiation. Domestic NZD supplier and operational payments should be executed through the Payments New Zealand Bulk Electronic Clearing System (BECS) and Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS), using the same high-integrity reconciliation reference fields.
