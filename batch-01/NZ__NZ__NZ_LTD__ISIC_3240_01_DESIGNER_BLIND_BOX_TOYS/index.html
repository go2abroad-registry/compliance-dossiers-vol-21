Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

New Zealand law imposes no minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or capital-lock-in account requirement. A company may be incorporated with zero issue value, and shares may be nil-paid, partly paid, or fully paid as resolved by the board. Share capital may be denominated in New Zealand dollars or a foreign currency at the shareholders' election.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: approval of an evacuation scheme and annual fire systems inspection under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand (Fire Safety, Evacuation Procedures, and Evacuation Schemes) Regulations 2018, including a building Warrant of Fitness for fire alarm, sprinkler, and emergency lighting systems. Structural safety: load certification for rotocasting ovens, injection machines, and finishing booths; electrical installation inspection certificate under the Electricity Act 1992 and AS/NZS 3000 wiring rules. Occupational clearance: Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 compliance, airbrush booth ventilation and capture velocity testing, respiratory protection to AS/NZS 1716:2012, hazardous substances worker training and certification, and an employee hazard register covering noise, chemical exposure, and manual handling risks.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake), New Zealand Government; online portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first obtain a New Zealand IRD number, which is the sole TIN and is also the GST registration number. Apply through myIR, or by paper application where online non-resident registration is unavailable, submitting certified certificate of incorporation, company extract, director/shareholder information, registered office details, and a local/foreign contact. For a non-resident company deriving New Zealand-sourced income or making taxable supplies in New Zealand, IRD number registration is mandatory; GST registration becomes compulsory once NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies is exceeded in a 12-month period, and is available voluntarily below this threshold. Non-resident offshore suppliers of remote services may register through the simplified offshore GST registration channel in myIR without establishing a local fixed place of business.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Look-through company (LTC) election for close-held companies, causing tax transparency; qualifying company (QC) election in restricted legacy cases; GST grouping election under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; and hybrid and branch mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act 2007 which operate as mandatory anti-avoidance overrides rather than free-form check-the-box elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; compulsory for resident and non-resident suppliers alike once the threshold is met, with voluntary registration below the threshold and for offshore suppliers of remote services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST-registered supplier may zero-rate exported tangible goods when the goods are exported from New Zealand by the supplier within 28 days of the time of supply and the supplier retains evidence of export. Supplies of services to a non-resident recipient are zero-rated when the recipient is outside New Zealand at the time of performance and the services are not excluded services (excluded services include those directly connected with real property in New Zealand, physical goods in New Zealand, or performed in New Zealand in the physical presence of the recipient). International freight, transport, and arrangement services are also zero-rated. Offshore remote services supplied to New Zealand-resident consumers do not qualify for zero-rating and may be subject to GST under the offshore supplier rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration / New Zealand Trade Single Window electronic export entry
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / cargo manifest evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement/foreign-currency remittance evidence from the non-resident buyer
  • GST return worksheet and internal zero-rating schedule with country-of-destination records

No real-time fiscal clearance, API tax clearance, or digital stamping regime. Tax invoices must be issued by GST-registered suppliers and contain prescribed particulars. GST returns (GST101) are filed electronically through myIR at required 1-, 2-, or 6-month intervals. E-invoicing via the Peppol framework is voluntary, not mandated, and is not connected to any VAT clearance approval.

Inland Revenue reviews zero-rated export claims through GST return matching, export-assurance checks, and risk-based audits. If export evidence is missing, incomplete, or false, the zero-rating is reversed, output GST is retrospectively assessed for the original taxable period, refunds are clawed back, and use-of-money interest is charged from the original date of over-claim. Shortfall penalties apply at statutory rates for unacceptable tax positions (20%), abusive tax positions (40%), and evasion (up to 75%), with higher criminal penalties and prosecution available for knowingly fraudulent documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains unrestricted full convertibility under IMF Article VIII obligations for both current and capital account transactions. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a freely floating currency with no surrender, conversion, or notification requirements imposed on residents or non-residents. Profit and dividend remittances, royalty payments, management fees, and capital repatriations are wholly liberalised and require no RBNZ approval. The only friction points are tax-related rather than FX-related: non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at up to 30% domestic rate or reduced treaty rate, and Thin Capitalisation / transfer-pricing documentation duties under the Income Tax Act 2007. No balance-of-payments reporting is required for commercial trade, bank wires, or FX dealings. Bank counterparties perform only AMLCFT-mandated sanctions and source-of-funds checks, which do not amount to exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender, mandatory repatriation, or mandated conversion ratios governing export receipts in New Zealand. An exporting company may retain export proceeds in any freely convertible currency in domestic foreign-currency accounts or in offshore accounts without restriction. Settlement currency is purely a commercial contract matter. The only compliance expectations are indirect: proceeds must be recorded in the company's audited financial statements and taxed on an accrual basis for NZ income tax, and the underlying trade documentation (commercial invoice, bill of lading, customs declarations) must support the payment if a bank conducts an AMLCFT review of a high-value inbound credit.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ / Te Pūtea Matua) - the central bank and prudential regulator; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) supervises the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) for payment services and non-bank financial institutions; sanctions administration falls under the New Zealand Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade (MFAT). New Zealand has no separate foreign exchange administration authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderately viable (Feasibility Rating: Medium-High) but not as a sole treasury backbone. Under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the AMLCFT Act 2009, an offshore EMI must be registered on the New Zealand FSPR and join an approved dispute-resolution scheme to lawfully operate into NZ. In practice, established cross-border EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Currenxie, dLocal) service NZ-incorporated exporters and offer attractive mid-market FX and faster settlement into Chinese supplier currencies; however, primary e-commerce acquirers (Shopify Payments, Stripe, and local POS acquirers) commonly settle only into a licensed NZ bank account and not into offshore EMI rails. The recommended architecture is a tier-1 NZ bank operational account as the anchor receiving account, with a registered EMI used as a satellite account for cost-efficient, high-frequency outbound trade disbursements and multi-currency sweeping.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for equity funding: SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the NZ operating account with transaction reference 'Share capital contribution', purpose code corresponding to 'Direct Investment-EQUITY', supported by a directors' share-allotment resolution and a Companies Office share-register update filed within 20 working days. No RBNZ exchange-control approval or report is required. For director loans: MT103 purpose codes 'Intercompany Loan' or 'Directors Loan' with a signed loan agreement containing commercial terms (interest rate, maturity, repayment waterfall) to satisfy any transfer-pricing and thin-capitalisation audit considerations. For inbound funds from related parties, the beneficiary bank will apply AMLCFT screening on source-of-funds; therefore the sending bank and the NZ bank require a copy of the transaction rationale, a source-of-wealth statement, and the group organisational chart. Overseas Investment Act 2005 screening does not apply for a standard capital subscription into the described designer-toy operating company unless the entity controls sensitive land, media broadcast assets, or carries significant business assets exceeding NZ$100 million aggregate revenue, which is out of scope for the stated ISIC 3240 profile. The OIO post-approval pathway should still be documented in the corporate file for comfort.Clearing rails for settlement of imports and outsourced manufacturing: (a) Telegraphic transfer via SWIFT MT103 for deposits and progress payments, using purpose codes 'Trade payment - raw materials' or 'Trade payment - services'; executed in USD, CNY, or NZD with same-day value where both banks are SWIFT-connected; dual-control approval through the bank's digital payment factory; (b) Irrevocable documentary letter of credit (LC) at sight issued under UCP600 for larger production runs, in USD or CNH, with the Asian manufacturer's bank as advising/negotiating bank; the LC is opened under the NZ bank's trade exposure line and requires the presentation of commercial invoice, packing list, full-set clean-on-board bill of lading, inspection certificate, and beneficiary certificate; (c) Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) under URC522 for mid-size balances that do not merit LC cost; and (d) Open-account settlement with SWIFT MT103 netting where the supplier relationship matures and credit insurance is in place. All outbound payments must be screened against sanctions lists and verified under the 'payer pays' rule adopted by the NZ banking industry. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be kept when the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZ$10 million for goods, services, and intangibles in a fiscal year, or when related-party interest-bearing loans exceed NZ$2 million in aggregate principal; however, de minimis exceptions may apply to taxpayers with no permanent establishment and total turnover below NZ$1 million. Master File and Local File documentation is required for significant taxpayers, typically where the combined cross-border related-party dealings exceed NZ$100 million in annual turnover, or where Inland Revenue issues a notice requiring such documentation.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain standalone 'Economic Substance Regulations' analogous to Caribbean jurisdictions; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (Section BG 1, Income Tax Act 2007) and judicial doctrines require genuine economic substance and commercial justifications for cross-border arrangements. Treaty benefits are denied where the principal purpose of an arrangement is obtaining a treaty benefit, and entities engaged solely in passive holding activities without functional risk-mitigation presence are subject to recharacterisation under anti-avoidance provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Diminishing Tax Liability)

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994; maximum penalty of 5 years' imprisonment and/or a fine of NZ$50,000 per charge. Civil liability includes a shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax plus substantial late payment interest charged at the prevailing rate.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable as shadow principals when they actively participate in defalcation or deliberate concealment; courts may impose disqualification orders under the Companies Act 1994, and criminal restitution orders are possible. While travel bans are not a standard tax remittance remedy, extradition proceedings may arise in cases of cross-border tax fraud.
Shell Company / Insubstantial Economic Presence

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of treaty-based withholding rate reductions; recharacterisation under Section BG 1 resulting in denial of deductions and imputation credits; deregistration of the company under Section 318 of the Companies Act 1994 for failure to carry on a lawful business; administrative penalties up to 150% of the tax adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for business debts where insolvent trading occurs (Section 135 of the Companies Act 1994); the company's corporate veil can be pierced under Section 304 for sham units; directors in offshore trust structures may face personal liability on the basis of de facto directorship and management.
Transfer Pricing Abuse

Statutory sanction: Compensatory adjustment under Subpart GC; imposition of a transfer pricing penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall where contemporaneous documentation is absent or deficient; further shortfall penalty of 20% for failure to keep adequate records.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer pricing documentation may be subject to fines under Schedule 8 of the Tax Administration Act; they can also be held personally liable for penalties where the actions amount to aiding and abetting a fraudulent shortfall; in egregious cases, professional disqualification or extradition to a treaty partner is possible.
AML/CTF & Economic Substance Irregularities

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 up to NZ$4 million per charge for corporate entities; deregistration of the director and shareholder records under the Companies Act 1994; freezing orders for assets connected to unlicensed financial services.

Director personal liability: Officers responsible for AML/CTF compliance face civil liability penalties of up to NZ$300,000 per individual; repeated non-compliance can result in imprisonment for up to 7 years under Section 348 for money laundering facilitation; the High Court can order a travel ban in restraint of proceeds.
