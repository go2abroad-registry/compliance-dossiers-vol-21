Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For a foreign or offshore International Company (CK_IC) with New Zealand nexus: first determine whether the entity derives New Zealand-sourced income, has a New Zealand permanent establishment, or makes taxable supplies in New Zealand. If no nexus exists, no New Zealand IRD number or GST number is required and the entity remains in the tax-exempt zone. If nexus arises, apply through myIR for a non-individual IRD number using the Inland Revenue non-resident/non-individual application process; provide certified certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director and shareholder identity documents, evidence of any New Zealand PE or agent, and, where applicable, register as an overseas company with the Companies Office under Part 18 of the Companies Act 1993. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident International Company with no New Zealand taxable presence and no New Zealand-sourced income; by default outside the New Zealand income tax base, with qualifying offshore services eligible for zero-rated GST treatment; effectively an offshore exempt tax profile).

Available election forms

New Zealand does not have a US-style check-the-box regime. The relevant mechanisms are: (i) Look-Through Company (LTC) election for a closely held New Zealand company under the Income Tax Act 2007, making qualifying companies fiscally transparent; (ii) New Zealand GST group registration election under section 54A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and GST branch registration election; (iii) hybrid mismatch outcomes under Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007, which are mandatory rather than elective but can be influenced by foreign entity classification elections; and (iv) overseas entity classification elections made under the foreign jurisdiction of incorporation, which New Zealand will respect subject to its own substantive tax characterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 actual or expected taxable supplies in New Zealand during any 12-month period; a separate NZD 60,000 threshold also applies to non-resident suppliers of remote or digital services supplied to New Zealand consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

New Zealand GST zero-rates qualifying exports of goods and services under Schedule 1 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. For game 3D art outsourcing services, the supply is zero-rated where the services are provided to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are not supplied for use or consumption in New Zealand. This applies to cross-border B2B creative services such as 3D asset sculpting, character rigging, and art production delivered to offshore game publishers. Physical goods exports are zero-rated when the goods are physically exported from New Zealand. Supplies made to a New Zealand-resident publisher, or services consumed in New Zealand, are standard-rated at 15% unless another statutory exemption applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUS) and approved export entry for physical goods; not required for pure cross-border digital or contracted services
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from New Zealand; for services, substitute digital delivery and acceptance records
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange receipt from the offshore client evidencing foreign-currency settlement
  • Executed service agreement or statement of work identifying the non-resident game publisher-client and the jurisdiction where the services are consumed
  • GST tax invoice annotated 'Zero-rated' and citing the applicable Schedule 1 / section 11 Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 basis
  • Recipient tax residency certificate or foreign GST/VAT registration confirmation evidencing the recipient is outside New Zealand

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of invoices. A valid GST tax invoice must be issued within 28 days of supply and must contain the supplier name, supplier GST number, invoice date, recipient name, description, quantity, price, and GST treatment. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR. Electronic invoicing is voluntary but interoperable through the New Zealand Peppol network (NZePS); Inland Revenue provides APIs for approved software providers, but clearance remains a post-lodgement risk review rather than an issuance-time authorization.

Inland Revenue may audit zero-rated export claims and may reopen assessments within the statutory reassessment period, including an unlimited period where fraud or evasion is established. If the zero-rating substantiation fails, GST at 15% is assessed on the consideration, input tax credits are clawed back, late payment penalties apply, and use-of-money interest (UOMI) accrues from the original due date. Culpability-based shortfall penalties range from 20% for lack of reasonable care, 30% for an unacceptable tax position, and 50% for an abusive tax position, up to 100% for evasion or an intent to evade. Deliberate fraudulent export zero-rating may also give rise to criminal prosecution and director personal liability. Where the offshore purchaser is a related party, transfer pricing adjustments may additionally increase the New Zealand taxable income of the exporting entity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised capital and current account convertibility regime. There are no exchange control restrictions under New Zealand law affecting the free conversion of the New Zealand dollar, the repatriation of capital, the remittance of dividends, service fees, royalties, or the settlement of intercompany payables. The New Zealand dollar is a freely floating fiat currency with no differential treatment between residents and non-residents, and no distinction of consequence between capital account and current account items for conversion purposes. Profit remittance hurdles are effectively nil, provided the underlying transactions are genuine commercial dealings, properly documented, and do not trigger statutory anti-money laundering reporting thresholds. No withholding or remittance levy is imposed exclusively on foreign payments; only standard income tax and GST rules apply at the transaction level.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. A company may retain export receivables in any foreign currency, maintain foreign currency bank accounts offshore or in New Zealand without limit, and convert to New Zealand dollars or any other currency at commercial rates as business needs dictate. There is no requirement to remit export earnings to New Zealand, no obligation to exchange foreign currency into NZD, and no central bank approval or surrender certificate is required. The only mandatory overlay is the general record-keeping obligation under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and AML/CFT legislation, requiring accurate books and records traceable to underlying contracts.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), operating pursuant to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, in coordination with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) for the administration of the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009; the RBNZ acts as the sole monetary and prudential regulator, while foreign exchange policy is fully liberalised with no separate exchange control authority requiring conversion approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI and cross-border B2B payment platforms are viable as secondary cash-flow rails but cannot substitute for a primary licensed New Zealand banking relationship. Under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 and the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013, only RBNZ-registered banks and licensed non-bank deposit takers may accept deposits from the public in New Zealand. Platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, OFX, and PayPal operate under foreign wallet, funds-transfer, or EMI licences and are lawful ancillary channels for low-value disbursements and receipts, but they are not permitted to offer deposit accounts to a New Zealand-registered company as a primary treasury facility. Banks will assess the company's use of such EMIs as part of their transaction-monitoring profile, and the compliance team will require full transparency regarding all payment-service-provider relationships, corresponding IBANs, and the absence of any layering or routing structures that obscure beneficial ownership. The overall feasibility rating for a hybrid EMI-plus-primary-bank model is 'Medium-High', conditional on complete disclosure and documentary support.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The capital injection should be routed by the offshore parent or founding shareholders as a clean SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfer from a prime correspondent bank to the company's New Zealand account. The remitter must use a purpose code consistent with ISIC 7490 professional services, most commonly 'STE' (payment for services) or 'REU' (reinvestment of earnings), or, if using the ISO 20022 structured remittance format, the code '794' for business and professional services; cash and unentered cheques are not acceptable. The remitter's instruction field must cite the board resolution number, the phrase 'Equity contribution against share issue', and the post-issue share register reference. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory New Zealand threshold is prescribed for mandatory Local File or Master File documentation. However, Inland Revenue requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for all material cross-border related-party transactions. Country-by-Country reporting applies to major multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least NZ$1.2 billion (equivalent to EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute comparable to certain offshore jurisdictions. Instead, Inland Revenue applies the general anti-avoidance rules, substance-over-form principles, controlled foreign company rules, and transfer pricing rules to deny tax benefits obtained through shell arrangements. An International Company claiming tax-exempt status on 3D game art outsourcing income must demonstrate genuine commercial substance, including real employees, offices, contracts, decision-making, and business risk outside New Zealand. If the central management and control of the International Company is located in New Zealand, it becomes a New Zealand tax resident and is taxable on worldwide income, causing the tax-exempt-zone treatment to be disregarded.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Non-Declaration of New Zealand-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Under sections 143B and 143C of the Tax Administration Act 1994, the taxpayer is liable to a tax shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the evaded tax, and the Crown may prosecute criminally. Upon conviction, fines may be imposed up to NZ$50,000 and imprisonment for up to five years may be ordered.

Director personal liability: A director who aids, abets, incites, or conspires in the evasion of tax may be personally liable for the company's unpaid tax and penalties. The corporate veil may be pierced for civil restitution, and directors may face travel bans while prosecutions are pending, asset restraint orders, and imprisonment.
Shell Company with No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may invoke the general anti-avoidance rules to reconstruct the arrangement, deny treaty benefits, cancel the tax-exempt status, and assess additional tax. Civil shortfall penalties for abusive tax positions can reach 100% of the tax shortfall, increasing to 150% for evasion. Directors may be disqualified under the Companies Act 1993.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or document sham transactions can be jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall and penalties. Courts may lift the corporate veil, order personal restitution, impose travel bans, and enter convictions resulting in imprisonment for false statements or perjury.
Failure to File and Non-Compliance with New Zealand Withholding Obligations

Statutory sanction: Late filing and late payment penalties apply together with use-of-money interest. A withholding agent who knowingly fails to withhold can be held liable for the unpaid NRWT, and civil penalties may be imposed up to 200% of the under-withheld amount if the failure is deliberate.

Director personal liability: Directors with responsibility for payment and filing obligations can be personally liable under the Tax Administration Act for the withheld tax, interest, and penalties if funds were withheld but not remitted. Travel bans may be imposed in the course of criminal prosecution, and imprisonment is possible for fraudulent conversion of retained tax.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may adjust the consideration to the arm's length amount and reallocate income to New Zealand. Shortfall penalties apply for transfer misstatements: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion, in addition to use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute false transfer pricing documentation or know of a pricing scheme designed to avoid New Zealand tax may be personally liable for penalties and interest. The corporate veil may be pierced in insolvency, and custodial sentences may be imposed for fraudulent documentation or knowingly misleading the Commissioner.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a body corporate may be fined up to NZ$10 million for serious AML/CFT failures, and individuals may be fined up to NZ$100,000 and imprisoned for up to five years. Civil penalty orders, compliance ratcheting orders, and registration cancellation may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who knowingly engage in or permit money laundering activities can be held criminally liable. The court may impose travel bans, freeze personal assets, order civil restitution to the Crown, and impose custodial sentences of up to five years or more for related fraud offences.
Misrepresentation of Tax Exempt Zone Status and Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may deem the International Company to be resident in New Zealand and assess tax on worldwide income at 28%, together with penalties for evasion or abusive tax positions. Any tax-exempt status under the entity's home jurisdiction does not override New Zealand taxing rights.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false residency declarations or fail to notify Inland Revenue of a New Zealand permanent establishment are exposed to personal liability for tax shortfalls, civil penalties, travel bans, and criminal prosecution. Imprisonment may result from fraudulently altering records or misleading the Commissioner.
