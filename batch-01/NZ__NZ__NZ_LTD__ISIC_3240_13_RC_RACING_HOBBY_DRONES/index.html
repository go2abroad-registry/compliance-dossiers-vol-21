E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: New Zealand Limited Companies are separate taxable persons subject to corporate income tax at 28% on net income computed under New Zealand tax law; resident companies are subject to tax on worldwide income with certain foreign income exemptions and active foreign equity attribution rules; non-resident companies are taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income (subject to applicable double tax agreements). No automatic pass-through is available, but qualifying closely held companies may elect look-through company status to be treated as tax transparent.

Available election forms

(a) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under sections HB 1-HB 4 of the Income Tax Act 2007, available to companies with five or fewer natural-person shareholders (or permitted corporate shareholders), with election filed on IRD form IR101 and effective from a specified date; (b) Dividend imputation election to maintain an imputation credit account and attach imputation credits to dividends; (c) Hybrid mismatch elections and notifications under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 to align New Zealand tax treatment with foreign hybrid arrangements, including joint elections for dual-entity structures under the branch mismatch rules; (d) Foreign Investment Fund (FIF) attribution method elections for interests in foreign entities; (e) Non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) or approved issuer levy (AIL) election in respect of interest and dividends paid to non-residents.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (a person must register for GST once the total value of taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any 12-month period exceeds NZD 60,000, or once it is reasonably expected to exceed that threshold; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold in certain circumstances, including for persons making mainly zero-rated supplies)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (a) goods exported by the supplier from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply, provided the exporter holds appropriate evidence of export; (b) goods supplied to a non-resident person who is not in New Zealand at the time of supply and who exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days (with certain exceptions for goods retained in New Zealand for consumption); (c) services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, when the services are supplied for use or enjoyment outside New Zealand and are not directly connected with land or goods in New Zealand, with specific exceptions for integral services physically performed in New Zealand; (d) the supply of a business as a going concern, and certain supplies of fine metals (investor gold) under prescribed conditions. For FPV drone exports, zero-rating requires demonstrable physical export of the carbon-fiber airframes, brushless motors, and assembled units from New Zealand, and the supplier must hold shipping and payment evidence to substantiate that goods left New Zealand within 28 days of the invoice date.

  • Customs Export Declaration (approved by New Zealand Customs Service, including the Electronic Export Manifest and Trade Single Window data)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (duly signed and dated, showing the consignor and consignee, vessel/flight details, and port/airport of loading and discharge)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds from the non-resident purchaser
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration and shipping documents
  • Evidence of the date of export (e.g., departure certificate, container tracking report, or carrier certificate) to prove the 28-day dispatch requirement

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is voluntary but promoted through the PEPPOL network and the New Zealand e-Invoicing solution (supported by IRD and the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment); GST returns and payments are filed electronically via myIR on a one-month, two-month, or six-month frequency depending on annual turnover. There is no requirement for real-time API transmission to IRD at the point of invoice issuance, but taxpayers must retain complete records of invoices (including electronic data) and make them available to IRD on request; GST invoices require specific prescribed content (tax supplier details, amount exclusive of GST, GST amount, and GST-inclusive total).

Inland Revenue audits export zero-rating claims by verifying the physical export timeline and documentary consistency across customs records, shipping manifests, bank remittances, and the taxpayer's GST return. Failure to export goods within 28 days of the time of supply requires the supplier to account for GST at 15%; retrospective adjustments may be triggered if the supplier ceases to hold satisfactory evidence of export. Penalties include: a shortfall penalty of 20% of the tax shortfall for failure to take reasonable care; 40% for gross carelessness; 100% for evasion or an arrangement with a dominant purpose of defeating the GST Act; and 150% for abusive tax avoidance, together with use-of-money interest (presently 8.08% per annum for taxpayer underpayments). Knowingly claiming a false export zero-rating may constitute an offence under sections 143 and 144A of the Tax Administration Act 1994, punishable by fines up to NZD 50,000 for a company and imprisonment up to 5 years in serious cases. In addition, clawback of input tax credits claimed on purchases attributable to the export may be required, and the IRD may recharacterize a transaction where economic substance is absent, resulting in the full GST amount being reassessed with penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand's currency, the New Zealand Dollar (NZD), is fully convertible under both current and capital account transactions. There are no residual exchange controls or any requirement for central bank approval for current account payments, trade settlements, dividend distributions, loan repayments, or capital repatriation. The current account is fully liberalized; capital account proceeds derived from genuine investments by non-residents are freely repatriated, subject only to standard corporate law solvency tests, tax clearance formalities under the Income Tax Act 2007, and compliance with the Overseas Investment Act 2005 where the investment involves sensitive land, fishing quota, or significant business assets. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal: the company must satisfy the Companies Act 1993 dividend test, ensure any imputation credits and non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) are correctly attributed, and file the relevant tax return to obtain a certificate of exemption if required by the bank. In practice, no exchange control 'green light' or prior approval exists for ordinary retail drone export manufacturing operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender requirement on export proceeds. All export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or freely converted into NZD at bank-offered exchange rates. There is no legal timeline for converting export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to channel such proceeds through a designated domestic account. For reporting purposes, the RBNZ collects BOP data on an aggregate basis; individual transaction-level reporting is not required unless the transaction involves $100,000 or more and is captured by the bank's own reporting obligations under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act 1996 (as amended by the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009).

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) as the central banking, currency issuance, and banking supervision authority; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) does not regulate FX controls but supervises AML/CFT obligations for certain financial institutions; the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) regulates foreign investment approval for certain sensitive assets, but not general FX conversion or remittance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) have limited viability as the primary banking rail in New Zealand. They are not recognized as 'banks' or 'registered banks' under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989, and they do not provide New Zealand local account numbers or access to the local payments infrastructure (e.g., NZ ClearLink). An NZ Ltd may use an offshore EMI as a supplementary collection or disbursement conduit if the EMI is properly licensed under its home jurisdiction, but the RBNZ and the Financial Markets Authority would not grant such an entity a New Zealand banking license unless it establishes a locally incorporated subsidiary and complies with the NZ banking prudential framework. For the FPV drone business, a pragmatic compliance approach is to maintain a primary relationship with an NZ tier-1 bank for local clearing and statutory trust requirements, and treat the offshore EMI strictly as an optional low-limit receivable channel for customer deposits, subject to full AML/CFT due diligence. The viability rating is 'Low-Medium' because of regulatory ambiguity, lack of NZ deposit guarantee (under the RBNZ's Crown deposit guarantee scheme if applicable), and the absence of NZD clearing and settlement functionality.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a non-resident parent or ultimate beneficial owner injecting capital into NZ_LTD, the compliant routing is as follows: (1) board resolutions of NZ_LTD approving the issue of shares or execution of a shareholder loan agreement; (2) any Overseas Investment Act 2005 approval is NOT required if the investor is not acquiring 'sensitive land', 'significant business assets', or interests in fishing quota; if the investment is in a retail drone manufacturing business, it falls outside OIO screening thresholds and no prior approval is required; (3) settlement wire must be sent via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank to the NZ bank account, with payment details containing 'Share Subscription' or 'Paid-up Capital' and the purpose code indicating 'capital transfer' (under the OECD Balance of Payments classification, use purpose code 'DFIN' for direct investment into a company's capital, or 'AFIN' for portfolio-type investment, depending on whether the investor controls the company); (4) the NZ bank may require an SOLID (Statement of Liability, Income and Deductions) or a tax IRD number to allocate the inbound payment to equity; (5) all inbound funds should be received in NZD to avoid FX conversion at the NZ receiving end, and the remitter must use the investor's own bank account or a verified trust account; (6) proof of source of funds (payslips, sale contracts, or an audited balance sheet) is retained in the AML/CFT file. No compulsory NZD conversion ratio applies.For outbound trade payments to overseas suppliers of carbon-fiber sheet, brushless motors, flight controllers, and assembly components, the preferred clearing rails are: (a) SWIFT MT103 for outright wire transfers under each supplier pro-forma invoice; (b) Commercial documentary Letters of Credit (L/C) using SWIFT MT700 issuance and MT707 amendments for larger, repeat shipments where the bank's risk appetite permits; (c) SWIFT MT202/MT202 COV for bank-to-bank reimbursement under an L/C, if applicable; and (d) for smaller recurring payments, a batch FX payment service enabled through the bank's online portal, using purpose code 'GEN' (general merchandise trade) or 'SC02' (trade goods) as referenced by the RBNZ's BOP system. The bank will screen each transaction against the Sanctions and AML lists, and the beneficiary must be a legitimate trading company compliant with New Zealand's export control rules.
