Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Tāke), New Zealand. Online portal: Inland Revenue myIR (www.ird.govt.nz).

E-government filing portal

For an NZ limited company incorporated under the Companies Act 1993, an IRD number (tax file number) is automatically issued upon registration with the New Zealand Companies Office and IRD's integrated registration. A foreign company carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand must apply for an IRD number using form IR742 'Non-resident company IRD number application', providing certified copies of its incorporation certificate, constitution, and evidence of a New Zealand physical or registered office. If the entity is required to register for GST (value of NZ taxable supplies exceeds NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period, or for non-resident remote services suppliers supplying to NZ residents), the application is made in myIR or via form IR356; the same IRD number is used as the GST registration identifier. There is no separate VAT registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand does not employ a US-style check-the-box regime. However, eligible close-held companies may elect look-through company (LTC) status to be fiscally transparent, using form IR878 and satisfying the eligibility criteria under the Income Tax Act 2007. Hybrid mismatch rules (Part FM) may require elective adjustments to the deductibility or inclusion of cross-border payments. There is no elective bifurcation between corporation and pass-through; entity-level taxation is the default and LTC is the only substantive pass-through election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in taxable supplies made in New Zealand (or for non-resident suppliers of remote services, NZD 60,000 of supplies to New Zealand-based consumers) in any 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated if they are exported from New Zealand by the supplier or an agent (or supplied to a recipient outside New Zealand and removed within 28 days of supply) and the supplier retains documentary evidence of export, including customs declarations and movement records. Services are zero-rated as exported services under section 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where they are supplied to a non-resident recipient outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, or the services relate directly to out-of-New-Zealand property, land, or activities. Remote services supplied to non-resident individuals who are outside New Zealand at the time of supply are zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (via New Zealand Trade Single Window for goods exceeding NZD 1,000)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency)

New Zealand does not require real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping of invoices for GST compliance. E-invoicing is voluntary but standardised via the Peppol network (PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0). From 2022, government agencies must be able to receive e-invoices, and suppliers to government may be required to issue e-invoices through Peppol. No API-based clearance or real-time tax transaction reporting applies to private-sector B2B/B2C transactions.

Inland Revenue actively audits export zero-rating claims. A failure to substantiate zero-rating with the required export documents results in the deemed supply being reassessed to 15% GST due, plus shortfall interest from the original due date. Penalties for shortfall: 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 20% for gross carelessness, 50% for an abusive tax position, and 100% for evasion; false input tax credits may be clawed back with interest. Criminal prosecution may be initiated for fraudulent export zero-rating claims, carrying fines of up to NZD 50,000 or imprisonment for tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible under a free-floating exchange-rate regime with no capital controls, no surrender requirements, and no central-bank approval for remittances. Both current-account transactions (export proceeds, royalties, dividends, management fees) and capital-account transactions (share capital subscriptions, shareholder loans, loan repurchases, and liquidation distributions) may be executed freely in either NZD or any permitted foreign currency. There are no profit-remittance hurdles beyond ordinary taxation: non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) applies to dividends (commonly 0-15% depending on imputation credits and tax treaty), interest (often reduced from 15% to 2% via the Approved Issuer Levy), and royalties (5-15% under most treaties); no exchange-control permit, no waiting period, and no prior government clearance is required for outbound profit transfers. New Zealand-domiciled companies may legally hold multi-currency foreign-currency accounts at domestic banks and offshore depositaries, and no distinction is drawn in practice between capital-account and current-account convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. New Zealand maintains no statutory timeline for repatriation of export receipts, no mandatory conversion ratio into NZD, and no obligation to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or any state authority. Exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely in an approved foreign-currency account, deploy it directly against offshore supplier invoices for natural hedging, or convert it bilaterally with a licensed bank. The only compliance obligations relevant to a cosplay-prop manufacturing exporter are: (i) zero-rated GST treatment under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 supported by proper export documentation (bill of lading, customs proof, NZ Customs entry); (ii) arm's-length transfer pricing documentation under the Income Tax Act 2007 for intercompany loans or related-party transactions; and (iii) sanctions and AML screening of counterparties under the AML/CFT Act 2009 and applicable United Nations sanctions regulations.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand Te Pūtea Matua (RBNZ), operating under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, is the central bank and prudential regulator of the New Zealand banking system; however, New Zealand abolished its exchange-control apparatus in 1984-1985 and has no central foreign-exchange administration. Non-resident investment and foreign-ownership screening is conducted by the Overseas Investment Office (OIO / Toitū Te Whenua) under the Overseas Investment Act 2005, while anti-money-laundering and counter-financing-of-terrorism supervision of banks is shared among the Financial Markets Authority (FMA), the RBNZ, and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High as a supplementary payment rail; Low as a primary treasury counterparty. Offshore digital e-money institutions and financial-service-provider-registered platforms (Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut Business, Ebury) are legally available in New Zealand as FSPs under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, but are not registered banks, are not licensed deposit takers, and do not offer the full suite of commercial lending, documentary trade finance, or SWIFT correspondent services that a tier-1 bank provides. They are most viable for holding small working capital, making low-cost MT103-equivalent supplier transfers in USD, CNY, GBP, or EUR, and receiving marketplace payouts; however, they remain exposed to sudden de-risking, account freezes, and the absence of RBNZ prudential backing. Recommended posture for NZ_LTD: maintain the master corporate relationship at ANZ, Westpac, or BNZ; deploy one EMI (Airwallex or Wise Business) for inbound Stripe/Shopify US-sourced receipts and Chinese supplier payments in CNY; keep aggregate EMI exposure below NZD 100,000; and never route the capital-escrow or tax-remittance trail through an EMI because banks and the Inland Revenue require settlement into onshore NZ bank accounts for audit confidence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from overseas shareholders or trusts should be sent as a SWIFT MT103 (or the ISO 20022 pacs.008 equivalent where the sending bank is in SEPA or the UK) to the New Zealand tier-1 bank, with the NZ clearing account formatted as NZD/Bank-Branch-Account (e.g., ANZ 01-XXXX-XXXXXXXX-XX) and the payment reference 'Capital contribution per shareholder resolution dated DD/MM/YYYY'. The remitter must include the NZ bank's foreign-exchange and reporting purpose code for inward investment (the bank's electronic platform will map the transaction to the standard non-financial corporate investment category), attach a certified shareholder resolution and board authority, and provide CRS and FATCA self-certification in advance. No RBNZ approval is required; however, if the counterparty is resident in a FATF grey-listed state, the financial-crime team should receive pre-notification plus 6 months of source-of-funds statements. If structured as a shareholder loan rather than equity, the loan agreement must be supplied, interest must be at arm's length for transfer-pricing and NRWT purposes, and the remittance should be coded as intercompany financing to permit clean outgoing repayment later.Supplier payments for fabrics, synthetic wig fibres, trims, and prop materials should use: (i) SWIFT MT103 electronic transfers from the NZ bank's online treasury platform for values up to NZD 50,000, including same-day conversion using the bank's FX desk at the 2pm Reserve Bank NZD fixing for pricing confidence; (ii) confirmed or advised irrevocable commercial letter of credit (LC at sight) for consignments above NZD 50,000, opened by the NZ tier-1 bank and advised through the supplier's Chinese or Hong Kong bank, with Incoterms stated (typically FOB or CIF) and copies of the bill of lading, commercial invoice, and packing list presented against a complying presentation; (iii) documentary collections (D/P or D/A) where the supplier relationship is mature and LC fees are unwarranted; and (iv) Stripe and Shopify Payments card rails for retail e-commerce, with settlement routed by direct entry into the NZD operational account. Royalties and brand-licence fees to overseas IP owners must be disbursed by MT103 with a clear royalty purpose code and with appropriate NRWT deducted at source before remittance. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High as a supplementary payment rail; Low as a primary treasury counterparty. Offshore digital e-money institutions and financial-service-provider-registered platforms (Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut Business, Ebury) are legally available in New Zealand as FSPs under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, but are not registered banks, are not licensed deposit takers, and do not offer the full suite of commercial lending, documentary trade finance, or SWIFT correspondent services that a tier-1 bank provides. They are most viable for holding small working capital, making low-cost MT103-equivalent supplier transfers in USD, CNY, GBP, or EUR, and receiving marketplace payouts; however, they remain exposed to sudden de-risking, account freezes, and the absence of RBNZ prudential backing. Recommended posture for NZ_LTD: maintain the master corporate relationship at ANZ, Westpac, or BNZ; deploy one EMI (Airwallex or Wise Business) for inbound Stripe/Shopify US-sourced receipts and Chinese supplier payments in CNY; keep aggregate EMI exposure below NZD 100,000; and never route the capital-escrow or tax-remittance trail through an EMI because banks and the Inland Revenue require settlement into onshore NZ bank accounts for audit confidence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million aggregate annual cross-border associated-party transactions triggers the expectation of contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation; Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for NZ groups with consolidated revenue of at least NZD 1.15 billion.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing regime. However, economic substance is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule (Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1), corporate tax residence tests, transfer pricing risk/reward alignment, and anti-shell enforcement. A cosplay-prop apparel manufacturer must demonstrate genuine local staff, physical premises, real inventory flows, and board decision-making to support treaty claims, R&D credits, and TP positions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty of 150% of the tax shortfall under Tax Administration Act 1994 s 141D; criminal conviction under s 143B with imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fine up to NZD 50,000 for individuals or NZD 100,000 for companies; back-dated assessments and removal of statutory time bars.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who knowingly participate may be prosecuted as principals, face personal criminal fines, civil restitution orders, travel bans, and director disqualification; Inland Revenue may pursue directors personally where the company is used as a sham.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: General anti-avoidance rule re-characterisation under Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1/s GB 1 cancelling the tax advantage; shortfall penalty of 100% for an abusive tax position under s 141E; company deregistration under Companies Act 1994 s 318; income may be attributed to controlling persons.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for resulting tax shortfalls where they signed false returns; overseas directors may face travel restrictions and extradition requests; director disqualification under the Companies Act 1994 may follow.
AML/CFT Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, maximum pecuniary penalties of NZD 200,000 for individuals and NZD 2 million for companies per contravention; money laundering under Crimes Act 1961 s 243 carries imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers can be personally prosecuted for knowingly causing the company to breach AML/CFT obligations; civil penalties and legal costs may be recovered personally; courts may impose disqualification orders.
