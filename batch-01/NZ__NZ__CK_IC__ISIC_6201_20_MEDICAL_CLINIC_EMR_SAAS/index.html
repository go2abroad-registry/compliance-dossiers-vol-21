Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: the CK_IC International Company is treated as a non-resident/exempt company for New Zealand income tax purposes so long as its management and control (central management) are outside New Zealand and it derives no New Zealand-source income. New Zealand taxation applies on a territorial basis only, so offshore-sourced EMR/SaaS subscription revenue is outside the New Zealand income tax net; no entity-level income tax, no capital gains tax, and no dividend withholding tax on exempt foreign-sourced income.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory/elective mechanisms: (i) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under the Income Tax Act 2007 for closely held NZ-resident companies (up to five owners, tax transparency); (ii) GST group registration election under s 55 Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; (iii) hybrid mismatch election/compliance measures under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018; (iv) non-resident 'exempt company' status for a company wholly owned by non-residents that carries on no business in NZ and derives no NZ-source income; and (v) voluntary GST registration for non-resident suppliers of remote services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period. Non-resident suppliers of 'remote services' (including cloud SaaS/EMR software) to New Zealand resident consumers are subject to the same NZD 60,000 threshold and must register under the offshore supplier GST regime; voluntary registration is permitted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under ss 11 and 11A Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, the 0% export rate applies where: (i) tangible goods are physically exported from New Zealand within 28 days of supply; (ii) services are supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply (this includes B2B offshore SaaS/EMR subscriptions to foreign medical clinics where the services are performed and consumed outside New Zealand); (iii) services relate directly to land or moveable goods situated outside New Zealand; or (iv) the supply is international carriage. Remote digital services supplied to New Zealand resident end consumers are outside zero rating and attract 15% GST under the offshore supplier registration regime.

  • New Zealand Customs electronic Cargo Export Declaration (CusMod single-window export entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank/foreign-exchange statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Evidence of the offshore recipient's non-resident status (overseas tax identification number or business registry extract) for service zero-rating

New Zealand has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-stamping, or government digital stamping for invoices. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates over the Peppol network ('NZ e-Invoicing'); since 1 April 2022 all New Zealand central government agencies are required to accept e-invoices. GST returns and payments are filed electronically through the myIR portal or certified accounting/Xero software; no transactional pre-clearance by the Inland Revenue Department is required.

The Inland Revenue Department and New Zealand Customs jointly audit export zero-rating claims. Failure to substantiate export, or non-repatriation of export proceeds within the required window, causes the supply to be recharacterised as taxable at 15% GST with use-of-money interest and retrospective refund clawback. Civil shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply at 20% (failure to take reasonable care), 40% (abusive tax position), and 100% (tax evasion); fraudulent zero-rating/export refund claims attract criminal prosecution, penalty tax on top of clawback, and disqualification from the New Zealand Customs electronic clearance system.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD, ISO-4217) is a free-floating, fully convertible currency. New Zealand has accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement; consequently there are no current-account or capital-account restrictions, no FX prioritisation, no utilisation ratios, and no convertibility queues. SaaS subscription income from US medical clinics, equity injections from non-resident shareholders, shareholder loan repayments, and profit repatriation are all unrestricted as a matter of exchange-control law. Commercial banks are free to set bid/offer spreads, and settlement is carried out through the CLS system for NZD/USD. The only material friction in repatriation is tax-related: statutory non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at 15% applies to dividends, interest, and royalties paid by a New Zealand payer, though an applicable double tax agreement may reduce that rate, and the IC's tax-exempt classification means no New Zealand source taxable income arises from its treasury operations. Profit remittance to non-resident owners therefore requires no government approval and is limited solely by documentary compliance and board-approved treasury policy.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand abolished all mandatory surrender and conversion obligations for export receipts on 10 December 1984. There is no statutory timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, no retention ceiling, and no FX allocation scheme applicable to export proceeds. A medical-clinic EMR SaaS licence sold into the United States is conceptually an export of services under the Balance of Payments framework, but the resulting USD receipts may be held abroad or in a New Zealand foreign-currency account indefinitely, may be converted to NZD at any time, and may be used directly for offshore operational disbursements without RBNZ permission. The only ongoing obligations are record-keeping duties under section 51 of the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and, if the entity is selected in a Statistics New Zealand/RBNZ statistical survey, the classification of receipts under Balance of Payments item E422 Software/Cloud Export Proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand – Te Pūtea Matua (RBNZ), the sole statutory banking regulator and apex monetary authority, supplemented by the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for financial-services conduct and the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) for narrow ex-ante screening of sensitive land and significant business assets. The governing framework is the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, the Deposit Takers Act 2023, the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and the Companies Act 1993. New Zealand maintains no separate foreign-exchange licensing or exchange-control administration; the RBNZ's regulatory power attaches to monetary policy and the stability of registered banks rather than to the approval of individual cross-border remittances. For the EMR SaaS International Company, no FX permit, no OIO consent, and no RBNZ pre-approval is required for the treasury architecture described herein.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: SUPPLEMENTARY HIGH / PRIMARY LOW. New Zealand does not maintain a distinct electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence class; an offshore digital EMI such as Airwallex or Wise may lawfully operate as a registered money remitter or stored-value facility under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the AMLCFT Act 2009, but it is not a New Zealand registered bank for the purposes of the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021. Consequently, such an EMI cannot lawfully accept call deposits from the public, cannot lend, and provides no protection under the Depositor Compensation Scheme. For this International Company the practical architecture is: (a) a Tier-1 New Zealand bank account as the primary real-money treasury ledger, capital-escrow vessel, and settlement account for clinic counterparties; and (b) a licensed EMI sidecar, integrated with Stripe_Billing and Bank_Transfer rails, for fast multi-currency merchant settlements, contractor sub-accounts, and multi-region payment localisation. The EMI must be independently licensed in its home jurisdiction, maintain robust sanctions/PEP screening, and accept the same enhanced-due-diligence dossier required by the Tier-1 banks before the treasury committee will commit floating balances above USD 50,000.

Compliant protocol: (1) execute an unconditional share-subscription agreement or shareholder loan instrument; (2) instruct the remitter's bank to send SWIFT MT103 with beneficiary field 59 containing the exact IC corporate name, field 50K the true investor name, and field 70 annotated 'EQUI – Equity Subscription / Direct Investment Inflow (BOP DI1)'; (3) route the funds exclusively to the dedicated Capital Escrow Account at the Tier-1 New Zealand bank — never commingle with operating revenue; (4) deliver to the bank the subscription schedule, board resolution, constitutive documents, and a source-of-wealth certificate for each investor before the funds are released to the operational ledger; (5) confirm that the transaction does not trigger the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which is inapplicable to SaaS intangibles and intangible-position treasury balances; (6) book the receipt for Balance of Payments statistical purposes as 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' if the transaction is sampled by Statistics New Zealand/RBNZ; and (7) retain the MT103 confirmation and investor-side KYC pack for at least 10 years under the AMLCFT Act 2009. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: SUPPLEMENTARY HIGH / PRIMARY LOW. New Zealand does not maintain a distinct electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence class; an offshore digital EMI such as Airwallex or Wise may lawfully operate as a registered money remitter or stored-value facility under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the AMLCFT Act 2009, but it is not a New Zealand registered bank for the purposes of the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021. Consequently, such an EMI cannot lawfully accept call deposits from the public, cannot lend, and provides no protection under the Depositor Compensation Scheme. For this International Company the practical architecture is: (a) a Tier-1 New Zealand bank account as the primary real-money treasury ledger, capital-escrow vessel, and settlement account for clinic counterparties; and (b) a licensed EMI sidecar, integrated with Stripe_Billing and Bank_Transfer rails, for fast multi-currency merchant settlements, contractor sub-accounts, and multi-region payment localisation. The EMI must be independently licensed in its home jurisdiction, maintain robust sanctions/PEP screening, and accept the same enhanced-due-diligence dossier required by the Tier-1 banks before the treasury committee will commit floating balances above USD 50,000.
