Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand; official online portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity, including an International Company used as a venture capital general partner, must obtain a New Zealand tax identity only where it derives New Zealand-source income or carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand. The procedure is: (1) assess whether the entity must be registered as an overseas company on the New Zealand Companies Register and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN); (2) apply to the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) for an IRD number using the prescribed non-resident company IRD number application, accessible through the IRD website and myIR; (3) provide certified copies of the entity's incorporation or registration certificate, constitutional documents, director and ultimate beneficial owner details, registered address, and a New Zealand address for service where applicable; (4) register for GST in the same myIR filing if projected taxable supplies in New Zealand exceed the registration threshold or if voluntary registration is requested; (5) engage a New Zealand tax agent or legal representative for ongoing compliance if the entity has no physical presence in New Zealand. If the entity has no New Zealand taxable nexus, no IRD number or GST registration is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (for this International Company: classified as a non-resident foreign entity; foreign-source business income and capital gains are outside the New Zealand territorial tax net; New Zealand-sourced income is taxable only to the extent derived through a New Zealand taxable activity or fixed establishment; no domestic New Zealand income tax holiday or special economic zone exemption is otherwise available).

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. A qualifying New Zealand-resident closely held company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under the Income Tax Act 2007, causing income, expenses, and credits to flow through to up to five individual owners, but this election is generally unavailable to a foreign International Company. Limited partnerships are statutorily transparent for New Zealand income tax purposes. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections FH 1-FH 11 of the Income Tax Act 2007 may recharacterise the entity or deny deductions in respect of certain cross-border payments. There is no elective tax-exempt-zone regime under New Zealand law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services are also required to register and charge New Zealand GST once this threshold is exceeded unless the supply qualifies for zero-rating)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 60 days after the time of supply and retains transport and customs documentation proving export. Services: zero-rating applies where the services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, and the services are not supplied directly in connection with New Zealand land or moveable personal property situated in New Zealand at the time of supply; certain offshore intermediary services also qualify. The supplier must hold sufficient documentary evidence of the non-resident recipient, the nature of the service, the place of supply, and the actual export/payment chain. New Zealand does not impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for GST zero-rating; the 180-day period is used as the documentary and banking benchmark for inward remittance evidence, and longer delays must be explained and justified to the Inland Revenue Department.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry) filed through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window and endorsed with the export approval or departure reference
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill / Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice / GST tax invoice expressly stating 'Zero-rated for GST - Export Supply'
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT / bank transfer advice evidencing receipt of the export proceeds from the offshore customer
  • Packing list, export cargo report, consignment note, and any certificate of origin or non-manipulation certificate where required by customs or the IRD

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory pre-approval, or digital stamping regime applies in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary but implemented through the PEPPOL network; New Zealand government agencies are required to receive e-invoices. IRD accepts electronic tax invoices and GST returns filed via myIR and supports third-party software integration and IRD gateway APIs. Tax invoices must comply with the GST invoice content requirements in section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

Inland Revenue may audit any zero-rated export supply and retrospectively assess the 15% GST if the documentary evidence of export or non-resident supply is insufficient or false. The clawback carries use-of-money interest from the original GST due date, plus late payment penalties (initially 2% of the amount outstanding) and a shortfall penalty ranging from 20% to 100% depending on intention, including 100% for abusive tax avoidance or evasion, under the Tax Administration Act 1994. If goods are diverted back into the New Zealand market or the export declaration is false, the supplier becomes liable for GST on that supply, may be delisted from future zero-rating privileges, and can face criminal penalties under the Customs and Excise Act 2018.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible with a free-floating exchange rate regime; New Zealand has no exchange-control legislation restricting current or capital account payments, and the Cook Islands uses the NZD as its official currency, meaning the CK_IC structure faces zero local convertibility restrictions. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, portfolio subscriptions, profit repatriation, and carried-interest distribution, are permitted without prior Reserve Bank screening. The practical conversion hurdles are not FX controls but: (i) the enhanced AML/CFT verification of inbound LP capital under the New Zealand Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009; (ii) the risk that the IC's central management and control is exercised from New Zealand, which would deem the offshore entity tax-resident in New Zealand under Income Tax Act 2007 s YB 3(2) and destroy its Tax-Exempt Zone status under the Cook Islands International Companies Act 1981-82 (as amended); and (iii) the absence of a New Zealand-Cook Islands double tax agreement, which subjects NZ-source management fees and interest payments to the default non-resident withholding tax rates of 15% (royalties), 15% (dividends), and 15% (interest) unless the approved issuer levy (AIL) of 2% is elected for interest paid to the IC. Profit remittance from the GP fee and carry accounts is classified as an invisible transfer and is freely remittable in any OECD currency without surrender, levy, or timing restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable to the CK_IC venture capital general partner entity, which does not engage in merchandise export trade. New Zealand abolished all surrender requirements for export proceeds in 1984, and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand imposes no mandatory conversion ratio on foreign-currency receipts. For balance-of-payments statistical purposes, RBNZ transaction monitoring applies only through the Bank's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) or balance-of-payments framework, with a reporting threshold historically set at NZD 1 million per transaction; no conversion is mandated. In the Cook Islands, Exchange Control Act 1979 (Cap 60) applies only to residents, and an International Company licensed under the International Companies Act 1981-82 is statutorily exempt from all exchange-control requirements, including the former requirement to surrender export receipts; the IC may therefore retain indefinitely any foreign-currency earnings in its offshore multi-currency bank accounts.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) is the central bank and prudential supervisor for all New Zealand-licensed banks and non-bank deposit takers; the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) is the licensing and supervisory authority for the Cook Islands International Company; the New Zealand Treasury exercises residual foreign-investment screening authority under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 for sensitive New Zealand assets. No foreign-exchange control authority exists in either jurisdiction because both have fully liberalised capital and current account regimes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. New Zealand does not maintain an e-money institution (EMI) licensing regime comparable to the European Economic Area; a non-bank payment service provider operating in or into New Zealand must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, become a reporting entity under the AML/CFT Act 2009, and join an approved dispute-resolution scheme. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currencycloud, Wise Business, or MultiPass) can provide the CK_IC with segregated multi-currency collection and disbursement accounts, and are operationally viable as pre-funding rails for capital-call wires and SWIFT transfers; however, they are prohibited from accepting deposits in New Zealand unless licensed as a Non-Bank Deposit Taker (NBDT) with Reserve Bank approval, none of the leading EMIs holds such approval, and their accounts do not offer direct access to the Reserve Bank's settlement system (ESAS). An EMI may therefore be used solely as a payment execution layer, subject to the same enhanced KYB/AML checks as a tier-1 bank, but the IC must maintain its primary treasury and escrow balances at a licensed bank to satisfy LP-audit, fund-administration, and statutory-trust expectations. Legal constraint also arises under section 12 of the International Companies Act 1981-82, which requires the IC's registered office and licensed trust/corporate service provider to remain in the Cook Islands, but does not restrict the IC from opening transactional accounts with any regulated foreign financial institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital subscription wires must be executed via SWIFT MT103 with a mandatory reference to the capital-call notice number and the LP's designated investor code. The IC's New Zealand bank account—preferably a dedicated Capital Escrow Account at BNZ or ANZ—serves as the exclusive collection point. The purpose of the payment must be expressed as 'Equity contribution to venture capital fund pursuant to Limited Partnership Agreement' with IMF Balance of Payments classification 'P31 – Portfolio investment, equity' or, for active controlling investments, 'E1 – Foreign Direct Investment, equity capital'. Where the receiving bank requires a local purpose code, the transaction should be tagged under the New Zealand Treasury 'Equity securities' classification or the bank's internal 'Capital Raising' ticket. All inbound funds are covered by the fund's FATCA and CRS registration, and the bank reports the receipt to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand under the BOP statistical framework. The compliant operational protocol comprises the following five steps: (1) the GP issues a formal capital-call notice at least ten business days prior to the drawdown date; (2) each LP instructs its bank to send an MT103 referencing the notice ID and the LP investor code; (3) the receiving bank matches the reference, confirms the LP's identity, and credits a segregated escrow account with a 'cleared funds' status; (4) funds are held in trust pending verification that the drawdown condition precedent has been satisfied and that no third-party financing of the subscription is involved; and (5) after verification by the fund administrator, the escrowed funds are swept to the primary portfolio investment operating account for deployment. Cash-in-transit, informal value-transfer systems, and third-party netting are strictly prohibited.Outbound portfolio disbursements are executed through SWIFT MT103 for equity investments, bridge loans, follow-on funding, and management-fee flows; the IC operates multi-currency accounts (NZD, USD, EUR, GBP, JPY) with its tier-1 bank to avoid redundant FX conversion. For high-value interbank cover transactions where the IC maintains accounts at different banks, the rail is SWIFT MT202 COV (General Financial Institution Transfer) with the required cover details; for portfolio companies financed by trade-related working capital, the bank may issue Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 via SWIFT MT700 with the corresponding settlement via MT756, but this is an ancillary facility. The central liquidity-clearing rails are: (i) MT103 straight-through processing with SWIFT gpi (Global Payments Innovation) end-to-end tracking for all equity and debt deployments; (ii) MT202/MT202 COV for treasury conversions and cleared-fund settlement; (iii) automated FX spot and forward settlement instructions transmitted via the bank's electronic treasury workstation; and (iv) Delivery-versus-Payment (DVP) settlement through CLS Bank for major-currency conversions above USD 10 million to eliminate settlement risk. Every outbound wire must carry the IC's Legal Entity Identifier (LEI), a remittance-purpose code, reference to the investment-committee approval minute, and the fund's tax identifier; the bank pre-screens each wire against the fund's cap table and investment policy to ensure that no disbursement exceeds the approved deal envelope or violates negative covenants.NZD/USD deliverable FX forwards (tenor up to 12 months, extendable to 24 months upon credit approval) used to hedge capital-call currency mismatch and portfolio disbursement exposure.FX swaps (spot vs forward) for intra-fund liquidity management across the multi-currency account structure without incurring unnecessary conversion spreads.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) to convert portfolio-company debt facilities from USD or EUR into NZD or between funding and investment currencies.NZD plain-vanilla European-exercise FX options (over-the-counter) for hedging uncertain drawdown timings, follow-on investment contingencies, and M&A warehousing pipelines.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted or non-deliverable emerging-market currencies (including CNY, KRW, INR, and TWD) where portfolio companies invoice or settle in those currencies; these are booked through the bank's offshore booking centres and cleared on a non-deliverable basis at maturity.CLS-settled Delivery-versus-Payment FX transactions for major currencies (NZD, USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, AUD) to mitigate settlement risk on large capital-call conversion amounts.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. New Zealand does not maintain an e-money institution (EMI) licensing regime comparable to the European Economic Area; a non-bank payment service provider operating in or into New Zealand must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, become a reporting entity under the AML/CFT Act 2009, and join an approved dispute-resolution scheme. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currencycloud, Wise Business, or MultiPass) can provide the CK_IC with segregated multi-currency collection and disbursement accounts, and are operationally viable as pre-funding rails for capital-call wires and SWIFT transfers; however, they are prohibited from accepting deposits in New Zealand unless licensed as a Non-Bank Deposit Taker (NBDT) with Reserve Bank approval, none of the leading EMIs holds such approval, and their accounts do not offer direct access to the Reserve Bank's settlement system (ESAS). An EMI may therefore be used solely as a payment execution layer, subject to the same enhanced KYB/AML checks as a tier-1 bank, but the IC must maintain its primary treasury and escrow balances at a licensed bank to satisfy LP-audit, fund-administration, and statutory-trust expectations. Legal constraint also arises under section 12 of the International Companies Act 1981-82, which requires the IC's registered office and licensed trust/corporate service provider to remain in the Cook Islands, but does not restrict the IC from opening transactional accounts with any regulated foreign financial institution.
