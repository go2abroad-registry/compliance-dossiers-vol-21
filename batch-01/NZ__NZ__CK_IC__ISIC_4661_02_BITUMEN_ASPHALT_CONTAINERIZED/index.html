Competent corporate registry

Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No 105), principally ss 12 to 19 (incorporation procedure), ss 34 to 39 (registered office and records), ss 128 to 137 (appointment and duties of directors), and ss 189 to 200 (share capital and shares). The 'International Company' is a company limited by shares incorporated under this statute, with the descriptor 'International' denoting its non-resident-owned trading profile.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The Companies Act 1993 imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital. A company may be incorporated with zero issued capital; shares may be issued for no cash or for a nominal credit to capital. No escrow account or mental deposit is required under New Zealand law. For an international trading entity the subscribers commonly issue 1,000 ordinary shares at NZD 1.00 each, fully paid upon non-cash consideration or a small banked subscription amount.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Territorial Authority confirming compliance with the Building Act 2004 and the Fire and Emergency New Zealand (Fire Safety, Evacuation Procedures, and Evacuation Schemes) Regulations 2018. Structural load inspections are required for any shelving, racking, or mezzanine used for bagged or drummed bitumen. Occupational clearance under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 must be obtained by submitting a health and safety policy, worker engagement procedures, hazard register, and spill-response plan, together with proof of emergency lighting and fire extinguisher servicing.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (New Zealand Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake), official portal myIR at www.ird.govt.nz; export clearance is administered separately by the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign or offshore International Company (CK_IC): (1) determine whether the entity carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand or derives New Zealand-sourced income; if it does, register as an overseas company at the New Zealand Companies Office to obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). (2) Apply through Inland Revenue's myIR portal for an IRD number and complete the GST registration application once a New Zealand GST nexus exists, noting the statutory threshold of NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period. (3) If the entity has no New Zealand taxable activity and derives no New Zealand-sourced income, it is not required to hold an IRD number or GST registration, and its offshore income falls outside the New Zealand tax net under tax-exempt zone or foreign-company exemption principles. (4) Non-resident suppliers of remote services or low-value imported goods to New Zealand consumers must register for GST once the NZD 60,000 12-month consumer-supply threshold is exceeded. (5) New Zealand-incorporated companies are automatically issued a 13-digit NZBN and IRD number at incorporation; a non-resident exporter may also voluntarily register for GST, even without local activity, to support zero-rating and input tax recovery.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation for New Zealand-resident companies, which are subject to the 28% corporate income tax rate on worldwide income; Territorial_Scope limits the taxation of non-resident companies to New Zealand-sourced income; Tax_Exempt_Zone treatment is available for a non-resident International Company (CK_IC) that maintains governance and economic substance outside New Zealand and therefore has no New Zealand taxable nexus; pass-through eligibility is limited to Look-Through Companies (LTCs) with five or fewer shareholders and is not the default classification for a trading corporate vehicle.

Available election forms

New Zealand has no check-the-box election equivalent. Available statutory mechanisms include: (i) the Look-Through Company (LTC) election for closely held New Zealand companies under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007; (ii) voluntary GST registration and the election between the invoice basis and payments basis for GST output and input tax under section 19 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; (iii) hybrid and branch mismatch rules under sections FH 1 to FH 13 of the Income Tax Act 2007 that recharacterise tax outcomes for hybrid entities such as an offshore International Company and require notifiable arrangement declarations in certain cases; (iv) the active business exemption and controlled foreign company attribution elections for CFCs; and (v) double tax agreement elections that preserve the absence of a New Zealand permanent establishment so that export trading profits remain outside New Zealand source taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory GST registration applies to any person carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand with total taxable supplies exceeding NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period under section 51 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value imported goods must register once taxable supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in 12 months; voluntary registration is available at a lower threshold subject to Inland Revenue approval, and a non-resident exporter not carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand may register to access zero-rating and refund recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of drummed and bagged penetration grade bitumen are zero-rated under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply and retains contemporaneous documentary evidence of physical export. Related offshore services and logistics facilitation supplied to non-resident buyers qualify for zero-rating under section 11A where the recipient is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed and none of the statutory carve-outs apply, namely services in connection with land in New Zealand, services in connection with moveable personal property in New Zealand, or personal services performed in New Zealand. For containerised wholesale asphalt trading, the contract should pass title overseas, the container must be loaded on the exporting vessel, and payment must be received in foreign currency from the non-resident buyer; zero-rating remains available even where storage, drumming, and handling occur in New Zealand immediately before export, provided the goods are exported before the recipient obtains possession.

  • Customs export declaration (lodged electronically in the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or airway bill evidencing physical export and containerised movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or TT wire confirmation evidencing foreign-currency settlement by the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and signed sales contract naming the non-resident buyer and showing Incoterms placing export responsibility on the supplier
  • Container tracking and port loading records (container number, vessel name, departure date) proving goods left New Zealand within 28 days of supply
  • Certificate of Origin and customs valuation documentation supporting the declared export value

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based authorisation, or digital stamping regime applies to New Zealand tax invoices. e-Invoicing is voluntary under the PEPPOL framework, with mandatory adoption phased from 1 April 2022 for GST-registered businesses with annual turnover above NZD 20 million. Tax invoices, whether paper or electronic, must contain the statutory particulars required by sections 24 to 24GA of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, and all invoices and records must be retained for 7 years. Exporters must lodge customs export declarations through the New Zealand Customs Trade Single Window, but no pre-clearance approval from the tax authority is required for each individual supply.

Inland Revenue and the New Zealand Customs Service audit export zero-rating claims against shipping manifests, bank settlement records, and tax invoices. If the goods are not exported within 28 days, or the documentary bundle is incomplete, the supply is recharacterised as taxable at 15% GST and the supplier must account for output tax with retrospective reassessment. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for evasion or egregious conduct, and 100% for an abusive tax position, plus use-of-money interest compounding until payment. Fraudulent zero-rating arrangements attract criminal liability under sections 143A to 144 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 for evasion and assisting evasion, with fines up to NZD 150,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; for companies, liability may also be imposed on responsible directors and officers. The standard four-year time bar for assessments does not apply in cases of fraud, misrepresentation, or wilful default, allowing unlimited retrospective clawback of improperly claimed GST refunds.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both current account (trade in goods and services and remittances) and capital account (equity, loans, investments) transactions without prior approval by the Reserve Bank or the FMA. Profit repatriation, dividend distribution, intra-group loan repayment, and trade settlement may be freely executed through any registered New Zealand bank. For a non-resident Cook Islands International Company (tax-exempt, no New Zealand source business), the critical compliance hurdle is not convertibility but proving the absence of New Zealand management/control and providing a complete beneficial-ownership trail; if these are established, no New Zealand withholding tax or income tax attaches to offshore payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rule, mandatory conversion ratio, or forced repatriation timeline exists in New Zealand. Exporters and cross-border traders are not required to convert receipts into NZD or remit funds through the central bank. However, for statistical and AML/CFT purposes, banks report cross-border transactions under New Zealand's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) and Balance of Payments classifications. Underlying shipping and payment documents must be retained to evidence the commercial transaction. Bitumen/wholesale trading proceeds may be kept in any freely convertible currency and disbursed at the company's discretion.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand - Te Pūtea Matua (monetary policy, prudential regulation of registered banks and payment systems); Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for conduct and financial service provider licensing under the Financial Market Conduct Act 2013; Department of Internal Affairs / Ministry of Justice for AML/CFT oversight; Overseas Investment Office (OIO) for foreign investment screening (not applicable to wholesale bitumen trading unless sensitive New Zealand assets are acquired).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Conditional / Medium-Low for a Cook Islands International Company. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Revolut Business, Wise Business) can provide NZD accounts and cross-border payment routing, but they are not licensed banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021. They must be registered as financial service providers under the Financial Service Providers Act 2008 and comply with the AML/CFT Act 2009. Legal constraints: they cannot take deposits in the banking sense, cannot offer overdraft or credit lines, and must safeguard client funds in a segregated account held at a New Zealand registered bank. Many EMIs decline applicants that are tax-exempt offshore entities without substantial economic activity; if used, the company must present the same KYC package required by a bank, including a legal opinion confirming non-resident status. Fee structures are attractive for MT103/TT disbursements, but LC issuance and trade finance are not generally available on EMI rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for high-value interbank transfers) from the investor's bank to the company's New Zealand receiving account. The remittance information (Field 70) must include 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION / CAPITAL INJECTION' and any relevant invoice or board resolution reference. New Zealand does not require central bank approval, but the receiving bank must capture the economic purpose for Balance of Payments statistical reporting (typically BOP equity capital code E09/E10). The company must upload supporting evidence: board resolution approving the issuance, share certificate, proof of receipt from the investor, and source-of-funds documentation. If the capital injection is denominated in USD, the funds can be held in a USD clearing account or converted to NZD at the prevailing spot rate. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Conditional / Medium-Low for a Cook Islands International Company. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Revolut Business, Wise Business) can provide NZD accounts and cross-border payment routing, but they are not licensed banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021. They must be registered as financial service providers under the Financial Service Providers Act 2008 and comply with the AML/CFT Act 2009. Legal constraints: they cannot take deposits in the banking sense, cannot offer overdraft or credit lines, and must safeguard client funds in a segregated account held at a New Zealand registered bank. Many EMIs decline applicants that are tax-exempt offshore entities without substantial economic activity; if used, the company must present the same KYC package required by a bank, including a legal opinion confirming non-resident status. Fee structures are attractive for MT103/TT disbursements, but LC issuance and trade finance are not generally available on EMI rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand transfer pricing rules follow the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. There is no fixed statutory Local File / Master File threshold in NZD, but the IR402 International Dealing Schedule must be filed for related-party cross-border dealings exceeding NZ$1 million for goods, NZ$500,000 for services or financial arrangements, and for arrangements exceeding NZ$1 million in aggregate for other dealings. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be available upon request for transactions above these thresholds or where the Inland Revenue risk assessment requires it.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a separate codified Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute. However, the general anti-avoidance rule (Section BG 1, Income Tax Act 2007) and the reconstruction power (Section GC 1) apply to disregard artificial or sham arrangements. The CFC regime and DTA anti-abuse provisions (Principal Purpose Test) deny treaty benefits to shell companies lacking commercial substance. For the Tax_Exempt_Zone IC, a non-resident-owned holding or trading company should maintain board minutes, real decision-making, and arm's length remuneration for any New Zealand activities to withstand scrutiny.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Section 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994: imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to NZ$50,000 for individuals and NZ$200,000 for companies; civil penalties up to 150% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the evasion can be personally liable for the tax shortfall, face imprisonment up to 5 years, and be disqualified from managing companies under the Companies Act 1993.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; recharacterisation under Section BG 1 and GC 1; reassessment at the full 30% NRWT with shortfall penalties of 100% plus UOMI.

Director personal liability: Directors operating an empty shell without records, meeting minutes, or substance may be found personally liable for tax avoidance and aiding evasion; personal fines and director disqualification apply.
Transfer Pricing Failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to arm's length; shortfall penalties: 20% (lack of reasonable care), 40% (gross carelessness), or 100% (abusive tax position); plus UOMI on the adjusted tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for transfer pricing shortfalls where they sign false or misleading returns, under the Tax Administration Act 1994 sections 34C and 149; additionally they can be sued for breach of director duties.
AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to NZ$2 million per breach for a company; criminal penalty for directors/officers up to NZ$200,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; AML/CFT registration suspension or revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers personally liable for failure to implement an AML/CFT compliance programme; fines up to NZ$100,000 per offence and imprisonment up to 2 years; possible disqualification from financial services.
Imputation Credit / Dividend Strip Abuse

Statutory sanction: Imputation credit debit under section OB 42; penalty tax of up to 100% of the credit improperly used; NRWT reassessment at 30% plus UOMI; criminal prosecution for false declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise incorrect imputation credit entries are personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties under sections 149 and 150 of the Tax Administration Act 1994.
