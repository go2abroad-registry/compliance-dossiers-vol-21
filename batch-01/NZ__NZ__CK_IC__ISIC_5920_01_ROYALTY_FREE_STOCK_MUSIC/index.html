Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Te Tari Taake, New Zealand, administered through the myIR secure online portal at www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated or tax-exempt International Company must apply to the IRD for a non-resident IRD number using form IR742. If the entity makes taxable supplies in New Zealand exceeding the GST registration threshold, it must separately register for GST under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 using form GST-101. A tax-exempt Cook Islands International Company with no New Zealand-source income or taxable supplies in New Zealand is generally not required to obtain an IRD number, but may voluntarily register to recover input tax or to obtain definitive zero-rating status where the relevant statutory conditions are satisfied.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. A qualifying non-resident entity may elect to join a GST group for consolidated GST accounting under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 govern the New Zealand tax treatment of hybrid financial instruments and reverse-hybrid entities. A tax-exempt International Company may validly seek zero-rating of exported supplies where the statutory nexus tests are met, but no broader entity classification election is available for offshore tax-exempt structures.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is available under section 11(1)(c) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the recipient of the supply is a non-resident and is outside New Zealand at the time of the supply. For royalty-free stock music licences and audio sample subscription services, zero-rating applies to recurring subscriptions, download licences, and commercial soundscape licensing when the customer is not in New Zealand and the services are not directly connected with New Zealand land or physical moveable property. The supplier must hold sufficient commercial evidence of the non-resident status of each customer, including payment processor records, customer due-diligence information, and contracts.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, tax stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing clearance regime applies. Electronic invoicing in New Zealand is voluntary and follows the Peppol framework. GST-registered persons must file returns electronically through the IRD myIR portal or via gateway/API-integrated software, retain all underlying invoices, and preserve complete digital documentation for zero-rated export supplies.

The IRD may reassess zero-rated output tax and claw back the GST not previously accounted for if the documentary trail or non-resident status cannot be established. A shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall applies for an abusive tax position, together with use-of-money interest. Knowingly fraudulent export zero-rating claims constitute an offence under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the Crimes Act 1961, exposing the taxpayer to monetary fines and imprisonment. All records must be retained for at least seven years, and a failure to maintain adequate evidence will result in denial of the zero-rating benefit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible on both the current and capital account. New Zealand maintains an open capital and financial account with no foreign-exchange control regulations, no exchange-rate approval regime, and no restriction on the repatriation of capital, profits, dividends, or royalties. However, statutory withholding taxes (NRWT, or non-resident withholding tax) may apply to New Zealand-sourced interest (unless an Approved Issuer Levy is elected), dividends (30% statutory rate, reduced under applicable double-tax agreements), and royalties (15% statutory rate, or 5% under some DTA treaties). For an International Company (IC) structured to be tax-transparent or non-resident for NZ tax purposes, foreign-sourced licensing income is generally outside the New Zealand income tax net, provided the effective management and control of the company is not situated in New Zealand. Inbound capital injections and outbound trade settlements are unrestricted in NZD or any convertible foreign currency, subject to bank-level AML/KYC due diligence, transactional threshold reporting (e.g., NZD 10,000 for cash or enhanced verification triggers), and the Overseas Investment Act 2005 if the transaction involves sensitive land or a significant business asset. Profit remittance to a non-resident parent is permitted without central-bank approval, but the entity should hold a clear tax profile (IRD non-resident tax status letter) and comply with exchange-of-information disclosure obligations under CRS/FATCA where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion timelines, or minimum conversion ratios for New Zealand residents or for non-resident International Companies operating through a New Zealand bank. Export and licensing receipts (including stock-music subscription royalties collected via Stripe, PayPal, or SWIFT) may be retained in original foreign currency, deposited into offshore foreign-currency accounts, or converted to NZD at the company's commercial discretion. The only applicable requirements are standard AML/CFT reporting, the maintenance of adequate records supporting the underlying commercial transaction, and, for a New Zealand ring-fenced company, the ability to demonstrate that retained foreign-currency balances are not being used to evade tax or hide assets from the New Zealand Inland Revenue Department.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), acting under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, and the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for market conduct and AML/CFT enforcement under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High. New Zealand does not have a distinct 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) licensing tier. Instead, fintech payment and money-transfer services (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Visa Direct, or PayPal) operate as financial service providers (FSPs) registered with the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), subject to AML/CFT compliance and, in certain cases, to the Reserve Bank's Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2022. For an International Company focused on digital royalty-free stock music licensing, payment fintechs are highly viable as an auxiliary collection and cross-border clearing rail, particularly for receiving USD/EUR recurring subscription revenues and facilitating low-value payouts to musicians/contributors in 40+ countries. However, a full-service New Zealand bank must still anchor the liquidity structure to hold the capital escrow, manage FX hedging, and provide a regulated settlement account. Offshore digital EMIs cannot operate as a primary bank for a New Zealand-registered entity because they do not hold a New Zealand banking licence, are not members of the Retail Payment System, and do not provide the same legal certainty for capital escrow or tax-filing purposes. Consequently, we recommend a dual-layer structure: (1) primary bank account with ANZ or BNZ for capital and large-value clearing; (2) one or more fintech EMI accounts for day-to-day digital subscription aggregation and low-cost overseas payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial capital injection should be effected as a wire transfer via the SWIFT MT103 service from the parent entity or subscriber to the IC's dedicated capital escrow account at a New Zealand tier-1 bank. The MT103 narrative must carry the IC's New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) or client code, the text 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION - ISIC 5920', and a Security/FX code 'EQU' (or the equivalent ISO 20022 purpose code if the bank uses that standard). Under the New Zealand banking convention, for direct-investment equity injection, the Treasury remittance should be tagged with the Standard Balance of Payments code '539' (Other Direct Investment Capital) or '520' (Share Capital), as requested by the receiving bank. The sub-custodian bank will perform a 'source of funds' verification on the remitter; therefore the remitter must provide a bank account statement showing a credit trail, and the corporate bank must also be shown a board resolution approving the capital injection and the escrow instruction signed by the IC's authorised signatories. The escrow account is blocked for daily operations until the bank confirms full completion of the 'know the beneficial owner' and 'source of wealth' checks. Once verified, the capital balance is released to the operational account in line with the liquidity mandate. For collection of recurring subscription royalty flows, the payment rail is through Stripe/PayPal using their daily settlement ACH/direct deposit to the operational account; the incoming clearing text will show 'STRIPE PAYOUT' or 'PAYPAL MERCHANT SETTLEMENT'. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High. New Zealand does not have a distinct 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) licensing tier. Instead, fintech payment and money-transfer services (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Visa Direct, or PayPal) operate as financial service providers (FSPs) registered with the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), subject to AML/CFT compliance and, in certain cases, to the Reserve Bank's Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2022. For an International Company focused on digital royalty-free stock music licensing, payment fintechs are highly viable as an auxiliary collection and cross-border clearing rail, particularly for receiving USD/EUR recurring subscription revenues and facilitating low-value payouts to musicians/contributors in 40+ countries. However, a full-service New Zealand bank must still anchor the liquidity structure to hold the capital escrow, manage FX hedging, and provide a regulated settlement account. Offshore digital EMIs cannot operate as a primary bank for a New Zealand-registered entity because they do not hold a New Zealand banking licence, are not members of the Retail Payment System, and do not provide the same legal certainty for capital escrow or tax-filing purposes. Consequently, we recommend a dual-layer structure: (1) primary bank account with ANZ or BNZ for capital and large-value clearing; (2) one or more fintech EMI accounts for day-to-day digital subscription aggregation and low-cost overseas payouts.
