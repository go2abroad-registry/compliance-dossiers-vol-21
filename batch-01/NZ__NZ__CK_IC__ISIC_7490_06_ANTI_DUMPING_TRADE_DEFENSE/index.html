Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Tauhake), New Zealand — the statutory authority administering income tax, Goods and Services Tax (GST), and employer obligations; all registrations, filings, and taxpayer interactions are conducted through the myIR secure online portal at www.ird.govt.nz.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident and foreign-incorporated entities obtain a New Zealand IRD number (and, where applicable, GST registration recorded under the same IRD number) by: (1) assessing statutory nexus — compulsory registration arises where the entity derives New Zealand-source income, carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand, or makes taxable supplies connected with New Zealand that exceed the registration threshold; (2) submitting an online application through myIR after RealMe identity verification, or lodging the prescribed paper forms, notably IR596 for non-resident company registration and IR742 for GST registration; (3) providing certified supporting documentation, including the foreign certificate of incorporation, register of directors and shareholders, registered office address, ultimate beneficial ownership declaration, and, where required, evidence of appointment of a New Zealand agent for service of process; and (4) receiving the IRD number via confirmation letter or myIR portal notification, ordinarily within one to two weeks for paper applications and more quickly for online applications. Where the CK_IC International Company operates wholly offshore with no New Zealand-source income and no taxable activity in New Zealand, compulsory registration is not triggered; however, voluntary GST registration remains available to preserve input credit recovery or to issue compliant zero-rated invoices for export and offshore services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the CK_IC International Company is classified as a non-resident exempt offshore vehicle: it does not acquire New Zealand tax residency by virtue of foreign control and offshore central management and control; New Zealand income tax applies only on a territorial basis to New Zealand-source income, and foreign-source income is exempt absent a New Zealand permanent establishment. For GST purposes, supplies made by the IC to non-residents in respect of offshore services fall outside the New Zealand GST net, and the IC is not chargeable to GST where its supplies connected with New Zealand do not exceed the registration threshold.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box entity election, and the OECD hybrid-mismatch rules under subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 are mandatory and not elective. Statutory elections and mechanisms relevant to the CK_IC structure include: (i) voluntary GST registration for entities below the compulsory turnover threshold; (ii) election of the GST accounting basis, being either the payments (cash) basis or the invoice (accruals) basis; (iii) GST group registration election under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for commonly owned companies, treating intra-group supplies as outside the scope of GST; (iv) loss grouping and offset election between companies with 66% or greater common ownership; and (v) the financial-arrangements cash-basis calculation election under subpart EW for qualifying low-value taxpayers. The look-through company (LTC) election is generally unavailable to CK_IC because the presence of corporate shareholders disqualifies eligibility.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 — compulsory registration arises where total taxable supplies connected with New Zealand exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period; for non-resident suppliers of remote services and electronic marketplace operators, the same NZD 60,000 threshold is applied to supplies made to New Zealand consumers. Voluntary registration is available below the threshold where a taxable activity is carried on.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under sections 11 and 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for CK_IC trade-remedy defense consultancy supplies where: (1) the recipient is a non-resident located outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed; (2) the services are not directly connected with land situated in New Zealand or with goods physically situated in New Zealand at the time of supply; (3) for remotely supplied services, the statutory remote-services rules zero-rate business-to-business (B2B) supplies made to a GST-registered non-resident recipient; and (4) documentary evidence of the offshore supply is obtained and retained. Legal defense and consulting services performed on behalf of overseas importers in foreign or New Zealand anti-dumping proceedings typically qualify where the recipient is a non-resident outside New Zealand. Supplies made to a New Zealand resident client, such as a New Zealand exporter engaged in foreign anti-dumping investigations, are standard-rated and must be invoiced at 15% GST.

  • Outward Customs Export Declaration (NZCS export particulars) or equivalent proof-of-export record
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, or Airway Bill evidencing physical export movement where goods are involved
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank wire advice evidencing receipt of export or consultancy proceeds in foreign currency
  • Executed service agreement specifying the performance location outside New Zealand and the non-resident status of the recipient
  • GST zero-rated tax invoice containing the required zero-rating certification and New Zealand supplier details

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or transactional API clearance regime exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and is implemented over the PEPPOL network, with mandatory applicability only to suppliers invoicing New Zealand central government agencies under the government e-invoicing framework. Inland Revenue receives transactional GST data through periodic GST returns filed via myIR; entities must maintain machine-readable accounting records sufficient to produce valid GST tax invoices, credit notes, and export substantiation, and must retain all relevant records for a statutory period of seven years.

Inland Revenue audits zero-rating claims against the full documentary bundle; failure to substantiate the non-resident and offshore character of the supply results in reassessment at the standard 15% GST rate, with late payment interest accruing from the original due date. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 escalate to 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, and 150% for fraudulent or willful evasion; criminal prosecution may additionally impose fines and imprisonment for knowingly false or misleading export invoices. Export proceeds should be repatriated and documented within approximately 180 days, and material deferral without commercial justification may trigger anti-avoidance recharacterization and retrospective clawback of zero-rating benefits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has full capital and current account convertibility under IMF Article VIII status. There are no general foreign exchange controls restricting the conversion or repatriation of profits, dividends, or capital. Non-resident investors may freely remit profits and capital, subject only to standard AML/CFT verification and, for certain sensitive or large investments, the Overseas Investment Act 2005 screening regime. No material profit-remittance hurdles exist for routine consulting service income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements apply. All export receipts may be retained in any currency, freely used, or repatriated without RBNZ approval. Standard bank record-keeping requirements under the AML/CFT Act 2009 apply.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate with restrictions. Offshore digital EMIs not licensed or registered in New Zealand face substantial onboarding barriers because they are not recognized as 'reporting entities' under the AML/CFT Act 2009 and cannot directly open settlement accounts with NZ banks. A locally incorporated entity using a licensed non-bank payment service provider or an FSP-registered financial adviser may achieve viable shadow-banking capacity, but account opening friction and custodial concentration risk remain elevated. For full treasury functionality, tier-1 institutional banking remains strongly preferred.
