Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

New Zealand law imposes no minimum paid-up or issued capital requirement. A company may incorporate with one or more shares and the total consideration may be nil or in accordance with the board's valuation. No escrow account or capital deposit is mandatory. Shares must be allotted and the consideration recorded in the share register.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake) — official taxpayer portal: myIR (https://myir.ird.govt.nz); export and customs fiscal interface: New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS) Trade Single Window (TSW).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (CK_IC) engaged in cross-border express courier operations must: (1) if carrying on business in New Zealand, be registered at the New Zealand Companies Office to obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) and satisfy the overseas company registration requirements under the Companies Act 1993; (2) apply for an IRD number (tax file number) through the myIR portal using the applicable Inland Revenue company registration application, disclosing its non-resident status, New Zealand permanent establishment analysis, and representative agent or address for service in New Zealand; (3) register for Goods and Services Tax (GST) via myIR when taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period, or voluntarily to enable input tax credit recovery; (4) retain the IRD confirmation notice, NZBN certificate, and GST registration certificate as the statutory tax identity record for customs export declarations, zero-rating substantiation, and GST return filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand has no general US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections and elective regimes include: (a) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 for qualifying close companies, whereby income, losses, and credits flow through to shareholders; (b) voluntary GST registration election before reaching the NZD 60,000 threshold; (c) GST group registration election under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; (d) functional currency election for keeping accounts in a foreign currency; (e) Foreign Investment Fund (FIF) attribution method elections for offshore portfolio equity interests; and (f) hybrid and branch mismatch position disclosure rules under Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007, which operate as ordering and denial rules rather than elective check-the-box treatments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in a 12-month period (applying both the current-year and next-year turnover tests); non-resident suppliers of remote services and cross-border digital services supplying New Zealand consumers are also required to register when total supplies reach or exceed NZD 60,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 applies to: (a) exported goods supplied by a GST-registered exporter where the goods are physically exported from New Zealand within 28 days after the time of supply and the supplier is the exporter of record under the New Zealand Customs export entry regime; (b) international transport of goods, including cross-border express courier and time-definite small parcel carriage; (c) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the services are not supplied to another GST-registered person in New Zealand and are not directly connected with land or chattels in New Zealand (the offshore services test); and (d) ancillary loading, handling, and freight-forwarding services performed in New Zealand that are directly connected with the outward movement of goods. All zero-rated export supplies require the exporter to maintain contemporaneous commercial, customs, transport, and banking evidence to support input tax refund claims.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Export Declaration (export entry filed via the Trade Single Window ecosystem)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / courier consignment note evidencing physical export and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-currency receipt evidencing payment from the offshore consignee or customer

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance regime. New Zealand operates a voluntary PEPPOL e-invoicing framework; there is no statutory requirement for digital stamping, continuous transaction control, or tax-authority API pre-clearance of invoices. GST-registered persons must file GST returns (form GST101) electronically via myIR, typically on a two-monthly filing cycle, and the time of supply is determined by invoice issuance, payment receipt, or the removal of goods, whichever occurs first.

Inland Revenue and the New Zealand Customs Service jointly scrutinise export zero-rating claims, placing the evidentiary burden on the supplier to prove export within 28 days and the offshore character of the supply. Failure to substantiate results in retrospective GST assessment at the standard 15% rate, denial or clawback of input tax credits, use-of-money interest (UOMI), escalating late payment penalties, and shortfall penalties under section 141 of the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. The statutory time bar for reopening assessments is generally four years, with longer or unlimited time periods applicable where zero-rating has been claimed on false or misleading documents or in cases of fraud or evasion. Criminal liability includes fines and imprisonment of up to five years for individuals under sections 143 to 147 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, together with separate Customs offences for falsified export declarations and shipping documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalized, freely convertible currency regime. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted for residents and non-residents: no RBNZ approval is required for inward capital contributions, profit remittances, dividend distributions, or shareholder loans. Profit remittance is effected at market rates via any registered bank, subject only to standard Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) under the Income Tax Act 2007, resident-withholding tax on interest where applicable, and cross-border transaction reporting thresholds under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act) and the RBNZ Balance-of-Payments statistical surveys. Principal practical hurdles in remittance are documentation-driven: verified beneficial ownership, valid residency certificates for NRWT relief, and the banks' enhanced due-diligence (EDD) triggers for offshore tax-exempt companies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, forced conversion, or repatriation timeline applies to export receipts. Exporters may denominate and hold proceeds in foreign currency accounts without RBNZ restriction. The only statutory duties are statistical and evidentiary: (a) exporters above Statistics New Zealand / RBNZ reporting thresholds must complete monthly or quarterly Balance-of-Payments (BOP) surveys via IRD Business/Stats NZ; (b) supporting documentation (invoices, airway bills, settlement confirmations) must be retained for at least 7 years under the Tax Administration Act 1994; and (c) cross-border wire instructions must carry full originator information per the AML/CFT (Requirements for Low-Value Exchange Transactions) Regulations — effectively abolishing any mandatory conversion ratio (0% conversion requirement).

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — statutory apex under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989; foreign-exchange policy is liberalized with no dedicated FX-control ministry. The Overseas Investment Office (Toitū Te Whenua) separately screens foreign direct investment under the Overseas Investment Act 2005.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for payment-transmission and multi-currency wallet functions; Low for full treasury/lending functions. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise Business, Currenxie, MultiPass) are not registered banks in New Zealand and have no direct access to the Reserve Bank's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS). They can lawfully provide cross-border remittance and collected-funds services only through licensed partner banks, and if they solicit NZ business they must register as a 'financial service provider' (FSP) and reporting entity under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the AML/CFT Act. Critical legal constraints: (a) no ability to issue Commercial Letters of Credit or supply trade finance instruments; (b) no NZD direct-debit (ACH) membership in the NZ ACH clearing house; (c) incoming multi-currency balances may be restructured by the EMI as unsecured contractual claims, not safeguarded deposits eligible for depositor-compensation coverage; and (d) they generally impose turnover caps and sectoral exclusions on 'courier/logistics merchants' until audited transaction history is supplied.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank bundling) from the funding shareholder to the NZ beneficiary bank, with a clear purpose narrative: 'Paid-up capital contribution — [IC legal name] — board allotment ref. [No.]'. The receiving bank requires a completed AML/CFT purpose-of-transaction form; full originator information (name, address, account) is mandatory for all wire transfers of NZD 1,000 or above under the AML/CFT wire-transfer supervision framework. The bank will pre-clear the injection against the corporate CIP and will credit a Capital Escrow Account; funds are released to the Operating Account upon receipt of a directors' certificate and, where shares are issued, an allotment return lodged with the NZ Companies Office. No exchange-control authorization is required, but the source jurisdiction's outward-remittance law must be verified by the remitting bank. The beneficiary must hold an IRD number and, if the funds remain in an offshore-status account, must complete CRS/FATCA declarations to prevent interest-and-dividend reporting mismatches. Recommend splitting capital injection and operating funding into separate MT103 messages to preserve a clean audit trail.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct service-fee payments to courier partners, airline fuel/freight invoices, and interline settlements, with invoices embedded in the corporate compliance folder; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT720) for high-value fleet, aircraft-lease, and logistics-infrastructure acquisitions, with documents (commercial invoice, airway bill AWB, packing list, certificate of origin) negotiated through the NZ issuing bank; (3) New Zealand domestic ACH Direct Debit (through Payments NZ clearing with ESAS settlement) for recurring local payables such as warehouse rent, salaries, and utility costs; (4) Real-time domestic conversion via RBNZ ESAS for NZD urgent funding.
