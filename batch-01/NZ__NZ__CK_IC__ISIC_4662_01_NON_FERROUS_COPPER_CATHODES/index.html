Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Tāke), New Zealand's national tax authority, operating the myIR Secure Online Services portal (https://myir.ird.govt.nz) and the public IRD website (https://www.ird.govt.nz). Customs and export control administration rests with the New Zealand Customs Service (Ngā Rōpū Tiaki Taonga o Aotearoa) via its Trade Single Window system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain an 8-9 digit New Zealand IRD number. The Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC) applies as a non-resident company by submitting the non-resident company IRD number application (through myIR or the applicable IRD non-resident paper forms) together with: (i) certified Cook Islands certificate of incorporation, constitution, and registered agent certificate; (ii) director and ultimate beneficial owner identification; and (iii) contact details including a New Zealand address or NZ-resident agent where a taxable activity is anticipated. Where the entity carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand (e.g., wholesale supplies of copper cathodes), a simultaneous GST registration application must be filed; the same IRD number operates as the GST registration number. For export activities, the entity must additionally register as a client of the New Zealand Customs Service to lodge electronic export declarations through the Trade Single Window.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Territorial_Scope: For New Zealand income tax, the CK_IC is a non-resident foreign company (not incorporated in New Zealand, and lacking New Zealand head office or central management and control) taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income; no pass-through treatment is available. Formation-jurisdiction status: Tax_Exempt_Zone (the Cook Islands International Company is statutorily exempt from Cook Islands income tax under the International Companies Act).

Available election forms

New Zealand has no check-the-box entity classification regime. Statutory elections/mechanisms available: (i) voluntary GST registration below the NZD 60,000 threshold; (ii) look-through company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 — available only to New Zealand-resident closely held companies, and therefore unavailable to the non-resident CK_IC; (iii) CFC active-business exemption and attributed income elections for New Zealand-resident controllers; (iv) joint related-party election for zero-rating of exported goods where the GST Act conditions are satisfied; and (v) hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act 2007 subpart FH and Tax Administration Act 1994 Part 4B, effective for income years beginning on or after 1 July 2018) which operate by mandatory adjustment with a statutory ordering rule; a taxpayer may seek Commissioner approval to vary the ordering where a foreign hybrid mismatch regime requires a different sequence of adjustments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period (compulsory registration); voluntary registration is available below the threshold for persons carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand. Non-resident suppliers of remote/digital services to New Zealand consumers must register under the offshore supplier rules if their supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11 of the GST Act 1985, a supply of goods is zero-rated where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply (or such further period as the Commissioner allows), and where the supplier holds the customary evidence of export. Supplies of goods to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply are also zero-rated if the goods are subsequently exported. For offshore services (including trading, brokerage, or arbitrage support), zero-rating applies where the services are supplied to a non-resident outside New Zealand and are not supplied in connection with New Zealand land, goods situated in New Zealand when the services are performed, or services physically performed in New Zealand. For Grade A copper cathodes, the supplier must be the exporter of record, title must pass to the overseas buyer (e.g., FOB/CIF Incoterms or LME warrant transfer), and the goods must physically leave New Zealand; goods sold on an LME warrant without entering or leaving New Zealand are supplied outside New Zealand and fall outside the New Zealand GST net entirely, meaning no GST output tax is charged and no New Zealand input tax credit is available for related costs.

  • New Zealand Customs Service electronic Export Declaration (Customs export entry lodged via the Trade Single Window) identifying HS code 7403.11.00 (refined copper cathodes), gross weight, value, and overseas destination
  • Original negotiable Bill of Lading (or Sea Waybill) evidencing shipment from the New Zealand port of loading to the foreign destination port, consigned to the overseas buyer
  • LME-certified warehouse warrant or quality certificate evidencing Grade A refined copper cathode deliverable status
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by the CK_IC in the name of the exporter
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 payment confirmation, or documentary-letter-of-credit settlement document evidencing receipt of export proceeds within the repatriation deadline
  • Evidence of physical export within 28 days of the time of supply (e.g., Customs departure certification, port manifest, container tracking or load-port tally)
  • Export sale contract or LME sale confirmation incorporating the Incoterms rule and evidencing offshore transfer of title and risk

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous control, or digital tax-stamping mandate applies. GST is return-based: registered persons file periodic returns electronically via myIR and remit net tax. E-invoicing follows the Peppol framework — central government agencies must be able to receive Peppol e-invoices, while B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary. Export declarations, however, must be lodged electronically with the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window; no transaction-level API clearance is imposed on GST suppliers.

A zero-rating claim that fails the statutory export requirements recharacterises the supply as standard-rated, rendering the supplier liable for 15% GST on the consideration plus use-of-money interest under section 120 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 from the due date of the offending return. Civil shortfall penalties under section 141 of that Act apply at graduated rates: 20% for failure to exercise reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% where evasion or similar offence is established. Retrospective reassessments may be issued within the statutory time bar (generally 4 years, extended in cases of evasion) and GST refunds already paid are clawed back with interest. Dishonest or false export documentation exposes the entity and its officers to prosecution under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 (penalties up to NZD 250,000 per offence and forfeiture of goods) and the Crimes Act 1961 (tax evasion offences carrying imprisonment for natural persons). The Commissioner may also deregister the taxpayer, require security or bond for future registration, and publish penalty determinations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a freely floating currency fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions, and New Zealand satisfies IMF Article VIII obligations. There is no general exchange-control approval, quota, or conversion requirement applicable to a Cook Islands International Company engaged in wholesale copper cathode trading. Inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, intercompany funding, trade settlements, dividend distributions, loan repayments, and profit remittances may be effected freely in any convertible currency. The only consent-based or quantitative gates relevant to a foreign IC are: (i) the Overseas Investment Act 2005 for acquisitions of sensitive land, significant business assets, or fishing quotas, with ordinary wholesale metals trading and working-capital deployment normally beneath the notifiable thresholds; (ii) the Financial Sanctions Act 2019 regime administered by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade; and (iii) New Zealand tax obligations under the Income Tax Act 2007, including non-resident withholding tax on interest and dividends, and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for imported services and commission structures. Practical friction is not FX-convertibility but rather bank compliance verification of source of funds, payment purpose, and beneficial ownership.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised capital account and imposes no statutory timeline for surrendering export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation quota. A copper cathode exporter may retain USD receivables indefinitely in onshore or offshore foreign-currency accounts, net them against payable invoices, or convert them into NZD through any registered bank without central-bank approval. The operative obligations are record-keeping and statistical reporting: the collecting bank must capture accurate balance-of-payments purpose categories (for example 'goods — metals and minerals' or 'trade credit') for each cross-border transaction; the exporter must retain underlying invoices, LME warrants, bills of lading, and SWIFT instructions for AML/CFT and Inland Revenue audit purposes; and any foreign funds parked in a bank account must be reconciled in a manner consistent with the stated commercial activity.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) is the statutory central bank, prudential regulator of registered banks, and overseer of the national wholesale settlement system (ESAS). The Overseas Investment Office (OIO) administers foreign-investment screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005. The New Zealand Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) and the Department of Internal Affairs supervise anti-money-laundering and countering-financing-of-terrorism obligations. Sanctions administration is conducted by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade. New Zealand has no dedicated foreign-exchange-control ministry and issues no exchange-control permits.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for offshore digital EMIs and MEDIUM for locally registered non-bank payment providers. New Zealand has no standalone electronic-money-institution licensing regime; non-bank payment service providers must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR), belong to an approved dispute-resolution scheme, and be reporting entities under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. Offshore digital EMIs cannot directly access the New Zealand clearing infrastructure, cannot hold settlement accounts at the Reserve Bank, and cannot participate in ESAS (Exchange Settlement Account System) or the NZ Clear retail clearing network. In practice, an offshore EMI may be used only as a USD payment gateway in front of a New Zealand-licensed sponsor bank that performs the ultimate NZD settlement. The Cook Islands IC should expect significantly slower onboarding, enhanced beneficial-ownership review, and possible rejection where the EMI is incorporated in a low-tax jurisdiction with no demonstrable substance or New Zealand client-facing presence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: equity injections and shareholder loans should be routed by SWIFT MT103 (or equivalent) to the New Zealand bank's corresponding account in USD, EUR, or NZD, with field 70 (Remittance Information) quoting the IC legal name, the target account number, and the caption 'Capital injection — Cook Islands International Company'. The receiving New Zealand bank must allocate the correct balance-of-payments classification, normally 'direct investment — equity capital inward' for share injections or 'other investment — loan inward' for shareholder debt. The IC must present a board resolution authorising the injection, a written loan agreement where applicable, and source-of-funds documentation for each contributing shareholder. No Reserve Bank permission or OIO consent is required where the capital is deployed as ordinary working capital for wholesale copper cathode trading; however, if the injection includes the acquisition of an existing New Zealand business, significant business assets, or sensitive land, an Overseas Investment Act clearance may be required before funds may be applied.
