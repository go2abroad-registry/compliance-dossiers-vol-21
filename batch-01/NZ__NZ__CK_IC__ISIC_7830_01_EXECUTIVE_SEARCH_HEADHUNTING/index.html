Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake), Government of New Zealand; digital self-service portal 'myIR' at https://www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A non-resident International Company (IC) incorporated under the Cook Islands International Companies Act 2012 and administered outside New Zealand is not automatically required to hold a New Zealand IRD number unless it derives New Zealand-source income or elects to register for GST. Procedure for a foreign entity to obtain a statutory TIN/VAT ID: (1) confirm non-resident status by ensuring central management and control is not exercised in New Zealand and no New Zealand permanent establishment exists; (2) if the IC derives New Zealand-source income (e.g., executive search fees for placements in New Zealand or fees sourced to New Zealand), complete Inland Revenue registration forms IR596 (Non-Resident Company Registration) and IR742 (Non-Resident Taxpayer Information) and submit through myIR or by post to Inland Revenue, PO Box 39090, Wellington; (3) for GST purposes, if a GST registration obligation is triggered, file form GST1 via myIR providing the IC's incorporated name, registered agent address in the Cook Islands, proof of incorporation, and details of the New Zealand representative; (4) where the IC has no New Zealand nexus, it must nonetheless obtain a Cook Islands tax exemption certificate under the International Companies Act to evidence its zero-tax status; (5) maintain a New Zealand-approved non-resident contractor withholding tax (NRWT) registration only if NZ-source withholding obligations exist. No minimum capital or physical presence is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: The IC is a non-resident foreign company benefiting from a statutory zero-tax regime in its jurisdiction of incorporation; it is outside the New Zealand corporate income tax net on foreign-sourced executive search and headhunting fees, provided no permanent establishment and no central management and control in New Zealand. Entity_Level_Taxation: The company is a separate legal person and any New Zealand-source income is taxed at the entity level at the corporate rate of 28%; no pass-through treatment is available. Territorial_Scope: New Zealand taxes only income sourced within New Zealand for non-resident companies; offshore fees for C-suite search and tech headhunting services supplied to overseas clients are foreign-sourced and exempt. Pass_Through_Eligible: Not applicable — a non-resident IC is not eligible for New Zealand Look-Through Company (LTC) status, which is restricted to NZ-resident close companies.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime and does not permit unilateral hybrid entity elections. Available mechanisms for a non-resident IC: (1) selection of tax residence through governance — avoiding central management and control in New Zealand; (2) application of a double tax agreement (e.g., NZ–Cook Islands limited treaty network or applicable tie-breaker rules) to claim non-resident status and relief from NZ taxation; (3) the hybrid mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 may apply to deny deductions in New Zealand if the IC's zero-tax status creates a hybrid mismatch with an NZ payor; the IC may, however, elect to align its tax characterisation under the OECD Hybrids Tool and provide substantiation to the counterparty; (4) election to register for GST on a voluntary basis even if below the threshold, to claim input credits on NZ expenses; (5) no LTC election available for the IC as a non-resident company; no check-the-box election is available in New Zealand.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 annual taxable turnover for supplies made from a New Zealand fixed establishment or in the course of a New Zealand taxable activity; NZD 130,000 annual threshold for suppliers of remote services (cross-border digital and offshore services) to New Zealand-resident consumers. Executive search and headhunting services supplied by a Cook Islands IC to overseas clients are outside the NZ GST net, as the supply is made by a non-resident supplier with no NZ establishment and the recipient is a non-resident. If the IC invoices New Zealand-resident business clients for headhunting services, the NZ client self-assesses GST under the reverse charge mechanism (section 8(4B) of the GST Act), and the IC is not required to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: exports are zero-rated if the supplier exports the goods within 28 days of the time of supply and retains customs documentation proving physical removal from New Zealand. For services: executive search, headhunting, and talent advisory services are zero-rated under section 11(1) of the GST Act where the recipient is a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, and the services are not supplied in connection with land in New Zealand or goods situated in New Zealand at the time of supply. Where the IC supplies C-suite executive search and specialist tech headhunting services to a foreign corporate client (offshore retainer and success-fee model), the supply qualifies for 0% GST provided the client is not resident in New Zealand and is outside New Zealand when the service is performed; the IC must obtain and retain written evidence of the client's non-resident status, the export service contract, dated invoices, and bank receipts of inbound remittance in foreign currency.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Trade Single Window lodgement) — applicable if goods are exported as part of the supply
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from New Zealand within 28 days of supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from the New Zealand bank showing receipt of export proceeds from the overseas client
  • Fully executed executive search and headhunting services agreement identifying the non-resident client and the offshore nature of the engagement
  • Dated zero-rated tax invoice (GST 11) issued by the IC to the overseas client
  • Certificate or official documentary evidence of the client's non-resident tax status (e.g., foreign tax registration, certificate of incorporation, or board resolution confirming offshore location)
  • Bank statements / Wise B2B transaction records evidencing receipt of retainer wire payments and success fees from overseas

New Zealand has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls, and no digital stamping requirement. Inland Revenue does not require API-based clearance of tax invoices prior to issue. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and based on the Peppol framework; government agencies require Peppol-enabled e-invoicing for B2G transactions, but private B2B use is optional. GST returns (GST101) must be filed through myIR on a one-monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly filing cycle, and GST payments are due by the 28th day of the month following the taxable period. For zero-rated exports, the supplier must retain sufficient documentation to substantiate the zero-rating, but no pre-clearance or real-time validation is mandated.

Inland Revenue actively audits export zero-rating claims under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and the Tax Administration Act 1994. If a zero-rating claim is found to be unsupported by the required documentary evidence, or if the supply is ultimately determined to be taxable, the IC is liable for the shortfall in GST output tax at 15%, plus use-of-money interest calculated from the original due date. Shortfall penalties escalate by culpability: 20% for failing to take reasonable care, 40% for an unacceptable tax position, 100% for gross carelessness, and 150% for fraudulent intent or evasion. In addition, late payment penalties accrue at 1% per month (plus an initial 10% penalty on the 7th day after the due date and a further 10% on the 30th day). Fraudulent zero-rating or deliberate misrepresentation exposing export documentation is a criminal offence under sections 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994, punishable by fines of up to NZD 250,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years; directors of the IC may also be pursued personally for knowingly filing false returns. Where the IC has issued a GST refund claim based on exported services and the claim is clawed back, Inland Revenue may also impose a further statutory interest charge and, for repeated non-compliance, seek an order for tax pooling arrangements or compulsory deregistration of the GST number.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully convertible currency under a floating exchange rate regime. There are no exchange controls restricting current account or capital account transactions, and no prior regulatory approval is required for profit remittance, dividend distribution, or liquidation repatriation. Non-resident shareholders may freely repatriate lawful profits after satisfying the applicable Companies Act solvency and distribution tests, bank AML/KYC and beneficial ownership verification, and any tax clearance requirements. Practical hurdles are limited to documentary due diligence, source-of-funds evidence, and bank compliance checks rather than currency convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand does not impose any statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirements on export receipts or service revenues. Exporters and cross-border service providers may retain foreign currency proceeds indefinitely, with no legally mandated conversion ratio, amount, or timeline. Nevertheless, all cross-border payments remain subject to reporting, sanctions screening, AML/CFT customer due diligence, suspicious transaction reporting, and income tax disclosure obligations under New Zealand law.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), acting in coordination with the Financial Markets Authority and the Department of Internal Affairs under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High as a supplementary cross-border disbursement rail for outbound retainer wires and low-value B2B transfers, particularly Wise B2B; Low as a primary treasury account because offshore digital EMIs are not registered banks in New Zealand and cannot provide direct NZD clearing through the Exchange Settlement System. Legal constraints: an offshore EMI must operate only under a valid New Zealand financial service provider arrangement or an equivalent registered-bank exemption, must satisfy AML/CFT Act obligations, and must not hold client funds in a manner that constitutes unregistered bank custody. The CK_IC should maintain at least one fully regulated New Zealand bank account for capital escrow, FX settlement, and high-value transactions, using the EMI solely as a supplementary service-retainer payment channel.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Offshore tax-exempt International Company status without full economic-substance evidence

Defense evidence: Certificate of incorporation and continuance, international company constitution, certificate of good standing, official tax exemption ruling or tax residence certificate, statutory register of shareholders and directors, audited financial statements, and a written commercial-substance memorandum explaining the executive-search revenue model, client relationships, and reason for using the tax-exempt vehicle.
Non-resident directors and beneficial owners spread across multiple jurisdictions

Root cause: Non-resident directors and beneficial owners spread across multiple jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Notarially or consularly certified passports and proof of residential address for each director and beneficial owner, AML/CFT-compliant individual due diligence questionnaires, bank reference letters from regulated financial institutions in each source jurisdiction, signed declarations of lawful source of wealth, and detailed biographical information relevant to senior executive search and headhunting integrity.
Absence of local New Zealand physical presence or local economic substance

Root cause: Absence of local New Zealand physical presence or local economic substance

Defense evidence: Registered office arrangement with a licensed New Zealand service provider, local corporate services agreement showing professional administration, virtual office or co-working access agreement, entity governance documentation (resolutions, accounting records, meeting minutes), and evidence that treasury and client-relationship functions are professionally managed from or through the New Zealand jurisdiction.
Unclear source of funds for inbound capital injection and shareholder contributions

Root cause: Unclear source of funds for inbound capital injection and shareholder contributions

Defense evidence: Original bank statements of the funding shareholder for at least the preceding six to twelve months, shareholder resolution authorising the capital contribution, loan or subscription agreement, SWIFT payment order linking the remittance to the escrow account, sworn declaration of lawful source of funds, and evidence of tax compliance or tax clearance from the remitting jurisdiction.
High-volume retainer wires and cross-border disbursements through Wise B2B or similar non-bank payment channels

Root cause: High-volume retainer wires and cross-border disbursements through Wise B2B or similar non-bank payment channels

Defense evidence: Commercially signed executive-search retainer agreements, fee schedules, sample invoices, placed-candidate evidence and placement records, underlying client due diligence files, a treasury policy governing permitted payment rails, and transaction-level reconciliation reports linking each outbound payment to a specific retainer or operational obligation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital should be routed by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the shareholder's or investor's regulated bank directly to the CK_IC's New Zealand bank account. New Zealand does not require exchange-control approval, but the remittance narrative must clearly state the economic purpose, e.g., 'equity contribution' or 'subordinated loan for working capital', and must reference the relevant shareholder resolution or finance agreement. The receiving New Zealand bank will conduct source-of-funds verification, sanctions screening, and beneficial ownership review before crediting the capital escrow account. A complete due-diligence pack, including corporate authorisations, constitutional documents, tax status, and funding bank statements, should be forwarded to the relationship manager before the wire is initiated to avoid delay or compliance rejection.Outbound trade settlements for executive-search retainers, operating costs, and candidate onboarding fees should be executed as SWIFT MT103 through the New Zealand banking system. Domestic NZD payments clear through the Exchange Settlement System, while cross-border FX and international payments settle via SWIFT GPI and correspondent banking channels. Commercial letters of credit may be used only for ancillary procurement or trade transactions; service retainers are normally paid by direct MT103 wire transfer.
