Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Tāke) of New Zealand; online electronic portal: myIR at ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident company deriving New Zealand-sourced income or undertaking taxable activity in New Zealand must obtain a New Zealand IRD number. For a non-resident company not incorporated in New Zealand, the prescribed application is the Inland Revenue non-resident company registration process, typically via Inland Revenue form IR596 (Non-resident Company IRD Number Application). The application requires certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered office address, directors' names and contact details, ultimate beneficial ownership particulars, and, where a fixed place of business exists in New Zealand, details of the New Zealand agent or representative. Applications may be lodged electronically through myIR where the entity can establish an account or by paper submission to Inland Revenue, after which the IRD number is issued and used for income tax, GST, employee withholding, and other tax compliance obligations. For New Zealand-incorporated limited companies, the Companies Office automatically exchanges registration data with Inland Revenue, and the company receives an IRD number as part of incorporation without a separate application unless one is required for special domestic law purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A New Zealand limited company is subject to entity-level taxation at the corporate income tax rate of 28%. The principal statutory pass-through election is the Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, available to a New Zealand resident company with no more than five shareholders, all of whom must be individuals or qualifying trust estates; once elected, the company is treated as fiscally transparent, and profit/loss flows through to shareholders. New Zealand resident companies also operate an imputation credit account system that attaches imputation credits to dividends, but no discretionary check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists; New Zealand's hybrid and branch mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 operate automatically to deny deductions for tax outcomes arising from hybrid financial instruments and hybrid entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; a separate NZD 60,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of remote services supplied to New Zealand resident consumers during any 12-month period. Once the threshold is exceeded, compulsory GST registration is required; voluntary registration may be allowed where the business carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand or supplies remote services to New Zealand residents.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of goods for export is available under section 11(1)(a) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the goods are supplied by a GST-registered supplier and are exported by the supplier to an overseas destination within 28 days after the time of supply. Services supplied to a non-resident outside New Zealand are zero-rated under section 11A, provided the services are not supplied in connection with New Zealand land or moveable personal property situated in New Zealand, and the supplier holds written records sufficient to establish the recipient's non-resident status. Remote services supplied by a non-resident to a New Zealand GST-registered recipient are zero-rated in B2B transactions where the recipient provides their GST registration number; supplies of remote services to New Zealand resident consumers are subject to GST at 15% and do not qualify for export zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration in the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window (CUS export entry message) evidencing physical export of the goods from New Zealand
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the port or airport of loading in New Zealand and the overseas destination of the goods
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract evidencing the supply of the zinc alloy die-cast scale model automotive replicas to the overseas buyer
  • Packing list and proof of export within the 28-day statutory window
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 bank credit confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds from the overseas buyer for the exported goods

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate. GST-registered businesses must file GST returns electronically through the Inland Revenue myIR system on a two-monthly, six-monthly, or, for larger businesses, one-monthly filing basis. Tax invoices must comply with the content requirements of section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, including supplier name and GST number, date, supply description, amount, and GST charged. E-invoicing via the PEPPOL network is voluntary in New Zealand and is not a compliance clearance mechanism; the anti-fraud regime in New Zealand relies on GST return matching, invoice documentation audits, and Inland Revenue data analytics rather than electronic invoice authentication.

If the export zero-rating cannot be substantiated with the required customs and foreign exchange documentation, Inland Revenue may reassess the supply as taxable at 15% GST, claw back any input tax credits claimed, and impose shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Shortfall penalties range from 20% for failing to take reasonable care or taking an unacceptable tax position, up to 150% for evasion or a dominant purpose of tax evasion. Interest on unpaid tax accrues under the use-of-money interest rules. Additionally, incorrect export declarations may trigger prosecution by the New Zealand Customs Service under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, and the statutory time bar for reassessment is generally four years but is extended indefinitely where tax evasion or fraud is involved.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange-control approvals, no central bank licences for trade remittances, and no restrictions on repatriation of dividends, branch profits, or liquidation proceeds. Practical profit-remittance hurdles are purely fiscal: (i) non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at 15% on fully imputed dividends, generally reduced to 5%–15% under New Zealand's double tax agreements, e.g., the NZ–China DTA provides 5% where the recipient holds at least 10% of the voting power, otherwise 15%; (ii) NRWT of 15% on interest and royalties, subject to treaty relief and the approved issuer levy (2%) alternative on interest; (iii) thin capitalisation limitation on interest deductibility for non-resident-owned groups, with a 60% debt-to-asset safe harbour for income years beginning on or after 1 July 2023; and (iv) the requirement that the New Zealand entity hold sufficient imputation credits for dividends to be described as fully imputed. There is no withholding tax or capital gains tax on the disposal of shares in a New Zealand trading company by a non-resident seller unless the shares are land-rich or the disposal falls within the five-year bright-line/land-related property rules. A company incorporated in New Zealand is tax-resident under section YD 2 of the Income Tax Act 2007 even if managed offshore, which preserves treaty eligibility and demonstrates OECD BEPS substance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. New Zealand repealed all foreign-exchange surrender requirements in the 1980s. An exporter of die-cast scale automotive models is under no statutory obligation to repatriate, convert, or surrender export proceeds within any timeline. The exporter may retain NZD or any foreign currency in a registered-bank foreign-currency account or an offshore account. Compliance obligations are limited to: (i) filing complete export entries through New Zealand Customs Service using the Trade Single Window for each shipment for statistical, tariff, and biosecurity purposes; (ii) applying a GST zero rate to export invoices under section 11(1) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 with evidence of exportation within the statutory timeframe; (iii) disclosing proceeds as revenue in financial statements and income tax returns; and (iv) providing the receiving bank with a lawful purpose description on inbound wires, typically SWIFT purpose code 'TCQ' or 'Trade settlement', for AML/CFT record-keeping under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. Transfer pricing rules in Part 4 of the Income Tax Act 2007 require arm's length pricing and currency terms for export sales, and the New Zealand resident company must demonstrate real economic substance for OECD/multinational group credibility.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand – Te Pūtea Matua (RBNZ). The RBNZ is the central bank and prudential regulator of registered banks under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. It is also co-supervisor for AML/CFT purposes together with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. For foreign exchange, New Zealand operates an open, unregulated market: the RBNZ does not issue exchange-control licences, imposes no surrender obligations, and administers no capital-account approval regime. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible under IMF Article VIII. The only substantive gatekeeper for foreign direct investment is the Overseas Investment Act 2005 (OIA) administered by the Overseas Investment Office (Land Information New Zealand), which applies to sensitive land and significant business assets above statutory thresholds.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium-to-Low as a primary treasury rail. New Zealand has no passporting regime for non-resident electronic money institutions: an offshore EMI, such as a UK or EU EMI, cannot hold itself out as a New Zealand registered bank and cannot provide core transactional banking services in New Zealand. The use of offshore fintech platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business, and HSBC Global Wallet is legally permissible for ancillary collections, multi-currency wallets, and small-value payables, but viability is qualified: (i) New Zealand registered banks apply cautious de-risking to pay-through arrangements and often require the EMI to maintain its own segregated account with a local registered bank; (ii) PayPal inbound settlement into a New Zealand bank is permitted and common for low-risk e-commerce collectibles, provided PayPal is identified as the originator with a 'Trade' or 'Retail goods' purpose code; (iii) PayPal merchant receipts are treated as ordinary trade revenue but lack the documentary robustness of SWIFT MT103 wires, so monthly reconciliation and invoice traceability are essential for AML/CFT comfort; and (iv) the entity should maintain its primary operating account, borrowing facilities, letter-of-credit lines, and ISDA hedging relationship with a prudentially regulated New Zealand bank. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital into NZ_LTD must be routed by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank positioning) from the investing shareholder's bank to the entity's New Zealand registered account. The wire instruction should carry: (i) SWIFT Category Purpose Code 'CT' (Capital Transfer) or 'TC' (Trade) with free-text description 'Equity subscription / shareholder loan to NZ_LTD – ISIC 3240 die-cast collectibles manufacturing'; (ii) the New York or Sydney correspondent settlement account of the receiving New Zealand bank with the correct BIC for the New Zealand branch; (iii) the beneficiary account in the name of NZ_LTD with the IRD taxpayer number on file; (iv) pre-advice to the relationship manager of the supporting board resolution, share subscription agreement, shareholder loan agreement, and proposed use of funds so the bank can classify the receipt as capital rather than assessable revenue; and (v) a prior assessment under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 where the investor is acquiring 25% or more of a New Zealand business and the 'significant business assets' threshold (NZ$100 million for a non-consortium investor, NZ$200 million for a consortium investor, in each case excluding land) is exceeded. New Zealand imposes no exchange-control reporting on the receiving bank for capital inflows; however, NZ_LTD must record the capital in a dedicated capital escrow category in its books, maintain the Companies Office share register update, comply with thin capitalisation rules on any shareholder debt, and disclose related-party cross-border balances in the annual International Dealings Schedule if the shareholder loan is from a non-resident related party.For supplier payments covering zinc alloy, moulds, tampo inks, tooling, and sub-assembly services, predominantly to Guangdong and Hong Kong vendors, the primary clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 high-value wire transfers for supplier prepayments and invoice settlement, using purpose code 'TC' and the description 'Trade settlement – raw materials for die-cast scale models'; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit opened as revolving irrevocable documentary credits (MT700) for large, repetitive orders, with negotiation against full set clean-on-board bills of lading, signed commercial invoice, and packing list; (iii) bank trade finance suites such as import lines and shipping guarantees where the New Zealand bank funds the documentary payment and releases the goods in transit; (iv) domestic and regional telegraphic transfers in USD or CNY via the foreign-currency account for smaller vendors; and (v) PayPal for direct-to-consumer sales of 1:18 collector replicas, with USD balances swept daily to the NZD operating account. For European and North American wholesale buyers, inbound settlement should arrive by SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'TCQ' and references to the commercial invoice, the buyer's customs import entry, and the Incoterms; larger buyers may pay by letter of credit or documentary collection in accordance with ICC UCP 600 and ISBP 745. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-to-Low as a primary treasury rail. New Zealand has no passporting regime for non-resident electronic money institutions: an offshore EMI, such as a UK or EU EMI, cannot hold itself out as a New Zealand registered bank and cannot provide core transactional banking services in New Zealand. The use of offshore fintech platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business, and HSBC Global Wallet is legally permissible for ancillary collections, multi-currency wallets, and small-value payables, but viability is qualified: (i) New Zealand registered banks apply cautious de-risking to pay-through arrangements and often require the EMI to maintain its own segregated account with a local registered bank; (ii) PayPal inbound settlement into a New Zealand bank is permitted and common for low-risk e-commerce collectibles, provided PayPal is identified as the originator with a 'Trade' or 'Retail goods' purpose code; (iii) PayPal merchant receipts are treated as ordinary trade revenue but lack the documentary robustness of SWIFT MT103 wires, so monthly reconciliation and invoice traceability are essential for AML/CFT comfort; and (iv) the entity should maintain its primary operating account, borrowing facilities, letter-of-credit lines, and ISDA hedging relationship with a prudentially regulated New Zealand bank. Feasibility rating: auxiliary cross-border rail – Medium; sole treasury solution – Low.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File/Master File requirement; Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting applies to NZ resident multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of NZ$130 million or more. Transfer pricing documentation is expected for cross-border related-party transactions exceeding NZ$1 million in aggregate (low-risk threshold) and must be prepared contemporaneously for any material transaction.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are enacted in New Zealand. However, the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1, Income Tax Act 2007) and related judicial doctrines require genuine economic substance and commercial purpose. A company without real business activity may be treated as a shell, and arrangements designed to avoid tax can be voided by the Commissioner. Actual die-casting manufacturing and tampo printing in NZ constitute real economic substance; no additional ESR filing is required.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty up to 150% of the tax shortfall, plus use-of-money interest; criminal prosecution may result in fines up to NZ$250,000 for a company (and NZ$50,000 for individuals) and/or imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or knowingly assist in evasion face personal criminal prosecution, civil recovery of unpaid tax and penalties, and disqualification. Inland Revenue can seek orders restraining departure from New Zealand (travel bans) and asset freezing orders.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may apply s BG 1 to disregard the arrangement, deny treaty benefits, assess full New Zealand tax at 28% on all income, and impose a shortfall penalty of 40% to 150% depending on carelessness or fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable as shadow principals for the company's tax liabilities under the Companies Act 1993 (s 376) if they allowed the company to trade with no substance; civil penalties and costs orders may attach.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may re-determine the taxable income under the arm's length principle, raising an assessment with shortfall penalties: 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, and 150% for fraud/evasion; plus UOMI.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign defective transfer pricing documentation or approve redirected profits face personal liability for the penalty amount if corporate veil-piercing applies; criminal liability may arise for evasion.
AML/CFT and Financial Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Breaches of the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 can attract civil penalties up to NZ$2 million per contravention for a body corporate; criminal penalty for a financing of terrorism offence is up to NZ$2 million and 7 years imprisonment. Additionally, directors face disqualification under the Insolvency Act 2006.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for civil penalty orders and may be banned from managing companies. Travel bans can be imposed while criminal investigations are pending; individuals involved in laundering proceeds of crime are liable to imprisonment up to 7 years.
