Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under section 200 of the Companies Act 1993, a New Zealand company must maintain a registered office in New Zealand that is a physical address at which legal documents can be served; it cannot be a P.O. Box alone. A virtual office supplying a physical street address and mail-forwarding may satisfy the minimum Companies Office standard, but for this medical-imaging export business, the Inland Revenue Department and New Zealand banks will expect substantive local presence, including a local director, accessible statutory records, a dedicated business telephone and data trail, and evidence of commercial operations. Pure commercial mailboxes are generally rejected by AML/CFT compliance officers and banks because they undermine customer due diligence and transaction monitoring. Accordingly, a virtual office is acceptable only as a secondary service address and not as the principal registered office, principal operational site, or bank account business address.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department – New Zealand (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake; official portal: ird.govt.nz; secure portal: myIR at myir.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned NZ limited company must first be incorporated with the New Zealand Companies Office. After incorporation, apply to the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) for an IRD number via the non-resident company IRD number application process, submitting proof of incorporation, constitutional documents, director and ultimate beneficial owner details, NZ registered office address, and any certified English-language translations. Once the IRD number is issued, activate GST registration in myIR if annual taxable supplies are or are likely to exceed NZD 60,000, or voluntarily if the company makes only zero-rated exports. New Zealand does not issue a separate VAT identification number; the GST number is the IRD number with GST registration status and must be quoted on tax invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

An NZ limited company is taxed as a separate entity at the 28% company rate. Closely held companies meeting NZ resident close-company ownership tests may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) treatment, causing current tax items to flow through to shareholders. No US-style check-the-box election exists in New Zealand. Hybrid mismatch and branch mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act 2007 operate mandatorily and restrict deduction/non-inclusion mismatches on hybrid instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of annual taxable supplies made in New Zealand, including remote/digital services supplied to NZ consumers; voluntary GST registration is permitted for exporters and other persons carrying on a taxable activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported by the supplier from New Zealand qualify for GST zero-rating only if the supplier physically exports the goods within 28 days of the time of supply and the goods are not subsequently used or consumed in New Zealand. Services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply may be zero-rated where the service is performed directly for the non-resident and is not supplied to a person in New Zealand or in connection with goods in New Zealand. A Color Doppler Ultrasound Console manufactured in New Zealand and sold to an offshore buyer qualifies for zero-rating when the goods are actually exported under NZ Customs procedures and all export documentation is retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged with New Zealand Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank evidence of export proceeds repatriation
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin where required by the destination jurisdiction

New Zealand does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or transaction-level API filing for GST. E-invoicing via the PEPPOL network is voluntary. Tax invoices, whether paper or electronic, must contain the supplier's GST number, invoice date, supplier and recipient details, description, amount, and GST charged or a zero-rated notation. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR on a periodic (usually monthly or two-monthly) basis.

Inland Revenue audits export zero-rating claims strictly. If goods are not exported within 28 days, are diverted to New Zealand use, or the export evidence is incomplete, the supply becomes taxable at 15% GST. Any previously refunded GST or zero-rating benefit is clawed back via amended assessments, with use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 ranging from 20% for reasonable-care and unacceptable-position failures to 100% for abusive tax positions and 150% for evasion. Directors can also incur civil liability, and knowingly false zero-rating documentation may support criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully and freely convertible across both the current account and the capital account. New Zealand maintains no exchange-control legislation, no capital transfer licensing regime, and no administrative filters or approvals for profit remittance, dividend distributions, loan repayments, or repatriation of subscribed capital. The only financial leakage attached to outbound distributions is the Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) imposed under the Income Tax Act 2007 — at the treaty-reduced rate of 15% for fully imputed dividends (5% for AU/NZ treaty investors with a 10% or greater shareholding) — which is a fiscal charge rather than a convertibility restriction. The RBNZ operates an independent free-floating exchange-rate policy, intervening only in exceptional disorderly-market conditions, and the NZD is a G20 deliverable currency settled via continuous linked settlement (CLS). Accordingly, the colour Doppler ultrasound console exporter faces zero statutory convertibility hurdles and may invoice, hold, and reallocate foreign currency without RBNZ notification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil statutory surrender or repatriation obligation. All exchange controls, including the obligation to surrender export proceeds, were repealed in 1984. There is no statutory timeline within which the NZ_LTD exporting entity must return export receivables to New Zealand, and no mandatory conversion ratio of foreign currency earnings into NZD is prescribed. The medical device exporter may retain export proceeds indefinitely in an authorised Foreign Currency Account (FCA), may apply them directly to offshore supplier settlements, or may convert them to NZD at will. The only applicable accounting and tax standard is the requirement to recognise export revenue in NZD equivalent for financial reporting under NZ IFRS and to account for any realised or unrealised FX gains/losses for income tax purposes — neither of which imposes a treasury timing obligation.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for ancillary collection/disbursement rails, but Low for primary trade-finance, LC, and statutory treasury infrastructure. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may lawfully hold NZD balances and issue NZD domestic account details, provided they operate through an authorised New Zealand reporting entity registration under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (supervised by the Department of Internal Affairs). However, such EMIs are not registered banks under the Deposit Takers Act 2022; they do not accept deposits in the legal sense, cannot offer documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700), do not participate in the RBNZ Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS), and are not CLS members for NZD settlement. Given the ISIC 3250 export model requiring Swift LC and TT/Wire rails, the use of a full-service commercial bank is both legally and operationally mandatory; EMIs are strictly complementary tools for low-value supplier disbursements and expense payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COV where upstream aggregation is required) to the NZ bank account of the NZ_LTD, using the bank's unique NZD account IBAN/account number and the wiring narrative structured as: '/INV/ SHARE SUBSCRIPTION — NZD [amount] — Equity contribution' or 'CAPITAL INJECTION — TRADE INVESTMENT'. The remitting account should be the ultimate foreign shareholder's corporate account to preserve source clarity. The injection is matched in the internal reconciliation system against a board special resolution passed per section 51 of the Companies Act 1993, an updated shareholder ledger, and the Companies Office capital filing. For the manufacture of medical devices, the transaction is classified under BOP/BPM6 code 'E1 — Direct Investment Equity Capital', and the Economic Purpose code for bank reporting is '5051 — Direct Investment'. If the capital exceeds NZD 100 million or involves 'sensitive land' or 'significant business assets' within the meaning of the Overseas Investment Act 2005 (unlikely for a standard registration-manufacturer operation), OIO consent is required; for the described entity, no OIO consent is needed. The funds must settle into the Capital Escrow subclass account and then transfer once told to the Operational Account by verified instruction.Outbound supplier payments for components and inbound raw materials are cleared through two rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account settlements, purchase order advances, and recurring supplier invoices, using standard purpose codes 'Trade in Goods — General Merchandise' and referencing the invoice number and sales agreement; and (ii) Irrevocable Confirmed Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700 / MT710 with MT720 amendments) for high-value or first-transaction imports of ultrasound transducer components and display modules, providing documentary guarantee to the seller. New Zealand-dollar local interbank liquidity is settled through the RBNZ-operated Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) on a real-time gross settlement (RTGS) basis, with the clearing house ensuring finality at the RBNZ. Where USD/EUR disbursements are made directly from the company's Foreign Currency Account, clearing is performed via the bank's offshore correspondent RTGS (e.g., BNZ through National Australia Bank, ANZ through ANZ Group's New York branch), using the applicable local clearing system (CHIPS/Fedwire) to the beneficiary. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High for ancillary collection/disbursement rails, but Low for primary trade-finance, LC, and statutory treasury infrastructure. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may lawfully hold NZD balances and issue NZD domestic account details, provided they operate through an authorised New Zealand reporting entity registration under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (supervised by the Department of Internal Affairs). However, such EMIs are not registered banks under the Deposit Takers Act 2022; they do not accept deposits in the legal sense, cannot offer documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700), do not participate in the RBNZ Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS), and are not CLS members for NZD settlement. Given the ISIC 3250 export model requiring Swift LC and TT/Wire rails, the use of a full-service commercial bank is both legally and operationally mandatory; EMIs are strictly complementary tools for low-value supplier disbursements and expense payments.
