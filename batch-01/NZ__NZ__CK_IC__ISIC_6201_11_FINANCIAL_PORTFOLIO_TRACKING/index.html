Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue – Te Tari Taake (Inland Revenue Department, New Zealand Government); online portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir)

E-government filing portal

Foreign-incorporated entities that derive New Zealand-source income or make taxable supplies in New Zealand must first obtain an IRD number by filing the Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application (form IR596) with Inland Revenue, attaching the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, register of directors and shareholders, and evidence of a New Zealand address for service. Once the IRD number is issued, GST registration is completed through the entity's myIR account (GST Act 1985 s 51). If the foreign entity carries on business in New Zealand through a place of business, it must also register as an overseas company on the New Zealand Companies Office register (Companies Act 1993 s 335) before commencing business; the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) is then issued automatically and can be used to update tax registrations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not maintain a check-the-box election regime. The only elective classification mechanisms are the look-through company (LTC) election for qualifying close companies (Income Tax Act 2007 s HA 11) and the portfolio investment entity (PIE) election. Hybrid mismatch rules (ITA 2007 ss FH 1–FH 16) apply automatically and cannot be elected out of. GST law allows a joint election for GST grouping (GST Act 1985 s 55) and an election to apply the zero-rating rules to land supplies. A foreign international company automatically becomes New Zealand-resident for income tax if it is incorporated in New Zealand or if its centre of management is in New Zealand (ITA 2007 s YD 2); no election can override that statutory residency test. The entity's home-jurisdiction tax-exempt status is a foreign-law attribute; it is not recognised as a New Zealand tax exemption and does not prevent territorial taxation of New Zealand-source income.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. New Zealand has accepted Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement and maintains no exchange-control permissions, quantitative limits, or repatriation restrictions on dividends, interest, royalties, or liquidation proceeds. The only fiscal frictions on cross-border profit remittances are the non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) regime (30% statutory headline rate, with treaty-reduced rates ranging from 0% to 15% depending on the recipient's jurisdiction and shareholding) and, for qualifying interest, the Approved Issuer Levy at 2% as an alternative to NRWT. No OIO approval is triggered for a non-resident capital injection into a non-sensitive ISIC 6201 software/FinTech business unless the transaction crosses the statutory 'significant business assets' threshold or involves 'sensitive land'. Inbound equity for a portfolio-tracking application is therefore ordinarily unambiguously permissible.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no time-limited repatriation deadline for export receipts. An entity may retain subscription, licence, and services consideration in the original foreign currency indefinitely in a New Zealand multi-currency account. The only reporting obligations are routine cross-border transaction statistics collection by the Reserve Bank and standard AML/CFT record-keeping requirements.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the central bank and prudential supervisor of registered banks; the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) within Land Information New Zealand (Toitū Te Whenua) administers foreign investment screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Low as a principal treasury venue; Moderate only as an auxiliary inbound collection channel. New Zealand has no standalone e-money institution (EMI) licence. An offshore digital EMI cannot operate as a New Zealand settlement bank or hold client funds without registration on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers Act 2008 and compliance with the AML/CFT Act 2009. Practical constraints include inability to open a New Zealand dollar account with a New Zealand Bank Identification Number (BIC/NZ settlement account), lack of direct participation in the Payments New Zealand clearing system, and limited suitability for capital escrow. Global EMIs such as Wise or Payoneer may be used for card-acquirer flows (including Stripe payouts and Apple IAP proceeds), but the operating company should maintain a tier-1 New Zealand bank account as the sole aggregation and disbursement node.
