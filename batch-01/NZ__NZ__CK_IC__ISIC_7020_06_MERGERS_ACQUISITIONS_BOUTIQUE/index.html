Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake); digital portal: myIR Secure Online Services (myir.ird.govt.nz); business gateway via NZ Post.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident International Company (CK_IC) must apply for an Inland Revenue Department (IRD) number using Form IR742 (Application for an IRD Number for a Non-Resident Company), which can be filed physically or through an NZ tax agent. For Goods and Services Tax (GST) purposes, the company must submit Form GST1 via myIR to register for GST if it carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand and exceeds the NZD 60,000 annual threshold, or if it is a non-resident supplier of remote services or low-value goods to New Zealand consumers exceeding that threshold. The statutory tax identification number is the 8-to-9 digit IRD number, generally formatted XX-XXX-XXX.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A non-resident International Company (CK_IC) must apply for an Inland Revenue Department (IRD) number using Form IR742 (Application for an IRD Number for a Non-Resident Company), which can be filed physically or through an NZ tax agent. For Goods and Services Tax (GST) purposes, the company must submit Form GST1 via myIR to register for GST if it carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand and exceeds the NZD 60,000 annual threshold, or if it is a non-resident supplier of remote services or low-value goods to New Zealand consumers exceeding that threshold. The statutory tax identification number is the 8-to-9 digit IRD number, generally formatted XX-XXX-XXX.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a United States-style check-the-box election. Relevant statutory elections and alignment mechanisms include: (1) election under the hybrid and branch mismatch rules in Part FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 to neutralise double non-taxation outcomes; (2) election under section FB 27 for offshore group funding arrangements; (3) election into the active business exemption under the CFC rules for a non-resident company's controlled foreign company interests; (4) voluntary GST registration election under section 51 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for non-residents carrying on a taxable activity, even below the mandatory threshold; and (5) group registration election under section 55 of the GST Act 1985 allowing two or more companies to file consolidated GST returns.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (gross taxable supplies in any 12-month period); non-resident suppliers of cross-border remote services or low-value goods to New Zealand consumer recipients are also required to register for GST once the NZD 60,000 annual threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating applies to exports of goods where the goods are supplied in the course of export and are exported by the supplier, and to services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not supplied in connection with land in New Zealand or physically performed in New Zealand. For the M&A advisory boutique, cross-border advisory services supplied to a non-resident client outside New Zealand for the benefit of that client outside New Zealand may be zero-rated under sections 11 and 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. The supplier must maintain contemporaneous evidence to substantiate the non-resident status of the recipient and the offshore situs of the services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (submitted via the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window / EXF declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency consideration
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sale/purchase agreement
  • GST return worksheet (GST101) identifying zero-rated export supplies
  • Documentary evidence of the non-resident status of the overseas recipient (e.g., third-party tax register extract or statutory declaration)

New Zealand does not mandate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime for e-invoicing. E-invoicing is permitted and may be adopted voluntarily, including through the PEPPOL network using the Peppol BIS 3.0 standard. GST returns are filed electronically via myIR, and invoices may be issued electronically without government pre-approval. There is no mandatory API-based clearance or invoice stamping requirement.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to intensive Inland Revenue audit scrutiny. If a zero-rating claim is fraudulent or fails statutory substantiation requirements, the supply may be recharacterised as taxable at the standard 15% GST rate, giving rise to a shortfall penalty under section 141 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 of up to 150% of the tax shortfall, plus use-of-money interest from the due date. Where fraud or evasion is proven, criminal prosecution may be pursued under the Tax Administration Act 1994 or the Crimes Act 1961, with potential imprisonment and confiscation of proceeds. The 'unjust enrichment' principle may also be invoked by the Commissioner to deny refunds where the entity has not borne the GST or has passed on the cost.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility and is an IMF Article VIII member. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) floats freely, and no exchange-control approvals, licences or balance-of-payments restrictions apply to resident or non-resident entities. Capital injection and profit remittance are freely executable through authorised banks. Profit remittance by way of dividends, interest, royalties and service payments is subject only to ordinary tax withholding rules (non-resident withholding tax) and, where applicable, reduced rates under a New Zealand double tax agreement or the approval of a tax-residency certificate. There are no hurdles associated with currency convertibility, no surrender requirements, and no requirement to re-import trade proceeds into New Zealand.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters, international companies and advisory boutiques may hold export receipts and other foreign-currency revenues in offshore or domestic foreign-currency accounts indefinitely, may convert them at any time at freely floating market rates, and may use them directly for cross-border disbursements without central bank approval or a statutory timeline.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) — Te Pūtea Matua, as the prudential supervisor of banks and the operator of core wholesale payment and settlement infrastructure, together with the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) for foreign investment screening in sensitive New Zealand assets. New Zealand does not operate a dedicated exchange-control agency; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) supervise AML/CFT compliance under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium — feasible as a settlement auxiliary for success-fee wires and low-value cross-border disbursements, but not as a substitute for a New Zealand tier-1 banking relationship. Legal constraints under New Zealand law: an offshore digital EMI must hold a New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) licence or otherwise rely on an appropriately registered financial service provider, must implement a compliant AML/CFT programme under the AML/CFT Act 2009, and cannot use the title or business model of a bank without registration as a bank under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021. An offshore EMI may be used for FX conversion and low-value routing if the International Company maintains a master operating account with a registered New Zealand bank and the EMI is approved by that bank as an ancillary settlement platform.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be routed via SWIFT MT103 for customer transfers or MT202 COV for cover-through-correspondent payments. Payment narratives must identify the economic purpose precisely, such as share capital contribution, shareholder loan, working-capital support, or acquisition bridge. New Zealand has no statutory central-bank purpose code, but banks require sufficient purpose description for AML/CFT monitoring and internal balance-of-payments classification. Verification should include a board resolution authorising the inbound transfer, a share subscription or loan agreement, passport copies of senders and ultimate beneficial owners, source-of-funds evidence, and applicable FATCA/CRS self-certification forms. If the inbound transfer is in respect of an overseas investment in sensitive New Zealand assets, an approved OIO consent must be presented before settlement.Outbound trade and M&A-success-fee disbursements settle through SWIFT MT103 customer-to-bank credit transfers, MT202 interbank payment orders, and documentary instruments such as commercial letters of credit under UCP600, standby letters of credit and bank guarantees. Domestic high-value NZD legs are settled through the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) in real-time. Escrow releases should be executed as pre-agreed SWIFT instructions supported by signed release certificates, completion memoranda and invoice-based fee schedules. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Non-resident dividend withholding tax (NRWT) of 30% on gross dividends; 15% on fully imputed dividends according to the imputation ratio. For NZ residents, resident withholding tax (RWT) on dividends is 33% (or 28% if the shareholder company is wholly owned and elects 0%? Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory de minimis threshold is prescribed in the Income Tax Act 2007; however, the Inland Revenue expects contemporaneous documentation where connected-party cross-border payments exceed NZD 1 million annually, with a stricter approach for management, IP, and financing services where any materiality requires documentation. For CbC reporting purposes, an NZ-resident group member is required to file CbC report when the ultimate parent group consolidated revenue is at least NZD 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section BG1 and section GA 1, supplemented by the controlled foreign company (CFC) attribution rules, the financial arrangement rules, and the Dealing with Associated Persons rules. An entity with no genuine business, staff, or economic activity in New Zealand but claiming tax benefits will be reconstructed under GAAR. For International Company structures registered in a zero-tax zone (e.g., a Cook Islands International Company), the Economic Substance Regulations of that jurisdiction would apply if the company is not resident in New Zealand; for a New Zealand-resident company, economic substance is tested through the business continuity and dividend imputation rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994; conviction punishable by a fine up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the evaded tax plus use-of-money interest at the statutory rate.

Director personal liability: A director who acts with intent to evade tax is jointly and severally liable for the shortfall penalties and may be convicted under the Tax Administration Act; civil enforcement may include restitution orders, asset bans, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may apply GAAR to nullify tax avoidance arrangements and reconstruct the transaction at arm's length, resulting in additional tax, shortfall penalties of up to 100%, and use-of-money interest. A shell company may be assessed directly as a 'tax avoidance arrangement' under sections BG1 and GA 1.

Director personal liability: Directors who are 'arrangers' to the tax avoidance arrangement may be personally liable under section 41 of the Tax Administration Act for penalty up to NZD 100,000; if the shell is operated to evade tax, individuals can be convicted and ordered to make civil restitution.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: IRD may adjust gross income and taxable income to an arm's length value under section GC 7 and impose transfer pricing penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall for deliberate evasion, or 20% for failure to keep sufficient documentation under section 141EB, plus interest.

Director personal liability: A director who signs a deficient transfer pricing document or who intentionally misprices cross-border payments may be treated as an accessory to the tax shortfall and be personally assessable; in egregious cases, criminal penalties and travel bans apply.
AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Breaches of the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 attract criminal sanctions, including fines up to NZD 2 million for an individual and up to NZD 10 million for a body corporate, and imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering; civil penalties may also be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who knew or were recklessly indifferent can be convicted personally; the court may order that the director make good the AML fine, and the Serious Fraud Office may obtain travel bans and asset protection orders.
