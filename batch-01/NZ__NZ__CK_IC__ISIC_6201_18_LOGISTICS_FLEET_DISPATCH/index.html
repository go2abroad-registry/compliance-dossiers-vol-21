Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally permissible for a low-risk logistics tech SaaS company in New Zealand, provided that the company maintains a physical registered office in New Zealand, which is required under section 189 of the Companies Act 1993. The registered office must be a physical location, not a P.O. Box, and the company must have a registered agent who is resident in New Zealand. For the purposes of the Companies Office, the registered office may be the office of the company's professional services firm, but the use of a virtual office for day-to-day operations is acceptable, provided that the company maintains full records and adequate commercial substance. However, the Inland Revenue Department ('IR') and New Zealand banks may reject a purely virtual address for tax registration and bank account opening if the company cannot demonstrate substantive operations such as having employees in New Zealand, local revenue generation, or a physical presence for audit and legal service of process. For this logistics fleet dispatch SaaS business, the IR will likely require that the company has a local New Zealand director, maintains a compliant employee payroll system if it hires staff, and can justify its transfer pricing policies under section GC 3 of the Income Tax Act 2007. Virtual offices are permissible for the initial 90-day pre-incorporation period but must be upgraded to a physical operational office before the first financial statement is filed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - Under the Income Tax Act 2007 (ss YA 2 and YD 2), a company is tax resident in NZ if incorporated in NZ, has its head office in NZ, or has its centre of management in NZ. The CK_IC International Company fails all three residence tests and is therefore a non-resident company. New Zealand applies a territorial (source-based) restriction of income tax to non-residents: only NZ-source income (e.g., interest, royalties, land-related services and New Zealand employment income) is taxed, whereas all foreign-source business profits - including logistics fleet dispatch software subscriptions, telematics SaaS licences and route-optimisation fees charged to offshore fleet operators - are exempt from New Zealand income tax. The entity is also outside the New Zealand capital gains tax system (there is no general CGT) and is not subject to provisional tax or the NZ interest limitation rules of 2021-2024 given the absence of NZ taxable nexus and borrowing.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not maintain a US-style check-the-box regime, but provides the following statutory elections: (1) Look-through Company (LTC) election under the Income Tax Act 2007 for closely held NZ incorporated companies (not applicable to a non-resident Cook Islands IC); (2) GST grouping election under Part 7 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 permitting registration of a single GST group, and statutory apportionment and attribution elections for mixed-use activities; (3) reverse-charge election for imported remote services under the GST Act 1985 which allows a GST-registered NZ recipient to return GST at 15% on offshore B2B supplies instead of requiring the offshore supplier to register; (4) CFC/FIF branch-equivalent, attributable-income and de minimis elections for New Zealand resident shareholders of controlled foreign enterprises; (5) transitional residency election of up to 48 months for newly arrived individuals; and (6) hybrid mismatch elections - including elective branch combination, timing and deductible/non-deductible alignment elections implementing OECD BEPS Action 2 (ss EY 1 to EY 16 of the Income Tax Act 2007) - which are available by way of contemporaneous documentation and IRD disclosure.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account under the IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no general exchange control restrictions, no central bank approval thresholds, and no requirement for prior authorization on inbound or outbound capital movements. Profit remittance of dividends, interest, royalties, and branch profits is freely permitted, subject only to standard tax compliance: non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at 15% (or reduced treaty rate under NZ's double tax agreements, e.g. 5% for significant shareholding dividends), resident withholding tax (RWT) on interest, and the requirement that the entity be fully tax-resident and compliant with the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) for Foreign Investment Fund (FIF) and thin capitalization rules. No capital account conversion lock-up period applies; however, banking counterparties may impose internal economic substance and source-of-funds diligence before processing large cross-border flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines under New Zealand law. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained in foreign currency bank accounts indefinitely. For entity and tax compliance, all foreign currency gains and losses must be translated to NZD for financial reporting using an acceptable accounting standard (NZ IFRS or NZ GAAP) and reported annually to the IRD. Banks may apply internal due diligence for receipts from sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions in accordance with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act).

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) together with the New Zealand Treasury and the Financial Markets Authority for prudential and market conduct oversight; no separate Foreign Exchange Administration exists in New Zealand.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-to-Low for primary treasury custody. Offshore digital EMI and payment service providers (e.g. Wise Platform, Airwallex, or global neobanks) are not registered banks in New Zealand and cannot provide capital escrow or statutory clearing privileges; they operate under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 (FSP Act) and typically require registration on the FSP register with an approved dispute resolution scheme. Such EMIs are viable solely as auxiliary high-speed corridors for low-value SaaS subscription collections and supplier disbursements in USD/EUR, provided the EMI holds an NZ FSP license, has a New Zealand resident agent for service of process, and is not the sole repository of corporate funds. The primary operating and escrow accounts must reside with an NZ-incorporated registered bank for audit and IRD transparency.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-to-Low for primary treasury custody. Offshore digital EMI and payment service providers (e.g. Wise Platform, Airwallex, or global neobanks) are not registered banks in New Zealand and cannot provide capital escrow or statutory clearing privileges; they operate under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 (FSP Act) and typically require registration on the FSP register with an approved dispute resolution scheme. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% corporate income tax rate under the Income Tax Act 2007 for New Zealand-resident companies; non-resident companies derive New Zealand-source income are taxed at the same 28% rate on net taxable income attributable to a New Zealand fixed establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) imposed on dividends paid by a New Zealand company to non-resident shareholders under the Income Tax Act 2007, Subpart RF.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is expected for related-party cross-border transactions exceeding NZD 1 million annually. A mandatory Local File and Master File will be required where the New Zealand operating group has revenue over approximately NZD 50 million, and country-by-country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue above approximately NZD 1.1 billion.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. However, the general anti-avoidance rule under section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, the transfer pricing reconstruction rule under section GB 2, and the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development Permanent Establishment and BEPS principles require genuine economic substance, operational substance, and business purpose. A shell International Company without employees, contracts, back-office control, or risk-bearing capacity may be recharacterised as tax-resident in New Zealand or denied treaty benefits if the effective management and control are in New Zealand.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Intentional Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under sections 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994. Maximum penalties for a corporation are fines up to NZD 250,000 plus the full core tax owing; responsible individuals may be imprisoned up to 5 years. The Commissioner will also impose a 100% shortfall penalty for evasion with intent, plus use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or authorise tax evasion are personally liable as principals or accessories. Courts may order civil restitution, director disqualification, asset preservation orders, and travel bans or bail conditions restricting movement during prosecution.
