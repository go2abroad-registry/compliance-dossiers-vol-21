Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand (Te Tari Taake) — national tax administration and central filing portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir). Border and export entry control is administered by the New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS / Ngā Ratonga Tiaki O Taonga) through the Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Corporate/business registration: if the CK_IC (International Company) carries on business in New Zealand, it must first be registered as an overseas company on the New Zealand Companies Register under the Companies Act 1993 and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). Step 2 — IRD number: a non-resident foreign company applies to Inland Revenue using form IR742 ('IRD number application – non-resident company'), attaching certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents or trust deed (where trust-owned), directors' and shareholders' particulars, and proof of a registered office; Inland Revenue issues an 8–9 digit IRD number. If the entity is incorporated in New Zealand, incorporation through the Companies Office automatically produces the IRD number. Step 3 — GST registration: register for New Zealand GST via myIR (online application, with the IRD number serving as the GST registration number) either mandatorily when taxable supplies in or to New Zealand exceed NZD 60,000 in the preceding 12 months, or voluntarily in order to claim input tax credits and zero-rate export supplies. A non-resident online retailer may use the simplified offshore supplier GST registration channel under the remote-services and low-value-goods regimes, which does not require a New Zealand bank account or a resident tax representative. Step 4 — Status confirmation: file a non-resident declaration and, where desired, obtain a private binding ruling from Inland Revenue confirming the absence of a New Zealand taxable presence for offshore e-commerce sales.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The CK_IC is classified as a tax-exempt-zone / international-company structure: offshore-source income (foreign online retail sales, FOB/CIF export proceeds, and non-New-Zealand trust income) falls outside the New Zealand income tax base. New Zealand-source income, such as domestic retail sales, services physically performed in New Zealand, or income from New Zealand land, is subject to entity-level corporate income tax at 28%, with no pass-through or transparent treatment available to a non-resident IC. For GST (indirect tax) purposes the entity is a separate taxable person chargeable at the headline rate of 15% on New Zealand taxable supplies.

Available election forms

New Zealand has no check-the-box classification election. The statutory election and adjustment mechanisms available include: (1) voluntary GST registration under section 51 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; (2) zero-rating election for exported goods and qualifying offshore services under sections 11 and 11A of the GST Act 1985; (3) qualifying company (QC) election under the Income Tax Act 2007, restricted to New Zealand-resident close companies and generally unavailable to a non-resident IC; (4) look-through company (LTC) election, limited to up to five New Zealand-resident natural-person shareholders and therefore unavailable where the IC has corporate or offshore trust shareholders; (5) GST grouping election under sections 55–57 of the GST Act for New Zealand-resident related entities; (6) hybrid mismatch neutrality and imported-mismatch adjustment rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 — these are compulsory and cannot be elected out; and (7) application for a private binding ruling to obtain statutory certainty on the deemed tax treatment of the structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12-month period for New Zealand domestic supplies (mandatory once exceeded; voluntary registration permitted below the threshold). For non-resident suppliers of 'remote services' to New Zealand consumers, and for non-resident sellers of 'low-value goods' (goods valued at or below NZD 1,000 imported without a full customs entry), compulsory GST registration arises once cumulative supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period. Oversized furniture items valued above NZD 1,000 fall outside the low-value-goods collection regime; where such goods are sold for delivery into New Zealand, import GST at 15% is assessed by the New Zealand Customs Service on the New Zealand consignee, while export deliveries are zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under section 11(1) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days after the time of supply (or such longer period as the Commissioner permits), the supplier holds verifiable evidence of physical export (customs export declaration, bill of lading or air waybill, and freight/insurance documentation), and the goods are not consumed in New Zealand. Services: zero-rated under section 11A where the recipient is a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, subject to statutory carve-outs for services relating to New Zealand land, services supplied to New Zealand-resident individuals, and services relating to imported goods other than consumables. For the CK_IC online retail of oversized furniture and home decor: sales concluded on CIF, FOB, or EXW terms with delivery to a foreign address qualify for zero-rating if risk and title pass offshore and the goods physically leave New Zealand within the 28-day export window. Where the IC sells through an electronic marketplace, the marketplace operator may be deemed the supplier under the GST Act's marketplace rules, in which case the IC must not charge GST on those supplies and the marketplace accounts for the output tax.

  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (carrier-issued negotiable transport document evidencing despatch from New Zealand)

New Zealand imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no government API stamping, and no digital tax-stamp requirement. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the international Peppol interoperability framework, administered in New Zealand by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE). Inland Revenue does not require pre-clearance or real-time authentication of invoices; however, GST returns (Form GST101) must be filed electronically through myIR or IRD-certified accounting software connected via the Inland Revenue Gateway API. A supplier must issue a tax invoice to a GST-registered recipient within 28 days of the time of supply, and records must be retained for 7 years and made available electronically to Inland Revenue where the Commissioner requires.

Inland Revenue subjects zero-rated export claims to periodic audit and may compel the taxpayer to produce the complete export documentation bundle within a prescribed statutory response time. If the documentation is inadequate, false, or the goods did not in fact leave New Zealand, the zero-rating is reversed, and the supplier is assessed for GST at 15% on the full consideration, together with use-of-money interest under Part VII of the Tax Administration Act 1994 from the original filing date. Civil shortfall penalties apply on a graduated scale: 20% for failure to take reasonable care; 40% for gross carelessness or an unacceptable tax position with a dominant purpose of tax avoidance; 100% for an abusive tax position; and 150% for evasion or a similar act. Fraudulent export zero-rating may additionally trigger criminal liability — imprisonment of up to 5 years and/or a fine of up to NZD 50,000 per offence under the Tax Administration Act 1994, or up to 7 years' imprisonment and/or a fine of up to NZD 15,000 under the Crimes Act 1968 for obtaining a pecuniary advantage by deception. The Commissioner may also re-open assessments up to 4 years after the end of the relevant assessment period (extending to 8 years where fraud or evasion is established), enabling full retrospective clawback of all refunded GST together with compound statutory interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a free-floating, fully convertible currency, with IMF Article VIII status and no exchange control laws restricting current or capital account transactions. Capital inflows, profit remittances, dividend distributions, and repatriation of invested capital are generally unrestricted, provided corporate governance and tax compliance are met. However, inward investments that acquire sensitive land, farmland, or significant business assets may require consent under the Overseas Investment Act 2005, and non-resident investors must obtain an IRD number, file appropriate returns, and satisfy non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) obligations on dividends, interest, and royalties. There are no separate capital account licensing regimes for ordinary e-commerce treasury operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline for export proceeds surrender and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters and e-commerce operators may retain foreign currency revenues in licensed foreign currency accounts domestically or in offshore accounts. All export receipts must nevertheless be captured in balance-of-payments statistics under the Statistics Act 2022, be fully traceable for tax and AML/CFT audit purposes, and be converted only through authorized channels that satisfy the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's reporting thresholds. No forced conversion to NZD exists for the oversized home furniture online retail model.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), exercising prudential supervision under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021; foreign exchange policy coordination with the New Zealand Treasury; overseas investment screening administered by Toitū Te Whenua Land Information New Zealand under the Overseas Investment Act 2005; tax-related foreign payment reporting under the Inland Revenue Department (IRD).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs may operate in or service New Zealand only after registration as a financial service provider under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and compliance with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 as a reporting entity supervised by the Department of Internal Affairs. Full deposit-taking business requires a banking license under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. An offshore EMI may provide payment and clearing services via local settlement accounts and partner banks, but it cannot legally take deposits or offer New Zealand-regulated credit products. The e-commerce treasury should therefore use an EMI as an auxiliary collections/clearing rail, with primary liquidity held in a tier-1 New Zealand commercial bank.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the New Zealand Financial Reporting Act 2013, a company is a 'large' entity and must have independently audited financial statements if it meets any one of: annual total revenue exceeding NZD 30 million, total assets exceeding NZD 60 million, or 100 or more full-time employees. Smaller companies are exempt from mandatory audit unless shareholders holding at least 5% of votes request one.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% NRWT on gross dividends paid by a New Zealand-resident company to non-resident shareholders (Tax Administration Act 1994, s NG 2); imputation credits are not refundable to non-residents; full or partial supplementary dividend mechanisms can reduce the combined tax burden to 15% for eligible treaty-listed corporate recipients.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full contemporaneous OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation (Local File) is required for related-party cross-border controlled transactions above NZ$10 million per annum; a simplified Low-Risk International Dealings approach applies below that threshold. Country-by-Country Reporting, Master File, and Local File are mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated global revenue of NZ$1.14 billion or more.
