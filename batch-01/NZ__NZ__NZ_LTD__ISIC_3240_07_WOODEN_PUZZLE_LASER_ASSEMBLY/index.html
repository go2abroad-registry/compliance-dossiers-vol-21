Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue; Te Tari Taake), Government of New Zealand — national tax administration portal: myIR (secure online services) accessible at https://www.ird.govt.nz, coordinating with the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window (JADE) for export-related tax administration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned New Zealand limited company (NZ_LTD) obtains a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) automatically upon incorporation via the New Zealand Companies Office. For tax purposes, the entity must procure an IRD number (Inland Revenue tax file number/TIN) and, where relevant, GST registration as follows: (1) If the company is incorporated with an intended New Zealand tax presence, the IRD number is commonly issued at incorporation or applied for through Form IR742 'Application for IRD Number' in myIR; (2) For a shelf company or newly acquired non-operating entity, submit Form IR742 online, attaching the certificate of incorporation, constitution, registered office address, full shareholder and director particulars, and the overseas tax identification details of ultimate beneficial owners; (3) Assess GST registration under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 — mandatory where annual taxable supplies in New Zealand equal or exceed NZD 60,000, and available on a voluntary basis from the commencement of any taxable activity, using the standard online GST registration (including the non-resident pathway in myIR); (4) If the company will have New Zealand employees, register as an employer for PAYE, KiwiSaver, and ACC levies; and (5) Obtain a Customs client code and link it to the NZBN and IRD number through the Trade Single Window before lodging export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a New Zealand-registered Limited company is a New Zealand tax resident if incorporated in New Zealand or if its centre of strategic management and control is in New Zealand; it is taxed on worldwide income at the flat corporate rate of 28% under the income tax imputation regime. The entity is, however, Pass_Through_Eligible only if a valid Look-Through Company (LTC) election is made under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, in which case income, losses, tax credits, and GST attributes flow through to qualifying shareholders. Territorial_Scope — New Zealand taxes resident companies on worldwide income but statutorily exempts most foreign-sourced dividends, certain foreign-sourced interests, and active business income of CFCs in grey-list jurisdictions. Tax_Exempt_Zone — Not applicable; New Zealand has no general tax-free export processing zones or special economic zones for this activity.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. The statutory elections available to an NZ_LTD include: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under Subpart HB, Income Tax Act 2007 (Form IR461), requiring unanimous shareholder consent, all shareholders being qualifying persons (individuals, certain trusts, or permitted foreign residents, generally from NZ, Australia, the United States, or the United Kingdom), and causing full fiscal transparency; (2) Consolidated group election under Subpart IG of the Income Tax Act 2007 for wholly-owned groups of companies to be treated as a single entity; (3) GST grouping election under Section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and the associated-persons joint registration rule; (4) Hybrid mismatch arrangement notifications and documented compliance under Subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 (BEPS Action 2) where structured cross-border hybrid entities or instruments are involved; and (5) CFC and branch-equivalent attribution elections under Subpart CQ for offshore activities. The historical Qualifying Company (QC) election regime is closed to new companies but grandfathered elections may continue.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period derived from a taxable activity carried on in New Zealand. For non-resident suppliers of remote services (including electronically supplied services and digital products), the same NZD 60,000 registration threshold applies to supplies made to New Zealand-resident consumers under the simplified non-resident GST regime in Schedule 3A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Once registered, the supplier accounts for GST on all taxable supplies connected with New Zealand; registration is also compulsory from the date the entity begins a taxable activity if it is clear that turnover will exceed the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a supply of goods is eligible for mandatory zero-rating at 0% GST where the supplier has delivered the goods, or arranged for the goods to be delivered, out of New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply and retains the prescribed evidence of export. The laser-cut wooden puzzle products qualify as exported goods when physically shipped to a non-New Zealand destination under a contract of sale or as part of the supplier's business. Services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply may be zero-rated where the services are physically performed outside New Zealand; digital and remote services consumed offshore by non-residents fall outside the scope of New Zealand GST entirely. The zero-rating must be claimed at the time of supply and is subject to strict evidentiary substantiation, including a direct nexus between the supplier, the goods, and the physical export movement.

  • New Zealand Customs Service electronic Export Declaration (Trade Single Window / JADE export entry)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing consignment to a non-New Zealand destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank remittance advice/SWIFT MT103 record evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and delivery docket
  • Carrier's cargo manifest or customs-endorsed export evidence showing the goods left New Zealand within 28 days of supply
  • Certificate of Origin (only where preferential tariff treatment is claimed under New Zealand's free trade agreements)

New Zealand does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate, and there is no tax-authority API clearance platform. E-invoicing is voluntary and follows the Peppol international framework (PEPPOL BIS 3.0, operated by the New Zealand Peppol Authority). Notwithstanding the voluntary e-invoicing regime, every GST-registered person must, within 28 days of a taxable supply, issue a compliant tax invoice to another GST-registered recipient showing supplier name, address and GST registration number, date of supply, description of goods, and either the GST-exclusive consideration plus the GST amount or the GST-inclusive consideration. Export entries must be lodged electronically with the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window (JADE). Where the NZ_LTD sells goods through electronic marketplaces such as Amazon or uses Stripe as a payment facilitator, the marketplace operator may be deemed the supplier for GST purposes on remote services supplied to New Zealand consumers under Sections 8I to 8K of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, thereby shifting GST collection compliance to the platform in certain circumstances.

Inland Revenue and the New Zealand Customs Service conduct integrated data-matching between GST zero-rated export supplies and physical export declarations lodged through JADE. Where a supplier claims zero-rating without valid export evidence, or where the goods are not exported within the statutory 28-day window, the supply is recharacterised as a standard-rated supply and GST at 15% is assessed retrospectively; any GST refund previously paid is clawed back with use-of-money interest accruing from the original due date. Civil shortfall penalties under Sections 141 and 141C to 141E of the Tax Administration Act 1994 escalate with culpability: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 150% for evasion or an intentional act. Fraudulent zero-rating claims also attract criminal prosecution under Section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994, including significant fines and terms of imprisonment, together with director personal liability for unpaid GST under the director penalty regime. The IRD's standard four-year reassessment period is extendable indefinitely where evasion or fraud has occurred, allowing retrospective recovery of wrongly refunded GST.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand Dollar (NZD) is fully and freely convertible across both current and capital account transactions. Since the abolition of exchange controls in 1984, New Zealand has maintained a liberalised, non-restricted cross-border capital regime: there are no prior approval requirements, no quantitative limits on inbound or outbound remittances, no surrender obligations, and no registration of foreign investment at bank level (except for sensitive sectors under the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which does not apply to a standard toy manufacturing and assembly business). Profit remittance and dividend distribution to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted in timing and amount, subject only to standard Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) at treaty rates (commonly 15% under applicable double tax agreements) and ordinary AML/CFT documentary verification of the transaction's economic substance. The NZD is a freely floating G10 commodity currency with deep two-way liquidity in onshore and offshore (offshore NZD / NZX-traded forwards) markets, ensuring efficient execution for corporate hedging and treasury operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender, conversion, repatriation, or minimum-holding timeline for export proceeds. Export receipts arising from Amazon marketplace sales, Stripe settlement, or direct wholesale invoicing may be retained in foreign currency accounts at registered New Zealand banks, held offshore, or converted to NZD entirely at the discretion of the exporter. The only applicable compliance overlay is the record-keeping and customer due diligence (CDD) framework under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, which requires reporting entities to maintain transaction records (including underlying commercial invoices, shipping documents, and settlement statements) for at least five years from the date of the transaction.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), operating under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, together with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for market conduct and anti-money-laundering oversight delegated under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate feasibility with strict structural constraints. New Zealand does not maintain a dedicated Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime equivalent to the UK's FCA EMI framework or the EU's EMD2; domestic non-bank payment service providers are regulated under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and the AML/CFT Act 2009 as reporting entities, and they are not licensed as full clearing institutions or settlement members. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury, and similar BaaS platforms) may lawfully provide supplementary collection rails, sub-accounts for marketplaces, and cost-eff e-ficient FX conversion, but they cannot function as a substitute for a primary corporate banking relationship with a registered New Zealand bank. The Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) operated by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand grants settlement finality only to registered banks and licensed settlement participants; therefore, all liquidity concentration, working-capital facilities, and NZD clearing finality must reside at a Tier-1 domestic bank. Recommended architecture: use Stripe and Amazon Pay as front-end merchant acquirers; route net settlement funds through an offshore EMI gateway or directly via SWIFT to the primary NZ bank operational account; sweep foreign-currency balances to the NZ base account on a daily or T+1 basis to eliminate sub-account credit risk. The EMIs should never hold strategic reserves, and their aggregate balance should be capped at a de minimis working-capital threshold (e.g., 20% of monthly turnover) to comply with prudent treasury policy.

Wise Fit: High

Moderate feasibility with strict structural constraints. New Zealand does not maintain a dedicated Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime equivalent to the UK's FCA EMI framework or the EU's EMD2; domestic non-bank payment service providers are regulated under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and the AML/CFT Act 2009 as reporting entities, and they are not licensed as full clearing institutions or settlement members. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury, and similar BaaS platforms) may lawfully provide supplementary collection rails, sub-accounts for marketplaces, and cost-eff e-ficient FX conversion, but they cannot function as a substitute for a primary corporate banking relationship with a registered New Zealand bank. The Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) operated by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand grants settlement finality only to registered banks and licensed settlement participants; therefore, all liquidity concentration, working-capital facilities, and NZD clearing finality must reside at a Tier-1 domestic bank. Recommended architecture: use Stripe and Amazon Pay as front-end merchant acquirers; route net settlement funds through an offshore EMI gateway or directly via SWIFT to the primary NZ bank operational account; sweep foreign-currency balances to the NZ base account on a daily or T+1 basis to eliminate sub-account credit risk. The EMIs should never hold strategic reserves, and their aggregate balance should be capped at a de minimis working-capital threshold (e.g., 20% of monthly turnover) to comply with prudent treasury policy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections are routed via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) from the overseas shareholder's bank to the New Zealand entity's designated operational or capital escrow account held at a Tier-1 registered New Zealand bank, with the receiving institution advised of the transaction narrative (e.g., 'Issuance of ordinary shares under Companies Act 1993 – shareholder equity injection' or 'Interest-bearing director loan for working capital'). New Zealand does not mandate a central-bank purpose-code framework for inward remittances, but the receiving bank's AML/CFT case-management system will request a purpose descriptor and may require the underlying equity subscription agreement or loan agreement to be uploaded as supporting evidence. Where the injection exceeds ordinary board-authorised thresholds, the bank may require a board resolution and a source-of-funds declaration prior to crediting the account. All inward remittances settle in NZD via the bank's own books or through correspondent SWIFT clearing with same-day value for NZD (if received before system cut-off) and T+1 for foreign currency conversions. No exchange-control permission, registration, or surrender requirement applies.Outbound disbursements for raw materials (pre-cut wooden boards, laser consumables, non-toxic paints, packaging), logistics freight, Amazon FBA storage fees, customs duties, and overseas design or marketing subcontractors are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfers) and bank-hosted batch payments / autoFX facilities for recurring vendor payments. For larger documentary trade flows, a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) issued under UCP600 rules and advised through the bank's international trade desk is permissible, though generally commercially unnecessary for a low-value, high-frequency toy manufacturing export model. Domestic New Zealand-dollar settlement runs through the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS), providing irrevocable settlement finality; foreign-currency disbursements in USD, AUD, EUR, and GBP are cleared via the bank's correspondent network with same-day or T+1 execution depending on currency cut-off times and nostro availability. Trade disbursements should be supported by a treasury payment register matching invoices, customs declarations, and marketplace fee statements to ensure complete auditability under the AML/CFT Act 2009 record-keeping obligations.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forward contracts (NZD/USD, NZD/AUD, NZD/EUR, NZD/GBP) booked with a registered New Zealand bank, specifying notional amount, fixing date, and settlement via gross delivery, supported by underlying trade-exposure evidence (sales orders, purchase orders, or forecast export receivables); no special regulatory approval required.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not applicable or generally permissible for the New Zealand Dollar onshore, because the NZD is a fully deliverable free-floating currency; a synthetic NDF overlay would be redundant, and onshore banks will not ordinarily book NZD NDFs except in unusual counterparty-credit or settlement-facility scenarios, so the treasury should rely on deliverable forwards and FX swaps instead.Cross-currency FX swaps (NZD/USD and NZD/AUD) for temporary liquidation of surplus foreign-currency balances into NZD working capital, with simultaneous forward re-exchange, used when marketplace settlement currencies lag disbursement currency needs.Zero-cost collars and plain-vanilla FX call/put options on NZD crosses, permitted subject to the bank's credit approval, a board-approved treasury hedging policy, and documented underlying exposure, to protect Amazon marketplace revenue and Amazon Pay settlement volatility over seasonal sales peaks (e.g., Q4 Christmas retail cycle).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory dollar threshold for all taxpayers, but contemporaneous documentation is generally expected for related-party cross-border transactions exceeding NZ$1 million in annual aggregate. Larger multinationals should hold Master File/Local File documentation. CbC reporting applies to NZ-headquartered groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million (approx. NZ$1.3 billion).

Economic substance scope: While New Zealand does not have UAE-style ESR, general anti-avoidance rules (BG 1, GA 1) disregard artificial arrangements lacking commercial substance. Companies must have real economic presence in New Zealand for tax residence and treaty benefits. Central management and control determines residence under section YD 5.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to NZ$50,000 (first offence) or NZ$100,000 (subsequent) under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994, plus shortfall penalties up to 150%.

Director personal liability: Directors participating in evasion may face imprisonment up to 5 years, personal liability for tax shortfalls, travel bans, and civil recovery orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act.
Shell Company / Inadequate Substance

Statutory sanction: Company may be treated as non-resident, making it ineligible for NZ DTA benefits. Inland Revenue may assess based on 'place of effective management'. Strike-off possible under Companies Act 1993.

Director personal liability: Directors may breach section 135/136 duties, leading to personal liability for company debts, director disqualification up to 5 years, and in fraud cases imprisonment up to 7 years.
AML/CFT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to NZ$2 million per contravention for companies under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009; criminal imprisonment up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly involved may be personally liable for civil penalties, face travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment under the Proceeds of Crime Act.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Commissioner may adjust to arm's length. Shortfall penalties: 20% for lack of reasonable care; 40% gross carelessness; 150% fraud/evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who aid or abet incorrect documentation face personal penalties and possible imprisonment up to 5 years for tax evasion.
Chronic Late Filing and Non-Payment

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalties: NZ$250–$500 per month; late payment penalties: 1% immediate, 4% after 7 days, 1% monthly; interest accrues. Inland Revenue may enforce via garnishee, bankruptcy, or liquidation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid GST or PAYE if involved in the decision to withhold. Director bans up to 5 years possible.
