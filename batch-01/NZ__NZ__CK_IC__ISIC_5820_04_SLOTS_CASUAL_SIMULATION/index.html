Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand Government — online portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that derives New Zealand-source income or is required to register for GST must obtain an IRD number. Application is made via the IRD's Non-Resident Company IRD number application form (IR742) or through the myIR portal. The entity must provide certified constituent documents, proof of foreign tax residence, and details of its New Zealand business activities. IRD numbers are issued by post/fax; a non-resident company without a fixed establishment in New Zealand may also need to appoint a New Zealand agent for service of notices if carrying on a taxable activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime. However, an International Company incorporated under the Cook Islands International Companies Act may be treated as a non-resident company for New Zealand tax purposes if central management and control and its place of effective management are outside New Zealand. No hybrid mismatch election is generally available; New Zealand has hybrid mismatch rules (Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007) that may recharacterise payments, but no elective check-the-box mechanism exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 turnover in any 12-month period for goods and services supplied in New Zealand by a person carrying on a taxable activity; non-resident suppliers of remote/digital services to New Zealand consumers must register under the GST on remote services rules when their taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period (no physical presence threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods (removed from New Zealand) and to certain services supplied to non-residents who are outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the services are not supplied in connection with land or goods in New Zealand and are not physically performed in New Zealand, or are supplied with a close connection to the recipient's business outside New Zealand. For a Cook Islands International Company with no New Zealand fixed establishment, supplies of offshore gaming services to non-resident customers would generally be outside the scope of New Zealand GST; if registered, zero-rating of exported digital/remote services may apply under section 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

  • Customs Export Declaration (NZ Customs Trade Single Window / CusMod export entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/forex proof of foreign currency receipt
  • Contract or invoice evidencing supply to a non-resident
  • Evidence that goods left New Zealand or services delivered offshore (e.g. IP logs, delivery records)

New Zealand does not require real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of invoices. Electronic invoicing is permitted and Peppol e-invoicing is supported voluntarily. GST returns are filed electronically via myIR on a six-monthly, two-monthly, or monthly basis depending on turnover and registration election. There is no mandatory continuous transaction controls or API clearance mechanism for invoices.

If a GST-registered person zero-rates an export without adequate documentation or without the foreign exchange proceeds being received within the statutory time frame, the supply is recharacterised as taxable at 15% GST, with interest and penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 (e.g. late payment penalties, shortfall penalties up to 150% of the tax shortfall for evasion, and use-of-money interest). Fraudulent zero-rating schemes may attract criminal penalties under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994 (offence of evasion or similar), fines up to NZD 50,000 for the first offence and imprisonment in serious cases, plus retrospective clawback of any refunds and deemed taxable supply adjustments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full IMF Article VIII status and operates a freely floating and fully convertible New Zealand dollar. No exchange control legislation restricts current account or capital account transactions, and no central bank approval is required for profit repatriation, dividend payments, royalty remittances, or equity injections. Non-resident investors may remit dividends and capital freely; any withholding taxes (e.g., non-resident dividend withholding tax or royalty withholding tax) are tax matters only and do not require foreign exchange authorization. The sole capital-control-adjacent mechanism is the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which applies to acquisitions of sensitive land and fishing quota; it does not apply to digital publishing, software licensing, or social casino game operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the Reserve Bank or any mandatory conversion ratio. A New Zealand-resident exporter may retain export receipts in foreign currency accounts denominated in USD, EUR, AUD, or any other freely convertible currency without time limitation. Proceeds from digital publishing and virtual game subscriptions are treated as current account receipts and are not subject to repatriation deadlines. All cross-border receipts remain subject to standard anti-money laundering (AML) reporting obligations, including large-value transaction reporting and suspicious transaction reporting thresholds only.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not registered banks in New Zealand and cannot lawfully hold capital escrow balances in the manner of a licensed deposit-taker. They may be accepted as money remitters or payment service providers if registered under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and enrolled in the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 supervision regime. In practice, an EMI's merchant policy often prohibits or heavily restricts 'social casino' or 'virtual slots' merchants on intermediary risk grounds, and the Medium_Risk_Enhanced banking classification will trigger additional due-diligence and source-of-funds review. Therefore, EMIs may be used only for ancillary inbound/outbound settlement sweeps, not for the primary regulated treasury account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends paid to non-residents; fully imputed dividends may attract no additional NRWT where the supplementary dividend regime is used; DTA relief may reduce the residual rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a standard 31 March balance date, the return is due on 7 July if filed online, or 30 June for paper returns; for non-standard balance dates, the due date is the last day of the fourth month after balance date (electronic) or third month after balance date (paper). Tax agents may obtain an extension of time to 31 March of the following income year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$1 million: cross-border related-party transactions exceeding NZ$1 million in an income year require contemporaneous Local File documentation; multinational groups with consolidated global revenue of NZ$1.3 billion or more additionally require a Master File and Country-by-Country Report filed with Inland Revenue.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) code; nonetheless, economic substance is embedded in the general anti-avoidance rules, the permanent establishment definition, and DTA entitlement tests. A Cook Islands / foreign International Company or other shell that cannot demonstrate real management, employees, premises, and decision-making in New Zealand will be denied New Zealand tax residence, treaty certificates, and may be subject to reconstruction assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraudulent Returns)

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under ss 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994; imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to NZ$50,000 for individuals and NZ$200,000 for companies; civil shortfall penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded, plus late payment penalties and use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly signs a false return is an accessory and may be personally assessed for the full shortfall, disqualified from managing companies for up to 5 years, subjected to asset preservation orders, face cancellation of New Zealand travel arrangements while serving sentence, and be extradited in cases of cross-border fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation (Profit Shifting)

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may reconstruct the transaction under ss GC 6–GC 14, impose transfer pricing penalties up to 100% of the shortfall (or 40% for failing to maintain prescribed documentation), and apply anti-avoidance penalties; interest accrues from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the mispriced arrangement are jointly and severally liable for corporate taxes where the company is a mere conduit or facade; they may face personal penalty assessments, director disqualification, and, if knowingly assisting fraud, criminal prosecution.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA relief; assessment of 28% corporate tax on all income; imposition of anti-avoidance penalties; the shell may be struck off under the Companies Act 1993 for no real business; promoters and advisers are also liable for penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Director personal liability: Where the company is a facade, courts may pierce the veil and hold the controlling director personally liable for unpaid tax, interest, and penalties; a travel restriction order may be issued while tax debts are outstanding, and directors face civil restitution and up to 5 years' imprisonment for fraudulent misuse of exempt-zone status.
AML/CFT Compliance Failure (Virtual Currency and IAP Railing)

Statutory sanction: Civil liability up to NZ$2 million for a reporting entity; criminal penalties for individuals up to NZ$300,000 and for bodies corporate up to NZ$4 million per breach; revocation of operating licenses; asset forfeiture and restraint orders.

Director personal liability: Directors of the reporting entity can be prosecuted personally where AML failures are systemic or reckless; each director faces fines, disqualification from managing any financial institution and, in cases of money-laundering facilitation, imprisonment up to 7 years.
Late Filing / Obstructing Audit

Statutory sanction: Automatic late filing penalties and late payment penalties, plus shortfall penalties for obstruction of 20%–100% of tax; every non-compliant year is assessed separately; Inland Revenue can obtain a production order and issue a non-compliance notification that blocks online government services.

Director personal liability: Failure to file is a strict liability offence; the director who controls the accounting function is liable for penalty fines and compromised data-access bans; where the failure is deliberate evasion, the director may be imprisoned and disqualified from serving as a director of any New Zealand company.
