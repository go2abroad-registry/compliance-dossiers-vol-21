Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (per the ELF_CUSTOM_CK_IC classification model). In New Zealand statutory terms, CK_IC is a non-resident foreign company taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income (source-based taxation under the Income Tax Act 2007, source rules in sections YD 4 and YD 5). Digital webtoon publishing and multilingual chapter-unlocking income derived from offshore consumers, Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, and non-resident syndication licensees constitutes foreign-sourced income and is excluded from the NZ income tax base absent a New Zealand permanent establishment, a NZ-resident agent concluding contracts on the IC's behalf, or income attributable to NZ activities. The IC is therefore not subject to the 28% NZ corporate income tax rate on its offshore business profits. Any NZ-source income (e.g., direct B2C digital sales to NZ consumers not intermediated by a marketplace operator, or NZ-sourced royalties from a NZ licensee) would be subject to NZ non-resident withholding tax, generally at the 15% treaty/NRWT rate, and any such direct consumer supplies would trigger NZ GST registration at the NZD 60,000 threshold.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity classification regime; classification follows statute. Available elections for CK_IC include: (1) GST registration and filing period elections under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985: monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly return periods (section 16); payments-basis vs invoice-basis accounting (section 19); voluntary GST registration (section 51); and GST grouping with a NZ-resident group company (joint election under section 55). (2) Hybrid and branch mismatch elections under Part GC of the Income Tax Act 2007 (inserted by the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018): where a payment or entity is treated differently in New Zealand and the counterparty jurisdiction, the IC may elect the OECD-conform compensatory adjustment to neutralise a double non-taxation outcome and must maintain contemporaneous hybrid mismatch documentation and lodge any required IRD disclosure schedules. (3) Balance date election: the IC may elect a non-standard accounting balance date, with Commissioner's approval, to align its NZ reporting with the offshore shareholder's balance date (Income Tax Act 2007, sections 38-39). (4) For GST, the IC may adopt the 'deemed zero-rated export' administrative treatment for remote services supplied to non-resident recipients, recording the recipient's non-resident status contemporaneously. (5) If the IC subsequently acquires a NZ GST-registered subsidiary or branch, it may elect GST cross-border and group elections to neutralise inter-company GST. No check-the-box election is available to treat the IC as a disregarded or transparent entity for NZ purposes; the IC is a taxable person in its own right for GST and a non-resident company for income tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000. Threshold rules: (i) Domestic nexus: any person carrying on a taxable activity must register for GST when the total value of taxable supplies in any 12-month period exceeds NZD 60,000, or has a reasonable expectation of doing so. (ii) Offshore remote-services nexus: from 1 October 2016, a non-resident supplier of 'remote services' (which includes digital webtoon content, chapter unlocks, and digital subscriptions) to New Zealand resident consumers must register when the aggregate value of those supplies to NZ consumers exceeds NZD 60,000 in any rolling 12-month period. (iii) Marketplace exception: where CK_IC's remote services are supplied to NZ consumers through an electronic marketplace (Apple App Store via Apple IAP and Google Play via Google Play Billing), the marketplace operator is the deemed supplier and is liable for NZ GST on those NZ consumer sales (marketplace operator rules, sections 8C, 8D, and 60C of the GST Act 1985), subject to the same NZD 60,000 threshold applied to the operator's NZ supplies. (iv) The NZD 60,000 threshold is measured on a GST-exclusive consideration basis; registration is compulsory from the day after the threshold is crossed and an application must be filed within 21 days (administrative practice for non-residents). (v) Voluntary registration is available to non-residents even below the threshold to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

CK_IC's digital webtoon publishing and multilingual chapter unlocking is a supply of 'remote services' (Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, section 8(4B)) and is zero-rated as an export under section 11(1)(m) where: (1) the recipient is non-resident and is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed (for digital content, deemed performed where the recipient is located, so an end-consumer outside NZ satisfies this test); (2) if the recipient is a NZ GST-registered person, the supply must not be made in connection with that recipient's NZ taxable activity, or the recipient has supplied its NZ GST number and declared that the services are wholly for its NZ taxable activity, in which case the supply is zero-rated under the B2B cross-border services rule. Specific application: (a) Sales through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing to offshore end-consumers: zero-rated; the platform operator is a non-resident recipient and the underlying supply to the operator is zero-rated; (b) Sales through Apple/Google to NZ end-consumers: not CK_IC's supply for GST purposes - the marketplace operator accounts for 15% GST on the NZ consumer sale, while CK_IC's platform settlement remains zero-rated; (c) Direct B2B licensing of the webtoon catalogue to non-resident syndication platforms, publishers, or aggregators: zero-rated under section 11(1)(m) upon receipt of the licensee's written non-resident declaration (name, address, foreign VAT/GST registration number where applicable, and confirmation that the services are not for a NZ taxable activity); (d) Direct B2C subscriptions to overseas readers via CK_IC's own website: zero-rated where geolocation, billing address, and payment method evidence conclusively places the consumer outside NZ. No GST refund arises on a zero-rated export because output tax is charged at 0%; input tax credits (e.g., on production software procured in NZ) are claimable only by a GST-registered person, so a non-registered IC would not file refund claims. If CK_IC voluntarily registers for GST, it may file monthly or six-monthly refund claims for NZ input tax attributable to export production inputs.

New Zealand imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, digital stamping, or pre-clearance API mandate for e-invoices. GST returns (form GST101) are filed electronically through the myIR portal, and GST liabilities are paid electronically by direct debit or bank transfer. Electronic invoicing through the PEPPOL network is voluntary in NZ (adopted 2022 for B2G interoperability) and is not a statutory precondition for input tax deduction; a valid tax invoice under the GST Act 1985 may be issued in paper or electronic form. There is no requirement for third-party fiscal certification of cash registers or invoicing software. CK_IC should nevertheless implement a compliant electronic invoicing workflow (issuing tax invoices or documentation for zero-rated exports, retaining marketplace settlement reports, and preserving 7 years of records) and, if voluntarily registered, should use the myIR portal's GST101 e-filing and integrate its accounting records with digital platform data for audit readiness.

Inland Revenue audits export zero-rating by focusing on the 'remote services' rules, marketplace operator attribution, and transfer pricing. If CK_IC or its marketplace operator misapplies zero-rating (e.g., the IC cannot substantiate that a consumer was outside NZ, or supplies to NZ consumers were incorrectly zero-rated on the IC's direct sales), Inland Revenue will: (1) reassess output GST at 15% on the full taxable value of the wrongly zero-rated supplies; (2) impose use-of-money interest (UOMI) on the resulting GST shortfall at the prescribed quarterly rate (historically between 8% and 10.5% for underpayment); and (3) assess shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for lack of reasonable care; 20% for an unacceptable tax position; 20% for a tax avoidance arrangement; 100% for an abusive tax position; and 150% for evasion or an equivalent offence. In cases involving false documentation (e.g., fabricated non-resident declarations or forged IRCs), criminal prosecution is available with fines of up to NZD 50,000 per offence and imprisonment of up to 5 years (Tax Administration Act 1994, sections 143A, 143B, and 144). Retrospective assessments may be raised within the 4-year amendment period under section 108 of the Tax Administration Act 1994; there is no time limit where the defect arises from fraud or evasion. Any clawback of zero-rated export treatment would reduce input tax credits proportionately and could trigger consequential adjustments to marketplace operator attribution. CK_IC must therefore retain all platform, geolocation, and remittance evidence for at least 7 years and implement a documented zero-rating substantiation matrix for immediate production in audit.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a free-floating exchange rate regime with full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and the Currency and Bank Notes Act. There are no statutory exchange control approvals for the conversion of NZD, no profit-remittance gatekeeping by the RBNZ, and no restrictions on the disposal of dividends, royalties, or loan repayments by non-resident entities. The Cook Islands IC, being a tax-exempt non-resident, can freely repatriate platform settlement proceeds; however, all remittances are screened under the AML/CFT Act 2009, the AML/CFT Regulations 2011 (which impose originator and beneficiary information obligations for wire transfers at or above NZD 1,000), and FATCA/CRS self-certification protocols. The only practical hurdle is documentary: the paying bank must obtain the entity's non-resident status, beneficial ownership declaration, and evidence that no New Zealand permanent establishment is created under the domestic PE test, as there is no comprehensive double taxation agreement between New Zealand and the Cook Islands.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export or digital-service receipts in New Zealand; exporters may hold foreign currency indefinitely in any deliverable currency. For the webtoon syndication scope, proceeds remitted from Apple IAP and Google Play arrive as USD gross-settlement after store commissions, and the receiving NZ bank will automate the FX conversion to NZD only upon the company's explicit instruction, at the then-prevailing interbank mid-rate plus an agreed spread. All receipts above NZD 10,000 trigger enhanced monitoring under the AMLCFT obliged-entity obligations, which requires an audit trail linking the platform payout statement to the inbound credit.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), acting as the apex monetary authority for NZD clearing and settlement, with supervisory coordination from the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) in respect of the Cook Islands International Company registration and any regulated financial activity in Rarotonga.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility for daily low-value USD/NZD sweeps from Apple IAP and Google Play via regulated New Zealand-registered e-money and payment institutions, provided the EMI is registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and maintains an AML/CFT compliance programme approved by the Department of Internal Affairs. Leading entities such as Wise and Airwallex (NZ branch) can accept a Cook Islands International Company after a four-to-eight-week onboarding cycle, subject to the same beneficial-ownership disclosure as Tier-1 banks.
