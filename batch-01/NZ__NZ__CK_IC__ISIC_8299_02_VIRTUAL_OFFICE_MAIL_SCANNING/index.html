Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office and mail-scanning business is an acceptable commercial activity in New Zealand, and the New Zealand company may itself provide such CMRA services to clients. However, the New Zealand company's own statutory registered office must be a physical New Zealand street address at all times under section 124 of the Companies Act 1993; it cannot be a post office box, a mail forwarding address without physical premises, or an electronic mailbox. The registered office must be open to the public during normal business hours and accessible for service of legal documents. The company may use a third-party registered agent or corporate service provider that maintains a physical New Zealand street address, provided the arrangement includes continuous access, mail-holding authority and prompt forwarding of legal documents. Because the CMRA business process includes on-boarding clients, collecting recurring payments through Stripe and credit cards, and receiving legal mail on behalf of clients, the company must also register as a reporting entity under the AML/CFT Act and maintain a New Zealand AML/CFT compliance programme before commencing mail scanning operations.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under the Companies Act 1993 for a private company limited by shares. The company may be incorporated with zero issued capital or with nominal capital of NZD 1.00 per subscriber share, and no escrow account or statutory capital deposit is mandated. Any agreed subscription or shareholder funding is contractual and recorded in the company's share register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11A of the GST Act 1985: (a) goods physically exported by the supplier are zero-rated (s. 11A(1)(a)-(c)); (b) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply are zero-rated, provided the services are not supplied directly in connection with land in New Zealand or goods in New Zealand (s. 11A(1)(k), subject to s. 11A(2)); (c) remote services supplied to a non-resident individual outside New Zealand under the cross-border B2C regime are zero-rated (s. 8(4B)); and (d) services supplied in connection with goods that are subsequently exported may be zero-rated where the services are limited to repair, maintenance, cleaning, or reconditioning (s. 11A(2)(b)(i)). For the virtual-office and mail-scanning business model (CMRA), zero-rating is defensible only where the supply is characterized as an administrative or virtual-office service to a non-resident recipient with the place of supply outside New Zealand; the physical act of receiving, opening, and scanning mail in New Zealand creates a nexus with goods in New Zealand that can standard-rate the entire supply. The supplier must therefore document the dominant element of the service, the non-resident status of the recipient, and the offshore delivery of the digital output, and should obtain an IRD product ruling where the mail-processing activity is material.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Export Transaction Record (ETR) / electronic export entry lodged in the Trade Single Window (TSW) for any physical mail or parcel shipments
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the mail-scanning service and the contents of any physical consignment
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement out of New Zealand
  • Inward payment evidence, comprising SWIFT MT103, bank inward remittance credit advice, or FX conversion confirmation from a New Zealand bank
  • Certificate of non-residence, such as a tax residence certificate (IRD form IR902 equivalent) or foreign business registry extract, evidencing the overseas status of each client
  • Executed service agreement designating the place of supply, the offshore performance obligations, and the electronic delivery of scanned records

No real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority pre-approval, or digital stamping regime applies. New Zealand operates a voluntary Peppol BIS 3.0 e-invoicing network (NZ e-Invoicing) administered by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment; the IRD accepts electronic invoices and records provided the taxpayer retains adequate documentary records in New Zealand or makes them electronically available for the statutory 7-year retention period under section 22 of the Tax Administration Act 1994.

The IRD may reassess wrongly zero-rated supplies within 4 years of the assessment, extended to 7 years for gross carelessness or fraudulent conduct, with no time limit for wilful evasion. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply at 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 150% for evasion, together with late payment interest under sections 120A-120D. Input tax credits claimed against exported supplies may be retrospectively clawed back with accrued interest. Criminal liability for GST fraud may be pursued under sections 228-229 of the Crimes Act 1961, carrying imprisonment up to 7 years. New Zealand imposes no exchange-control-based statutory obligation to repatriate export proceeds; the 180-day benchmark serves as the IRD and banking documentation convention for contemporaneous export-proceeds evidence, and retention of foreign currency beyond that period without legitimate commercial substance may trigger transfer-pricing review and potential recharacterization under section GC 6 of the Income Tax Act 2007. All zero-rating substantiation documentation must be retained for 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has maintained full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement since the 1980s, and the New Zealand dollar has floated freely since March 1985. There are no exchange-control approvals, surrender mandates, or non-resident profit-remittance hurdles. Dividends, interest, royalties, service fees, and liquidation proceeds may be remitted freely. The only constraints are tax-compliant withholding, namely non-resident withholding tax on dividends (default 15%, often reduced under a double tax agreement) and interest (default 15%); anti-money-laundering verification; and, for transactions above NZ$100 million in qualifying assets or sensitive land, consent under the Overseas Investment Act 2005. A Cook Islands International Company operating a New Zealand virtual-office CMRA can repatriate funds without central-bank consent provided the underlying information is properly disclosed to the reporting bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. No currency is required to be surrendered to the Reserve Bank. Exporters and service providers, including virtual-office and scanning operators, may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts offshore or convert them at any authorised dealer rate. Obligations are limited to reporting: banks must file prescribed cross-border transaction reports to the Financial Intelligence Unit through the banking reporting portal for cash and wire transfers at or above NZ$10,000, and Statistics New Zealand balance-of-payments surveys may collect aggregate invoice data, but no converting or depositing timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), with the Financial Markets Authority and the Department of Internal Affairs acting as co-supervisors under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. The Reserve Bank administers prudential oversight of banks and the real-time gross settlement system (Exchange Settlement Account System); exchange-control functions are limited to statistical balance-of-payments reporting and transaction monitoring.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. A licensed offshore electronic-money institution (e.g., CurrencyCloud, Airwallex, or Wise Business) with New Zealand financial-service-provider registration under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 may permit the International Company to open virtual multi-currency accounts for B2B collections and Stripe-adjacent payout routing. However, EMIs are not banks under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989, may not take deposits without a license or exemption under the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013, and are materially constrained for capital-escrow, trade-finance, or high-value settlement functions. The Reserve Bank treats them as non-bank payment service providers rather than clearing-system participants, so full NZD clearing (BECS) access is indirect via sponsor banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: the Cook Islands parent or the International Company's offshore treasury initiates a SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer, denominated in USD or NZD, to the Tier-1 New Zealand operating account, citing the recipient's exact legal name, its New Zealand overseas-company registration number if assigned, and the business purpose as intercompany capital injection or direct investment in the remittance information field. The receiving bank assigns the applicable balance-of-payments purpose code under the Reserve Bank's statistical reporting framework for direct investment in New Zealand equity or intercompany lending. Verification includes certified board resolutions from both parties, the intercompany loan or equity instrument, and source-of-funds evidence for each remittance; amounts at or above NZ$10,000 trigger mandatory AML/CFT threshold transaction reporting, and the funds must be traceable to the International Company's named beneficial owners before unrestricted use. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium. A licensed offshore electronic-money institution (e.g., CurrencyCloud, Airwallex, or Wise Business) with New Zealand financial-service-provider registration under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 may permit the International Company to open virtual multi-currency accounts for B2B collections and Stripe-adjacent payout routing. However, EMIs are not banks under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989, may not take deposits without a license or exemption under the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013, and are materially constrained for capital-escrow, trade-finance, or high-value settlement functions. The Reserve Bank treats them as non-bank payment service providers rather than clearing-system participants, so full NZD clearing (BECS) access is indirect via sponsor banks.
