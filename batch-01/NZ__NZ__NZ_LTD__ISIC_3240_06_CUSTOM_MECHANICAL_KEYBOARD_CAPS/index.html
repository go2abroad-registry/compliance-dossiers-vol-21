Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office (NZCO), an operational business unit of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), administered through the Companies Register at https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), sections 12–20 (incorporation procedure), s 25 (issuance of certificate of incorporation), s 107 (registered office), ss 149–157 (director appointments and consents), ss 189–200 (share transfers and allotment records), and s 213 (company constitution adoption). Supplementary regulation: Companies (Fees) Regulations 2023; Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 is not applicable to an exempt private company.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NZCN1 – Application for Incorporation of a Company (online lodgement authenticated via RealMe), accompanied by a Company Constitution (if deviating from the default rules in Companies Act 1993 Schedule 1), written consents to act as director, and a notice of registered office (Form NZCN2).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. New Zealand law imposes no minimum paid-up share capital requirement; shares may be allotted at an infinitesimal par value (e.g. NZD 0.01 per ordinary share). No escrow account, capital deposit, bank confirmation, or certification of capital payment is required at the time of incorporation. The company may lawfully commence business and open a corporate bank account immediately upon issuance of the certificate of incorporation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) Te Tari Taake, Wellington, New Zealand; primary official portal is myIR at www.ird.govt.nz. Export declaration and customs administration is performed by the New Zealand Customs Service (Te Mana Ārai o Aotearoa) through the Trade Single Window. IRD is the sole national competent authority for income tax, GST, PAYE, and international tax rules; NZ Customs is the border enforcement counterpart for export substantiation.

E-government filing portal

STEP 1 - Corporate registration: A foreign entity carrying on business in New Zealand must register as an overseas company with the New Zealand Companies Office (www.companiesoffice.govt.nz) and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). An NZ-resident limited company (NZ_LTD) is incorporated under the Companies Act 1993 and receives its NZBN, Companies Register number, and IRD number through the integrated incorporation filing. STEP 2 - IRD number application: Non-resident and overseas-incorporated companies apply for an IRD number on form IR742 via myIR or paper, submitting certified certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director and 25%+ shareholder identity details, the NZBN or overseas registration number, and evidence of a New Zealand resident agent for service of process. Resident NZ limited companies receive the IRD number automatically upon incorporation or may apply online through myIR. STEP 3 - GST registration: After IRD number issuance, register for GST in myIR; compulsory registration arises when taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period. Exporters may voluntarily register to recover input credits; non-resident suppliers may appoint a GST representative. STEP 4 - Employer schemes: If employees are engaged, register for PAYE, KiwiSaver, and ACC through myIR. STEP 5 - Customs exporter registration: Register with NZ Customs for a Client Code via the Trade Single Window to lodge electronic export entries; obtain tariff classification advice for polymer keycaps (commonly HS 3926.90 or as computer peripheral parts) and confirm no export licensing restrictions apply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: An NZ limited company is a separate taxable legal person under the Income Tax Act 2007, subject to a flat 28% corporate income tax rate with full dividend imputation. It is not a pass-through entity by default. A tax-resident NZ company is taxed on worldwide income, subject to foreign dividend exemptions and active CFC/foreign branch relief; a non-resident company is taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income (territorial nexus). No tax-exempt zone status applies to this entity or jurisdiction.

Available election forms

Available elections and regimes include: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election - a closely held NZ company with five or fewer eligible owners may elect under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 to be treated as a transparent pass-through entity (form IR745). (2) Imputation Credit Account (ICA) election - a company may elect to maintain an ICA and attach imputation credits to shareholder dividends. (3) Loss offset election - wholly-owned group companies may elect to transfer losses within the group. (4) GST group registration election - related companies may elect under s 55 of the GST Act 1985 to file a consolidated GST return and disregard inter-company supplies. (5) GST apportionment and mixed-use asset elections under s 91A. (6) Controlled foreign company reporting elections, including branch-equivalent and active business relief provisions. New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box classification regime; hybrid and branch mismatch rules under subparts FH and FM of the Income Tax Act 2007 deny deductions for unintended hybrid outcomes, and no elective hybrid blocker is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period triggers mandatory GST registration (s 51 GST Act 1985). Non-resident offshore suppliers of remote services and low-value goods (goods valued under NZD 1,000) to New Zealand-resident consumers must also register once their New Zealand-resident turnover exceeds NZD 60,000 per annum (ss 54C-54D). Exporters may register voluntarily below the threshold to recover input tax; the threshold is calculated exclusive of GST using a 12-month lookback and reasonable expectation look-forward test.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: A supply of goods is zero-rated under s 11(1)(a)-(b) of the GST Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand before the earlier of the time of supply or the 28th day after the time of supply, or where goods are supplied to a non-resident and exported before the 28th day, provided the goods are not subsequently reimported or diverted into the New Zealand domestic market. For NZ_LTD's custom mechanical keyboard keycap business (ISIC 3240 / NAICS 334118), zero-rating applies to injection-moulded double-shot PBT keycaps exported directly to overseas distributors, retailers, and end customers when the company holds valid customs export entries, transport documents, and payment remittance evidence. Services: Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated under s 11(1)(d) where the non-resident recipient is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed and the services are not directly connected to New Zealand land or goods situated in New Zealand; remote and digital services supplied to non-residents (design, prototyping, SaaS, technical support) are zero-rated under s 11(1)(mc) and related provisions. The exporter must establish the non-resident status of the recipient, the foreign performance or export of goods, and the absence of use in New Zealand. Input tax attributable to zero-rated export supplies is fully creditable, and a cash refund is payable when the registered exporter is in a net input-tax position.

  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical departure of the goods from New Zealand to the overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/foreign-exchange confirmation (SWIFT MT103 or NZ bank foreign-currency receipt) evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice expressly stating 'Zero-rated for GST (export)' and detailing the goods, value, HS tariff classification, and overseas consignee

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance regime, mandatory digital stamping, continuous transaction controls, or API-based tax clearance. E-invoicing is voluntary and is promoted through the New Zealand e-Invoicing framework based on Peppol standards (OASIS UBL 2.1 and PINT). Since 1 April 2022 central government agencies accept Peppol e-invoices for B2G transactions. All GST-registered persons must file returns electronically via myIR (GST101 or GST101A), selecting monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly filing periods. Tax invoice compliance under s 24 of the GST Act 1985 requires a tax invoice to be issued within 28 days of the time of supply for taxable supplies above NZD 50; simplified tax invoices are available for supplies below NZD 1,000 exclusive of GST; buyer-created tax invoices are permitted only with IRD approval; all e-invoice and tax records must be retained for seven years.

Audit scrutiny: Inland Revenue cross-matches GST returns with NZ Customs export entries, shipping and airfreight manifests, bank and payment-service-provider FX flows, and commercial documentation; IRD may issue formal information requests under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and conduct site visits to verify the physical export and the existence of the overseas customer. If zero-rating is disallowed, the supply is reassessed at 15% GST with use-of-money interest (UOMI) running from the original return due date. Civil shortfall penalties under Part IX of the Tax Administration Act 1994 are: 20% of the tax shortfall for lack of reasonable care; 10% for an unacceptable tax position; 50% for an abusive tax position; and 100% for evasion or a similar act, with possible escalation for repeated conduct. The standard four-year reassessment window is extended without limitation where fraud or evasion is involved. Criminal liability: knowingly making a false export declaration to Inland Revenue or NZ Customs is prosecutable under s 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the Customs and Excise Act 2018, exposing a body corporate to fines up to NZD 250,000 and responsible individuals to imprisonment for up to five years; directors and officers may be held personally liable. Repayment of the clawed-back GST does not discharge criminal liability, and successful prosecution may also result in forfeiture of goods and director disqualification orders.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account under an open and liberalised regime. New Zealand maintains no material exchange controls, no prior approval requirements for foreign investment in the manufacturing sector, and no restrictions on the repatriation of dividends, branch remittances, interest, royalties, or loan repayments. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, portfolio flows, and offshore borrowing, are freely permitted subject only to general anti-money laundering obligations, Overseas Investment Act screening for significant business assets or sensitive land, and balance-of-payments reporting requirements to the Reserve Bank. For a custom mechanical keyboard keycap manufacturer (ISIC 3240 / NAICS 334118), no sector-specific FX licence is required, and profit remittance to non-resident shareholders may be executed freely via commercial banks in NZD or any freely convertible currency without exchange control approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. New Zealand imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no timeline for repatriation of export receipts. Exporters may retain export earnings in foreign currency accounts at registered New Zealand banks or offshore accounts, and may convert to NZD at freely negotiated market rates. This creates zero cash-flow friction for the NZ Ltd entity's global e-commerce sales routed through Shopify Payments and Stripe, and no compliance calendar or conversion obligation is triggered for receipts from the sale of custom mechanical keyboard keycaps.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for fully offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business) acting as the primary treasury anchor. These providers do not hold a New Zealand banking licence under the Reserve Bank deposit-taking framework and generally cannot offer ESAS settlement access, domestic direct debit mandates, or full NZD clearing. Some, such as Wise, operate with a New Zealand financial service provider registration and can offer NZD receipt routes using partner banks, which supports low-value e-commerce disbursements only. For the target entity, the legally robust architecture is a hybrid: a tier-1 New Zealand bank account for all settlement-grade liquidity and capital transactions, supplemented by an FMA-registered authorised payment provider for cross-border currency conversion and vendor payments in jurisdictions where the bank's correspondent network is less efficient.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a non-resident shareholder capital injection into the NZ Ltd: (a) execute a SWIFT MT103 wire from the investor's offshore bank to the receiving New Zealand bank's SWIFT BIC using the beneficiary's BIC and NZD-denominated account number; (b) in the remittance advice, cite the correct Reserve Bank of New Zealand balance-of-payments purpose code – 'Direct Investment: Equity Capital' (BOP code 3410) for share subscription, or 'Intercompany Lending' (BOP code 3440) for shareholder loans; (c) include the NZ company's IRD number and the bank-assigned Customer Owner Identification Number (COIN) as remittance references; (d) for investment amounts exceeding the Overseas Investment Act screening threshold (currently NZD 100 million for significant business assets, or for sensitive land transactions), file an Overseas Investment Office consent application before remittance; for a low-capital custom keycap manufacturer, no OIO consent is ordinarily required; (e) provide the bank with a certified share register, signed subscription agreement, board resolution, and a declaration that the funds originate from a legitimate commercial source. The NZD is freely convertible, so the inward remittance will be credited immediately in NZD, or in the original currency if a foreign-currency account is used, with no surrender or escrow period.Trade settlements for the procurement of PBT resin, double-shot mould tooling, and injection-moulding equipment may be executed via: (a) standard SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers through all tier-1 New Zealand banks, using the 'Goods Trade – Import of Raw Materials' BOP classification (code 4100); (b) commercial documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700 form) opened by the New Zealand bank in favour of resin suppliers or mould manufacturers in China, Taiwan, or Japan, with sight or usance tenor (typically D/P 120 days) compliant with UCP 600 and Incoterms 2020 rules; (c) e-commerce settlement flows from Shopify Payments and Stripe are captured into the NZD operational account via the Reserve Bank's real-time gross settlement system (ESAS – Exchange Settlement Account System), with standard T+1 payout cycles; (d) high-volume disbursements to suppliers in Asia may be batched through the bank's online payments portal using SWIFT MT101 instructions to optimise FX conversion rates; and (e) all disbursements over NZD 50,000 are subject to enhanced counterparty due diligence in line with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.Outright forward foreign exchange contracts (FX forwards) for USD/NZD, EUR/NZD, and CNH/NZD with tenors of up to 12 months to fix accounts receivable and payable conversion rates.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) executed offshore for currencies for which no deliverable NZD market exists within New Zealand, used exclusively to hedge emerging-market procurement exposures such as CNY/NDF positions.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) to hedge foreign-currency-denominated debt or capital injections and align interest cash flows with the NZD functional currency.Zero-cost collars and vanilla FX options (call/put structures) providing asymmetric downside protection for highly variable e-commerce settlement volumes.FX swaps (spot-to-forward and forward-to-forward) executed through the tier-1 bank's treasury desk to efficiently roll over foreign-currency working capital positions without residual market risk.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for fully offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business) acting as the primary treasury anchor. These providers do not hold a New Zealand banking licence under the Reserve Bank deposit-taking framework and generally cannot offer ESAS settlement access, domestic direct debit mandates, or full NZD clearing. Some, such as Wise, operate with a New Zealand financial service provider registration and can offer NZD receipt routes using partner banks, which supports low-value e-commerce disbursements only. For the target entity, the legally robust architecture is a hybrid: a tier-1 New Zealand bank account for all settlement-grade liquidity and capital transactions, supplemented by an FMA-registered authorised payment provider for cross-border currency conversion and vendor payments in jurisdictions where the bank's correspondent network is less efficient.
