Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand, headquartered at 55 Featherston Street, Wellington; e-services portal myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz. Company incorporation, NZBN issuance, and annual return filing are administered by the New Zealand Companies Office (https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz), which transmits registration data to Inland Revenue.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — NZ-incorporated entity: incorporate online via the Companies Office electronic register; an Inland Revenue Department (IRD) number and a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) are issued automatically upon registration. Step 2 — Non-resident or foreign-incorporated entity: submit Form IR742 (Application for IRD Number for a Non-Resident Company) to Inland Revenue, attaching certified certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, the shareholders and directors register, and a statement of New Zealand-source activities; lodgement may be effected electronically through myIR or by a New Zealand tax agent. Step 3 — GST registration: file Form IR356 or apply via myIR, nominating the filing frequency (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly); non-resident suppliers of remote services use the myIR non-resident registration path and may register with a non-resident business address. Step 4 — The statutory IRD number is an 8-9 digit identifier (format nnn-nnn-nnn) and must be quoted on all tax invoices, GST returns, customs export declarations, and income tax filings; the full procurement timeline is 1-5 business days for the IRD number and immediate issuance of the NZBN upon incorporation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The CK_IC International Company is a limited-liability company assigned the Tax-Exempt-Zone profile. Under the territorial operation of the Income Tax Act 2007, income derived from offshore sources (international student placement fees and golden visa residence advisory fees invoiced to non-resident clients located outside New Zealand) is not chargeable to New Zealand income tax where the company is not a New Zealand tax resident (i.e., control and management is exercised offshore, or a double-tax-agreement tie-breaker deems it resident in another jurisdiction) or, if resident, where the income is exempt foreign-sourced income. New Zealand-source income — including commissions invoiced to New Zealand educational institutions and fees from clients physically present in New Zealand — remains within the New Zealand tax net and is taxed at the corporate rate of 28% unless the Look-Through Company election applies.

Available election forms

Available elections under New Zealand law: (i) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, allowing income, expenditure, and tax credits to flow through to shareholders (available to companies with five or fewer qualifying New Zealand-resident shareholder individuals or trustees); (ii) treaty non-resident election: where a double-tax-agreement tie-breaker determines residency in another state, the company may be treated as a non-resident for New Zealand tax purposes; (iii) grey-list and active-business exemptions under the Controlled Foreign Company rules (subparts CQ and EX) to prevent attribution of foreign income where the CK_IC holds offshore subsidiaries; (iv) hybrid and branch mismatch ordering elections under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018; (v) GST statutory elections: voluntary GST registration for non-resident suppliers, the going-concern zero-rating election under section 11(1)(m) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, and the export-evidence election under section 11A(4) permitting zero-rating of exported services where the supplier holds the prescribed non-resident evidence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period. The threshold applies to a person carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand and to a non-resident supplier of remote services (including online advisory, education placement, and immigration consulting) to New Zealand-resident consumers, measured by the value of supplies made to New Zealand consumers. Once the threshold is exceeded, or where there is a reasonable expectation that it will be exceeded in the next 12 months, registration is compulsory and must be applied for within 21 days of the end of the month in which the threshold was crossed. Voluntary registration is available below the threshold, including for non-residents wishing to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies where goods are exported from New Zealand under section 11(1)(a) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, with physical removal from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply and retention by the supplier of the full export documentation pack. Services (international student placement and golden visa residence advisory): zero-rating applies under section 11A(1)(a) where the services are supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, the services are not supplied in connection with New Zealand land or goods situate in New Zealand, and the recipient does not acquire the services for personal consumption while physically present in New Zealand. Services physically rendered in New Zealand to clients present in New Zealand, and commission charges invoiced to New Zealand educational institutions, are standard-rated at 15%. The supplier must hold sufficient evidence of the recipient's non-resident status at the time of supply to sustain the zero-rating claim. The 180-day inward remittance benchmark is applied as the compliance standard for export proceeds substantiation; New Zealand legislation does not impose a separate foreign-exchange repatriation statute, and zero-rating evidence operates through the 28-day export window and the applicable GST return timelines.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Electronic Export Declaration (Tranzit/JETTR transaction set) bearing the Unique Consignment Reference (UCR) and validated export entry number
  • Original Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin matching the export declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank-issued Foreign Exchange conversion record, including SWIFT MT103 credit advice, showing receipt of export proceeds from the offshore client in foreign currency
  • Offshore-client verification pack: certified copy of passport or government-issued identification, non-resident declaration, and written confirmation of the client's location outside New Zealand at the time the service was performed
  • Service delivery evidence: signed consulting engagement letter, placement confirmation from the New Zealand education provider, visa lodgement receipt, and dated advisory deliverables

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance API reporting, or digital stamping of invoices. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates on the Peppol PINT interoperability framework via accredited New Zealand access points; the Inland Revenue Department myIR system accepts standard GST returns (Form GST101) and does not perform per-invoice fiscal validation. Statutory tax-invoice rules apply: a standard tax invoice must be issued within 28 days of the time of supply for taxable supplies exceeding NZD 50 (GST-inclusive); a simplified tax invoice may be used for supplies between NZD 50 and NZD 1,000; tax invoices and zero-rating evidence packs must be retained for 7 years and produced to Inland Revenue on demand. E-invoicing via the Peppol network requires a Peppol ID and an access-point service contract, but remains a commercial choice rather than a statutory mandate.

Inland Revenue operates a dedicated export zero-rating compliance program and cross-references GST returns with New Zealand Customs Service export data and banking records. Where an export claim fails substantiation (e.g., no export declaration, invoice raised after expiry of the 28-day export window, payment received from a New Zealand bank account or New Zealand-issued payment card, or evidence that the client was in New Zealand when the service was performed), the 15% GST is clawed back as output tax, together with use-of-money interest at the prescribed Inland Revenue rate (periodically reset; approximately 10.8% per annum as at 2025) and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% for evasion or fraudulent intent, with criminal prosecution available under sections 143A and 143B. For the migration-advisory product line, the general anti-avoidance provisions (sections BG1 and GA1 of the Income Tax Act 2007) and the promoter-penalty regime (section 141EB of the Tax Administration Act 1994) may be invoked if the CK_IC markets or implements a zero-rating or tax-exempt structure that is artificial or lacking economic substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand legally accepts its IMF Article VIII obligations, providing full convertibility on both current and capital-account transactions. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a free-floating currency with no administrative convertibility approvals, no surrender requirements, and no distinction between current and capital account in terms of FX availability. Profit remittances from a Cook Islands International Company (IC) operating in or through New Zealand are freely transferable abroad, subject only to domestic taxation rules, including non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) or approved issuer levy (AIL) on dividends, interest, royalties, and certain service fees. The Overseas Investment Act 2005 may impose prior consent for certain capital inflows into sensitive land or significant business assets, but it does not create any FX convertibility impediment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratios, no forced delivery period, and no requirement to repatriate export or service-fee receipts into New Zealand within a prescribed timeline. A Cook Islands IC earning student placement and immigration advisory service fee income may retain its foreign currency proceeds in offshore and onshore foreign-currency accounts indefinitely, but all funds held at New Zealand registered banks are subject to AML/CFT record keeping and taxation disclosure obligations under the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Financial Transactions Reporting Act 2022 (as applicable).

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the sole monetary and financial stability authority; for foreign exchange and trade, there is no separate Foreign Exchange Administration body. The Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) jointly administer the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, which governs all FX-related financial institutions and reporting entities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium — offshore digital electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, MultiPass) are viable as supplementary payment and FX conversion rails only; they are not licensed under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 as banks and cannot provide fiduciary escrow or client trust accounts. To offer NZD-denominated services, an offshore EMI must register as a financial institution under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and appoint a New Zealand face or agent. For a Cook Islands International Company with immigration advisory activity, an EMI cannot replace an NZ-incumbent bank, because the Immigration Advisers Licensing Act 2007 requires client funds to be held in a New Zealand trust account with a registered NZ financial institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from the Cook Islands IC parent to its New Zealand branch or subsidiary should be routed via SWIFT MT103 using standard purpose codes: 'TREA' for treasury operations, 'INTD' for intercompany financing, or 'SECU' for securities-related capital injection. The bank will require a board resolution, a signed capital contribution agreement, and an Overseas Investment Office (OIO) clearance letter if the funds are to be applied toward sensitive land or a significant business asset (NZ$100 million threshold) under the Overseas Investment Act 2005. For currencies such as CNY, INR, or BRL with capital-control considerations, the bank may require a deliverable or non-deliverable forward to fix the NZD cost; no repatriation restrictions apply on the equity once lawfully injected. Verification includes a notarised schedule of beneficial owners, certified tax status of the IC, and a statement demonstrating that the funds derive from legitimate commercial activity.Outbound disbursements to educational institutions and overseas agents are normally cleared through SWIFT MT103 messages via the NZ bank's correspondent banking network. For very large transactions (above NZ$50,000) involving pre-payment of tuition or escrow release, banks offer SWIFT MT100, MT202/MT205 cover transfers, but MT103 is the standard because it carries full remitter and beneficiary details for AML/CFT screening. Where a performance or advance-payment guarantee is requested by a partner university, a commercial letter of credit (LC) may be issued under UCP 600, provided the supporting invoice, enrolment verification, and proof of insurance are presented. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium — offshore digital electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, MultiPass) are viable as supplementary payment and FX conversion rails only; they are not licensed under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 as banks and cannot provide fiduciary escrow or client trust accounts. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory resident withholding tax (RWT) on dividends is 33% at the top marginal rate for non-resident shareholders where the dividend is not fully imputed. For dividends with full imputation credits, no further withholding is required for resident companies. Non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends is 30% (reduced to 15% under the New Zealand–China DTA and generally 15% for other treaty jurisdictions with a 15% portfolio rate; 0% for a 10%+ corporate shareholder under most treaties, subject to limitation of benefits).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand TP documentation follows OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines: contemporaneous documentation is required by Inland Revenue when the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZD 10 million (local file master file benchmarks aligning with OECD Action 13). Master File is required where group revenue exceeds NZD 1 billion and specified transaction thresholds are met; Local File is required for transactions exceeding NZD 5 million for related-party service/sales transactions, NZD 1 million for related-party financing, and NZD 1 million for related-party IP royalty transactions. Inland Revenue's transfer pricing dispute threshold is NZD 6 million of gross income adjustment per taxpayer in relation to the dispute, and documentation must be prepared by the due date of the return.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime akin to the UAE. However, general anti-avoidance rules (BG1 Income Tax Act 2007) and the 'business test' under tax residence law require commercial substance. A CK_IC International Company claiming non-resident status must not be managed and controlled from New Zealand, must have a real foreign place of business and maintain board meetings and strategic control overseas. All arrangements must reflect economic substance (employees, premises, actual decision-making and risk assumption) to avoid being recharacterised as a New Zealand-resident company or as a sham.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Tax Administration Act 1994 (evasion or similar offence, sections 143A and 143B): fine up to NZD 50,000 for individuals and NZD 250,000 for companies, plus up to 7 years imprisonment for individuals. Civil shortfall penalties up to 150% of the tax shortfall for abusive tax positions, plus use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for knowingly assisting in tax evasion, and can face personal criminal liability, pecuniary penalties, imprisonment up to 7 years, and obligation to pay the company's tax shortfall under civil penalty rules. Travel bans may be imposed where assets are dissipated or the individual is likely to leave New Zealand.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the company as a New Zealand tax resident, assessment on worldwide income at 28%, imposition of shortfall penalties up to 100-150%, loss of foreign income tax credits, and potential cancellation of registration under the Companies Act 1993 and Financial Reporting Act 2013.

Director personal liability: Directors who permit operation of a shell company with false declarations may be disqualified from managing companies under the Companies Act 1993, personally liable for unpaid tax under the tax avoidance recovery provisions, and subject to criminal penalties for false declarations to Inland Revenue.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML/CFT) and Unlicensed Immigration Advice

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Immigration Advisers Licensing Act 2007: a fine of up to NZD 100,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 7 years for unlicensed immigration advice. AML/CFT penalties: civil penalties up to NZD 2 million for the body corporate for each contravention, and criminal fines up to NZD 400,000; Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) must be filed and a compliance officer appointed.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers can be prosecuted personally under the AML/CFT Act (individual liability up to NZD 200,000 fine and/or imprisonment), subject to personal pecuniary penalties, orders for civil restitution, travel bans in connection with criminal proceedings, and disqualification from immigration advisory practice.
Transfer Pricing and Cross-Border Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of taxable income under s GC8 Income Tax Act 2007, applying the arm's length standard with penalties up to 150% for transfer pricing adjustments or documentation failure. Interest limitation rules may disallow interest deductions entirely for certain excessive related-party debt.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign transfer pricing documentation that is false or misleading can face penalties for aiding and abetting, personal liability for the shortfall penalty, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion, including imprisonment up to 7 years.
Fraudulent Profit Repatriation and Dividend Withholding Evasion

Statutory sanction: A non-resident withholding tax deficiency is assessed at the statutory 30% dividend rate (or 15% interest/royalty rate) plus penalty tax of 150% for evasion and use-of-money interest. A company that fails to withhold NRWT is jointly and severally liable for the tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable under the Companies Act for causing the company to distribute assets where the company is insolvent or where the distribution is made without regard to tax obligations; personal liability for NRWT arises as the withholding agent; criminal liability applies for knowingly evading withholding tax (fines up to NZD 250,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years).
