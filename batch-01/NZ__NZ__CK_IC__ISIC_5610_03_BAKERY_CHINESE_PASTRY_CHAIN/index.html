VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months or reasonably expected in the following 12 months for resident businesses; nil threshold for non-resident persons carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand (s51(1)(c) GST Act 1985); separate registration and collection rules apply to non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value imported goods under sections 8B and 80D of the GST Act 1985

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available to GST-registered persons for: (a) goods exported from New Zealand by the supplier within 28 days of supply (s11(1)(a)); (b) supplies to customs-controlled areas for export and supplies of certain ships and aircraft (s11(1)(c)-(h)); (c) services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed, where the services are not supplied to a person in New Zealand for a taxable activity carried on in New Zealand (s11(1)(m)); and (d) remote services supplied to non-resident recipients at the place of residence or business location outside New Zealand (s8B and s11A). For a Chinese pastry bakery chain, zero-rating covers frozen bakery goods physically exported and overseas franchise master-license services supplied to offshore franchisees; domestic retail/POS sales attract 15% GST.

  • NZ Customs Service Trade Single Window (TSW) Electronic Export Declaration / Customs Export Transaction record
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of bakery goods from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds in a New Zealand bank account

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, revenue authority API validation, or digital stamping regime exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary for B2B and statutory for B2G suppliers using the Peppol BIS 3.0 network (mandated for central government procurement since April 2022). GST returns are filed electronically via myIR on a monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly cycle; Inland Revenue does not pre-clear individual invoices.

Inland Revenue may retrospectively disallow zero-rating and reassess output tax at 15% where export documentation is deficient, goods do not physically leave New Zealand, services are enjoyed in New Zealand, or proceeds are not repatriated within the deemed commercial timeframe. Civil shortfall penalties apply under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 75% for abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or fraudulent GST refund claims; use-of-money interest (UOMI) accrues on unpaid or incorrectly refunded amounts. Assessment time limits are 4 years generally and 7 years for evasion or abusive tax positions (s108 TAA 1994). Criminal sanctions for GST and export fraud include fines up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment under ss143A and 143B TAA 1994, and false or misleading export declaration offences under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 may trigger additional penalties and forfeiture of goods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully convertible, freely floating exchange rate regime with no exchange controls administered by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand. Both current account transactions (trade in goods and services, royalties, franchise fees, dividends) and capital account transactions (equity injections, loan repayments, foreign direct investment, capital repatriation) are convertible into NZD without prior regulatory approval under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021. Practical remittance hurdles are limited to: (i) non-resident withholding tax (NRWT 30% default, treaty reductions available) on outbound dividends, interest, and royalties; (ii) the Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) mechanism which may reduce effective related-party interest withholding to 2% where statutory conditions are satisfied; and (iii) AML/CFT source-of-funds verification applied at the banking layer. There is no capital gains repatriation tax, no exchange surrender obligation, and no balance-of-payments reporting mandate for private sector transactions. The NZD is a G10 freely traded currency and the central bank does not shadow-peg or impose convertibility bands.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand abolished exchange controls and export proceeds surrender requirements in 1984. There is no statutory timeline within which export receipts must be repatriated to New Zealand, no mandated conversion ratio into NZD, and no requirement to surrender foreign currency proceeds to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand. The bakery franchise and central kitchen operator may invoice and retain funds in any negotiable currency, may hold foreign currency accounts with registered NZ banks, and may net intercompany trade flows, subject only to transfer-pricing documentation, tax clearances, and the bank's AML transaction monitoring framework.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium (secondary rail only; not suitable as primary treasury bank). New Zealand does not operate a discrete electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime comparable to the UK or EU. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise, Currencycloud) generally operate in New Zealand as registered financial institutions under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act) and must be supervised by the Department of Internal Affairs or the Financial Markets Authority; they cannot access Payment NZ's Core Payment System (CTP) settlement infrastructure or issue native NZD account numbers without partnering with a registered New Zealand bank. For a Chinese pastry chain International Company, an offshore EMI may be used as a supplementary rail for high-volume low-value payouts and cross-border franchise royalty sweeps, but it must not be the sole treasury clearing mechanism. Key legal constraints: cross-border EMIs must comply with the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, obtain money remitter registration where they hold or transmit funds in New Zealand, and maintain segregated client accounts with a registered NZ bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for shareholder capital injection from China/Hong Kong parent: the International Company's New Zealand bank receives a cleared wire via SWIFT MT103 (with bank-to-bank MT202 for the corresponding leg). The remittance instruction must state the International Company's legal name, IRD number, and the reference field 'Capital contribution - share subscription - [IC entity name] - pursuant to board resolution dated [date]'. The payer must also transmit the corporate resolution, share subscription document, and source-of-funds evidence under the bank's wire-transfer information requirements implementing FATF Recommendation 16 (travel rule). Because New Zealand imposes no central bank foreign-exchange approval, purpose codes are not mandated by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand; the bank applies internal purpose categories such as 'Capital Injection' or 'Equity Contribution', and the transaction is recorded in the Companies Office share registry. Inbound funds can be converted to NZD at the interbank rate or held in a foreign-currency account (CNH/USD) to preserve optionality for future import payables. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on New Zealand-source dividends is 30% for non-treaty residents. Under an applicable double tax agreement, the rate is generally reduced to 15% (or 5% for a portfolio shareholder; certain treaties may provide 0%-5% for companies with substantial holding). Fully imputed dividends may qualify for a reduced 5% rate under domestic law if the non-resident holds at least 80% and satisfies the imputation election requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

A company's tax return and annual payment of residual income tax are due on the 7th day of the month following the balance date (e.g., 7 July for a 31 March balance date). Provisional tax is generally payable in three instalments (28 August, 15 January, and 7 May for a standard balance date).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand has no statutory base-level documentation threshold; however, Inland Revenue expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZ$10 million, or where individual category transactions exceed NZ$1 million. A Master File is generally prepared when group turnover exceeds NZ$50 million. Documentation must be provided within 30 days of an IRD request.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have separate Economic Substance Regulations like Crown Dependencies. Instead, substance is enforced through: (1) tax residence rules under the Income Tax Act 2007; (2) transfer pricing rules under s GC6-GC14; (3) the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG1); and (4) the OECD Multilateral Instrument for treaty abuse. A non-resident International Company claiming reduced WHT rates must demonstrate real management and control, staff, and premises in its claimed residence jurisdiction. A passive royalty shell without this substance will be denied treaty relief, and the arrangement may be recharacterised with full corporate tax, interest, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Misstatement of Taxable Income

Statutory sanction: Under s 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994, evasion with intent carries a fine of up to NZ$50,000 (first offence) or NZ$100,000 (subsequent) and imprisonment up to 5 years. A shortfall penalty of 150% of the tax avoided applies (s 141E). Inland Revenue may also assess GST penalties (up to 150%) and late payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly participated in creating false returns are personally liable for shortfall penalties and can be prosecuted criminally. The court can impose directors' disqualification for up to 10 years, travel bans under the Insolvency Act, and civil recovery of corporate assets under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue will apply s BG1 to void the arrangement. The royalty income may be recharacterised as New Zealand-source income of a taxable PE; corporate tax at 28% plus non-resident withholding tax at 30% (or treaty rate if applicable) is assessed. Penalties for abusive tax positions are up to 100% of the tax shortfall; evasion penalties up to 150%. The company may also be struck off under the Companies Act 1993 for failing to maintain a registered office or respond to Registrar enquiries.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell company face personal liability for the company's tax liabilities if they allowed the company to trade while insolvent (Companies Act 1993, s 289). In addition, they can be personally assessed for shortfall penalties and prosecuted for aiding in producing misleading returns. Travel bans, property restraint, and up to 5 years' imprisonment may be imposed.
Anti-Money Laundering / Counter-Financing of Terrorism Breach

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties of up to NZ$2 million per contravention (up to NZ$4 million for a subsequent contravention). Concurrent criminal liability under the Crimes Act 1961 for money laundering (knowingly dealing with criminal proceeds) carries imprisonment up to 14 years. Asset forfeiture can be ordered under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under s 60 of the AML/CFT Act if they knew or should have known of the contravention. Criminal liability for money laundering can result in imprisonment up to 7 years (or 14 years for knowing receipt of property derived from serious crime). Directors may be disqualified from managing companies and may have personal assets restrained pending forfeiture.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Unsupported Royalty Payments

Statutory sanction: The payer is liable for the NRWT itself if not deducted. The full 30% withholding tax on cross-border payments is collected from the New Zealand resident payer (or PE), plus late payment penalties and interest. Criminal liability for failure to account is a separate offence under the Tax Administration Act, carrying imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow NRWT to go unpaid are jointly and severally liable for the tax, penalties, and interest. They face civil recovery, director disqualification, and criminal prosecution with imprisonment. Personal asset seizures and travel bans may be ordered by the court.
