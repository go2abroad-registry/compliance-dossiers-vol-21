Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, a business unit of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), operating the New Zealand Register of Companies via the online portal at companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), ss 12–19 (application, registration and certificate of incorporation), s 23 (registered office), s 24 (address for service), s 36 (consideration for shares), s 42 (allotment of shares), ss 151–153 (appointment and consent of directors), s 159 (filing obligations); Income Tax Act 2007, ss YC 1–YC 2 (company residence) and ss CW 2–CW 61 (exempt foreign-sourced income); Tax Administration Act 1994, s 111 (registration).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of a Company (section 12 of the Companies Act 1993), filed online through the JIL (Join In Line) electronic form, accompanied by consents to act as director, shareholder allotment particulars and registered office information.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 21 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Companies Act 1993; subscriber shares may be issued at NZD 1.00 per share or for nil premium; no escrow account is required. The tailored International Company vehicle (ELF_CUSTOM_CK_IC) may be capitalized with one ordinary share of NZD 1.00.

Funding & unlock path: No civil-defense permit is required for an office-based digital publisher; however, under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 (HASWA) the company must implement a worker-participation system and maintain a hazard register. If any premises are leased, the company must obtain a Building Warrant of Fitness (BWoF), annual fire-safety compliance certification from Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ), and an approved fire evacuation scheme under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand (Fire Safety, Fire Evacuation Procedures, and Evacuation Schemes) Regulations 2018. WorkSafe New Zealand does not issue a separate occupational clearance for ICT-class businesses.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Companies Act 1993; subscriber shares may be issued at NZD 1.00 per share or for nil premium; no escrow account is required. Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — no industrial licensing board mandates a permit for ISIC 5813 digital magazine publishing. The territorial authority (city or district council) under the Building Act 2004 and the Resource Management Act 1991 would have jurisdiction only if the company later leases physical office or warehouse premises; MBIE exercises no ex-ante licensing control over digital publishing content or platforms.

Category: No operating licence exists for digital magazine publishing in New Zealand. Digital subscription journals and sponsored-media placements are treated as ordinary commercial internet content and are not subject to broadcast licensing by the Broadcasting Standards Authority or NZ On Air. Voluntary content classification under the Films, Videos, and Publications Classification Act 1993 may be undertaken but is not a statutory precondition to lawful operation.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (Te Tari Taake), administered by the Commissioner of Inland Revenue; national online tax portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/), with New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) interoperability and a single integrated IRD number registry for income tax and GST.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity, including a Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC), obtains its New Zealand IRD number (TIN) by lodging Form IR742 (Non-individual IRD Number Application) with Inland Revenue, or via the myIR online registration channel, accompanied by a certified certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents or company extract, registered agent and office details, director and shareholder identity documentation, and evidence of foreign economic substance. If the entity will carry on a taxable activity in New Zealand, it must first register as an overseas company with the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993. GST registration under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 is then completed online through myIR using the issued IRD number; there is no separate GST/VAT identifier distinct from the IRD number. A tax-exempt offshore vehicle such as the CK_IC must still register for GST when it makes taxable supplies in New Zealand or supplies remote digital services exceeding NZD 60,000 to New Zealand-resident consumers in any 12-month period; voluntary GST registration is also available to enable zero-rating and input credit recovery.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not apply a US-style check-the-box election. The available statutory election and classification mechanisms include: (i) the look-through company (LTC) election for close companies under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, which flows income, expenses and tax credits through to shareholders; (ii) GST accounting-basis elections under section 19A of the GST Act 1985 (payments basis for smaller businesses and certain non-resident businesses); (iii) controlled foreign company (CFC) non-attribution and grey-list elections under Subpart CQ and section YD 2 of the Income Tax Act 2007; and (iv) hybrid-mismatch elections and safe-harbour exclusions under Part FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 implementing OECD BEPS. A Cook Islands International Company is not entitled to a transparency election; its New Zealand classification is determined exclusively by statutory tax-residence and permanent-establishment rules, and central management and control in New Zealand would render it a New Zealand tax resident without elective mitigation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 — mandatory registration when annual taxable supplies, including remote or digital services, reach or exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration is available without threshold to non-resident suppliers and zero-rated exporters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports are zero-rated under section 10(1)(b) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the goods are physically exported by the supplier within 28 days of the time of supply. Services, including digital magazine subscriptions and sponsored media placements sold through the portal, are zero-rated under section 11A(1)(k) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where supplied to a non-resident recipient outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not supplied directly in connection with New Zealand land or goods. Digital advertising and subscription revenue settled through Stripe and AdSense qualifies for 0% GST when the supplier retains electronic substantiation of recipient non-residence, including billing addresses, IP-geolocation data, payment-rail settlement records, and signed subscriber or advertiser contracts. Supplies of remote digital services to New Zealand-resident consumers remain subject to 15% GST under the cross-border remote-services rules. New Zealand imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline for export proceeds; the 180-day parameter recorded below is a prudent forex-realization and documentation-retention benchmark adopted by international compliance pipelines rather than a statutory requirement.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window (TRADES) — applicable to physical goods exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill with export endorsement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange bank settlement record
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Digital delivery logs, IP-geolocation records, subscription and advertiser contracts, and Stripe or AdSense settlement reports for zero-rated remote services

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based pre-clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates on the PEPPOL framework under the New Zealand e-Invoicing initiative. GST returns, invoices and payments are administered through myIR without any government fiscal authorization or pre-approval stamp; statutory tax-invoice particulars required by section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 remain mandatory.

Inland Revenue conducts a risk-based export zero-rating review program, triangulating New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS) export departures, GST returns, payment-rail data and bank remittance records. Where zero-rating is not substantiated, the output GST is clawed back at 15% with use-of-money interest from the original due date, plus late payment penalties under section 139 of the Tax Administration Act 1994. Shortfall penalties under sections 141A to 141D of that Act escalate by culpability: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 20% for an unacceptable tax position (40% where both accumulations apply), 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or for knowingly providing false or altered documents. Criminal prosecution under sections 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994 may follow for deliberate GST filing offences, potentially including imprisonment for evasion. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims may further result in cancellation of voluntary GST registration, denial of refund offsets, and a permanent adverse compliance-risk rating on the taxpayer's Inland Revenue profile.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has no general exchange-control legislation. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. Profit remittances, dividends, royalties, interest, and loan repayments can be freely transferred abroad without exchange-control approval, subject to non-resident withholding tax, the approved issuer levy, and standard anti-money-laundering / FATCA / CRS reporting. The practical means of exercising convertibility is through registered commercial banks offering NZD and foreign-currency accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender rule applies in New Zealand. Exporters are free to retain export receipts in foreign-currency bank accounts, manage them through hedging instruments, or convert them into NZD at their discretion. There is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Filing obligations arise only under tax and AML/CTF frameworks, not under foreign-exchange surrender rules.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — central bank and prudential regulator for registered banks; exchange-control functions are not separately administered by any government body because New Zealand operates a liberalised, no-approval foreign-exchange regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary payout and aggregation rail, Low as a primary treasury banking rail. New Zealand does not maintain a distinct e-money institution (EMI) licence; an offshore digital EMI may only service NZD flows if it holds funds with a registered New Zealand bank or operates through a licensed non-bank deposit-taker arrangement. Thus Stripe and AdSense payout aggregation is viable, but escrow, capital-injection, and tax-sensitive clearing flows should be settled through locally registered commercial banks to satisfy the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) rate on dividends paid by a New Zealand-resident company to a non-resident shareholder is 30%; this is reduced to 15% where the dividend is fully imputed under the New Zealand imputation system and the shareholder has provided a valid IR372 shareholder dividend certificate; treaty rates may further reduce the rate (e.g., 15% under the NZ–Australia and NZ–US double tax agreements for portfolio dividends, and 5% under the NZ–Australia DTA for dividends paid to a company holding 80% or more of the voting power). For the CK_IC International Company repatriating profits to non-resident shareholders, no New Zealand NRWT attaches where the dividend is declared out of foreign-sourced exempt income and the company is not a New Zealand tax resident.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border related-party transactions in excess of NZ$1 million annually mandate the preparation of a transfer pricing Local File in New Zealand; a Master File is mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated global revenue exceeds NZ$1 billion. The CK_IC's intercompany advertising inventory transactions, platform licensing fees and management charges must be benchmarked against these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: The CK_IC International Company is simultaneously exposed to (i) the New Zealand General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under sections BG 1 and GA 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, which voids any arrangement whose sole or dominant purpose is tax avoidance, and (ii) the Cook Islands Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) applicable to International Companies, which require that a company deriving income from relevant activities (including intellectual property holding, distribution and service centres, and digital content monetisation) demonstrate substantial activity, board management and physical presence in the Cook Islands. Failure to satisfy the ESR economic substance test for a zero-tax structure causes the entity to be treated as resident in a non-compliant jurisdiction, with consequential imposition of NZ and international anti-hybrid, CFC and controlled foreign corporation adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Deliberate Under-declaration of Digital Revenue

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under sections 141E and 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994; a deliberate tax shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax; evasion assessment plus use-of-money interest; criminal fine up to NZ$50,000 for a company and imprisonment for individuals for up to five years for knowingly filing a false tax return.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who knowingly participate in, or direct, the concealment of revenue are jointly and severally liable for the shortfall penalty and may be disqualified from company management, subjected to a travel ban by Inland Revenue on exit clearances, and imprisoned for up to five years; civil recovery proceedings may pierce the corporate veil to recover any diverted profits.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance (ESR Non-Compliance)

Statutory sanction: Under the Cook Islands International Companies Amendment Act and ESR framework, a fixed penalty of approximately US$10,000, plus US$20,000 for each successive year of non-compliance and up to two years' imprisonment for responsible officers; under New Zealand law, the GAAR re-characterises the arrangement and imposes a 100% abusive tax avoidance shortfall penalty on the full amount of tax thereby avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for the shortfall penalty under section ND 5 of the Tax Administration Act; each director is personally liable for the ESR annual penalties; a director of a non-compliant shell may face a travel ban, disqualification under the Companies Act 1993, and criminal prosecution for knowingly operating a sham entity.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Inadequate Documentation

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may reconstruct the taxable income under the arm's length principle and issue a reassessment with a shortfall penalty of 20% (not taking reasonable care) to 40% (taking an unacceptable tax position) of the additional tax, plus late payment penalties and use-of-money interest; deliberate TP suppression attracts a 100–150% penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false transfer pricing policies or destroy TP documentation face personal liability under the Tax Administration Act for assisting in tax avoidance, with civil penalties equal to the company's shortfall, director disqualification orders, and imprisonment for up to five years for falsifying records.
Money Laundering and AML/CFT Breach in Digital Payment Flows

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a company may be fined up to NZ$2 million for each compliance failure and an additional NZ$200,000 per director or senior manager; the Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze assets and impose civil penalty orders; severe breaches carry imprisonment for individuals of up to two years.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and directors face personal criminal liability for reckless AML/CFT non-compliance, including fines up to NZ$200,000 and imprisonment up to two years; courts may order travel bans and asset restraint orders, and a conviction disqualifies the director from managing any New Zealand company.
Abusive Treaty Shopping and False DTA Relief Claims

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue will deny the treaty benefit, reassess the full 30% dividend or 15% royalty/interest NRWT, apply a 150% evasion shortfall penalty, and charge use-of-money interest from the original payment date; the arrangement is void under the GAAR and any tax authority receiving the payment under the treaty may also impose a 25% penalty under the BEPS Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute or certify false treaty relief certificates are personally liable for the full underpaid withholding tax plus penalty, may be prosecuted for forgery under the Crimes Act 1961 (up to ten years' imprisonment), and face travel bans and extradition where cross-border fraudulent documents are involved; corporate veil-piercing remedies enable recovery from personal assets.
