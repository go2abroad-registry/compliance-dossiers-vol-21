Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement under New Zealand law. The company may be incorporated with a nominal subscribed capital of one (1) ordinary share per subscriber at NZ$1.00 each, issued as fully paid at incorporation. Consideration for the issue of shares must be known and received before allotment under s 73 of the Companies Act 1993; capital may be contributed in cash or in kind, and no capital duty or escrow deposit applies.

Funding & unlock path: No structural, civil-defence or occupational clearance is required for the non-resident shell company. If a liaison office or warehouse is opened in New Zealand: (i) Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) operational fire safety certification and an evacuation scheme under s 55 of the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017; (ii) Building Warrant of Fitness and compliance schedule under the Building Act 2004 for any occupied premises exceeding 100m²; (iii) WorkSafe New Zealand workplace notification and a written Health and Safety at Work policy, including first-aid provisions, hazard register and accident reporting under the Accident Compensation Act 2001; (iv) where racked storage exceeds 5m in height, a structural load inspection certificate from a Chartered Professional Engineer is required.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand; secure digital portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz; customs interface via New Zealand Customs Service

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated International Company applies for an Inland Revenue (IRD) number using form IR742 (Non-Resident Company Registration), supported by the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director and ultimate beneficial owner disclosures required under New Zealand AML/CFT rules, and a registered New Zealand address for service or an appointed New Zealand tax agent. Submission is made through the myIR portal or by an authorised tax agent; the IRD number is ordinarily issued within 2-10 business days. GST registration for non-resident companies is processed separately under the special non-resident GST registration regime, optionally through a New Zealand agent or branch, with GST returns filed on the standard return cycle.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. The available statutory election and classification mechanisms include: (i) voluntary GST registration before the compulsory NZD 60,000 turnover threshold; (ii) GST group election under s 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; (iii) GST branch election under s 56 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; (iv) functional currency election under s 7A of the Income Tax Act 2007; and (v) hybrid mismatch entity/arrangement treatment under Subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 implementing the OECD BEPS hybrid mismatch rules. An International Company structured as a non-resident company is outside the New Zealand income tax net for foreign-source income, provided central management and control is not exercised in New Zealand.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; voluntary registration permitted below this threshold; non-resident suppliers carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand are subject to the same compulsory registration threshold, with special rules for offshore remote-services suppliers

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods is available under s 11(1)(c) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within the statutory timeframes (generally within 28 days of delivery to the purchaser, or within 28 days after payment if the supplier does not make delivery) and retains prescribed evidence of export. Battery-grade lithium carbonate powder physically shipped to non-resident buyers qualifies if the export entry, shipping documents, and payment records align. Services to non-residents may qualify under s 11A (remote services) when the services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are not performed in New Zealand. The exporter must hold sufficient documentation to demonstrate the goods were actually exported, including transport documentation showing movement to an overseas destination and non-return to New Zealand.

  • Validated New Zealand Customs export entry (Customs Export Declaration lodged via the Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract describing battery-grade lithium carbonate powder and purchaser
  • Packing list, weight certificate, and grade/lot analysis certificate for the lithium carbonate
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment evidence confirming receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • GST zero-rating evidence log maintained per s 24B of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, including proof of export timing and destination

No real-time fiscal clearance, compulsory government clearance API, or digital stamping mandate exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the PEPPOL network via accredited service providers. GST-registered suppliers must issue compliant tax invoices under s 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, showing the supplier's GST number, invoice date, description and quantity of supplies, consideration, and GST amount; simplified tax invoices are permitted for supplies under NZD 1,000, and buyer-created tax invoices are allowed in limited circumstances. Returns are filed electronically through myIR on a one-month, two-month, or six-month cycle, with no government pre-clearance of individual invoices.

The Inland Revenue may audit export zero-rating claims within the standard four-year reassessment period, with no time limit where fraud or evasion is involved; exporters must retain substantiating records for seven years. Incorrect zero-rating gives rise to retrospective reassessment at the 15% GST rate, recovery of any refunded output GST, use-of-money interest from the original refund date, and shortfall penalties under s 141 of the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. Knowingly creating false export documentation or fraudulent zero-rating claims can additionally trigger criminal prosecution under s 143A and s 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994, with imprisonment of up to five years, fines, and debarment from future zero-rating privileges.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalized, free-floating currency regime with no exchange controls on either the current or capital account. Non-resident companies and offshore vehicles enjoy unrestricted conversion of NZD into foreign currency and vice versa; there are no surrender, repatriation, licensing, or prior-approval requirements for trade-related payments, capital injections, loan servicing, or profit remittance. The only gateway legislation is the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which screens non-resident acquisitions of 'sensitive' New Zealand land and significant business assets; it does not capture a treasury/procurement vehicle purchasing battery-grade lithium carbonate (ISIC 4662 / HS 2836.91) and does not impede ordinary trade settlement. Profit remittance and dividend flows are unrestricted from an FX standpoint; the only practical obstacles are fiscal, namely New Zealand non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on NZ-source dividends/interest and the approved issuer levy (AIL) alternative. For a Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC) classified as non-resident of New Zealand and not carrying on business through a permanent establishment in NZ, trading income from offshore lithium purchases and resales is not NZ-source income under section YD 4 of the Income Tax Act 2007.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export receipts, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to open a domiciliary foreign-currency account. Export proceeds and receivables in USD, EUR, CNY, AUD or any other currency may be retained offshore, netted against import payables, or converted at commercial banks at free-market rates. Residual compliance obligations are limited to: (i) bank reporting of balance-of-payments statistics to the Reserve Bank for transactions above NZD 50,000; (ii) AML/CTF record-keeping under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009; and (iii) transfer-pricing documentation for related-party commodity flows.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), the sole monetary authority and currency issuer for New Zealand and the Cook Islands, where New Zealand dollars are legal tender. No separate foreign-exchange administration exists; exchange-control regulations were abolished with the 1984/85 float and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 (consolidated by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021), so no ministerial or central-bank approval is required for foreign-currency transactions.

Box 10 Taxable income after deduction of carry-forward losses. Loss carry-forward for a non-resident IC requires 49% shareholder continuity across the loss year and the deduction year. Late filing penalty: NZD 250 for a company failing to file the IR4 by the due date; late payment penalty on unpaid RIT: 1% on the day after the due date, 4% on the 7th day, then 1% per month cumulative; use-of-money interest applies (currently 9.53% for underpayment in 2025, subject to quarterly change); repeated non-filing may result in default assessments and prosecution fines up to NZD 50,000.
Box 11 Tax on taxable income at 28%. Non-resident IC taxed only on NZ fixed establishment income at 28%; offshore procurement arbitrage profits carry 0% NZ tax. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory NZD threshold for Local File/Master File; CbC reporting is mandatory where consolidated group revenue exceeds NZD 680,000,000. Inland Revenue practice requires contemporaneous TP documentation for all material cross-border related-party transactions; transactions below NZD 1,000,000 per counterparty are generally treated as low risk. Commodity trading (including battery-grade lithium carbonate) must be benchmarked against arm's-length quoted market prices at the contract date.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) codifying substance requirements; instead the Commissioner applies the central management and control test (s. YD 2) to determine tax residence, and the general anti-avoidance rule (s. BG 1) with the associated reconstructive rule (s. GA 1). A Cook Islands IC that is 'managed and controlled' from New Zealand (board meetings, strategic decisions, or bank signatories located in NZ) is a NZ tax resident on worldwide income, notwithstanding its Cook Islands tax-exempt status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (s. 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994)

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction: imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine up to NZD 50,000 per tax type/period; civil shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax; plus use-of-money interest and compensatory interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly make or assist false returns may be prosecuted personally; on conviction they face imprisonment and fines; Inland Revenue may issue a director penalty notice for unpaid PAYE/GST, and the court may order restitution and disqualification from managing companies for up to 5 years (Companies Act 1993 s. 382).
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation under s. BG 1 and s. GA 1 (the arrangement is void against the Commissioner) with reassessment at 28%; shortfall penalty of 100% for an 'abusive tax position' (or 150% if later found to be evasion); CFC attribution of undistributed income to NZ shareholders under the CFC rules can apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be judged not to have acted in good faith (Companies Act 1993 s. 135/136); civil restitution for unpaid tax, travel bans may be sought by the Commissioner where assets are being dissipated, and criminal liability for dishonesty under the Crimes Act 1961 (ss. 228-229, imprisonment up to 7 years).
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Commissioner's adjustment to an arm's-length price under Subpart GC; shortfall penalty of 20% (unacceptable tax position), 100% (abusive tax position), or 150% (evasion) applied to the TP adjustment; interest from the due date; potential CbC disclosure penalty for failure to file.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve unrealistic intercompany pricing without obtaining benchmarking are exposed to civil penalties for breach of directors' duties (s. 137 Companies Act 1993); in aggravated cases, personal liability for the tax shortfall plus conviction for tax evasion.
AML/CFT Red Flags (Medium_Risk_Enhanced)

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009: civil penalty up to NZD 2,000,000 for a body corporate and NZD 100,000 for an individual per contravention; orders to adopt remedial programmes; asset forfeiture under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1991 for laundering proceeds of taxation offences.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for a failure to maintain an AML/CFT programme (s. 59-61); penalties of up to NZD 100,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years for wilful blindness; travel bans and freezing orders may be issued pending prosecution.
