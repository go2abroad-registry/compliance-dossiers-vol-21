Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department, New Zealand (Te Tari Taake) — national tax authority; operational portal 'myIR' at https://www.ird.govt.nz. Customs/export administration is executed by the New Zealand Customs Service (Te Mana Ārai o Aotearoa) through the Trade Single Window (TSW).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident International Company (CK_IC under Cook Islands International Companies law) is not obliged to obtain a New Zealand IRD number unless it carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand, derives New Zealand-sourced income, or voluntarily applies for GST registration. Procedure: (1) confirm non-resident status under ss.YD2 and YD4 Income Tax Act 2007 — incorporation in Cook Islands with management and control outside New Zealand establishes non-residence; (2) if New Zealand taxable activity starts, submit the appropriate registration application (form IR596 for non-resident companies / IR427 for GST) through myIR, attaching the certificate of incorporation, registered office and director particulars, and evidence of the Cook Islands International Companies Act registration; (3) for voluntary GST registration of a non-resident supplier, file the GST registration application (form IR356) via myIR; (4) if a physical place of business in New Zealand is established, the IC must first register as an overseas company with the New Zealand Companies Office (Companies Act 1993) and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) before IRD registration; (5) the IRD issues the IRD number and GST number electronically through myIR. No statutory minimum capital requirement applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone / Non-Resident Company. The CK_IC is incorporated under Cook Islands International Companies law, managed and controlled outside New Zealand, derives no New Zealand-sourced income, and maintains no fixed establishment or permanent establishment in New Zealand; it therefore falls outside the New Zealand territorial income tax net. No New Zealand entity-level income tax is levied on its offshore CRO management revenue, and the home jurisdiction applies a zero-tax regime for international companies. Should the IC derive New Zealand-sourced passive income (dividends, interest, royalties), non-resident withholding tax applies; GST applies only to taxable supplies made in New Zealand.

Available election forms

New Zealand has no US-style check-the-box election. Applicable statutory mechanisms include: (1) CFC and FIF attribution rules (subparts CQ–CZ and EX, Income Tax Act 2007), which may attribute income to New Zealand-resident shareholding interests but are not applicable where no NZ shareholder holds a controlling or effective interest; (2) thin capitalisation and interest apportionment elections for non-resident-owned companies; (3) voluntary GST registration election for non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in New Zealand, permitting input credit recovery; (4) branch equivalent tax account (BETA) elections for certain controlled foreign companies; (5) hybrid and branch mismatch rules (Part GB, Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018) applying as compulsory anti-avoidance rules with limited ordering elections; (6) double tax agreement tie-breaker applications and mutual agreement procedure (MAP) access under New Zealand's tax treaties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 — compulsory registration applies to any person carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand whose taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period (including projected turnover). Non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand-resident consumers and suppliers of low-value imported goods also trigger registration at NZD 60,000 (remote services rules effective 1 October 2016; low-value goods rules effective 1 December 2019). Registration below the threshold is voluntary. The CK_IC, deriving no New Zealand taxable supplies and carrying on no New Zealand taxable activity, has no GST registration nexus and no GST impost on offshore CRO fees.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the GST Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods within 28 days of supply and maintains evidence of export. Services are zero-rated under section 11A where (a) the services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed and the supply is made for the purpose of a taxable activity carried on by the non-resident outside New Zealand, or (b) the services are supplied in connection with goods outside New Zealand or goods in New Zealand that are subsequently exported, provided the services are supplied for the purpose of a taxable activity carried on outside New Zealand. For CRO/trial-management services, zero-rating is available only where no part of the services is physically performed in New Zealand (e.g., offshore project management, data analytics, regulatory coordination conducted outside New Zealand); site monitoring, patient-facing trial operations, or any services physically rendered in New Zealand are standard-rated at 15%. Services supplied to New Zealand-resident consumers or to a non-resident physically present in New Zealand at the time of performance are taxable.

  • Customs Export Declaration — filed electronically via the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window (TSW/TradeNet) for any physical trial materials or comparator drugs exported
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill — evidencing shipment from New Zealand and discharge at the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — SWIFT MT103 or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency within the repatriation deadline
  • Export supply contract and CRO service agreement with the non-resident sponsor
  • Performance evidence for exported services — project plan, milestone deliverables, workpapers, and communications log demonstrating offshore performance

No mandatory real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime operates in New Zealand. Voluntary e-invoicing on the Peppol network (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) is mandatory only for suppliers invoicing central government agencies (B2G mandate, since April 2022). Standard GST invoices must satisfy content requirements under section 24 of the GST Act 1985 (supplier name and GST number, date of issue, description of supply, GST-inclusive amount, and GST component) and be retained for 7 years. No API-based tax clearance or continuous transaction control exists; electronic records are accepted if readily convertible to a readable form.

The Inland Revenue Department may audit zero-rated export transactions and re-assess GST at 15% with use-of-money interest where export evidence is not timely obtained or the statutory conditions (28-day export rule for goods / offshore performance rule for services) are not substantiated. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply at 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 150% for fraudulent intent or evasion. Late filing and late payment penalties accrue progressively (up to 10% of unpaid tax) with compounding interest. Criminal tax evasion offences (ss.143B–143E TAA 1994) expose the company to fines up to NZD 250,000 and responsible officers to imprisonment for up to 5 years. Unsupported zero-rating claims may be clawed back retroactively without time bar in cases of evasion or fraud; for a non-resident IC with no New Zealand taxable nexus, no New Zealand GST exposure or clawback arises.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) operates under a fully floating exchange-rate regime with unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. New Zealand imposes no exchange controls, no cross-border transfer restrictions, and no quantitative limits on equity injections, loan repayments, dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees payable to non-residents. The sole procedural encumbrances are the customer due-diligence and record-keeping duties imposed under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act), which compel reporting banks to verify the economic substance and beneficial ownership of every remittance but do not limit amounts. For a Cook Islands International Company (tax-exempt zone, non-resident-owned) operating as a global Contract Research Organization (CRO), profit repatriation and intercompany milestone settlement are fully executable via standard SWIFT MT103 rails once the beneficial-ownership and source-of-funds dossier has been accepted by the onboarding bank. The NZD is additionally the primary settlement currency of the Cook Islands, providing a natural currency alignment between the entity's offshore domicile and its operating treasury jurisdiction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export receipts or cross-border service receivables. A Contract Research Organization billing multinational pharmaceutical sponsors may retain 100% of contracted milestone payments in any invoiced currency (USD, EUR, GBP, AUD, JPY, CHF) or convert them freely into NZD at dealer-determined spot or forward rates. Financial institutions apply the Reserve Bank's Balance of Payments (BOP) purpose-of-transaction codes when processing credits for statistical reporting, but no central-bank approval, conversion mandate, or repatriation deadline attaches to such proceeds. The CK IC may therefore hold foreign-currency treasury balances offshore or onshore without regulatory penalty.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), Wellington, New Zealand; ancillary supervision of the Cook Islands International Company registry and financial services licensing is exercised by the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC), while all treasury banking, SWIFT clearing, and FX dealing in this blueprint are routed exclusively through the New Zealand banking system subject to RBNZ prudential oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM - PERMITTED ONLY WITH NEW ZEALAND FSP LICENSING AND SUBJECT TO TIER-1 BANK EDD, BUT OPERATIONALLY CONSTRAINED. Offshore digital EMI providers (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currenxie, Decta, and similar non-bank payment institutions) may lawfully service the CK IC as a secondary collection or disbursement layer only if they (i) hold a recognized cross-border payment/e-money license from a comparable supervisory authority, and (ii) maintain an intermediary correspondent account with a New Zealand-registered bank or use a locally licensed subsidiary. Under the AML/CFT Act 2009 and the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, any provider operating in New Zealand must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR); where the provider issues e-money or provides financial advice, additional licensing under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and the FSPR licensing regime is required. Because the major New Zealand banks classify EMI-originated credits as higher-risk non-bank financial institution (NBFI) flows, incoming funds from such providers typically trigger Enhanced Due Diligence, prolonged value-date holds, and possible rejection at the beneficiary bank. Accordingly, the recommended treasury architecture is to use EMIs strictly for low-value operational disbursements to clinical trial sites and subcontractors, while all sponsor milestone wires, escrow funding, and capital injections must be executed through direct SWIFT correspondent banking at ANZ or BNZ. Under no circumstances should the CK IC appoint an unlicensed offshore EMI as its primary treasury keeper, as this would invite immediate regulatory scrutiny from both the Reserve Bank of New Zealand and the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All capital injections into the CK IC must traverse the SWIFT network as MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank transfer) messages directed to the chosen tier-1 New Zealand bank, with the beneficiary account opened in the exact legal name of the Cook Islands International Company as registered with the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission. The payment must carry the correct Balance of Payments (BOP) purpose code recognized by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand: for equity subscriptions and share-capital injections use the direct-investment purpose code 'PD' (Direct Investment - Capital Contribution) or the bank-specific equivalent such as 'Equity Injection - Share Subscription'; for intercompany shareholder loans use a loan-purpose code such as 'Loans - Intercompany' and attach an executed board resolution authorizing the debt instrument. The correspondent bank will verify the originating bank's SWIFT BIC, the beneficiary account ownership, the consistency of the originator's name with the UBO declaration on file, and the source-of-funds documentation. Any single injection equal to or exceeding NZD 200,000 equivalent will automatically trigger Enhanced Due Diligence and a 24-hour value-date hold pending verification of the underlying capital-contribution agreement, signed resolutions, and shareholder register extract. The preferred inbound routing is a direct wire from a recognized global financial institution (e.g., Citibank, JPMorgan, HSBC, Deutsche Bank) to ANZ's Wellington or Auckland swift-processing unit, with the purpose-code field completed exactly and a PDF copy of the shareholder resolution and source-of-funds records pre-delivered to the onboarding relationship manager.Outbound treasury disbursements are cleared through two complementary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfers for all milestone-wire settlements to pharmaceutical sponsors, investigator sites, central ethics committees, CRO subcontractors, and regulatory filing agencies; and (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits) for procurement of trial supplies, medical devices, diagnostic reagents, and cold-chain logistics where shipment documents - air waybill, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and certificate of analysis - are required to trigger payment. Settlement currency may be NZD, USD, EUR, GBP, CNY, JPY, or CHF as stipulated in the underlying clinical-trial contract; where the invoiced currency differs from the account currency, an FX conversion is executed at the bank's dealing desk either on a spot basis or under a pre-arranged forward hedge. Every outbound wire must embed the RBNZ BOP purpose codes (for example, code 1A for business services or code 8A for trade credits) so that each payment reconciles back to the milestone schedule; treasury records must be retained in full for no less than seven years under section 55 of the AML/CFT Act 2009. For China-bound trial supply purchases where deliverable CNY hedging is constrained, a sight Commercial LC opened in USD with the Chinese beneficiary is the definitive clearing method to avoid convertibility friction; for EU and North American site payments, a standard SWIFT MT103 referencing the MCSA milestone number and invoice identifier provides an auditable regulatory trail.Deliverable FX forwards in NZD/USD, NZD/EUR, NZD/GBP, and NZD/AUD with tenors of up to 24 months, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a New York law election or the bank's standard Forex Dealing Terms, subject to an approved credit line with ANZ or BNZ dealing desksCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS) in NZD/USD and USD/EUR for crystallizing long-dated CRO milestone receipts and matching multi-year sponsor payment streams against fixed operating costsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) confined to CNY, KRW, INR, and IDR exposures where deliverability in the onshore market is restricted, allowing the CK IC to hedge clinical trial disbursements into Asian investigative sites without breaching local exchange-control rulesVanilla FX options (European-style calls and puts) in major pairs (NZD/USD, EUR/USD, GBP/USD) with premium paid upfront, where the CK IC acts strictly as a premium payer and never as an option writerFX swaps for temporary liquidity switching between USD milestone receivables and NZD operating accounts, enabling same-day funding of payroll, ethics-committee fees, and investigator payments without residual currency riskPre-settlement foreign-exchange spot transactions in all major liquid pairs under the bank's dealing terms, available on the same value date as the underlying milestone wire for immediate conversion of sponsor receipts

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM - PERMITTED ONLY WITH NEW ZEALAND FSP LICENSING AND SUBJECT TO TIER-1 BANK EDD, BUT OPERATIONALLY CONSTRAINED. Offshore digital EMI providers (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currenxie, Decta, and similar non-bank payment institutions) may lawfully service the CK IC as a secondary collection or disbursement layer only if they (i) hold a recognized cross-border payment/e-money license from a comparable supervisory authority, and (ii) maintain an intermediary correspondent account with a New Zealand-registered bank or use a locally licensed subsidiary. Under the AML/CFT Act 2009 and the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, any provider operating in New Zealand must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR); where the provider issues e-money or provides financial advice, additional licensing under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and the FSPR licensing regime is required. Because the major New Zealand banks classify EMI-originated credits as higher-risk non-bank financial institution (NBFI) flows, incoming funds from such providers typically trigger Enhanced Due Diligence, prolonged value-date holds, and possible rejection at the beneficiary bank. Accordingly, the recommended treasury architecture is to use EMIs strictly for low-value operational disbursements to clinical trial sites and subcontractors, while all sponsor milestone wires, escrow funding, and capital injections must be executed through direct SWIFT correspondent banking at ANZ or BNZ. Under no circumstances should the CK IC appoint an unlicensed offshore EMI as its primary treasury keeper, as this would invite immediate regulatory scrutiny from both the Reserve Bank of New Zealand and the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission.
