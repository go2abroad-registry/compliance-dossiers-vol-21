Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office (NZCO), a business unit of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), Wellington, operating the electronic Companies Register and NZBN (New Zealand Business Number) registry at companiesoffice.govt.nz.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), particularly sections 10-17 (incorporation and registration), 22-28 (company names), 42 (shareholder entitlement), 122-135 (directors duties), 152-161 (director residency and consent), 183-189 (registered office and records); the Financial Reporting Act 2013 for accounting and reporting obligations; the Income Tax Act 2007 for tax characterization of the International Company; the AML/CFT Act 2009 for customer due diligence requirements imposed on the registered office provider.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form 1 'Application for Registration of a Company' lodged electronically through the New Zealand Companies Office online portal (REGNZ), together with a consent to act as director (Form 3), notice of registered office (Form 6), and a signed Constitution (Articles of Incorporation) adopted under section 15 of the Companies Act 1993.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 12 business days (SOP sum)

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Tāke) of New Zealand; operational portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz); corporate registry interface via the New Zealand Companies Office / New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) registry administered by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE).

E-government filing portal

For an International Company structured as a non-resident tax-exempt entity, an IRD number is generally not required as a prerequisite for incorporation. If tax registration is required (owing to New Zealand-sourced income, GST registration on taxable activity, or non-resident remote-supply GST liability), the certified procedure is: (1) register the company with the New Zealand Companies Office to obtain an NZBN and company entity number; (2) file an IRD number application for a company in myIR using form IR 596, indicating non-resident or exempt status if applicable; (3) where the company makes taxable supplies in New Zealand or remote supplies to New Zealand consumers and exceeds the NZD 60,000 threshold, register for GST via myIR (non-resident offshore suppliers use form IR 590); (4) fully execute the 'International Company Tax Exemption Declaration' to the IRD if claiming absence of tax liability; (5) maintain the IRD number for invoicing, customs, and banking documentation where the entity elects to obtain it voluntarily. A company classified as Tax_Exempt_Zone with no New Zealand taxable activity and no New Zealand-source income is not required to file income tax returns, but must file where an IRD registration has been issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not administer the US check-the-box regime. Domestic statutory elections relevant to an International Company include: (a) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, which makes a qualifying closely-held company tax-transparent; (b) election to apply the branch equivalent method (BEM) for attributable CFC income under the controlled foreign company rules; (c) election to register, or exclude registration, for GST under the non-resident supplier (remote services) provisions of section 60 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; and (d) hybrid mismatch elections, which are heavily restricted and must not be used to circumvent BEPS rules in subpart FE of the Income Tax Act 2007.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies (excluding GST) in a rolling 12-month period; for non-resident suppliers of remote services (including SaaS, API monitoring, and developer infrastructure tools) supplied to New Zealand resident consumers, the same NZD 60,000 registration threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exports applies to goods that are physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier at or before the time of supply, or are exported within 28 days of the time of supply, with customs and transportation evidence. Zero-rating of services (including software-as-a-service, API monitoring, and cloud infrastructure tools) applies when the services are supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are received, provided the services are not directly in connection with New Zealand land or tangible property, are not supplied to a person present in New Zealand, and are not consumed in New Zealand. B2B subscriptions and API monitoring licences are typically zero-rated when invoiced to overseas customers with foreign payment details and overseas usage evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window (export entry code and validation status).
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing shipment from New Zealand to the foreign destination.
  • Inward remittance certificate / foreign exchange settlement advice issued by the settling bank, evidencing receipt of export proceeds.
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the GST zero-rating classification.
  • Electronic delivery records, logs, or signed delivery receipts confirming the offshore destination or offshore customer consumption.

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance through IRD APIs for tax invoices. E-invoicing operates through the Peppol interoperability network and is accepted by the IRD; government agencies are enabled for Peppol-based e-invoicing but supplier transmission remains optional. All GST invoices must nonetheless contain the statutory particulars prescribed in the GST Act 1985, and both e-invoices and paper invoices are admissible. There is no requirement for foreign suppliers to obtain digital clearance before issuing an export zero-rated invoice.

The Inland Revenue Department audits zero-rated export claims using cross-referenced customs, shipping, and bank settlement data. All supporting documentation must be retained for seven years after the end of the income year. If a zero-rated supply is overturned, the IRD assesses GST output tax at 15% on the supply value, plus use-of-money interest from the due date of the original return. Civil shortfall penalties apply tiered: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or similar conduct. Fraudulent export zero-rating schemes attract criminal penalties under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994, including fines up to NZD 50,000, imprisonment up to five years, and through increased assessment periods (no time bar in cases of evasion) the Commissioner may claw back stale refunds and re-open previous assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully floating exchange rate regime with no capital controls, no currency surrender requirements, and no restrictions on capital account convertibility for foreign-owned entities. Current account items such as software export receipts, dividends, interest, and service fees are freely convertible into NZD or any foreign currency. Profit remittances by a non-resident-owned International Company are unrestricted from a banking perspective; however, the payer must satisfy Inland Revenue non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) deduction rules on dividends, interest, and royalties, and must maintain transfer-pricing documentation for intercompany payments to avoid adjusted tax assessments. No prior Reserve Bank approval is required for either inbound investment or outbound remittance, and there are no surrender rules obligating conversion of foreign currency export proceeds into NZD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from cloud software, SaaS subscriptions, and developer infrastructure services must be repatriated to New Zealand through a registered bank under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, but there is no mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory surrender timeline. Foreign currency receipts may be retained in approved foreign currency accounts (including USD or AUD transit accounts) with a New Zealand-registered bank, provided the entity maintains supporting trade invoices, contract documents, and a reconciliation trail to the accounting records. For trade settlement and tax compliance, the company may convert export proceeds at the prevailing market rate on the date of settlement or the date of recognition under NZ IFRS 15, as elected in its accounting policy.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) and the New Zealand Financial Markets Authority (FMA) acting in coordination with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade for sanctions administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-to-High feasibility for offshore digital EMIs used as ancillary collection/disbursement rails, but not as a full substitute for a New Zealand-registered bank account. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not prudentially regulated by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand and do not offer New Zealand retail deposit protection. Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, an offshore EMI that provides payment services to a New Zealand entity may be treated as carrying on business in New Zealand if it maintains a local customer-facing operation, which would require registration as a financial service provider under the Financial Service Providers Register and compliance with New Zealand AML/CFT obligations. Therefore, legally binding clearing should continue through a Tier-1 licensed bank, while EMI accounts may be used only for narrow purposes such as receiving overseas disbursements or paying foreign vendors where the latency of SWIFT is operationally disadvantageous. A full reliance on an offshore digital EMI would materially increase jurisdiction-stripping risk, undermine the treasury substance proof required for Inland Revenue and bank KYC, and is not recommended.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Tax-exempt International Company with non-resident ownership and no New Zealand tax presence

Root cause: Tax-exempt International Company with non-resident ownership and no New Zealand tax presence

Defense evidence: Certified certificate of incorporation, register of directors and shareholders, beneficial ownership declaration form signed by each ultimate individual owner, a current statutory certificate of good standing from the New Zealand Companies Office, and a formal legal opinion from a New Zealand-qualified lawyer explaining the tax-exempt classification and the company's limited activities. Also provide a board resolution confirming the operational substance in New Zealand (cloud export business) and a completed IRD entity profile, where applicable.

Root cause: Low capitalisation and future inbound capital injection from offshore parent

Defense evidence: Share subscription agreement, loan note or intercompany financing agreement evidencing the capital injection, source-of-funds declaration from the sending bank, the corporate treasury policy, and a projection of the company's contractual revenue backlog demonstrating genuine software-export activity. Banks may request consecutive bank statements from the remitting entity in the foreign jurisdiction and an explanation of the purpose of the injection (working capital, infrastructure spending, or development costs).

High-frequency automated revenue collection via Stripe Billing and Mercury ACH rails in USD

Root cause: High-frequency automated revenue collection via Stripe Billing and Mercury ACH rails in USD

Defense evidence: Stripe Billing contract, the Merchant of Record agreement (if applicable), a full export of transaction-level settlement reporting, and a written treasury reconciliation policy demonstrating that all funds are swept to the New Zealand bank account on a regular schedule. Defense evidence: A complete currency map of every client and vendor jurisdiction, sanctions screening certificates for each counterparty, and a legal attestation that no transaction involves a sanctioned country or person. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections (equity or shareholder loans) from a non-resident parent to the New Zealand International Company must be wired via SWIFT MT103/202 to the primary New Zealand bank account using the proper beneficiary name, BIC, and account number. The instruction should clearly state the purpose code appropriate to the Foreign Exchange/Investment type, normally 'Equity investment in non-resident enterprise' (code 301) or 'Intercompany loan' (code 302) under the OECD/IMF Balance of Payments classification used by New Zealand reporting banks. For equity, the receipt must correspond to an updated share register showing an allotment of shares to the non-resident parent, and the corporate board resolution must authorise the share issue. For a shareholder loan, the injection must be evidenced by an executed loan agreement, an interest rate and maturity schedule, and transfer-pricing documentation that supports arm's length terms. The bank may require a signed declaration under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 identifying the source of wealth and source of funds when the amount exceeds NZD 50,000, and all large-value inbound transfers may be reported to the FIU. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-to-High feasibility for offshore digital EMIs used as ancillary collection/disbursement rails, but not as a full substitute for a New Zealand-registered bank account. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not prudentially regulated by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand and do not offer New Zealand retail deposit protection. Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, an offshore EMI that provides payment services to a New Zealand entity may be treated as carrying on business in New Zealand if it maintains a local customer-facing operation, which would require registration as a financial service provider under the Financial Service Providers Register and compliance with New Zealand AML/CFT obligations. Therefore, legally binding clearing should continue through a Tier-1 licensed bank, while EMI accounts may be used only for narrow purposes such as receiving overseas disbursements or paying foreign vendors where the latency of SWIFT is operationally disadvantageous. A full reliance on an offshore digital EMI would materially increase jurisdiction-stripping risk, undermine the treasury substance proof required for Inland Revenue and bank KYC, and is not recommended.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No general statutory monetary threshold triggers a mandatory New Zealand Local File or Master File. New Zealand applies an arm’s-length standard and may require records sufficient to explain pricing. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to an ultimate parent or surrogate entity in New Zealand that is part of a qualifying multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million, with the relevant filing and notification obligations. Inland Revenue may request transfer-pricing documentation and transaction records regardless of transaction size.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no separate general Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Economic substance is nevertheless relevant through the transfer-pricing arm’s-length principle, permanent-establishment rules, controlled foreign company rules, financial-arrangement rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, treaty anti-abuse provisions, and the general anti-avoidance rule. A DevOps or cloud-tools company should maintain genuine functions, people, decision-making, assets, risks, development records, customer contracts, service-delivery evidence, and commercially rational intercompany pricing in New Zealand where profits are allocated there.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution may result in fines and imprisonment under New Zealand tax administration and Crimes Act provisions. Tax remains recoverable, with use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties that can reach 150% for evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who intentionally participate in evasion may face personal criminal liability, fines, imprisonment, civil recovery, and disqualification consequences. A company does not shield deliberate personal criminal conduct. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax sanction but may arise from court processes or enforcement circumstances.
GST and Indirect Tax Misclassification

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid GST, use-of-money interest, late filing and payment penalties, shortfall penalties, and possible prosecution for intentional conduct.

Director personal liability: Personal exposure may arise for knowing involvement, dishonest conduct, breach of fiduciary duties, or liability under insolvency and tax recovery provisions. Insolvent trading and asset stripping may create separate civil claims.
Transfer Pricing and Profit Diversion

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may make a transfer-pricing adjustment, impose tax on additional income, apply interest and shortfall penalties, and require documentation. Treaty relief may be denied where anti-abuse or beneficial-ownership conditions fail.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for breach of duties, reckless trading, misleading records, or participation in tax misconduct. Transfer-pricing adjustment alone does not automatically create criminal liability.
Withholding Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: The payer may owe the withholding tax, late-payment penalties, use-of-money interest, and shortfall penalties. Deliberate withholding-tax fraud may be prosecuted.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors or officers may incur personal exposure where they knowingly authorize non-remittance, misuse trust monies, or participate in fraud. Ordinary company withholding liability does not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Shell Company and Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: General anti-avoidance rules, transfer-pricing adjustments, treaty benefit denial, withholding-tax reassessment, penalties, interest, and possible criminal prosecution for fabricated or dishonest arrangements may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil claims, restoration orders, compensation, disqualification, and criminal liability for fraud or false records. The corporate veil may be disregarded in exceptional cases involving sham, fraud, agency, or statutory liability.
AML, Sanctions, and Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: The Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act, sanctions legislation, and Crimes Act may impose compliance orders, civil pecuniary penalties, forfeiture, substantial fines, and imprisonment for serious offending.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers may be personally prosecuted for knowing participation, aiding, abetting, or consent to offending. Assets may be restrained or forfeited. Travel restrictions may arise from criminal proceedings, bail, sentencing, or asset-recovery processes but are not automatic.
Insolvency and Director Duties

Statutory sanction: Potential liquidation, recovery of transactions, compensation orders, penalties, director disqualification, and liability under the Companies Act 1993 and insolvency law. Unpaid PAYE, GST, and withholding amounts may receive priority or separate recovery treatment.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil restitution, compensation, contribution to company debts, disqualification, and imprisonment where the conduct is criminal. Corporate limited liability is not a defense to reckless trading, fraud, or knowing breach of statutory duties.
Employment, PAYE, and Contractor Misclassification

Statutory sanction: Backdated PAYE, interest, penalties, holiday-pay and employment entitlements, penalties under employment legislation, and possible prosecution may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal consequences for reckless or dishonest payroll conduct, breach of duties, or participation in deliberate withholding failures. The company remains primarily liable for ordinary employment debts.
