Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake) – official portal: www.ird.govt.nz; business registration and GST filing via myIR secure online services and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) registry at www.business.govt.nz.

E-government filing portal

For an International Company (CK_IC) treated as a non-resident foreign company, the statutory tax identifier is an IRD number. Procedure: (1) Obtain an NZBN from the New Zealand Companies Office if the entity is registered as an overseas company carrying on business in New Zealand; (2) Complete IR596 (Non-Resident Individual/Company Tax Registration) and file with Inland Revenue; or, for GST registration specifically, file IR830 (GST Registration) through myIR; (3) Provide certified supporting documentation (certificate of incorporation, director details, registered office, evidence of non-resident status) as required by Inland Revenue; (4) If the entity is incorporated in New Zealand but claims non-residence for tax, it must also lodge a declaration or obtain a residence determination under section YD2 of the Income Tax Act 2007.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident International Company with no New Zealand-sourced income; outside the New Zealand income tax net and not subject to entity-level NZ tax on foreign-source income; may still be liable for GST on remote supplies into New Zealand if the statutory threshold is exceeded).

Available election forms

New Zealand does not have a check-the-box regime. Entity classification is determined by domestic incorporation and central management-and-control rules under section YD2. An International Company may elect to be treated as a New Zealand tax resident if its central management and control is exercised in New Zealand, which would then bring worldwide income into the NZ tax net; alternatively, it may rely on the non-resident territorial tax regime. For GST, a non-resident supplier of remote services may elect to register under the simplified 'remote services' rules and apply zero-rating where the recipient is a GST-registered business (B2B). Hybrid mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 restrict arbitrage, and no separate New Zealand 'look-through' election is available for international companies except for the standard Look-Through Company (LTC) rules that apply only to NZ-resident close-held companies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 – a compulsory registration threshold applies to any person, including a non-resident international company, that makes taxable supplies in New Zealand in a 12-month period. For remote services (e.g., digital design subscriptions) supplied to New Zealand-resident consumers, the same NZD 60,000 threshold applies. If the International Company only supplies services to non-residents for consumption outside New Zealand, no GST registration is required unless the supplies connect with New Zealand.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies are eligible for zero-rated GST treatment under sections 11 or 11A of the GST Act 1985 if: (a) the supply is of goods that are exported from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply; or (b) the supply is of services to a person who is not resident in New Zealand and the services are not supplied for use/consumption in New Zealand (i.e., the services are performed remotely – such as UI/UX design subscriptions – and are not directly connected with New Zealand real property or movable property; or (c) the supply is of 'remote services' (electronically supplied services, such as subscription design platforms) to a non-resident business recipient that is GST-registered. The International Company must obtain and retain documentary evidence demonstrating the non-resident status of the recipient and the offshore consumption or exportation of the service, including the recipient's overseas billing address, foreign IP address, contract signed outside New Zealand, and evidence of payment from a foreign bank account.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Customs Service – Trade Single Window) for physical goods; for services a detailed invoice or contract identifying supply type, value, and non-resident client
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (for physical exported goods) or equivalent electronic delivery logs evidencing offshore delivery of the subscription access
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (PDF) issued by a New Zealand bank certifying receipt of funds from abroad
  • Foreign exchange contract or SWIFT transfer records linking the payment to the non-resident client
  • Written supply agreement executed offshore showing that the UI/UX design services are consumed outside New Zealand

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. GST returns are filed electronically via the myIR portal. E-invoicing is facilitated through the Pan-European Public Procurement On-Line (Peppol) framework, adopted mandatorily for New Zealand government departments since 2022, but private sector suppliers are encouraged rather than mandated to use it. There is no prior online approval of invoices; however, every GST-registered person must issue a valid tax invoice for taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) and retain sufficient digital evidence (contract, bank records, IP address, export documentation) to satisfy audit requirements. The Inland Revenue Department can require voluntary disclosure of electronic records under the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Inland Revenue scrutinises zero-rated export claims under section 11A and will claw back the input tax and assess GST at the standard 15% rate where the zero-rating is not substantiated within the required timeframe. Penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 include: a shortfall penalty of 20% (absence of reasonable care) or 50% (gross carelessness or abusive tax position) of the resulting GST shortfall; up to 100% for evasion or a fraudulent intent. In addition, use-of-money interest accrues from the original return due date. Where a GST refund has been claimed fraudulently, criminal prosecution can lead to fines of up to NZ$50,000 for an individual and NZ$250,000 for a company, plus imprisonment of up to five years. The International Company must retain zero-rating substantiation records for at least seven years and must comply with any Commissioner-issued record-keeping requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full current account and capital account convertibility for the New Zealand dollar (NZD). There are no general restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, loan repayments, or dissolution proceeds for non-resident shareholders. The only meaningful hurdles are tax compliance obligations, including the requirement to maintain a New Zealand tax file number or to obtain an exemption from withholding tax if the payment qualifies under an applicable double tax agreement. Inbound capital injections and outbound operational disbursements may be effected freely, provided the underlying activity is not on a sanctions list and the instruction passes standard anti-money laundering and know-your-customer controls. Practical convertibility hurdles arise mainly at the commercial banking layer through correspondent bank due diligence on the ultimate beneficial owner and the declared business purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no mandatory surrender or repatriation timeline on export proceeds. Receivables may be retained offshore or converted into NZD at the discretion of the exporter. However, the New Zealand tax framework requires all foreign income of a New Zealand tax-resident entity to be declared on a worldwide basis, and transfer pricing rules require export transactions with associated enterprises to be conducted on an arm's-length basis. For the International Company structure, no statutory surrender framework is applicable, but bank account conduct rules require the underlying trade invoice and evidence of service delivery to support the clearing and crediting of export subscription revenues to the corporate operating account.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), operating jointly with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the New Zealand Exchange Control regime administered under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for offshore digital EMIs, with heightened compliance friction. Legal constraints include the requirement under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's AML/CFT supervision framework that all account providers—whether domestic banks or registered financial institutions—conduct equivalent due diligence on non-resident companies. Purely offshore EMIs, such as digital banking platforms incorporated in other jurisdictions, may be permitted to open deposit accounts for the International Company only if they maintain a registered financial services provider (FSP) presence in New Zealand or rely on an inbound correspondence banking relationship. The preferred EMI route is through licensed New Zealand FSPs such as Wise New Zealand Limited or specialised fintech treasury platforms that offer segregated client money accounts and SWIFT-grade clearing rails. The practical constraint is that subscription revenue collected through Stripe Billing or Wise must ultimately settle into a New Zealand domestic bank account or an approved foreign currency settlement account to avoid the EMI being treated as a financial intermediary with additional reporting obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from the International Company's shareholder to its New Zealand operating account must be structured as a direct SWIFT MT103 wire transfer denominated in NZD or foreign currency, with the payment purpose coded as 'Equity Capital Subscription' or 'Shareholder Loan Contribution'. The relevant purpose code is not a static New Zealand customs code, but the instructing bank's transaction description should accurately state 'Capital Injection to NZ entity for design agency operations' to satisfy both NZ AML/CFT conduct rules and the New Zealand Companies Office reporting requirement in the annual return. Verification requires the provision of a shareholder resolution, a signed application for allotment or loan agreement, and the corporate certificate of incorporation in the bank's internal compliance system. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — statutorily exempt Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC) under the International Companies Act; however, 28% is the New Zealand corporate headline rate if the same entity is found to be a New Zealand tax resident through central management and control in NZ.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends paid by a compliant Cook Islands International Company out of tax-exempt profits; Cook Islands imposes no dividend withholding or dividend distribution tax. If the CK_IC is nevertheless treated as a New Zealand tax-resident company, the domestic NRWT rate on non-resident dividends is 30%, with a 15% rate typically applied under several NZ double tax agreements for portfolio dividends and 0% for shareholder dividends where the treaty so provides.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions in an income year triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation under s.78B of the Tax Administration Act 1994. A CbC report must be filed for multinational groups with consolidated annual group revenue of NZD 1.3 billion or more. In addition, the IRD may compel documentation for smaller transactions where a tax-exempt intermediate entity is interposed and there is no commercial rationale.

Economic substance scope: The Cook Islands Economic Substance (International Companies) requirements apply; an IC carrying on a relevant activity — which includes provision of services, headquarters, or IP-rich digital design activities — must demonstrate real economic presence in the Cook Islands through local offices, local qualified employees, board meetings directed and managed in the Cook Islands, and adequate operating expenditure. The ESR filing and any annual confirmation must be made through the FSDA, and information is automatically exchangeable with the New Zealand/Australian tax authorities under exchange-of-information arrangements. Failure to satisfy ESR means the tax exemption is withdrawn and significant penalties are imposed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Managing and Controlling the IC from New Zealand

Statutory sanction: The IC is likely to be recharacterised as a New Zealand tax resident or as having a New Zealand permanent establishment; the Tax Exempt Zone status is void; IRD may assess all worldwide income at 28% with a shortfall penalty of up to 150% for evasion and criminal prosecution for the officers concerned.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly involved in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax/penalties; the corporate veil may be pierced under s.135 of the Companies Act (reckless trading) and s.142 TAA; personal civil recovery, travel restrictions in relation to IRD debt, and imprisonment for criminal tax evasion are all available.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Contravention (ESR)

Statutory sanction: FSDA will impose economic substance penalties, withdraw the exemption, apply default interest and late fees, and may strike off the company; the company will be reported to the New Zealand IRD and to foreign tax authorities through automatic exchange of information.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent may be personally fined for filing false ESR/beneficial ownership declarations; the directors can be disqualified and made personally liable for restitution to creditors if the stripping of assets leaves the company insolvent.
AML/CFT Compliance (UBO and Payment Railing)

Statutory sanction: Under the Cook Islands AML/CFT Act and the Financial Intelligence Unit enforcement framework, the company and its MLRO are exposed to substantial monetary penalties, civil forfeiture, and imprisonment; the company can be added to the national AML risk register and its banking services terminated.

Director personal liability: The Money Laundering Reporting Officer, directors, and beneficial owners face personal criminal liability, travel bans by FIU order, and seizure of funds; dishonest concealment of a true beneficial owner is treated as a predicate offence for money laundering.
Disguised Profit Extraction / Repatriation Abuse

Statutory sanction: IRD may recharacterise the payments under the general anti-avoidance rule (s.BG1 and s.GB21 of the Income Tax Act 2007), apply NRWT at 30% to dividends, assess the shareholder for personal tax, and impose evasion shortfall penalties plus use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve unlawful distributions or knowingly join in sham arrangements must restore the company’s funds (Companies Act 1993, s.60/financial assistance rules), and they can be made personally liable for amounts paid away; travel bans can be imposed to secure recovery.
