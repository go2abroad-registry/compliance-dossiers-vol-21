Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies in New Zealand; a company may be incorporated with one or more shares issued for cash, partly paid, or non-cash consideration without escrow or capital deposit accounts. For this entity, one ordinary share of NZD 1.00 is subscribed and paid at allotment to establish membership and solvency, with further shares issuable by board resolution under s 99 of the Companies Act 1993.

Funding & unlock path: Occupational health and safety clearance under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 (HSWA), including a written HSWA policy, hazard register, emergency evacuation plan, first aid provisions, and appointment of a health and safety officer; building warrant of fitness (BWOF) per the Building Act 2004 and Building (Earthquake-prone Buildings) Amendment Act 2016 for the leased premises, including fire alarm and automatic sprinkler system verification; Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) fire evacuation scheme approval for premises holding more than 100 persons under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017; civil defence emergency preparedness plan aligned with the National Civil Defence Emergency Management Plan 2015; and mandatory ACC (Accident Compensation Corporation) employer registration for worker injury cover.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; statutory self-service portal is myIR operated at https://www.ird.govt.nz (including myIR secure online services for corporate taxpayers and agents).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity such as an International Company (IC) without a New Zealand permanent establishment: (1) apply for an IRD number using Form IR596 (Application by a company for an IRD number) or via myIR after creating a non-resident company profile; attach certified certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered office address, and beneficial ownership declaration; (2) appoint a New Zealand agent for service of process where no physical place of business exists; (3) register for GST using Form GST1 or myIR GST application only when a taxable activity in New Zealand is carried on and annual turnover exceeds NZD 60,000, or on a voluntary basis for business-to-business supplies and cross-border remote services; (4) rely on the New Zealand tax-exempt zone classification for offshore-source income, maintaining a zero income tax filing obligation while retaining any necessary GST registration solely for New Zealand-source supplies of goods and services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not recognise the United States-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections relevant to an International Company include: (a) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under section HB 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, rendering an eligible New Zealand-resident company fiscally transparent for income tax purposes; (b) Qualifying Company election under the Companies Act 1993 transitional rules (closed to new elections since 1 April 2010); (c) consolidated group election under sections HB 1-HB 4 of the Income Tax Act 2007, permitting group aggregation for tax purposes; (d) GST group registration election under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; and (e) hybrid mismatch and branch mismatch elections under the hybrid rules in Part 4B of the Income Tax Act 2007 for structured cross-border arrangements, where a foreign entity tax classification may be disregarded or re-characterised in accordance with the OECD BEPS hybrid framework.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 annual taxable turnover (12-month rolling basis); the same threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value imported goods supplied to New Zealand consumers, where registration is compulsory if total cross-border supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for New Zealand GST applies to: (a) goods exported by the supplier within 28 days of supply, provided the goods do not subsequently return to New Zealand or are not subjected to a taxable supply in New Zealand; (b) services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time services are performed, where the recipient acquires the services for the purposes of a business carried on outside New Zealand; and (c) offshore services performed outside New Zealand. For an ESG carbon accounting and offset brokerage company selling audit advisory services to non-resident clients, zero-rating requires clear documentary evidence that the recipient is non-resident, the supply is consumed outside New Zealand, and no New Zealand land or goods remain within New Zealand. Carbon offset brokerage services may be zero-rated only if the underlying offset units are recognised as exported intangible services; IRD may recharacterise brokerage fees as New Zealand-sourced if the contract negotiations, risk management, or legal performance occur in New Zealand.

  • NZCS Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Declaration / TPP exit declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident customer

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance regime, no tax-authority API pre-clearance, and no digital stamping obligation. New Zealand operates a voluntary Peppol-compliant e-invoicing network administered through the E-Invoicing NZ framework; e-invoices must be issued and retained in a format accessible to IRD on request, but there is no continuous transaction control or pre-approval by the Inland Revenue Department.

The Inland Revenue Department and New Zealand Customs Service jointly audit zero-rating claims. Exporters are liable to repay any GST input tax credits recovered on goods or services that are not exported within the statutory timeframe or that are subsequently reimported or diverted to a taxable use in New Zealand; use-of-money interest applies on all clawed-back amounts. Civil shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply up to 150% of the resulting tax shortfall in cases of abusive tax positions or evasion, and an additional 100% penalty may be imposed for fraudulent or wilful acts under section 141E of the Tax Administration Act 1994. Criminal prosecution under section 144A of the Tax Administration Act 1994 and section 253 of the Crimes Act 1961 can lead to imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines of up to NZD 50,000 for an individual or NZD 250,000 for a company. In practice, IRD applies retrospective reassessment for up to four years, extended to ten years in cases of evasion or fraudulent activity involving export zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and free convertibility on both current and capital account under the freely floating New Zealand dollar (NZD). There are no exchange-control approvals, no capital-account licensing, and no restrictions on profit or dividend repatriation, loan repayment, or liquidation remittances to non-resident shareholders. The only regulatory gates are the Overseas Investment Act 2018 for acquisitions of sensitive land or significant business assets (not engaged by an ISIC 7020 ESG carbon audit and offset brokerage company), the Companies Act 1993 overseas-company registration requirement if the entity carries on business in New Zealand, and bank-level AML/CFT and sanctions screening. Profit remittance from New Zealand is subject to non-resident dividend or interest withholding tax under the Income Tax Act 2007, reduced only where an applicable double tax agreement provides relief; the Cook Islands International Company's exempt-zone tax classification does not automatically eliminate New Zealand-source withholding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts or service fee income; foreign currency export proceeds and professional fee receivables may be retained offshore or onshore in any currency without surrender. The only ongoing obligations are accurate reporting to the Reserve Bank for Balance of Payments statistics and compliance with the AML/CFT Act 2009 record-retention rules. The corporate Scope 1-3 carbon auditing and offset brokerage service revenue is treated as service export income, fully fungible and freely usable for treasury purposes.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), operating under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021; the Monetary Policy Committee sets monetary policy, while the Reserve Bank jointly supervises banks with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) compliance. No separate Foreign Exchange Administration exists because New Zealand has no exchange-control statute.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Conditional Medium-High. New Zealand does not maintain a standalone e-money institution (EMI) licensing category; non-bank payment providers must operate through a registered bank partner for settlement and are supervised for AML/CFT purposes by the Financial Markets Authority or the Reserve Bank. Offshore digital EMIs (UK FCA-authorised, Australian AFSL/ACL holders, or Singapore MPI holders) cannot hold on-balance-sheet NZD deposits or issue NZD e-money in New Zealand without a local banking/AML/CFT registration; they may, however, be used as a supplementary disbursement rail for small retail carbon offset micro-purchases through master-account arrangements. The CK_IC should maintain its primary treasury and clearing relationship with an NZ tier-1 bank and use an offshore EMI only as a secondary, non-critical channel subject to a group treasury policy cap.
