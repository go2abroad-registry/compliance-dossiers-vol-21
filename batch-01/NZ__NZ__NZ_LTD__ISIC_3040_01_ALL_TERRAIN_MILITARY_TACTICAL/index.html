Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand law imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement for a limited company. Share capital may be subscribed in full or in instalments as specified in the constitution; there is no escrow, blocked capital, or paid-in capital verification requirement. However, directors must satisfy solvency certification requirements under sections 52 and 161, and any issued shares must be fully paid if required by shareholders' agreement or constitution.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certification under section 22A of the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017, obtained after full inspection of fire suppression systems, sprinklers, escape routes, and emergency lighting coverage. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand, administered under the Tax Administration Act 1994; electronic portal: myIR at ird.govt.nz; business registration integrated with the New Zealand Companies Office and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) registry.

E-government filing portal

For an NZ-incorporated limited company, the Companies Office automatically issues a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) at incorporation and Inland Revenue simultaneously issues an IRD number for income tax and GST. For a foreign non-resident entity not incorporated in New Zealand that carries on business in New Zealand: (i) apply via the myIR non-resident business registration pathway; (ii) submit certified constitutional documents (certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association or equivalent charter), director declarations, and evidence of a New Zealand registered office or appointed resident agent for service of notices; (iii) declare the intended taxable activity and branch accounting method; (iv) Inland Revenue issues a dedicated IRD number and, where annual taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed NZD 60,000, a GST registration number; (v) non-resident suppliers of remote services or low-value imported goods are separately captured by the compulsory GST registration rules; registration must be completed before commencing taxable activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For an NZ-incorporated limited company, the Companies Office automatically issues a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) at incorporation and Inland Revenue simultaneously issues an IRD number for income tax and GST. For a foreign non-resident entity not incorporated in New Zealand that carries on business in New Zealand: (i) apply via the myIR non-resident business registration pathway; (ii) submit certified constitutional documents (certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association or equivalent charter), director declarations, and evidence of a New Zealand registered office or appointed resident agent for service of notices; (iii) declare the intended taxable activity and branch accounting method; (iv) Inland Revenue issues a dedicated IRD number and, where annual taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed NZD 60,000, a GST registration number; (v) non-resident suppliers of remote services or low-value imported goods are separately captured by the compulsory GST registration rules; registration must be completed before commencing taxable activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None - New Zealand has no check-the-box classification regime and no elective hybrid-mismatch mechanism; hybrid mismatch rules (Subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007, transposing OECD BEPS Action 2) are mandatory anti-avoidance provisions. Available statutory elections for eligible entities include the Look-Through Company (LTC) election under Subpart HB (restricted to closely held qualifying companies, generally incompatible with a large defense hardware manufacturer), loss grouping elections under Subpart IC, and the Māori Authority election under Subpart HF.

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Section 11(1) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 applies to a supply of goods exported by the supplier within 28 days of the time of supply, or delivered to a recipient who is outside New Zealand and removed from New Zealand. The all-terrain military tactical vehicles supplied to a foreign defense buyer must: (i) be physically exported within 28 days of the time of supply; (ii) be covered by a valid New Zealand Customs Service export entry and any required controlled-goods export permit under New Zealand export control regulations for military equipment; (iii) be paid through an approved cross-border rail (government direct wire or diplomatic letter of credit) with verifiable foreign exchange repatriation; and (iv) be supported by retained customs-endorsed export evidence. Qualifying services physically or directly connected to the exported goods and supplied to a non-resident recipient for use wholly outside New Zealand are zero-rated under Section 11A.

  • Customs Export Declaration - New Zealand Customs Service Export Entry (electronic export entry lodged via the Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill) evidencing shipment from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) - equivalently a New Zealand bank inward remittance advice or foreign exchange settlement record evidencing FX repatriation of the contract proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the tactical vehicles and their military classification
  • Export permit / controlled-goods licence issued by the New Zealand Customs Service for military equipment
  • Proof of export within 28 days of time of supply (customs endorsed movement reference)

Not applicable - New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal invoice clearance, government API pre-clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates via the Peppol network; GST returns are filed electronically through myIR on a two-monthly taxable period (monthly or six-monthly options available), with no fiscal seal, SAF-T, or pre-clearance filing requirement.

Where the 28-day export requirement is not met or the supporting export documentation is deficient, the zero-rating is invalidated and GST at 15% becomes payable on the full consideration together with use-of-money interest. The Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes shortfall penalties of 20% for inadequate tax positions, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% (150% for abetting) for evasion; late payment penalties accumulate at 1% immediately plus 4% per quarter on unpaid GST; the standard reassessment amendment window is four years, with no time bar in cases of fraud or evasion. Knowingly fraudulent export zero-rating claims attract criminal prosecution (fines and imprisonment under Part 4A of the Tax Administration Act 1994), retrospective clawback of any GST refunds, and loss of GST registration eligibility.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has a fully liberalised and open capital account. Formal exchange controls were dismantled in 1984, and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not impose restrictions on the purchase, sale, or holding of foreign currency. Both current account flows (trade, services, royalties, and interest) and capital account flows (equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, and direct investment) may be freely converted into New Zealand dollars and outward converted without central bank approval. Exporters may settle New Zealand-origin receipts in any convertible currency and are not required to surrender earnings to the central bank. Profit remittance from a New Zealand limited company is legally unrestricted but must comply with the solvency test in the Companies Act 1993 and is subject to non-resident withholding tax where applicable. For an ISIC 3040 vehicle manufacturer, the real constraint is therefore not FX convertibility but the New Zealand export control regime, the need for a Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade export permit, and possible Overseas Investment Act clearance for non-resident shareholdings; none of these prevents conversion of NZD for legitimate trade.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rule applies in New Zealand. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no prescribed repatriation period, and no obligation to sell export proceeds for NZD. An exporter may maintain 100% of foreign currency export receipts in an onshore or offshore New Zealand-authorised bank account. The only compliance duties are normal AML/CFT record-keeping, accurate payment purpose classification, and conversion date recognition for income tax and GST; the exporter may choose to convert at its commercial discretion.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua) acting as the monetary authority and central bank; foreign exchange administration is liberalised and no separate FX centralisation agency is used.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low, and the entity should not rely on a fintech EMI as a clearing bank. New Zealand law does not statutorily ban EMIs from the market, but mainstream EMIs prohibit defence and weapons-related sectors in their terms, decline ISIC 3040 end-uses, and do not issue documentary letters of credit or support government/diplomatic payment rails. EMIs also cannot provide a registered New Zealand branch deposit-taker relationship that will be accepted as the bank of record for a special-license defence exporter. Any digital-only account that is opened would be closed upon transaction monitoring review. Therefore, a New Zealand Tier-1 commercial bank with sanctioned correspondent banking is mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For inbound equity capital, the wiring bank must use SWIFT MT103 with the full corporate name and New Zealand company number in the beneficiary field. The remitter's bank will include a purpose code such as FDI equity subscription or capital cancellation, and the receiving bank will apply the BOP purpose code 'equity injection' or 'capital contribution' for New Zealand balance-of-payments reporting. The subscription proceeds should be received in NZD or foreign currency on a segregated Capital Escrow Account and credited against the share allocation in accordance with the Companies Act 1993. A capital verification certificate must be issued by a New Zealand chartered accountant only after the cleared bank funds are in the company's account. If any non-resident shareholder subscribes, the company must first obtain an Overseas Investment Office clearance/declaration or a written external legal opinion specifying that the investment falls below the significant business asset or sensitive land thresholds; this is particularly relevant for defence-sensitive capability.Outbound settlement for military tactical vehicles will be executed via three verified rails. First, SWIFT MT103 direct bank transfer is used for down payments and milestone invoices from the purchasing government's central bank or treasury, with Field 70 containing the export permit number and invoice reference. Second, an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit subject to UCP600 is preferred for the main tranche, issued by the buyer's bank in favour of the New Zealand exporter, with negotiation against the required presentation set (commercial invoice, packing list, customs and MFAT export permit, certificate of origin, port-to-port transport documents, and a certificate of final acceptance). Third, a Documentary Collection at sight DP is acceptable for smaller-ticket or repeat ballistic-hull orders, where the bank releases the bill of lading only against payment. All payment messages must be pre-screened against UN, OFAC, and EU consolidated sanctions lists, and no cash, traveller's cheque, or crypto-asset settlement is permitted.Deliverable NZD/USD forward contracts for committed contract amounts, with a maximum tenor of 24 months and no speculative profit motive, executed under an ISDA Master Agreement or an NZ bank treasury line.Cross-currency swaps only for material long-term foreign-currency borrowings linked to project finance, where both principal and interest flows are supported by the military supply contract.Non-deliverable forwards are generally unavailable and unnecessary for NZD because the currency is fully deliverable and liberalised; NDFs may only be approved for hedging incidental non-deliverable emerging-market currency receipts and only when a Tier-1 bank arranges them as a back-to-back hedge against the underlying invoice.FX options are not within the standard treasury remit and will be considered only for firm bid bonds or tenders where the purchase order is contractual; naked options are prohibited.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% non-resident withholding tax on gross outbound dividends. No domestic free-zone reduction is available. Under most New Zealand income tax treaties the rate is reduced to 15% generally and to 5% for a corporate shareholder beneficially owning at least 10% of the payer's voting power. Fully imputed dividends may benefit from the foreign investor tax credit regime in specific circumstances.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: IRD expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for cross-border associated-party transactions where annual consideration with a connected person meets or exceeds NZD 1,000,000. A Master File is expected for globally significant groups, and a Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for a New Zealand-resident ultimate parent of a multinational group with consolidated revenue of at least NZD 1.2 billion (approximately EUR 750 million). Defense transactions with government-related parties and sovereign instrumentalities will be scrutinised for pricing, benefit, and economic substance.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. Economic substance is nevertheless required through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1, the anti-artificial transaction rules, thin capitalisation rules under subpart FE, and the nexus requirement in s DA 1. For a defense manufacturing NZ limited company, IRD will identify the core income-generating activities and verify that board meetings, engineering decisions, procurement, bank accounts, and operational control are actually carried on in New Zealand. A shell company receiving military procurement flows without staff, premises, or real substance will be disregarded and its tax benefits reversed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent filing of defense contract income

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 s 141E imposes a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the evaded tax; s 143B creates the criminal offence of evasion or similar offence punishable by imprisonment for up to five years. The Commissioner may reconstruct income, deny deductions, charge use-of-money interest, and issue a departure prohibition order under s 157.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the evasion can be personally assessed for the company's tax shortfall; the corporate veil will be pierced because the company was used as an instrument of fraud. Courts may order restoration of company assets under Companies Act 1993 s 301, and directors may be restrained from travelling, have personal bank and trust assets frozen under the Criminal Proceeds Recovery Act 2009, and face extradition and imprisonment for up to five years.
Transfer pricing, thin capitalisation and artificial intercompany arrangements

Statutory sanction: IRD will adjust consideration to arm's length under Income Tax Act 2007 s GC 7, apply thin capitalisation adjustments under subpart FE, deny deductions under s DA 1, and impose shortfall penalties under TAA ss 141C and 141D. Arrangements designed to defeat the purpose of the transfer pricing rules can be voided under s GA 1.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the related-party agreements and cause the company to trade while insolvent face personal liability under Companies Act 1993 s 135 for reckless trading and s 137 for breach of the duty of care. Civil recovery may extend to director's personal assets, and knowingly certifying false transfer pricing documentation may expose the director to prosecution for tax fraud and a travel ban pending payment of the adjusted tax liabilities.
AML/CFT breach and shell company abuse in military procurement payments

Statutory sanction: The Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 imposes civil penalties of up to NZD 2,000,000 for a body corporate and up to NZD 200,000 for an individual director for serious non-compliance. Money laundering under Crimes Act 1961 s 243 carries imprisonment for up to seven years. Breach of defense export licensing conditions can lead to forfeiture of the tactical vehicles, revocation of export privileges, and criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the payment flows without effective AML controls are personally liable for the civil penalties and may be banned from managing companies under Companies Act 1993 s 385. The corporate veil will be pierced where the company is a facade for laundering procurement proceeds, and directors' personal assets, homes, and overseas bank accounts may be restrained under the Criminal Proceeds Recovery Act 2009. Directors can be blacklisted, prohibited from leaving New Zealand, extradited to the requesting state, and imprisoned for money laundering or sanctions evasion.
