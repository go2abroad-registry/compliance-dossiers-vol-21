Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, an operational arm of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), accessible through the New Zealand Companies Register at www.companiesoffice.govt.nz.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No 105), principally sections 10, 12, 20, 21, 24, 34, 35, 122, 126, 131, 132, 150 and 156; complemented by the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 for any public offer provisions and the Companies (Fees) Regulations 2018.

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is required under the Companies Act 1993. The International Company (IC) may be incorporated with nil issued capital; where share capital is issued, it may be in any currency, need not be paid up at incorporation, and no escrow account or capital deposit obligation applies. Shares may be issued to non-resident shareholders without restriction.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required only for physical premises under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017, including a fire evacuation scheme if more than 10 persons are employed; building warrant of fitness must be maintained for any building with a compliance schedule; structural load inspection is not applicable for standard office fit-out unless mezzanine or extraordinary server rack loading is installed, in which case a structural engineer's certificate is required; workplace clearance is required via WorkSafe New Zealand registration for ACC and compliance with the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; online portal myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz (myIR secure online services for registration, GST returns, and taxpayer account management; NZBN obtained via the Companies Office at https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident International Company (CK_IC) with no fixed establishment or taxable activity in New Zealand, no NZ IRD number is statutorily required. If an NZ tax presence arises, the foreign entity must apply for an IRD number using the relevant non-resident application form (IR596 for companies) available through myIR; if the entity is incorporated or registered in NZ, obtain an NZBN through the Companies Office and then apply for the IRD number through the NZBN registration workflow. For GST purposes, registration is mandatory only if the entity makes taxable supplies in New Zealand in excess of the NZD 60,000 threshold, or voluntarily elects registration; a wholly offshore helpdesk SaaS provider supplying exclusively to non-resident customers outside New Zealand should not register for NZ GST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box election and does not recognise hybrid mismatch check-the-box elections for foreign entities. The CK_IC International Company is classified under New Zealand tax law as a non-resident company; absent a New Zealand permanent establishment or New Zealand-sourced income, it falls outside the NZ income tax base. Tax elections available in the NZ indirect tax regime include voluntary GST registration and GST grouping under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, but these are inapplicable to a wholly offshore zero-rated helpdesk business unless an NZ GST nexus is created.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000: compulsory GST registration applies to any person carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand whose total supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value imported goods supplied to consumers in New Zealand are also subject to the same NZD 60,000 compulsory registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

New Zealand zero-rates the supply of exported goods where the supplier exports the goods to a destination outside New Zealand within 28 days of supply, and zero-rates remote services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not in New Zealand when the services are performed, provided the supply has no direct nexus with land or goods in New Zealand. Multi-channel omni-helpdesk and conversational bot cloud services supplied as SaaS to overseas customers fall within the zero-rated remote-services category and therefore carry 0% GST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (approved Customs entry evidencing physical exportation of goods, where goods are supplied)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (on-board evidence of export delivery to a destination outside New Zealand)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign payment receipt (evidencing offshore consideration and the non-resident status of the recipient)

None. New Zealand has no national real-time fiscal clearance, no government digital stamping, and no statutory electronic invoicing mandate. E-invoicing may be voluntarily used through the PEPPOL network and is accepted by Inland Revenue for record-keeping purposes; GST compliance is effected through periodic GST returns filed via myIR, not through transaction-level tax clearance.

Inland Revenue may audit zero-rating claims and require the registered person to produce evidence of export or non-resident recipient status within a statutory timeframe. Fraudulent or unsubstantiated export zero-rating triggers a GST shortfall penalty of up to 150% for evasion, abatement of GST refunds, use-of-money interest from the date of the erroneous refund, and prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Strict liability penalties may apply where documented export evidence is not retained for the statutory seven-year record-keeping period; retrospective GST assessments and clawbacks can reach back up to four years for non-fraudulent errors and indefinitely for fraud, including criminal sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no exchange controls, no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distributions, or liquidation proceeds, and no licensing requirements for currency conversion. The only substantive friction is the Overseas Investment Act 2021 consent regime, which is asset-specific (sensitive land, fishing quotas, and significant business assets above designated foreign-investment thresholds) rather than transaction-specific. A remote helpdesk/SaaS business with no New Zealand-sourced physical assets or sensitive land triggers no such consent; therefore the IC can freely repatriate profits and convert NZD to any major currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no prescribed FX conversion ratio. Exporters and service providers may hold foreign currency indefinitely in offshore or onshore settlement accounts. For balance-of-payments statistical purposes, reporting banks must capture transaction-level purpose codes through the New Zealand Foreign Exchange reporting gateway and may request underlying commercial invoices or service contracts; failure to provide such documentation creates a banking-process impediment, not a regulatory violation.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), with the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) overseeing capital-account entry requirements only for sensitive assets; there is no standalone Foreign Exchange Control Agency. All foreign-exchange transactions must comply with the Reserve Bank of New Zealand prudential reporting framework and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act) administered by the Ministry of Justice and Department of Internal Affairs.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM-LOW. New Zealand has no dedicated e-money institution (EMI) statute comparable to the UK or EU e-money frameworks. An offshore digital EMI cannot independently access the Reserve Bank's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) and cannot provide a fully compliant local clearing relationship without a physical New Zealand presence. Foreign EMIs may operate as money remitters under a registration from the Department of Internal Affairs under the AML/CFT Act, but they cannot onboard the IC as a banking-grade institutional client. Strategic recommendation: use a domestic tier-1 NZ bank as the settlement anchor for Stripe Billing and PayPal rails, with any offshore EMI restricted to ancillary multi-currency wallet support subject to the NZ bank's outsourcing approval.

30% statutory NRWT on dividends paid to non-residents. The rate is reduced to 15% for fully imputed dividends (dividends carrying a full imputation credit). DTA relief may reduce the rate to 15% or 5% for a substantial shareholding (typically 10% or more).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a standard 31 March balance date, the filed IR4 return is due by 7 July for a self-filer. If the taxpayer uses a registered tax agent, the return may be filed up to 31 March of the following income year. Provisional tax installments for a 31 March balance date fall on 28 August, 15 January, and 7 May.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million (administrative safe-harbor for maintaining contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation); Country-by-Country reporting applies to ultimate parent entities of multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750 million or more. No mandatory local file / master file threshold is prescribed by statute, but documentation must be prepared and retained if cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZD 10 million in an income year.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a separate 'Economic Substance Regulations' regime equivalent to offshore financial centres. However, legal and commercial substance is central to determining tax residence and the application of the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR). A company incorporated in New Zealand is ordinarily tax resident; a foreign-incorporated company may also be resident if its central management and control are in NZ. Inland Revenue can re-characterise, disregard, or reconstruct arrangements lacking genuine commercial substance under the GAAR and transfer pricing rules. A taxpayer that is a pure shell without real personnel, premises, or business risk may be denied treaty benefits and subjected to re-assessments and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Concealment of Income

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty of 150% of the resulting tax shortfall under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Criminal prosecution for evasion may result in a fine of up to NZD 100,000 for an individual and NZD 250,000 for a body corporate, plus imprisonment for up to 5 years. Unpaid tax, penalties, and interest remain collectable through forced asset recovery.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns or participate in concealment are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax shortfall. Directors may be disqualified, subjected to asset restraint orders, and in criminal proceedings may face bail conditions restricting international travel, ultimately leading to imprisonment.
Artificial Shell Company / Abuse of Tax-Residency

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may invoke the GAAR to disregard the shell structure, re-determine the true resident taxpayer, and assess additional tax. A shortfall penalty of 100% for an abusive tax position may be imposed, plus interest and penalties. The company may also face deregistration under the Companies Act 1993 for lack of proper office or statutory declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors of an artificial shell can be personally liable for unpaid taxes assessed on the company if they were involved in the arrangement. Court may order repayment of assets stripped out, issue director disqualification, and in serious cases impose civil contempt or criminal penalties including travel restrictions while proceedings are ongoing.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue will make a transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income, resulting in additional corporate tax at 28%, use-of-money interest, and shortfall penalties of 20%, 50%, or 100% depending on whether the position was not reasonably arguable, taken without reasonable care, or unsubstantiated. The OECD-approved methods are applied.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in pricing decisions with intent to shift profits may be personally liable for penalties if they signed incorrect tax returns as directors. They can be sued for breach of directors' duties for exposing the company to excessive tax risk, may face asset preservation orders, and in flagrant cases, extradition or travel bans may be imposed by a criminal court once enforcement proceedings are initiated.
Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT)

Statutory sanction: The payer company is liable for the unpaid NRWT plus late payment penalties (1% upfront and 1.5% monthly) and interest. A shortfall penalty of 30% may apply for failing to take reasonable care, or 100% for deliberate wrongdoing. Inland Revenue may also recover the tax directly from the directors.

Director personal liability: Under the Tax Administration Act, directors are personally liable for tax amounts the company is required to withhold and pay to the IRD, including NRWT on dividends, interest, and royalties. This liability is strict and extends to a board member who knows or ought reasonably to have known that the tax was not being paid. Travel bans may be issued by the court in enforcement proceedings for fraudulent concealment of withheld tax.
