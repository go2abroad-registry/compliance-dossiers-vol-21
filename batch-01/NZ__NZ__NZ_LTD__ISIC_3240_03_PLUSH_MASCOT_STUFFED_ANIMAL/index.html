Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) — the national tax authority; online portal myIR. New Zealand Customs Service operates the Trade Single Window (TSW)/Joint Electronic Trade Single Window (JETSW). Company registration and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) system are administered by the New Zealand Companies Office.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident entities owning or operating an NZ Ltd do not directly hold the company's IRD number; the NZ Ltd is the taxpayer. Step 1: Incorporate with the Companies Office — an NZBN and a company IRD number are automatically allocated. Step 2: If the NZ Ltd is already live and needs to confirm or replace its IRD identity, access myIR via the NZBN, verify registered directors and ultimate beneficial ownership, and use the Inland Revenue business tax registration flows to activate GST and PAYE filing obligations. Step 3: For a non-resident company that is not incorporated in New Zealand but is carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand, file the non-resident company IRD number application with Inland Revenue by completing the relevant myIR non-resident entity form, attaching certified certificate of incorporation or equivalent commercial registry extract from the parent jurisdiction, registered office address, principal officers, and a declaration of the New Zealand taxable activity. Step 4: Apply for GST registration via myIR after the company is in existence; IRD will issue the GST number (same IRD number with a GST suffix) and activate the myIR account. Step 5: Apply for a New Zealand Customs customer/exporter code through the Trade Single Window for customs declarations. There are no statutory minimum capital or share capital filing fees for IRD purposes; registration is effective from the date specified on the application and is free of charge.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand does not have a US-style check-the-box election. An eligible closely held New Zealand company may elect look-through company (LTC) status under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, causing profits and losses to flow through to qualifying shareholders if the statutory composition and residency requirements are met. A standard non-resident-owned NZ Ltd cannot elect general pass-through treatment; it remains entity-level-taxed at the 28% company rate. LTC status is not available to all foreign groups and is separate from the imputation credit account (ICA) regime, which an NZ company may elect to maintain to attach imputation credits to dividends. Hybrid mismatch provisions in Subpart FH may apply to deny deductions in cross-border arrangements; no further elective classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of total taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period, or NZD 60,000 projected taxable supplies for the next 12 months; non-resident suppliers of remote services and electronic marketplaces must register for GST at the same NZD 60,000 threshold when supplying remote services to New Zealand resident consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under sections 11 and 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, manufactured plush mascots and stuffed animals are zero-rated for New Zealand GST if they are physically exported by the supplier (or by an on-sale exempt non-resident customer in qualifying circumstances) within the relevant statutory export window (normally 28 days from the time of supply), and the supplier retains contemporaneous evidence of export. The supply must be a taxable supply in New Zealand; the goods must leave New Zealand and be delivered to a foreign consignee, and the supplier must hold a complete audit trail linking the invoice to the export entry, transport documentation, and foreign settlement. Services performed by the NZ OEM manufacturer for an offshore customer are zero-rated if the services are supplied to a person who is not a New Zealand resident and who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, unless an exclusion in section 11A applies (for example services directly in connection with land in New Zealand). Remote/digital services ultimately consumed in New Zealand by New Zealand residents are not zero-rated and remain subject to 15% GST under the cross-border remote services regime.

  • New Zealand Customs Service electronic Export Entry lodged through the Trade Single Window/JETSW (no paper Bill of Entry is used in New Zealand)
  • Customs export clearance record or movement reference showing the goods have left New Zealand customs control
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list supported by the correct New Zealand Tariff/HS classification (Chapter 95 for plush toys)
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill for sea freight, or Master/Air Waybill for air freight, evidencing shipment to the non-resident buyer
  • Signed purchase order/sales contract with Incoterms showing risk, insurance, and freight passing to the offshore customer
  • Foreign exchange repatriation evidence: a bank TT credit advice, foreign exchange transaction confirmation, or inward settlement statement from a New Zealand registered bank showing receipt of the export proceeds from the non-resident buyer (functional equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate / FIRC)

No mandatory real-time tax clearance, continuous transaction controls, anti-fraud digital stamping, or API fiscalisation is imposed by Inland Revenue. Suppliers must issue and retain GST tax invoices that comply with the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 record-keeping rules. E-invoicing is voluntary in New Zealand and operates through the Peppol network. For exported goods, the mandatory electronic declaration is the Customs export entry lodged through the Trade Single Window/JETSW, which is a border-control clearance mechanism rather than an e-invoicing fiscal stamp.

Inland Revenue may assess output GST at 15% for any export sale treated as zero-rated if the documentary export trail is incomplete, inconsistent, or does not prove physical export within the statutory time window. Unsupported zero-rating gives rise to a tax shortfall under the Tax Administration Act 1994, attracting use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties of 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for an unacceptable tax position, or up to 100% for a wholly or partly abusive tax position, with prosecution available in the most serious civil fraud and evasion cases. Under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, false export declarations and false evidence of export are separate offenses and may result in penalties, forfeiture of goods, and criminal conviction. The exporter should expect audit scrutiny where the same goods are declared for export but re-entered to New Zealand, where freight documentation does not match weights or container numbers, or where payment receipts are absent or unrelated to the consignment. Retrospective clawback of input GST previously claimed on production costs and additional GST on the full consideration can also be assessed in the same adjustment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar is fully and freely convertible on both current and capital account. There are no prior-approval mechanisms, foreign-exchange allocation quotas, or discriminatory conversion taxes applicable to an NZ Ltd engaged in plush mascot OEM production and export. Dividends, loan interest, trade receivables, capital redemptions, and liquidation proceeds may be repatriated to non-resident shareholders or counterparties through registered banks without material FX impediment, subject only to standard non-resident withholding tax obligations and Companies Office corporate formalities. Exchange rates are market-determined, and banks set commercial spreads without regulatory restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender rules. There is no statutory requirement to repatriate export earnings, convert foreign currency into NZD, or hold export proceeds in a New Zealand bank account for any prescribed period. An exporter of plush toys and stuffed animal mascots may freely retain foreign currency, hold receivables through overseas payment platforms, or settle in the currency agreed with the buyer. As a prudential practice, an exporter should retain shipping, customs, and commercial invoices to satisfy bank due diligence and enable efficient clearing of inbound trade payments.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) as the central bank and prudential regulator, operating in conjunction with the Overseas Investment Office for foreign-investment screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005. New Zealand maintains an open exchange-control system; no exchange-control permits, currency conversion licenses, or surrender obligations are imposed on ordinary corporate trade or capital transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH as a supplementary cross-border payment rail and medium as a sole primary banking relationship. Offshore digital electronic money institutions such as Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut Business, and Currencycloud are not registered as banks in New Zealand, are not permitted to hold deposits as a banking business, and are not covered by New Zealand deposit protection. They are nevertheless viable for USD, EUR, GBP, and CNH remittances, platform payouts, and low-value trade settlements, provided they meet their home-jurisdiction AML/CFT obligations and maintain verifiable transactional records. Legal constraint: a New Zealand operating company should retain at least one tier-1 local bank account as the liquidity anchor and for statutory regulatory recognition; an EMI account can be used as a treasury overlay, but not as the exclusive settlement venue for trade or tax obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward capital for the New Zealand Ltd should be routed through SWIFT MT103 or high-value telegraphic transfer to the company's tier-1 bank account in NZD or, where preferable, USD to the bank's USD correspondent account. New Zealand does not prescribe a government purpose code for every inward payment, but the bank's remittance field must clearly identify the payment as 'Shareholder Loan', 'Equity Contribution', or 'Trade Proceeds' accompanied by the company legal name. The bank will apply sanctions screening and source-of-funds verification, so the remitting account must be held in the name of the same subscribing shareholder or lender. Board minutes, an updated share register, and a written loan agreement should be available. Where the transaction involves sensitive land, aquaculture, or significant business assets above the Overseas Investment Act thresholds, prior consent from the Overseas Investment Office may be required; for ordinary trading and asset-light plush toy OEM operations, cash capital contributions are freely permitted without exchange-control consent.Outbound supplier settlements to overseas factories, particularly Chinese or Southeast Asian plush manufacturers, should be executed through: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct bank-to-bank supplier payment; (2) a confirmed at-sight or usance documentary letter of credit where supplier trade risk requires bank interposition; and (3) platform-based payout rails such as Wise, Airwallex, PayPal, or Stripe for low-value recurring royalties or small consignments. Domestic New Zealand dollar clearing is settled through NZClear, the RBNZ-operated real-time settlement system. For every trade disbursement the bank will expect supporting evidence including the commercial contract, pro forma invoice, packing list, and transport document. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH as a supplementary cross-border payment rail and medium as a sole primary banking relationship. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand does not prescribe fixed dollar thresholds for Local File or Master File documentation; mandatory documentation applies where cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZ$0 on a transaction-by-transaction assessment, or where the taxpayer is a significant enterprise; the IR407 non-resident contractor's tax return and transfer pricing disclosure are required for annual non-resident contractor payments; contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared where the transaction value breaches de minimis thresholds of NZ$0 under the transfer pricing guidelines, and larger taxpayers must file an International Dealings Schedule (IR901).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand applies a general anti-avoidance rule under s BG 1 and s GA 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Economic Substance Guidelines may apply under the tax residence rules for companies incorporated offshore; a New Zealand incorporated company is tax resident unless its centre of management is outside New Zealand; the commercial substance doctrine tests arrangements against their legal form and economic reality, including the New Zealand controlled foreign company rules under the CFC regime that attribute passive income from low-taxed offshore subsidiaries to New Zealand shareholders.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994; maximum fine of NZ$50,000 for each offence; evaded tax plus penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall and civil penalty of 100% for evasion or fraudulent intent; imprisonment for tax evasion is available through Crimes Act charges where applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for penalties and unpaid tax under s 169 of the Tax Administration Act if tax was withheld and not remitted; personal liability orders, property seizure, travel bans under judgment enforcement, and imprisonment for up to five years may apply for egregious evasion.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits under the principal purpose test; recharacterisation of transactions under the general anti-avoidance rule; 100% shortfall penalty for abusive tax position; deregistration of company under the Companies Act 1993 and loss of limited liability protection if used to defeat creditors or tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for debts where they allow the company to carry on business with an intention to defraud creditors (civil and criminal liability under s 320 of the Companies Act); disqualification as a director and compensation orders may award restitution to Inland Revenue.
AML and Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties against reporting entities up to NZ$2 million for serious and repeat breaches; criminal penalties for natural persons up to NZ$100,000 and for companies up to NZ$2 million; the High Court may impose pecuniary penalties and freeze orders under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers can be subject to individual sanctions, dual civil-criminal liability orders, exclusion orders, and individual pecuniary penalties where they knew or ought to have known of compliance failures; extradition is possible for serious money laundering offences.
Transfer Pricing and International Tax

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income; shortfall penalty generally 20% of the resulting tax, increasing to 100% for evasion or lack of reasonable care; disclosure penalties for failure to file the International Dealings Schedule (IR901); interest on unpaid tax applies.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for avoidable tax liabilities where the company is insolvent and they participated in pricing decisions without reasonable proper enquiry; penalties may be assessed against directors personally where they signed false returns.
