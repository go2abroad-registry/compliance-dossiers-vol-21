Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; online portal: myIR at https://www.myir.ird.govt.nz (primary taxpayer portal) with official guidance published at https://www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A non-resident company deriving New Zealand-source income or carrying on a GST-registrable taxable activity in New Zealand must first obtain an IRD number (the statutory TIN). Procedure: (1) Complete IRD form IR596 - Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application; (2) lodge via the myIR online portal, through an approved tax agent, or by paper to Inland Revenue; (3) attach certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered office address, director details, and ultimate beneficial ownership information; (4) concurrently file GST registration form IR356 if the company makes or intends to make taxable supplies exceeding the NZD 60,000 threshold (or if voluntary GST registration is sought); (5) upon verification, IRD issues the IRD number, which is then used for income tax, GST, PAYE, and all customs/GST interface obligations. Non-resident companies are not required to maintain minimum capital for tax registration purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A New Zealand company is a separate taxable person taxed at the entity level on worldwide income at a flat corporate rate of 28% under the full imputation regime; non-resident companies are taxable only on New Zealand-source income unless the foreign-source income is derived through a New Zealand branch. A Look-Through Company (LTC) election, if validly made, reclassifies the entity as transparent/pass-through for income tax purposes. Territorial scope rule: New Zealand residents are taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credits; non-residents are taxed on New Zealand-sourced income only. No tax-exempt zone classification applies to standard onshore manufacturing operations.

Available election forms

Available elections under the Income Tax Act 2007: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HG, allowing qualifying close-held companies with five or fewer shareholders to be treated as fiscally transparent; (2) Consolidated group election under Part FM, allowing a wholly-owned New Zealand corporate group to elect to be treated as a single consolidated entity for income tax and imputation purposes; (3) imputation group election, enabling wholly-owned New Zealand-resident companies to pool imputation credits; (4) branch equivalent tax account (BETA) election for non-resident companies carrying on a branch in New Zealand; (5) hybrid mismatch rules under subpart FH apply automatically to cross-border structures and are not elective, requiring assessment of all NZ and foreign hybrid arrangements (including hybrids, reverse hybrids, and imported mismatches). Qualifying Company (QC) elections are no longer available for newly incorporated companies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000: GST registration is compulsory once the value of taxable supplies in any 12-month period exceeds NZD 60,000 (including export sales unless zero-rated and excluded from the turnover count where properly zero-rated); voluntary registration is available from the first dollar of taxable activity. Offshore suppliers of remote services to New Zealand consumers are also subject to a NZD 60,000 registration threshold under the remote services regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods may be zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the GST Act 1985 if the goods are exported from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply and the supplier has in its possession the prescribed evidence of export at the time the return is filed. The goods must be supplied to a non-resident customer and must be exported by the supplier or by an agent on the supplier's behalf; title and practical possession must pass to the customer overseas. Canned boba/tapioca pearls (ISIC 1030) qualify for zero-rating where the goods leave New Zealand within the statutory timeframe, the export is supported by a NZ Customs export declaration, and the supplier retains contemporaneous documentary proof. Services are zero-rated under section 11A only when supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, and the services are not connected to New Zealand land or physical property; export manufacturing services supplied to an offshore principal may qualify where the criteria are met.

  • NZ Customs Export Declaration (export entry lodged electronically via the Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank-certified evidence of receipt of foreign currency proceeds from the overseas buyer)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • GST zero-rating documentary evidence worksheet retained in the supplier's records (including contract of sale and insurance/transport documents)

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, authorisation, digital stamping, or orchestrated API clearance mandate for GST invoices. The regime is self-assessment based, with GST returns filed electronically through myIR on a 1-month, 2-month, or 6-month filing cycle. E-invoicing through the Peppol network is permitted and voluntary; central government agencies are required to receive e-invoices via Peppol, but suppliers are not legally mandated to issue them. No national e-invoicing clearance database or transaction-level reporting obligation applies to ordinary exported goods.

IRD cross-matches NZ Customs export entry data, GST returns, and bank remittance records to verify zero-rating claims. If zero-rating is claimed without valid export evidence or where goods do not leave New Zealand within 28 days of supply, the supply is reassessed at 15% GST with use-of-money interest running from the original due date. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply as follows: 20% of the tax shortfall for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or fraudulent intent. Criminal prosecution may also be commenced for knowingly providing false or misleading documents. The reassessment limitation period is generally four years from the filing of the return, extended to seven years where no return is filed, and unlimited in cases of evasion or fraud. Exporters must retain supporting documents (customs entries, shipping documents, remittance evidence, contracts) for at least seven years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account. There are no exchange-control approvals, surrender requirements, or central-bank restrictions on the remittance of dividends, loan interest, management fees, royalties, or invested capital from an NZ limited company to non-resident shareholders. Payments are nevertheless subject to banking-level anti-money-laundering verification, sanctions screening under New Zealand and allied jurisdictions, and New Zealand income-tax withholding regimes applicable to non-resident payees.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory repatriation windows, and no required conversion ratios. Exporting companies may retain sales receipts in foreign currency, provided the resulting foreign-currency receivables, bank balances, and exchange movements are properly reflected in financial statements and are taxable under New Zealand’s worldwide income rules. Customs export entries must still be filed through the Trade Single Window, and trade-finance lenders may require export proceeds to be routed to a designated collection account as security.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand — Te Pūtea Matua. New Zealand maintains no separate foreign-exchange-control administration; the Reserve Bank supervises prudential banking conduct while sanctions and counter-terrorism financing screening are implemented under New Zealand’s Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and United Nations sanctions regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-to-Low for primary treasury use. Offshore digital EMI and payment-service platforms may provide convenient vendor-payment corridors or a secondary settlement wallet, but they are not prudentially regulated banks in New Zealand and cannot lawfully provide ordinary banking deposit services to an NZ company without an appropriate licence. A New Zealand-incorporated manufacturer should maintain its principal operating and settlement account at a Reserve-Bank-licensed bank. An offshore EMI can complement, not replace, the local banking relationship; legal constraints arise under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and depositor-protection principles applicable to deposit takers.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$10 million – IRD expected materiality threshold for contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation covering cross-border related-party transactions; NZ$1.12 billion group consolidated revenue triggers Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR). No statutory local-file/master-file threshold exists, but IRD can require documentation for smaller transactions based on risk.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no separate Economic Substance Regulations of the UAE type; however, economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in Income Tax Act 2007 s BG1, judicial substance-over-form doctrine, and the Principal Purpose Test in double-tax treat agreements. A NZ Ltd used solely as a shell or conduit without real management, employees, premises, or genuine business activities will be denied treaty benefits and may be subject to anti-avoidance recharacterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under Tax Administration Act 1994 ss 143 and 147B; on indictment, imprisonment up to five years and/or fines up to NZ$50,000. Civil criminal penalty exposure includes shortfall penalties of 40% (lack of reasonable care), 75% (unacceptable tax position), or 150% (evasion or abusive tax position), plus use-of-money interest on unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute false returns or direct fraudulent transfer pricing may be jointly and severally liable for the company’s unpaid taxes; criminal conviction can result in travel bans, disqualification under the Companies Act 1993, and forfeiture orders under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment and reconstruction under s BG1 / s GA1.0 (anti-avoidance) to deny tax benefits; DTA relief denied under the Principal Purpose Test; penalties up to 100–150% of the avoided tax; strike-off under Companies Act 1993 s 318 for failure to carry on business or for serious abuse of corporate form.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for breach of fiduciary duties and duties to act in good faith and not to trade dishonestly (Companies Act ss 131, 135); courts may impose civil compensation, disqualification orders, and restraint/travel bans pending asset recovery.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: IRD tax adjustment to the arm’s length profit; shortfall penalties of 20% (unacceptable tax position) up to 100% (dominant arrangement); denial of interest deductions for non-arm’s-length loans; late payment interest applies. CbCR threshold breaches attract additional reporting penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or approve pricing policies knowing they defeat NZ tax liability can be held personally liable for penalties and interest; also subject to private damages actions for breach of director duties and criminal liability for false certification of TP documentation.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under AML/CFT Act 2009, companies and directors face civil penalties up to NZ$4 million for serious compliance failures; criminal liability includes imprisonment up to two years for knowingly laundering money; freezing orders and forfeiture are available under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false trade documents or authorise suspicious payment routing render themselves personally liable to criminal prosecution; asset restraint orders, travel bans, passport suspension, and seizure of personal assets can be imposed to secure confiscation.
