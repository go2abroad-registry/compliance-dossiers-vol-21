Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

New Zealand imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a company limited by shares. Shares may be issued at no par value, with consideration being fixed by the board; accordingly the first board resolution authorises 1,000 ordinary shares at NZ$1.00 each (NZ$1,000 total) credited as fully paid. No escrow account or capital deposit requirement applies to a standard retail holding and e-commerce company.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) clearance: leased office or warehouse premises require an approved fire evacuation scheme where the floor area exceeds 500 square metres or the employee count exceeds 10, in accordance with the Fire Safety and Evacuation of Buildings Regulations 2006. WorkSafe NZ registration and a general risk assessment are mandatory employer duties under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including first-aid facilities, an accident register, and hazard controls for manual handling of heavy camping gear. Structural load inspection certificates are not required for standard commercial warehousing, but the building must hold a current building warrant of fitness and must not have an uncleared earthquake-prone building status under the Building Act 2004 earthquake-prone building provisions.

Paid-in capital rule: New Zealand imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a company limited by shares. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Tauhake), New Zealand Government; taxpayer portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz); Companies Office (New Zealand) for overseas company registration

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity is not required to register for New Zealand income tax unless it is a New Zealand-resident company or derives New Zealand-source income. For GST, registration is compulsory for any person, including an International Company, carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand with annual supplies over NZD 60,000, or for non-resident suppliers of remote services / low-value imported goods supplied to New Zealand consumers (no threshold applies to such non-resident suppliers). Procedure: (1) confirm non-residence and absence of permanent establishment; (2) apply online via myIR using the 'Non-resident GST registration' / 'Overseas company' application; (3) submit company incorporation certificate, overseas GST/VAT equivalent certificate, directors' details, a New Zealand address for service, and the intended start date of supplies; (4) receive a New Zealand GST number and a 9-digit IRD number. For a company already carrying on business in New Zealand, register as an overseas company with the Companies Office and complete the IRD 'New Zealand company registration' form IRD501. Where treaty relief is needed, the International Company may also apply to Inland Revenue for a certificate of non-residence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand has no U.S.-style check-the-box election. The only domestic corporate pass-through election is the look-through company (LTC) election under subpart HA of the Income Tax Act 2007, available to a company with five or fewer shareholders and certain other eligibility conditions; an International Company that is non-resident and derives no New Zealand-source income will not utilise the LTC election. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are subject to statutory adjustments under subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007, but no substantive election exists; entity classification for New Zealand tax follows accounting substance, control, and ownership tests. Double tax agreement tie-breaker memoranda may be filed where residence is ambiguous.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (annual taxable supplies threshold); zero threshold for non-resident suppliers of remote services and cross-border low-value imported goods (goods valued at or below NZD 1,000) supplied to New Zealand non-business consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods physically exported by the supplier from New Zealand to a destination outside New Zealand, provided the goods are exported within 28 days of payment (or within any further period the Commissioner allows) and the prescribed export evidence is held (section 111, GST Act 1985). Services may be zero-rated as offshore services under section 11A where supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed. Internet retailing of outdoor camping and sports gear to overseas customers qualifies when the supplier sends the goods to an overseas address and the export is properly evidenced.

  • Customs Export Declaration (lodged electronically via the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and purchase order framing the export sale
  • Bank receipt or Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of overseas proceeds in the exporter's account

No real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no API-based government e-invoicing mandate in New Zealand. e-Invoicing is voluntary and PEPPOL-based; a registered person must nevertheless issue a compliant tax invoice and retain sufficient transactional records. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR on the monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly filing cycle. Customs export declarations are lodged electronically through the New Zealand Trade Single Window (Customs CSD).

Inland Revenue may audit export zero-rating claims and recover any overclaimed refund (clawback) together with use-of-money interest. The Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes civil shortfall penalties: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 150% for evasion or an arrangement designed to defeat GST, and 25% for an abusive tax position. Criminal prosecution for GST fraud can result in imprisonment. A retrospectively invalid zero-rating claim may be reassessed as a taxable supply at 15% GST, with the tax itself recoverable in addition to penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a freely floating currency with full convertibility on both capital and current account transactions. There are no exchange control restrictions under New Zealand law governing the conversion of NZD or remittance of profits, dividends, or capital abroad for non-resident entities. The only practical hurdles are bank-level KYC/AML verification of underlying commercial substance and the requirement to report certain international transactions to the Reserve Bank under the Balance of Payments framework. Profit remittance is subject to standard non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) or an approved Domestic Withholding Payment regime if tax-resident in New Zealand, but an exempt International Company with no New Zealand tax nexus may remit freely after satisfying banking due diligence. The New Zealand Overseas Investment Act 2005 applies only to sensitive land and significant business assets, not to routine e-commerce capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no mandatory surrender or compelled conversion of export proceeds. Export receipts, including payments for cross-border internet retail of outdoor camping equipment, may be retained in any foreign currency or converted to NZD at commercial rates without any statutory timeline or ratio requirement. Exporters must, however, provide accurate transaction reporting for balance-of-payments purposes; this is facilitated by banks through standard ‘V’ prefix (goods) and service codes, but does not involve remittance surrender or conversion mandates.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), operating under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, with delegated foreign exchange administration and prudential supervision powers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Legally viable and commercially feasible at a High rating for a low-risk e-commerce entity, provided the offshore digital EMI does not hold itself out as a New Zealand bank and does not conduct reserve-banking activities in New Zealand. Such EMIs must not accept deposits from the New Zealand public unless licensed under the Prudential Regime. For NZD settlement, the EMI typically operates through a sponsorship or partner arrangement with a registered New Zealand trading bank. Key constraints include AMLCFT due diligence under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AMLCFTA), registration under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 (FSPA) if providing financial services to New Zealand residents, and compliance with the Financial Markets Authority's (FMA) rules. For the subject International Company with no local operations, using a foreign-incorporated EMI (e.g., Wise or Airwallex) to hold EUR/USD and pay global suppliers is feasible and avoids many NZ bank physical-presence requirements, while a NZD collection account via an EMI that routes to a NZ banking partner remains practical for payouts to New Zealand domestic vendors.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from the non-resident ultimate parent are executed via SWIFT MT103 transfers to the account of the New Zealand International Company, using the purpose code 'Equity Capital Injection' under New Zealand's balance-of-payments reporting standards. Each wire must reference the underlying shareholder resolution and share allotment register, state the transaction's nature (DE equity capital with no goods/services in return), and comply with the bank's source-of-funds affirmation. If the incoming amount exceeds NZD 100,000 or is for the purchase of sensitive New Zealand business assets, supplemental Overseas Investment Act screening and RBNZ reporting may be triggered; for a pure e-commerce trading vehicle these thresholds do not apply. Verification includes matching the remitter's corporate name against the shareholding registry, providing TIN or unique transaction identifier, and submitting a statutory declaration of non-involvement in money laundering or terrorism financing.Supplier payments and international trade settlements are cleared primarily through SWIFT MT103/MT202 messages for direct wholesale remittances (e.g., Chinese camping-goods factories, Vietnamese textile suppliers, logistics brokers). For high-value or repetitive procurements, a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) advised through a New Zealand confirming bank provides title security and uses the UCP 600 framework. Daily e-commerce settlements from Stripe, PayPal, and card acquirers are cleared through Automated Clearing House (ACH) in USD/EUR/GBP or NZD and then pooled via a multi-currency wallet or settlement bank. The clearing rails must be aligned with the merchant agreement's payout currency, and all outbound disbursements for trade must carry ISO 20022 transaction codes identifying the merchandise category (i.e., camping/hiking gear under ISIC 4791) and the underlying commercial invoice number. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% (standard New Zealand corporate rate imposed by Income Tax Act 2007, Schedule 1, Part B)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

NRWT of 15% on fully imputed dividends and 30% on unimputed dividends paid by a New Zealand-resident company to non-resident shareholders (Income Tax Act 2007 ss NF 1–NF 8); DTA-protected rates commonly 5%–15% (e.g., NZ–US DTA provides 5% on dividends to a 10%+ corporate shareholder; NZ–Australia DTA provides 15% general/5% on qualifying shareholdings).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million aggregate annual consideration for cross-border related-party arrangements, triggering mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation (Income Tax Act 2007 s GC 8 and Tax Administration Act 1994 s 78D); Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting required for NZ-resident multinational groups with consolidated global revenue above NZD 1.1 billion (EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute in the Cayman/BVI sense; however, an International Company claiming foreign-source income exemption must demonstrate genuine economic substance and non-resident management and control (Income Tax Act 2007 s YD 2). The General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s BG 1) permits Inland Revenue to reconstruct a shell entity as a New Zealand tax resident and impose full tax at 28% on worldwide income. The Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 likewise requires reporting entities operating retailer payment rails (Stripe/PayPal) to maintain verified customer due diligence and beneficial-ownership records.
