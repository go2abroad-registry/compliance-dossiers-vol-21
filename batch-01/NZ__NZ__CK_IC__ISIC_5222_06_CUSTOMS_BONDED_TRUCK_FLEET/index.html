Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand; online portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz; export and customs clearance also coordinated by New Zealand Customs Service (Te Mana Ārai o Aotearoa) through the Trade Single Window (TSW) platform.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident International Company (IC) must obtain a New Zealand IRD number before commencing any NZ-source supply chain activity. Procedure: (1) Determine if the IC is a 'non-resident company' under the Income Tax Act 2007 s YD(3)–(4); (2) If the IC is not registered with the NZ Companies Office, submit Form IR596 'Non-resident companies and IRD numbers' to IRD, attaching certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitution, register of directors, and proof of offshore management and control; (3) If GST registration is required or voluntary, complete GST registration (Form IRD or via myIR) using the same IRD number; (4) Where the IC carries on a taxable activity through a registered branch in NZ, first register the overseas company on the NZ Companies Register and obtain an NZBN, then apply for the IRD number via myIR; (5) Submit original supporting documents to IRD within 30 days; IRD issues the final IRD number confirmation. No minimum capital or resident director is required for an offshore IC, but a statutory 'agent for service of process' in NZ may be needed if the IC files a Companies Act register as an overseas company.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (in substance, a non-resident IC is outside the NZ income tax base for non-NZ-sourced income; NZ applies territorial sourcing rules to non-residents, taxing only NZ-source income attributable to a NZ permanent establishment (PE) or deriving from NZ land/mineral/timber. A purely offshore-managed bonded truck fleet earning from airport feeder services conducted entirely outside NZ with no NZ PE is therefore effectively zero-rated for income tax and treated as an exempt offshore operation; if a NZ PE is inadvertently created, the PE profits become taxable at 28% corporate rate. The entity retains entity-level limited liability; it is not a pass-through entity for NZ domestic purposes unless it elects look-through company status, which is generally unavailable to a non-resident IC.)

Available election forms

NZ does not have a 'check-the-box' regime. Relevant elections: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under the Income Tax Act 2007 ss HB 1–HB 20, available only to NZ-resident close companies (not available to non-resident ICs); (2) Qualifying Company (QC) election (grandfathered, closed to new companies); (3) GST registration and GST grouping election under Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 s 55 (allowing a GST group of related companies); (4) GST land zero-rating election and 'choice' to recover GST on entertainment; (5) Hybrid mismatch rules under ss FH 1–FH 13 do not permit elective opt-out in respect of a foreign hybrid entity; however, the IRD allows the taxpayer to submit a 'hybrid mismatch factual statement' and may accept a corresponding taxpayer election in the foreign jurisdiction for neutralisation purposes. NZ also permits a non-resident company to elect to file consolidated NZ tax returns only if it has a NZ PE and forms a wholly-owned group with NZ residents, subject to the 66% common ownership test.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in a 12-month period for a person carrying on a taxable activity in NZ; a non-resident IC supplying international transport (including bonded road feeder services for airport cargo) to non-residents may register voluntarily from the first dollar because most supplies are zero-rated, but compulsory registration applies when turnover exceeds NZD 60,000; for remote services (including digital or cross-border transport facilitation) supplied to NZ-resident consumers, registration is compulsory when total supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period (and NZ is the unique jurisdiction applying GST to remote services to NZ consumers).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under s 11 of the GST Act 1985 to goods physically exported from NZ within 28 days of the time of supply, including goods carried by a bonded feeder truck toward an international airport for export. Zero-rating also applies under s 11A to services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside NZ when the services are performed, to services performed in connection with goods situated outside NZ, and to international transport services (including international freight movement and ancillary land transport of export cargo) under s 11A(1)(e). A bonded truck fleet operator must evidence: (a) the customer is a non-resident airline, freight forwarder, or non-resident consignor; (b) the cargo is exported within 28 days of the supply; (c) the service is directly connected to the international movement of goods; and (d) the vehicle operates under a valid Customs bonded carrier licence and GPS seal protocol. Where any of these conditions fails, or the goods are diverted to domestic consumption, the supply is taxable at 15%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusDec / Export Entry) lodged electronically via Trade Single Window, approved by NZ Customs before flight/truck departure
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Master/HAWB Air Waybill issued by the airline or consolidator, confirming the international airport destination outside NZ
  • Bonded cargo movement manifest and GPS-seal report showing seal integrity and truck route from the Customs-controlled area (CCA) to the airport export area, including the approved 'bonded carrier licence' number
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and inward remittance certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice evidencing FX payment from the non-resident airline or importer of record

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing regime. e-Invoicing is voluntary and based on the Peppol PINT framework, supported by IRD's e-Invoicing service. Mandatory digital clearance applies only at the border: exporters and customs bonded carriers must lodge export/import entries and bonded cargo movements through the Joint Border Management System (JBMS) / Trade Single Window (TSW), using EDI or API interfaces, with cargo status reports and bonded transit authorisations transmitted in near real-time. GPS-sealed bonded trucks require Customs-approved electronic seals or GPS position logging integrated with the TSW 'Bonded Carrier Movement Application'. There is no digital stamping of invoices; however, GST returns and invoices must be filed/retained electronically in myIR, and the IRD mandates electronic filing for all GST-registered persons.

IRD and NZ Customs jointly audit export zero-rating claims by bonded carriers. If documentary records (export entries, B/L, GPS seal logs, and FX evidence) are not retained for 7 years, or if goods are not exported within 28 days of supply, the zero-rating is retrospectively reversed and GST at 15% is imposed on the full value of the supply plus use-of-money interest under s 120 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 (from the original tax return date). Shortfall penalties apply under s 141 TAA: 20% for lack of reasonable care (s 141B), 40% for an abusive tax position (s 141C), and 150% for evasion (s 141D). Knowingly creating false CusDec or bonded transport records triggers criminal liability under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 s 378, with fines up to NZD 50,000 for a first offence, up to NZD 100,000 for subsequent offences, or up to 5 years' imprisonment in aggravated cases involving fraudulent GST refunds; the NZ Customs Forfeiture regime can also seize the truck fleet for tariff evasion.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully convertible currency regime with no general exchange-control legislation. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account purposes. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, interest, royalties, loan repayments, and repatriation of invested capital to non-residents are lawful without prior RBNZ consent. The practical hurdles are not regulatory but documentary and compliance-driven: the underlying transaction must be genuine, supported by commercial contracts, priced at arms' length, and demonstrably free of money-laundering or terrorist-financing risk. Tax-related impediments, such as non-resident withholding tax and thin-capitalisation rules under the Income Tax Act 2007, must be managed concurrently to ensure a clear remittance pathway.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation in New Zealand. Exporters are not required to repatriate foreign-currency receipts within any mandated timeline, nor are they required to convert a fixed percentage of export proceeds into NZD. No surrender ratio applies to customs-bonded road feeder service revenues. Nevertheless, banks will require accurate balance-of-payments purpose coding for cross-border receipts, and the settlement currency, invoice value, and transport documentation must be consistent. For foreign-currency accounts, the exporter may retain, convert, or disburse export proceeds freely, subject only to the bank's AML/CFT monitoring and sanctions screening.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ), Te Pūtea Matua; prudential supervisor and licensing authority for all registered banks in New Zealand. New Zealand maintains no separate foreign-exchange control agency. The RBNZ, in concert with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), administers the regulatory perimeter for foreign-exchange dealings, while the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act 2009) governs customer due diligence, record-keeping, and suspicious transaction reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. New Zealand does not operate a separate digital EMI (electronic money institution) licence. Any entity that receives and holds customer money must be a registered bank, a licensed non-bank deposit taker, or otherwise fall within an express exclusion under the Banking Prudential Requirements. Offshore EMIs can operate only as auxiliary treasury corridors if they hold New Zealand Financial Service Provider registration, appoint a New Zealand AML/CFT reporting entity, and route NZD payments through a correspondent bank with a genuine New Zealand banking licence. For a customs-bonded truck fleet requiring documentary trade settlement and capital-account transparency, reliance on a pure offshore EMI as the primary treasury rail is not recommended; a registered bank account is the only robust infrastructure for escrowed capital, SWIFT trade payments, and secure FX execution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital routing for the International Company is effected through an SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident parent or beneficial owner to the company's New Zealand operational account, with the remittance clearly labelled as 'equity injection' or 'shareholder loan' for balance-of-payments purposes. No RBNZ exchange-control approval is required. The receiving bank will verify that the remitter matches the disclosed beneficial owner and will request supporting documents: the share allotment resolution, shareholders' agreement, fleet asset purchase agreement, and a statutory declaration as to source of funds. To avoid misclassification, the payment should be sent to the Capital Escrow Account first and only later released to the operational account upon fulfilment of conditions precedent in the share subscription or loan agreement.Outbound trade disbursements are cleared through SWIFT MT103 and MT202 cover payments for foreign-currency obligations, and through Commercial Letters of Credit for airport terminal charges, interline carrier fees, fuel purchases, and major fleet-related settlements. For domestic New Zealand payments, the Bulk Electronic Clearing System (BECS) and ACH Direct rails provide low-cost NZD clearing. All outbound disbursements must be matched to supporting transport invoices, customs bonds, terminal service statements, and the bonded trucking service contract. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium. New Zealand does not operate a separate digital EMI (electronic money institution) licence. Any entity that receives and holds customer money must be a registered bank, a licensed non-bank deposit taker, or otherwise fall within an express exclusion under the Banking Prudential Requirements. Offshore EMIs can operate only as auxiliary treasury corridors if they hold New Zealand Financial Service Provider registration, appoint a New Zealand AML/CFT reporting entity, and route NZD payments through a correspondent bank with a genuine New Zealand banking licence. For a customs-bonded truck fleet requiring documentary trade settlement and capital-account transparency, reliance on a pure offshore EMI as the primary treasury rail is not recommended; a registered bank account is the only robust infrastructure for escrowed capital, SWIFT trade payments, and secure FX execution.
