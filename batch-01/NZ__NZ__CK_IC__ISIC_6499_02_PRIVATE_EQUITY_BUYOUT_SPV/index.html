Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, a dedicated registry division of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), operating the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) platform

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (Act No. 105 of 1993) as amended, especially ss 12(1), 13-17, 20, 21, 96-104 (share capital), 122-127 (constitution and registered office), 153-160 (directors), and 189 (board resolutions); Companies Act 1993 (Incorporation of Companies) Regulations 2000; New Zealand Business Number Act 2016, s 9; Income Tax Act 2007, ss BC 1, CA 1, CD 1 and YD 2 (residence and foreign-sourced income treatment); Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008. The Companies Act 1993 does not create a separate statutory class of 'International Company'; the target's 'International Company' designation (ELF_CUSTOM_CK_IC) is a structuring characterisation of a New Zealand-incorporated company limited by shares whose income, assets and counterparties are exclusively offshore, enabling DTA relief or non-assessable foreign-sourced income treatment.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic Application for Registration of a Company (Companies Office incorporation form, bearing the statutory declaration of compliance under s 14 Companies Act 1993), together with the proposed constitution (Articles of Association) and statutory consents to act as director and shareholder

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 24 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital, minimum initial capital or escrow account requirement under the Companies Act 1993; shares may be issued at nil par value with consideration payable in cash, in kind or by way of shareholder loan set-off. The model SPV will typically allot 100 ordinary shares of NZ$1.00 each, subscribed for in full by the sole non-resident investor, credited fully paid without injected external escrow capital.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required for any occupied office under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand (Fire Safety, Evacuation Procedures, and Evacuation Schemes) Regulations 2018, evidenced by a building warrant of fitness and an approved evacuation scheme; structural load inspection and earthquake-prone building certification under the Building Act 2004; electrical safety clearance under the Electricity (Safety) Regulations 2010; and a general workplace clearance issued by WorkSafe New Zealand under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 covering first aid, emergency lighting, safe egress, and welfare facilities. For the non-resident SPV that uses only a registered agent's address, no physical occupation clearance is required; the company maintains a written 'situs declaration' confirming the absence of a New Zealand physical establishment.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital, minimum initial capital or escrow account requirement under the Companies Act 1993; shares may be issued at nil par value with consideration payable in cash, in kind or by way of shareholder loan set-off. The model SPV will typically allot 100 ordinary shares of NZ$1.00 each, subscribed for in full by the sole non-resident investor, credited fully paid without injected external escrow capital.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial licensing board is engaged for ISIC 6499 (Leveraged Buyout Acquisition Syndicate and Special Purpose Company) because the commercial activity comprises passive financing and holding of equity interests, which is not a licensed industry under New Zealand law. The monetary authorities with conditional oversight are the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand only if the SPV expands into crowd-funding, managed investment scheme, or public lending activities. For any physical office, the competent licensing authority is the local Territorial Authority (City/District Council) on building occupancy, together with WorkSafe New Zealand for occupational safety.

Category: Not Applicable – no operating permit is required for the SPV's financing activity; if the vehicle historically maintains a registered office only and no plant or premises, the building occupancy certificate under the Building Act 2004 (if a back-office is occupied) is the only physical clearance instrument. Any optional registration on the Financial Service Providers Register is a 'Regulation Registrar' service title, not an industry operating licence.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (IR) — New Zealand's national tax authority; online portal 'myIR' at https://www.ird.govt.nz. New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS) administers export clearance via the Trade Single Window (TSW).

E-government filing portal

A Cook Islands International Company (CK IC) is not automatically registered for New Zealand tax. If the SPV derives New Zealand-sourced income or carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand, it must: (1) obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) and register as an overseas company under the Companies Act 1993 where required; (2) apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number using the IR596 (non-individual) registration or through an approved tax agent; (3) register for GST on the same myIR account (Form IR372 or IR375 for non-resident GST registration) once the NZD 60,000 threshold is exceeded, or voluntarily to claim zero-rated refunds; (4) register as an employer if it has NZ PAYE obligations. As a CK IC, it should also obtain its Cook Islands TIN from Cook Islands Revenue Management under the Cook Islands Tax Administration Act 1997; under the New Zealand–Cook Islands double tax agreement the SPV's residence is determined by its place of effective management, and if non-resident in New Zealand its foreign-source business profits are not subject to New Zealand income tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (Cook Islands International Company). For New Zealand tax purposes the SPV is a non-resident company with Territorial_Scope: income tax is imposed only on New Zealand-sourced income (Income Tax Act 2007 s YD 4), not on foreign-source income; the entity-level classification is Entity_Level_Taxation for any NZ-source income, and the home-jurisdiction (Cook Islands) IC exemption applies to its offshore operations. — A Cook Islands International Company (CK IC) is not automatically registered for New Zealand tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Cook Islands International Company). For New Zealand tax purposes the SPV is a non-resident company with Territorial_Scope: income tax is imposed only on New Zealand-sourced income (Income Tax Act 2007 s YD 4), not on foreign-source income; the entity-level classification is Entity_Level_Taxation for any NZ-source income, and the home-jurisdiction (Cook Islands) IC exemption applies to its offshore operations.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or equivalent transparent-entity election is available in New Zealand. Statutory elections relevant to this SPV: (i) New Zealand resident shareholders may elect the fair dividend rate (FDR) or cost-based method under the foreign investment fund (FIF) rules for their interests in the CK IC; (ii) the CK IC may elect GST grouping under s 55 of the GST Act 1985 with other NZ-registered group members; (iii) GST accounting-basis elections (invoice, payments, or hybrid) under s 20; (iv) election into the NZ branch-equivalent attribution regime under Part EX of the Income Tax Act 2007 for controlled foreign companies (CFC); (v) limited elections to neutralise hybrid mismatch outcomes under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018. No statute provides a US-style classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any consecutive 12-month period (s 51 GST Act). A non-resident supplier of remote services (digital, electronic, or telecommunications services) to persons in New Zealand must register under the offshore supplier registration regime once the value of such supplies exceeds NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period. Voluntary registration is available to non-resident businesses carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand, which is commonly used to recover GST on NZ domestic inputs and to zero-rate exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under s 11 (goods) and s 11A (services) of the GST Act 1985: (i) goods are zero-rated where they are exported by the supplier or by the recipient within 28 days of the supply (or within a period the Commissioner allows) and the supplier holds sufficient documentary evidence of export; (ii) services are zero-rated where the services are supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed, the services are not supplied to a private consumer in New Zealand, and the services are not directly in connection with land in New Zealand or personal property in New Zealand. For a private equity syndication SPV, fee-type services (management, advisory, origination) supplied to offshore investors may satisfy s 11A; however, lending, syndicated loan, and financial intermediation services are exempt financial supplies (s 3 and s 20(3)) rather than zero-rated, so no GST refund arises on those income streams. Refund substantiation requires retained evidence of offshore status of the recipient, place of supply and use, export documentation, and proof of payment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUS 40 or Electronic CUS 40 processed through the Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) naming the CK IC SPV as shipper or consignor
  • Customs departure record / port export list certifying that the goods left New Zealand within 28 days of supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT wire records evidencing full offshore payment of the export consideration
  • Commercial invoice and underlying export or cross-border service contract
  • Foreign buyer non-resident status evidence (certificate of residence, registration documents) for zero-rated services under s 11A
  • Logistics provider certificate of export or supplier's export confirmation statement

New Zealand has not implemented mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, API-based clearance, or digital stamping. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR (IR372 for standard registration; IR375 for offshore supplier registration) on a monthly or two-monthly cycle depending on turnover. E-invoicing is voluntary (Peppol network); Customs export declarations are filed electronically via the NZCS Trade Single Window (CUS 40 EDI). No government e-invoicing mandate is in force.

Inland Revenue and the New Zealand Customs Service audit export zero-rating positions for circular trade, round-tripping, and fictitious export schemes. A wrongfully zero-rated supply is treated as subject to GST at 15%; the Commissioner may reassess within the standard 4-year period (section 108 Tax Administration Act 1994) and without time limit in cases of evasion or fraud. Shortfall penalties under sections 141A–141D of the TAA 1994: 20% for lack of reasonable care or an unacceptable tax position, 40% for an abusive tax position, and up to 150% for evasion with obstruction; use-of-money interest applies from the due date. Criminal liability under sections 143A and 144 of the TAA 1994 includes fines up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years for knowingly providing false export documentation, with retrospective clawback of GST input credits claimed on the same supplies plus associated penalty tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar is freely convertible in both current and capital account transactions under IMF Article VIII. There is no general exchange-control approval regime for dividend, interest, royalty, loan or equity remittances by non-residents. Practical obstacles arise instead from AML/CFT verification, CRS/FATCA reporting, non-resident withholding tax characterization on New Zealand-sourced payments, and Overseas Investment Act 2005 thresholds for sensitive land, strategic assets and significant business assets. A non-resident CK_IC that does not carry on a taxable New Zealand business can repatriate capital and profits freely, subject to the producing bank's documentary due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no export proceeds surrender duty, no statutory repatriation window, and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain receipts in foreign-currency accounts inside or outside New Zealand, convert them at commercial exchange rates when commercially needed, and are merely required to retain supporting trade documentation for tax and AML/CFT audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua); the Financial Markets Authority and the Department of Internal Affairs AML/CFT Group administer complementary market conduct and anti-money-laundering registration; no separate central exchange-control office exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary payment corridor; Low as a sole treasury account provider. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed as registered banks in New Zealand and cannot accept demand deposits within the meaning of the Deposit Takers Act 2022. They may provide cross-border payment execution, multi-currency wallets and FX conversion, but final NZD clearing must settle through an NZ-registered bank or approved settlement system participant. Such EMIs must also be registered under the AML/CFT Act 2009 with the Department of Internal Affairs. For a leveraged buyout SPV, a full institutional banking relationship remains mandatory for capital escrow, syndicated loan drawdowns and outbound acquisition wires; EMIs may be used only for low-value operational expenses where the principal treasury account remains a licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Sponsor equity and shareholder loan injections must arrive by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer to the SPV capital escrow account, referencing the investor name and 'Capital Contribution / Equity Subscription' in the payment narrative. Syndicated LBO debt is routed via MT103 from each lender, or by MT202 COV through the facility agent, into a designated collection account. The SPV should appoint one New Zealand bank as treasury settlement agent to ensure clean aggregation and matching against drawdown notices. The bank will require a board resolution, executed subscription or equity commitment agreement, facility agreement and a source-of-funds declaration before first credit. New Zealand has no national purpose-code table; banks rely on SWIFT narrative and internal classification such as 'Direct Investment - Equity' or 'Loan Advance'. Overseas Investment Office consent is not required unless the acquisition involves sensitive land, strategic assets or other assets above the statutory thresholds; if those thresholds are triggered, the investor must obtain consent before settlement and the bank must receive evidence of that consent.Acquisition completion, deferred consideration, loan repayments and trade settlement use SWIFT MT103 for customer transfers and MT202 COV for bank-to-bank reimbursements under documentary credits. Commercial letters of credit are issued via SWIFT MT700, with amendments under MT707 and confirmations through MT710. NZD payments are cleared the same business day through the Reserve Bank's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS); non-NZD wires settle through correspondent banking networks. Where a CLS-eligible currency pair is involved, foreign-exchange settlement occurs through CLS to eliminate Herstatt risk. Every outbound payment must match a board-approved disbursement schedule and be supported by the relevant SPA drawdown notice, vendor invoice, completion statement, or loan repayment schedule. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary payment corridor; Low as a sole treasury account provider. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed as registered banks in New Zealand and cannot accept demand deposits within the meaning of the Deposit Takers Act 2022. They may provide cross-border payment execution, multi-currency wallets and FX conversion, but final NZD clearing must settle through an NZ-registered bank or approved settlement system participant. Such EMIs must also be registered under the AML/CFT Act 2009 with the Department of Internal Affairs. For a leveraged buyout SPV, a full institutional banking relationship remains mandatory for capital escrow, syndicated loan drawdowns and outbound acquisition wires; EMIs may be used only for low-value operational expenses where the principal treasury account remains a licensed bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border related-party transactions of NZ$2 million or more in aggregate per income year trigger the IR1061 International Dealings Schedule and concurrent Local File documentation; multinational groups with consolidated revenue of approximately NZ$1.2 billion or more must maintain a Master File and file Country-by-Country reports (OECD-aligned thresholds under s GC 13 Income Tax Act 2007).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations statute; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1 Income Tax Act 2007), business-purpose doctrine, thin-capitalisation rules (75% earnings-stripping cap), and OECD-compliant transfer-pricing reconstruction (ss GC 1-GC 14) require substantive board governance, local investment-decision making, and arm's-length risk allocation in a PE buyout SPV. A shell International Company with no employees, premises, or strategic control inside the jurisdiction risks recharacterisation and loss of the tax-exempt position.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filing

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty up to 150% of the evaded tax (s 141E TAA), late payment penalties, use-of-money interest, and reassessment for the full limitation period; criminal conviction under s 143B TAA carries a fine of up to NZ$50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns are personally liable for the shortfall penalties and may be imprisoned; courts can order civil restitution of the evaded tax under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: IRD adjustment to arm's-length income with a 40% shortfall penalty for non-arm's-length arrangements (s 141EA TAA) or 20% for an unacceptable tax position, plus use-of-money interest; dedicated administrative penalties for failure to provide documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to personal liability for reckless trading if the resulting tax debt renders the SPV insolvent (s 135 Companies Act 1993); disqualification orders and credit-certainty enforcement can follow.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Application of the GAAR: denial of the tax-exempt classification, recharacterisation of income as assessable, shortfall penalty of 40% for tax avoidance (up to 100% for abusive positions under s 141D TAA), and mandatory disclosure of the avoidance arrangement (ss 140B-140D TAA).

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil: directors are liable for the company's unpaid tax, subject to departure prohibition orders for tax debts over NZ$10,000, and exposed to prosecution for knowingly orchestrating a sham corporate structure.
Anti-Money Laundering Violations in PE Deal Flow

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to NZ$2 million per breach for a body corporate and NZ$200,000 for an individual under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009; imprisonment up to 7 years for money laundering under s 243 of the Crimes Act 1961.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for AML programme failures; travel bans and asset freezes are obtainable under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009 pending disgorgement of illicitly sourced buyout funds.
