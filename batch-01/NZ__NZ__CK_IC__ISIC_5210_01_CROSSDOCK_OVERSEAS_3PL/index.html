Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office (Ngā Kaihautū Kamupene o Aotearoa), a business unit of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), operating the New Zealand Companies Register and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) register.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), sections 10 and 12 (formation and name restrictions), sections 14-15 (registration and certificate of incorporation), sections 87-90 (share allotment and share registers), sections 122-124 (share capital), sections 145-153 (directors and appointment of officers), and section 189 (registered office and address for service). The Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 does not apply because the company is a private exempt company.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NZCN1, formally titled Application for Registration of a Company (Incorporation Application), submitted electronically through the Companies Office online portal, resulting in the issuance of a Certificate of Registration and the automatic allocation of a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 26 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies under the Companies Act 1993; a company limited by shares may issue shares of no par value with a nil or nominal paid-up amount. No escrow, trust account, or stated capital deposit is required at incorporation, and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted without exchange control approval.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) approval of a fire evacuation scheme is required for warehouse premises with more than 10 employees. A Building Warrant of Fitness with annual inspections by an Independent Qualified Person is mandatory under the Building Act 2004. Structural load certification is required for racking, mezzanine floors, and heavy mechanical handling equipment. The company must maintain a compliant workplace under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including worker engagement, a hazard register, emergency plans, and incident notification to WorkSafe New Zealand for any notifiable event within 48 hours.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies under the Companies Act 1993; a company limited by shares may issue shares of no par value with a nil or nominal paid-up amount. No escrow, trust account, or stated capital deposit is required at incorporation, and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted without exchange control approval.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No dedicated industrial licensing board exists for ISIC 5210 warehousing and storage activities in New Zealand. Regulatory competency is distributed among the local territorial authority for building and land-use consent, WorkSafe New Zealand for occupational health and safety, and the Environmental Protection Authority together with the regional unitary council for environmental and discharge regulation.

Category: No statutory operating licence applies to cross-dock warehouse and third-party logistics fulfillment services under New Zealand law. The company must instead maintain a Building Warrant of Fitness under the Building Act 2004 where a compliance schedule exists, obtain any required resource consent or permitted activity compliance under the Resource Management Act 1991, and register as a person conducting a business or undertaking (PCBU) with no separate licence but with statutory duties under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue, Te Tari Taake) — New Zealand's national tax authority administering income tax, Goods and Services Tax (GST), and employer obligations; online portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz. Export customs administration is separately handled by the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window (TSW) at https://www.customs.govt.nz.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity, including an offshore International Company (CK_IC) engaged in cross-border e-commerce fulfilment, must first determine whether it carries on a 'taxable activity' in New Zealand under section 6 of the GST Act 1985 or derives New Zealand-source income under sections YD4 to YD8 of the Income Tax Act 2007. If nexus is established, the entity must apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number. Procedures: (i) a non-resident company not registered on the NZ Companies Register files Form IR742 (IRD number application) together with certified incorporation documents, registry extracts, and director/shareholder details; (ii) GST registration uses Form IR596 or the myIR non-resident GST workflow and is compulsory when taxable supplies made in New Zealand exceed or are expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period; a non-resident supplying remote services to NZ consumers must register once NZ-consumer turnover exceeds NZD 60,000, with voluntary registration available below that threshold; (iii) companies incorporated on the NZ Companies Register obtain an IRD number automatically at incorporation. A CK_IC operating a purely offshore cross-dock and overseas 3PL warehouse, with no physical presence, staff, or inventory in New Zealand, generally has no NZ tax registration obligation; the entity should maintain documentary proof that title, custody, and physical export movement occur outside New Zealand.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — In its home jurisdiction, the Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC) is ordinarily tax-exempt as an international company under the Cook Islands International Companies Act 2008-09, subject to satisfying the exempt international company attestation and having no disqualifying local shareholder categories. For New Zealand tax purposes, a non-resident tax-exempt foreign company is not automatically exempt: it is taxed only on New Zealand-source income and on income attributable to a New Zealand fixed establishment or permanent establishment. In the absence of a PE arising from pure offshore cross-dock, warehousing, or 3PL order-fulfilment activity, no NZ entity-level income tax is imposed, and the entity is treated as outside the NZ GST net. If a PE or fixed establishment in New Zealand is found, the NZ branch becomes subject to NZ corporate income tax on attributed income and the entity must register and account for GST on NZ taxable supplies.

Available election forms

New Zealand has no check-the-box entity classification election. The available statutory and administrative elections relevant to a CK_IC cross-border fulfilment structure include: (1) GST grouping election under sections 55 to 55B of the GST Act 1985 for related companies to file consolidated GST returns; (2) look-through company (LTC) election for NZ-incorporated close companies under sections HB 1 to HB 11 of the Income Tax Act 2007, not available to offshore international companies; (3) branch versus subsidiary election principles for foreign companies deriving business income through a New Zealand PE, including application of the PE attribution rules under section YD5; (4) controlled foreign company (CFC) attribution elections where NZ-resident shareholders hold interests in the CK_IC; and (5) hybrid mismatch rule elections under subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007, which apply to cross-border hybrid arrangements involving the CK_IC and require adjustment of deductions, income inclusions, or the application of the 'taxed once' and 'deduction/no inclusion' outcomes. Any election must be made via the relevant NZ return or disclosure form in myIR within the statutory timeframes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (compulsory registration threshold under section 51(1)(b) of the GST Act 1985, based on actual or expected taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any 12-month period; the same NZD 60,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand consumers; a non-resident with only zero-rated export supplies and no NZ fixed establishment has no compulsory registration obligation unless it makes standard-rated supplies from goods held in a New Zealand warehouse).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a GST-registered supplier zero-rates exported goods under section 11(1)(a) of the GST Act 1985 where the goods are physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier or the supplier's agent within 28 days after the time of supply, or within any extended period allowed by the Commissioner, and the supplier retains credible evidence of export. Services: section 11A zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed, provided the services are not performed directly in connection with New Zealand land or a New Zealand-resident recipient and the economic benefit is received offshore; this covers offshore warehousing, order fulfilment, cross-dock handling, freight forwarding support, and logistics management supplied by a non-resident 3PL to overseas merchants. Remote services supplied to NZ consumers by a non-resident seller using a New Zealand fulfilment hub are standard-rated at 15% unless the goods are thereafter exported; B2B remote services supplied to a GST-registered New Zealand business may be zero-rated under the reverse-charge mechanism in section 8(4B) of the GST Act 1985.

E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the PEPPOL network, with the New Zealand Peppol Authority hosted within Inland Revenue. GST is self-assessed and remitted via periodic returns (form IR372) submitted electronically through myIR. A seven-year statutory record-keeping obligation applies under section 22 of the Tax Administration Act 1994. Export zero-rating claims are audited jointly by Inland Revenue and the New Zealand Customs Service. Civil penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 include shortfall penalties of 20% for failure to take reasonable care or for an unacceptable tax position, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion under section 141, in addition to criminal prosecution under sections 143A and 143B with fines up to NZD 50,000 for a first offence and imprisonment of up to five years for evasion. Director and officer personal liability may arise. False export declarations may also constitute offences under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 and the Crimes Act 1961. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully convertible, free-float currency regime. There are no exchange-control laws, no requirement for central-bank approval on current-account or capital-account transactions, and no foreign-exchange allocation quotas. Profit remittance (dividends, interest, royalties) by the International Company (IC) to non-resident beneficiaries is unrestricted and limited only by the general non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) obligations under New Zealand tax law; no FX visa, no capital-export licensing, and no prior notification to RBNZ is required for ordinary trade remittances. The only administrative touch-points are (i) RBNZ Balance of Payments / International Investment Position (BoP/IIP) statistical reporting for external borrowings exceeding NZ$1 million, and (ii) Overseas Investment Office (OIO) screening for 'sensitive land' or 'significant business assets' — neither of which constrains the cross-dock e-commerce fulfilment treasury flows. Within the IC's Tax-Exempt-Zone classification, tax gross-up and NRWT neutralisation must be evidenced by an IRD tax-residency certificate and the applicable double-tax-agreement election before the first distribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. The IC may retain 100% of export proceeds in free-balance foreign currency (USD, CNY, EUR, AUD) held in offshore or onshore accounts, convert to NZD at any time at market rates, or deploy those proceeds directly to fund overseas fulfilment-centre inventory. No centralised export liquidation or repatriation certificate is required. For customs and statistical purposes only, export entries must be reported through the New Zealand Customs Service Electronic Single Window (ESW) with the applicable working-code tariff classification aligned to ISIC 5210 / NAICS 493110, but this does not trigger any FX surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ; Māori: Te Pūtea Matua) — sole central bank and prudential supervisor of registered banks, with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) jointly regulating capital-market conduct and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) registering and supervising non-bank money-service businesses and EMI providers under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Protocol: (1) The overseas parent or trust makes a SWIFT MT103/MT202 remittance to the IC's RBNZ-authorised bank account using a contemporaneous payment narrative: 'Equity injection under Companies Act 1993 — IC treasury — Tax-Exempt structure — working capital for ISIC 5210 cross-dock operations'. (2) The beneficiary bank maps the credit internally to RBNZ BoP/IIP statistical category 'Direct Investment — Equity Capital'; no prior FX licence is required, but the bank will obtain the UBO KYC pack and the IC board resolution authorising the account before accepting the first credit. (3) For capital-escrow treatment, direct the funds into a designated interest-bearing Capital Escrow sub-ledger, with a client-moneys / trust-account confirmation under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 where applicable; nominal share capital and shareholder advances must respectively be supported by a share-register entry and a formal loan agreement. (4) If the contribution crosses the OIO 'significant business assets' threshold (approximately NZ$100 million for non-partner-country investors and higher for partner-country investors, being Australia and the United States) or involves 'sensitive land', obtain a prior OIO consent certificate under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 before the wire exits the clearing system; for this fulfilment-hub model, the threshold review is typically not triggered because the domestic asset value remains far below the statutory cap. (5) Within five business days of receipt, the bank issues the advising receipt, SWIFT acknowledgment, and daily availability confirmation in the IC's treasury view; a one-off internal audit file is retained containing the source-of-funds trail, the board resolution and the exact purpose-code mapping.Clearing rails are tiered by value and counterparty jurisdiction: (i) SWIFT MT103 (cover MT202/MT202COV) direct-pay protocol for high-value inventory procurement and overseas-warehouse rents, executed through the Tier-1 bank with SWIFT GPI cross-border tracking and FX Global Code compliant execution; (ii) UCP600-compliant Commercial Letters of Credit — issued as sight or usance LCs via the IC's New Zealand bank through SWIFT MT700/MT701 and confirmed, where necessary, through a Hong Kong or Singapore confirming house — for large consignment purchases from APAC manufacturers, using MT707 for amendments and MT754 with the shipping-document drawdown package for payment; (iii) local ACH rails (via Airwallex or Wise) for high-frequency low-value disbursements to supplier networks, cross-dock labour, courier tariffs and marketplace payouts in 60+ jurisdictions (United States via ACH/RTP/CHIPS, Eurozone via SEPA Instant, United Kingdom via FPS, Australia and New Zealand via BECS/PayTo, Singapore via FAST), with IC treasury retaining a nostro buffer in each settlement corridor; and (iv) USD/EUR clean collections for third-party logistics-warehouse charges where no LC was drawn. A settlement policy of gross payments above USD 250,000 requiring SWIFT MT103, amounts between USD 5,000 and USD 250,000 being local-ACH optimised, and amounts below USD 5,000 being batched, is recommended to minimise transaction leakage.Deliverable FX Forwards (outright or window) between NZD and USD, CNY, EUR and AUD — fully permissible without RBNZ approval, available from one-week to twelve-month tenors under standard ISDA/CSA or bank master agreements for the IC as a wholesale eligible investor.FX Swaps (including overnight index swaps and single-currency overnight rollovers) — permitted for managing short-dated funding and rolling cross-dock nostro buffers without creating open FX risk.Cross-Currency Swaps (NZD/USD) — permissible where the IC's escrow is funded in NZD but trade payables are USD or CNY denominated, executing a medium-tenor principal-and-interest exchange with a licensed bank or derivatives dealer.FX Options (plain-vanilla calls and puts) — permissible as over-the-counter hedging instruments under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 wholesale-investor exemption, enabling a contractual ceiling on procurement-currency movements while retaining downside gain if the NZD strengthens.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) — permissible only for the non-convertible or exotic underlying leg (e.g., CNY, IDR, INR) where the NZD or USD leg is deliverable; for a plain NZD/USD hedge a deliverable forward is the default instrument, and a purely NZD-on-NZD NDF would not be recognised as ordinary hedging since the NZD is fully convertible.Listed NZD/USD Futures on ASX — permissible and exchange-traded for high-visibility treasury positions, although OTC forwards normally provide better tenor alignment with the fulfilment pipeline.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: High (for operational FX, mass payouts and collection) / Medium (for statutory capital escrow where a licensed bank is mandatory). Airwallex (New Zealand) Limited is a registered financial-services provider and DIA-supervised money remitter under the AML/CFT Act 2009, fully authorised to run multi-currency global accounts, local ACH rails, and MT103/SWIFT payment corridors for the IC's cross-dock 3PL disbursements; Wise N.Z. Limited is additionally viable for low-ticket seller-proceeds payouts. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs in New Zealand are not 'registered banks' and cannot take custodial deposits in the statutory sense — a Capital Escrow Account housing shareholder equity must be held at an RBNZ-registered bank to preserve clean segregation and audit output; (ii) EMIs cannot issue Commercial Letters of Credit or provide UCP600 trade-finance instruments; (iii) EMI settlement into FX-constrained jurisdictions (e.g., China) requires the EMI to operate through local licensed partners, so the IC should retain a SWIFT-anchor bank for high-value settlements. Recommended structure: Tier-1 registered bank for escrow/LC/trade core plus Airwallex for last-mile fulfilment, supplier payout, and marketplace collections across 60+ markets.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand's transfer pricing documentation threshold is NZ$10 million in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions per income year. Where the aggregate value of transactions covered by the transfer pricing rules exceeds NZ$10 million, the taxpayer is expected to prepare and maintain contemporaneous documentation, including a Master File and Local File consistent with OECD guidance, and to file the IR4J transfer pricing disclosure schedule.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have a separate 'Economic Substance Regulations' code (e.g., as in CARICOM-style regimes), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, prior case law (e.g., Peterson, Ben Nevis Forestry), and the anti-conduit provisions require that an entity possess real commercial substance. A cross-dock 3PL entity must maintain genuine physical presence in New Zealand for the warehouse operation, assume operational risks, employ staff or engage real service providers, and perform substantive logistics functions. An entity used merely as an invoicing hub with no actual warehousing or fulfilment activity is not recognised for tax purposes and risks reconstruction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberately Concealed Income

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Administration Act 1994 (TAA) sections 141B and 143, evasion may attract a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the evaded tax, a fine on conviction of up to NZ$250,000 (individual) or NZ$500,000 (company), and imprisonment for up to four years (section 143B).

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly participate in or direct the evasion may be held criminally liable; under TAA section 149A, a director may be personally liable for company tax debts and penalties when the company has failed to pay and the director knew or should have known of the unpaid tax, or when assets are stripped to defeat Inland Revenue collection. Travel bans and asset restraint orders may be imposed under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may adjust the taxable income to the arm's length consideration under the Income Tax Act 2007 sections GC 6-GC 8. Shortfall penalties apply: 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for lack of reasonable care, or 150% for fraudulent avoidance; documentation penalty of up to NZ$20,000 may be imposed for failure to maintain required transfer pricing records under TAA section 141FB.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for the company's tax liabilities and transfer pricing penalties if they were knowingly party to the non-compliance, failed to ensure the company made adequate tax provision, or permitted the company to enter into a tax avoidance transaction that strips the company of assets.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Section BG 1 GAAR allows Inland Revenue to nullify the tax advantage, reconstruct the arrangement, and impose shortfall penalties up to 100% of the resulting tax shortfall. The Companies Office may deregister the company for not carrying on business, which dissolves the entity and removes limited liability protection; also, director disqualification under the Companies Act 1993 may apply.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the company is a sham or facade; directors may be personally liable for unpaid company taxes and to creditors for fraudulent trading under section 301 of the Companies Act 1993 (if the business was carried on with intent to defraud creditors), and may face imprisonment for perjury if false declarations are made to Inland Revenue or the Companies Office.
AML / CFT and Money Laundering Breaches

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a reporting entity may face a civil penalty of up to NZ$2.5 million for the first breach and up to NZ$5 million for a subsequent or continued breach, plus infringement notices. Criminal money laundering offences under the Crimes Act 1961 carry imprisonment of up to seven years per offence; property obtained through significant criminal activity may be forfeited under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who directed, participated in, or facilitated AML/CFT non-compliance are individually liable, with penalties up to NZ$100,000 per individual per offence; they may be disqualified from managing companies. A director convicted of money laundering can be sentenced to imprisonment and prohibited from holding directorships under the Companies Act 1993.
