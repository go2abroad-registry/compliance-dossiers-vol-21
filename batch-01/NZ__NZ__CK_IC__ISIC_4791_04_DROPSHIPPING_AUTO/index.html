Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up or authorised capital is prescribed by the Companies Act 1993 (NZ); the company must allot and issue at least one share at incorporation. No escrow or capital-holding account is mandated. For the International Company (Tax-Exempt Zone) structure, issued shares must be held by non-resident shareholders and working capital is typically provided by non-resident shareholder loans; the company must not carry on business in New Zealand in order to preserve the exempt treatment.

Funding & unlock path: Not required for the current non-physical automated dropshipping platform. Should the Company open premises in New Zealand, it must obtain: (i) a fire safety certificate and emergency egress approval under sections 65 to 70 of the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017; (ii) structural load inspection and a Code Compliance Certificate (CCC) under the Building Act 2004 building consent regime; and (iii) workplace health and safety clearance from WorkSafe New Zealand under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including signage, electrical inspection, and racking or packing-station safety verification.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; online taxpayer portal myIR (https://services.ird.govt.nz/myir/).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity, including a Cook Islands International Company, that carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand, derives New Zealand-sourced income, or is required to register for New Zealand GST must apply to the Commissioner of Inland Revenue for an IRD number. Non-resident companies apply using the prescribed non-resident company registration form (typically IR742) supported by the certificate of incorporation, registered office address, director and shareholder details, and any New Zealand business address. Applications may be lodged through myIR or by paper. The IRD number is issued once registration is accepted. Where a non-resident company has a fixed place of business in New Zealand or central management and control is exercised in New Zealand, it may become resident and registrable for income tax purposes; otherwise it is registered as a non-resident company and taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — a Cook Islands International Company is outside the New Zealand income tax base unless made resident under domestic law by central management and control being exercised in New Zealand or by deriving New Zealand-sourced income through a fixed establishment. As a non-resident entity, it is subject to New Zealand income tax on New Zealand-sourced income only, and the profits of a genuine offshore international company are not within the New Zealand taxing net.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity classification regime. Relevant statutory elections include: (a) voluntary GST registration where turnover is below the NZD 60,000 compulsory threshold; (b) GST grouping election for closely related companies under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; (c) transitional resident tax election for eligible new residents under section 220 of the Income Tax Act 2007; and (d) application of New Zealand's hybrid and branch mismatch rules (Income Tax Act 2007 subparts FH and FE), which may recharacterise payments or deny deductions to counteract hybrid mismatch outcomes. There is no statutory election to create a New Zealand 'tax exempt zone' for a foreign international company; its tax-exempt status derives from non-residence and the territorial scope of New Zealand taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 — compulsory GST registration applies to a person carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand where the value of supplies in any 12-month period exceeds or is likely to exceed NZD 60,000. Non-resident suppliers of remote services, digital goods, or goods delivered via automated dropshipping models are also required to register under the non-resident supplier registration rules once supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (a) goods supplied by a GST-registered person that are exported from New Zealand by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf, provided the supplier has ownership or possession of the goods at the time of supply and the goods are exported within the statutory timeframe (generally within 28 days of the time of supply, or under a contractual obligation to export); (b) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed, provided the services are not supplied directly in connection with New Zealand land or movable property and are not performed while the recipient of the supply is in New Zealand; and (c) certain international transport and freight services. For automated dropshipping operations, zero-rating on the New Zealand-registered operator requires either physical export of goods from New Zealand or valid offshore services treatment under section 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Customs Service electronic export entry / TID)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export from New Zealand
  • Commercial Invoice and purchase order relating to the supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement showing foreign currency settlement and receipt of export proceeds
  • Proof of physical export within the statutory 28-day timeframe (e.g. customs movement records and carrier evidence)
  • Any agency or dropshipping agreement demonstrating that the New Zealand supplier is the principal exporter and has ownership or possession of the goods at the time of supply

New Zealand does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control regime. E-invoicing is voluntary and supported through the New Zealand Peppol network. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR, but invoice-level data is not transmitted to the Inland Revenue Department on a transaction-by-transaction basis and no digital stamping of invoices is required. For exported goods, supporting documentation such as customs export declarations and transport documentation must be retained to substantiate zero-rating.

The Commissioner of Inland Revenue may disallow zero-rating and assess GST where documentary or substantive conditions are not satisfied. Consequences include: additional GST at 15% on the original supply value, use-of-money interest, late payment penalties, shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 (up to 150% of the tax shortfall for evasion, and up to 40% for substantial understatement without taking reasonable care), and criminal prosecution in fraudulent cases with fines and imprisonment. Where an exporter fails to repatriate funds or cannot substantiate export within the statutory period, the zero-rating is retrospectively clawed back and the supplier becomes liable for the full GST amount plus penalties and interest.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised free-floating exchange rate regime with complete current-account and capital-account convertibility; the Exchange Control Act was repealed in 1984 and no Reserve Bank approval or exchange licence is required for any cross-border transfer, dividend, profit remittance, or debt repayment. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a globally deliverable currency with an extremely liquid spot and forward market. No restriction applies to a non-resident tax-exempt International Company (CK_IC) wishing to repatriate profits to shareholders, provided the entity does not derive New Zealand-sourced income and does not create a permanent establishment in New Zealand. Two compliance caveats apply: (a) the Overseas Investment Act 2005 requires consent only where the non-resident acquires 'sensitive land' or 'significant business assets' located in New Zealand, which is not the case in a borderless automated dropshipping operation; and (b) the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, effective 1 December 2019, requires a non-resident supplier of low-value goods (each item NZD 1,000 or less) to New Zealand consumers to register, collect, and remit 15% NZ GST once its New Zealand low-value-goods supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period; failure to collect GST is a treasury sanction that can be triggered by the Inland Revenue Department. Profit remittance of the post-GST margin is fully convertible with zero statutory withholding where the distribution is made by the tax-exempt IC out of its non-New Zealand-sourced trading income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no export proceeds surrender rules in New Zealand: no statutory surrender ratio, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no forced conversion of foreign currency into NZD. Exporters and automated e-commerce operators may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts in onshore foreign-currency accounts or offshore accounts indefinitely; may convert to NZD at any time through a licensed dealer; and may settle intercompany or supplier balances out of such accounts without Reserve Bank notification. The only administrative obligations are statistical, i.e., the banks' Balance of Payments reporting to the Reserve Bank and the Inland Revenue Department's exchange-of-information rules. The CK_IC's effective mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent and the statutory surrender period is indefinite or none.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) acts as the monetary authority for New Zealand and, on a de facto basis, for the Cook Islands, whose licensed financial institutions use the New Zealand dollar as legal tender and settle through Reserve Bank clearing systems; the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) is the licensing and AML/CFT supervisor for the CK_IC's registered agent and any Cook Islands licensed bank, and the FSC administers the International Companies Act regime under which the CK_IC is incorporated.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for the tax-exempt International Company under the Cook Islands International Companies Act 1981-2020; 28% New Zealand company rate applies if the International Company becomes New Zealand tax-resident or derives New Zealand-source income through a fixed establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on distributions by the tax-exempt International Company; once New Zealand-resident company status is triggered, dividend withholding operates on an imputed 28% basis for residents, and non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) applies at 30% domestic / 15% treaty-reduced rates on dividends paid by a New Zealand-resident company.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand imposes no codified statutory dollar threshold for Local File / Master File documentation; Inland Revenue requires contemporaneous arm's length documentation for cross-border associated-party transactions, with a practical compliance focus above NZ$10 million aggregate; Country-by-Country reporting applies to New Zealand-resident groups with consolidated group revenue of at least NZ$1.2 billion.

Economic substance scope: The Cook Islands International Companies Act mandates a local registered office and licensed registered agent but imposes no formal Economic Substance Regulation comparable to the UAE ESR; however, New Zealand's Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) and Foreign Investment Fund (FIF) attribution rules scrutinise the substance and location of central management and control - central management and control exercised from New Zealand triggers New Zealand taxation and displaces the tax-exempt status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion (New Zealand Tax Administration Act 1994)

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty for evasion of 150% of the tax shortfall under section 141E TAA, plus use-of-money interest; criminal prosecution under section 143B TAA with imprisonment of up to 5 years; the arrangement is void under section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate face joint and several personal liability for the company tax shortfall under section 149A TAA; disqualification orders by the New Zealand Companies Office; civil recovery, travel enforcement orders, and imprisonment of up to 5 years for aiding evasion.
