Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Tauhake, IRD), Wellington, New Zealand; official taxpayer portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir/); IRD business online services and Trade Single Window (New Zealand Customs Service) for export and fiscal documentation.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine any New Zealand tax nexus. A Cook Islands International Company (IC) acting as a Single Family Office must obtain a New Zealand IRD number if it carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand, derives New Zealand-source income (e.g., interest from NZ bank deposits, dividends from NZ-resident companies, property income, or fees for services physically performed in NZ), or becomes liable for GST on supplies to NZ-resident consumers. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope - A Cook Islands International Company is treated by Inland Revenue as a non-resident company, not a pass-through vehicle, and is taxed at entity level only on New Zealand-source income under Sections YD 4 and YD 5 of the Income Tax Act 2007. Tax_Exempt_Zone treatment (full exemption from Cook Islands domestic tax under the Cook Islands International Companies Regulations 2018) is recognised by the Cook Islands but has no effect on the New Zealand tax classification; New Zealand applies ordinary non-resident company entity-level taxation with territorial sourcing.

Available election forms

New Zealand has no check-the-box or statutory entity-classification election. Available elections relevant to a foreign single family office include: (1) voluntary GST registration by a non-resident under Section 54C GST Act 1985 where the NZD 60,000 threshold is not reached; (2) GST grouping election under Section 55 GST Act 1985 for associated NZ-resident entities; (3) zero-rating election for supplies to non-residents subject to contemporaneous export documentation; (4) provisional tax instalment calculation and interest-free tax pooling choices under Part MF of the Tax Administration Act 1994; (5) functional-currency election under Section 4A Income Tax Act 2007; and (6) hybrid-mismatch rule elections, choices and safe-harbour assumptions under Subpart FH Income Tax Act 2007 where cross-border payments involve Cook Islands or other foreign tax differentials.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 annual taxable supplies threshold under Section 51 GST Act 1985. A non-resident Cook Islands IC supplying 'remote services' (including investment management, custody, advisory and back-office services) to New Zealand-resident consumers, or supplying low-value imported goods (consignment value not exceeding NZD 1,000), must register and charge GST under Section 54C if the NZD 60,000 12-month turnover threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods may be zero-rated under Section 11(1)(a) GST Act 1985 if the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand (physical removal from NZ) before the expiry of 28 days after the time of supply and the exporter retains documentary proof of export. Services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed qualify for zero-rating under Section 11A(1)(a) where the services are not connected with New Zealand real property or tangible goods. The Cook Islands IC must prove the recipient is outside NZ, the services are physically performed in NZ but received offshore, and the contract/invoice/remittance trail is consistent; financial services (investment custody, advisory, fund administration) supplied to a non-resident offshore trust or family principals may be zero-rated if the foreign-recipient and offshore-benefit tests are met. Exempt financial services remain GST-exempt rather than zero-rated when the recipient is in NZ, so careful characterisation and documentation are critical.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export entry lodged with the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (irrevocable evidence of physical export from NZ)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or SWIFT MT103/MT202 evidence of receipt of export proceeds)
  • Commercial invoice and executed supply contract identifying the offshore recipient
  • Packing list and carrier weight certificate
  • Certificate of Origin (where required by destination-country rules)
  • GST return excerpt (Form GST101) referencing the zero-rated export schedule

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the New Zealand Peppol Authority on the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 standard. Inland Revenue requires GST-registered persons to file returns (Form GST101) electronically via myIR by the 28th day after the end of each taxable period. IRD is promoting e-invoicing uptake and has signalled possible phased mandates for larger GST vendors, but no generally binding electronic-invoicing clearance or government API stamping requirement is currently in force for the Cook Islands IC.

Inland Revenue audits export zero-rating through desk reviews and field examinations with strict focus on contemporaneous evidence of physical export within 28 days, proof of payment during the applicable adjustment period, and the offshore status of the recipient. Inadequate documentation triggers clawback of the zero rating with GST reassessed at 15%, plus use-of-money interest under Section 120 Tax Administration Act 1994. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims attract shortfall penalties of 20% for failure to take reasonable care (s 141A), 40% for gross carelessness (s 141C), 100% for an abusive tax position (s 141D), and up to 150% where tax avoidance was the dominant purpose; criminal liability for tax evasion under Section 143A exposes responsible persons to fines up to NZD 250,000 and imprisonment up to two years for a first offence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account. There are no exchange-control restrictions, no central-bank approvals, no quantitative limits, and no tiered exchange rates applicable to inbound capital contributions, intercompany loans, dividend distributions, interest and royalty remittances, or liquidation proceeds. Profit repatriation is structurally unrestricted and is subject solely to general tax obligations, namely non-resident withholding tax on dividends at the statutory 30% rate or a reduced rate under an applicable double-tax agreement, as well as to corporate-law solvency tests for distributions under sections 52 to 56 of the Companies Act 1993 and the directors' duty to preserve creditor interests. The 'Tax_Exempt_Zone' classification attributed to this International Company does not trigger any exchange-control barrier; treasury planning should nevertheless document the reinvestment-versus-distribution rationale and maintain arm's-length documentation for any intragroup financial flows to avoid deemed-dividend or thin-capitalisation adjustments under the Income Tax Act 2007.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or conversion requirements exist in New Zealand. Since the abolition of exchange controls in 1984, exporters and licensed entities are under no obligation to repatriate receipts, to convert foreign currency into New Zealand dollars, or to observe any statutory timeline or percentage surrender ratio. Foreign-currency receipts may be retained indefinitely in offshore bank accounts, swept into onshore foreign-currency accounts, or converted at any commercial bank at freely negotiated market rates. This regime applies equally to the International Company and to the Single Family Office treasury, which may hold foreign-currency balances as natural hedges without registration, reporting, or approval requirements.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), constituted under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 as the central bank, monetary authority, and prudential supervisor of registered banks; it administers no exchange-control licensing because New Zealand operates a freely floating, fully convertible currency. The Overseas Investment Office (Toitū Te Whenua, a unit of Land Information New Zealand) acting under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 is the complementary authority screening foreign investment in sensitive land and significant business assets, and its clearance must be considered where the Single Family Office vehicle acquires New Zealand sensitive assets; the New Zealand Police Financial Intelligence Unit serves as the financial intelligence authority under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-Low for this entity profile. New Zealand maintains a Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Disputes Resolution) Act 2008, and several digital cross-border payment and electronic-money-type providers such as Airwallex, Wise Business, and Payoneer operate in or service the New Zealand market. However, for a non-resident-owned International Company structured as a Single Family Office, feasibility is constrained by four factors: (i) most digital EMIs decline complex family-office, trust-linked, and tax-exempt investment-holding structures as enhanced-risk; (ii) EMIs generally cannot provide securities custody, private-banking lending, dedicated relationship management, or institutional clearing services; (iii) an offshore EMI without an FSPR-registered New Zealand nexus may not lawfully provide ongoing regulated financial services to a New Zealand entity, and unregistered activity constitutes a compliance breach; and (iv) the 'Medium_Risk_Enhanced' AML classification assigned to the private-investment-holding scope will cause EMIs to impose restrictive velocity caps and rejection-prone transaction monitoring. The prudent blueprint is to use EMIs exclusively as ancillary inbound collection or low-value disbursement rails, never as the primary treasury, custody, or clearing backbone; the primary liquidity relationship must reside with a tier-1 New Zealand bank or a global private bank operating a New Zealand branch.

The prudent blueprint is to use EMIs exclusively as ancillary inbound collection or low-value disbursement rails, never as the primary treasury, custody, or clearing backbone; the primary liquidity relationship must reside with a tier-1 New Zealand bank or a global private bank operating a New Zealand branch.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing sequence is as follows. First, the family's originating private bank or the principal's personal account initiates an international wire via SWIFT MT103 to the International Company's account at a New Zealand tier-1 bank, naming the beneficiary exactly as registered on the New Zealand Companies Register, together with the bank's BIC and the NZD or foreign-currency account number. Second, the wire instruction must carry the applicable Balance of Payments purpose code that the receiving bank can map to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand and Statistics New Zealand's cross-border reporting framework; for an equity capital injection the correct economic classification is 'Financial account – Direct investment – Equity injection or capital contribution', while a documented intercompany loan must be classified under the direct-investment lending category and be backed by an executed facility agreement. Third, the remitter's bank will apply its own outward-remittance controls, including source-of-funds attestation and, where the home jurisdiction restricts capital outflows, production of the relevant central-bank or FX approval. Fourth, upon arrival, the New Zealand receiving bank applies enhanced due diligence: the entity's corporate documents, register of ultimate beneficial owners, source-of-wealth declaration, and a completed purpose-of-payment form must have been pre-cleared to prevent rejection of the inward international transfer; any mismatch between the beneficiary's registered address and the bank's KYC file is a primary cause of frozen or returned funds. Fifth, post-receipt corporate formalities must be completed: an equity injection requires a board resolution and share-issuance minutes to be filed in the company's records, whereas a loan injection requires entry in the loan register and documentation of an arm's-length interest rate to avoid a deemed-dividend adjustment at year-end. Where the home jurisdiction operates exchange controls, the family should also obtain and archive the outward-remittance approval as part of the New Zealand bank's audit trail.The approved clearing infrastructure comprises the following rails. First, SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfers are used for operational disbursements such as advisory fees, investment-management fees, accounting and legal fees, director fees, and periodic dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders. Second, SWIFT MT202 COV bank-to-bank cover transfers are used where the payment route passes through an intermediary correspondent bank requiring an independent cover instruction. Third, SWIFT MT700 and MT707 messages are used for the issuance and amendment of commercial letters of credit governed by UCP 600 where the family office's investment mandate includes trade-related settlement. Fourth, domestic New Zealand-dollar disbursements are cleared through the Payments NZ infrastructure, comprising the Bulk Electronic Clearing System for batched low-value payments, the High Value Clearing System for urgent high-value transfers, and real-time final settlement via the Reserve Bank's Exchange Settlement Account System; custody-related securities transfers are settled through NZClear or through the custodian bank's institutional instruction channels. Fifth, every outbound payment must carry complete originator and beneficiary information in compliance with the New Zealand AML/CFT wire-transfer provisions and the FATF travel rule, and must bear an accurate Balance of Payments purpose code. For disbursements above the bank's enhanced-review threshold, the treasury must lodge supporting documentation with the payment instruction, including invoices, board resolutions, dividend minutes, non-resident withholding tax computations, and underlying investment contracts, to prevent suspension. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% for New Zealand resident companies; non-resident companies are taxed at 28% on New Zealand-source income. A qualifying Tax-Exempt Zone International Company may be effectively exempt on qualifying offshore income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends paid by a New Zealand company to a non-resident shareholder; treaty rates may reduce this to 0%-15% depending on the DTA, shareholding percentage, and beneficial ownership. Fully imputed dividends receive credit for New Zealand corporate tax but remain subject to NRWT at the dividend stage.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 1,000,000 aggregated annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation. New Zealand country-by-country reporting applies for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least NZD 1.125 billion. Local File, Master File, and country-by-country documentation must follow OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to offshore tax-exempt jurisdictions, but the general anti-avoidance rule in the Income Tax Act 2007 (section BG 1 and related reconstruction provisions) disregards arrangements whose dominant purpose is tax avoidance and that lack commercial reality or economic substance. The Commissioner may reconstruct an arrangement, deny tax benefits, impose shortfall penalties and interest. For a Single Family Office, economic substance must be evidenced by genuine local board meetings, actual investment decision-making, qualified New Zealand staff or external service providers, office premises, and complete governance documentation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under the Tax Administration Act 1994, sections 143A-143B, with fines up to NZD 50,000 for an individual, NZD 250,000 for a body corporate, and imprisonment for up to 5 years; in addition, a civil shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall and use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause or permit evasion are personally liable for criminal fines and imprisonment. The corporate veil may be pierced for fraud; IRD may issue a departure prohibition order and pursue civil recovery against personal assets.
Artificial Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule permits IRD to void the arrangement, reconstruct income and deductions, impose an abusive tax position penalty up to 100% of the tax shortfall, and charge interest from the due date. Denial of loss offsets and treaty benefits may also result.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for corporate tax debts where the company is insolvent and the director allowed reckless trading under the Companies Act 1993. Civil restitution, voidable transaction recoveries, and director penalties are available.
AML/CFT and Banking Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 may reach NZD 2 million for individuals and NZD 10 million for body corporates; criminal liability for money laundering can result in imprisonment of up to 7 years, asset forfeiture and confiscation under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who are willfully blind may be criminally charged, subjected to civil pecuniary penalties, disqualified from managing entities, and restrained from departing New Zealand pending investigation or prosecution.
Improper DTA / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: NRWT is reassessed at the full statutory rates: 30% for dividends and 15% for interest/royalties. Shortfall penalties of 20%-150% plus interest apply, and the non-resident may be denied treaty relief permanently for the underlying arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute false declarations of residence or knowingly participate in treaty abuse face fines under the Tax Administration Act, possible disqualification, personal liability for the unpaid tax, and extradition or travel restrictions.
Failure to Withhold and Remit NRWT

Statutory sanction: The paying company loses deduction rights for non-compliant payments and becomes liable for the full NRWT amount at 30% or 15%, plus late payment penalties, use-of-money interest, and possible prosecution for failure to account.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable as responsible persons for the unremitted NRWT under the Tax Administration Act responsible tax collector provisions; travel bans, property liens, and director disqualification may be imposed.
