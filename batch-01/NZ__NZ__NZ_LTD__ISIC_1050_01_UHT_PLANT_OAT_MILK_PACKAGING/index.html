Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None; the Companies Act 1993 does not prescribe a minimum subscribed or paid-up capital for a limited company. The company must issue at least one share, but the par value and paid-up amount are determined by the subscribers/directors at incorporation. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand; online portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity deriving New Zealand-source income or carrying on taxable activity in New Zealand must register with the Inland Revenue Department. The entity must first obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) through the New Zealand Companies Office or the NZBN Register, then use the myIR portal to apply for an IRD number. Where the entity expects to supply taxable goods or services in New Zealand, it must concurrently register for GST using the same IRD number and may be required to appoint a New Zealand resident agent for service of notices if the entity has no fixed place of business in New Zealand. Supporting documents include certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, proof of non-resident director appointments, and evidence of the New Zealand banking or payment arrangements. Registration must occur before the first taxable supply or employer obligations arise.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in New Zealand; all limited companies are automatically treated as companies taxable at the corporate rate. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 operate by statute and disallow certain deductions or require income inclusion to the extent of a mismatch, with no elective hybrid mismatch regime. A company may elect to become a look-through company (LTC) under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 if it meets the five-owner eligibility criteria, but that election is available only to closely held companies with no corporate shareholders and is not available to a foreign-owned large manufacturing subsidiary. The company may also elect to be part of a consolidated group under subpart FM of the Income Tax Act 2007 for income tax purposes if it is at least 66% commonly owned with other group members.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period, or NZD 60,000 of expected taxable supplies in the next 12 months; non-resident suppliers of remote services are subject to reverse-charge registration rules with the same NZD 60,000 threshold, subject to the special remote-services registration rules under section 54 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the supply of goods that are exported by the supplier or delivered to a purchaser who exports the goods, provided the goods are exported within 28 days of the supply and the supplier retains the prescribed evidence of export. Zero-rating also applies to offshore services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, but no deduction is available to the non-resident for services directly in connection with land or moveable personal property in New Zealand. A UHT plant producing oat milk for export must verify that each supply is a direct export sale under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and that title and risk pass to the overseas buyer; merchant-export and drop-shipping arrangements require additional documentary evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Customs Service, approved exporter system), Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of foreign currency payment, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and evidence of the timing of export within 28 days of supply.

There is no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and based on the Peppol framework, with a voluntary adoption target for business-to-business transactions. GST returns are filed electronically through the myIR portal or approved software, typically on a two-monthly, six-monthly, or monthly basis depending on turnover and registration choice. No pre-clearance API or fiscal data stamping is required before issuing invoices, but taxpayers must maintain sufficient electronic records and use prescribed GST invoice content under section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

Inland Revenue may impose shortfall penalties of 20% for unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% for abusive tax position or evasion. False GST refund claims attract recapture of the credited input tax plus interest under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and the Income Tax Act 2007. Inland Revenue may reassess GST returns and recover the tax shortfall, and serious fraudulent export zero-rating may be prosecuted criminally under the Tax Administration Act 1994, with penalties of up to NZD 50,000 for an individual and up to NZD 250,000 for a company, plus imprisonment in cases of evasion. The Commissioner also has power to issue a notice of proposed adjustment and apply the 7-year reassessment period for knowingly providing false information.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised exchange-control regime. The New Zealand dollar is freely convertible under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement; there are no central bank approvals, exchange-rate differentiation, surrender ratios, or quantitative limits on capital account transfers, dividend remittances, trade payments, or foreign-currency borrowing. Profit remittance from NZ_LTD to overseas shareholders is restricted only by tax: Non-Resident Withholding Tax on dividends (statutory 30%, reduced to treaty rates commonly 10-15%, or 0% NRWT/2% Approved Issuer Levy on qualifying related-party interest). Inward direct investment is unrestricted from an FX perspective but is screened by the Overseas Investment Office under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 where sensitive land is acquired or where a 25%+ overseas interest is taken in significant business assets valued at NZD 100 million or more.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand abolished mandatory exchange-control surrender of export receipts in the financial liberalisation of 1984-1985. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds in onshore Foreign Currency Accounts with registered banks or in any offshore account. There is no statutory conversion ratio, timeline, or Reserve Bank accounting requirement; the only continuing obligation is the tax exposure of a New Zealand tax resident on worldwide income under the Income Tax Act 2007.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), acting under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021; AML/CFT supervision is jointly exercised with the Financial Markets Authority for market conduct, the Department of Internal Affairs for money service businesses, and the Companies Office for corporate registry disclosure.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low as the primary treasury rail; recommended only as an ancillary payment channel. New Zealand does not recognise an EU-style electronic-money-institution passport, and offshore digital EMIs must separately register as Financial Service Providers under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and comply with AML/CFT Act 2009 supervision (typically with the Department of Internal Affairs as the money service business supervisor). Platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, and OFX can execute low-value TT/MT103-equivalent disbursements, payroll, and supplier payments economically, but they cannot issue or confirm Commercial Letters of Credit, hold a statutory capital subscription escrow, or provide a full New Zealand dollar current-account banking relationship. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the inward equity injection: (1) The overseas shareholder instructs its bank to send a SWIFT MT103 to the NZ bank of NZ_LTD, crediting the dedicated NZD capital-subscription escrow account. Field 50 (ordering customer) must match the shareholder of record; Field 59 must name NZ_LTD with the precise account number; Field 70 (remittance information) must state EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION SHARES NZ_LTD - UHT PLANT PROJECT - reference to the Companies Office registration. New Zealand does not maintain a statutory cross-border purpose-code schedule, so banks rely on the BPM6-aligned narrative in Field 70 plus their internal remittance-reason matrix. (2) The NZ bank completes CDD verification of the remitter against the Companies Office share register, confirms no OIO consent is required (or that OIO consent has issued), and credits the escrow account via the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) correspondent routing. (3) Disbursement to the operational account occurs only upon execution of the banking mandate and release of the capital escrow instruction signed by the directors. For intercompany shareholder loans, Field 70 should state INTERCOMPANY LOAN - PRINCIPAL DRAW 1, supported by a signed facility agreement; the 2% Approved Issuer Levy route should be considered to optimise interest withholding. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory NZD threshold for Local File or Master File preparation, but contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is required for all cross-border related-party transactions and must be produced to Inland Revenue within 30 days of request. Mandatory Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) applies when multinational group consolidated annual revenue is at least NZD 1.35 billion (approximately EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, the general anti-avoidance rule (Income Tax Act 2007, s BG 1) and the reconstruction rule (s GA 1) disregard arrangements with tax avoidance as a dominant purpose or lacking commercial reality. For a UHT oat milk manufacturing company, substantive local premises, operational employees, production risk, and standalone decision-making must be demonstrable to preserve treaty access, transfer pricing positions, and imputation credits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent False Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Tax Administration Act 1994: fine up to NZD 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years, plus a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the evaded tax, full repayment of unpaid tax, and use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who aid, abet, counsel, or procure tax evasion face personal criminal prosecution, civil restitution, and imprisonment. The corporate veil may be pierced for unpaid PAYE, GST, and other withholding taxes under the recovery provisions; courts may impose travel bans and asset-restraint orders during fraud investigations.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Anti-avoidance provisions may void or recharacterise the arrangement, impose a shortfall penalty of 100% for an abusive tax position (or 150% for evasion), deny treaty relief, and permit the Registrar of Companies to deregister the company.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified from managing companies and held personally liable for debts arising from insolvent trading or fraudulent use of the entity. Civil piercing of the veil and compensation orders are possible; serious fraud results in imprisonment.
AML/CFT and Financial Crime Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (up to NZD 200,000 per contravention for a body corporate, with higher cumulative penalties for repeated breaches), plus confiscation of proceeds under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are exposed as responsible persons; wilful non-compliance may result in personal fines, disqualification, travel restrictions, and imprisonment. The corporate veil may be pierced where the director directed or permitted the offending conduct.
Profit Repatriation / NRWT Evasion

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the disguised payment as a deemed dividend subject to 30% NRWT; shortfall penalty up to 100% (abusive tax position) or 150% (evasion); IRD may issue assessment and seizes assets; criminal prosecution for responsible officers.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally assessable for unpaid NRWT and other withholding liabilities; they may face civil suits for breach of fiduciary duty, travel bans in serious cases, and imprisonment for intentional tax evasion.
