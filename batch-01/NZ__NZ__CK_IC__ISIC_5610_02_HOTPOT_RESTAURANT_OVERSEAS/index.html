A virtual office or serviced-office address is permissible as the registered office of a New Zealand company provided it is a physical New Zealand street address at which documents may be served and at which the registered agent is available to accept service on the company's behalf; a postal box is not acceptable (Companies Act 1993, ss 184–185). Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department of New Zealand (Te Tari Taake); taxpayer online portal: myIR (https://myIR.ird.govt.nz). Export customs counterpart: New Zealand Customs Service, Trade Single Window (https://www.customs.govt.nz).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident International Company (IC) requiring a New Zealand tax file number (IRD number) or GST registration: (1) determine whether an NZ tax nexus exists - the IC is outside the NZ tax net if it has no NZ central management and control, no NZ permanent establishment, and derives no NZ-source income; (2) if registration is needed (e.g., to recover GST on condiment purchases for export, or to comply with NZ withholding obligations), complete Inland Revenue form IR742 'Application for IRD number - non-resident company' and attach certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitution, directors' register and director passport pages, all translated into English; appoint a New Zealand resident agent for service if the IC has no NZ premises; (3) submit the application by post or through a myIR non-resident access arrangement; the IRD issues the IRD number within approximately 12-20 working days; (4) for GST, file form IR356 (GST registration) via myIR simultaneously, electing a monthly filing period if the IC expects net GST refunds from export zero-rating; (5) if a new NZ-incorporated subsidiary is used, incorporation through the New Zealand Companies Office (https://companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz) automatically issues an NZ Business Number (NZBN) and IRD number without separate application. No registration fee is payable; an NZ bank account and an NZ contact address are practical prerequisites for GST refund processing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand has no US-style check-the-box regime. Relevant statutory mechanisms: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under Income Tax Act 2007 subpart HB - available only to closely held NZ-resident companies with five or fewer shareholders, not applicable to the offshore IC; (2) Consolidated group election under subpart IC for NZ-resident corporate groups; (3) optional non-resident GST registration under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 s 51(3), allowing a non-resident carrying on a taxable activity in NZ to register (and recover input GST) even where only zero-rated export supplies are made; (4) hybrid mismatch rules under ITA subpart FH (Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018) are applied mandatorily and cannot be elected away; (5) branch equivalent tax (BETA) election under subpart FK applies to banks only.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in New Zealand in any consecutive 12-month period; the same NZD 60,000 threshold has applied since 1 December 2019 to non-resident suppliers of remote services to NZ consumers. Overseas hotpot restaurant turnover (dining and condiment sales made outside NZ) is not NZ GST-exposed; NZ retail sales of condiments are standard-rated at 15%; exports of condiments from NZ are zero-rated. A non-resident may voluntarily register below the threshold under s 51 GST Act 1985 to recover input tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods (e.g., hotpot condiments and sauces) are zero-rated under s 11(1)(a) GST Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods from NZ within 28 days of the time of supply, the goods are delivered or consigned to a destination outside NZ and are not subsequently imported into NZ, and the recipient is a non-resident present outside NZ at the time of supply. Services related to the overseas restaurants (e.g., franchise, management, or dining support services) are zero-rated under s 11A where the services are performed outside NZ or are supplied to a non-resident outside NZ in connection with things outside NZ. Overseas dining sales themselves are supplies made outside NZ and fall outside the charging provision (s 8) entirely. Where the IC is GST-registered, input tax on NZ costs is fully creditable against zero-rated export output tax, yielding a net refund entitlement.

No real-time fiscal clearance, no API-mandated transaction stamping, and no digital stamping regime exists in New Zealand. Electronic filing of GST returns via myIR is compulsory for all GST-registered persons; returns are filed monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly depending on turnover. Tax invoices must be issued within 28 days of the time of supply and contain the statutory content (supplier name, IRD number, 'tax invoice' wording, recipient details for supplies over NZD 1,000). The Peppol BIS 3.0 e-invoicing network is available voluntarily for B2B and B2G exchange; central government agencies may accept Peppol invoices, but e-invoicing is not mandated by law.

New Zealand applies a retrospective reassessment window of four years from the end of the assessment period (s 108 TAA 1994), extended to ten years where evasion or fraud is established. Fraudulent export zero-rating triggers: (1) shortfall penalty of 20% of the tax avoided for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion (s 141 TAA); (2) use-of-money interest (s 120 TAA); (3) criminal liability under s 143B TAA - imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to NZD 50,000 for an individual, and a fine of up to NZD 250,000 for a company; (4) separate false-declaration offences under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 with penalties up to NZD 250,000 per declaration and forfeiture of goods. Inland Revenue's export refund integrity programme specifically audits zero-rated food and condiment exporters; the full substantiation bundle must be retained and produced on request, and refunds may be frozen pending verification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; the New Zealand dollar is fully convertible with no currency controls. Both capital account and current account transactions may be freely executed without prior approval, subject to standard anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing (AMLCFT) compliance. Dividends, branch profits, interest, and royalties may be remitted freely to non-residents, but statutory resident withholding taxes (RWT/NRWT) or the approved issuer levy regime may apply depending on the payment type and recipient jurisdiction. There are no profit repatriation moratoria or balance-of-payment restrictions. Local banks exercise contractual judgment in executing remittances, and each transfer is subject to standard sanctions screening and transaction monitoring.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion rules apply to export proceeds in New Zealand. There is no obligation to convert foreign currency earnings into NZD within any specified timeline, nor to hold them in government-designated escrow accounts. Exporters may freely hold foreign currency bank accounts overseas or domestically. The only compliance overlay is under the AMLCFT Act 2009, which requires banks to conduct due diligence and report suspicious or large cash transactions; technological reporting via the Financial Transactions Reporting (FTR) rules applies where applicable, but this is not an exchange-control surrender mechanism.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) is the apex central bank with exclusive authority over monetary policy, currency issuance, and financial system stability; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) regulates market conduct and derivatives issuers. Foreign exchange administration and cross-border capital oversight are also vested in the Reserve Bank under the Reserve Bank Act 1989, while prudential supervision of registered banks is governed by the Banking Prudential Requirements (BPR) framework. New Zealand does not maintain a separate Foreign Exchange Administration, and exchange-control approvals are not compulsory except where the Overseas Investment Act 2005 applies to sensitive land or significant business assets.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate to Low for a standalone treasury hub. New Zealand has no prudential license category for an electronic money institution (EMI) equivalent; offshore digital EMI providers are generally not permitted to hold client funds or provide full local clearing account services without partnering with a locally registered bank via a white-label arrangement. A foreign non-bank entity (such as an international card issuer or payment aggregator) may provide merchant acquiring, card-processing, and multi-currency settlement services, but it cannot become a substitute for a domestic bank account to clear NZD via NZClear. For the hotpot restaurant enterprise, the legal restrictions imply that the operational payroll, GST remittance, merchant settlement, and supplier disbursement rails must be anchored in at least one Tier-1 bank account; an EMI may be used only as a complementary funding/wholesale FX execution channel, and this must be disclosed to the main relationship financial institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the Cook Islands parent into New Zealand must be wired via SWIFT MT103 with aze purpose code detailing outward remittance of capital or shareholder loan (e.g., 'Equity capital for establishment of direct-operated hotpot restaurant' in the remittance information field). The funds should be credited to the New Zealand operating entity's dedicated capital contribution account. Before remittance, the bank requires: (i) certified board resolutions authorizing the capital injection; (ii) share allotment forms or a signed loan agreement; (iii) a letter from the New Zealand entity's compliance officer; (iv) beneficial ownership declarations; and (v) evidence of source of funds. Exchange-control approval is not required, but the bank may quarantine the funds for 1 to 5 business days. If the transfer is denominated in CNY, USD, or AUD, the bank may offer a spot FX conversion into NZD; otherwise, a foreign currency account can be maintained. All remittances must comply with the Financial Transactions Reporting Act and the Reserve Bank's electronic transfer guidelines.Trade payments for imported food products, condiments, packaging, and restaurant equipment can be routed through: (1) Direct SWIFT MT103 for open-account trade payments; (2) Documentary letters of credit issued via MT700/MT707 for high-value supplier contracts with Asian exporters; (3) Collection bills under URC522 if the supplier requires documentary security; and (4) card-based merchant acquiring/POS settlement through the local EFTPOS/Visa/Mastercard rails. Settlement of payments in NZD uses the NZClear system for same-day high-value funds transfers; cross-currency settlements use corresponding SWIFT relationships with major clearing banks in Sydney, Singapore, Hong Kong, Shanghai, and the United States. For compliance, each outward remittance must include the correct BOP purpose code (e.g., 2002 for payment for imports of food, P010 for business services, or 'barter' categories when applicable). The local merchant acquiring settlement is settled via the three-party acquiring network (e.g., Worldline New Zealand, Windcave) into the operating account on a T+1 basis, net of agreed fees and reserves. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderate to Low for a standalone treasury hub. New Zealand has no prudential license category for an electronic money institution (EMI) equivalent; offshore digital EMI providers are generally not permitted to hold client funds or provide full local clearing account services without partnering with a locally registered bank via a white-label arrangement. A foreign non-bank entity (such as an international card issuer or payment aggregator) may provide merchant acquiring, card-processing, and multi-currency settlement services, but it cannot become a substitute for a domestic bank account to clear NZD via NZClear. For the hotpot restaurant enterprise, the legal restrictions imply that the operational payroll, GST remittance, merchant settlement, and supplier disbursement rails must be anchored in at least one Tier-1 bank account; an EMI may be used only as a complementary funding/wholesale FX execution channel, and this must be disclosed to the main relationship financial institution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million aggregate cross-border related-party revenue generally requires Local File documentation. Master File required where group revenue exceeds NZD 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply OECD-style economic substance regulations directly, but GAAR (BG 1/GA 1) and transfer pricing rules require commercial substance and arm's length outcomes. Shell entities without substance face recharacterisation and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraud

Statutory sanction: Fines up to NZ$50,000 (individual) or NZ$100,000 (company) and penalties up to 150% of tax shortfall; imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid tax, subject to civil recovery, travel bans, prohibition from managing companies, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: Deductions disallowed, GAAR recharacterisation, penalties up to 100% of shortfall, potential registration cancellation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documents may face civil penalties, personal liability, disqualification, and travel restrictions.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violation

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to NZ$2 million per contravention; criminal penalties for individuals of imprisonment up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers can be personally liable, with fines up to NZ$300,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years.
Failure to File Transfer Pricing Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Initial NZ$20,000 penalty, recurring NZ$50,000 per year, plus transfer pricing adjustments and shortfall penalties up to 50%.

Director personal liability: Directors causing non-compliance may be personally liable for penalties and subject to public naming and debarment.
Withholding Tax Evasion on Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Payer liable for uncollected NRWT, penalties up to 150%, late payment penalties, and use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly involved may be personally liable for withholding liabilities and face imprisonment up to 4 years.
Unlicensed Food Business Operation

Statutory sanction: Fines up to NZ$50,000 for the company, daily fines, closure orders, and seizure of goods.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers may face fines up to NZ$500,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years for consenting to contraventions.
