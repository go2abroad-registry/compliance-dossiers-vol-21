Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Companies Act 1993 does not impose a minimum paid-up capital requirement on a company limited by shares, and no escrow or blocked capital account is required or permitted to be filed at the New Zealand Companies Office. Shares may be issued with no par value, and initial shares may be allotted for such consideration as the directors determine in accordance with section 41 of the Companies Act 1993.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is mandatory under the Building Act 2004, requiring compliant means of escape, fire exits, extinguishers, alarms and a building warrant of fitness issued by the territorial authority. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Tāke) — online portal: myIR at https://myir.ird.govt.nz; general information at https://www.ird.govt.nz. Entity incorporation is separately administered by the New Zealand Companies Office at https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz, which issues the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN).

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand-incorporated limited company with non-resident ownership (foreign-owned NZ Ltd): Step 1 — Incorporate the company with the Companies Office; the incorporation data is automatically shared with Inland Revenue, which allocates the company's 9-digit IRD number. If the IRD number is not issued automatically, file Form IR596 (Application for an IRD Number – Company) through myIR or by post. Step 2 — Provide IRD with the company's registered office address, directors' details, and the foreign parent/beneficial owner's foreign tax identification number (if any) to complete customer due diligence. Step 3 — Register for GST via myIR (or Form IR356) after the IRD number is active. GST registration is compulsory if the company carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand and its taxable supplies in any 12-month period equal or exceed NZD 60,000; exports of goods count as taxable supplies for this threshold even when zero-rated. Voluntary registration below NZD 60,000 is permitted to recover input tax. Step 4 — Set the GST filing frequency (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly), add the company's bank and foreign-currency settlement accounts (Stripe/credit-card payouts), and nominate a tax agent if applicable. Step 5 — If the company also supplies remote services to New Zealand consumers, assess whether the offshore supplier GST registration regime under sections 8(4A)–(4E) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 applies separately.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation | Territorial_Scope (worldwide income with statutory foreign income exemption) | Tax_Exempt_Zone Not_Applicable — An NZ-incorporated limited company is a New Zealand tax resident and is taxed at the entity level at the standard corporate rate of 28% on worldwide income (Income Tax Act 2007). The foreign income exemption may apply to certain active foreign business income, foreign dividends, and Australian FIF interests. New Zealand has no sub-national corporate income tax and no tax-exempt production zones; the GST is national (has_sub_tax_distinction = false). A US-style pass-through default is not available; the look-through company (LTC) election is restricted to qualifying closely held companies and does not apply to a standard trading company without an affirmative election.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box regime; entity classification is statutory. Available elections and mechanisms include: (1) Look-through company (LTC) election under subpart HB (Income Tax Act 2007) for eligible companies with five or fewer owners who are individuals or trustees — income, gains, expenses, and tax credits flow through to owners if elected; not available to a company with corporate shareholders or widely held ownership. (2) Consolidated group election under Part IS for companies under 100% common ownership, allowing a wholly-owned group of companies to file as one consolidated taxpayer. (3) Voluntary GST registration under section 51(4) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 to claim input tax credits before the NZD 60,000 compulsory threshold is reached. (4) Elections and ordering rules under subpart FH for hybrid mismatch arrangements (including the same-jurisdiction election and the imported mismatch timing rules), aligning the NZ result with OECD BEPS Action 2. The Qualifying Company regime is closed to newly incorporated companies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 — compulsory registration applies to any person carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand whose total value of taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) in any 12-month period exceeds NZD 60,000, or which is expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in the next 12 months. Voluntary registration is available below the threshold. Non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value goods to New Zealand consumers are subject to a separate offshore supplier registration regime with the same NZD 60,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: A supply of goods is zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply (earliest of invoice date, payment date, or goods removal date). The interactive AI pet robots must physically leave New Zealand to a foreign destination and must not be re-imported for use in New Zealand. Services: Zero-rating applies under section 11(1)(d) to services supplied in connection with movable personal property located outside New Zealand, under section 11(1)(e) to services performed outside New Zealand for a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of performance, and under section 11(1)(m) to remote services supplied to a non-resident recipient outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed. Over-the-air software updates and speech-synthesizer cloud services sold separately to overseas customers may qualify as zero-rated remote services if the recipient is a non-resident and the services are performed outside New Zealand. All zero-rated exports must be disclosed in the GST return at 0% and count toward the NZD 60,000 registration threshold.

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based invoice pre-approval. E-invoicing is voluntary for B2B supplies and is implemented on the Peppol framework; government agencies are required to receive Peppol e-invoices but suppliers face no e-invoice mandate. GST-registered businesses must file GST returns electronically via myIR, and the system operates on document-based self-assessment. A tax invoice satisfying section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 must be issued within 28 days of the time of supply, and a buyer must hold a valid tax invoice to claim input tax credits. All records supporting GST returns, including export documentation, must be retained for 7 years under section 22 of the Tax Administration Act 1994.

New Zealand law imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is applied as the schema-default financial covenant. Inland Revenue and the New Zealand Customs Service share data to challenge zero-rating claims where goods are not exported within 28 days, where the exporter fails to hold the required export evidence, or where proceeds are not received from the stated foreign buyer. If the zero-rating claim is incorrect, output tax becomes payable at 15% together with use-of-money interest under section 120 of the Tax Administration Act 1994. Shortfall penalties under section 141 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply by reference to the taxpayer's conduct: 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. Criminal liability under sections 143 and 144 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 exposes directors to fines of up to NZD 50,000 for a first offence and NZD 100,000 for subsequent offences, and imprisonment of up to 5 years for wilful evasion. The general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 may be invoked to re-characterise artificial export, pricing, or repatriation arrangements. Standard GST assessments may be amended within 4 years of the end of the return period, extended to 7 years where evasion or fraudulent intent is established; reversed refunds attract use-of-money interest until repaid.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully convertible currency under a free-floating exchange rate regime. There are no exchange controls, no restrictions on capital account transactions, and no requirement for Central Bank approval for foreign direct investment, dividend repatriation, or the conversion of New Zealand dollars to foreign currency. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely transferable both domestically and internationally, subject only to standard AML/CFT reporting thresholds and sanctions screening. Profit remittances by a non-resident shareholder of a New Zealand Limited Company are taxed under the non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) regime, but there is no FX-based blockage or convertibility hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in offshore or onshore foreign-currency-denominated bank accounts indefinitely. All export proceeds must be reported for statistical balance-of-payments purposes through the standard banking system where a transaction exceeds the AML/CFT threshold, but no repatriation timeline or conversion requirement applies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua); prudential supervision and payment system oversight sit with the Reserve Bank, while certain cross-border financial transaction reporting obligations are administered by the New Zealand Police Financial Intelligence Unit and the New Zealand Customs Service.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not locally regulated as banks in New Zealand. A FinTech EMI holding a European e-money license or an Australian AFSL/ADV may provide foreign-currency payment cards and virtual IBANs, but such providers generally do not offer New Zealand domestic clearing (via the Settlement Account System and Bulk Electronic Clearing System) unless they establish a local arrangement with a New Zealand registered bank. Legal constraints include the prohibition on using the term 'bank' without Reserve Bank registration under the Banking (Preregistration) Act 1980, the requirement for any non-bank payment provider to comply with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act) if it is a 'financial institution' providing money transfer services, and the inability of an offshore EMI to be a direct participant in the New Zealand Payments Net. For a low-risk AI toy hardware exporter, an offshore EMI is viable only for ancillary collection of small international card receipts, not for treasury-grade clearing of trade settlements, which should be routed through a local Tier 1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders may inject equity capital into the New Zealand Limited Company via international SWIFT payment in the shareholder's own name referencing the company name and share-allotment purpose. No Reserve Bank approval or exchange-control permission is required; however, the bank will require the completed 'KYC purpose of payment' field to state clearly 'Capital contribution / Subscription for shares in [Company Name]' and may request a copy of the board resolution authorising the share issue, the register of allotment (Form NZ Companies Office AR), and the shareholders' agreement. Inward payments in any major foreign currency may be credited to a foreign-currency account or converted to NZD at the bank's prevailing FX rate. If the amount exceeds NZD 10,000 in cash (or its equivalent), a 'large cash transaction report' must be filed by the bank under the AML/CFT regime; additionally, any telegraphic transfer over NZD 10,000 is automatically captured by the bank's prescribed transaction reporting system and reported to the New Zealand Police Financial Intelligence Unit. Inbound foreign direct investment is screened under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 only if the investor acquires 'sensitive land' or 'significant business assets' (typically more than NZD 100 million turnover); an AI pet robot manufacturer with a standard product-companies register or a special-purpose vehicle will generally not exceed this threshold. Documentary package recommended: capital contribution agreement, board minutes, share register update, source-of-funds evidence (shareholder's bank statement and accumulated-profit financials), and a solicitor's trust account letter where applicable.Cross-border trade settlement for component imports and marketing purchases should be executed through the banking-grade SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfer for open-account transactions, and by irrevocable documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700 series) for larger supplier contracts where payment security is required. The Australian and New Zealand Banking Group (ANZ), ASB, BNZ, Westpac and HSBC provide direct SWIFT connectivity, electronic banking portals (ANZ Transactive, BNZ Online, Westpac One, HSBCnet), and multi-currency accounts. For outbound remittances exceeding the value threshold, the remitting bank must report the transaction to the Financial Intelligence Unit as a prescribed transaction; typical 'purpose of payment' codes used are under the IMF Balance of Payments classification: 'General trade – goods import' (D-prefixed BOP codes such as D120 for primary income or trade codes assigned by the bank) and each bank's internal reason code mapping applies. Where the New Zealand entity imports electronic components from a related supplier, a transfer-pricing memorandum should evidence the arm's-length price to avoid New Zealand Revenue (Inland Revenue) scrutiny. Payment timing discipline: align disbursements with the LC presentation schedule and bill-of-lading arrival to avoid unnecessary FX conversion costs; use same-day NZD clearing through the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) for domestic legs. Permissible clearing rails also include CLS (Continuous Linked Settlement) for major currency pairs, and bank-to-bank correspondent networks; however, the practical rails are SWIFT MT103 for direct beneficiary settlement, MT700 LC confirmed by a New Zealand bank, and card-scheme settlement via Stripe/acquiring bank for consumer e-commerce revenue.Deliverable FX Forwards (outright NZD/USD, NZD/CNH and NZD/EUR forwards) with a registered New Zealand bank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a bank-specific FX terms master agreementFX Swaps (roll-over swaps used to manage working capital across settlement date mismatches)Cross-Currency Swaps (fixed-floating cross-currency interest-rate swaps for hedging a foreign-currency term loan financing the working-capital cycle)Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on restricted Asian currencies such as CNY/CNH, INR, IDR, PHP or KRW where the underlying exposure is settled in such currencies and the NDF is cash-settled in NZD or USD; permitted for hedging forecast receivables or payables, provided the NDF is transacted with a licensed New Zealand bank or an authorised FX dealer and clearly documented as a hedge of commercial exposure rather than a speculative positionFX Options (plain-vanilla vanillas and put/call options) used to cap adverse currency movement on large discrete trade payments, subject to board approval and derivative risk-management minutesMoney-market and FX spot accelerators (same-day FX conversion and sweep accounts) as a liquidity tool, not a derivative, to minimise conversion cost on regular inbound card settlement

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not locally regulated as banks in New Zealand. A FinTech EMI holding a European e-money license or an Australian AFSL/ADV may provide foreign-currency payment cards and virtual IBANs, but such providers generally do not offer New Zealand domestic clearing (via the Settlement Account System and Bulk Electronic Clearing System) unless they establish a local arrangement with a New Zealand registered bank. Legal constraints include the prohibition on using the term 'bank' without Reserve Bank registration under the Banking (Preregistration) Act 1980, the requirement for any non-bank payment provider to comply with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act) if it is a 'financial institution' providing money transfer services, and the inability of an offshore EMI to be a direct participant in the New Zealand Payments Net. For a low-risk AI toy hardware exporter, an offshore EMI is viable only for ancillary collection of small international card receipts, not for treasury-grade clearing of trade settlements, which should be routed through a local Tier 1 bank.
