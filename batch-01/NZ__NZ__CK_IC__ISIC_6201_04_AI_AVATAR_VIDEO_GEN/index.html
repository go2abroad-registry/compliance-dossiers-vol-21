Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum authorized, issued, or paid-up share capital applies to a New Zealand company under the Companies Act 1993 (s 10 abolishes the concept of authorized capital). Shares may be allotted for nil or nominal consideration without escrow, deposit, or capitalization account requirements. The operative statutory constraint is solvency-based: under ss 42–43, directors must believe on reasonable grounds that the company will satisfy the solvency test immediately after each share allotment. For the CK_IC entity, the international company classification imposes no separate capital benchmark.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

No pass-through or look-through classification applies to a non-resident corporate vehicle under New Zealand law.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; tax classification follows statutory residency (sections YC 2-YC 3, ITA 2007). Available statutory elections and regimes that a CK_IC may utilise include: (1) GST grouping election (sections 55-56, GST Act 1985) — related New Zealand companies may elect to be treated as a single GST group; (2) Approved Issuer Levy election (section 32J, Stamp and Cheque Duties Act 1971) — election by an approved issuer to pay 2% AIL on qualifying interest paid to non-residents in substitution for 15% NRWT; (3) hybrid mismatch ordering and timing elections (Subpart FH, ITA 2007) — allows the IC to align deduction timing and prevent double-dip outcomes under New Zealand's BEPS hybrid rules; (4) Foreign Investment Fund (FIF) elective accounting for resident shareholders, though inapplicable to the non-resident IC itself; (5) election for the GST treatment of cross-border remote services under section 8B of the GST Act 1985 to characterise the AI avatar supply as an export of services eligible for zero-rating.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The CK_IC may claim 0% GST on its exported AI avatar video-generation services under the following cumulative criteria: (a) the supply constitutes a supply of services to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed (section 11A(1)(c), GST Act 1985); (b) for automated/remote services — including cloud-delivered AI video synthesis, virtual avatar generation, and SaaS subscriptions — supplied to non-resident recipients located offshore, the supply qualifies as an export of remote services and is zero-rated; (c) the recipient is not in New Zealand at the time the service is rendered or consumed, and the service is not physically performed in New Zealand; (d) the supply is not made to a person in New Zealand and is not connected with a taxable activity carried on in New Zealand by the recipient; (e) the supplier holds contemporaneous evidentiary material confirming the non-resident status of the recipient, including a foreign tax registration number, geolocation/IP address logs, a registered business address outside New Zealand, and payment instruments originating offshore. The zero-rating applies to B2B and B2C remote digital services provided the recipient and the consumption location are outside New Zealand.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand Dollar (NZD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. New Zealand maintains no exchange control regime, no surrender requirements, and no balancing restrictions on the repatriation of dividends, royalties, or loan principal by non-resident investors. Profit remittance hurdles arise exclusively from tax source rules, not FX rules: dividend and royalty payments to a non-resident recipient attract Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) at 15% unpaid or 10% under an applicable double tax agreement if the recipient qualifies, and interest can be lowered to 2% via the Approved Issuer Levy (AIL). The Tax-Exempt Zone classification of the International Company does not erase the payer's withholding obligations under sections RF2 and RF8 of the Income Tax Act 2007. Capital inflows from non-resident shareholders are unrestricted unless the investment acquires significant New Zealand business assets, sensitive land, or fishing quota requiring OIO consent under the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which is not triggered by SaaS intellectual property licensing, receivables purchasing, or payment settlement flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no export proceeds surrender obligations under New Zealand law. The International Company may retain 100% of its export earnings in foreign currency and may hold settlement balances in offshore digital platforms (Stripe, Paddle, Wise) without restriction. Foreign-currency receipts are aggregated for Balance of Payments statistical collection by the Reserve Bank and Statistics New Zealand, and a reporting entity whose total annual cross-border turnover exceeds NZD 1 million may be selected for the quarterly BOP enterprise survey; however, completion is a statistical return and does not mandate conversion, deposit, or repatriation timelines. For prudential accounting, export proceeds should be recognised at spot settlement date using RBNZ daily reference rates and matching hedges must be properly designated under the IC's accounting policy to avoid revaluation volatility.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ / Te Pūtea Matua), acting as the prudential regulator and operator of the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) and the NZClear high-value settlement system. Exchange control derived from the Crown's residual foreign-exchange powers under the Public Finance Act 1989, administered in practice through the Overseas Investment Act 2005 by the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) for investment-specific screening, and the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 for instruments requiring regulated offer disclosure. For routine trade and SaaS export flows, no RBNZ licence, prior approval, or ministerial consent is required.
