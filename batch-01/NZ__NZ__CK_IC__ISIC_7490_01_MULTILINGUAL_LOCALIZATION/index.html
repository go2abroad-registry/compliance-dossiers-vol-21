Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake), Government of New Zealand; digital portal: myIR (https://www.myir.ird.govt.nz), supplemented by the New Zealand Companies Office (https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz) for corporate registration and NZBN issuance.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity (e.g., a Cook Islands International Company) carrying on business in New Zealand must: (1) register as an overseas company with the New Zealand Companies Office on the Companies Register and obtain a New Zealand Company Number and New Zealand Business Number (NZBN); (2) apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number using form IR596 (non-resident company carrying on a taxable activity in NZ) or IR742 (non-resident company deriving New Zealand-sourced income without a taxable activity); (3) provide certified certificate of incorporation, constitution, directors' and shareholders' details, the registered office or New Zealand agent for service of process, and the date of commencement of New Zealand business; (4) register for GST on the same application or subsequently via myIR, specifying the expected turnover to trigger the appropriate filing frequency (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly); and (5) in parallel, register as an employer with IRD (IR334/IR335 forms) if the New Zealand operation will employ staff in-country.

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box classification regime. Available elections applicable to a foreign International Company include: (i) GST group registration election under sections 55-58 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 to consolidate the New Zealand branch with other group companies; (ii) election to file GST on a payments basis under section 19G of the GST Act 1985 where taxable supplies do not exceed NZD 2,000,000 in a 12-month period; (iii) election to zero-rate cross-border services on a B2B basis with written confirmation from the non-resident recipient; and (iv) application of the hybrid and branch mismatch neutralisation rules in Part FM and sections FH 1-FH 11 of the Income Tax Act 2007, which require the entity and its New Zealand permanent establishment to document any hybrid financial instrument or branch payment arrangements and align tax outcomes to avoid double non-taxation.

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies (including remote/digital services such as multilingual translation and media dubbing) made in New Zealand or supplied to New Zealand resident non-GST-registered consumers in any rolling 12-month period, which triggers mandatory registration; voluntary registration is permitted where a taxable activity is carried on, and cross-border B2B supplies to GST-registered New Zealand recipients are reverse-charged and excluded from the registration threshold for non-resident suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the GST Act 1985 for: (i) supplies of goods physically exported by the supplier within 28 days of the time of supply (section 11(1)(a)-(ab)); (ii) services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the services are not supplied to a New Zealand-resident private consumer and are not directly in connection with land situated in New Zealand or goods in New Zealand at the time of supply (section 11(1)(c)-(d)); (iii) services supplied to a non-resident person for the benefit of goods that are in New Zealand at the time of supply but are subsequently exported (section 11(1)(e)); (iv) remote services supplied by a non-resident supplier to a New Zealand GST-registered business (B2B zero-rating); and (v) translation, localization, and media dubbing services delivered entirely offshore to foreign clients, where the supplier holds a valid certificate of non-residence signed by the overseas customer (the 'as far as we know' certificate under section 11(3)) and the services are performed or enjoyed outside New Zealand.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EXD) lodged through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window (for physically exported goods such as master media or dubbing assets)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange (FX) settlement advice from a New Zealand bank or a Wise B2B / Payoneer payout record evidencing receipt of consideration from the overseas buyer
  • Signed certificate of non-residence of the overseas customer (IRD-approved 'as far as we know' certificate)
  • Original GST tax invoice and the underlying contract or purchase order identifying the non-resident recipient and the services performed
  • Proof of offshore performance and delivery (production files, dubbing studio logs, or digital delivery confirmation to the foreign client)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transaction-level government clearance regime exists in New Zealand. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Peppol network (Te Papa Whakatu) supporting PEPPOL BIS 3.0. Inland Revenue requires all registered taxpayers to file GST returns (GST101) electronically through myIR, which is the statutory digital filing channel, but no API-based pre-approval of invoices is mandated. Tax invoices must be issued within 28 days of the time of supply, and all GST records must be retained for seven years.

Inland Revenue actively audits zero-rating claims and may re-characterise a supply as standard-rated where statutory conditions are not met, causing the GST to be clawed back together with penalties and use-of-money interest. If exported goods are not exported within 28 days or are diverted to the New Zealand market, the supply is standard-rated in the period of divergence. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 are: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 150% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion; fraudulent zero-rating, forged export documentation, or deliberately falsified FX repatriation records also attract criminal liability under sections 143A-144 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, with maximum fines of NZD 50,000 for individuals and NZD 250,000 for companies, liability for reputational disqualification, imprisonment of up to 5 years in serious evasion cases, and extension of the standard 4-year assessment period where fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. New Zealand maintains no exchange controls, no foreign-exchange approval gates, and no limitations on the repatriation of dividends, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance is subject only to standard tax obligations, including Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) at domestic or applicable double-tax-agreement rates, and the bank's ordinary documentary verification. Capital inflows and outflows can be freely routed through any Registered Bank, provided transactions meet the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's statistical reporting thresholds for balance-of-payments purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender, no minimum conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for repatriation of export receipts. The International Company may retain 100% of foreign-currency earnings in domestic or offshore foreign-currency accounts for an unlimited period and may convert to NZD at market rates at its sole discretion. Reporting applies only for statistical balance-of-payments purposes through the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's international transactions reporting system, not for exchange-control purposes.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua); statutory oversight of the New Zealand Foreign Exchange Act 2010, the AML/CFT Act 2009, and the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989, with the Financial Markets Authority and the Department of Internal Affairs supervising non-bank financial institutions

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise is rated High for international collections and disbursements because it is a New Zealand-founded, FMC-registered, globally licensed EMI with compliant NZ AML/CFT registration and local settlement rails in NZD, USD, EUR, GBP, AUD, and 30+ corridors. Payoneer is rated Medium: it is fully viable for outbound disbursements to localization contractors and media vendors and for inbound collections from global marketplaces, but it cannot substitute for a Registered Bank because it cannot provide credit, commercial letters of credit, or formal FX hedging. Offshore EMIs not registered under the NZ AML/CFT Act 2009 face a legal constraint: they cannot maintain solicited relationships with New Zealand counterparties and must not handle core treasury settlement flows of an International Company without a locally registered agent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections are routed via SWIFT MT103 to the New Zealand bank account with a compliant beneficiary reference of 'CAP-CONTRIBUTION / CK_IC / NZL' or 'INTERCO-LOAN' plus a transaction purpose code aligned to ISIC 7490 services capitalisation. The remitting bank must include the International Company's legal entity identifier, the ultimate remitter's name and account number in field 50, and a detailed purpose-of-payment text in field 70 to avoid AML/CFT suspension. For equity injections, the board capital-resolution and updated shareholder register must be lodged with the New Zealand Companies Office, and the receiving bank will perform source-of-funds verification for any credit above NZD 10,000 equivalent. If the injection derives from a connected offshore trust, the trust deed, protector consent, and an NRWT exemption declaration must accompany the funds within 48 hours of receipt.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for cross-border royalties, translator fees, and SaaS or media-platform settlements, with ISO 20022 payments via Payments NZ for domestic NZD legs; beneficiary bank and BIC or IBAN must be verified through a KYB check. For large-scale dubbing or co-production contracts, a Commercial Letter of Credit issued under UCP600 and advised by the vendor's bank provides irrevocable settlement security. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% (Income Tax Act 2007, Schedule 1, Part A) for New Zealand-resident companies; a non-resident company is taxed at the same 28% rate but only on New Zealand-source income and income attributable to a New Zealand branch or permanent establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% statutory non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends paid by a New Zealand-resident company to a non-resident shareholder (Income Tax Act 2007, s RF 7). Resident shareholders are subject to resident withholding tax (RWT) at the statutory default rate of 33% (28% election available for resident companies). Most New Zealand DTAs reduce the dividend NRWT rate to 15% for portfolio investments and 5% for corporate shareholdings of 10% or more (subject to treaty-specific limitations and beneficial-ownership clauses).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

A company with a standard 31 March balance date must file the IR4 by 7 July (non-tax-agent); terminal tax for the same standard balance date is payable by 7 February of the following income year. IRD-approved tax agents may receive extended filing deadlines (commonly to 28 February of the following year). Non-standard balance dates carry IRD-adjusted due dates.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a 31 March balance-date company, provisional tax instalments are due on 28 August, 15 January, and 7 May of the relevant income-year cycle; terminal tax is due 7 February following the balance date (non-tax-agent) or 7 April with an approved tax agent.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Monthly returns are due on the 20th of the month following payment; annual reconciliation of withholding accruals is completed in the IR4.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand transfer-pricing legislation is contained in sections GC 6–GC 14 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and requires all cross-border related-party dealings to be priced on an arm's-length basis. There is no legislative de minimis threshold, but IRD administrative guidance (Transfer Pricing Guidelines) states that full contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation should be maintained where aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZ$10 million; simplified documentation is expected for transactions between NZ$3 million and NZ$10 million; below NZ$3 million, the IRD still requires a reasonable written analysis of the pricing position. For this localization agency (ISIC 7490), intercompany translation services, voice-talent fees, project-management charges, and any payable or receivable balances with related parties in the Cook Islands, Asia-Pacific, or Europe must be supported by OECD-compliant benchmarking.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, the IRD enforces equivalent substance requirements through: (i) the general anti-avoidance rule (Income Tax Act 2007, s BG 1) permitting the Commissioner to void arrangements whose dominant purpose is tax avoidance; (ii) the company-residence rule (s YD 2) under which a foreign-incorporated company becomes a New Zealand tax resident if its head office is in New Zealand or its directors exercise central management and control in New Zealand; and (iii) the thin capitalisation rules (s FE) restricting debt deductions of multinational groups. For the 'Tax Exempt Zone' classification of the CK_IC International Company to hold, the entity must maintain offshore board meetings, offshore decision-making, a substantive physical presence outside New Zealand, and derive no income sourced in New Zealand. A bare shell with New Zealand-resident directors, local project management, or New Zealand bank/fintech accounts (Wise B2B, Payoneer) collecting global localization revenue is presumptively at risk of being re-characterised as a New Zealand-resident taxpayer or as an abusive tax arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abusive Tax Position

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax for evasion or a related offence, 100% for an 'abusive tax position', 40% for gross carelessness, and 20%–40% for an unacceptable tax position (Tax Administration Act 1994, s 141); criminal conviction under s 143A attracts a fine up to NZ$250,000 (body corporate) or NZ$50,000 (individual) and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; falsifying records under s 143B carries an additional 5-year imprisonment exposure; the Commissioner can issue a departure prohibition order under s 157 preventing directors from leaving New Zealand while tax debts are outstanding.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the company is used as a sham or where directors 'direct, authorise, acquiesce in, or participate in' tax evasion: directors and shadow controllers are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax shortfall; personal prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years is available; a civil liability notice may be issued against directors for unpaid withholding tax (PAYE/GST) under the director penalty provisions; travel bans under s 157 apply to individuals with outstanding tax liabilities.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The IRD will disregard the offshore entity under GAAR (s BG 1) and assess the economic income as derived by the New Zealand PE or by the underlying New Zealand actors; 100% abusive-tax-position shortfall penalty; 150% if evasion is shown; the arrangement is void against the Commissioner; criminal prosecution for false declarations under s 143B; the foreign company is also exposed to Companies Office deregistration for non-compliance with the Companies Act 1993.

Director personal liability: Directors who permit the company to be used as a sham may be personally assessed as a result of the 'clean-skin' test under the Companies Act 1993 (permitting the court to grant leave to pierce the veil where the company is carrying on a sham); directors face civil liability for losses caused; prosecution for fraud (Crimes Act 1961 s 240-241) with imprisonment up to 7 years; departure prohibition orders may be imposed for assessed tax.
