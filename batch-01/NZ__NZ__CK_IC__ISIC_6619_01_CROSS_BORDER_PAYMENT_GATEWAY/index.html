Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required to incorporate an ordinary limited company under the Companies Act 1993, and no escrow deposit is mandated at incorporation. Shares may be allotted for nil or nominal consideration, provided shares are not issued at a discount. However, for a cross-border payment gateway, the board must maintain segregated settlement accounts and, if the PSP holds or controls client funds, comply with payment-network trust/escrow rules. The higher capital thresholds of the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 (NZD 15 million for a registered bank; NZD 5 million for a non-bank deposit taker) are not engaged unless the entity elects to take deposits or operate as an authorised deposit-taking institution.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and evacuation scheme approval from Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017; Building Warrant of Fitness under the Building Act 2004; structural load clearance for computer room raised floors and server racks; electrical certificate of compliance for high-voltage, UPS and network equipment; workplace clearance and health-and-safety induction under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015; and a civil-defence emergency plan covering earthquake, tsunami and power-outage scenarios for continuous payment-rail availability.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue; Te Tari Taake) — official online portal: myIR at www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Determine New Zealand tax nexus: a foreign-incorporated International Company (IC) must obtain a New Zealand IRD number if it derives New Zealand-sourced income, carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand, or supplies remote or digital services (including cross-border merchant acquiring, clearing and payment gateway services) to New Zealand-resident consumers or businesses; a pure offshore tax-exempt IC with no New Zealand nexus requires no New Zealand registration. Step 2 — Apply for the statutory TIN (IRD number): submit the Inland Revenue 'Non-resident company registration' application (form IR742, or the online application through myIR), attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, directors' and shareholders' registers, the ultimate beneficial ownership structure, the contract with the acquiring bank and card schemes, and a board resolution recording that central management and control is exercised outside New Zealand. Step 3 — Register for goods and services tax (GST): file the GST registration application in myIR selecting the taxable activity category for financial services/payment processing, entering the actual or expected 12-month taxable supplies, and choosing the monthly or two-monthly tax period; non-resident suppliers of remote services must use the dedicated non-resident service supplier registration track and nominate an address for service in New Zealand and, if required, a New Zealand tax agent. Step 4 — Obtain ancillary statutory identifiers: if the IC establishes a New Zealand branch or registered place of business, register as an overseas company on the New Zealand Companies Register and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN); concurrently register as a Financial Service Provider on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and as a reporting entity under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 with the Department of Internal Affairs, which are statutory licensing prerequisites for the Visa/Mastercard direct acquiring and Fedwire clearing rails. Step 5 — Evidence the tax-exempt classification: retain the IC's home-jurisdiction tax exemption certificate, annual return and the offshore International Companies Act registration record so that Inland Revenue accepts the Tax_Exempt_Zone status for income tax purposes and applies GST only to New Zealand-targeted supplies; because the IC's residence jurisdiction does not maintain a double tax agreement with New Zealand, reliance is placed exclusively on the domestic non-resident sourcing rules in the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 to limit New Zealand exposure to New Zealand-sourced income and New Zealand-resident consumer supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not administer a United States-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows the substantive form under the Companies Act 1993, the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. The available statutory elections and alignment mechanisms for the IC payment gateway structure are: (1) voluntary GST registration (GST Act 1985 s 51) for supplies below the NZD 60,000 threshold; (2) GST group registration (GST Act 1985 s 55) enabling the IC and any New Zealand branch or associated gateway entity to file a single consolidated GST return; (3) the non-resident remote services simplified registration and return regime under the GST Act 1985 (applicable to offshore suppliers of electronic services) with an election to zero-rate business-to-business supplies made to New Zealand GST-registered recipients; (4) the Look-Through Company (LTC) election under Income Tax Act 2007 ss HA 4-HA 6, available only to closely held New Zealand-resident companies and therefore unavailable to the non-resident IC unless the IC becomes managed and controlled in New Zealand, in which case entity-level taxation at 28% is the default; (5) the financial services input tax attribution election and de minimis threshold under GST Act 1985 s 20(3H) and s 20C, which affects the recoverability of GST on acquired services where the IC also supplies exempt domestic financial services; (6) the hybrid mismatch and cross-border alignment rules under Income Tax Act 2007 part FH, which require the IC to file a hybrid mismatch disposition notice where deduction/non-inclusion outcomes would otherwise arise; and (7) the branch equivalent method option under the transfer pricing rules for any New Zealand permanent establishment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. New Zealand maintains no exchange controls, no quantitative restrictions, and no prior approval requirements on cross-border fund transfers, consistent with its Article VIII IMF obligations under the Bretton Woods system. Profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriation to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted from a banking and foreign-exchange perspective; the only statutory constraints are the imposition of architecture non-resident withholding tax on dividends and the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which applies exclusively to certain sensitive land or significant business assets. The International Company and its cross-border payment gateway operation may freely hold multi-currency accounts, contract foreign currency settlements, and transfer funds offshore without Reserve Bank consent or conversion mandates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters, licensed payment service providers, and online merchant acquirers may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts without repatriation obligation, maintain nostro and wallet balances in any freely convertible currency, and execute offshore settlements directly through authorized financial institutions or registered clearing channels. Cross-border earnings from acquiring services are treated as ordinary business receipts and are subject only to standard corporate income-tax measurement and source-category characterization under New Zealand domestic law, not to any foreign-exchange custody or surrender regime.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), with the Financial Markets Authority as conduct regulator for financial services and the Department of Internal Affairs as AML/CFT supervisor for payment service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) within the target payment gateway model carry a Medium viability rating. Registration as a Financial Service Provider under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 is mandatory, and a New Zealand resident agent is required for foreign-owned entities conducting regulated financial services. Direct membership of the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) is legally limited to registered banks, so an offshore EMI without a New Zealand banking booking entity must clear through a tier-1 domestic bank sponsor or obtain a Department of Internal Affairs reporting-entity designation under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. The practical constraints include the absence of passported EMI licensing privileges from OECD or EU home states, reinforced capital-adequacy expectations from sponsoring banks, and the requirement to maintain segregated statutory trust accounts for merchant funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or subordinated shareholder funding is executed through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 credit transfers with the payment narrative expressly stating the transaction as 'direct investment in cross-border payment gateway operations' or 'intercompany financing for merchant settlement capital', and the receiving bank mapping the transaction to the appropriate Balance of Payments direct-investment classification for New Zealand statutory reporting. All outbound credit transfers are batched and pre-funded through a dedicated operational clearing account maintained with a tier-1 domestic settlement bank to eliminate intraday liquidity gaps within the Exchange Settlement Account System window. Statutory deliverableDeadline window Government feeDelinquency sanctions
Annual Return (International Company Act 1981-2012, Section 74) Within one calendar month after each anniversary of incorporation (the annual 'return date'); a substituted return date is available with Registrar approval; filing is effected electronically by the licensed registered agent. $310 USD Statutory daily default penalty (NZD 50 per day of continued default under Section 74 of the Act); if the annual return and government fee remain unpaid for more than six months, the Registrar may publish a strike-off notice in the Cook Islands Gazette and remove the company from the International Companies Register. Annual PSP License Renewal and Compliance Return (Financial Services Development Act 2019) Annually on or before the license anniversary (specified in the FSC license instrument; typically 31 December); audited financial statements must accompany the compliance return within six months of the company's financial year end. $3100 USD Non-renewal automatically suspends the payment service license; the FSC may revoke the license after 21 days' written notice. Carrying on regulated payment activities without a valid license is an offence under the Financial Services Development Act 2019 punishable by fines up to NZD 100,000 and possible administrative strike-off of the company.
Annual Beneficial Ownership (UBO) Confirmation / Register Update Filed concurrently with the Annual Return within one month of the incorporation anniversary; share transfers or beneficial ownership changes occurring during the year must be notified to the registered agent within 14 days and the UBO register amended within 21 days of each change. $0 USD Contravention of the beneficial ownership obligations constitutes an offence punishable by fines of up to NZD 20,000 per breach; additionally, the FSC may issue compliance directions, suspend the filing privileges of the registered agent, and ultimately strike the company off the International Companies Register.
