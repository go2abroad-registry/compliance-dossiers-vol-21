Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, an operational unit of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), with primary registry functions performed through the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) portal and the Companies Register.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No 105), as amended, including sections 12 (Application for registration), 13 (Registration of company), 14 (Date of incorporation), 15 (Certificate of incorporation), 16 (Registered office), 17 (Constitution of company), and Schedule 2 (default constitution); Companies Regulations 1994; Financial Reporting Act 2013 for statutory financial reporting obligations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand; official portal: www.ird.govt.nz / myIR secure online services. For the Tax-Exempt International Company (CK_IC) incorporated under the Cook Islands International Companies Act 1981-82, the administrative authorities are the Cook Islands Financial Services Development Authority (FSDA) and the Registrar of International Companies (Rarotonga), which issue the Certificate of Incorporation, annual licence and Certificate of Tax Exemption; the Cook Islands Ministry of Finance and Economic Management (Inland Revenue Division) administers the Value Added Tax Act 1997, from which the exempt International Company is statutorily relieved.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned CK_IC International Company obtains no New Zealand IRD number or GST registration while it preserves Tax-Exempt status by maintaining central management and control outside New Zealand, deriving no New Zealand-source income and conducting no business in New Zealand. Procurement procedure: (1) Engage a licensed Cook Islands international company agent; (2) file the Memorandum and Articles of Association with the Registrar of International Companies and pay the annual government fee; (3) receive the Certificate of Incorporation and apply for a Certificate of Tax Exemption through the FSDA/Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; (4) if the entity subsequently establishes a physical presence in New Zealand, leases premises, employs New Zealand staff, or derives New Zealand-source income, it must apply to the IRD via myIR for an IRD number (used for income tax) and, if taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold, for GST registration on the same TIN; (5) in the Cook Islands, the International Company pays the annual licence fee in lieu of income tax and does not obtain a local TIN or VAT number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident-owned International Company with statutory exemption from Cook Islands income tax, VAT, stamp duty and exchange control, and with no New Zealand resident tax status provided central management and control remains offshore and no New Zealand-source income arises)

Available election forms

Neither New Zealand nor the Cook Islands operates an IRS-style 'check-the-box' regime. Available elections are: (a) voluntary GST registration under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 (NZ) for suppliers below the NZD 60,000 threshold; (b) election to be treated as a New Zealand Look-Through Company (LTC) under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, available only to New Zealand tax-resident companies and therefore incompatible with the CK_IC Tax-Exempt classification; (c) hybrid mismatch self-assessment elections under subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 for NZ-resident corporates that are relevant only if the entity ceases its exempt offshore status and becomes NZ tax resident; (d) the CK_IC may elect to declare income under the Cook Islands International Companies Act merely to evidence its offshore transparent regulatory status, but no hybrid or transparent tax election is available to it.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (annual taxable supply threshold for mandatory New Zealand GST registration under section 51 of the GST Act 1985; a CK_IC is outside this regime as a non-resident supplier unless it makes supplies that connect with New Zealand; the Cook Islands VAT threshold for local supply is equivalent to NZD 60,000 in annual turnover, but International Companies are statutorily exempt regardless of turnover)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the New Zealand GST Act 1985, export zero-rating applies to (i) goods physically exported by the supplier within 28 days of supply (section 11(1)(a)); and (ii) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not supplied directly in connection with land in New Zealand, nor directly in connection with goods in New Zealand (section 11(1)(m)); cross-border trade policy analysis, foreign stakeholder lobbying and government-relations advisory services rendered to foreign government bodies and non-resident clients qualify where no New Zealand nexus exists; if the CK_IC remains a non-resident, non-GST-registered tax-exempt vehicle, it charges no NZ GST and must substantiate its offshore status; a New Zealand-resident supplier must instead retain documentation proving the foreign recipient, offshore performance, and absence of NZ land/goods connection to sustain the zero rate, with the right to claim input credits attributable to exported supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Tradelog export entry or Sea/air cargo manifest export approval)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or Certificate of Shipment evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 / bank ADR confirmation) evidencing receipt of foreign currency consideration
  • Executed consulting/retainer agreement with the non-resident government or foreign stakeholder client
  • Tax invoice issued in accordance with GST Act 1985 section 24 and section 24B (for zero-rated B2B services)
  • Prescribed Memorandum of Understanding or service delivery report evidencing offshore performance of lobbying and policy-analysis activities

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or mandatory electronic invoicing regime exists in New Zealand; e-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Peppol network (IRD e-invoicing framework) with no digital fiscal stamping requirement; GST-registered suppliers may issue paper or electronic invoices provided they satisfy record-keeping rules under section 22 of the Tax Administration Act 1994; a Tax-Exempt CK_IC issues fee agreements and invoices for lobbying/consultancy retainers without IRD fiscal stamping, but must retain an auditable trail of out-of-scope supplies and, if ever GST-registered, of zero-rated exports.

The IRD treats export zero-rating as a high-risk integrity area; false claims attract assessment of the GST shortfall plus use-of-money interest, a 20% shortfall penalty for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion of a tax law (Tax Administration Act 1994, sections 141A-141E); criminal conviction for knowingly providing false export documentation or fraudulent GST refund claims may result in imprisonment of up to five years and fines of up to NZD 50,000 per offence; for a CK_IC International Company, any finding of actual or disguised business carried on in New Zealand, or of central management and control exercised inside New Zealand, retrospectively extinguishes the Tax-Exempt status, re-characterises the entire fee income as New Zealand-sourced taxable income, imposes back-dated provisional tax and GST, triggers late-filing penalties of NZD 250-NZD 1,500 per return, and exposes directors to personal liability under section 149 of the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the New Zealand dollar under IMF Article VIII; there are no exchange control approvals, no prior licensing of FX transactions, and no quantitative ceilings on outward remittances. Profit repatriation is frictionless except for ordinary tax withholding: dividends are subject to Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) at 30% unless reduced under a Double Tax Agreement, interest at 15% or Approved Issuer Levy at 2%, and royalties at 15%. A qualifying Tax-Exempt International Company engaged in government-relations advisory services is not within the 'significant business assets' threshold of the Overseas Investment Act 2005 (typically NZD 200 million or above), so no OIO consent is required for routine treasury operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters and service suppliers may retain proceeds in any permitted currency and convert at market rates without central bank approval. The only statutory obligations are: (i) physical cash or bearer negotiable instruments of NZD 10,000 or more must be disclosed on the New Zealand Border Cash Report when entering or leaving New Zealand; (ii) prescribed transaction reports for cash and electronic transfers exceeding NZD 10,000 under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009; and (iii) standard suspicious transaction reporting. No specific purpose-code approval is mandated for SWIFT credits, but banks require a compliant narrative purpose for record-keeping.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) in conjunction with the Department of Internal Affairs' AML/CFT Group, the Financial Markets Authority, and the Overseas Investment Office for cross-border foreign exchange administration, clearing settlement through the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS), and foreign investment screening.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium. New Zealand does not issue a distinct 'EMI' licence; offshore digital EMIs typically register as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under the AML/CFT Act 2009, supervised by the Department of Internal Affairs, or as Financial Service Providers (FSPs) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008. Wise (formerly TransferWise) holds an FSP registration and Airwallex holds an MSB registration, both legally able to service New Zealand incorporated entities including International Companies. Feasibility is medium for a Government-Relations Advisory entity because enhanced due diligence is triggered by the Medium-Risk Enhanced classification; EMIs may restrict certain corridors, impose narrative purpose-code scrutiny, or reject overseas trust-owned tax-exempt ICs. Tier-1 bank rails remain the most defensible for cross-border clearing and for maintaining banking relationships with foreign government and state-owned counterparties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for inbound capital: (i) pass a corporate resolution and update the share register where injection exceeds NZD 50,000; (ii) remit via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign treasury to the receiving New Zealand bank, using the narrative beneficiary reference 'CAPITAL INJECTION' together with the company identifier, and include the ISO 20022 / purpose code for capital contribution where the bank uses CBPR+; (iii) funds clear into the NZD account through the RBNZ ESAS settlement system; (iv) no exchange control approval is required for the credit, but banks will apply standard AML/CFT identification and record the purpose; (v) if the remitter is a foreign government or state-owned entity, enhanced due diligence applies and the underlying lobbying or advisory mandate must be evidenced by contract; (vi) any physical cash or bearer instrument of NZD 10,000 or more must be disclosed on the Border Cash Report; (vii) confirm OIO consent is not triggered because the entity sits below the significant-business-assets threshold.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for retainer fees, trade settlement, and service disbursements, with NZD local operating payments settled through the New Zealand Bulk Electronic Clearing System (BECS) and high-value domestic payments through the High-Value Clearing System (HVCS) settled on ESAS. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% for fully imputed dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, subject to reduction under an applicable double tax agreement; 15% statutory rate for non-imputed dividends if paid from a wholly-owned subsidiary to an Australian-resident parent company under the Australia/NZ DTA; otherwise the imputation-attached dividend rate is 30% and the non-attributed dividend rate is 30%, reduced to treaty rates.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The IR4 return is due no later than 7 July following the end of the standard 31 March balance date. For a company with a non-standard balance date, the filing deadline is the 7th day of the seventh month after the balance date (i.e. six months and seven days after the balance date). Inland Revenue may grant extended filing deadlines for agents.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand transfer pricing documentation must be prepared for any cross-border related-party transaction in which the aggregate value exceeds NZ$1,000,000 (goods or services) or NZ$4,000,000 (loans, guarantees, or intangible property) per income year, and a Master File or Local File is required for significant cross-border related-party dealings; statutory documentation must be contemporaneous and filed with Inland Revenue within 30 days of request. A higher threshold applies to non-resident multinational groups with consolidated group revenue under NZ$60 million where Inland Revenue may exempt such groups from large documentation obligations if the transactions are of low risk.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code. However, the Income Tax Act 2007 includes a general anti-avoidance rule (sections BG 1 and GB 1), a substantial economic substance test for certain financing and anti-thin capitalisation arrangements (thin-capitalisation rules under section FE 2), and an interest limitation regime for residential land. For a company providing lobbying and government-affairs services, the economic substance analysis turns on real offices, locally engaged staff performing core lobbying functions, actual management and control being exercised in New Zealand or the treaty source jurisdiction, and commercial substance overriding paper arrangements. Inland Revenue can recharacterise contracts, deny non-resident withholding tax treaty benefits, and assess the New Zealand company on income diverted to offshore shell entities under controlled foreign company and transfer-pricing rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: A person who evades tax faces prosecution under sections 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994, with a maximum fine of NZ$50,000 for the first offence and NZ$100,000 for subsequent offences. In addition, the shortfall penalty for evasion is 100% of the tax shortfall, plus interest on unpaid tax at the prescribed rate. Inland Revenue may also issue an assessment based on the best information available and seize assets under distress collection.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers who aided, abetted, or knowingly participated in evasion may be personally liable for penalties and tax shortfalls, and they may receive a term of imprisonment under section 143B for up to five years. The court may order civil restitution and impose travel bans as a condition of bail. Directors jointly and severally liable where company assets are distributed with intent to defeat creditors, under section 292 of the Companies Act 1993.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may apply the general anti-avoidance rule or transfer-pricing rules to disregard the structure, reattribute income to the non-resident shareholding company or to the New Zealand fixed establishment, and impose a 40% or 100% shortfall penalty. The company may also be struck off the New Zealand Companies Register under the Companies Act 1993 for having no director resident in New Zealand (unless an approved substitute), failing to maintain a registered office, or failing to file annual returns. The Registrar may prosecute failure to maintain the register under section 360(2), with a fine up to NZ$10,000.

Director personal liability: Directors of a company carrying on business with insufficient assets may be liable to the Crown for unpaid tax under the 'director of a company acting in a reckless or fraudulent manner' provisions of the Companies Act 1993 (sections 135 and 588FD of the Australian Corporations Act analogies are not directly applicable; NZ sections 135 and 301 allow court orders requiring directors to compensate the company for loss caused by reckless trading). Neglect to file the annual return and no real place of business exposes directors to a pecuniary penalty and a compliance order. Where the entity acts as a disguised conduit for a foreign trust, the foreign trustee and directors may be personally liable for tax on accumulated trust income under the foreign trust disclosure regime.
Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: The Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 imposes civil penalties of up to NZ$2 million for reporting entities that fail to conduct CDD or comply with record-keeping, suspicious transaction reporting, or prescribed transaction reporting. The Financial Markets Authority or the Department of Internal Revenue's supervisory authority may issue formal warnings, public censure, and suspension orders. Knowingly facilitating money laundering can result in criminal penalty of imprisonment for up to 7 years under the Crimes Act 1961; participation in an organised criminal group carries a maximum of 10 years and a fine of up to NZ$5,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers of an AML/CFT reporting entity are personally liable for the entity's breach if they knew or recklessly allowed the breach, or failed to perform corporate obligations. Under section 94 of the AML/CFT Act, individuals may be personally fined up to NZ$300,000 per civil offence, and directors who fail to ensure proper procedures face disqualification from managing a company under the Companies Act. Warehousing criminal proceeds may trigger forfeiture orders against personal assets of directors and beneficial owners under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.
Foreign Trust Disclosure and Controlled Foreign Company Rules

Statutory sanction: Failure to register a foreign trust results in non-compliance notices and default assessments. The trustee is liable for a civil penalty of NZ$2,000 per month for each month of late filing, and the trust's income becomes assessable at the trustee's marginal tax rate (28% for companies) plus penalties of 20% to 100% depending on intent. A New Zealand-resident company with a controlled foreign company (CFC) in a no-tax or low-tax jurisdiction must attribute dividends and passive income; failure to file Form IR461 causes the whole CFC income to be deemed derived. A fine up to NZ$50,000 may be imposed for knowingly failing to furnish the information.

Director personal liability: Where the New Zealand International Company acts as trustee and the foreign trust is subsequently found to be a sham or tax-avoidance arrangement, directors and the trustee entity are jointly and severally liable for the trust's tax liabilities and are subject to civil penalties. Tax liability may attach to non-resident directors who exercise management control from overseas if the 'core of control and management' is deemed located in New Zealand; Inland Revenue may pursue recovery via reciprocal enforcement in DTA jurisdictions and travel bans may apply under extradition arrangements.
