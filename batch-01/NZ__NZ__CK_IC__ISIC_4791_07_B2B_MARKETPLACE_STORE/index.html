Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a United States-style check-the-box regime. The closest statutory elections for an international company are: (1) the election under section YD 2 Income Tax Act 2007 to be treated as a New Zealand resident company by virtue of incorporation in New Zealand, a head office in New Zealand, or centre of management in New Zealand; (2) the GST grouping election pursuant to section 55 Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; (3) the domestic reverse charge and hybrid mismatch elections under the Income Tax (Hybrid and Branch Mismatch) Rules 2018; (4) the branch equivalent tax credit election for Australian resident companies; and (5) where applicable, the look-through company (LTC) election under sections HB 1 to HB 7 Income Tax Act 2007 for a close-held New Zealand resident company. No election is available to convert an exempt international company into a taxable-resident entity without first establishing a physical presence or a New Zealand place of effective management.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period, including remote services supplied to New Zealand-resident consumers and low-value imported goods subject to the marketplace facilitator provisions under section 60C Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; compulsory registration applies once the threshold is exceeded, while voluntary registration is available below the threshold for persons carrying on a taxable activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST applies under section 11 Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 to: (1) supplies of goods physically exported from New Zealand by a GST-registered supplier where the exporter holds the goods at the time of supply and the goods are exported within 28 days of the time of supply, or are delivered to a shipping agent or carrier for export; (2) supplies of services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not supplied to and performed for a person in New Zealand and are not connected with land or goods in New Zealand; (3) supplies of goods and services in the course of an approved international trade zone or recognised export-linked activity. For the B2B marketplace operator, the platform's service fees charged to overseas wholesale merchants qualify for zero-rating where the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not performed in New Zealand; where the platform facilitates sales of goods to New Zealand-based buyers, the marketplace facilitator rules require GST at 15% on the supply value unless the goods are exported, in which case the underlying supply and the facilitator fee can be zero-rated with adequate evidence.

  • New Zealand Customs Service electronic export declaration (EXF form filed via the Trade Single Window or CusMod)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of goods from New Zealand
  • Foreign currency sales invoice issued to the non-resident purchaser
  • Certificate of origin or certificate of shipment where applicable
  • Supplier declaration from the receiving merchant confirming non-resident status and offshore location
  • Inward foreign currency remittance certificate or SWIFT/FX receipt evidencing receipt of export consideration
  • Packing list and shipping instructions retained in the supplier's records
  • GST tax invoice showing the zero-rated supply and the statutory zero-rating endorsement

New Zealand does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital tax stamp regime. There is no mandatory pre-clearance API, no SAF-T periodic submission obligation, and no state-side invoice authorisation requirement. Electronic invoicing is voluntary but the New Zealand Government supports the Pan-Asian Peppol e-invoicing standard, and taxpayers filing via myIR or the IRD Gateway are required to use digital authentication (myIR login or gateway credentials). GST returns must be filed electronically or on paper by the statutory due date, and registered persons must issue a tax invoice within 28 days of supply containing all prescribed GST invoice details.

The Inland Revenue Department and New Zealand Customs Service jointly monitor export zero-rating claims. Where facts objectively show that goods were not exported within 28 days or that services were consumed in New Zealand, IRD will withdraw the zero-rating, reassess GST at 15%, and recover the resulting GST as a clawback under sections 11B and 100 Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Shortfall penalties under sections 141A to 141E Tax Administration Act 1994 apply at escalating rates: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for abusive tax position or evasion, and up to 150% where an arrangement is notified as tax avoidance. Use-of-money interest accrues under Part 7 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 from the original due date. Where fraudulent documentation is presented, criminal liability arises under sections 143A and 144 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, and the NZ Customs Service may prosecute under the Customs and Excise Act 2018; the extended period of assessment is 10 years for fraud or evasion. Directors and platform operators may also incur personal liability under the Companies Act 1993 for allowing the company to trade while GST obligations remain unpaid, and GST registration may be cancelled in cases of a pattern of abusive zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility, consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no exchange controls, no prior-approval gates for inward or outward remittances, and no restrictions on profit, dividend, interest, royalty, trade, or capital repatriation flows under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 or any subordinate FX regulation. The NZD is a freely floating currency; conversion between NZD, USD, CNH, CNY, and other currencies is unrestricted at commercial bank dealing rates. The only practical constraints on cross-border value movement are (i) compliance obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 by the banking and payment chain, and (ii) the residual Overseas Investment Act 2005 consent regime, which triggers only for transactions involving 'sensitive' land, fishing quota, or 'significant business assets' above the statutory threshold (generally NZD 100 million for non-exempt foreign investors) — a threshold that the CK_IC B2B marketplace platform, holding no such NZ assets, does not approach. Profit and capital remittance by the Cook Islands International Company is unrestricted; the only material 'remittance hurdle' is tax leakage, namely New Zealand non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at the 30% statutory rate on any NZ-sourced dividends or interest, reducible by an applicable double-tax treaty (commonly to 15% for portfolio investors and 5% for substantial corporate shareholders). This is a tax matter, not an FX-control matter.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio whatsoever: 0% of export or marketplace receipts must be repatriated, and no deadline applies. The CK_IC, operating as an electronic B2B wholesale platform under ISIC 4791, may lawfully retain USD, CNH, CNY, AUD, or EUR collection balances offshore, or convert and repatriate them at its own treasury discretion. The sole export-related compliance requirements are (i) customs/statistical reporting for physical goods movements under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, and (ii) AML/CFT-driven verification that proceeds credited to the banking ledger match an identifiable commercial transaction evidenced by platform settlement statements, commercial invoices, and SWIFT MT103 references. Best practice is to maintain a full receivables-to-cash reconciliation file so that foreign-currency credits never present as 'unexplained remittances' to the NZ banking partner.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the sole central bank, monetary authority, issuer of NZD, and prudential supervisor of registered banks; it is also the AML/CFT supervisor of New Zealand banks under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. The Financial Markets Authority (FMA) supervises financial advice, derivatives, and non-bank financial service providers, while the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) supervises all other non-bank AML/CFT reporting entities. The Reserve Bank operates no exchange-control licensing regime for trade or capital transactions; its statutory mandate under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 is monetary and financial stability, not the per-case approval of cross-border payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Viability rating: High as an ancillary collection, FX-conversion, and disbursement utility; Low-Medium as the sole primary treasury spine. Legal constraints: New Zealand has no separate 'EMI' banking-licence class; bank-like deposit-taking is the exclusive preserve of banks registered under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989. Offshore and regional digital payment firms — including Airwallex (New Zealand) Limited and Wise Payments New Zealand Limited — may lawfully provide multi-currency store-value settlement, safeguarded (non-lending) wallets, and SWIFT MT103 disbursements provided they are registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and are compliant AML/CFT reporting entities supervised by the DIA or FMA. For the CK_IC, the decisive constraint is that such fintechs will apply the same beneficial-owner CDD vault as a bank, requiring full attribution of natural persons through the Cook Islands International Company chain and the underlying trust before onboarding. Digital EMIs are fully capable of supporting Alibaba Pay / Alipay+ merchant settlement flows, but they cannot host the capital escrow account, issue commercial letters of credit, or extend trade credit without a licensed-bank partner. Practical blueprint: use Airwallex or Wise as a rapid-e-KYC collection and conversion rail for small-ticket B2B receivables and Alibaba Pay settlement, with daily/weekly treasury sweeps into the tier-1 NZ master account; reserve all capital injections, LC disbursements, and hedging relationships for the registered bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for the CK_IC capital escrow: (1) pre-notify the injection in the customer's Expected Activity Profile by filing the board resolution, share subscription schedule, source-of-funds bank statements, and expected value date; (2) route the capital as a single SWIFT MT103 from the beneficial owner's or trust's private-banking account to the CK_IC's NZD Capital Escrow Account at the lead tier-1 bank; SWIFT field 70 must read 'EQUITY INJECTION — SHARE SUBSCRIPTION — CK_IC' with purpose category 'Capital Contribution / Unrequited Transfer' — never use 'Payment for Goods' or an ambiguous 'Loan' narrative unless a written shareholder loan facility exists; (3) because New Zealand imposes no exchange-control approval, no Reserve Bank or FMA pre-authorisation is required, but best practice is a bank-side 'new money' alert submission and a gpi-tracked SWIFT UETR reference; (4) post-receipt verification includes the MT910 credit advice, the gpi tracker report, and contemporaneous updating of the IC's share register; (5) the incoming capital may remain NZD or be FX-swapped into USD or CNH without restriction; (6) if the capital is intended for onward trade finance or lending, it should be documented separately as a shareholder loan facility with a written term sheet and loan accounting entries, so that distributions remain characterised correctly for withholding-tax and solvency purposes.Two-track clearing architecture plus a platform settlement loop: Track A — SWIFT Wire: MT103 for all regular CNY, USD, and NZD supplier settlements, with purpose codes such as 'Commercial Payment — Payment for Goods', supported by the commercial invoice and Incoterms 2020 evidence; for Chinese onshore beneficiaries, routing via a CIPS-enabled correspondent bank under SWIFT MT103 with MT202 COV cover where required. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability rating: High as an ancillary collection, FX-conversion, and disbursement utility; Low-Medium as the sole primary treasury spine. Legal constraints: New Zealand has no separate 'EMI' banking-licence class; bank-like deposit-taking is the exclusive preserve of banks registered under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989. Offshore and regional digital payment firms — including Airwallex (New Zealand) Limited and Wise Payments New Zealand Limited — may lawfully provide multi-currency store-value settlement, safeguarded (non-lending) wallets, and SWIFT MT103 disbursements provided they are registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and are compliant AML/CFT reporting entities supervised by the DIA or FMA. For the CK_IC, the decisive constraint is that such fintechs will apply the same beneficial-owner CDD vault as a bank, requiring full attribution of natural persons through the Cook Islands International Company chain and the underlying trust before onboarding. Digital EMIs are fully capable of supporting Alibaba Pay / Alipay+ merchant settlement flows, but they cannot host the capital escrow account, issue commercial letters of credit, or extend trade credit without a licensed-bank partner. Practical blueprint: use Airwallex or Wise as a rapid-e-KYC collection and conversion rail for small-ticket B2B receivables and Alibaba Pay settlement, with daily/weekly treasury sweeps into the tier-1 NZ master account; reserve all capital injections, LC disbursements, and hedging relationships for the registered bank.

Turnover: Under the Financial Reporting Act 2013 and Companies Act 1993, a company must have its financial statements audited if it is not a small or medium company. A company qualifies as small or medium if it is not an issuer, has fewer than 50 employees, and either total assets are NZ$5 million or less or total revenue is NZ$10 million or less. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years and/or a fine up to NZD 50,000 (individual) or NZD 250,000 (company); in addition, a tax shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the evaded tax under Section 141D of the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted as principals; travel bans and asset freezing orders under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009; civil restitution for unpaid tax and interest.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule; full 28% corporate income tax on New Zealand-derived income; shortfall penalties of 20% to 150%; use-of-money interest; director disqualification under the Companies Act 1993.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for knowingly signing false returns; reckless trading liability under Section 135 of the Companies Act 1993; court-ordered travel bans and prohibition on leaving New Zealand may be imposed for serious tax debts.
Transfer Pricing Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's length price under Subpart GC of the Income Tax Act 2007; 20% penalty for an unacceptable tax position; 150% penalty if the dominant purpose is tax avoidance; use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved non-arm's-length pricing may be held personally liable for the company's tax shortfall penalties under Section 34A of the Tax Administration Act 1994 if the company fails to pay.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009: corporate penalty up to NZD 2,000,000; individual imprisonment up to 2 years for non-compliance; conviction for money laundering under the Crimes Act 1961 may result in imprisonment up to 7 years and criminal forfeiture of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil penalties and criminal prosecution if they knowingly facilitate money laundering; travel restrictions and asset forfeiture may be court-ordered; personal liability can extend to amounts laundered through the company.
