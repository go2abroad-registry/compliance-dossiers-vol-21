Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), particularly sections 12 (incorporation by registration), 13 (constitution), 14 (certificate of incorporation), 15 (separate legal personality), 16 (application for incorporation), 17 (issue of incorporation certificate), 22 (company name restrictions), 34 (shares without par value), 39 (no authorized capital), 60 (share subscription consideration), 126-127 (registered office), 122 (board resolutions), 151 (director residency requirement), 153 (appointment and number of directors); ancillary: Tax Administration Act 1994 and Income Tax Act 2007 for tax registration; Customs and Excise Act 2018 for voluntary AEO accreditation; Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 for workplace compliance.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form 1 – Application for Incorporation of a Company, filed electronically through the online Companies Register portal (companiesoffice.govt.nz).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Nil statutory minimum paid-up capital. New Zealand law has abolished the concept of authorized capital; the Companies Act 1993 requires no escrow account and no capital deposit prior to incorporation. Shares of no par value may be issued for such consideration as the directors bona fide determine under section 60. One hundred percent non-resident ownership is permissible without exchange-control approval or Overseas Investment Act 2005 consent, provided the company does not acquire sensitive land, fishing quota, or strategically significant assets.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certification and Building Warrant of Fitness issued by the territorial authority under the Building Act 2004, confirming structural load capacity and means of escape; emergency evacuation and civil-defence response plan under the Civil Defence Emergency Management Act 2002; and occupational workplace clearance under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including a PCBU risk assessment, first-aid provision, and incident reporting procedures.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand — official portal myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz); for Authorized Economic Operator (AEO) certification and customs security compliance, the competent authority is the New Zealand Customs Service (Te Mana Ārai o Aotearoa) under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, with AEO mutual recognition agreements administered by the customs agency.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (CK_IC) must obtain an Inland Revenue (IRD) number. Procedure: (1) file form IR742 (Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application) containing legal name, incorporation details from the New Zealand Companies Office, address of registered office, and particulars of directors; (2) annex certified true copies of the Certificate of Incorporation, Constitution, and a certificate of good standing from the home jurisdiction; (3) where New Zealand GST registration is required, complete form GST1A or lodge through myIR with evidence of New Zealand taxable activity; non-resident applicants must disclose a New Zealand agent or authorized tax representative and may be required to provide a security deposit pursuant to section 62B of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the IRD deems collection at risk; (4) for the CK_IC's offshore AEO supply-chain audit services, the non-resident company status is confirmed by declaring the absence of a New Zealand permanent establishment; the IRD number is issued within 5-10 working days; (5) if no taxable activity in New Zealand is conducted, a TIN may still be issued for withholding tax and compliance purposes; registration of the company shareholding structure under the Companies Act 1993 is a prerequisite.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The CK_IC (International Company) is treated as an offshore non-resident company under New Zealand's territorial basis of taxation; it is assessable only on New Zealand-sourced income (section YD4, Income Tax Act 2007). Foreign-sourced income derived from the offshore AEO supply-chain audit services is exempt from New Zealand income tax in the absence of a New Zealand permanent establishment. This classification is consistent with a Tax_Exempt_Zone treatment because the entity's non-New Zealand revenue stream is placed entirely outside the New Zealand tax net, and no domestic CFC attribution arises where the CK_IC holds an offshore subsidiary shareholding in a grey-list jurisdiction under sections EX 20 and EX 21.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box system; however, the following statutory elections and elective regimes are available to the CK_IC: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, permitting qualifying shareholders to treat the company as fiscally transparent; (2) GST group registration election under sections 55-55B of the GST Act 1985, allowing inter-group taxable supplies to be disregarded and consolidated returns; (3) GST branch election under section 58 of the GST Act 1985 for a foreign company's New Zealand branch; (4) Consolidated group company election under Part IS of the Income Tax Act 2007 where a 100% New Zealand-owned corporate group exists; (5) Non-resident company election to voluntarily register for GST under section 51(3) to claim input tax credits for costs incurred in making zero-rated export supplies; (6) hybrid mismatch instruments are addressed by default ordering rules in sections FH 1-FH 15, not by elective check-the-box; the CK_IC may not elect to be disregarded as a separate entity, but it can structure its offshore income with explicit documentation of non-NZ source to preserve the zero-tax outcome.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 — registration is compulsory when the value of taxable supplies exceeds NZD 60,000 in any 12-month rolling period (section 51(1), GST Act 1985); for non-resident companies, voluntary registration is permitted at any level of taxable activity, but where the registered person is a non-resident, a security may be required and a New Zealand agent must be appointed (section 62B).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods may be zero-rated under section 11(1)(c) GST Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods to a place outside New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply, and the goods are not subsequently used in New Zealand. Offshore services, including AEO certification and security audit consultancy, qualify under section 11A(1)(a) where the service is supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, and section 11A(1)(c) for services supplied in connection with movable property outside New Zealand; additionally, services physically performed outside New Zealand are zero-rated. The 85% test under section 11A(3) applies where services are supplied in connection with goods imported into New Zealand, and these are excluded from zero-rating. The AEO audit supply-chain compliance services must be performed for the non-resident client's benefit outside New Zealand and must not be reasonably believed by the CK_IC to be supplied to a person in New Zealand; written evidence from the offshore client confirming offshore presence at the time of performance is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration — New Zealand Customs Service Electronic Export Declaration (JE600 series or Integrated Cargo System exit confirmation message) evidencing physical export of goods, where the AEO supply chain transaction includes physical movement
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill — original negotiable transport document evidencing shipment to the foreign destination and consignee outside New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) — bank-issued certificate or SWIFT MT103 message confirming receipt of foreign currency consideration from the non-resident client, referencing the corresponding GST zero-rated invoice
  • Commercial Invoice and zero-rated supply contract — detailed service agreement describing the AEO audit scope, offshore delivery location, client non-resident status, and applicable ISIC 7020 activity
  • Certificate of Non-Residency of Client — overseas company registry extract, certificate of incorporation, or New Zealand IRD letter confirming the client's non-resident status
  • Written confirmation from the non-resident client that they were outside New Zealand at the time the audit services were performed — signed and dated service acceptance statement
  • New Zealand Customs AEO certificate reference (where applicable) — evidence of the CK_IC's AEO status or its role as an AEO service provider

No real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no government pre-approval of invoices apply in New Zealand. Since 2020, the Peppol network has been adopted as the national e-invoicing standard; from 1 July 2022, all New Zealand government departments and agencies must receive e-invoices via Peppol (Aotearoa e-Invoicing framework). Inland Revenue does not operate a mandatory transaction-level reporting clearance system; however, the self-assessment regime imposes strict GST invoice content requirements under section 24 of the GST Act 1985 and Regulations 8-11 of the GST Regulations 2009: invoice must state supplier name, supplier GST number (IRD/GST registration number), recipient name and address (where known), invoice date, description of supplies, and for zero-rated supplies the notation 'zero-rated' must appear. API-based machine-readable Peppol BIS 3.0 formats (UBL/XML) are recommended for compliance with the AEO supply-chain audit file submission requirements to New Zealand Customs.

Inland Revenue applies stringent verification of zero-rating claims; failure to produce the mandatory documentation bundle results in recharacterization of the supply as taxable at 15% GST, with assessment of additional tax, use-of-money interest from the original due date, and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. Criminal liability may arise under section 143A TAA (fines up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years) and under the Crimes Act 1961 for fraudulent GST returns. Where the 180-day FX repatriation benchmark is exceeded, Inland Revenue may presume non-arm's-length or artificial structuring and invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (BG 1) to deny zero-rating retrospectively; the CK_IC must document commercial justifications for extended payment terms. Under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, false AEO declarations and certifications attract penalties of up to NZD 10,000 for each false statement, suspension or revocation of AEO status, and loss of mutual recognition benefits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand imposes no exchange controls and maintains full NZD convertibility on both current and capital account, consistent with its obligations under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Inbound capital injection, shareholder loans, outbound profit repatriation, dividend remittance, and trade settlements are freely permitted without central-bank approval, licensing, or quantitative limits. There are no 'profit remittance hurdles' in the FX sense; the only frictions are (i) statutory non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends and interest (up to 15% under domestic law, subject to applicable double-tax agreements), (ii) bank-level AML/CFT due diligence and enhanced documentation for non-resident corporate clients, and (iii) OIO screening only if the transaction acquires a 'significant business asset' (NZ$100M or more for non-wholly-owned overseas investors) or sensitive land — thresholds that are not triggered by a low-value AEO supply-chain audit services company under ISIC 7020. Services export receipts are current-account items and are wholly unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand has no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export proceeds of either goods or services. Exporters may lawfully retain receipts in NZD or any foreign currency, in any onshore or offshore account. For the ISIC 7020 AEO supply-chain audit model — which economically constitutes an export of services — the relevant residual obligations are: (i) Customs and Excise Act 2018 documentation only where physical goods cross the border (not applicable to pure audit services), (ii) Income Tax Act 2007 rules requiring translation of foreign-currency income into NZD for assessable-profit calculation under NZ IFRS, and (iii) Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 record-keeping (five years) and suspicious-transaction reporting for receipt patterns exceeding NZ$10,000 in cash or presenting typology indicators.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the central bank of New Zealand, responsible for monetary policy, sole currency issuance (New Zealand dollar, NZD), prudential supervision of registered banks and non-bank deposit takers, and operation of the national high-value payment systems (ESAS and NZClear). Foreign-exchange and AML/CFT administration for banks is co-supervised across three bodies: the Reserve Bank (registered banks), the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) (licensed financial advice and market services), and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) (non-bank remitters and EMIs). The Overseas Investment Office (OIO) exercises a separate foreign-investment screening mandate under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 for sensitive land and significant business assets, but this does not amount to a general exchange-control function.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound equity or shareholder funding into the New Zealand banking perimeter: (i) remitter executes a SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pacs.008) priority credit transfer to the IC's Tier-1 NZD account; (ii) the payment message must contain a concise purpose narrative such as 'Equity Contribution — AEO Supply Chain Audit Business' or 'Intercompany Shareholder Loan — Working Capital' to support bank screening; (iii) New Zealand does not statutorily prescribe purpose codes, but the receiving bank will classify the flow for RBNZ Balance of Payments statistical reporting (e.g., Direct Investment Equity Capital or Other Business Services); (iv) for amounts above NZ$50,000 the bank will invoke enhanced due diligence requiring the executed loan agreement or board resolution, source-of-funds evidence (bank statements, audited financial statements), and confirmation of CRS/FATCA classification; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium for low-value operational inbound/outbound flows (sub-NZ$100,000) and Low for capital escrow, structural liquidity, or any Customs-security guarantee. Legal Constraints: Wise (Wise New Zealand Limited) and Airwallex (Airwallex New Zealand) are registered Financial Service Providers (FSPs) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and are licensed AML/CFT reporting entities supervised by the Department of Internal Affairs; however, they are not registered banks, may not accept deposits, and client funds are safeguarded client money (e-money) rather than deposit-protected funds. The Deposit Takers Act 2023 further restricts the use of the 'bank' descriptor and the taking of callable funds to licensed deposit takers. Consequently, the International Company may use EMI rails for invoice collection and subcontractor settlement, but Tier-1 bank accounts remain mandatory for equity escrow, intercompany treasury concentration, and AEO-related security instruments.
