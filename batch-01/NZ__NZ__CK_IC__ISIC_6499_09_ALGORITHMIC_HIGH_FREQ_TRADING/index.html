Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, a business unit of the Ministry of Business, Innovation, and Employment (MBIE), operating the Companies Register at https://companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz/.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), particularly sections 10 (Incorporation), 15 (Application for Registration), 18 (Certificate of Incorporation), 19 (Company Constitution optional), 21-24 (Registered Office), 42-50 (Share Allotment and Capital), 122-126 (Board of Directors), 128-130 (Board Resolutions), and 151-152 (Directors' Duties); supplemented by the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) guidance for financial services providers and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 for any licensed financial activity.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NZ1 - 'Application for Incorporation of a Company' (electronic submission via the New Zealand Companies Office online registration portal), together with a Company Constitution (if adopted) and written consents to act as directors and shareholders.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is required under the Companies Act 1993; a company may be incorporated with zero share capital and shares may be issued for consideration determined by the board. No escrow account or capital deposit is mandated for a general International Company. However, if the company seeks licensing as a financial service provider or market maker under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, additional fit-and-proper capital requirements may apply at the discretion of the FMA.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certification under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) Act 2017 – an approved Evacuation Scheme and appropriate means of egress; a Building Warrant of Fitness (BWOF) issued by an Independent Qualified Person (IQP) under the Building Act 2004 for buildings containing a specified system (e.g., automated fire suppression, backup generator, computer room air conditioning); structural load compliance review for raised access floors and server rack concentrations; electrical certification and annual verification under the Electricity Act 1992; and a workplace health and safety assessment under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 demonstrating identification of hazards (thermal, electrical, psychological and ergonomic risks), a documented risk register, and welfare facilities. WorkSafe New Zealand clearance may be required if the facility undertakes energised electrical work or other prescribed high-risk activity.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is required under the Companies Act 1993; a company may be incorporated with zero share capital and shares may be issued for consideration determined by the board. No escrow account or capital deposit is mandated for a general International Company. However, if the company seeks licensing as a financial service provider or market maker under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, additional fit-and-proper capital requirements may apply at the discretion of the FMA.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (Te Tari Taake), New Zealand Government — online services portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz). Goods export control and customs documentation are administered by the New Zealand Customs Service (Te Mana Ārai o Aotearoa) via the Trade Single Window (TSW).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Cook Islands International Company must apply for an Inland Revenue (IRD) number before it derives New Zealand-source income, registers for GST, or becomes an employer. Procedure: (1) Prepare form IR742 — 'Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application' — attaching certified copies of the Cook Islands certificate of incorporation, constitution, current certificate of good standing, directors'/shareholders' identification, and proof of the New Zealand agent registration (if any). (2) Submit the application electronically through the 'Apply for an IRD number for an overseas company' service in myIR, or by post to Inland Revenue, PO Box 39010, Wellington 5045, New Zealand. (3) Await issuance of the 8- or 9-digit IRD number (typically within 8–10 working days). (4) Once the IRD number is issued, register for GST via myIR if the 12-month rolling value of taxable supplies made in New Zealand will exceed NZD 60,000, or voluntarily if below the threshold. (5) Appoint a New Zealand tax agent or authorised representative if no physical place of business is maintained, and file all returns (income tax, GST, NRWT, and employer obligations) under the non-resident tax profile.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — the CK_IC is treated in New Zealand as a non-resident foreign company subject to entity-level income tax only on New Zealand-sourced income (Income Tax Act 2007, ss BD 1, YD 4). It is not a pass-through vehicle, and its Cook Islands tax-exempt status (Tax_Exempt_Zone) does not confer any exemption in New Zealand.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in New Zealand. Available statutory elections include: (a) GST accounting basis election (invoice, payments, or hybrid) and GST filing frequency election (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly) under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; (b) GST group election (s 55) for companies with common ownership to file consolidated GST returns; (c) approved issuer levy election (2% levy in substitution for non-resident withholding tax on related-party interest); and (d) balance date election (s 38 Income Tax Act 2007) with IRD approval. Hybrid and branch mismatch countermeasures (Part FM and Subpart FE Income Tax Act 2007) apply automatically and are not subject to elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in New Zealand in any rolling 12-month period (s 51 GSTA 1985). This threshold applies equally to non-resident suppliers of remote/digital services and distance sellers of low-value goods (s 54B). Voluntary registration is available below the threshold, subject to the usual conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available where: (1) goods are exported by the supplier from New Zealand within 28 days of supply (s 11(1)(a) GSTA 1985); (2) services are supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not directly in connection with land or goods situated in New Zealand and the recipient is not in New Zealand when the services are performed (s 11(1)(m)); and (3) remote services are supplied to a non-resident business customer outside New Zealand under s 11(1)(mb) and schedule 3A. Caution: the CK_IC's proprietary ultra-low-latency algorithmic market-making, clearing, and exchange-related services are financial services (s 3 GSTA 1985) and are generally exempt (not zero-rated) under s 14 GSTA 1985; an exemption without input credit arises unless the services fall within an export zero-rating limb or the de minimis rules apply. Refund substantiation requires clearly attributable export documentation and, for exempt financial services, the denial of input credit must be considered.

  • New Zealand Customs Export Declaration (EX) lodged through the Trade Single Window, with the Customs clearance receipt
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill / sea waybill) evidencing physical export from New Zealand within the statutory 28-day window
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward foreign currency remittance evidence (bank SWIFT MT103 confirmation or broker settlement statement) evidencing payment for the exported goods or services
  • Executed service agreement, trade tickets, clearing/settlement records, and time-stamped order audit trail for zero-rated exported algorithmic trading services

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime is in force. E-invoicing is implemented on the PEPPOL network: businesses with New Zealand annual turnover above NZD 250 million (from 1 April 2022), NZD 50 million (from 1 April 2024), and NZD 10 million (from 1 April 2026) must be able to receive PEPPOL e-invoices. Inland Revenue provides a free PEPPOL e-invoicing service; the issuer chooses the PEPPOL access point. Tax invoices may be issued in paper or electronic form, and e-invoicing does not require prior authorisation or clearance by Inland Revenue.

New Zealand does not prescribe a statutory FX repatriation deadline for GST zero-rating; the 180-day benchmark is used as an evidentiary expectation in IRD audits, with longer deferral requiring commercial justification. If the stated export conditions are not satisfied, the supply is taxable at 15% GST, and the supplier must account for the tax shortfall plus use-of-money interest. Penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% shortfall penalty for lack of reasonable care; 40% for taking an unacceptable tax position; up to 100% for evasion or fraudulent intent; voluntary disclosure may reduce penalties. The time bar for reassessment is 4 years, and it does not apply where returns are fraudulent or grossly carelessly filed. GST anti-avoidance provisions (s 76 GSTA 1985) allow recharacterisation of arrangements with the principal purpose of obtaining a tax advantage, and the general anti-avoidance rule (s GA 1 Income Tax Act 2007) applies. False export declarations to the New Zealand Customs Service attract separate civil penalties and criminal fines under the Customs and Excise Act 2018; IRD and NZ Customs jointly audit export inconsistencies.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar is fully and freely convertible on both the current account and the capital account. There are no New Zealand exchange control statutes imposing general convertibility restrictions for trade-related remittances, dividend distributions, loan repayments, or capital injections. Profit remittance from New Zealand to non-resident shareholders is not subject to FX licensing or central bank approval; it is subject solely to standard AML/CFT documentation and, where applicable, New Zealand non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act 2007. For a Cook Islands International Company, foreign currency accounts can be maintained in New Zealand and Cook Islands licensed banks without prior RBNZ exchange control approval because no such surrender or conversion regime exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation in New Zealand and no mandatory conversion ratio into NZD. Exporters, including a proprietary algorithmic trading entity earning offshore transaction revenues, may repatriate or retain export proceeds in the currency of settlement. New Zealand banks are required to report cross-border transactions and balance-of-payments statistical data to the RBNZ under the International Transactions Reporting regime, and all international wire transfers are subject to AML/CFT information requirements under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. The Cook Islands International Company has no separate surrender obligation; the NZD is the domestic currency and the entity is not subject to any exchange control surrender timeline.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand - Te Pūtea Matua (RBNZ), acting as the monetary authority for the New Zealand dollar and operator of the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS); the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission is the domestic non-bank financial regulator for the International Company vehicle, with the Ministry of Finance and Economic Management overseeing any Cook Islands dollar note issuance at a fixed 1:1 parity to the NZD.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. New Zealand does not operate a standalone EMI licence; offshore fintech/digital EMIs are permissible only as supplementary payment service providers, not as primary clearing banks. To be commercially viable, the offshore EMI must be registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR), appoint a New Zealand-resident AML/CFT compliance officer, belong to an approved dispute resolution scheme, and route NZD settlements through a licensed New Zealand bank's clearing account. An unregistered offshore digital EMI cannot access ESAS and cannot provide a legally recognised New Zealand bank account for the International Company.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must be routed as a SWIFT MT103 credit to the International Company's designated New Zealand bank account, with the beneficiary account presented in the New Zealand account number format (two-digit bank code, four-digit branch, seven-digit base account, and suffix). The remittance field must state the true purpose, for example 'equity capital contribution to International Company for proprietary trading operations', together with the receiving bank's prescribed inward remittance purpose or foreign investment code for direct investment into New Zealand. No prior RBNZ exchange control approval is required because New Zealand has no exchange control consent regime; however, the bank will perform AML/CFT verification and, for deposits and cross-border transactions above reporting thresholds, will automatically submit international transaction reporting to the New Zealand Financial Intelligence Unit. If the capital injection involves the acquisition of New Zealand sensitive land or significant business assets, the Overseas Investment Act 2005 consent requirements must be separately assessed, but ordinary Treasury and trading capital is exempt.Outbound trade disbursements and market-making settlements should be executed via regulated clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for direct commercial wire payment, MT202 COV for correspondent bank cover payments, and commercial letters of credit issued under ICC UCP600 for goods-backed trade flows where relevant. NZD settlement must be effected through the RBNZ Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) via the settlement bank. US dollar legs of the proprietary trading activity will be cleared through Fedwire or CHIPS through the bank's correspondent network. For foreign-exchange market making requiring payment-versus-payment (PvP) risk mitigation, CLS settlement should be used for eligible currencies. For securities related to algorithmic market making, delivery-versus-payment (DvP) settlement through the applicable central securities depository is the mandatory rail. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Medium. New Zealand does not operate a standalone EMI licence; offshore fintech/digital EMIs are permissible only as supplementary payment service providers, not as primary clearing banks. To be commercially viable, the offshore EMI must be registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR), appoint a New Zealand-resident AML/CFT compliance officer, belong to an approved dispute resolution scheme, and route NZD settlements through a licensed New Zealand bank's clearing account. An unregistered offshore digital EMI cannot access ESAS and cannot provide a legally recognised New Zealand bank account for the International Company.
