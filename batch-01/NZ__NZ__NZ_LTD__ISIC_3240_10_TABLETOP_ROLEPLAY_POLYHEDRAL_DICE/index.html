Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand company law imposes no minimum issued, subscribed, or paid-up share capital. Shares may be issued for any consideration determined by the directors in accordance with the Companies Act 1993. There is no requirement for a blocked escrow account. If a company elects to issue shares before lodgment, the subscribers must execute consent and the issued shares must be fully or partly paid as stated in the Share Register. The Companies Office does not mandate any escrowed capital arrangement for non-resident owners.

Funding & unlock path: WorkSafe New Zealand health and safety clearance required under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including documented plant and chemical risk assessments. Civil Defence fire permits and structural safety certification are administered by Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) and the local Building Consent Authority. The production premises shall require a Building Warrant of Fitness and evacuation scheme if the industrial space has public access or an occupancy load exceeding 100 persons. Fire protection measures include an automatic fire suppression system or portable extinguishers rated for Class B fires, proper separation of resin and hardener storage from ignition sources, an emergency shower and eyewash station in areas where epoxy amines are in use, and compliance with the New Zealand Electrical Code of Practice for wiring installations. An annual fire inspection certificate must be retained.

Paid-in capital rule: None. New Zealand company law imposes no minimum issued, subscribed, or paid-up share capital. Shares may be issued for any consideration determined by the directors in accordance with the Companies Act 1993. There is no requirement for a blocked escrow account. If a company elects to issue shares before lodgment, the subscribers must execute consent and the issued shares must be fully or partly paid as stated in the Share Register. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake, IRD); official online portal and digital services: https://www.ird.govt.nz (MyIR secure login for taxpayers and agents).

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand-incorporated limited company (NZ_LTD), the Companies Office automatically registers the company for income tax with Inland Revenue and issues a unique IRD number (tax file number / TIN) at incorporation. For a foreign entity not incorporated in New Zealand, the procedure is: (i) assess New Zealand-source income or taxable activity presence; (ii) file Form IR596 (Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application) with Inland Revenue, or apply through MyIR if eligible; (iii) provide certified constitutional documents, directors'/officers' identification, and evidence of the appointed New Zealand agent for service of notices; (iv) obtain the IRD number; (v) separately register for GST under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 using Form IR356 (GST Registration) or via MyIR, mandatory if taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period; (vi) optionally obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) for inter-agency / e-invoicing purposes. No minimum capital is required for a New Zealand limited company.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A New Zealand limited company is a separate tax-resident entity (if incorporated in New Zealand) subject to income tax at the corporate rate of 28% on worldwide income. Non-resident companies are taxed only on New Zealand-source income. The company is not a pass-through entity; shareholders are taxed separately on dividends under the full imputation credit system. Territorial scope: New Zealand taxes resident companies on worldwide income with specific statutory exemptions (e.g., active foreign dividends, CFC/FIG grey-list rules). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A New Zealand limited company is a separate tax-resident entity (if incorporated in New Zealand) subject to income tax at the corporate rate of 28% on worldwide income. Non-resident companies are taxed only on New Zealand-source income. The company is not a pass-through entity; shareholders are taxed separately on dividends under the full imputation credit system. Territorial scope: New Zealand taxes resident companies on worldwide income with specific statutory exemptions (e.g., active foreign dividends, CFC/FIG grey-list rules). No tax-exempt zone status is available in New Zealand.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification regime. Available statutory elections and administrative mechanisms include: (i) voluntary GST registration below the NZD 60,000 threshold; (ii) GST group registration election under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 allowing wholly-owned companies to be treated as a single taxable person; (iii) imputation credit account election enabling a New Zealand company to maintain an imputation account; (iv) group loss offset election under subpart IC of the Income Tax Act 2007; (v) several disclosure/override rules for hybrid mismatch arrangements under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 – these are not elective but require taxpayers to self-assess and report cross-border mismatch outcomes; (vi) election to apply the 'exempt foreign branch' rules? Not available for companies. There is no bifurcated entity election comparable to the US LLC pass-through.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 – GST registration is mandatory when the total value of taxable supplies in New Zealand exceeds NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period or is expected to exceed that threshold in the next 12 months. Non-resident suppliers of remote services or digital products to New Zealand residents must also register once their NZ taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 per annum. Voluntary registration is permitted for persons making taxable supplies below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

New Zealand applies GST at 0% (zero-rating) to: (a) goods exported from New Zealand where the goods leave New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply (or within 12 months for goods supplied under a conditional sale or sale on approval); (b) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not supplied in connection with a taxable activity carried on in New Zealand (or with land in New Zealand); (c) supplies of certain intellectual property, international transport, or raw materials for manufacturing exported goods, where statutory conditions are met. For the RPG dice manufacturing business, export sales of physical polyhedral dice are zero-rated upon presentation of sufficient evidence of export. Offshore services (e.g., licensing of tabletop game designs or custom casting services) may be zero-rated if the recipient is a non-resident and the services are not consumed in New Zealand. No foreign-exchange repatriation deadline is imposed by New Zealand law; export proceeds may be held offshore. The only statutory temporal requirement is the 28-day (or 12-month) exportation window.

  • Electronic Export Declaration filed with the New Zealand Customs Service (via the Trade Single Window/CusDec electronic system) evidencing the physical export of the goods
  • Bill of Lading (sea cargo) or Air Waybill (air cargo) issued by the carrier to the New Zealand exporter, showing the consignee outside New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice / GST tax invoice issued to the overseas buyer describing the goods, quantity, and export value
  • Packing list describing the packing details, marks, and container/shipment particulars
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds where the underlying sales contract requires payment prior to or on export
  • Any New Zealand Certificate of Origin or product conformity documentation if required by the importing jurisdiction

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), digital stamping, or government API clearance is imposed on invoices. New Zealand has adopted the voluntary Peppol-based e-Invoicing network (NZ e-Invoicing). GST-registered suppliers may raise electronic tax invoices using Peppol-enabled accounting software. All tax invoices must meet the content requirements of sections 24-24D of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. GST returns (Form IR305) are filed electronically via MyIR on a one-month, two-month, or six-month cycle depending on taxable supplies. Business records must be retained for at least seven years for IRD audit purposes.

Inland Revenue may reassess GST if the zero-rating conditions are not met (e.g., goods are not exported within 28 days, or services are not genuinely performed to a non-resident outside New Zealand), resulting in a retrospective clawback of the 15% GST, plus use-of-money interest from the original due date. Penalties for fraudulent or deliberately misleading export zero-rating claims are imposed under the Tax Administration Act 1994 (sections 141 and 143A), including an initial shortfall penalty of 20% for 'not taking reasonable care', 40% for 'unacceptable tax position', 100% for 'gross carelessness', or 150% for 'fraud or evasion', and criminal fines of up to NZD 50,000 per offence (first offence) or NZD 100,000 (subsequent offence) with imprisonment up to five years for evasion. Additionally, false or misleading export declarations to the New Zealand Customs Service may trigger penalties under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, separate from IRD penalties. Documentary evidence must be retained for seven years; failure to produce proof of export on audit will generally lead to reversal of the zero rating, interest, and penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar is fully freely convertible on both current and capital accounts. There are no exchange-control approvals, surrender requirements, or mandatory conversion rules applicable to inbound or outbound transactions. Profit remittance is unrestricted from a currency perspective; dividends are repatriable subject only to standard tax mechanics, namely the imputation credit system and non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act 2007 for dividends not fully imputed (treaty rates typically reduce NRWT). Inbound equity and shareholder debt are permitted without exchange-control consent, although the Overseas Investment Act 2005 may be triggered for investments involving sensitive land or significant business assets, which is not relevant to a small goods-exporting manufacturing entity. Thin-capitalisation rules under subpart FE of the Income Tax Act 2007 may cap deductible interest where the company is more than 50% non-resident-controlled.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline applies to export receipts in New Zealand. An exporter may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency or New Zealand dollar accounts at any registered bank in New Zealand, hold funds offshore, apply them directly to imports or debt service, or convert to NZD at commercial rates at any time without notifying the Reserve Bank of New Zealand.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), established under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989; it is the sole central bank, the issuer of New Zealand dollars, and the prudential supervisor of all registered banks under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. AML/CFT supervision is shared among the Reserve Bank, the Financial Markets Authority, and the Department of Internal Affairs under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. New Zealand maintains no standalone foreign-exchange control administration; exchange controls were abolished in 1984.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. An offshore digital EMI may lawfully provide cross-border payment and collection services into New Zealand only if it is registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and, where it conducts money remittance or holds customer funds, registered as an AML/CFT reporting entity with the Department of Internal Affairs. The statutory term 'bank' is protected under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989, but digital EMI platforms (for example Wise, Airwallex, or OFX) can operate as licensed money remitters in New Zealand without a full banking licence. Such EMIs are viable for collections and trade payments, while a Tier-1 New Zealand bank account remains the anchor for capital escrow, settlement conversion, and credit facilities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity subscription is routed by the non-resident shareholder via SWIFT MT103 from its remitting bank to the company's New Zealand bank account, with field 70 remittance information stating 'Equity contribution – subscription of shares in NZ_LTD'; alternatively, a shareholder loan is routed with the reference 'Shareholder loan agreement dated [date]' and the corresponding loan agreement retained for verification. No New Zealand exchange-control approval or Overseas Investment Act 2005 consent is required for ordinary equity or working-capital facilities of a goods-exporting SME, provided no sensitive land is involved and business assets remain below the statutory thresholds. The receiving New Zealand bank will apply standard AML/CFT source-of-funds checks, requiring the board resolution approving the capital raise, the share subscription agreement, the remitter's bank statement, and a completed source-of-funds questionnaire. The bank may credit the NZD operating account or a designated foreign-currency account, with conversion executed at commercial spot rates.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier telegraphic transfers, with the beneficiary account and invoice number stated in the remittance narrative; (ii) commercial letters of credit issued through the Tier-1 bank's trade finance desk and negotiated under UCP 600, with conforming documents presented before maturity; (iii) SWIFT gpi for end-to-end tracking of cross-border payments; and (iv) New Zealand domestic clearing infrastructure operated by Payments NZ, with finality via the Reserve Bank's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) for NZD settlements. For marketplace receipts, PayPal and Stripe operate as third-party payment aggregators settling into the company's New Zealand bank account, and outbound disbursements for refunds or supplier payouts may be executed via the bank's automated payment (AP) system or API-driven payment initiation. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium. An offshore digital EMI may lawfully provide cross-border payment and collection services into New Zealand only if it is registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and, where it conducts money remittance or holds customer funds, registered as an AML/CFT reporting entity with the Department of Internal Affairs. The statutory term 'bank' is protected under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989, but digital EMI platforms (for example Wise, Airwallex, or OFX) can operate as licensed money remitters in New Zealand without a full banking licence. Such EMIs are viable for collections and trade payments, while a Tier-1 New Zealand bank account remains the anchor for capital escrow, settlement conversion, and credit facilities.
