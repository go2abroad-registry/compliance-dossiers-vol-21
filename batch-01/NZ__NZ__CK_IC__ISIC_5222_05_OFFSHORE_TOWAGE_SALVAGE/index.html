Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake), Wellington, New Zealand; digital portal: myIR at https://ind.ird.govt.nz; supplementary customs portal for physical export declarations: New Zealand Customs Service / Trade Single Window; business registration registry: New Zealand Companies Office and New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) register.

E-government filing portal

A foreign offshore entity must first determine whether it has New Zealand source income or carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand. If no New Zealand source income and no fixed establishment, no compulsory IRD number is required; the entity may nevertheless obtain an IRD number for treaty identity or banking purposes using the applicable non-resident company registration application. If income tax registration is required, the non-resident company applies for an IRD number through myIR or by paper application, providing certified incorporation documents, director/shareholder identification, registered office details, and appointment of a New Zealand agent for service. For GST, the same IRD number serves as the GST registration number; the entity must file a GST registration application if taxable supplies in New Zealand are expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period, or if voluntary registration is desired. The New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) may be obtained through the Companies Office and used for government and banking identification, but the NZBN is not a substitute for the IRD number. International Company (IC) status in the tax-exempt zone is preserved as long as the entity does not carry on activities creating New Zealand source income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The CK_IC International Company is treated as a non-resident offshore entity for New Zealand tax purposes. It is not a pass-through vehicle; it is a limited liability taxable entity in principle, but its active foreign-source income from offshore towage and maritime salvage is outside the New Zealand income tax net provided no fixed establishment exists in New Zealand and the services are not sourced in New Zealand. Entity-level taxation of any New Zealand-source income would apply only if the IC carries on activities through a permanent establishment or derives income from New Zealand land, natural resources, or other New Zealand-source assets.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; classification follows the legal form and source rules. Relevant statutory elections available to a qualifying offshore company and its New Zealand group counterpart include: voluntary GST registration under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; election between invoice basis, payments basis, or hybrid basis for GST accounting; GST group registration election; consolidated group treatment for wholly owned New Zealand-resident companies under the Income Tax Act 2007 Part IS; provisional tax estimation method election; and approved issuer levy election for certain cross-border interest. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act 2007 are mandatory and cannot be broadly elected out, although limited exemption elections are available in prescribed mismatch structures. No check-the-box election is available to convert an International Company into a disregarded entity for New Zealand tax purposes.

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any 12-month period; registration is compulsory once that threshold is exceeded, voluntary below the threshold; non-resident offshore supplies that are zero-rated under section 11B of the GST Act 1985 do not count toward the threshold unless supplied through a New Zealand fixed establishment or connected with New Zealand goods/land. Separate registration and GST return obligations apply to offshore suppliers of low-value imported goods under the GST Act 1985.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime is in force in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Peppol network; IRD does not pre-clear invoices. GST-registered suppliers must issue tax invoices meeting section 24 of the GST Act 1985, using their IRD/GST number, supplier and recipient identification, taxable supply description, time of supply, GST rate, and amount. For zero-rated export services, the tax invoice should state the zero-rating basis, e.g., 'Section 11B, GST Act 1985'. Invoices may be transmitted electronically through EDI, email, PDF, or Peppol, and the underlying records must be retrievable for audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has a fully convertible currency in both the current and capital accounts. NZD/USD and NZD cross-rates are freely determined on a spot T+2 basis, and the Reserve Bank does not impose convertibility ceilings, capital repatriation controls, or prior approval for ordinary operating and treasury flows. New Zealand is an IMF Article VIII member, therefore international payments and transfers are unrestricted. Profit remittance from an International Company is constrained not by foreign-exchange convertibility but by non-resident withholding tax (NRWT): under the Income Tax Act 2007, dividends and interest paid to non-residents are subject to NRWT at a 30% default rate (or a lower income-treaty rate, e.g., 15% under the New Zealand-Australia double-tax agreement), unless the dividend is fully imputed or an exemption applies. For a tax-exempt International Company structure, remittance is best executed as a non-taxable return of capital or exempt intercompany distribution, supported by a formal board declaration and a capital-maintenance solvency statement under the Companies Act 1993, and, where prudent, an Inland Revenue ruling confirming the exemption. No separate profit-remittance tax or foreign-exchange approval exists under New Zealand law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand does not require exporters or cross-border service providers to surrender, convert, repatriate, or process export proceeds; there is no statutory timeline (such as the 90-day or 180-day surrender regimes common in other jurisdictions) and no mandatory conversion ratio. A maritime towage and salvage enterprise may retain 100% of Lloyd's Open Form awards, hire, and disbursement recoveries in USD or any foreign currency, whether held offshore or onshore. The only obligations are indirect: (i) banks report cross-border current and capital-account transactions to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand for balance-of-payments statistics; (ii) the AML/CFT Act requires prescribed transaction reporting for physical cash and international wires above NZD 10,000, and suspicious transaction reports without threshold; and (iii) tax legislation may require attribution and reporting of foreign currency balances where the entity is New Zealand tax-resident. Treasury should not contract any NZD conversion or liquidation of export proceeds; USD operating accounts and offshore escrow are fully compatible with this zero-surrender regime.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) is the central bank and prudential supervisor of registered banks under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) supervises market conduct and non-bank financial institutions; the anti-money-laundering and countering-financing-of-terrorism (AML/CFT) regime is set out in the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and is enforced for registered banks by the Reserve Bank, for financial advice and non-bank lending licensees by the FMA, and for money-service businesses, money remitters, and high-value dealers by the Department of Internal Affairs. New Zealand maintains no exchange-control bureaucracy: there is no central-bank permit requirement for foreign-exchange conversion, no surrender-of-export-proceeds regime, and no designated FX rationing; all inward and outward payments are subject only to standard AML/CFT reporting, tax-declaration obligations, and, for foreign investments, the Overseas Investment Act 2005 where the underlying asset constitutes sensitive land or significant business assets.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium as an ancillary payment channel; Low as a standalone treasury or custody bank. Offshore digital EMI platforms, including cross-border business neo-banks such as Airwallex, Wise Business, Currenxie, and Revolut Business, are not licensed as registered banks in New Zealand under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. They cannot accept New Zealand call deposits, issue escrow letters, issue or confirm documentary letters of credit for maritime supply, or provide clearing guarantees. Such EMIs may offer remittance, multi-currency wallets, and card issuance only through an NZ-registered money-service business or a tier-1 bank sponsorship, and every such arrangement must satisfy New Zealand AML/CFT registration. The blueprint therefore treats EMIs as an optimisation layer for low-value operating suppliers in APAC corridors only, with a hard cap of 10% of monthly treasury flow and no overdraft, hedging, or escrow role.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections will travel from foreign equity holders or lenders into the New Zealand International Company's NZD/USD operating account at a tier-1 bank. Recommended compliant route: (i) the remitter sends a SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer from their home treasury account to the New Zealand bank's correspondent, with purpose code 'EQUI' or 'SHARECAPITAL' and narrative describing 'International Company share subscription; ISIC 5222 offshore towage and salvage'; (ii) the receiving New Zealand bank performs sanctions screening and correspondent-bank due diligence; (iii) funds are credited within one to two banking days, after which Treasury records the mandated purpose code, the remitter name, and the underlying subscription or loan agreement reference in the ERP; and (iv) for amounts exceeding NZD 100,000, board-minute resolutions and signed subscription documents are pre-positioned with the Relationship Executive to avoid post-cycle compliance holds. No invasive FX-control approval is required; however, if the inbound funds will finance the acquisition of assets constituting significant business assets or sensitive land under the Overseas Investment Act 2005, an Overseas Investment Office exemption application must be filed where applicable before settlement. All wires must be dispatched under an internal treasury counterparty list and a third-party-remitter policy to prevent stranded funds.Disbursement clearing for maritime trade and salvage operations uses the following rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct customer wires for supplier invoices, crew payroll, agency fees, harbour dues, and bunker fuel, with full beneficiary bank, account, and purpose-of-payment details; (2) SWIFT MT202 COV for interbank cover payments where the beneficiary instruction routes through an intermediary; (3) commercial documentary letters of credit issued or confirmed by the New Zealand tier-1 bank under UCP600 and Incoterms 2020 for major equipment or subcontractor charter contracts, preferably 100% at-sight USD or deferred 90-day usance; and (4) SWIFT gpi for end-to-end traceability. For Lloyd's Standard Form Wire disbursements from escrow, including salvage security or award payments, the Treasury mandate requires a two-signature release with at least one NZ-resident signatory, a unique salvage reference comprising the IMO number and claim code, and a pre-approved counterparty whitelist; the bank executes as a regularised wire without overnight delay. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium as an ancillary payment channel; Low as a standalone treasury or custody bank. Offshore digital EMI platforms, including cross-border business neo-banks such as Airwallex, Wise Business, Currenxie, and Revolut Business, are not licensed as registered banks in New Zealand under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. They cannot accept New Zealand call deposits, issue escrow letters, issue or confirm documentary letters of credit for maritime supply, or provide clearing guarantees. Such EMIs may offer remittance, multi-currency wallets, and card issuance only through an NZ-registered money-service business or a tier-1 bank sponsorship, and every such arrangement must satisfy New Zealand AML/CFT registration. The blueprint therefore treats EMIs as an optimisation layer for low-value operating suppliers in APAC corridors only, with a hard cap of 10% of monthly treasury flow and no overdraft, hedging, or escrow role.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Transfer Pricing documentation: required where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZ$10 million in the income year; for high-risk intra-group financing, intangible, management, or recharging arrangements, documentation is required where the relevant transactions exceed NZ$1 million. Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting apply where the multinational group's consolidated revenue is EUR 750 million or above (approximately NZ$1.3 billion).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply a formal ESR statute, but s BG1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 (General Anti-Avoidance Rule) and the 'Parliamentary contemplation' core purpose test nullify arrangements that lack economic substance. An International Company claiming non-resident status and tax-exempt treatment for offshore towage must prove that central management and control, charterparty negotiation, voyage-planning decisions, and salvage arbitration authority sit physically outside New Zealand. A passport-style mail-dropped company with decision-making exercised from an Auckland office will be re-characterised as tax resident and fully taxable, with shortfall penalties attached.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under ss 143 and 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994, criminal evasion is punishable by a fine up to NZ$100,000 for a body corporate, alongside a civil shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the evaded tax; the company is also liable for the unpaid primary tax plus use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knew of or were party to the evasion face imprisonment for up to 5 years and fines up to NZ$50,000, personal assessment of the shortfall under s 34 of the Tax Administration Act, disqualification under the Companies Act 1993, and a departure prohibition order preventing the director from leaving New Zealand.
Shell Company / Abusive Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the entity as a New Zealand tax resident with worldwide income taxed at 28%; cancellation of tax benefits under the GAAR; shortfall penalties up to 100% of tax avoided (up to 150% if knowing evasion is established); suspension or revocation of maritime towage/salvage licences by Maritime New Zealand.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax shortfall where they directed the arrangement, may be disqualified for up to 5 years, and may face seizing of personal assets, travel bans, and imprisonment where dishonest deception is found.
AML / Counter-Terrorist Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a body corporate faces civil penalties up to NZ$2 million per contravention, with criminal prosecutions for wilful misconduct attracting fines up to NZ$2.5 million plus seizure of the property involved in the transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors are treated as 'officers' and may be personally civilly liable where the breach occurred with their express or implied authority; individual directors may also be prosecuted for money laundering offences and imprisoned for up to 7 years, and travel bans can be imposed to secure their attendance and payment of penalties.
