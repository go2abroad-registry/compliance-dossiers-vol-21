E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — A Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC) is a resident company of the Cook Islands but enjoys a statutory exemption from all Cook Islands income tax, asset transfer duty, stamp duty, capital gains tax, and exchange control in respect of all income and activities carried on outside the Cook Islands. The entity is therefore not subject to entity-level taxation on offshore health/fitness application revenue; this is a categorical offshore exemption (Corporate Tax Exempt Zone) rather than a pass-through election, and the exemption does not extend to any business carried on within the Cook Islands with domestic customers.

Available election forms

The International Companies Act 2012 permits the CK_IC to elect in its constitutive documents to adopt the status of a non-resident company for exchange-control and stamp-duty purposes, to migrate from the repealed International Companies Act 1981-82 by written election filed with the FSDA, and to elect to contract out of default share capital and board quorum provisions. No US-style check-the-box or OECD hybrid-mismatch election exists in Cook Islands law; the New Zealand Income Tax Act 2007 branch and GST elections (including the elective filing of consolidated GST returns and the s HG 4 group company election) are available only where a New Zealand PE or taxable presence is separately created, and their use does not derogate from the Cook Islands tax exemption.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Cook Islands maintains no exchange-control regime and the New Zealand Dollar is fully and freely convertible. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are liberalised: (i) current-account flows — Apple IAP and Google Play Billing net settlements in USD, cloud-services payments, software-licence fees, contractor disbursements — require no central-bank approval; (ii) capital-account flows — share-subscription injections, shareholder loans, intercompany funding, and dividend distributions — are unrestricted and require no prior licence, approval, or registration with the FSC or MFEM. Profit remittance by a Cook Islands International Company (IC) is entirely unfettered: under the International Companies Act 1981-82 (Cook Islands), an IC that does not carry on business in the Cook Islands is exempt from income tax, withholding tax, capital-gains tax, and stamp duty; therefore dividends, management fees, interest, and liquidation proceeds may be remitted to non-resident shareholders and creditors without deduction of any Cook Islands tax. The sole practical hurdle is not regulatory but banking-compliance-driven: the company must evidence its tax-exempt certificate, its registered-agent status in Rarotonga, and the lawful commercial substance of its ISIC 6201 digital-health and fitness telemetry operations to satisfy bank due-diligence and correspondent-bank clearance standards.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation requirement applies to export proceeds earned by a Cook Islands International Company. The full amount of foreign-currency receipts — including USD/EUR settlements from Apple and Google app-store sales — may be retained indefinitely in designated foreign-currency accounts without mandatory conversion into NZD, without any prescribed holding timeline, and with no minimum surrender ratio. The company is free to operate a multi-currency treasury (USD, NZD, EUR, or HKD) and to convert balances at market-determined rates for operational funding, hedging, or dividend distribution purposes, subject only to the bank's internal account-activity policy and the economic-substance description in the board-approved business plan.

Central bank supervision: Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) — the statutory prudential regulator of all licensed banks under the Cook Islands Banking Act 2009. The Cook Islands has no separate central bank and no domestic paper currency; monetary authority functions are exercised by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) as issuer of the New Zealand Dollar, which is the sole legal tender of the Cook Islands. The Cook Islands Ministry of Finance and Economic Management (MFEM) oversees national financial-sector policy, and the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) is the AML/CFT regulator for transaction monitoring and suspicious-activity reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW (direct on-boarding for a Cook Islands-domiciled International Company); MEDIUM-HIGH (indirect via a New Zealand operating subsidiary or branch). Global digital EMIs — including Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business, and Stripe Treasury — generally decline to on-board a company incorporated in the Cook Islands because of the jurisdiction's residual offshore-centre risk profile, periodic FATF/EU monitoring history, and the thin correspondent-bank appetite for Cook Islands BIC/routing prefixes. Direct EMI account opening in the name of the CK IC should be treated as unavailable unless the specific EMI's compliance department confirms acceptance in writing. The legally compliant and operationally effective alternative is to interpose a New Zealand-registered operating company (the app vendor and merchant of record) that holds the EMI and Apple/Google merchant accounts, with back-to-back intercompany service agreements upstream to the CK IC for IP licensing and a documented transfer-pricing policy. This preserves the tax-exempt IC structure while allowing access to modern payment infrastructure and low-cost FX conversion.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for the initial subscription of issued share capital and/or shareholder loans into the CK IC (ISIC 6201 app development funding): (i) settle via SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the shareholder's bank to the beneficiary account held at ANZ Bank (Cook Islands) Limited or Bank of South Pacific (Cook Islands) Limited; (ii) use ISO 20022 purpose codes 'EQUI' for paid-up capital and 'DEBT'/'INTR' for shareholder loans, and state in SWIFT Field 70: 'Subscription for ordinary shares of CK IC per board resolution dated [date]' or 'Intercompany shareholder loan for ISIC 6201 fitness-telemetry software development'; (iii) before remittance, the recipient bank must hold an executed Capitalisation Letter from the Cook Islands registered agent, a board resolution specifying share-count and share premium, and source-of-wealth documentation for the remitting shareholder; (iv) for any transaction passing through New Zealand, confirm that the Overseas Investment Act 2005 exemption applies — the CK IC is not acquiring 'sensitive New Zealand assets' in the ordinary course of capital injection, and no OIA consent is required; (v) funds above NZD 50,000 trigger an automatic request for the substance dossier described in the KYC defence section, so the dossier should be pre-positioned with the bank; (vi) grant destination: a segregated Capital Escrow Account in the IC's name; the funds may be moved to the operating account only after the share registers are updated, share certificates issued, and the bank's compliance officer releases the escrow ledger. (ii) any high-value or credit-sensitive trade settlement can additionally be effected by Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600) with the CK IC as applicant at the Rarotonga bank and the beneficiary's bank confirming; the documentary package (commercial invoice, electronic delivery receipt, inspection certificate where agreed) must be presented through the Swiss-API or documentary-collection desk of the issuing bank; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: LOW (direct on-boarding for a Cook Islands-domiciled International Company); MEDIUM-HIGH (indirect via a New Zealand operating subsidiary or branch). Global digital EMIs — including Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business, and Stripe Treasury — generally decline to on-board a company incorporated in the Cook Islands because of the jurisdiction's residual offshore-centre risk profile, periodic FATF/EU monitoring history, and the thin correspondent-bank appetite for Cook Islands BIC/routing prefixes. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: NZ$10 million (approx. USD 6.3M) related-party cross-border transactions; Master File: NZ$50 million (approx. USD 31.5M) group revenue threshold; Country-by-Country Report: NZ$1.14 billion group revenue threshold (OECD threshold as adopted).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand applies general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR, s BG1 Income Tax Act 2007) and transfer pricing rules under s GC 13-GC 15, aligning with OECD TP Guidelines. No separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in Cayman/BVI; however, the IRD will scrutinise substance through PE attribution, beneficial ownership, anti-conduit rules, and thin capitalisation. For a digital health app tracker, functional analysis must demonstrate that software development, data hosting, user contracts, and risk management are performed where the economic substance resides.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Administration Act 1994 s 143A and s 143B: strict liability penalty of 75% of the evaded tax for abusive tax position; evasion offence punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fine up to NZ$50,000 (individual) or NZ$250,000 (company); asset restraining orders and confiscation under Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly assist in evasion are personally liable for the company's unpaid tax via PAYE/GST director penalty notices (s 166 of Tax Administration Act 1994); civil restoration of funds; travel bans available under the Insolvency Act 2006; extradition to New Zealand possible under bilateral treaties.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: IRD recharacterisation and adjustment of taxable income; shortfall penalty of 20% for unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross lack of reasonable care, 100% for evasion; interest on unpaid tax at 9.91% p.a.; denial of deductions for non-arm's length payments.

Director personal liability: Directors can be made jointly and severally liable for TP shortfall penalties if they knowingly participate in arrangements lacking commercial rationale; personal liability orders under Companies Act 1993 for reckless trading; director disqualification orders up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the entity as having a New Zealand PE; taxation of all New Zealand-source income at 28%; imposition of NRWT and RWT obligations; penalties for failure to register with the Companies Office (NZ$500 per month) plus late filing penalties; possible strike-off and deregistration under Companies Act 1993.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell company face civil liability for unpaid taxes and penalties; New Zealand courts may pierce the corporate veil where the company is a mere façade; directors may face arrest on warrant for failure to pay fines, travel bans under the Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters Act, and bans on managing companies for up to 10 years.
AML/CFT Breach (Digital Payment Rails)

Statutory sanction: Under Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act): civil penalties up to NZ$1.1 million for entities and NZ$220,000 for individuals per breach; criminal penalties up to NZ$2 million and imprisonment up to 2 years for directors; court-ordered disclosure and freezing orders under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for AML/CFT compliance failures where they directed or acquiesced in the breach; the FMA and Department of Internal Affairs can issue infringement notices and seek disqualification; individuals may be prohibited from managing a financial services company for up to 10 years.
GST Non-Compliance (Digital Services)

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty NZ$250 plus NZ$50 per month; late payment penalty 1% plus 1.5% monthly; use-of-money interest; measures: GST evasion offence under Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 s 144B: imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to NZ$50,000; director penalty notices under s 183G.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid GST via director penalty notice if they fail to ensure GST is deducted when due; personal bankruptcy proceedings possible for directors who continue to trade while insolvent; travel restrictions and statutory demands.
