Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under the Companies Act 1993; a limited company may be incorporated with NZ$1.00 fully paid ordinary shares, typically NZ$100 to NZ$1,000 total. No escrow account or mandatory capital deposit rule applies; directors must, however, certify solvency in respect of share allotments and distributions under sections 52 and 53 of the Act.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Engineering Certificate and Building Warrant of Fitness (BWOF) issued under the Building Act 2004 and the Building Regulations 1992, incorporating a compliance schedule for automatic fire suppression, emergency egress, and smoke management systems satisfying Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) operational requirements; structural load inspection and certification from a Chartered Professional Engineer for cleanroom mezzanines, laser micro-machining frames, and solvent storage rooms; WorkSafe New Zealand occupational clearance under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 covering laser interlock protocols, compressed gas storage, chemical fume extraction, personal protective equipment, and emergency response plans, together with an occupation health monitoring regime for staff potentially exposed to solvent vapours and ionising radiation from analytical equipment.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under the Companies Act 1993; a limited company may be incorporated with NZ$1.00 fully paid ordinary shares, typically NZ$100 to NZ$1,000 total. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Tauhokohoko); taxpayer portal: myIR at www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A non-resident parent entity establishing a New Zealand limited company must first incorporate with the New Zealand Companies Office (Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment) and obtain a certificate of incorporation and a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). The foreign-owned NZ Limited company then applies to Inland Revenue for a company IRD number via the myIR portal or using the IRD application for a non-resident company; where the directors are non-resident, Inland Revenue may require certified identity documents and an appointed New Zealand resident agent. The IRD number is the statutory TIN and the same legal personality identifier is used for Goods and Services Tax (GST) registration, which must be effected simultaneously if the entity expects taxable supplies to exceed the registration threshold. Confirmation of GST registration is issued in writing with a GST registration number and filing obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a New Zealand incorporated limited company is a resident taxpayer assessed at the corporate income tax rate of 28% on its worldwide income; no look-through or pass-through status is available to foreign-owned NZ Limited companies. The territorial scope for non-resident shareholders is limited to New Zealand-sourced income and income derived through a New Zealand fixed establishment, subject to applicable double tax agreements; New Zealand maintains no national tax-exempt zone regime.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box classification. Statutory elections available include Look-Through Company (LTC) status under Subpart HG of the Income Tax Act 2007, restricted to companies with fewer than five owners all of whom are NZ-resident individuals or eligible trusts (foreign-owned NZ_LTD is ineligible); GST grouping elections under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 to treat related companies as a single taxable person; and hybrid mismatch elections under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018, including the election to determine the deductibility of hybrid financial instruments. Branch equivalent income and CFC attributions are default statutory mechanisms, not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (compulsory registration when annual taxable supplies in a 12-month period reach this threshold); voluntary registration is available below the threshold to claim input tax credits; registration is mandatory from the date it becomes reasonably foreseeable that supplies will exceed the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods are zero-rated under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods within 28 days after the time of supply and retains sufficient evidence of physical export; the recipient must be outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the goods must not be reasonably expected to be used in New Zealand. Class III medical devices, including coronary drug-eluting stents, qualify for the 0% export rate on this basis. Offshore services supplied to non-resident persons are zero-rated under section 11A where the services are not physically performed in New Zealand, the recipient is not in New Zealand when the services are performed, and the services are not directly connected with New Zealand land or goods situated in New Zealand at the time of supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Customs Service Export Entry / TSW declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand within 28 days of supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the NZBN and GST registration number
  • Certificate of Origin or export certificate issued by a recognised chamber of commerce where preferential tariff treatment is claimed

New Zealand imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or per-invoice government pre-approval mandate. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Peppol interoperability framework administered by Inland Revenue; GST-registered entities may exchange Peppol e-invoices through certified access points, and Inland Revenue provides a secure API (myIR Gateway and e-Tax) for system-to-system filing of GST returns and transmission of invoice line-level data on request. Export invoices must satisfy the GST invoice content requirements under section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, with the 30-day record retention rule applicable to all export supply documentation.

Export zero-rating is heavily scrutinised by Inland Revenue under the evasion and anti-abuse provisions of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and the Tax Administration Act 1994. Misdescribed or unsupported export claims trigger shortfall penalties of 20% of the tax shortfall for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 150% for an abusive tax position; false or fraudulent export declarations constitute tax evasion offences punishable by fines of up to NZD 50,000 for a first offence and imprisonment in serious cases. Any zero-rated output tax refunded is retrospectively clawed back with use-of-money interest from the date of refund, and the assessment period is automatically extended to 10 years where fraud or evasion is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full convertibility on both the current account (trade in goods, services, interest, dividends, and royalties) and the capital account (direct investment, portfolio investment, loans, and equity participations). There are no statutory restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, or capital, and no approval requirement for ordinary inbound equity or shareholder loan injections unless the transaction triggers the Overseas Investment Act 2005 thresholds relating to significant business assets, sensitive land, or strategic infrastructure. Profit remittance is permitted freely but is subject to non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at applicable rates (typically 15% under most tax treaties, 5% or 0% where a reduced treaty rate applies, and up to 30% in the absence of a treaty). The primary compliance hurdles are not exchange control but rather bank-driven AML/CFT due diligence, tax residency and permanent establishment considerations, and accurate classification of the payment under New Zealand income tax and anti-money laundering rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements in New Zealand. Exporters are free to receive, hold in foreign currency, convert, or repatriate trade proceeds at any time and in any amount without mandatory conversion to New Zealand Dollars (NZD). All receipts must, however, be routed through the corporate bank account with full remittance details, and transactions above the NZD 10,000 threshold trigger AML/CFT reporting obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. Transfer pricing documentation should support arm's length arrangements with related foreign distributors, but no surrender or retention ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) together with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) for AML/CFT supervision; no dedicated foreign exchange control authority exists as New Zealand operates a free-floating exchange rate regime without general exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Moderate viability with material limitations. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise Platform, Airwallex, Currencycloud, and similar licensed EMI/PSP providers) are legally permissible for ancillary cross-border payment routing, multi-currency collections, and low-value trade disbursements. They are not authorized banks in New Zealand and cannot provide deposit-taking, escrow-grade capital custody, trade finance, or overdraft facilities. Any EMI operating into New Zealand must hold relevant Financial Service Provider registration under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and be AML/CFT compliant, or operate through a locally licensed partner. For a Class III MedTech manufacturer, offshore EMIs are unsuitable as the primary banking channel for inbound equity capital, commercial letters of credit, or high-value SWIFT settlements, and should be limited to auxiliary treasury operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections and shareholder loans are routed through SWIFT MT103 transfers from the foreign shareholder's bank to the New Zealand entity's settlement account, with the remittance information field clearly stating the payment purpose as 'EQUITY INJECTION — SHAREHOLDER LOAN' and referencing the company registration number. No exchange control approval is needed, but the bank will require a funds provenance breakdown, board resolution, and capital structure update. International Bank Account Number (IBAN) and BIC/SWIFT codes are provided by the receiving New Zealand bank; funds are cleared through the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) in real time. The payment should be matched against an audited journal entry in the company's statutory financial records and notified to Inland Revenue for any applicable dividend or interest withholding characterization. For non-resident acquisitions exceeding the Overseas Investment Act thresholds, prior consent must be secured, but routine refinancing or operational equity in a new company below those thresholds proceeds without consent.Outbound settlements for raw materials, micro-machining components, packaging, and logistics are conducted through direct SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for low-value requests, and via Commercial Letters of Credit (irrevocable, at sight or deferred payment) for strategic pharmaceutical inputs or high-value polymer contracts. Acceptable rails include: (1) SWIFT MT103 T/T with invoice and purchase order reference; (2) Irrevocable Documentary Letter of Credit issued by the New Zealand bank and advised/confirmed by the exporter's bank; (3) Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) as a supplementary risk mitigation rail; (4) Open Account settlement through the corporate platform for pre-vetted counterparties. All disbursements require an underlying trade contract, tax invoice, and end-user or supplier compliance certificate, and must be screened against sanctions and denied-party lists. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise Platform, Airwallex, Currencycloud, and similar licensed EMI/PSP providers) are legally permissible for ancillary cross-border payment routing, multi-currency collections, and low-value trade disbursements. They are not authorized banks in New Zealand and cannot provide deposit-taking, escrow-grade capital custody, trade finance, or overdraft facilities. Any EMI operating into New Zealand must hold relevant Financial Service Provider registration under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and be AML/CFT compliant, or operate through a locally licensed partner. For a Class III MedTech manufacturer, offshore EMIs are unsuitable as the primary banking channel for inbound equity capital, commercial letters of credit, or high-value SWIFT settlements, and should be limited to auxiliary treasury operations.
