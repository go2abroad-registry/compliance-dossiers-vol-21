Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Government of New Zealand — online portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident/exempted company not carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand may apply for an IRD number using Form IR742 (Application for IRD number for a non-resident company). The application must be accompanied by certified incorporation documents, a signed declaration of directors and registered office, and, where relevant, evidence of a financial institution account. The application is submitted to Inland Revenue; if the company later registers for GST under the remote services rules, the IRD number is the functional TIN used for electronic returns and myIR enrolment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (the entity is a non-resident International Company incorporated in a tax-exempt jurisdiction, e.g. Cook Islands; it remains outside New Zealand income tax except to the extent that a New Zealand taxable activity, branch or source income exists; no New Zealand sourced digital game revenue is derived from New Zealand customers unless a fixed establishment or taxable activity arises in New Zealand)

Available election forms

New Zealand does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election. Available targeted elections include: (i) non-resident company election for approved issuer levy (AIL) at 2% on interest payable on qualifying debt, instead of non-resident withholding tax; (ii) election to file a consolidated tax return for wholly-owned NZ-resident company groups (not applicable to non-resident exempt vehicles); and (iii) hybrid mismatch elections/offsetting adjustments under the Hybrid and Mismatch Rules (Subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007). None of these alter the entity's Tax_Exempt_Zone classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (annual taxable supplies of goods and services in New Zealand; the same NZD 60,000 threshold applies to non-resident remote-services suppliers, including sellers of electronic/digital services and marketplace operators, under the GST Act).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An exported supply of goods is zero-rated if the goods are exported from New Zealand within 28 days of invoice, payment, or delivery; for services, including digital MMORPG content and in-app purchases supplied to non-resident customers, zero-rating applies if the supply is made to a non-resident outside New Zealand, is not materially connected with land or tangible goods in New Zealand, and is not physically performed in New Zealand. Where sales are executed through marketplace payout rails such as Apple App Store or Google Play, the marketplace operator generally accounts for New Zealand GST on NZ-consumer supplies, while the offshore developer's supply to the marketplace operator itself may qualify for zero-rating as a B2B service to a non-resident.

  • Customs export declaration / automated export entry (where physical goods are exported)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing New Zealand port/airport of departure and overseas destination
  • Commercial GST tax invoice marked 'zero-rated' (with supplier name, IRD/GST number, invoice date and supply description)
  • Non-resident customer evidence: IP geolocation record, billing address, bank or wallet payment location, customer registration data
  • Payment settlement proof: verified PSP payout statement, SWIFT/Telegraphic Transfer confirmation, or inward remittance certificate where FX repatriation is used
  • Foreign currency conversion or hedging record, where applicable

New Zealand does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping mandate for GST invoices. E-invoicing is voluntary and interoperable through the PEPPOL network under the New Zealand Peppol authority managed by Inland Revenue. Standard GST tax invoices issued for zero-rated exports require the supplier's name and GST number, invoice issue date, description of supply, amount, and a clear indication that the supply is zero-rated.

Inland Revenue may audit zero-rated export claims retrospectively. If statutory zero-rating criteria are not satisfied, GST is clawed back at 15%, with use-of-money interest from the original filing date. Civil shortfall penalties escalate from 20% (unacceptable tax position) to 40% (gross carelessness) and up to 150% (abusive tax position or evasion); knowingly making false export substantiation constitutes evasion under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and may lead to criminal fines, director/promoter exposure, and imprisonment. Early voluntary disclosure significantly mitigates these penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully freely convertible pursuant to New Zealand's acceptance of Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement; no exchange-control restrictions impede current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance is unrestricted for non-resident shareholders, subject only to Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) at treaty-resident rates (typically 5% on dividends and 10% on royalties/interest under applicable DTTs) or Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) for qualifying offshore borrowing. The only material regulatory gate is the Overseas Investment Act 2005: mandatory consent is triggered for non-resident acquisitions of significant business assets (NZD 100 million or more) or of sensitive land. A passive offshore International Company holding intellectual property and receiving global app-store payouts, with no NZ land or significant asset acquisition, faces negligible convertibility friction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds. Exporters and digital-content licensors may retain foreign currency indefinitely in offshore or domestic foreign-currency accounts. Compliance obligations are limited to (i) RBNZ Balance of Payments statistical reporting at de minimis survey thresholds, and (ii) AML/CFT reporting by reporting entities for wire transfers or cash transactions exceeding NZD 10,000. No exchange-rate conversion ratio or time limit is prescribed by law.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) is the apex monetary authority; the Overseas Investment Office (OIO), operating within Land Information New Zealand (LINZ), administers capital-flow screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005; the New Zealand Police Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) supervises AML/CFT reporting for cash and wire movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary settlement treasury accounts; MEDIUM-HIGH for ancillary rails only. Offshore digital EMIs (UK FCA e-money institutions, EU EMIs, or Singapore/ Hong Kong digital banks) do not enjoy passporting rights into New Zealand and are not recognised as registered banks under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. To touch NZ-dollar settlement rails they must register on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and obtain an AML/CFT compliance programme under the AML/CFT Act 2009 as a money or value transfer service (MVTS). In practice, a CK_IC treasury structure should use an NZ Tier-1 bank as the legal-entity operational account and use EMIs solely for FX conversion, virtual card issuance, or merchant payouts to remote contractors; the final settlement leg must land in the NZ bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from the parent or beneficial owner must be executed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 creditor-institution transfers using ISO 15022/20022 purpose codes: for equity subscription use purpose code 'C6' (Investment in a subsidiary in the receiving country) or 'T1'; for intercompany loans use 'T2' (Other capital transfers) with a signed intercompany loan agreement bearing arm's-length interest. The NZ receiving bank requires: (i) a board resolution approving the share allotment or loan drawdown; (ii) a share register update or loan agreement; (iii) Non-Resident Withholding Tax declaration (RW-NRWT or AIL election for interest); and (iv) a source-of-funds disclosure for amounts above NZD 10,000. Clearance settles same-day via the RBNZ Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) in AUD for the NZ leg; FX conversion into NZD or USD may be transacted at bank-treasury rates, and the entire flow must be entered on the entity's capital/loan schedule for OIO and IRD record purposes. Apportioned purpose code 'B2' (Other royalties and license fees) is prohibited for equity injections.Operational disbursements—royalties to IP sub-licensors, software-development subcontractors, cloud hosting fees, and marketing-agency payments—clear primarily via SWIFT MT103 single remittances or MT102 batch files with income purpose codes (Category C: 'Royalties and license fees', 'Software royalties', 'Fees for services'), accompanied by invoices, tax invoices, or statements. For large B2B content-development agreements, a Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP 600) opened via MT700, with documents-against-payment (D/P) terms, provides bank-confirmed settlement and mitigates counterparty risk; standby LCs may collateralise platform performance obligations. Domestic NZD disbursements use the Bulk Electronic Clearing System (BECS) and single-authority OIO (low value) rails; all outbound transfers above NZD 10,000 generate FIU reporting and must be tagged with the correct NZ IRD/FIU narrative to avoid compliance holds. 30% non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; reduced to 15% for portfolio shareholders and 5% for non-resident companies holding at least 10% of the voting power under most New Zealand double tax agreements, including the US-NZ and Australia-NZ DTAs.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) is mandatorily required where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZ$10 million in an income year. Country-by-Country Reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual group revenue exceeding NZ$1.125 billion. Even below the NZ$10 million threshold, the Commissioner may require documentation where transfer pricing risk indicators are present.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the doctrine is given effect through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s BG 1, Income Tax Act 2007), the arm's-length transfer pricing rules, the anti-hybrid mismatch rules, and the permanent establishment attribution rules. A shell International Company with no real presence, no commercial substance, and no decision-making function will have its affairs recharacterised, and the Commissioner may disregard the separate legal personality for tax purposes where the dominant purpose is obtaining a tax advantage.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting of Digital Revenue

Statutory sanction: Penal tax of 150% of the tax shortfall (Tax Administration Act 1994, s 141E); criminal conviction carrying a fine of up to NZ$50,000 for an individual and NZ$250,000 for a body corporate plus imprisonment for up to five years (s 143B); use-of-money interest is charged on all outstanding amounts; asset restraint and forfeiture proceedings can also be initiated under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors and officers who direct, authorise or knowingly participate in evasion are personally liable for the company's unpaid tax liabilities under the director recovery provisions; they face individual criminal prosecution, travel bans, disqualification from managing corporations, and imprisonment for up to five years.
Shell Company / Abusive Tax Avoidance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule voids the tax advantage and the Commissioner recharacterises the arrangement; a shortfall penalty of 100% of the tax shortfall applies for an abusive tax position (TAA s 141D); treaty relief may be denied under limitation-of-benefit or main-purpose clauses; the company may be deregistered under the Companies Act 1993 and its director disqualified.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structure or operate the shell company face personal liability for the tax shortfall and penalties under the director penalty regime; they may also be subjected to civil penalties, travel bans, and, in aggravated cases involving dishonest conduct, imprisonment for aiding tax avoidance.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may amend the taxable income to align with the arm's length standard under the transfer pricing rules, impose shortfall penalties up to 100% of the tax shortfall for avoidance, apply use-of-money interest, and penalise failure to file correct tax returns; Country-by-Country and Master File failures can trigger additional infringement notices and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign false transfer pricing documentation, or knowingly cause the company to understate taxable profit, are exposed to personal liability for corporate tax debts under the director liability provisions, and to prosecution for making false statements or omitting material particulars, with consequent fines, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to NZ$5 million for body corporates and NZ$500,000 for individuals per contravention; money laundering offences under the Crimes Act 1961 carry imprisonment up to seven years; regulatory enforcement orders, public warnings, injunctions, and forced deregistration of the financial service provider may also be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors of an AML/CFT-reporting entity that fails to conduct customer due diligence, report suspicious transactions, or maintain prescribed records can be personally fined and imprisoned; senior management may be subject to individual civil penalty proceedings and employment or professional disqualification.
