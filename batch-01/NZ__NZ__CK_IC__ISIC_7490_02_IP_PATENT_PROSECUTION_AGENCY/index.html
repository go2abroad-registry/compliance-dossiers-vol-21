Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Companies, New Zealand Companies Office, part of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), 15 Stout Street, Wellington 6011; online portal: New Zealand Companies Register (companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz).

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No. 105), ss. 12–19 (incorporation procedure and certificate), s. 22 (name restrictions), s. 26 (constitution), ss. 37–47 (classes of shares and issue), ss. 131–138 (directors' duties), s. 152 (requirements for directors), s. 153 (board decisions), s. 204 (registered office), ss. 378–382 (Registrar's powers); Income Tax Act 2007, subpart CW (foreign-sourced income exemption for non-resident-owned international business companies); Patents Act 2013 and Trade Marks Act 2002 (regulatory scope for IP prosecution).

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation Application (Form 1) under ss. 12 and 13 of the Companies Act 1993, integrated with the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) registration; including director and shareholder consents (Form 3 / Form 2 as applicable) submitted electronically via the Companies Register.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 29 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies under the Companies Act 1993; the Company may be incorporated with one or more shares of no par value. For the Tax-Exempt Zone classification of this International Company (CK_IC), the board shall maintain an issued and fully paid-up capital of NZD 1,000, represented by 1,000 ordinary shares held by a non-resident shareholder, thereby qualifying for the foreign-sourced income exemption under the Income Tax Act 2007. No escrow account, bank deposit certificate, or capital verification is required or accepted by the Registrar of Companies.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certification: the premises must obtain a Fire Evacuation Scheme approval from Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) and an annual fire safety compliance certificate under the Building Act 2004 (means of escape, fire alarms, extinguishers). Structural and Occupancy Clearance: a Code of Compliance Certificate (CCC) or existing Building Warrant of Fitness (BWOF) from the territorial authority, confirming structural load compliance, adequate seismic rating, and disability access under the Building Code. Occupational Health and Safety: workplace clearance and self-assessment under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 (HSWA) and WorkSafe New Zealand regulatory oversight, including a written health and safety policy, a risk register covering ergonomic and electrical hazards, first-aid provisions, and mandatory registration with the Accident Compensation Corporation (ACC) for the worker levy classification 'Office-Based Professional Services' (risk class low).

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies under the Companies Act 1993; the Company may be incorporated with one or more shares of no par value. For the Tax-Exempt Zone classification of this International Company (CK_IC), the board shall maintain an issued and fully paid-up capital of NZD 1,000, represented by 1,000 ordinary shares held by a non-resident shareholder, thereby qualifying for the foreign-sourced income exemption under the Income Tax Act 2007. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake) — online portal myIR (https://myir.ird.govt.nz); New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) issued through the New Zealand Companies Office; export controls administered by the New Zealand Customs Service via the Trade Single Window

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident International Company: (1) apply for an NZBN through the NZ Companies Office; (2) if carrying on business in New Zealand, register as an overseas company under the Companies Act 1993 unless exempt; (3) apply for an IRD number using form IR 742 (Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application) or through myIR; (4) register for GST in myIR where the statutory NZD 60,000 taxable-supplies threshold is met; (5) use the single IRD number as the common taxpayer identifier for income tax, GST, RWT, and any employer obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — as a non-resident International Company (CK_IC), the entity is outside the New Zealand income tax net with respect to offshore patent-prosecution and international trademark-registration revenue, provided it maintains no fixed establishment or permanent establishment in New Zealand and derives no New Zealand-sourced income. Registration for New Zealand GST (where applicable) creates a GST identity only and does not alter the entity's non-resident income-tax status. — For a non-resident International Company: (1) apply for an NZBN through the NZ Companies Office; (2) if carrying on business in New Zealand, register as an overseas company under the Companies Act 1993 unless exempt; (3) apply for an IRD number using form IR 742 (Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application) or through myIR; (4) register for GST in myIR where the statutory NZD 60,000 taxable-supplies threshold is met; (5) use the single IRD number as the common taxpayer identifier for income tax, GST, RWT, and any employer obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — as a non-resident International Company (CK_IC), the entity is outside the New Zealand income tax net with respect to offshore patent-prosecution and international trademark-registration revenue, provided it maintains no fixed establishment or permanent establishment in New Zealand and derives no New Zealand-sourced income. Registration for New Zealand GST (where applicable) creates a GST identity only and does not alter the entity's non-resident income-tax status.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections and mechanisms include: voluntary GST registration under s 51 Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; GST grouping election under ss 55–57 GST Act 1985; election to account for GST on a payments (cash) basis or invoice basis under s 19 GST Act 1985; look-through company (LTC) election under subpart HB Income Tax Act 2007 (not applicable to a foreign International Company); CFC income attribution elections under ss EX 57–58 Income Tax Act 2007; and branch-equivalent or PE election mechanisms under applicable double-tax agreements. Hybrid mismatch rules under Part FH Income Tax Act 2007 are mandatory anti-avoidance provisions with no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding or current 12-month period (s 51 GST Act 1985); non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand consumers are likewise required to register once their NZ taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000; voluntary registration is available below the threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under s 11(1) GST Act 1985 where the supplier is GST-registered and the goods are exported within 28 days of the supply. Services: zero-rated under s 11A GST Act 1985 where the recipient is a non-resident who is not in New Zealand at the time of supply, the services are performed remotely or otherwise consumed offshore, and the supplier holds sufficient documentary evidence. Patent prosecution and international trademark registration services supplied to non-resident IP owners qualify for zero-rating where the intellectual-property rights are sought, protected, or exploited outside New Zealand or the services are enjoyed offshore.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged through the New Zealand Trade Single Window (applicable to physical goods exports)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing exportation within 28 days of the supply
  • Signed service agreement or engagement contract identifying the non-resident client and the applicable offshore IP jurisdiction
  • Tax invoice together with the supplier-held documentary evidence file required under s 11A(2)(c) GST Act 1985
  • Bank remittance advice or SWIFT MT103 evidencing payment from the non-resident client

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping obligation. New Zealand has adopted the Peppol interoperability framework for e-invoicing, operational since 30 March 2022, on a voluntary basis; IRD accepts Peppol BIS 3.0 e-invoices. For goods exports, lodgement of export declarations through the Customs Trade Single Window is mandatory, but this is a customs process and does not constitute a GST clearance or digital stamping mechanism.

IRD may retrospectively assess output GST at 15% plus use-of-money interest (UOMI) where zero-rating evidence is inadequate, the supply is recharacterised as domestic, or the export conditionality is breached. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply at graduated rates: unacceptable tax position 20%, gross carelessness 40%, and evasion or intentional misrepresentation 150%. Criminal prosecution for fraud or evasion may result in fines up to NZD 250,000 for a company and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years (ss 143A–143B TAA 1994). The standard four-year assessment time bar applies, but no time bar applies to assessments involving fraudulent or deceptive conduct.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised current and capital account. The NZD is freely convertible onshore and offshore. There are no exchange control approvals or profit repatriation permits. Dividends, royalties, patent prosecution fees, and IP licensing remittances may be transferred abroad without foreign exchange restriction. Non-resident shareholders and IP beneficiaries must comply with domestic withholding tax (non-resident withholding tax on dividends, royalties under Schedule 3 of the Income Tax Act 2007) and, where applicable, the Overseas Investment Act 2005 for investments in sensitive land or significant business assets. A nontransparent tax-exempt entity classification may trigger enhanced scrutiny under New Zealand anti-avoidance and anti-money-laundering rules, but does not impose currency convertibility blockers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds. New Zealand does not mandate conversion to NZD and does not impose a repatriation timeline. Exporters and service providers may hold foreign currency earnings onshore or offshore. However, AML/CFT transaction reporting obligations apply for cash transactions above NZD 10,000 and for cross-border wire transfers in accordance with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. Patent prosecution agencies receiving international reimbursements must maintain documentary evidence of the underlying official patent office invoices and client agreements.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua); external administration oversight by the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) for restricted inward direct investment

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Conditional Feasibility. Authorised non-bank electronic money institutions and digital money remitters, notably Wise (formerly TransferWise) and Airwallex, hold Financial Service Provider (FSP) registration and are subject to the AML/CFT Act 2009. They can provide efficient low-latency cross-border payment rails for international patent office wires and client disbursements, particularly for low-risk recurring fee flows denominated in NZD, USD, GBP, EUR, and JPY. Legal constraints: they are not licensed as registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989; they cannot operate statutory client money trust accounts for a legal practice, and they may reject entities with beneficial ownership in high-risk jurisdictions or with tax-exempt offshore status. Therefore, a fintech EMI should be deployed as a secondary disbursement rail, never as the primary corporate treasury account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must be effected via SWIFT MT103 (or equivalent FAST/clearing message) from the non-resident shareholder's overseas bank to the New Zealand entity's NZD or foreign-currency operating account. The purpose code in the remittance instructions should be 'EC' (Equity Capital) or 'SI' (Shareholder Investment) on bank-specific inward remittance forms, accompanied by the board resolution authorising the issue of shares, updated company extract showing the shareholder, and, where required, OIO consent memoranda if the subscribed capital is applied to acquire sensitive commercial assets or land. If the entity is a tax-exempt international company with no New Zealand economic nexus, the injection should be labelled as a pure shareholder capital contribution and must be supported by source-of-funds evidence sufficient to prove no tax motivated avoidance or disguised Australian/Chinese taxable income.Outbound disbursements for trade and official fee settlements should be executed using the following rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 directly from the NZ corporate account to WIPO/IPONZ/USPTO/EUIPO designated receiving accounts for official patent and trademark fees, referencing the IP case number and invoice line; (ii) SWIFT MT103 or domestic high-value clearing to the patent prosecution agent's own dedicated foreign currency account when paying in local currency; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) are not advisable and are not structurally appropriate for professional services or intellectual property fees; the correct instrument is the professional invoice coupled with an irrevocable payment instruction. All outbound instructions must include the full SWIFT BIC of the counterparty, the invoice amount, the official fee suspense ledger reference, and the underlying PCT/patent/trademark application number.
