Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office (NZCO), an operating arm of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), Wellington, accessible via the Companies Register online portal (www.companiesoffice.govt.nz); the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) Register is administered under the same MBIE cluster.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), particularly ss 12 (Application for registration), 14 (Certificate of incorporation), 15-16 (Legal capacity), 18-24 (Constitution, names and officer consents), 28 (Constitutional content), 91A (Written resolutions), 128-130 (Share allotment and consideration), 151-153 (Directors' appointment and residency), 157-159 (Board powers), 189-191 (Board resolutions), 195 (Registered office service), and 386 (Rectification of register); supplementary framework includes the Financial Reporting Act 2013 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NZCN — Application for Registration of a Company (filed electronically in the Companies Register; accompanied by the constitution where a bespoke constitution is adopted and by director consent forms).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; the Companies Act 1993 permits shares to be issued for nil or nominal consideration provided the consideration is fully described in the register of allotments. No escrow account is required for a standard International Company (IC) incorporation. The sole shareholder may subscribe 1,000 ordinary shares of NZ$1.00 each or other stated par value, with paid-up capital being a matter of corporate contract rather than regulatory pre-condition. Foreign-exchange and capital-import rules under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 require screening only where sensitive New Zealand land or significant business assets are acquired, which does not apply to a shelf or trading-only bonded trucking entity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue), New Zealand Government; digital portal myIR (www.ird.govt.nz/myir); complementary customs authority: New Zealand Customs Service (www.customs.govt.nz) with the Trade Single Window (TSW) electronic portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated International Company (CK_IC) must procure its statutory identifiers sequentially: (1) apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number as a non-resident company using Form IR 596 (Non-Resident Company Registration), accompanied by a certified certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered office details, director and shareholder register, an executed New Zealand tax agent appointment, and the entity's foreign tax classification disclosure; (2) where taxable supplies in New Zealand are expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, file Form IR 356 for GST registration simultaneously, or make a voluntary GST registration election to claim zero-rated input credits; (3) obtain a Customs Client Code (Customs CCID) from the New Zealand Customs Service through the TSW to lodge export declarations and TIR Carnet transit data; and (4) where TIR Carnet movement is contemplated, register as the holder with the national guaranteeing association, provide a bank guarantee or surety bond to the IRU guarantee chain, and obtain an Authorised Economic Operator accreditation where voluminous cross-border freight is projected.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box classification system; however, the Income Tax Act 2007 provides targeted elections: (a) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under sections HB 1-HB 4, permitting qualifying closely-held entities to attain transparent pass-through treatment for NZ tax purposes; (b) entity classification disclosure for hybrid mismatch consistency under the OECD/G20 hybrid rules contained in sections FH 1-FH 16, requiring the non-resident IC to document its foreign legal personality to avoid double non-taxation; (c) the branch-equivalency election for non-resident companies that elect to treat a New Zealand branch as a permanent establishment for MAP/PE relief; and (d) the foreign-currency / functional currency election under the Income Tax Act 2007 s 4B for aligning financial reporting with the USD/NZD repatriation profile.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration threshold is NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, s 51); voluntary registration is available below the threshold for non-residents that exclusively make zero-rated offshore supplies; a non-resident International Company may register with no threshold entitlement in order to recover input tax attributable to its export freight and trucking operations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under sections 11 and 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 on the following cumulative conditions: (a) the CK_IC supplies goods that are exported by the supplier to a destination outside New Zealand and are physically shipped or delivered within 28 days of the invoice date; (b) for cross-border trucking services, the freight transport service is directly connected with the export of goods from New Zealand and the principal element of the transport service is performed in the overseas leg; the service is supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply; (c) in respect of TIR Carnet movements, the entire bonded itinerary must terminate in a foreign customs territory, with the TIR Carnet terminating formally at the destination customs office, and the CK_IC must be named as the exporter/consignor in the Carnet holder box as well as in the commercial invoice; (d) the CK_IC retains and makes available all documentation evidencing the export chain prior to claiming the zero rate; and (e) the consideration must be credited to a New Zealand bank account or a foreign account reporting to the New Zealand tax authorities in conformity with the FX repatriation covenant.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged via the Trade Single Window (TSW) with the New Zealand Customs release reference and the outbound TIR Carnet movement number
  • TIR Carnet (IRU guarantee voucher) containing the departure customs stamp at the New Zealand exit office and the destination customs certificate of termination from the final foreign customs territory
  • CMR International Consignment Note / multimodal Bill of Lading evidencing the cargo despatch from a New Zealand bonded facility
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a New Zealand-registered bank or the corresponding nostro bank, evidencing the same-counterparty forex credit attributable to the export invoice
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with Incoterms 2020 (FOB, EXW, or DAP) and the declared NZD consideration
  • Certificate of Origin (where a preferential tariff claim is advanced at the destination customs territory)

New Zealand operates the PEPPOL e-invoicing network (administered by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment); e-invoicing is voluntary for B2B transactions and mandatory for invoices rendered to the Crown. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous control, or digital stamping regime for GST invoices. For customs and export clearance, the New Zealand Customs Service mandates electronic lodgement of every export shipment and TIR Carnet transit declaration through the Trade Single Window (TSW) using structured XML/JSON schemas; machine-to-machine API connectivity is mandatory for Authorised Economic Operator and high-volume bonded trucking operators, and a valid TSW risk-assessment release response is required before the transport unit may be loaded or depart the port of exit.

The Inland Revenue Department and the New Zealand Customs Service conduct coordinated post-clearance audits of zero-rated export claims. Where a TIR Carnet is not terminated abroad, the goods return to New Zealand without a valid re-import entry, or the export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, the zero-rating is retrospectively reversed and GST is payable at 15% on the full export consideration. The Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes late payment penalties escalating from 1% monthly to 4% monthly, shortfall penalties ranging from 20% (lack of reasonable care), 40% (unacceptable tax position), 100% (abusive tax position) to 150% (evasion), and a four-year standard reassessment window (unlimited in cases of fraud). Criminal prosecutions may be instituted under section 143 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for knowingly falsifying export documentation, carrying a maximum fine of NZD 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. The Customs and Excise Act 2018, section 391, further punishes false customs declarations with a fine of up to NZD 100,000 and confiscation of the bonded goods; a fraudulent TIR Carnet claim additionally triggers revocation of the operator licence, forfeiture of the IRU guarantee bond, and permanent exclusion from the Authorised Economic Operator program.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account, consistent with New Zealand's IMF Article VIII status. NZD floats freely; there are no exchange-rate bands, conversion quotas, surrender obligations, or Central Bank licensing of treasury hedging. Dividends and branch profits can be remitted without FX authority approval, subject to applicable tax compliance: Non-Resident Withholding Tax at the general domestic rate of 15% or 30% depending on recipient status and treaty entitlement, with the Approved Issuer Levy framework available for offshore interest. For a tax-exempt International Company structure, the main friction is not currency control but banks' risk appetite and the obligation to evidence economic substance to the New Zealand Registrar of Companies if the vehicle is registered as an overseas company under Part 18 of the Companies Act 1993. Practical consequence: capital account inflows and treasury outflows must carry a documented commercial purpose (shareholder loan agreement, freight invoice, fuel invoice, TIR Carnet register) so that banks can perform their independent AML/CFT obligations without delay.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None under New Zealand law. Exporters, including companies earning cross-border trucking revenue (freight invoicing, fuel rebates, TIR Carnet transit fees, ancillary warehousing), are not required to: (i) repatriate export proceeds to New Zealand, (ii) surrender any percentage of foreign currency to the RBNZ, or (iii) convert export receipts into NZD within any statutory timeline. Foreign-currency earnings may be retained in offshore nostro/vostro accounts or in foreign-currency nominated NZD accounts at registered banks. The two mandatory compliance touchpoints are: (a) all physical cash or bearer-negotiable instruments in excess of NZD 10,000 crossing the border must be declared to the New Zealand Customs Service under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009; and (b) export documentation (invoices, CMR/TIR Carnets, bills of lading, freight manifests) must be preserved for a minimum of seven years to support zero-rated GST treatment of cross-border services and to defend any OECD/BEPS tax substance review.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the sole central bank and supervisory authority for all registered banks in New Zealand. Exchange-control functions formerly exercised under the Exchange Control Act 1953 (Foreign Exchange Control Regulations) have been substantively abolished since the 1984 liberalization; the RBNZ no longer confers permissions, approvals, or licences for ordinary cross-border payments, and there is no separate foreign-exchange administration body. Residual RBNZ/NZ Customs jurisdiction applies only to (i) physical cross-border movement of NZD or bearer-negotiable instruments at or above NZD 10,000, which requires a border cash declaration, and (ii) consolidated monetary statistics reporting by banks. The RBNZ prudential framework (Registered Bank regime under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021) is therefore the relevant gatekeeper for treasury counterparties rather than a currency-control regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium for supplementary payment execution, Low for primary banking and treasury. New Zealand has no standalone electronic-money-institution licence; payment service providers operate as registered financial-service providers under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and as money-changers or remitters under the AML/CFT Act. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Revolut Business) may offer NZD wallets and SWIFT/other clearing rails via their respective New Zealand FSP registrations and NZ bank sponsorship, but they cannot: (1) issue or guarantee TIR Carnets as the New Zealand national guaranteeing association requires a bank guarantee or standby credit from a locally incorporated registered bank; (2) provide commercial letters of credit under UCP 600 for import/export settlements; or (3) act as the executing branch for an NZ-resident finance lease or equipment facility. Recommended structure: use an EMI only as a low-cost payment bridge for small cross-border disbursements (tolls, road taxes, minor supplier payments) while the operational treasury and all TIR/Carnet-guarantee-related flows remain in a tier-1 NZ bank.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) codebook (as in the UAE/BVI); substance is instead enforced through: (i) the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1 Income Tax Act 2007), which voids arrangements whose dominant purpose is tax avoidance; (ii) the corporation residency rule (s YD 2), deeming a company resident if incorporated in NZ, or if its head office, centre of management, or directors are in NZ — a letterbox CK_IC controlled from Auckland is re-domiciled into the NZ tax net; (iii) subpart FE thin capitalisation, limiting the NZ entity's debt to 60% of net assets and interest claims of the foreign parent; (iv) the source rules (s YD 4) for international transport income; and (v) the business continuity test in s IA 5. Practical ESR proxies: at least one substantive director who is not a nominee, management meetings in the Cook Islands, a bank account and records held outside NZ, actual freight-related risk-bearing, and the substance of the TIR guarantee activities. Failure exposes the group to recharacterisation of the CK_IC as an NZ-resident company, voidance of the profit-sheltering structure, 50%/100% shortfall penalties, and potential criminal fraud charges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under s 143A/143B Tax Administration Act 1994: fine up to NZ$250,000 (company) and/or 5 years' imprisonment (individual) for evading assessment; civil shortfall penalty of 100% of the tax shortfall under s 141D (evasion) plus use-of-money interest at 8.42% p.a. from the filing date; late payment penalties of 1% immediately and monthly thereafter; suppression of the evasion results in a 3-year reassessment time bar extension.

Director personal liability: Directors, de facto directors and shadow controllers are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax and penalties where they arranged the company's affairs to evade tax (s 149 TAA); each director may be prosecuted individually; fraudulent trading is a criminal offence under s 383 Companies Act 1993, with the Court empowered to make civil restitution orders and declare the director personally liable for company debts; travel bans are routinely sought by IRD for outstanding evasion debts, and directors cannot be appointed to new boards while disqualified.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Arrangement void under s BG 1 Income Tax Act 2007; IRD reattributes all CK_IC income to the NZ economic owner and reconstructs the tax position; abusive tax position penalty of 50% (s 141C); evasion penalty of 100% (s 141D); reassessment time bar extended to 10 years; interest accrues until full settlement; non-filing of returns of the re-domiciled company is a further offence.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused the company to enter the sham face personal liability for the total tax shortfall; civil restitution of profits stripped offshore; the Secretary of IRD may issue a 'tax avoidance' freezing order on assets; travel bans apply where the restructuring creates unpaid crown debt above NZ$10,000; directors' disability under s 385 Companies Act 1993 bars management of any company for a minimum of 5 years.
