Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department, New Zealand (Te Tari Taake); online portal: myIR (Secure Online Services) accessible via www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (including a non-resident company or a Cook Islands International Company qualifying as an offshore vehicle) must obtain an IRD number (tax file number) and a separate GST registration number from Inland Revenue. Procedure: (1) Apply via myIR or file the relevant non-resident registration form (IR742 for non-resident companies); (2) Provide certified certificates of incorporation, deed of incorporation, charter, memorandum and articles of association, and evidence of New Zealand permanent establishment status where registration is sought on that basis; (3) Appoint a New Zealand resident agent for service of notices if the entity is not carrying on a taxable activity through a fixed establishment in New Zealand; (4) Register under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for a GST number if annual taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 or if zero-rating of exported goods or services is to be claimed; (5) Inland Revenue issues a 9-digit IRD number and a separate GST number; online registration is usually immediate for telecommunication, remote services, and low-value goods suppliers using the non-resident online GST registration portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand has no general 'check-the-box' entity classification election. Instead, the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 provide explicit statutory elections and statuses: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under sections HB 1 to HB 4 to render the entity fiscally transparent to shareholders; (2) Qualifying Company election (legacy transitional regime); (3) Joint Venture election under subpart HG to be taxed as a joint venture rather than a partnership; (4) GST grouping election under section 55 of the GST Act to treat related entities as a single registered person; (5) GST margin scheme election for land transactions; (6) Hybrid and branch mismatch elections under sections FH 1 to FH 11 aligning treatment with OECD BEPS Action 2; (7) Functional currency election under section DN 7 for tax accounting in a foreign currency; (8) Election to apply the Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) instead of non-resident withholding tax on related-party interest; and (9) GST cash-basis and payments-basis credit valuation elections under sections 19D and 19E of the GST Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period for domestic and non-resident suppliers; NZD 60,000 for remote services supplied to New Zealand residents and for online marketplace operators; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold, and compulsory registration applies where the threshold is exceeded or is reasonably expected to be exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating applies to: (1) goods physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier within 28 days of supply (or prior delivery to the exporter) under sections 11(1)(a) and 11(1)(b) of the GST Act 1985, supported by evidence of export; (2) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, where the services are supplied for and directly benefit a person outside New Zealand under section 11A, excluding services supplied in connection with land in New Zealand, goods in New Zealand, or services supplied to non-residents who have a fixed establishment in New Zealand and who acquire the services for the purpose of carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand; (3) international transportation of goods and passengers, including air cargo transport and services reasonably incidental to international carriage; (4) a New Zealand-based air cargo charter broker's commission or brokerage fee charged to a non-resident principal for the charter of an aircraft or air cargo capacity in international air transport, where the benefit of the brokerage service is received outside New Zealand; and (5) zero-rating requires that the supplier holds a valid GST registration number and issues a tax invoice expressly stating that the supply is zero-rated.

New Zealand has no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls, no digital stamping, and no pre-clearance of invoices by Inland Revenue. E-invoicing is voluntary and is transmitted predominantly over the Peppol network under the Australia-New Zealand e-invoicing framework; GST returns are filed electronically through myIR on a monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly cycle using the GST101 return; and business records, including tax invoices, must be kept in English for a minimum of seven years.

Inland Revenue scrutinises zero-rated export supplies closely, particularly in the freight forwarding and air cargo charter brokerage sector. If the supplier cannot substantiate physical export, the non-resident status of the recipient, or performance of the service outside New Zealand within the statutory evidentiary timeframe (goods must be exported within 28 days of supply; records must be retained for 7 years), the supply is recharacterised as taxable at 15% GST. The resulting assessment attracts unpaid GST and use-of-money interest under the Interest Act 2011 (standard rate updated quarterly). Fraudulent zero-rating claims or GST refund fraud, such as booking shell offshore charters, falsifying air waybills, or declaring non-existent cargo, constitute offences under sections 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994. The civil shortfall penalty for knowingly taking a tax position with a dominant purpose of evading tax is 150% of the tax shortfall under section 141E, with evasion attracting imprisonment of up to 5 years on conviction. Companies additionally face forfeiture orders under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009, loss of good-character status for licensing under the Civil Aviation Act 1990, revocation of Customs approved cargo security accreditation under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, and prohibition from holding a Customs Controlled Area operator licence. General shortfall penalties range from 20% for failure to take reasonable care to 150% for evasion, with an additional 100% penalty for abusive tax positions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully freely convertible under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Current-account payments for air-cargo services, charter hire, fuel, handling, and insurance are unrestricted; capital-account repatriation of equity, loan principal, dividends, and branch profits from a non-resident-owned International Company is likewise unimpeded by exchange control. There is no prior RBNZ approval for inbound foreign direct investment or outbound treasury remittance unless the transacted asset meets the OIA residual threshold for sensitive New Zealand assets. The only material hurdle for surplus remittances is the standard domestic withholding tax regime (Non-Resident Withholding Tax or Approved Issuer Levy) on NZ-source interest and dividends; profits derived entirely from foreign-source activity are not chargeable to New Zealand income tax and can be paid offshore without exchange-control restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio in New Zealand. Exporters and service providers, including air-cargo charter brokers, may lawfully retain foreign currency proceeds in foreign-currency bank accounts at licensed NZ banks or offshore. Nevertheless, the reporting burden is mandatory: the bank must report monthly balance-of-payments statistical data to the RBNZ on each cross-border transaction above NZD 15,000, and a prescribed transaction regime under the AML/CFT Act requires proper remitter and beneficiary identification for wires. Treasury best practice for this cargo-charter business is to invoice all charter services with clear IATA/CASS documentation and to ensure foreign-currency receipts are cleared within 30 days from the service billing date so as to preserve full audit trail for both RBNZ statistical and bank enhanced-due-diligence purposes.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the statutory central bank and sole prudential regulator of financial institutions. There is no separate FX administrative decree; exchange control remnants under the former Foreign Exchange Control Act 1952 have been formally repealed. The relevant instruments now comprise the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989, the Overseas Investment Act 2005, the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013. The Overseas Investment Office (OIO) restricts non-resident investments in sensitive NZ land, fishing quotas, and radio spectrum, but does not regulate ordinary corporate banking flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility for offshore digital EMIs. New Zealand recognises no dedicated 'Electronic Money Institution' licence, and only registered banks under the RBNZ can take deposits or hold client funds as principal. An offshore EMI may act solely as an arranger or sub-collection rail if registered on the Financial Service Providers Register and backed by a licensed NZ bank master account. For a Medium_Risk_Enhanced air-cargo charter broker, a fully EMI-hosted treasury would be a compliance blocker; the permissible structure is to use a reputable global payment provider (e.g., a Wise Business or Airwallex treasury capable) strictly as a gross-settlement collection sub-rail with T+1 sweeping into the ANZ or BNZ master account, with the EMI being neither the depositary nor the executing bank for final clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The parent or trust shareholder must fund the New Zealand IC using a direct equity injection pursuant to a written share-subscription agreement and board minutes. The routing procedure is as follows: (1) the remitting bank sends a SWIFT MT103 from its New York or Singapore clearing arm to the NZ receiving bank's main BIC (e.g., ANZBNZ22) with the beneficiary account number matching the operating account; (2) field 50K in the MT103 must contain the exact legal name and LEI of the shareholder mirroring the KYC file; (3) field 70 (Remittance Information) must carry the RBNZ Balance-of-Payments purpose identifier for 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' as per the RBNZ Banking Reporting System manual, together with the phrase 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – NO GOODS MOVING'; (4) the funds will settle over NZClear if denominated in NZD, or be converted at the bank's spot rate if received in USD; (5) no prior RBNZ approval or license is required, but the bank will report the inflow on its monthly External Data Return; and (6) if the total inbound exceeds NZD 21 million in a 12-month period and is connected to an NZ significant business asset, the OIO consent threshold must be checked, although a pure charter-broker IC with no land interest normally falls outside that trigger.For operational disbursements to aircraft operators, fuel uplift companies, and ground-handling agents, the primary clearing rail is the international SWIFT MT103 wire using the bank's correspondent network, with the instruction to use SWIFT Priority 'URGENT' and the cover method MT103 COVER routed via the bank's US correspondent (e.g., JPMorgan Chase, New York, or Citibank N.A., New York) for same-day Fedwire settlement of USD-denominated obligations. For high-value aircraft charter deposits, block-space fees, or undertakings on delivery of cargo units, the treasury must open a Commercial Letter of Credit under ICC UCP600 using SWIFT MT700, reimbursed through the issuing bank's USD account; for demand guarantees on lease-bailment a standby LC under ISP98 or URDG 758 provides the necessary clearing assurance. All outgoing remittances above NZD 15,000 are subject to automatic monthly statistical capture; a copy of the underlying supplier invoice must be retained on file to demonstrate a genuine commercial trade, and no outgoing wire is permitted without a matching treasury disbursement request referencing the commercial contract and the relevant air waybill.Plain Vanilla FX Forward between NZD and USD, AUD, EUR, GBP or CAD, entered into with an NZ-registered bank swap desk for periods up to 12 months and backed by a duly executed International Swap Dealers Association (ISDA) master agreement.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) for currencies not freely deliverable in New Zealand or for restricted emerging-market currencies (e.g., CNH, INR, IDR), with final settlement in NZD against the prevailing WM/R rate, subject to the Financial Markets Conduct Act hedging-to-business-income test.FX Swap (Rolling Spot-Next and Tomorrow-Next Swap) used purely for intra-period liquidity netting of cargo settlements and not for speculative position taking.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (CCIRS) used when matching an offshore USD borrowing or lease-payment obligation against NZD operational revenue, with the hedge documented and assessed under the hedge-accounting election of the entity.European-Style FX Zero-Premium Collar (Capped Forward) acquired from a licensed bank or swap dealer to protect charter receipts against extreme NZD appreciation, with a stated premium of zero and only intrinsic-value settlement.

New Zealand recognises no dedicated 'Electronic Money Institution' licence, and only registered banks under the RBNZ can take deposits or hold client funds as principal. An offshore EMI may act solely as an arranger or sub-collection rail if registered on the Financial Service Providers Register and backed by a licensed NZ bank master account. For a Medium_Risk_Enhanced air-cargo charter broker, a fully EMI-hosted treasury would be a compliance blocker; the permissible structure is to use a reputable global payment provider (e.g., a Wise Business or Airwallex treasury capable) strictly as a gross-settlement collection sub-rail with T+1 sweeping into the ANZ or BNZ master account, with the EMI being neither the depositary nor the executing bank for final clearing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$10,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Master File / Local File aligned with NZ transfer pricing guidelines and OECD BEPS Action 13). Country-by-Country Reporting applies to MNE groups with NZ$1,100,000,000 or more consolidated group revenue. Below the threshold, the arm's-length standard still applies and Inland Revenue may request documentation on review.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) analogous to the UAE/BCI regimes. Substance requirements are enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule (Income Tax Act 2007, s BG 1), the business continuity test for loss utilisation, anti-conduit and anti-sham doctrines, and the 'beneficial ownership' limitation in DTA articles. An air cargo charter broker company transacting cross-border commissions with no New Zealand employees, premises, negotiation function, or board decision-making in New Zealand is exposed to reclassification of profits, denial of treaty benefits, and GAAR reconstruction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty of 150% of the tax avoided (evasion category); criminal conviction under the Tax Administration Act 1994, s 143A with imprisonment up to 5 years and/or a fine up to NZ$50,000 for individuals and up to NZ$100,000 for companies; reassessment and back interest apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or direct the evasion are personally liable as parties to the offence; the corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent conduct under the Companies Act 1993, s 136 (dishonest dealings) and civil restitution may be ordered; the High Court may impose a departure prohibition order (travel ban) for unpaid taxation.
Shell Company / Artificial Structure

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rule (Income Tax Act 2007, s BG 1) voids the tax benefit; reconstruction of the arrangement at arm's length; shortfall penalty of 100% for an abusive tax position; possible reclassification as a New Zealand permanent establishment of the foreign principal with 28% entity tax and NRWT on deemed dividends.

Director personal liability: Directors can be pursued for 100% to 150% shortfall penalties as signatories of false returns; liquidators may seek orders for reckless trading under the Companies Act 1993, s 135; the Companies Office may strike off the shell and prohibit management of New Zealand companies; travel bans apply where significant tax debt remains unsatisfied.
AML/CFT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to NZ$200,000 for an individual and NZ$2,000,000 for a company per contravention; criminal penalties for reckless conduct include up to 2 years imprisonment; AML/CFT supervisors may suspend or cancel the statutory licence required for the air cargo charter brokerage.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable where they knowingly permit, or are grossly negligent in, AML compliance failures; debarment from directorship under the Companies Act 1993 and, where self-laundering of brokerage fees occurs, prosecution for money laundering with imprisonment up to 7 years under the Crimes Act 1961.
Unpaid Tax / Director Penalty Notice

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue serves a Director Penalty Notice under the Tax Administration Act 1994, making the director personally liable for the unpaid amount; UOMI and late payment penalties continue to accrue; refusal to pay after notice permits the Commissioner to issue a statutory demand, apply for bankruptcy, or secure a departure prohibition order against the director.

Director personal liability: Directors become civilly liable for the full unpaid tax debt and are also exposed to criminal prosecution for knowingly assisting the tax default; travel bans (departure prohibition orders) and asset freezing orders can be made; the director can be disqualified from managing companies for up to 10 years and, for fraudulent stripping of assets, imprisoned for up to 5 years.
