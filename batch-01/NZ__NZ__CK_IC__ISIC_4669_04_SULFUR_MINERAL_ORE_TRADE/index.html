Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) New Zealand (Te Tari Taake); official online portal myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz). Corporate and non-resident taxpayers use myIR for IRD number applications, GST registrations, return filings, assessments, and refund management.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated International Company (IC) must first determine whether it is carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand or deriving New Zealand-sourced income. If it requires a New Zealand tax identification number, either compulsorily or voluntarily, it must apply to the IRD for a non-individual IRD number using form IR596 (IRD Number Application – Non-Individual), attaching certified incorporation documents, constitutional documents, director and shareholder details, registered office address, and evidence of any New Zealand agent appointment. Where the IC will make taxable supplies in New Zealand or elects to register for GST, it must separately apply through myIR or submit the prescribed GST registration form (form IRD484); an IRD number is a prerequisite for GST registration. A non-resident IC whose activities are confined to offshore export trading and whose central management and control remain outside New Zealand may not be required to register for income tax or GST, but obtaining an IRD number should still be considered to support double tax treaty claims, banking compliance, charter-party documentation, and export zero-rating substantiation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box election system. The IC is nonetheless entitled to statutory elections relevant to New Zealand indirect tax and international tax compliance: voluntary registration for GST under section 51 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; application to treat certain cross-border services as outside the scope of GST; GST grouping election where multiple NZ taxable activities are carried on; branch exemption election under the Income Tax Act 2007 for a non-resident company carrying on business through a New Zealand branch; and qualifying foreign trust election where a foreign trust structure is present. New Zealand's hybrid and branch mismatch rules, enacted under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018, are mandatory and contain no elective opt-out; a Tax_Exempt_Zone entity must ensure that any offshore deduction is not the result of a hybrid mismatch involving New Zealand.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; compulsory registration applies when that threshold is exceeded or is likely to be exceeded. The same NZD 60,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of remote services supplied to New Zealand consumers. Voluntary GST registration is available below the threshold where export zero-rating or input credit recovery is commercially justified.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from New Zealand are zero-rated under section 11(1) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods within 28 days of the time of supply and retains acceptable evidence of export. For the IC's dry bulk marine transport and brokerage of granular elemental sulfur, zero-rating applies to a direct export sale where legal title and risk pass to a non-resident buyer, the goods are physically removed from New Zealand, and the export is effected by the supplier or on its behalf. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under section 11A where the recipient is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are not directly connected with New Zealand land or goods; offshore brokerage commission for the sale of sulfur to foreign buyers may therefore qualify for zero-rating if the contractual documentation clearly evidences the non-resident recipient and foreign performance.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Export Declaration (electronic declaration via Trade Single Window, formerly Export Entry form C2)
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading issued to order, evidencing despatch from New Zealand to the overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-issued foreign exchange receipt substantiating receipt of export proceeds in New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and signed purchase/sale contract identifying the non-resident buyer, Incoterms, and export route
  • Packing list and weight certificate for bulk granular elemental sulfur cargo
  • End-use or export certificate where customs control requires classification under HS tariff heading 2503.00 for sulfur

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in New Zealand. GST-registered suppliers must issue tax invoices containing the supplier's IRD number, total amount, GST component, and required particulars. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the New Zealand Peppol Authority using PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 standards; invoices need not be pre-cleared or stamped by the IRD, but electronic records must be preserved in an accessible format for seven years and made available on audit.

The IRD and New Zealand Customs Service cross-match export declarations, shipping manifests, GST returns, and banking records to verify export zero-rating claims. If goods are not exported within the statutory 28-day period, the supply becomes taxable at 15% GST and the refund is clawed back, with use-of-money interest accruing from the original return due date. Incorrect or fraudulent zero-rating claims may trigger shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for lack of reasonable care or an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% for evasion or an abusive tax position. Criminal prosecution may follow for deliberate evasion, with asset subpoenas, director liability, and disqualification from future GST registration; all export substantiation records must be retained for seven years from the date of supply.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains unrestricted current account and capital account convertibility. There is no general exchange control legislation, and no Reserve Bank approval is required for inbound or outbound fund transfers, dividend remittances, loan repayment, royalty payments, or trade settlement. Both residents and non-residents may freely hold, purchase, and sell foreign currency. The only practical frictions arise from anti-money laundering/counter-financing of terrorism screening under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, sanctions screening administered by the New Zealand Police and Customs, and withholding tax compliance for outbound payments. The Overseas Investment Act 2005 may restrict foreign investment in sensitive New Zealand land and significant business assets, but it does not restrict ordinary commodity trading operations of an International Company.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio in New Zealand. A New Zealand-based International Company trading granular elemental sulfur and mineral ores may retain export proceeds in any currency, in any onshore or offshore bank account, without obligation to convert foreign currency receipts into New Zealand dollars. Statutory reporting is limited to statistical data: goods exported from New Zealand must be reported through the New Zealand Customs Service electronic export entry system, and all bank settlement transactions forming part of New Zealands balance of payments must be classified by the reporting bank using New Zealand Reserve Bank purpose codes. Transfer pricing and general tax rules apply where related parties are involved, but no surrender or repatriation timeline is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Moderate as a supplementary treasury rail, but not as a primary banking solution. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Airwallex, and Multinet are not registered as New Zealand banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and therefore cannot offer direct exchange settlement account access, documentary letters of credit, or host-to-host SWIFT service bureaus. They may provide multi-currency payment wallets and money transmission services as non-bank financial service providers, subject to anti-money laundering compliance in their home jurisdiction and, if operating into New Zealand, potential registration under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008. For a Cook Islands International Company, EMIs are restricted to operational spend and temporary liquidity movements; they cannot replace a Tier-1 New Zealand bank for LC issuance, trade settlement, and escrow services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound shareholder and intercompany funding must arrive by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 through an approved Tier-1 correspondent bank into the New Zealand operational account. The payment narrative must explicitly state the purpose: EQUITY CAPITAL INJECTION or SHAREHOLDER LOAN, with the applicable New Zealand balance-of-payments statistical purpose code used by the receiving bank, such as code 100 for equity capital or code 200 for intercompany loans. Banks will require a board resolution, executed subscription agreement or loan agreement, source-of-funds evidence from the contributor, and confirmation that the Overseas Investment Act 2005 is not triggered. Any inbound transfer in excess of ordinary trade volume will be screened against sanctions lists and the entitys KYC risk profile; therefore all financing documents must be collected before the first transfer.Trade settlements will be executed through the following clearing rails: (1) direct SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers for supplier payments working capital, commissions, and freight costs; (2) commercial letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700 and confirmed through MT710 for structured purchases of granular elemental sulfur; (3) documentary collections using SWIFT MT400 when counterparty credit quality is adequate and LC costs are disproportionate; and (4) domestic New Zealand-dollar legs cleared through the Reserve Bank of New Zealands Exchange Settlement Account System under ISO 20022 messaging. Foreign-currency legs involving USD, EUR, and NZD may be netted through CLS where both counterparties support continuous linked settlement. For a Cook Islands International Company, EMIs are restricted to operational spend and temporary liquidity movements; they cannot replace a Tier-1 New Zealand bank for LC issuance, trade settlement, and escrow services.
