Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand Government; taxpayer portal: myIR (www.ird.govt.nz); company registry: New Zealand Companies Office (www.companiesoffice.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) through the New Zealand Companies Office, then apply for an IRD number for non-resident companies using form IR742 or online via a myIR account. The application requires certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director and ultimate beneficial owner details, registered office address, and a physical address in the home jurisdiction. No statutory minimum capital is required, and non-resident ownership is permitted without a local director mandate for foreign companies carrying on business in New Zealand (a local agent for service may be required). GST registration is a separate application under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, requiring a New Zealand contact person or agent.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style Check-the-Box regime and does not provide pass-through elections for corporate entities. Hybrid mismatch rules enacted under BEPS (Income Tax Act 2007, sections FH 6–FH 14) deny deductions for hybrid financial instrument arrangements and imported mismatches, but contain limited ordering and opt-in elections for deemed deductions. A non-resident company may elect to be treated as a New Zealand resident for tax purposes under the tie-breaker provisions of a double tax agreement, and a transitional resident may elect for the four-year exemption from most foreign income. For GST, a non-resident supplier making supplies below the NZD 60,000 threshold may elect voluntary registration to claim input tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. The NZD is freely floating with no central bank exchange-rate band, no approval requirement for inward or outward remittances, and no restriction on profit repatriation. For the CK_IC (Tax_Exempt_Zone model): pure offshore ERP/CRM subscription revenue from non-NZ customers incurs zero New Zealand exchange or tax friction; any NZ-source income attributable to a permanent establishment would be subject to New Zealand corporate income tax at 28%, after which after-tax profits may be remitted freely, subject to non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at the default 30% for dividends and 15% for royalties/interest under applicable double-tax agreements. Practical hurdles are limited to AML/CFT documentary evidence, thin-capitalisation / transfer-pricing documentation where the CK_IC capitalises an NZ subsidiary or branch, and the absence of any restriction on holding NZD or foreign-currency accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation exists in New Zealand. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0% and there is no statutory timeline for repatriation; exporters may indefinitely retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds in onshore Foreign Currency Accounts or offshore accounts. No reporting to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand is required, though transactional records must be retained for a minimum of five years under the AML/CFT Act 2009. For the CK_IC as global exporter of ISIC 6201 vertical CRM software, USD, EUR, AUD and NZD subscription income may remain un-converted in a multi-currency treasury ledger and converted at the desired commercial rate in accordance with the treasury hedging policy.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) as the central bank and sole monetary and FX policy authority; Financial Markets Authority (FMA) as the conduct regulator for financial service providers; Overseas Investment Office (OIO) within Land Information New Zealand for foreign investment screening; Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Authority (FISA) as the licensing and supervisory authority for the Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC) and its registered agents. No separate foreign-exchange-control administration exists in New Zealand; exchange controls are effectively nil under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 and OECD liberalisation commitments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility for the CK_IC software exporter: High, subject to three legal constraints. (1) Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Payoneer, Revolut) are permissible as inbound collection and outbound disbursement rails without a New Zealand FSP licence, provided the EMI does not carry on business in New Zealand under s.5 of the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008; the CK_IC may maintain an offshore EMI account outside New Zealand. (2) Direct Debit (BECS) origination requires an NZ clearing-bank sponsor; consequently, the Direct_Debit payment rail must be operated through a licensed NZ bank or a licensed payment aggregator such as Stripe Billing with an NZ acquiring bank, and cannot be executed by an offshore EMI acting alone. (3) AML/CFT Act 2009 obligations arise at the point of collection from NZ-domiciled customers; the merchant-of-record structure under Stripe Billing must clearly identify the CK_IC, and the EMI's KYC file must cover the full UBO chain. Overall rating: High for cross-border subscription collections and low-value disbursements; Medium for NZD-denominated local clearing absent an NZ bank co-sponsor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: (A) equity injections must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 from the CK_IC's Cook Islands or offshore bank directly to the authorised New Zealand Tier-1 bank account, with the remitter field reading exactly 'CK_IC (Cook Islands International Company)' and the order-information field stating 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION – CAPITAL INJECTION CK_IC'; the OECD purpose code should be 'TRF' (general transfer) or 'NTR' (non-trade services) as required by the receiving bank, and the beneficiary account must be in the name of the CK_IC. (B) Pre-notify the New Zealand relationship manager with the certified board resolution, share allotment form, capital contribution agreement, Cook Islands tax-exempt certificate, and UBO declarations before the wire arrives. (C) If the injection is structured as a shareholder loan rather than equity, use purpose code 'LOAN' and provide a signed facility agreement to prevent misclassification as trading revenue. (D) Funds must never be aggregated, split, or sourced from unrelated third parties; only a single remitter connected to the UBO chain is acceptable. (E) Any conversion of USD/EUR to NZD must be executed at the bank's commercial rate or an agreed forward rate under FMA fair-dealing rules, with the conversion contract documented. (F) All records must be retained for a minimum of seven years.Clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for all vendor, channel-partner, licence-fee, and infrastructure payments equal to or above NZD 25,000 (or foreign-currency equivalent), carrying invoice references and purpose codes such as 'SOFTWARE LICENSING FEE', 'HOSTING INFRASTRUCTURE LEASE', and 'OVERSEAS CONTRACTOR SERVICES'. (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits under UCP600) for hardware co-location or data-centre procurement where the counterparty requires payment security, issued by the NZ bank against a collateralised account balance. (3) BECS (Bulk Electronic Clearing System) Direct Debit and credit transfer for NZD-denominated local disbursements such as New Zealand cloud-backup providers, legal, and accounting services. (4) Stripe Payouts or Airwallex/Wise API transfers for low-value recurring contractor commissions below USD 2,000 per payment, with consolidated FX and reconciliation files.
