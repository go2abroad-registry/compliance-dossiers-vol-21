Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the Companies Act 1993; New Zealand companies are capitalized by no-par-value shares, and the board may issue shares for any consideration determined at allotment. No escrow account, capital deposit, or bank certificate is required for incorporation; however, a fully paid-up allotment of ordinary shares is recommended to satisfy bank due diligence and the Maritime New Zealand financial-eligibility review for the seafarer recruitment and placement service licence.

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand; online portal 'myIR' at myIR.ird.govt.nz; corporate registry functions are performed by the New Zealand Companies Office, with the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) as the public business identifier.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned or non-resident International Company (custom entity type CK_IC) establishing a crew recruitment and vessel crew management agency in New Zealand: (1) incorporate the company under the Companies Act 1993 or, if the foreign IC is already incorporated offshore, register as an overseas company under Part 18 of the Companies Act 1993 and obtain an NZBN; (2) apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number using form IR742 'Registration for a non-resident company' or the myIR on-boarding application; (3) register for Goods and Services Tax using form GST101 or myIR when taxable supplies to be made in New Zealand will exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, or elect voluntary registration earlier; (4) register as a PAYE employer via myIR if the agency will employ seafarers, office staff, or NZ-based crew; (5) if the agency supplies remote or cross-border payroll and crew management services to NZ-resident ship operators, complete the non-resident supplier GST registration for remote services under the same NZD 60,000 threshold; (6) obtain a New Zealand Customs Service client code (NZECS) only if the agency also exports physical goods or arranges international cargo; pure crew recruitment and payroll services do not require a customs client code.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: under the ELF_CUSTOM_CK_IC classification, the International Company is a non-resident company whose central management and control sit outside New Zealand and which derives no New Zealand-sourced income; it therefore falls outside the New Zealand income tax net under the Income Tax Act 2007 residence and source rules. If the IC becomes centrally managed and controlled in New Zealand (s YC(2) Income Tax Act 2007) or carries on a taxable activity through a permanent establishment in New Zealand, it will instead be taxed on a worldwide or New Zealand-source basis respectively at the 28% corporate rate, with the imputation credit regime applying to resident shareholders.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election; entity classification is primarily determined by statute. The principal elections relevant to the CK_IC are: (a) Look-Through Company election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, giving pass-through treatment for qualifying closely held companies; (b) GST group registration election under s 55 of the GST Act 1985, allowing the IC and its NZ subsidiaries to be treated as one GST-registered person; (c) financial arrangement rule election under the Income Tax Act 2007 (IFRS, fair value, or base price adjustment methods) for payroll and crew receivables; (d) hybrid mismatch response elections under Part 6B of the Income Tax Act 2007 where a foreign trust or offshore shareholder ring is present; (e) GST taxable period and accounting basis elections (payments, invoice, or hybrid basis) filed with Inland Revenue; and (f) provisional tax calculation basis elections (standard, estimation, ratio, or GST ratio methods).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully open capital account with no exchange-control legislation in force since the deregulation of 1984. The New Zealand dollar is freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. The Cook Islands, although a self-governing territory in free association with New Zealand, uses the New Zealand dollar as its official currency and applies no separate exchange-control regime. A Cook Islands International Company faces no statutory restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, return of share capital, repayment of shareholder loans, or remittance of liquidation proceeds. The only compliance gates for currency conversion and outward remittance are bank-level anti-money-laundering and countering-financing-of-terrorism verification obligations, including source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership documentation. No central-bank approval is required for any inward or outward payment. Profit remittance is fully current-account free, subject solely to corporate solvency documentation and standard documentary compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender rules apply in New Zealand or the Cook Islands. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation of export receipts, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to credit export proceeds to a designated local currency account. A Cook Islands International Company operating a ship crew recruitment agency may receive crewing fees, commission income, and management fees in any currency, hold those proceeds in any jurisdiction, and convert them at commercial dealing rates without any obligation to surrender foreign currency to the central bank or to any licensed dealer. The only applicable requirement is that all transactions be recorded in the company's books and that the economic substance threshold, if any, be satisfied under the Cook Islands International Companies Act.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ)

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to a Cook Islands International Company are unrestricted and require no central-bank approval. Compliant routing protocol: the remitting shareholder or group entity issues a SWIFT MT103 from its own bank with the remittance information field containing the International Company's registration number and the purpose description 'Capital Injection into International Company' or 'Share Subscription'. The receiving bank will require the company's board resolution authorizing the account opening and the injection, a register of beneficial owners, and source-of-funds evidence. Where the injection is structured as a shareholder advance or intercompany loan, an executed loan agreement with interest rate, maturity, and repayment schedule must be lodged with the receiving bank. NZD-leg payments in New Zealand are cleared through the Exchange Settlement Account System operated by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand with real-time gross settlement finality; USD, EUR, and AUD wires are processed via the correspondent banking network and confirmed by SWIFT MT103 acknowledgments. For Cook Islands-based receiving banks, settlement occurs through their SWIFT agency relationships, and funds are credited to the IC account upon receipt of the cover payment.Outbound disbursements for crew payroll, advances, and trade settlements flow through the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 single-wire transfers for individual seafarer salary payments, repatriation advances, emergency medical disbursements, and deferred-wage settlements; (ii) ISO 20022 pacs.008 payment messages for batch multi-currency payroll runs delivered through the bank's host-to-host API or SWIFT global-payment service, with each payment carrying a structured remittance reference; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit issued subject to UCP 600 for large-scale crew-provisioning contracts, vessel-operator service settlements, or procurement of marine supplies; (iv) standby letters of credit (SWIFT MT760) where the agency must provide a performance guarantee or collateral to shipowners or government authorities; (v) local NZD clearing via Payments New Zealand infrastructure where NZD-denominated payments to crew welfare funds or domestic subcontractors must be settled next-day net. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. New Zealand-incorporated or licensed electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, and other domestic non-bank payment service providers) generally decline to onboard a Cook Islands International Company due to its classification as an offshore, tax-exempt legal entity without New Zealand tax residence. Such entities are automatically flagged for enhanced due diligence under local AML/CFT rules and frequently excluded by emi platform onboarding policies. A small number of specialist cross-border payment platforms that accept Pacific-rim offshore companies may offer virtual multi-currency IBAN settlement, but they impose strict transaction velocity limits, payroll-batch restrictions, and require nominated compliance-officer sign-off and audited source-of-funds documentation. Legal constraint: no EMI may provide true deposit-taking services in New Zealand or the Cook Islands; an EMI account is only a payment-rail tool and cannot serve as the sole treasury repository. The primary banking anchor must remain a licensed bank in the Cook Islands or New Zealand.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% (New Zealand standard corporate rate); 0% for a qualifying Cook Islands International Company (Tax_Exempt_Zone) under the International Companies Act 1981-82 in respect of non-New Zealand-sourced income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

NZ NRWT on dividends: 30% (standard) / 0% for a Cook Islands IC distributing tax-exempt foreign-source profits where no NZ residence is established; if NZ-resident, a 30% NRWT applies subject to a DTA reduction (e.g., 15% for Australia, 5% for a 10%+ corporate shareholder under several treaties). No DTA exists between NZ and Cook Islands, so treaty relief is not available between those two territories.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: There is no statutory Local/Master File filing in NZ, but contemporaneous TP documentation must be prepared and retained when aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZ$10 million per annum (goods) or NZ$1 million (services, interest, and intangibles); for a Cook Islands IC, annual ESR records must be filed with the FSC for any relevant activity regardless of value; NZ: transfer pricing documentation / benchmarking must be produced within 30 days of an IRD request.

Economic substance scope: Cook Islands ESR applies under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act and Regulations: crew recruitment and vessel crew management are 'relevant activities' (provision of services); the IC must demonstrate (i) direction and management in the Cook Islands, (ii) adequate premises and employees, (iii) real operating expenditure in the Cook Islands, and (iv) core income-generating activities physically carried on there; annual economic substance declaration (Form) required; failure or 'shell' status subjects the IC to penalties, deregistration, and the risk of NZ tax residency (management and control test) with worldwide taxation at 28%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalties under s 141 Tax Administration Act 1994: 150% for evasion (and 100% for abusive tax position); criminal prosecution under s 143A TAA: fine up to NZ$50,000 (first offence) or NZ$100,000 (subsequent) and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; NZ IRD may issue a section 141? 'tax evasion' notice and assess 28% on adjusted profits plus interest

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in, direct, or knowingly permit tax evasion may be convicted personally; under s 143 TAA? (director liability) directors of a company that fails to deduct and pay PAYE/withholding payments may become jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax; reckless trading s 135 Companies Act 1993 exposes directors to personal liability for debts (including tax) and indemnification claims; travel bans and bankruptcy disqualification may follow criminal conviction
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Cook Islands International Companies Act and ESR regulations: monetary penalties from NZ$10,000 - NZ$20,000 per breach, revocation of international company status and deregistration; the company is also re-characterised as NZ-resident for tax purposes (deemed managed and controlled in NZ), attracting 28% corporate tax on worldwide income and a 30% NRWT on dividends; NZ anti-avoidance rules (s BG 1) may void the arrangement with penalties up to 150% of the shortfall

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors can be made jointly liable for the taxes owed if the corporate veil is pierced for 'sham', 'alter ego', or 'agency' findings; directors face personal liability to refund trust assets and statutory fines; asset preservation orders and travel bans are available where there is material dissipation risk
AML / CFT Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (NZ) and corresponding Cook Islands AML/CFT Act: penalties up to NZ$10 million for companies and NZ$500,000 for individuals per breach, plus enforceable undertakings and suspension of the statutory licence (maritime crew recruitment requires a licence under the Maritime Employment Act/IS Code); criminal sanctions for money laundering up to 7 years' imprisonment

Director personal liability: AML/CFT compliance officers and senior managers are personally liable for substantive breaches committed with consent or wilful blindness; directors may be disqualified from managing maritime recruitment agencies, barred from company directorship for up to 5 years, and subject to confiscation orders
