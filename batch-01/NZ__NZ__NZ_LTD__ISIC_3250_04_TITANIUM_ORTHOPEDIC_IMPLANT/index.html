UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: The identity verification and beneficial ownership framework for New Zealand is governed principally by the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act 2009), the AML/CFT (Definitions) Regulations 2011, and the directives of the New Zealand Police Financial Intelligence Unit. Company law requirements are in the Companies Act 1993 (name reservation, director disclosure, registered office and share registers; ss 20-23, 185-190) and the Companies (Registers and Other Records) Regulations 1993. There is no publicly searchable central UBO register; banking and corporate service providers are required to conduct customer due diligence and maintain confidential beneficial ownership records, with mandatory disclosure to the NZ Police Financial Intelligence Unit and the Department of Internal Affairs. Acquisition of a significant New Zealand business or assets by a non-resident may also engage the Overseas Investment Act 2005 and the associated beneficial ownership disclosure regulations.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The Companies Act 1993, section 185, only requires a registered office in New Zealand to which documents may be physically delivered and served. A virtual office is technically permissible if a real street address in New Zealand is held by a licensed registered agent or serviced-office provider and service of process is physically accepted. However, for an ISIC 3250 titanium orthopedic implant manufacturer, the Medsafe regulatory framework, ISO 13485 quality management certification, ACC and Worksafe health and safety obligations, and the Inland Revenue requirement of a valid physical business address render a purely virtual office commercially unacceptable. The company must be able to demonstrate a physical manufacturing or contract-manufacturing facility and quality assurance premises in New Zealand for statutory licensing purposes. Such physical substance is also required to substantiate the commercial reality of the company for banking, insurance, and export financing arrangements involving SWIFT wires and letters of credit.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake), Wellington, New Zealand; official online portal: myIR (myir.ird.govt.nz) and corporate registration via the New Zealand Companies Office (companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a New Zealand limited liability company must first incorporate with the NZ Companies Office to obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). The company is then registered for income tax and Goods and Services Tax (GST) through myIR using online services or the applicable IR registration forms (e.g., IR596 for non-resident/non-individuals). The IRD will issue the statutory IRD number (TIN), which serves as the unified tax identification number for income tax, GST, PAYE, and other tax obligations. Non-resident companies that are not required to file New Zealand income tax returns but are required to charge NZ GST on supplies of remote services may apply for GST registration separately via myIR. No physical presence is required for limited company registration, but a registered office and local agent/address in New Zealand must be maintained for company law purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available in New Zealand. Eligible close companies may elect look-through company (LTC) status under the Income Tax Act 2007, causing certain income, gains, losses, and deductions to flow through to owners; this election is generally limited to close companies with five or fewer natural-person owners. Hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory under the Taxation (Neutralising Arising from Hybrid Mismatch Arrangements) Act 2018; there are no elective overrides or safe-harbor classifications. Foreign-owned limited companies may not seek a pass-through election solely by choice, and must rely on statutory LTC rules or ordinary company taxation. Non-resident companies operating through a New Zealand branch cannot elect to be treated as a separate New Zealand-resident entity absent domestic incorporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; registration is compulsory when actual turnover in the past 12 months exceeds NZD 60,000 or when there are reasonable grounds to expect taxable supplies in the next 12 months to exceed NZD 60,000; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold, and non-resident suppliers of remote services are subject to the same NZD 60,000 registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods exported from New Zealand under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, provided the goods are exported by the supplier or under a supply to a non-resident who removes them from New Zealand within the statutory timeframe. Offshore services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed may be zero-rated under section 11A, provided the services are not supplied directly in connection with New Zealand land or tangible property. For orthopedic implant manufacturing, zero-rating of exported titanium implant products requires the manufacturer to demonstrate physical export, transfer of ownership outside New Zealand, and that the goods did not remain available for domestic use.

  • NZ Customs electronic Export Declaration (through the Trade Single Window / approved EDI message).
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical international carriage.
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or equivalent bank proof of foreign-currency receipt.
  • Certificate of export / Forwarder's Certificate of Receipt evidencing movement from New Zealand.

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping regime. GST invoices are self-issued by registered suppliers and must meet the content requirements of sections 23-24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, including supplier name, GST IRD number, invoice date, description, quantity, price, and GST amount or GST-exclusive/inclusive notation. Electronic invoicing (PEPPOL) is voluntary and non-exclusive; IRD accepts electronically filed GST returns via myIR or through approved tax software using IRD gateway/API channels. There is no statutory pre-clearance or government API invoice-stamping obligation for exports, but taxpayers must retain all source documents for audit purposes.

IRD and NZ Customs cross-match export declarations against GST returns and bank incoming remittances. Where export zero-rating is claimed incorrectly or without sufficient documentary support, the GST that would otherwise be charged is clawed back as output tax at the standard 15% rate, plus use-of-money interest from the original due date. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 may apply at 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and up to 150% for evasion. Knowingly fraudulent zero-rating claims are criminal tax evasion offences, subject to fines and imprisonment. The standard reassessment period is seven years, with no time limit where evasion is alleged. Refunds are withheld until the full evidentiary bundle is reconstructed and verified.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both the current and capital accounts. New Zealand has no exchange-control laws, no surrender requirements for inward or outward payments, and no restrictions on foreign-currency denominated accounts. Current-account transactions (trade, services, dividends, interest, royalties) settle freely. Capital-account transactions are likewise unrestricted; the only ex ante gate for non-resident direct investment is screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005, triggered by the acquisition of 25% or more of an entity owning sensitive land, fisheries, or significant business assets (NZ$100m+ threshold for most non-consent investors). Dividends paid to non-residents attract non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) of 15% (or reduced treaty rate, e.g., 15% under the NZ–US DTA, often 5% for certain holdings), but there are no foreign-currency conversion delays, pre-approval queues, or queues for repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline for export proceeds surrender and no mandated conversion ratio into NZD. New Zealand abolished foreign-exchange control in the 1980s. Exporters may retain receipts abroad in any currency indefinitely. Tax and reporting obligations apply: the New Zealand Inland Revenue (IR) requires accurate records in NZD, converted using prevailing exchange rates, for income tax and GST; exporters must declare offshore funds in annual tax returns and cannot use non-reporting to defer tax. Banks remain obligated to screen for money laundering under the AML/CFT Act 2009 but do not impose any surrender or repatriation deadlines.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) and the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for capital market conduct; banking and prudential regulation is solely vested in the Reserve Bank of New Zealand, which also administers the trans-Tasman banking supervision framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for offshore digital EMIs as primary treasury rail. Offshore EMI authorization alone does not confer the right to open NZD domestic accounts with a New Zealand bank sort code or to transact in the NZ retail payment system. EMIs may operate as agent/intermediary for certain FX transfers but are not protected by the Reserve Bank deposit-taking framework. New Zealand AML/CFT regulations require any entity providing financial services in New Zealand to register with the Department of Internal Affairs as a financial service provider and comply with the AML/CFT Act 2009. An offshore EMI could partner with a licensed New Zealand bank for white-labelled settlement, but the economics and compliance burden are poor relative to direct bank onboarding. The blue-print recommends direct Tier-1 bank onboarding and the use of banking-as-a-service only for contingency or small-value funding, never for escrow or trade settlement.
