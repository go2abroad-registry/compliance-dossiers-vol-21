Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure paper 'virtual office' is not accepted in New Zealand. The statutory registered office under s 186(2) Companies Act 1993 must be a physical address in New Zealand open to the public on working days at specified hours (minimum 9:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m., Monday to Friday). A registered-office/incorporation agent may provide the physical service address, document receipt infrastructure, and a person authorized to accept service (agent for service) - this is the statutory equivalent of a 'virtual office' and is commercially permissible, but the company must maintain at least an agent office in New Zealand and cannot use a PO Box only.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, a business unit of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), operating the New Zealand Companies Register at https://register.companiesoffice.govt.nz and the public registry portal at https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No 105), as amended, together with the Companies (Electronic Transactions) Regulations 2002. The applicable incorporation and governance provisions include sections 12-14 (application and certificate of incorporation), 15-17 (name and registered office), 30-39 (shares and capital), 124 and 126 (minimum directors and New Zealand residence requirement for directors), and Part 8 (records and registered office). New Zealand has no separate 'International Company' statute; the International Company (IC) designation under this dossier refers to a private company limited by shares incorporated under the general Companies Act 1993 and used to carry on international wholesale distribution of construction materials.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of a Company, filed under section 12 of the Companies Act 1993 through the Companies Office online portal at https://register.companiesoffice.govt.nz/inc-cq/.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by New Zealand law. A company limited by shares may be incorporated with zero issued shares. Issued shares may be fully or partly paid, and any non-cash consideration must be described and valued in accordance with the Companies Act 1993. No escrow account is required for share subscription funds; cash consideration is payable directly to the company's bank account. Distributions must satisfy the solvency test under section 52 of the Companies Act 1993.

New Zealand does not use a check-the-box entity election; classification and residency are determined statutorily. For the CK_IC the operative outcome is income-tax-exempt-zone treatment for offshore wholesale trading: under s 20 of the Income Tax Act 2007, a non-resident is not liable on income from a business of trading in goods where the business is carried on outside New Zealand and the goods are not sold or delivered in New Zealand, regardless of where contracts are signed. Protective and elective mechanisms include: (i) voluntary GST registration and the cash-basis election under s 19 of the GST Act, available to eligible businesses with turnover under NZD 2,000,000, permitting output tax to be accounted on receipts rather than invoices; (ii) election between invoice basis and payments basis under s 19(2)-(3); (iii) GST group registration under s 60 to consolidate associated NZ entities for VAT efficiency; (iv) hybrid-mismatch alignment elections under Part FM and s LA 6 of the Income Tax Act 2007 to neutralise imported hybrid mismatches and double-deduction outcomes arising from the Cook Islands entity's funding structure; (v) structural election-equivalent governance: because the Cook Islands is not a NZ 'grey list' jurisdiction for CFC purposes, the CK_IC should ensure board meetings and strategic management occur outside NZ to avoid resident-company status and CFC attribution; and (vi) no NZ permanent establishment election exists, but PE risk is mitigated by relying on the s 20 wholesale-trader exclusion and by ensuring no dependent agent, fixed place of business, or auxiliary-activity threshold is crossed under s 91AB.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised and freely convertible currency regime for the New Zealand Dollar (NZD). Both current-account transactions (trade, services, interest, dividends) and capital-account transactions (foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, loans, and repatriation of capital) are unrestricted for residents and non-residents without prior central bank approval. Profit remittances and dividend distributions by a non-resident-owned International Company in New Zealand are not subject to FX conversion ceilings or repatriation sequencing; the only material hurdles are tax compliance (non-resident withholding tax, 28% corporate rate for tax-resident entities, or an exempt-zero-rated regime under the specific CK_IC structure) and anti-money-laundering verification of the ultimate beneficial owner. The NZD is a free-floating currency, and there are no surrender, conversion, or retention restrictions on export proceeds. Practical friction arises exclusively from banking compliance, sanctions screening, and source-of-funds documentation rather than exchange-control regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation in New Zealand. Exporters are not required to convert foreign-currency earnings into NZD, nor are they subject to a prescribed repatriation timeline or minimum retention ratio. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand only imposes a statistical reporting duty for balance-of-payments purposes: resident corporate entities must report individual or aggregated transactions (including export settlements) exceeding NZD 10 million in a calendar month on the prescribed RBNZ form, and all trade settlement instructions must reference the correct customs export entry to enable alignment with the Customs and Excise Act 2018. For bulk Portland clinker exports, the commercial invoice, bill of lading, and port loading manifest must be retained for at least seven years to substantiate both the tax filing position and the bank's trade-surveillance record.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) as the national central bank, together with the New Zealand Treasury and the Financial Markets Authority for oversight of currency, balance-of-payments statistics, and financial market conduct. The Reserve Bank administers the Exchange Control regime solely for prudential and statistical purposes; it does not operate a discretionary foreign-exchange licensing system.

For a tax-exempt IC, enter zero or a nil imputation balance unless the IC has a New Zealand permanent establishment. File the imputation credit account annexure only if an ICA exists. Late filing of IR4 triggers a NZ$250 late-filing penalty and an additional NZ$50 penalty for non-electronic filing. Late payment triggers an initial 1% late-payment penalty, a further 1% after 7 days, and 1% per complete month thereafter. Use-of-money interest compounds on unpaid tax. Repeated non-filing can result in a Commissioner estimate, civil shortfall penalties, and prosecution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million - aggregate cross-border related-party transactions in an income year trigger mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation. Master File documentation and Country-by-Country Reporting are required for multinational groups with consolidated global revenue of at least NZD 1.3 billion, aligned to the OECD threshold.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no separate Economic Substance Regulations in the manner of offshore financial centres. The tax-exempt label of an International Company does not constitute an ESR certificate. Substance is tested through the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Commissioner's power to reconstruct artificial or sham arrangements. An IC used for bulk clinker ocean freight must demonstrate real chartering, cargo risk, invoicing, and decision-making; absence of commercial substance invites the Commissioner to reattribute profits to a New Zealand-resident enterprise.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax for evasion under Tax Administration Act 1994 s.141E, or 100% for an abusive tax position under s.141D, plus late payment penalties and use-of-money interest. Criminal conviction under TAA s.143B attracts a fine of up to NZ$50,000 for an individual, NZ$100,000 for a company, and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly procure or participate in false returns may be treated as principals or accomplices. Where the corporate veil is pierced, directors are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt, civil restitution, and criminal penalties; courts may impose director disqualification and travel bans where flight risk is established.
Shell Company and Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may disregard the IC under the sham doctrine or general anti-avoidance rule, reassess the group as a New Zealand-resident trader, impose corporate income tax at 28%, and apply shortfall penalties of up to 100%. No separate ESR penalty exists in New Zealand.

Director personal liability: Directors who trade recklessly in breach of Companies Act 1993 ss.135-137 are personally liable for losses caused. The corporate veil is pierced if the company was a façade or alter ego. Director liability may include compensation orders, disqualification under Companies Act s.382, and restriction on leaving New Zealand while criminal proceedings are pending.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Evasion and Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: The payer remains liable for the full NRWT not deducted, plus late-payment penalties and use-of-money interest. Civil shortfall penalties of up to 100% apply; willful non-payment is a criminal offence under TAA ss.143B, with fines up to NZ$100,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise a distribution without withholding tax are jointly responsible for the shortfall. If the company is insolvent, directors may be personally liable for breaching the Companies Act solvency-test provisions; criminal directors face disqualification, travel restraints, and personal asset confiscation.
AML / CTF and Trade Sanctions Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a reporting entity that knowingly or recklessly breaches its obligations is liable on conviction to a fine of up to NZ$2.5 million. Money laundering under Crimes Act 1961 s.243 carries imprisonment of up to 7 years. Tainted cargo and proceeds are subject to forfeiture under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or allow unlawful transactions may be prosecuted as parties. Individual fines, confiscation of personal assets, disqualification from managing companies, and temporary travel bans may be imposed. The corporate form does not shield directors where the company is used purely as an instrumentality of money laundering.
