Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand; online portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity obtains a New Zealand IRD number as a non-resident company by submitting Form IR742 (Application for Registration by a Non-Resident Company) to Inland Revenue, together with a certified certificate of incorporation and any required constitutional documents from the home jurisdiction. Following IRD number allocation, the entity may separately apply for GST registration via myIR using Form GST101A (Registration of Non-Resident Company) once the relevant New Zealand GST nexus threshold is met. The International Company (CK_IC) structure is treated as a non-resident company and may obtain a dedicated IRD number without maintaining a physical New Zealand registered office or resident director for tax registration purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone / Non-Resident Territorial Scope: The International Company is treated as outside the New Zealand full tax net; only New Zealand-sourced income is assessable, while foreign-sourced income remains exempt. No pass-through eligibility exists; the entity is taxed at entity level on New Zealand-sourced income only, with non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) or approved issuer levy applying to certain outbound payments, and the entity is not subject to New Zealand income tax on offshore business income derived from VPN/digital services sold to non-resident customers.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not offer a check-the-box or elective entity-classification regime comparable to the US. The default statutory classification of a company is a separate taxable entity. Available mechanisms include: (i) voluntary GST registration for non-resident suppliers of remote services even below the NZD 60,000 threshold in order to claim input tax credits; (ii) the remote-services registration mode under section 54B of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, which allows non-resident digital suppliers to GST-register solely for NZ-resident consumer supplies; (iii) hybrid mismatch adjustment elections under sections FH 1 to FH 14 of the Income Tax Act 2007 to neutralise double non-taxation outcomes; and (iv) the approved issuer levy election (2% instead of NRWT on offshore interest) for New Zealand-sourced interest paid to non-resident investors.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies made in New Zealand over any 12-month period; for non-resident suppliers of remote services (including VPN and digital privacy applications), the same NZD 60,000 threshold applies based on supplies made to New Zealand-resident consumers. Voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) goods physically exported from New Zealand within 28 days of supply (or within 60 days for hire-purchase or conditional sale supplies); (ii) services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed (section 11A, GST Act 1985); and (iii) remote or digital services, including VPN network privacy applications, supplied to a non-resident recipient where the recipient is not in New Zealand at the time of supply. The supplier must hold reasonable documentary evidence of the recipient's non-resident status, such as geolocation/IP data, registered business address, non-resident payment gateway country codes, or contractual declarations. Supplies of digital services to New Zealand-resident consumers are reverse situations and remain taxable at 15% GST under the remote-services regime.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUSDEC) lodged electronically in the Trade Single Window (TSW) for physical goods exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement out of New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or equivalent bank statement evidencing foreign currency settlement from the overseas customer
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order describing the digital nature of the VPN/privacy software service and confirming the non-resident status of the buyer for services supplies

New Zealand imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API transaction-screening mandate. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates on the Peppol interoperability framework with UBL 2.1/Peppol BIS 3.0 standards via accredited Access Points. GST returns are filed periodically through myIR (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly) and must be accompanied by a digital GST return data transmission; documentary retention obligations apply under section 51 of the GST Act 1985. No fiscal seal or real-time tax authority pre-approval of invoices exists.

Inland Revenue may retrospectively claw back zero-rated GST where the exporter fails to substantiate timely export, non-resident supply, or foreign currency repatriation within the evidenced timeframe. Misdeclaration triggers reassessments, GST shortfall penalties under Schedule 1 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 (up to 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or fraudulent intent), plus use-of-money interest under sections 120–124 of the TAA 1994. The reassessment period is generally four years, extended to seven years for fraudulent or knowingly incorrect GST returns. Knowingly providing false export documentation may constitute an offence under section 143B of the TAA 1994, carrying criminal penalties including imprisonment. All zero-rating substantiation records must be retained for at least seven years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalized foreign-exchange and capital-control regime. There are no exchange controls restricting current-account or capital-account convertibility, and no Reserve Bank approval is required for inbound capital injections, profit remittances, dividend distributions, royalty payments, or liquidation proceeds. Both residents and non-residents may freely transact in New Zealand dollars (NZD) and foreign currencies. The New Zealand dollar is a freely floating and freely convertible currency. The only relevant statutory gateways are sector-specific foreign-investment screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 for sensitive land and significant business assets, and general AML/CFT obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. For the tax-exempt CK_IC vehicle, profit repatriation and capital return are not subject to exchange-control permission, but must be supported by auditable corporate documentation, beneficial-ownership declarations, and source-of-funds evidence retained by the paying bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio apply to export receipts in New Zealand. Exporters are not obliged to surrender foreign-exchange proceeds to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand or to convert them into NZD within any prescribed period. Export proceeds may be credited directly to a foreign-currency account or an NZD account, and may be held, hedged, or converted at commercial discretion. The only reporting requirement is indirect and statistical: banks and financial institutions report international transactions for balance-of-payments purposes under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's International Transactions Reporting requirements, with no surrender, repatriation, or conversion condition attached.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua), established under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989; for the Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC), the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission performs financial-services licensing and AML/CFT supervision, while monetary and currency operations remain anchored to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand through New Zealand-dollar circulation and New Zealand-dollar clearing arrangements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High, but subject to legal constraints. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) may offer multi-currency virtual accounting and settlement through partner banks, and some UK/EU-established EMIs with New Zealand Financial Service Provider (FSP) registration or a New Zealand AML/CFT reporting entity can provide viable payment rails. However, an offshore EMI cannot independently represent a domestic New Zealand bank account number, cannot perform deposit-taking or lending without New Zealand regulatory authorizations, and must route NZD clearing through an established local banking partner or an NZD-clearing bank. The CK_IC treasury can use such EMIs for low-value operational flows from Stripe and Paymentwall, but should maintain a primary corporate account at a licensed New Zealand commercial bank for high-value capital movements, escrow arrangements, and hedge execution.
