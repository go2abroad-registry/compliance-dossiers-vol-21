Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up or issued share capital is required for a New Zealand limited liability company under s 38 of the Companies Act 1993. The company may be incorporated with nominal or even zero capital; no escrow account is mandated by the Companies Office. Share capital may be expressed in any currency. If the entity elects an 'International Company' tax classification, no additional capital contribution is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: If any physical e-sports venue or office is occupied, the building must hold a current Building Warrant of Fitness (BWoF) together with an annual fire evacuation scheme approved by Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017. The evacuation scheme must include a wardening structure and fire training logs. Structural load inspections: A chartered professional engineer must issue a structural load clearance certificate confirming compliance with NZS 1170.5 (earthquake) and AS/NZS 1170.1 (imposed actions), especially for esports spectator seating and server rack installations. Workplace clearance: WorkSafe New Zealand requires the company to provide a safe working environment under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015; for the digital workforce this includes an up-to-date remote-work health and safety policy, incident register, and first-aid arrangements; for any physical event operation, a workplace health and safety audit must be completed before opening to the public.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up or issued share capital is required for a New Zealand limited liability company under s 38 of the Companies Act 1993. The company may be incorporated with nominal or even zero capital; no escrow account is mandated by the Companies Office. Share capital may be expressed in any currency. If the entity elects an 'International Company' tax classification, no additional capital contribution is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (New Zealand Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake); taxpayer portal: myIR via ird.govt.nz; ancillary business registries: New Zealand Companies Office and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) register.

E-government filing portal

A foreign (non-resident) entity has no obligation to obtain a New Zealand IRD number unless it derives New Zealand-source income, carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand, or must register for GST. A non-resident company with New Zealand tax obligations applies for an IRD number using Inland Revenue form IR742 (Non-Resident Company Tax Registration), submitted to Inland Revenue by post or through the myIR portal; the same IRD number is used for GST registration (form IR356 or online myIR application). A non-resident supplier of remote/digital services to New Zealand consumers whose supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period must register for GST under the remote services rules using the simplified online registration in myIR. An entity incorporated in New Zealand under the Companies Act 1993 automatically receives an IRD number via the Companies Office issuance of the NZBN and Companies Register number, with the IRD number subsequently confirmed in myIR.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; entity tax status is determined by the Income Tax Act 2007. A New Zealand-resident close company (five or fewer natural person shareholders) may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status to be treated as a fiscally transparent pass-through entity for all New Zealand tax purposes. There is no standalone hybrid mismatch election; the BEPS hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory statutory provisions (Income Tax Act 2007, subpart FH) that deny deductions or include amounts in income where hybrid entities or financial instruments create mismatch outcomes. For the offshore International Company (CK_IC) in this mandate, the entity is classified as a non-resident company and is taxed on a territorial basis: it is liable to New Zealand income tax only on New Zealand-source income and is outside the New Zealand corporate tax net unless it has a New Zealand permanent establishment or its central management and control is exercised in New Zealand, in which case entity-level taxation at 28% applies, subject to any available LTC election for a qualifying resident close company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; the same NZD 60,000 annual threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of remote services (including digital and esports platform services) to New Zealand-resident consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for 0% GST under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when physically exported from New Zealand within the statutory timeframe and evidenced by customs documentation. Exported services qualify under section 11A (services to non-residents) or section 11B (remote services) where the supplier is GST-registered, the recipient is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, and the services are not supplied for consumption in New Zealand. Esports community platform services (tournament matchmaking, subscriptions, digital goods, and platform fees) supplied to non-resident players and communities are zero-rated as remote services where the supplier maintains contemporaneous evidence of the recipient's non-resident status (for example IP address, billing address, or payment method) and payment originates offshore.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Customs Service export entry evidencing physical export, classification, and value)
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent Air Waybill / through-shipping document evidencing dispatch from New Zealand)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank-issued confirmation of foreign currency proceeds received from the offshore purchaser)

No real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory pre-authorisation of invoices, and no digital fiscal stamping in New Zealand. GST is reported and paid through periodic returns lodged electronically via myIR (typically two-monthly, six-monthly, or quarterly depending on turnover). E-invoicing follows the voluntary Peppol framework; all New Zealand government agencies are required to be able to receive e-invoices, but private businesses have no mandatory e-invoicing obligation. Inland Revenue provides API-based e-services (IR e-services / Gateway) for software-to-Inland Revenue lodgement, but there is no transactional clearance or stamping requirement for individual invoices.

Inland Revenue audits zero-rated export claims and may require the full export documentation bundle. Failure to substantiate, or fraudulent claims, trigger reassessment of 15% GST on the supply; late payment penalties (1% per month or part-month under section 142A of the Tax Administration Act 1994); use-of-money interest; and shortfall penalties of 20% (unacceptable tax position), 40% (dominant purpose of tax avoidance), or 150% (abusive or evasive position). Criminal prosecution for evasion or intentional misstatement under section 143A may result in fines up to NZD 250,000 for a company and imprisonment of responsible officers, plus forfeiture and penalties under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 for false export documentation. Retrospective clawback extends across all open assessment periods, generally seven years, and may extend indefinitely where fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully convertible currency regime without exchange controls under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989. Both current account transactions (dividends, royalties, service fees) and capital account transactions (equity injections, loan repayments, acquisition of assets) are freely permitted, with no requirement for central bank approval for profit repatriation or liquidation remittance. The only relevant obstacles are tax compliance obligations (e.g., non-resident withholding tax on dividends and interest, and potential thin capitalisation rules) and standard AML/CFT customer due diligence, neither of which constitutes a currency convertibility barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. New Zealand imposes no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency in offshore or New Zealand foreign-currency accounts. For balance-of-payments reporting, reserve and monetary statistics reporting obligations attach to registered banks and certain financial institutions, not to the operating export entity.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for payment connectivity, Low for replacement of core banking. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Stripe, PayPal, and Wise Business are viable for collection and disbursement flows of the esports platform, but they cannot serve as the primary regulated deposit-taking institution for corporate treasury. Under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, any entity providing financial services in or to New Zealand must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and hold adequate dispute resolution arrangements. Offshore EMIs are therefore permitted to support payment processing only where they do not constitute unauthorised deposit-taking; capital reserves and statutory escrow balances must be held with a locally licensed New Zealand bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or quasi-equity injections must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the beneficial owner's or subscribing shareholder's account in a reputable jurisdiction. New Zealand does not prescribe a fixed statutory purpose code, but the payment narrative should clearly read 'Capital Contribution' or 'Shareholder Loan' and be accompanied by an internal company resolution, an executed subscription agreement, and a New Zealand Business Number reference where available. The receiving bank will apply enhanced KYCC and may require a FATCA/CRS self-certification (Form W-8BEN-E and CRS FFI form where applicable), as well as evidence of the source of funds. For amounts exceeding NZD 10,000, alack of a clear commercial purpose will trigger an internal suspicious transaction reporting obligation; a compliant capital injection file avoids this outcome.For international supplier and vendor payments, SWIFT MT103 is the standard clearing rail. For high-value or documentary trade settlements, commercial letters of credit (MT700) and documentary collection (MT400/MT422) are available through New Zealand banks using the messaging and settlement infrastructure underlying the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS). Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends paid by a New Zealand resident company to a non-resident shareholder; fully imputed dividends qualify for a reduced rate of 15%, and a double taxation agreement (DTA) may lower the rate further (e.g., 5% for a 10% corporate shareholder under the NZ-Australia DTA). In the Tax-Exempt-Zone International Company structure, distributions of offshore profits are not subject to New Zealand dividend NRWT when no New Zealand-source income is involved.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand has no statutory Local File/Master File monetary threshold filed with the tax return; the IRD's transfer pricing compliance guidelines require contemporaneous documentation for cross-border related-party transactions in the risk zone of NZ$10 million aggregate goods or NZ$3 million services/royalties/interest, while mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more (approximately NZ$1.3 billion).
