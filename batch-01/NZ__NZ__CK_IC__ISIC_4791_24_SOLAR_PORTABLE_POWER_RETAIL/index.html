Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake), New Zealand; digital portal: myIR via www.ird.govt.nz. Export verification authority: New Zealand Customs Service (Te Mana Ārai o Aotearoa) operating the Trade Single Window for export declarations.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident International Company must obtain a New Zealand IRD number before carrying on any New Zealand taxable activity. Procedure: (1) execute Form IR742 'Non-resident company IRD number application', attaching the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered office details, ultimate beneficial ownership particulars, and any New Zealand fixed establishment address; (2) file the application with Inland Revenue by post or through a registered tax agent; (3) upon IRD number allocation, register for Goods and Services Tax (GST) through myIR where taxable supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, or voluntarily if the offshore supplier elects the marketplace/operator collection model under the GST Act 1985; (4) maintain contemporaneous records of New Zealand consumer sales, payment gateway reports, cargo movements, and customs declarations. New Zealand does not issue a separate VAT identification number; the IRD number is the sole statutory taxpayer identifier for income tax, GST, employer obligations, and withholding taxes.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone - A Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC) that is neither incorporated in New Zealand nor has its head office or centre of management in New Zealand is a non-resident foreign company and is outside the New Zealand income tax net for foreign-sourced income (Income Tax Act 2007, ss YC 2-YC 14). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - A Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC) that is neither incorporated in New Zealand nor has its head office or centre of management in New Zealand is a non-resident foreign company and is outside the New Zealand income tax net for foreign-sourced income (Income Tax Act 2007, ss YC 2-YC 14). The entity is therefore tax-transparent only to the extent that distributions, interest, royalties, or New Zealand-source business income attributable to a fixed establishment attract non-resident withholding tax or limited source taxation. The entity operates within a territorial-scope exempt classification for offshore profits; GST registration is triggered separately by the NZD 60,000 threshold on supplies to New Zealand consumers, and the entity cannot claim pass-through eligibility for New Zealand tax purposes.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box entity classification election. Relevant statutory elections available to the CK_IC structure: (1) GST grouping election under section 55 of the GST Act 1985 for associated/non-resident enterprises; (2) GST cash-basis and payments-basis elections under sections 19A-21A of the GST Act 1985; (3) election by non-resident sellers of low-value goods to use the electronic marketplace operator collection model under sections 60B-60H of the GST Act 1985; (4) hybrid and branch mismatch neutrality elections and surplus-ordering elections under Subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 transposing OECD BEPS Action 2; (5) provisional tax and the option to use the GST ratio method for eligible small enterprises. Look-through company (LTC) elections under section HA 2 are unavailable because LTC status requires a New Zealand-resident close company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies to New Zealand consumers in any 12-month period; the threshold applies to resident suppliers, non-resident suppliers of remote services, and non-resident suppliers of low-value imported goods (individually valued at or below NZD 1,000) under the distance-selling and electronic marketplace operator rules, with compulsory registration once exceeded and voluntary registration available by election (GST Act 1985, ss 51-60H).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST applies to supplies of goods physically exported from New Zealand by a GST-registered supplier in the course of sale under section 11(1)(a) of the GST Act 1985, provided the goods are exported within 28 days of the supply and the supplier obtains and retains documentary evidence of export; zero-rating also applies to services supplied to non-resident recipients offshore under section 11A(1)(k). A non-resident offshore retailer selling portable solar power products to New Zealand consumers is not eligible to claim zero-rating for goods that never enter New Zealand; such sales constitute remote services or low-value goods subject to 15% GST once the NZD 60,000 threshold is met. Any zero-rating claim is substantiated by the statutory records and is subject to IRD audit verification of the end-to-end physical export chain.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window / Customs Export Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-exchange credit advice evidencing receipt of export consideration
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Purchase Order / Export Sales Contract
  • GST zero-rated supply records required under GST Act 1985 s 24F(5)
  • Container, packaging, and photographic evidence of export movement where applicable
  • Certificate of Origin and supplier declaration where claimed under trade agreements

No real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory digital stamping, and no continuous transaction control or API clearance regime exists in New Zealand. The IRD supports an optional Peppol e-Invoicing framework through the New Zealand Peppol Authority; tax invoices, credit notes, and debit notes must meet GST Act 1985 sections 24-24F, with simplified invoices permitted for supplies up to NZD 1,000 and buyer-created tax invoices by agreement. GST returns (IR372) and other filings are transmitted electronically through myIR with no tax authority pre-approval or fiscal stamping of individual invoices, but all records must be preserved in paper or electronic form for seven years.

The IRD may reassess GST and claw back the full 15% output tax where zero-rating is claimed without physical export within the statutory 28-day window (GST Act 1985, s 11(8)), imposing use-of-money interest (UOMI) assessed daily. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply to incorrect GST positions: 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for an abusive tax position, and 100% for evasion or deliberate suppression. Criminal sanctions under sections 143A-144B TAA expose the entity and its directors/principal officers to fines up to NZD 150,000 and imprisonment up to eight years for tax evasion or knowingly assisting evasion, with joint and several liability for company officers. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims also extend the reassessment period beyond the ordinary four-year time bar, expose the entity to deregistration of its IRD number, and may trigger publication of tax default disclosures and exclusion from the voluntary disclosure mitigation regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both current account and capital account transactions. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a free-floating fiat currency with no licensing, approval, or surrender framework. Dividend remittances, intercompany loan repayments, trade payments, and profit repatriations from the CK_IC are not blocked by foreign exchange law; the only hurdles are ordinary bank KYC/AML clearance and non-resident withholding tax obligations under the Income Tax Act 2007. The Overseas Investment Act 2005 is the sole capital screening regime and it applies only to sensitive land, national security lines, and significant business assets with an acquisition value of NZD 100 million or more per transaction; a direct online retail business distributing portable solar and battery products is below the screening thresholds and is outside the consent regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable in New Zealand. There is no statutory timeline for repatriating export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to surrender export receipts in NZD or foreign currency. A merchant may retain settlement funds in any convertible currency, maintain offshore accounts, and apply proceeds according to commercial terms. The only incidental duties are statistical: reporting banks file RBNZ IMAP (International Merchandise and Payments) aggregated returns for cross-border flows above NZD 1 million, and the underlying trade invoices must be retained for tax and customs verification. No export licence is required for portable solar generators or lithium battery systems, but transport documentation under UN38.3/IEC 62133 must accompany physical shipments.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) - the central bank, prudential supervisor, and final settlement operator for the New Zealand banking system. In the Realm of New Zealand framework, the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission regulates the Cook Islands International Company registry, but all banking, clearing, FX dealing, and liquidity operations for the CK_IC entity will be conducted through licensed New Zealand banks and therefore fall within the RBNZ regulatory perimeter, the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989, the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989, and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. New Zealand has no separate foreign exchange administration; exchange control was abolished in 1984.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 (annual aggregate of cross-border related-party transactions); above this threshold the Inland Revenue expects full OECD-aligned Master File and Local File contemporaneous documentation; below this threshold simplified documentation and benchmarking may be accepted.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime comparable to offshore financial centres. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, anti-conduit rules, and OECD BEPS-derived substance guidance apply; transactions or entities lacking economic substance may be recharacterised or disregarded for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax under section 141 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, plus interest; criminal conviction for tax evasion may attract imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or participate in the evasion can be prosecuted as principals; the corporate veil may be pierced if the company is a mere facade; Inland Revenue may issue a departure prohibition order where outstanding tax debt exceeds NZ$10,000.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may apply section GB 1 and the GAAR to reconstruct the arrangement, tax the profits as arising in New Zealand, and assert corporate tax residence in New Zealand if central management and control is exercised here; penalties up to 100% of the tax shortfall for an abusive tax position.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for unpaid tax under the Companies Act 1993 where the company traded recklessly or allowed insolvent trading; criminal exposure under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994 for assisting in tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue can adjust taxable income to an arm's-length amount, disallow excess deductions and interest, impose shortfall penalties (150% for evasion, 40% for an unacceptable tax position), and charge use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in setting intra-group pricing may be liable for signing incorrect returns; personal liability arises if they knowingly cause the company to enter a tax avoidance arrangement while insolvent; egregious cases may lead to criminal prosecution.
Failure to File / Return Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalties (NZ$50 per month up to NZ$250 maximum) plus late payment penalties of 1% immediately, 4% after seven days, and 1% per month thereafter; unpaid tax becomes a preferential debt and may be enforced through liquidation of the company.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unremitted GST and PAYE under the statutory director liability provisions of the Tax Administration Act 1994; repeated compliance failures can trigger management banning orders under the Companies Act 1993.
