Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not compliant as the sole registered office for a company in the commercial manufacturing sector. Section 19 of the Companies Act 1993 requires a physical address in New Zealand where documents can be served and which is open to the public. More importantly, the Ministry for Primary Industries will reject a virtual address for the mandatory Food Control Plan registration under the Food Act 2014, and the Inland Revenue will not accept it as the principal place of business for GST and provisional tax purposes. Therefore virtual office use is rejected for all licensing, tax, and banking purposes in this industry.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand law does not impose any minimum paid-up share capital, share premium, escrow, or blocked capital account requirement for a limited company. The board may issue shares for any lawful consideration determined by the directors; the shares in the Ltd carry no par value and must be fully paid in cash or assets as recorded in a share subscription agreement and share register.

Funding & unlock path: The facility must obtain a Compliance Schedule and Building Warrant of Fitness required by the Building Act 2004 after structural and fire-engineered inspections; a Fire and Emergency New Zealand fire safety and evacuation scheme approval covering means of escape, smoke detection, alarm and suppression systems; a Hazardous Substances location certificate under the Hazardous Substances and New Organisms Act 1996 if cleaning chemicals, refrigerants or other substances are stored; and a WorkSafe New Zealand health and safety clearance covering plant and machinery guarding, electrical safety, personal protective equipment, occupational exposure limits and an emergency response plan.

For a non-resident company intending to carry on business or create taxable supplies in New Zealand: (1) incorporate or register as an overseas company with the Companies Office to obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). (2) Apply for an IRD number (tax file number) using form IR596 (Company IRD number application) or through myIR if already registered as a company. (3) Provide certified copies of constitutional documents, director details (passport, proof of address), and evidence of the New Zealand business relationship (e.g., client contracts, HSBC etc.). (4) Once the IRD number is issued, the company must register for GST if taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period (or can voluntarily register from the first day of supply?). (5) For non-resident companies that are not carrying on a physical business in New Zealand but supply remote/digital services to NZ consumers, the offshore supplier registration path via myIR is available. (6) If the entity already has a New Zealand tax presence, the IRD number can be requested via the 'Non-resident company' registration section; no tax residency election is permitted without actual substantive economic presence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 gross taxable supplies (both goods and services) made in New Zealand in any 12-month period, mandatory once exceeded (GST Act 1985, s.51). Voluntary registration is permitted at or above NZD 60,000; supplies under this threshold are not subject to GST unless registered. Non-resident suppliers of remote services (including e-commerce, digital services) are subject to the same threshold under the offshore supplier registration regime for GST on cross-border services supplied to NZ-resident consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (including freeze-dried pet raw meat) are zero-rated under s.11(1) of the GST Act 1985 if the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply. The supplier must hold the goods in New Zealand at the time of supply and either export them in their own name or deliver them to an offshore recipient (with part of the contract performance occurring in NZ). Services directly connected with the exported goods (e.g., freight forwarding, cold-chain logistics) and certain offshore services under s.11A may also be zero-rated. For zero-rating to apply, the supplier must retain sufficient evidence that the goods have physically crossed the New Zealand border and are delivered outside the customs territory, including proof of export within the statutory timeframe.

  • Customs Export Declaration (submitted via New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window – export entry code and manifest document)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing carriage from New Zealand to the overseas destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by the NZ supplier
  • Bank foreign exchange / inward remittance credit advice (bank-generated reference) showing payment receipt from the overseas buyer, as substantiation of the cross-border transaction (note: New Zealand does not issue an FIRC because no exchange controls apply; the bank's SWIFT credit note serves as equivalent evidence)

No real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates on the PEPPOL (Pan-European Public Procurement On-Line) network via IRD-endorsed software, using the NZ e-Invoice standard (NZ TePai). GST returns and other indirect tax filings must be submitted electronically through myIR or certified software; there is no prior tax authority authorisation of individual invoices or continuous transaction controls. However, taxpayers must maintain robust electronic records of all issued and received invoices for at least 7 years.

If zero-rating is claimed but fails to meet the statutory export test (including the 28-day physical export requirement and evidence sufficiency), the GST shortfall becomes assessable immediately, triggering use-of-money interest from the original filing due date. Additionally, a shortfall penalty of 20% applies for an unacceptable tax position or 40% for any gross carelessness; deliberate falsification of export documents attract a 150% penalty for evasion, plus criminal penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 (up to NZD 250,000 fine and/or imprisonment). The IRD may claw back any refund for unsubstantiated export zero-rating for the previous seven years, and the Customs and Excise Act 2018 imposes separate penalties for false export declarations. Proper retention of the customs export declaration, bills of lading, and remittance records is essential to defend any audit and to sustain the zero-rating position.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a liberal and fully convertible cross-border currency regime with no separate centralised foreign-exchange control authority and no requirement for transaction-by-transaction exchange approval. The New Zealand dollar floats freely, and both current-account and capital-account transactions are effectively open to residents and non-residents. Capital injections, intercompany loans, trade settlements, dividend distributions, and direct investments may be converted at any registered New Zealand bank at market rates without surrender or repatriation restrictions. There is no statutory obligation to convert export proceeds into New Zealand dollars, and no restriction on holding foreign-currency account balances. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand oversees prudential stability and bank liquidity, while transaction monitoring is driven by AML/CFT reporting obligations rather than exchange controls. The only meaningful impediments to profit remittance are standard corporate governance formalities: dividend declarations must satisfy the solvency test under the Companies Act 1993, and non-resident withholding taxes on dividends and interest apply unless reduced by a relevant double-taxation or treaty provision. Accordingly, the principal FX planning focus for the New Zealand freeze-dried pet food manufacturing entity is not conversion permission but liquidity management, hedging discipline, and compliance with bank conduct screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender regime exists in New Zealand. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign-currency export proceeds in licensed bank accounts, convert at their election, and repatriate funds freely. There is no statutory timeline for conversion and no mandatory conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua) acting as the central bank and prudential regulator, with exchange administration functions administered under the Companies Act 1993 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to Medium with significant legal caveats. New Zealand does not issue a dedicated electronic money institution licence; non-bank providers must be incorporated or registered as financial service providers under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and must comply as reporting entities with the AML/CFT Act 2009. Multinational EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and OFX can support payment origination in NZD, cross-currency conversion, and certain automated trade settlements, but they cannot provide indigenous operating account services with credit facilities, documentary collections, import letters of credit, or pre-export finance. For a capital escrow requirement and a physical-goods manufacturing treasury, offshore EMIs should be supplementary rails only, never the primary corporate banking arrangement, because most EMIs lack local clearing governance, disaster recovery, and confirming-bank relationships needed for ISIC 1080 export contracts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity contributions should be routed through a single cleared SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's account to the New Zealand entity's NZD operational account, with the remittance field clearly referencing the share allotment resolution and company code. The endorsement should be limited to capital contribution and equity injection to avoid misclassification as revenue. Because New Zealand has no foreign-exchange control approval requirement, banks will not issue a separate approval stamp; instead the bank's AML/CFT team will validate the corresponding account identifiers, screen the counterparty, and match the inbound funds to the company's issued capital register. Once the funds are cleared, the shareholders must pass a written resolution, record the allotment in the Companies Office register, and ensure the chartered accountant provides a capital verification certificate confirming the funds have been received and are at risk in the business.For operating imports of raw meat, freeze-drying consumables, and packaging, the company should use SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for standard supplier payments under 30 to 60 day terms. For large equipment or bulk commodity orders where the exporter requires bank risk mitigation, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued by the New Zealand bank, typically SWIFT MT700, with payment against presentation of a full set of clean on-board bills of lading plus commercial invoice and sanitary-phytosanitary certificates, is the appropriate rail. Documentary collections may be used for lower-risk agricultural inputs, but given the supply chain sensitivity and potential for customs disputes in meat shipment, an LC or MT103 with matching pre-shipment documentation offers better treasury traceability and audit support.Plain vanilla deliverable foreign-exchange forward contracts in NZD/USD, NZD/AUD, NZD/EUR, and NZD/CNH corridors with licensed New Zealand banks for committed purchase orders.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on Asian currencies such as CNY, IDR, and VND where onshore full deliverability is limited, provided the underlying is a genuine trade disbursement and the bank supports hedge effectiveness documentation under NZ IFRS 9.Cross-currency swaps executed with approved trading counterparties to convert fixed-rate foreign-currency debt into NZD obligations, subject to internal treasury policy and Companies Act solvency confirmation.Short-dated foreign exchange options or collars may be permitted for known purchase orders, but speculative trading and uncovered forward rolls are outside the standard low-risk underwriting appetite for a freeze-dried pet treat manufacturer.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Medium with significant legal caveats. New Zealand does not issue a dedicated electronic money institution licence; non-bank providers must be incorporated or registered as financial service providers under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and must comply as reporting entities with the AML/CFT Act 2009. Multinational EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and OFX can support payment origination in NZD, cross-currency conversion, and certain automated trade settlements, but they cannot provide indigenous operating account services with credit facilities, documentary collections, import letters of credit, or pre-export finance. For a capital escrow requirement and a physical-goods manufacturing treasury, offshore EMIs should be supplementary rails only, never the primary corporate banking arrangement, because most EMIs lack local clearing governance, disaster recovery, and confirming-bank relationships needed for ISIC 1080 export contracts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border related-party transactions exceeding NZD 1,000,000 in aggregate for an income year trigger the expectation of contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation; Country-by-Country reporting applies where the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds the NZD equivalent of EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation statute. However, substance is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule in Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1, specific anti-avoidance provisions, and treaty beneficial ownership rules. A manufacturing company must demonstrate genuine premises, employees, equipment, inventories, and strategic decision-making in New Zealand; otherwise Inland Revenue may recharacterise transactions, disallow treaty benefits, or cancel abusive arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False Manufacturing Records

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty of 150% of the tax shortfall under Tax Administration Act 1994 s 141; criminal conviction for tax evasion carries a fine of up to NZD 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years; use-of-money interest is charged on the unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who know of, promote, or participate in evasion may be prosecuted as parties; they face disqualification from company management, civil restitution orders, asset freezing, and travel bans pending investigation.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue adjusts consideration to an arm's length result under Income Tax Act 2007 ss GC 1–GC 14; shortfall penalties of 20%–40% apply for an unacceptable interpretation, rising to 100% for an abusive tax position, with use-of-money interest on the reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incorrect returns or knowingly approve sham pricing may incur personal liability for shortfall penalties and may be disqualified; director penalty regimes also apply to unpaid PAYE and GST connected with the company's operations.
Shell Company – No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: General anti-avoidance rule s BG 1 voids the arrangement; tax avoidance penalties up to 100% apply; denied treaty benefits; the company may be removed from the Companies Register after enforcement action.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for company debts where they permit the company to incur obligations with no reasonable prospect of performance; courts may lift the corporate veil and impose civil restitution, management bans, and travel restrictions.
AML/CFT Failure on Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: AML/CFT Act 2009 civil penalties up to NZD 2,000,000 per breach for a body corporate; criminal penalties of up to NZD 200,000 and/or 2 years imprisonment for serious non-compliance; infringement fees also apply.

Director personal liability: Senior officers and directors may be held personally liable for serious or repeated AML/CFT breaches, including substantial fines and disqualification from acting as company directors.
Unreported NRWT on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The payer company is liable for the unpaid NRWT, late payment penalties, use-of-money interest, and a shortfall penalty of up to 150% in cases of intentional non-declaration; base erosion payment rules may also deny deductions for the outbound payment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally assessed for NRWT that was deducted from payments but not paid to Inland Revenue; serious cases may trigger travel bans, asset freezing, and civil recovery proceedings.
