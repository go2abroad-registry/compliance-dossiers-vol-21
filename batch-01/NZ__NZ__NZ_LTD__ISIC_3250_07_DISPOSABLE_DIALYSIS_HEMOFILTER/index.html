Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue), Government of New Zealand – official online portal: www.ird.govt.nz and myIR secure online services

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity establishing a New Zealand limited company (NZ Ltd): (1) incorporate the company with the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993 and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN); (2) apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number via the online company incorporation process, using Form IR596 (Company/Entity IRD Number Application), or through the myIR portal; (3) register for Goods and Services Tax (GST) if the value of taxable supplies exceeds or is expected to exceed the NZD 60,000 registration threshold, or if taxable activities are commenced with the intention of doing so; (4) a non-resident incorporated foreign company operating through a New Zealand fixed establishment must similarly obtain an IRD number and assess NZ GST registration obligations on the same basis. The IRD number is the sole tax identification number used for all NZ tax purposes, including income tax, GST, PAYE, and withholding taxes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign entity establishing a New Zealand limited company (NZ Ltd): (1) incorporate the company with the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993 and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN); (2) apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number via the online company incorporation process, using Form IR596 (Company/Entity IRD Number Application), or through the myIR portal; (3) register for Goods and Services Tax (GST) if the value of taxable supplies exceeds or is expected to exceed the NZD 60,000 registration threshold, or if taxable activities are commenced with the intention of doing so; (4) a non-resident incorporated foreign company operating through a New Zealand fixed establishment must similarly obtain an IRD number and assess NZ GST registration obligations on the same basis. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style ‘check-the-box’ election exists under New Zealand law. The Look-Through Company (LTC) election is available to qualifying closely held companies under Income Tax Act 2007, sections HB 3–HB 11, and allows income, expenses, and tax credits to flow through to shareholders. The former Qualifying Company (QC) regime is closed to new elections. Hybrid mismatch rules and related anti-avoidance provisions under Subparts TY and TZ of the Income Tax Act 2007 apply to cross-border arrangements, restricting choices designed to create double non-taxation or inconsistent tax outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (per 12-month rolling period) for taxable supplies made in New Zealand; a similar threshold applies to non-resident suppliers selling low-value imported goods in certain circumstances; registration may also be required on a transaction-by-transaction basis for imported services under reverse-charge rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods may be zero-rated under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the goods are exported by the supplier as part of a sale, or are exported within 30 days of the time of supply, and the supplier retains sufficient evidence of exportation. Certain offshore services supplied to non-resident recipients are zero-rated if the services are supplied in the course or furtherance of a taxable activity and are not directly in connection with land in New Zealand or goods situated in New Zealand at the time the services are performed (or, for intellectual property, the rights are not exercised in New Zealand). In addition, supplies of exported goods to non-residents, temporary importation repair services, and some business-to-business cross-border services may qualify. The supplier must hold documentary evidence of exportation and payment (including inward foreign currency remittance evidence) to support any zero-rating or refund claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUSEXP) – New Zealand Customs Service export entry
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (airfreight) evidencing the movement of goods from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Transfer Notification evidencing payment of the exported supply, including foreign-currency receipts

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance system, digital stamping, or government API clearance is imposed by Inland Revenue. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Peppol framework (Peppol BIS Billing 3.0). GST returns are filed electronically via myIR, typically on a monthly or two-monthly cycle. While procurement transactions with New Zealand government agencies may require e-invoicing, this is a commercial/administrative requirement, not a tax clearance requirement. Invoices must contain prescribed details under sections 24-24D of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

Inland Revenue and the New Zealand Customs Service jointly scrutinise export zero-rating claims. Where export documentation is incomplete, goods are not actually exported within the statutory timeframe (30 days for most goods), or the supplier cannot substantiate receipt of payment, the GST deemed zero-rated is treated as taxable at the standard 15% rate. Penalties may be imposed under the Tax Administration Act 1994, including shortfall penalties ranging from 20% (lack of reasonable care) to 100% or 150% in cases of abusive tax avoidance or evasion, plus use-of-money interest on unpaid GST from the due date. Criminal prosecution is possible for knowingly providing false or misleading statements. Inward remittance evidence and export declaration records must be retained for a minimum of 7 years to withstand audit review and to avoid retrospective clawback of GST refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible in both current and capital account transactions under IMF Article VIII. There are no exchange-control restrictions on inward equity injection, shareholder loans, profit repatriation, dividend distributions, royalty remittances, interest payments, or trade settlements. Non-resident ownership of a New Zealand limited company is generally unrestricted, and no foreign-investment consent is required under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 (as amended) for an ordinary manufacturing subsidiary unless the entity holds 'sensitive land' (farm land, beachfront, or conservation land), fishing quota, or significant business assets above statutory thresholds (currently approximately NZD 100 million). A dialysis-hemofilter extrusion and potting plant financed by tangible equipment, working capital, and IP licences will ordinarily fall outside Overseas Investment Office (OIO) screening, provided the enterprise is not purchasing sensitive land and the gross business-asset value remains below the statutory trigger. Dividend and interest outbound remittances are freely transferable, subject only to non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at treaty- or domestic-validated rates, which is a tax compliance matter rather than an FX matter.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion obligation on export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts onshore or offshore, without any timeline mandate and without any requirement to convert foreign currency into NZD at a central-bank-determined rate. The only mandatory elements are (i) income-tax recognition and reporting: export earnings must be translated to NZD for Inland Revenue (IRD) assessment under applicable New Zealand accounting standards and foreign-currency translation rules, and reported in the company's income tax return; and (ii) anti-money-laundering compliance: receiving banks apply standard CDD to large inbound credits, requesting the underlying sales contract, invoice, and shipping evidence as a commercial due diligence measure, not as a forex-control measure.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), exercising banking supervision and financial-stability oversight under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 (as amended), together with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for registered-bank conduct and anti-money-laundering supervision under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. New Zealand maintains no separate foreign-exchange-control authority; the Reserve Bank sets prudential policy, but no prior exchange-control approval is required for cross-border payments, capital movements, or trade settlements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

New Zealand does not maintain a dedicated electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime comparable to the UK FCA EMI framework. Fintech payment providers operating in New Zealand must register as reporting entities under the AML/CFT Act 2009 and, if assumption of deposits exceeds the exclusion threshold, as non-bank deposit takers under the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Currencycloud may lawfully service NZ entities through partnership arrangements with licensed domestic banks, but they cannot provide a primary NZD account accessing the Reserve Bank's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) or the domestic clearing network. Viability rating for the NZ_LTD dialysis operation: Medium for client collections in USD/EUR and small-value supplier payments; Low for capital-escrow structures, confirmed letters of credit, documentary collections, and high-value trade settlements, which require a registered-bank relationship. Legal constraint: many Asian and EU counterparty banks will reject remittances to an EMI-based settlement account because the EMI lacks deposit-taking authority and a standard SWIFT BIC with full settlement membership; LC and trade-finance documentation customarily require a domestic bank to act as issuing, confirming, or presenting bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders fund the NZ subsidiary by SWIFT MT103 wire into the Capital Escrow Account maintained with ANZ, BNZ, or Westpac. The remittance must carry a clear purpose narrative, e.g., 'Contribution to share capital of NZ_LTD' or 'Shareholder loan to NZ_LTD', to enable end-to-end traceability under the AML/CFT Act and to satisfy the receiving bank's source-of-funds due diligence. The paying bank applies OFAC/EU/UN sanctions screening, and the receiving bank will request supporting documents for any credit above NZD 10,000. Standard proof bundle: executed share subscription agreement, board resolution allotting shares or approving the loan, notarised certificate of incumbency for the overseas lender/shareholder, and the overseas bank's credit advice demonstrating the corresponding debit. If the funds are equity, they must remain escrowed until the Companies Office registration of the share allotment is confirmed; if debt, the loan agreement is stamped and registered in New Zealand for thin-capitalisation tracking. No OIO consent or FX approval is required for an equity or loan injection into a manufacturing entity, unless the entity acquires sensitive land or the asset-value threshold is exceeded. The New Zealand receiving bank also issues a transaction confirmation to be filed by the auditor for IRD financial-transaction reporting.Raw-material imports (hollow-fibre polyethersulfone membrane, polyurethane potting compound, filter housings, tip caps, and sterilisation packaging) are cleared through one of three rails: (1) confirmed irrevocable commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700) opened by the NZ issuing bank in favour of the Asian or EU supplier, with a beneficiary confirmation and a full set of compliant documents; (2) SWIFT MT103 wire payment under a documentary collection (D/P or D/A) when the supplier presents transport documents through a collecting bank; or (3) a revolving LC structure for recurring monthly purchases, reducing the per-shipment document burden and securing more favourable supplier pricing. For medium-term supply arrangements, the NZ bank may offer a Bank Payment Obligation (BPO) on the SWIFT Trade Services Utility or a standby LC guarantee as a substitute for a conventional LC. The settlement protocol is strictly documentary: invoice, packing list, bill of lading or air waybill, certificate of origin, ISO 13485 certificate of conformance, and the beneficiary's signed non-embargo declaration. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A New Zealand company is compulsory-audited if it is a 'large company' under section 200 of the Companies Act 1993 (total assets at balance date exceed NZ$60 million and total revenue for the accounting period exceeds NZ$30 million, in each case unless the company is a subsidiary of a reporting entity); an audit is also compulsory for FMC reporting entities (issuers, managers of registered schemes, licensed insurers, non-bank deposit takers) under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013; for a private manufacturing SME producing disposable dialysis hemofilters, a statutory audit is not required merely by ISIC 3250 licensure, but a shareholder with at least 5% of the votes or 5% of the paid-up capital may require an audit by ordinary resolution under section 199, and the entity's articles may impose audit requirements. ISO 13485 certification audits for the medical device quality system are separate from the statutory audit.
