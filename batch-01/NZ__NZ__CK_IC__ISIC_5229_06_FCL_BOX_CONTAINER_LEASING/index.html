Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is required under the Companies Act 1993. A company may be incorporated with nil or nominal paid-up share capital. No escrow account, capital deposit, bank reference, or statutory capital trustee arrangement is required to proceed with incorporation. A company limited by shares must, however, comply with the solvency test under section 4 of the Act for any subsequent share buy-back, financial assistance, or distribution.

Funding & unlock path: For any physical operations in New Zealand: (i) obtain a Building Warrant of Fitness and any required fire safety compliance schedule for structures or container stacking areas that exceed building code thresholds, approved by the territorial authority and Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ); (ii) ensure structural load certification of any racking, gantries, or container stack foundations by a chartered professional engineer, demonstrating capacity under the Building Act 2004 and relevant NZS standards; (iii) provide a workplace health and safety management system under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, with worker engagement, hazard registers, and emergency procedures; (iv) where workers undertake height, lifting, or confined-space tasks, a written safe method statement (SSP) and trained supervision must be in place; (v) hold a valid employer ACC cover and workers' compensation insurance; and (vi) display the approved fire evacuation scheme where a worksite is occupied. These clearances are inapplicable to the pure container leasing and dispatch model with no physical New Zealand site.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is required under the Companies Act 1993. A company may be incorporated with nil or nominal paid-up share capital. No escrow account, capital deposit, bank reference, or statutory capital trustee arrangement is required to proceed with incorporation. A company limited by shares must, however, comply with the solvency test under section 4 of the Act for any subsequent share buy-back, financial assistance, or distribution.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - Non-resident International Company with no New Zealand permanent establishment and no New Zealand-sourced income falls outside the territorial ambit of New Zealand income tax; profits from offshore container leasing are not derived from NZ and are exempt from NZ income tax, subject to transfer pricing and the non-resident contractor provisions.

Available election forms

No check-the-box pass-through election is available under New Zealand law; eligible entities may elect for consolidated group income tax treatment under subpart IG of the Income Tax Act 2007, enter into loss grouping elections, or invoke the limited exemption for non-resident leasing income; hybrid mismatch rules under subpart FH disallow deductions for payments with double non-taxation outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 annual turnover in a 12-month period (mandatory), with voluntary registration available below threshold; non-resident suppliers making supplies to NZ consumers may be subject to remote services GST under the 'rules for cross-border supplies' on a reverse-charge or marketplace basis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated for GST when exported by the supplier within 28 days of the supply and the goods are permanently exported from New Zealand under section 11(B) of the GST Act 1985; services are zero-rated if supplied substantially to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed (offshore services), including the leasing/renting of goods for use solely outside New Zealand, subject to the B2B non-resident and 'directly in connection with land or moveable property in NZ' exclusions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs CS or approved electronic equivalent) confirming goods physically departed from New Zealand
  • Duly issued Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing foreign consignee and overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign currency conversion statement / banking credit evidence) showing payment received from offshore counterparty in NZD or freely convertible foreign Exchange within the statutory deadline

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system; e-invoicing is voluntary and interoperable via the Peppol network, with IRD providing the 'e-Invoicing' capability; taxpayers must retain electronic transaction records and file GST returns by prescribed filing dates via myIR; no government API pre-clearance is required for invoice issuance.

Incorrect GST refunds arise where documentation for zero-rating is incomplete or goods are diverted to the domestic market; the Commissioner of Inland Revenue may reassess GST at 15% within four years (or up to eight years for fraudulent evasion), impose late payment penalty of 1% + 1.5% monthly, charge use-of-money interest, and apply criminal penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 for knowingly furnishing false documents, including fines up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; export fraud also triggers clawback of the refund plus penalties under section 141EB.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised and freely convertible currency regime. Since the abolition of exchange controls in 1984, both current-account and capital-account transactions have remained unrestricted. There are no quantitative limits, no approvals for profit repatriation, and no restrictions on currency conversion for dividends, lease income, or capital returns. The only practical hurdles are (i) non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) of 15% on gross dividends and royalties (potentially reduced to 0% under an applicable double-tax agreement upon delivery of a New Zealand IRD tax-residency certificate), (ii) OIO consent where the investment involves sensitive land, fishing quota, or significant business assets above the statutory thresholds, and (iii) standard AML/CFT enhanced-due-diligence documentation for large USD/EUR flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender timelines and no mandated conversion ratios. New Zealand exporters may retain export proceeds indefinitely in foreign currency or convert them freely at licensed dealer rates. The only administrative duty is statistical reporting: any single transaction (or monthly aggregated flow) exceeding NZD 1 million must be reported in the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Balance-of-Payments surveys referenced against the bank's purpose-code classification, and the underlying export contract or lease schedule must be retained for AML/CFT audit.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) in tandem with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) as joint AML/CFT supervisor of registered banks, and the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) for foreign-investment screening thresholds.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for pure offshore digital EMIs. Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, any non-resident EMI carrying on money-remittance activities must register as a reporting entity (supervised by DIA or FMA), maintain a New Zealand agency or physical presence, and obtain a licensed bank sponsor for settlement. Without this, tier-1 banks will refuse pass-through payment access, and the EMI cannot access NZClear or the NZD settlement system. A compliant alternative is to appoint a locally licensed non-bank payment provider as the settlement agent.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% for non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends paid by a New Zealand company to a non-resident shareholder. A 33% resident withholding tax rate applies to domestic dividends; imputation credits and supplementary dividend rules may reduce the economic burden for New Zealand companies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a standard 31 March balance date, the IR4 must be filed by 7 July following the end of the income year. Terminal tax is due by 7 February of the following year. Provisional tax instalments are due on 28 August, 15 January, and 7 May. Non-standard balance date taxpayers must apply the equivalent statutory dates prescribed by Inland Revenue.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ Threshold: NZD 1 million aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions in an income year triggers a requirement to prepare and retain transfer pricing documentation in accordance with Inland Revenue's Transfer Pricing Guidelines. A separate Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) is required for New Zealand-resident ultimate parents of multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million (or NZD equivalent).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply a standalone economic substance statute of the type used in some offshore centres, but economic substance is nevertheless central to New Zealand tax residence and anti-avoidance analysis. An International Company will be treated as New Zealand-resident if it is incorporated in New Zealand, or if its management and control are exercised in New Zealand. Transactions without commercial substance may be recharacterized or reconstructed under the general anti-avoidance rule (section BG 1), the sham doctrine, and specific transfer pricing and interest limitation rules. For container leasing, the company must demonstrate genuine offshore decision-making, risk-bearing, and operational activity; mere use of a dormant shell in a tax-exempt zone while managing the fleet from New Zealand will risk full New Zealand taxation and penalty exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994: on conviction, a company may be fined up to NZD 250,000; officers may be imprisoned for up to 5 years and fined up to NZD 50,000. Civil shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the resulting tax shortfall apply, together with late payment penalties and use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers can be prosecuted personally, with imprisonment up to 5 years. Inland Revenue may seek director penalties for unpaid withheld taxes and civil restitution. Convicted directors may be disqualified from managing companies under the Companies Act 1994.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the company as a New Zealand tax resident, resulting in tax at 28% on worldwide income. Application of the GAAR may recharacterize or reconstruct transactions to deny the tax benefit. Shortfall penalties range from 20% for failure to take reasonable care to 100% for an abusive tax position, plus interest and late payment penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in creating or maintaining a sham structure may be jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax shortfall penalties. Repeated non-compliance can lead to disqualification orders and criminal sanctions if the conduct amounts to evasion.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may adjust the taxable income to the arm's length amount under sections GC 6 to GC 8 of the Income Tax Act 2007. Shortfall penalties can be imposed at 20% to 100% depending on culpability. In egregious cases, criminal penalties for evasion may also apply. Failure to prepare required transfer pricing documentation may increase penalties and reverse the burden of proof.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign non-arm's-length agreements can be held personally liable for penalties and interest to the extent they directed or acquiesced in the transaction. In serious cases, they may face criminal charges and be barred from serving as directors.
Failure to Withhold NRWT and Unfiled IR4

Statutory sanction: The full NRWT amount becomes recoverable from the New Zealand payer, along with late payment penalties of 1% initially, an additional 4% after seven days, and 1% per month thereafter, plus use-of-money interest. Failure-to-file penalties accrue at NZD 250 per return and per continuing 30-day period. Wilful non-compliance can lead to criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors can become personally liable for unremitted NRWT and other withholding taxes under the Tax Administration Act 1994. The personal liability attaches regardless of whether the company is subsequently placed into liquidation, and imprisoned where the failure was known and deliberate.
Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 may reach NZD 2 million for a body corporate for each serious non-compliance, and NZD 200,000 for an individual officer. Wilful or reckless breaches can trigger criminal liability. Additional actions include licence suspension, business registration cancellation, and asset restraint orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal financial penalties and criminal prosecution for recklessly or knowingly allowing the business to be used for money laundering. Regulators may seek disqualification of directors and travel bans, and imprisonment of up to 2 years can be imposed for serious offences.
