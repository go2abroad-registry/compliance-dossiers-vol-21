Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the CK International Company, being non-resident under the Income Tax Act 2007 and operating under an exempt offshore charter, is excluded from New Zealand entity-level income taxation in respect of all offshore-sourced commercial litigation revenues; New Zealand income tax applies only to income derived from sources in New Zealand under the territorial rules of sections YD 4 and YD 4B, and no look-through or flow-through election is deemed to apply. The entity is not a tax resident of New Zealand and has no New Zealand permanent establishment in respect of its cross-border litigation chambers activity.

Available election forms

New Zealand self-assessment law does not contain a 'check-the-box' classification election. Elective mechanisms available to this taxpayer: (a) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 — available only to companies with qualifying New Zealand-resident shareholders and therefore not available without restructuring of this offshore IC; (b) grandfathered Qualifying Company (QC) election — closed to newly incorporated entities; (c) hybrid and branch mismatch elections under subpart FH (Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018), including a compensating adjustment election where the IC is treated as fiscally transparent in the counterparty jurisdiction; (d) GST zero-rating election for exported services and remote services under section 11A(1)(k), (l) and (m) of the GST Act 1985; (e) option to voluntarily register for GST below the NZD 60,000 threshold in order to claim input tax credits and issue zero-rated invoices.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any 12-month period (section 51 GST Act 1985); a separate compulsory registration threshold of NZD 60,000 per annum applies to non-resident suppliers of remote services supplied to New Zealand residents (sections 8B and 60 GST Act 1985); voluntary registration permitted below the threshold; once registered, the supplier must remain registered for a minimum of two years unless registration is cancelled by the Commissioner.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST applies: (1) to goods physically exported from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply (section 11(1)(a) GST Act 1985); (2) to services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed, unless supplied directly in connection with land in New Zealand or moveable personal property situated in New Zealand at the time of supply (section 11A(1)(k)); (3) to remote services supplied to a non-resident while the recipient is in New Zealand (section 11A(1)(l)); and (4) to services supplied in connection with moveable personal property situated outside New Zealand (section 11A(1)(m)). Cross-border commercial litigation services — arbitration advocacy, offshore court appearances, legal research, asset tracing, expert testimony, and document review — supplied to an offshore client qualify for zero-rating where the recipient is a non-resident, the services are not connected to New Zealand land or New Zealand moveable property, and the supplier holds contemporaneous evidence of the offshore nexus; the supplier must be GST-registered and may recover input tax attributable to the zero-rated supplies through the ordinary refund mechanism.

  • Customs export declaration (Trade Single Window entry with New Zealand Customs Service clearance status and unique consignment reference) — applicable where physical exhibits or evidence items are exported as part of the litigation engagement and subject to the 28-day export rule
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods from New Zealand to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign-currency receipt advice (SWIFT MT103) evidencing receipt of consideration from the offshore client account
  • Executed litigation engagement letter and fee agreement demonstrating the non-resident status of the recipient and the offshore situs of performance

  • Itemised GST tax invoice issued under section 24 GST Act 1985 showing zero-rated supplies and citing the statutory basis under section 11A(1)(k), (l), or (m)
  • Detailed schedule of services performed outside New Zealand, including arbitration venues, hearing dates, travel records, and confirmation that no supply was made in connection with New Zealand land or New Zealand moveable personal property

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transactional invoice validation regime. The Government has mandated Peppol-compliant e-invoicing for central government agencies on the buy-side since 17 March 2022; private-sector suppliers may voluntarily adopt e-invoicing through certified Peppol service providers using the four-corner interoperability model. GST tax invoices issued under section 24 of the GST Act 1985 may be created, transmitted, and retained electronically, but must contain the prescribed particulars including the supplier's name and registration number, date of issue, description and quantity of the supply, consideration, and the amount of GST or a statement of zero-rating; there is no Inland Revenue API pre-clearance or unique invoice authorisation code, and record-keeping must enable the Commissioner to verify output tax and zero-rating claims on audit.

For the avoidance of doubt, New Zealand law does not prescribe a statutory foreign-currency repatriation window; the 180-day period stated above is applied as the contractual and evidentiary benchmark for confirming receipt of export consideration in zero-rating audits. Inland Revenue audits zero-rated export claims under the record-keeping and anti-avoidance provisions of the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the GST Act 1985. Where the zero-rating condition fails or the export documentation is deficient, the supply is re-characterised as a taxable supply at 15% GST, output tax becomes due from the time of supply, use-of-money interest runs under section 120 TAA 1994, and input credits are retrospectively clawed back. Civil shortfall penalties escalate by culpability: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or an arrangement intended to defeat the tax laws (section 141 TAA 1994). Late payment penalties accrue at 1% on day 8 and 4% on day 15 after the due date, followed by 1% per month (section 139A TAA 1994). Criminal prosecution under section 143A TAA 1994 for evasion or similar offences carries penalties of fines up to NZD 50,000 for an individual and NZD 250,000 for a body corporate, and imprisonment up to five years; absolute-liability offences under section 143B carry fines up to NZD 25,000 for an individual and NZD 100,000 for a company. Fraudulent export zero-rating schemes further expose the entity and its directors to disqualification proceedings, referral to the New Zealand Law Society under the Lawyers and Conveyancers Act 2006, and loss of practising certificates, in addition to the full monetary clawback and penalty exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the IMF Article VIII framework. There are no surrender, approval, repatriation, or licensing requirements for inward investment, operating profits, professional fees, litigation settlement proceeds, or shareholder distributions of an International Company. The Overseas Investment Act 2005 applies only to the acquisition of sensitive land or significant business assets above prescribed thresholds, neither of which is engaged by a commercial litigation chambers conducting ISIC 6910 multi-jurisdictional arbitration and asset tracing. Profit remittance hurdles are nil; funds held in an IOLTA trust account or operational account may be converted freely at licensed dealing banks, and there is no differential treatment for non-resident shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export receipts or export of services, including cross-border legal and arbitration services. Exporters and legal service providers may retain receipts in any convertible currency, hold net open FX positions within bank prudential limits, or convert at any licensed dealer without Reserve Bank approval. The only incidental obligation is statistical: financial institutions report balance-of-payments transactions to Statistics New Zealand for settlements typically at or above NZD 50,000, but this does not restrict the exporter and requires no central-bank filing by the International Company.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) is the central bank and prudential supervisor of registered banks, operating the high-value payment and settlement system (ESAS). The Financial Markets Authority (FMA) is the conduct and market regulator under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013. The Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) of the New Zealand Police supervises suspicious transaction reporting and AML/CFT intelligence. New Zealand abolished dedicated exchange-control legislation in 1984 with the repeal of the Exchange Control Act 1953; foreign exchange is liberalised, the NZD is fully floating, and the Reserve Bank intervenes only in systemic-stress or financial-stability scenarios.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as an ancillary FX and cross-border payment gateway; LOW as the primary trust or escrow clearing bank. New Zealand does not operate a bespoke electronic money institution licence category. A digital EMI or money-transfer provider must register on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and become a reporting entity under the AML/CFT Act 2009 with an approved AML/CFT programme. Offshore EMIs cannot directly access the Reserve Bank's ESAS settlement system or the domestic ACH clearing infrastructure, so same-day NZD trust disbursements require a sponsorship arrangement with a licensed registered bank. For IOLTA-grade client money, an unlicensed offshore EMI is legally constrained from acting as core custodian because professional trust funds must be held with an authorised deposit-taker under the Lawyers and Conveyancers Act 2006 where the chamber operates as a licensed practice.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must enter the IC's Capital Escrow or Operational Account via the SWIFT network (MT103) or through a correspondent bank routing into a major New Zealand bank's NZD cover account. The remitter must use the beneficiary's registered legal name and address, and include an ISO 20022 purpose code appropriate to the transaction (e.g., INTC for intra-group capital injection or BEXP where treated as business funding) in the structured remittance line; the free narrative must reference the board resolution date and capital-injection purpose. Same-day NZD settlement is achieved through ESAS; value-same-day crediting occurs once the cover confirmation is received. Bank-side verification under FATF Recommendation 16 requires full originator name, address, and account number; for first-time credits above NZD 50,000, the bank typically demands a certified board resolution, share-register or capital-allotment documentation, and a source-of-wealth statement before releasing funds from a hold period.Outgoing clearing rails are fourfold: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border payments to foreign counsel, expert witnesses, enforcement agents, settlement escrows, and court-parking accounts; (ii) ESAS high-value NZD payments and domestic ACH bulk for local operating disbursements; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600/ISP98) as a documentary conditional release mechanism where escrow-triggered settlement conditions must be satisfied; and (iv) the IOLTA trust disbursement rule, whereby every payment requires a written trust disbursement instruction, matter reference, beneficiary purpose code, and supporting invoice or award, and no transfer from trust to general operating account occurs without an approved billing tax invoice. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as an ancillary FX and cross-border payment gateway; LOW as the primary trust or escrow clearing bank. New Zealand does not operate a bespoke electronic money institution licence category. A digital EMI or money-transfer provider must register on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and become a reporting entity under the AML/CFT Act 2009 with an approved AML/CFT programme. Offshore EMIs cannot directly access the Reserve Bank's ESAS settlement system or the domestic ACH clearing infrastructure, so same-day NZD trust disbursements require a sponsorship arrangement with a licensed registered bank. For IOLTA-grade client money, an unlicensed offshore EMI is legally constrained from acting as core custodian because professional trust funds must be held with an authorised deposit-taker under the Lawyers and Conveyancers Act 2006 where the chamber operates as a licensed practice.
