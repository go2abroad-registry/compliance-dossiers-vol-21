Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a private company limited by shares under the Companies Act 1993. At least one share must be issued; shares carry no par value and may be issued fully paid, partly paid, or for non-cash consideration determined by directors. No escrow account or bank capital deposit is required for a non-deposit-taking professional services company. The incorporation application must specify the share classes (if any), the maximum number of shares issuable, and each shareholder's initial share subscription.

Funding & unlock path: Civil defence and occupational clearance is confined to the registered office premise: a current Building Warrant of Fitness (BWOF) and Annual Fire Safety Certificate must be maintained pursuant to the Building Act 2004 and the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017 for any leased professional office; WorkSafe New Zealand compliance under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 requires a documented worker participation system, hazard register, emergency evacuation procedure, and first-aid provision for all employees operating the virtual CFO platform. No further structural load, industrial equipment, or hazardous-substance clearance is required for the virtual professional-services classification.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) / Te Tari Taake, Wellington, New Zealand. Secure online portal: myIR at ird.govt.nz. For a Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC) relying on offshore tax-exempt status, the separate Cook Islands tax administration handles the exemption, but all New Zealand income tax, GST, PAYE, and withholding tax registrations and filings are administered by the New Zealand IRD.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated or non-resident entity, including an International Company (CK_IC), is not automatically registered for New Zealand tax. The entity must apply for a New Zealand IRD number if it derives New Zealand-sourced income, makes taxable supplies subject to New Zealand GST, employs workers in New Zealand, or becomes tax resident through central management and control being exercised in New Zealand. The standard procedure is: (1) confirm that the entity is not incorporated in New Zealand and therefore not automatically registered under the Companies Act 1993; (2) obtain the entity’s certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered office details, director details, and proof of non-resident status; (3) submit Form IR596 or use the non-resident application pathway in myIR; (4) for GST, file a separate registration application through myIR or the GST registration form; (5) if no New Zealand address is available, appoint a New Zealand tax agent; and (6) use the resulting IRD number for all New Zealand tax obligations, including GST, PAYE, and non-resident withholding tax. A CK_IC that earns only foreign-source income and has no New Zealand compliance nexus is not required to obtain an IRD number solely by reason of being an International Company.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone. The CK_IC International Company is an offshore, non-resident limited liability vehicle: it is not resident in New Zealand for income tax purposes unless its central management and control is in New Zealand. It is not a pass-through entity and not an ordinary New Zealand resident company. It is not subject to New Zealand corporate tax on non-New-Zealand-source income. A New Zealand-incorporated ordinary company carrying on the same outsourced virtual CFO activity would instead be an Entity_Level_Taxation resident company subject to 28% income tax on worldwide income. If New Zealand-resident shareholders control the CK_IC, the New Zealand controlled foreign company rules may attribute certain passive income to those shareholders despite the entity-level tax exemption.

Available election forms

New Zealand has no US-style check-the-box election. Entity classification follows legal form, place of incorporation, and tax residence. Available elections relevant to a New Zealand tax filing entity include: (a) look-through company (LTC) election under subpart HG of the Income Tax Act 2007 for qualifying New Zealand resident companies; (b) consolidated group election for New Zealand resident companies; (c) GST grouping and joint GST registration elections under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; (d) voluntary GST registration for non-residents making or intending to make taxable supplies in New Zealand; (e) provisional tax payment frequency options; and (f) adjustment and documentation positions under the hybrid mismatch rules in Part FH of the Income Tax Act 2007. A CK_IC cannot elect out of the New Zealand CFC attribution rules through a check-the-box mechanism, and the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 remains applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000. Compulsory GST registration arises when a person carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand and the value of taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any 12-month period exceeds NZD 60,000. A non-resident supplier of remote or digital services supplied to New Zealand resident consumers is also required to register when total supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period. Voluntary registration is available below the threshold if the supplier is carrying on a taxable activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods supplied in New Zealand may be zero-rated if the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand, normally within 28 days of supply, and retains contemporaneous export evidence. Services may be zero-rated under section 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the recipient is a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, and the services are not supplied directly in connection with New Zealand land, not supplied directly in connection with goods situated in New Zealand, and not supplied to a person resident in New Zealand at the time of performance. For outsourced virtual CFO services, zero-rating requires evidence that the client is a non-resident, the services are received and enjoyed offshore, and the supply is not consumed in New Zealand. Cloud-based delivery, use of Stripe or Wise, and an offshore contract are supporting factors but are not dispositive if management, staff, or service performance are in New Zealand.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Customs Service) for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment out of New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of offshore consideration
  • Signed service agreement or engagement letter identifying the non-resident client
  • GST-compliant tax invoice or e-invoice describing the exported services and the offshore recipient
  • Service delivery records, workpapers, IP logs, meeting records, and project files demonstrating offshore performance and foreign receipt of the services

New Zealand does not currently impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API clearance, or digital stamping requirement for e-invoices. Electronic invoicing is accepted and the New Zealand Government participates in the Peppol network, requiring government agencies to receive e-invoices from suppliers. The IRD does not operate a pre-clearance gateway; instead, GST-registered persons must issue compliant GST invoices, maintain complete electronic or paper records, and keep those records for seven years. E-invoice content must satisfy the GST invoice requirements, and the integrity, readability, and accessibility of the electronic record must be preserved.

The IRD may audit zero-rated export supplies to confirm physical export of goods or offshore receipt and enjoyment of services. If the supporting documentation is inadequate or the export or non-resident condition fails, the supply becomes standard-rated at 15%, the taxpayer must account for output GST, and any GST refund previously claimed is recoverable with use-of-money interest. Shortfall penalties apply under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, up to 40% for an unacceptable tax position, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. False or fraudulent zero-rating claims may also lead to criminal prosecution. A tax-exempt International Company whose substantive activity is in New Zealand, or whose New Zealand resident controllers use the exemption to avoid tax, may additionally be challenged under section BG 1 and the CFC attribution rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalized, free-floating exchange-rate regime with no exchange-control legislation. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unconditionally convertible; there are no prior-approval, licensing, quota, or repatriation-restriction mechanisms applicable to NZD or foreign-currency operations. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, intercompany loan servicing, principal repayments, and liquidation proceeds may be repatriated freely to any jurisdiction without central-bank consent, subject only to standard transaction-monitoring obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and the internal treasury policy of the correspondent bank. As a tax-exempt Cook Islands International Company (exempt under the Cook Islands International Companies Act 2011-12), the CK_IC faces no exchange-control overlay beyond the limitations, if any, contained in its own trust deed or instrument of incorporation and the NZD remains fully convertible against USD, EUR, GBP, and AUD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. New Zealand abolished all exchange controls following the 1984 currency float, and no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation applies to export receipts, foreign-currency earnings, or invoice collections. The CK_IC may retain export-derived service fees indefinitely in USD, EUR, GBP, or any other acceptable currency in multi-currency accounts, or convert them to NZD at prevailing market rates without filing any return to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand or the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission. Expatriation of funds is equally unrestricted.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the sole monetary authority and operator of the NZD settlement-asset system (Exchange Settlement Account System, ESAS) under which all NZD clearing and settlement for the International Company's treasury accounts occurs; the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) serves as the registry-tier corporate regulator of the International Company (CK_IC), while the New Zealand Financial Markets Authority (FMA) supervises the financial service providers used by the IC; the NZD is the functional and reporting currency of the IC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High — Wise (formerly TransferWise) is the preferred fintech/EMI partner: it is registered as a Financial Service Provider in New Zealand (FSP number 336766) and operates as a non-bank money remitter holding funds via safeguarding arrangements with Tier-1 NZ banks, with the ability to issue a NZD local bank-account number (BBAN) and execute SWIFT MT103 outbound USD/EUR/GBP payments. Airwallex and Payoneer are viable secondary EMIs for collecting virtual-CFO subscription fees and paying international subcontractors. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not licensed banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989; therefore funds held with Wise/Airwallex are not 'protected deposits' under the Depositor Compensation Scheme and carry counterparty credit risk; (ii) under the AML/CFT Act 2009, the EMI must apply enhanced due diligence (EDD) to a tax-exempt offshore International Company, which typically requires the CK_IC to provide certified registry documents from the Cook Islands Financial Services Development Authority (FISDA), a source-of-funds declaration, and evidence of the beneficial-ownership chain; (iii) the Cook Islands' removal from the FATF grey list in October 2024 has materially reduced onboarding refusals, but residual reputational caution remains at tier-1 banks and legacy compliance teams; (iv) the CK_IC must maintain a Cook Islands registered agent and a New Zealand nexus (management/control in Auckland or Christchurch) to satisfy both the EMI's jurisdictional policy and the bank's substance expectations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The subscribing shareholder (e.g., a Hong Kong or Singapore holding company) effects the capital injection as a SWIFT MT103 wire to the CK_IC's NZD operational account at ANZ Bank New Zealand or BNZ. The payment narrative must read 'Equity contribution — share subscription' (no mandatory purpose code is prescribed by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand, but the bank's internal form requires a standard reason-for-payment selection). For USD or EUR injections, the receiving bank applies a same-day spot conversion into NZD at the prevailing interbank rate plus a 2–3 pip margin after deduction of SWIFT correspondents' charges; treasury policy should instruct 'OUR' for all inward charges. No Overseas Investment Office (OIO) approval is required because the IC does not acquire 'sensitive land' or 'significant business assets' within the meaning of the Overseas Investment Act 2005, s. 12. The complete verification bundle comprises: a notarized board resolution approving the allotment, the share allotment register updated with FISDA, the Certificate of Incumbency, a bank reference from the sending bank, and a source-of-funds declaration; the bank may additionally require the trust deed if the shareholder is a trust vehicle.Operational disbursements (subcontractor fees, software subscriptions, bank charges, and lease payments) clear through: (i) the Payments NZ High-Value Clearing System (HVCS) and Bulk Electronic Clearing System (BECS) for domestic NZD payments; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border USD/EUR/GBP supplier and contractor payments, preferably via SWIFT GPI for real-time tracking; and (iii) Wise Business multi-currency rails with local-nostro conversion for low-value recurring technology subscriptions (Xero, QuickBooks, Stripe, and Notion). Dividend distributions to the shareholder execute via SWIFT MT103 with narrative 'Dividend distribution — IC Act 2011-12', supported by a signed solvency certificate and a board distribution resolution; no withholding tax applies because the CK_IC is tax-exempt and non-resident in the Cook Islands, and New Zealand source taxation does not arise on distributions made by a foreign IC to foreign shareholders. Commercial letters of credit are not commercially standard for virtual CFO services; where a client requires bank-assurance, a standby letter of credit (SBLC) or documentary collection (documents against payment) is available from ANZ or Westpac at issuance fees of 50–150 basis points per annum, but the IC should discourage LC usage in its standard terms. Treasury policy requires a minimum one-month NZD liquidity buffer of not less than NZD 50,000 in the operational account to cover payroll, statutory filing, and clearing obligations, and prohibits same-day disbursement of received funds pending 24-hour KYC-funds verification.Outright deliverable FX forward contracts in NZD/USD, NZD/EUR, and NZD/GBP for maturities of 1–12 months, executed under a 2018 ISDA FX Definitions master agreement or the New Zealand Financial Markets Association (NZFMA) standard terms; quoted at 20–30 basis points annualized for major NZD pairs; no Reserve Bank approval required.FX swaps and roll-over (tom-next, spot-forward, and forward-forward) for temporary liquidity conversion between NZD and USD/EUR without triggering a realized gain or loss for treasury reporting.Cross-currency swaps (CCIRS) for structural hedging of a multicurrency intercompany loan or shareholder advance, including principal and interest exposure, with settlement through the Tier-1 bank's derivatives desk.European-style vanilla FX options (calls, puts, straddles, and zero-cost collars) on NZD/USD and NZD/EUR, available once the IC executes an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Credit Support Annex (CSA) and posts the required margin.Natural hedging through multi-currency account pooling and notional cash-pooling (available at Westpac and ANZ), converting only the net residual multicurrency position at the monthly treasury close.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on NZD are legally permissible but commercially inadvisable and not used in New Zealand because the NZD is fully convertible and deliverable; the deliverable forward market offers materially tighter spreads and eliminates settlement risk; any proposal to execute an NDF should be rejected by the IC's treasury policy.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High — Wise (formerly TransferWise) is the preferred fintech/EMI partner: it is registered as a Financial Service Provider in New Zealand (FSP number 336766) and operates as a non-bank money remitter holding funds via safeguarding arrangements with Tier-1 NZ banks, with the ability to issue a NZD local bank-account number (BBAN) and execute SWIFT MT103 outbound USD/EUR/GBP payments. Airwallex and Payoneer are viable secondary EMIs for collecting virtual-CFO subscription fees and paying international subcontractors. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not licensed banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989; therefore funds held with Wise/Airwallex are not 'protected deposits' under the Depositor Compensation Scheme and carry counterparty credit risk; (ii) under the AML/CFT Act 2009, the EMI must apply enhanced due diligence (EDD) to a tax-exempt offshore International Company, which typically requires the CK_IC to provide certified registry documents from the Cook Islands Financial Services Development Authority (FISDA), a source-of-funds declaration, and evidence of the beneficial-ownership chain; (iii) the Cook Islands' removal from the FATF grey list in October 2024 has materially reduced onboarding refusals, but residual reputational caution remains at tier-1 banks and legacy compliance teams; (iv) the CK_IC must maintain a Cook Islands registered agent and a New Zealand nexus (management/control in Auckland or Christchurch) to satisfy both the EMI's jurisdictional policy and the bank's substance expectations.
