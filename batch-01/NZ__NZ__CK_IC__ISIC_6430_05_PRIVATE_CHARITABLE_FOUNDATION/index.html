Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand; official portal at ird.govt.nz with the myIR secure online services platform for entity registration, GST returns, and tax status maintenance.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity (e.g., a Cook Islands International Company) obtains a New Zealand IRD number by filing Form IR742 (Non-Individual IRD Number Application) via myIR or the IRD paper channel, submitting the overseas Certificate of Incorporation, constitutional documents, certificate of registered agent and registered office, ultimate beneficial ownership and controller declarations, and an explanation of expected New Zealand source income or taxable activity; IRD issues a unique 8-9 digit IRD number which also serves as the GST registration identifier where the entity elects or is required to register for GST. Where no New Zealand source income or taxable activity exists, the entity may remain IRD-numbered but dormant and non-filing, subject to annual notification obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: Non-resident International Company engaged exclusively in charitable endowment management and grant-making; not resident in New Zealand under s YB 3 of the Income Tax Act 2007 (not incorporated in NZ, no NZ head office or centre of management, no control exercised by NZ directors); no New Zealand-sourced income expected; eligible for the charitable purpose income tax exemption to the extent NZ-source income arises under Income Tax Act 2007 sections CW 36 to CW 42, otherwise territorial scope taxation applies only to NZ-sourced income.

Available election forms

New Zealand has no US-style check-the-box regime. Entity classification follows Income Tax Act 2007 subpart HA (eligible entities), under which the International Company is mandatorily a company for tax purposes. Available elections: (a) look-through company (LTC) election under s HB 1 for NZ-resident close-held companies, inapplicable to this non-resident IC; (b) GST group registration election under Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 s 55 where two or more companies carrying on a taxable activity jointly elect; (c) joint venture GST election under s 55B. Hybrid mismatch disallowance rules under Income Tax Act 2007 sections FH 1 to FH 16 operate automatically as mandatory anti-avoidance overrides and cannot be elected away; no elective hybrid mismatch relief is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (annual taxable turnover threshold; registration is compulsory once supplies made in New Zealand exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period or there are reasonable grounds to expect the threshold to be exceeded; voluntary registration is permitted for non-residents at any time).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 s 11 applies to: (1) goods exported by the supplier within 28 days of supply, with the mandatory Customs export entry record; (2) supplies of services to non-residents who are outside New Zealand at the time of supply under s 11A, where the services are not in connection with New Zealand land or goods situated in New Zealand; (3) remote services supplied to non-resident private consumers under s 8(4B); and (4) certain supplies relating to the Chatham Islands. A charitable endowment foundation's grants and donations are generally outside the GST charging provisions as they are not consideration for a supply, while investment management services supplied to non-resident philanthropic beneficiaries may qualify as zero-rated offshore services if all s 11A conditions are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged via the New Zealand Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand with port of departure and consignee details
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a New Zealand registered bank or approved overseas bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency consideration
  • Signed commercial invoice and contract of supply contemporaneous with the exportation
  • Chain-of-custody documentation where goods pass through third-party logistics providers

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in New Zealand. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and operates on the Peppol BIS 3.0 interoperability framework administered through e-invoicing.govt.nz; the Inland Revenue Department does not operate an API-based clearance gateway and accepts GST returns solely through myIR or standard gateway services such as Xero and MYOB; taxpayers using e-invoicing must preserve digital records and audit trails under s 75 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and s 22 of the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Export zero-rating is substantively scrutinised under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 s 11 and the Tax Administration Act 1994 shortfall penalty framework under sections 141A to 141K. Where goods do not in fact leave New Zealand within 28 days or services fail the non-resident criteria, the supply is re-characterised as taxable at 15% and the supplier must return output tax. Clawback of input credits previously claimed occurs under GST Act s 21, and interest is charged at the prescribed use-of-money interest (UOMI) rate. Culpable conduct penalties range from 20% of the tax shortfall for failure to take reasonable care up to 150% for evasion, and directors' personal liability can arise under Tax Administration Act 1994 s 149. Additionally, Customs and Excise Act 2018 offences carry fines up to NZD 15,000 for false export declarations. A taxpayer claiming zero-rating for services supplied to associated non-residents must satisfy the enhanced anti-avoidance and 12-month repatriation substantiation requirements, with statutory clawback in default.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised capital and current account regime. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible with no exchange control laws, no RBNZ approval requirement for foreign exchange transactions, and no restrictions on profit, dividend, capital, or grant repatriation. A private charitable foundation may freely receive foreign endowment capital and disburse grants abroad without exchange-control consent. The only operational constraints are bank-driven reporting duties: Balance of Payments (BoP) statistical reporting and AML/CFT prescribed transaction reports (PTRs) for cash or wire transactions above NZD 10,000. The Overseas Investment Act 2005 (OIA) screening applies only where a non-resident acquires sensitive land or significant business assets, which is generally inapplicable to passive endowment management and grant-making.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists in New Zealand. There is no obligation for exporters, or for any entity receiving foreign currency, to convert receipts into NZD or to remit proceeds to the RBNZ. Exporters and charitable foundations may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in offshore or onshore foreign-currency accounts, repay external debt, or convert at any freely negotiated bank rate.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ; Te Pūtea Matua) — the sole monetary authority and prudential regulator for the New Zealand banking system, with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) as co-supervisors of AML/CFT for financial service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-LOW for core treasury functions. Offshore digital EMI platforms may operate in New Zealand only by registering on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and becoming reporting entities under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. They cannot provide settlement finality, escrow grade custody, or SWIFT membership. Tier-1 NZ banks also treat unregulated EMIs as third-party processors and may restrict flows to or from them. For a private charitable foundation with endowment and grant distribution needs, a direct relationship with a licensed NZ bank is strongly preferred; an EMI is viable only for minor operational foreign exchange or low-value beneficiary payments, and only if the EMI proves full AML/CFT equivalence and transparent reconciliation of underlying beneficiaries.

Defense evidence: 12 months of donor bank statements; executed endowment, trust, or gift agreements; donor KYC and identity evidence; New Zealand Charities Registration certificate (or equivalent exempt status); tax clearance certificates from the donor's home jurisdiction; and a narrative memorandum explaining the commercial and philanthropic rationale.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound endowment capital, donations, and grants must be routed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers using the foundation's full legal name, registered NZ address, account number, and receiving bank SWIFT/BIC. The remitter must enter a purpose code recognised for non-trade transfers, e.g., 'GIFT' or 'Charitable Endowment Contribution', classified under New Zealand's Balance of Payments as 'other private sector transfers'. The receiving bank will apply prescribed transaction reporting (PTR) for amounts above NZD 10,000 and may request pre-submitted source-of-funds documentation for larger injections. For inbound transfers exceeding NZD 10,000, the foundation should provide donor KYC files, the endowment deed, and source-of-wealth evidence in advance. No RBNZ consent is required, but if the transfer is linked to acquisition of New Zealand sensitive land or significant business assets, an OIA screening application must be made.Outbound grant disbursements and trade-linked settlements use the following rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct international wire transfers via a New Zealand Clearing House member bank; (ii) the domestic leg settles through the RBNZ-operated high-value Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS); (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) through a tier-1 NZ bank for any goods-based scientific or charitable procurement. Every outbound transfer must include the beneficiary name, bank, SWIFT/BIC, and a purpose code such as 'Grant Distribution' or 'Charitable Contribution'. Standing-payment templates and batch processing are available for recurring grants.
