Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department of New Zealand (Te Tari Tāke) – operational online portal 'myIR' (https://www.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity conducting taxable activities in New Zealand (including a non-resident company carrying on business through a fixed establishment or deriving New Zealand-sourced income) must register for a New Zealand IRD number (tax file number). For GST purposes, non-resident businesses may register for GST if they supply goods or services in New Zealand. Registration is effected by filing the prescribed application with Inland Revenue via myIR or paper form IR742; for GST registration, Form IR322 may be used for non-residents. The application requires the entity's legal name, jurisdictional place of incorporation, registered office and agent particulars in New Zealand, intended business activities, and estimated turnover. The IRD number is issued upon successful validation; GST registration takes effect from the date specified in the application or the date liability arises, and a GST registration number is issued separately. Non-resident companies becoming liable to income tax in New Zealand should also complete company IRD registration and may need to appoint a New Zealand agent for service of process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

A foreign company not managed and controlled in New Zealand is generally treated as non-resident for New Zealand income tax purposes, subject to New Zealand tax only on New Zealand-sourced income and certain controlled foreign company (CFC) attribution rules under the Income Tax Act 2007. No general check-the-box election akin to the US regime exists; however, an entity may make elections regarding the recognition of losses under the CFC rules, the deemed tax credit election for foreign imputation credits, and residency alignment elections under dual-resident company provisions. Hybrid mismatch rules under Subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 may require the New Zealand entity or its investors to make adjustments where an offshore entity is treated as transparent in one jurisdiction and opaque in another; the statutory mechanism includes the requirement to file disclosure forms (IR547) and to apply the primary and defensive hybrid mismatch rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in New Zealand in any 12-month period, or from the first day a person intends to make taxable supplies in New Zealand and reasonably expects to exceed NZD 60,000 in the ensuing 12 months; non-resident suppliers of 'remote services' to New Zealand consumers must register once annual supplies exceed NZD 60,000 (same threshold), with an optional low-value goods registration threshold of NZD 60,000 for goods supplied to New Zealand consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a GST-registered person may zero-rate supplies of goods that are exported from New Zealand by the supplier, or supplied for shipping outside New Zealand, provided the goods are exported as part of a supply to a non-resident recipient and documentary evidence of export is retained. Services supplied to non-resident persons who are outside New Zealand when the services are performed are generally zero-rated as 'remote services', unless the services are supplied to a New Zealand resident or the services are supplied in connection with land or moveable property situated in New Zealand. Zero-rating also applies to the supply of a going concern, ongoing services to international airlines, and certain internationally connected transactions. The supplier must obtain and retain proof of export and evidence that the recipient is a non-resident; the zero-rating claim can be allocated to the relevant GST return period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Customs Service – Export Declaration / Export Entry via TSW system)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment of goods from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or certificate of foreign exchange receipt evidencing payment from the non-resident recipient

New Zealand does not currently mandate a real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance regime for GST purposes. A business-to-business (B2B) e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol standard is voluntary and operated through accredited access points. GST-registered persons must issue a tax invoice within 28 days of the time of supply and must retain sufficient electronic records; non-resident suppliers registered under the GST remote services regime may issue simplified tax invoices. There is no government API-based real-time clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance authorisation for invoices, although Inland Revenue may require electronic lodgement of GST returns via myIR or gateway software.

Export zero-rating is subject to Inland Revenue audit scrutiny, requiring contemporaneous documentary evidence that the goods have been physically exported within a reasonable time (normally within 28 days of supply, or within 60 days where supply is arranged by the recipient) and that the supply was made to a non-resident. Where the statutory conditions are not satisfied, GST is payable at the standard 15% rate and penalties and interest may be imposed under the Tax Administration Act 1994: late payment penalties of 1% per month on unpaid amounts plus imputed interest of approximately 9.23% (updated quarterly), shortfall penalties ranging from 20% (not taking reasonable care) to 150% (fraud or evasion), and a criminal fine up to NZD 50,000 for evading GST. A clawback may be assessed by amending the relevant GST return within the four-year statutory assessment period, or indefinitely in cases of fraud or gross negligence; the Commissioner may also issue an assessment for unpaid GST and tax shortfall penalties with retrospective effect to the time of supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the New Zealand Dollar (NZD) on both current and capital account dimensions, with no applicable exchange control legislation since the revocation of the Foreign Exchange Control Regulations in 1984. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repatriation for a New Zealand International Company are freely permissible without central bank approval, subject only to ordinary company law solvency tests and any applicable non-resident withholding tax or thin capitalization rules under the Income Tax Act 2007. The non-resident-owned entity is not subject to any currency conversion mandate, and the NZD is freely tradeable in both onshore and offshore markets. Limitations on foreign investment arise solely under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 for transactions involving 'sensitive land' or 'significant business assets' (the latter only relevant where the entity acquires such assets, not for the entity's basic establishment or ongoing foreign shareholder funding).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender regime. No mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no obligation to sell foreign currency earned from digital content distribution, licensing, or in-app purchase settlement into NZD. Exporters and digital-service sellers, including the vertical micro-drama streaming operator, may freely retain foreign currency earnings in offshore nostro accounts or onshore foreign-currency-denominated accounts at registered banks. The only compliance burden is statistical: registered banks must report cross-border transactions to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand and Statistics New Zealand under the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position reporting framework; however, the entity itself has no direct filing duty unless it qualifies as a reporting enterprise under the Financial Reporting Act 2013.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021; foreign exchange administration is decentralized without a dedicated FX control bureau, while registered banks and licensed financial service providers are supervised primarily by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand for prudential conduct and by the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for market conduct and disclosure obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High with legal constraints. Registered non-bank financial institutions operating as electronic money institutions (EMIs) in New Zealand, including Wise (formerly TransferWise) NZ, Airwallex NZ, OFX, and Ebury, offer multi-currency IBAN wallets and rapid settlement with valid Financial Service Provider (FSP) registration under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and as reporting entities under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. These EMIs provide viable treasury rails for receiving Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, and Stripe settlement proceeds in USD, EUR, AUD, and GBP, with auto-conversion into NZD or USD for onward disbursement. Legal constraints: (1) an offshore EMI without New Zealand registration cannot lawfully provide cross-border payment services to a New Zealand-based company, and the entity must ensure the EMI holds a valid FSP license and an agent/trustee arrangement if it is a foreign EEA/UK EMI passporting under NZ's Part 6 inbound registration; (2) EMIs are not eligible to provide credit facilities or hold term deposit liquidity, and their deposits are not guaranteed under the RBNZ depositor compensation scheme; (3) for LG round trip capital escrow, a licensed tier-1 commercial bank, not an EMI, must be the primary custodian of share capital, while an EMI may operate as a sub-account for operational clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is executed via SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the New Zealand International Company's Operational NZD or multi-currency account with a tier-1 NZ bank. No mandatory purpose code regime exists, but the remittance reference field must clearly identify the transaction as 'Share Subscription – APIC (Additional Paid-In Capital)' with a covering note stating the Companies Office file number and the relevant board resolution date. Remittance data should include the Overseas Investment Act 2005 exemption status (no consent required for a new startup entity not acquiring sensitive assets) and a statement that the funds constitute equity injection, not intercompany debt. The receiving bank will apply a standard 'non-resident company – shareholder equity' reason code for BOP statistical purposes. Upon confirmation of settlement, the company must (i) update the New Zealand Companies Office share register within 20 working days, (ii) file an annual return, and (iii) for tax-exempt International Companies, maintain the exemption certificate file. If the inbound transfer exceeds NZD 50,000, the bank will require the evidentiary dossier previously enumerated (source of funds, subscription agreement, SAFE approval) prior to crediting, with a typical same-day or next-business-day availability.Primary clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 (single payment) via the onshore bank's direct clearing network for content licensing fees, production services, IP royalties, and overseas contractor wages. For high-value production financing milestones (above USD 250,000) where an irrevocable payment undertaking is contractually required, a standard Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) with a confirmed advising bank can be established, but this is atypical for vertical micro-drama working capital cycles in which production-to-cash is rapid. App store settlement is handled through Apple's and Google's mass-payment systems, with funds remitted in USD, EUR, or NZD via wire transfers to the company's NZD account; for jurisdictions without direct settlement (e.g., incountry OTT partners), use SWIFT MT103 with OTT's designated account. All outbound disbursements should be made to accounts in the name of the contracted vendor (no third-party beneficiaries) and include commercial invoice numbers in the SWIFT field 72/77 to facilitate bank reconciliation. The banks in New Zealand currently support Fast Payment (Domestic Clearing) for local disbursements, but international outbound payments remain reliant on the SWIFT network with an expected value date of T+1 to T+3 depending on the destination. Treasury should maintain USD, EUR, and AUD clearing accounts at the primary bank to reduce conversion spreads and to filter app store settlement.FX Forwards (plain vanilla deliverable forward contracts up to 24 months by maturity) – permitted with all tier-1 NZ registered bank counterparties for USD/NZD, AUD/NZD, EUR/NZD, GBP/NZD and JPY/NZDNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) – permitted via NZ and international third-party counterparties for restricted currencies (CNY offshore, THB, PHP, IDR, VND, MYR, KRW, INR) with standard net-cash settled NDF conventions in USDCross-Currency Swaps (CCS) – permitted for converting long-term foreign-currency (especially USD) app store receivable streams into NZD liabilities, or for hedging an AUD- or USD-denominated production financing facility; requires a registered NZ financial service providerFX options (European-style call/put on spot and forwards) – permitted, but usually only utilized for highly committed production capex and are offered by the top four banks under standard ISDA documentation

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High with legal constraints. Registered non-bank financial institutions operating as electronic money institutions (EMIs) in New Zealand, including Wise (formerly TransferWise) NZ, Airwallex NZ, OFX, and Ebury, offer multi-currency IBAN wallets and rapid settlement with valid Financial Service Provider (FSP) registration under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and as reporting entities under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. These EMIs provide viable treasury rails for receiving Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, and Stripe settlement proceeds in USD, EUR, AUD, and GBP, with auto-conversion into NZD or USD for onward disbursement. Legal constraints: (1) an offshore EMI without New Zealand registration cannot lawfully provide cross-border payment services to a New Zealand-based company, and the entity must ensure the EMI holds a valid FSP license and an agent/trustee arrangement if it is a foreign EEA/UK EMI passporting under NZ's Part 6 inbound registration; (2) EMIs are not eligible to provide credit facilities or hold term deposit liquidity, and their deposits are not guaranteed under the RBNZ depositor compensation scheme; (3) for LG round trip capital escrow, a licensed tier-1 commercial bank, not an EMI, must be the primary custodian of share capital, while an EMI may operate as a sub-account for operational clearing.
