Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (NZ) — ss 12 (mode of incorporation), 13 (assent to incorporation), 15 (certificate of incorporation), 16 (company constitution), 199–201 (registered office and company records), s 172 (resident director requirement); supplemented by the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 (Parts 5 and 6 for financial advice and discretionary investment management licences), and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 for the Multi-Family Office's licensed financial activities.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The Companies Act 1993 prescribes no minimum paid-up capital or escrow account; the International Company may be capitalised with zero or nominal issued shares. However, to obtain the FMA Financial Advice Provider (FAP) / Discretionary Investment Management Service (DIMS) licence, the applicant must demonstrate a minimum financial capacity of the New Zealand dollar equivalent of approximately USD 18,500 (NZD 30,000) available on standby, evidenced by a bank letter, to sustain continuity of client portfolio advisory operations.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017 (approved Evacuation Scheme, six-monthly drill log, annual fire systems inspection); Building Warrant of Fitness and annual compliance schedule under the Building Act 2004 (including escape routes, sprinkler/alarm panels, emergency lighting); structural/earthquake-prone building assessment where applicable; WorkSafe New Zealand health and safety clearance under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 (hazard register, first aid, incident reporting); electrical safety certificate under the Electricity (Safety) Regulations 2010; and civil defence readiness in the form of a business continuity plan to protect client custody-wire and SWIFT banking rails during a natural disaster event.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; online self-service portal: myIR (accessed via www.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident International Company (IC) seeking a New Zealand tax identity must: (1) appoint a New Zealand-resident agent for service of notices; (2) complete Form IR742 (Application for an IRD Number for a Non-Resident Company) and file it with certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, and a register of directors and shareholders; (3) submit the application electronically through myIR or by post to Inland Revenue, PO Box 39050, Wellington Mail Centre 5045; (4) upon issuance of the IRD number (typically within 8–15 working days for paper and 1–2 working days for myIR), register separately for GST in myIR if NZD 60,000 or more of taxable supplies is made in any 12-month period, or voluntarily where zero-rating or input tax refunds will be claimed; (5) register for PAYE and FBT only if the IC employs personnel physically working in New Zealand. The IRD number operates as the single statutory tax identification number for income tax, GST, PAYE, FBT, and all other New Zealand taxes; there is no separate VAT registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand has no check-the-box regime. A non-resident International Company is taxed on a territorial basis: liability is limited exclusively to New Zealand-sourced income, while foreign-sourced income from offshore discretionary portfolio advisory and multi-family office activities remains outside the New Zealand tax net unless the company is managed and controlled in New Zealand or maintains a fixed establishment there. The company may elect: (a) GST grouping under sections 55–57 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 with New Zealand group members; (b) to be treated as a controlled foreign company shareholder if New Zealand residents hold 50% or more of the IC and their attributing interests trigger CFC income attribution; (c) to apply the NZ thin capitalisation and hybrid mismatch ordering rules under Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018, which disallow deductions for hybrid financial instrument payments and imported mismatched payments and override domestic treaty benefits where an offshore branch or hybrid entity is used.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold; non-resident suppliers of remote services (including digital and cross-border advisory services) to New Zealand consumers are also subject to the NZD 60,000 registration threshold under the GST (Remote Services) rules, with registration required once the threshold is exceeded unless the supplies are business-to-business and zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where: (a) goods are physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier within 28 days of the supply and the supplier holds contemporaneous evidence of export; (b) services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not physically performed in New Zealand and are not directly in connection with land in New Zealand or goods physically present in New Zealand at the time of supply; (c) for the multi-family office advisory model, discretionary portfolio advisory and asset-management services supplied to non-resident private client families will qualify for zero-rating where the services are provided for the purposes of a business or taxable activity carried on outside New Zealand and the recipient is not in New Zealand at the time the services are rendered. The supplier must retain a full audit trail evidencing the export, the non-resident status of the recipient, and the receipt of payment from offshore.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Electronic Export Declaration (Trade Single Window / AES export entry reference)
  • Commercial invoice and itemised advisory fee invoice describing the services or goods with HS codes and values
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of exported goods (for goods transactions)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export consideration into a New Zealand bank account from the non-resident purchaser
  • Packing list, certificate of origin, and freight insurance certificate supporting the customs declared value
  • Signed client mandate or discretionary investment management agreement confirming non-resident status of the recipient and offshore place of benefit

No real-time fiscal clearance regime, no API-based live invoice clearance, and no digital stamping or QR-code mandate exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the PEPPOL network (NZ e-Invoicing Service accredited by Inland Revenue). Tax invoices must contain the supplier's name and IRD number, date of issue, description of goods or services, GST-exclusive and GST-inclusive amounts, and the GST component, and must be issued within 28 days of the supply. GST returns are filed electronically via myIR under monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly filing cycles as allocated by Inland Revenue.

Inland Revenue may disallow zero-rating, claw back input tax credits, and assess output tax together with use-of-money interest for any GST shortfall. Civil shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply at graduated rates: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or intentional misleading conduct. Directors, shadow directors, and professional advisers involved in arranging a fraudulent export zero-rating scheme may incur personal liability as persons knowingly involved in the evasion. Criminal prosecution can result in imprisonment of up to 5 years, fines of up to NZD 50,000 for individuals and NZD 250,000 for companies, and forfeiture of goods under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 for false export declarations. Additionally, the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 imposes independent civil penalties for failure to maintain accurate wire-transfer and cross-border payment records for the custody and SWIFT payment rails used by the multi-family office.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand is an IMF Article VIII member: the New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible on both the current and the capital account, with no surrender, pledge, or repatriation restrictions. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repayment from the New Zealand-operating multi-family office to the Cook Islands International Company are unrestricted, subject to (i) non-resident withholding tax at treaty-reduced rates and (ii) OIO screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 for acquisitions of sensitive land, fishing quota, or significant business assets above statutory thresholds. The Cook Islands IC must preserve central management and control outside New Zealand in order to maintain non-residency for NZ tax purposes and to preserve the tax-exempt character of its income under Cook Islands law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no minimum conversion ratio, and no compulsory conversion timeline. Exporters and service providers may freely retain, convert, or deploy any foreign-currency receipts. Residual obligations are confined to (i) statistical reporting for the RBNZ balance-of-payments and international-investment-position surveys, (ii) border cash declarations under the AML/CFT Act 2009 for physical currency or bearer negotiable instruments equal to or exceeding NZD 10,000, and (iii) standard documentary substantiation required by the relevant NZ banking counterparty.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand — Te Pūtea Matua (RBNZ). New Zealand does not operate a separate foreign-exchange administration; the RBNZ is the consolidated monetary, prudential, and balance-of-payments statistical authority, while the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) of Toitū Te Whenua Land Information New Zealand administers the residual inbound foreign-investment screening regime under the Overseas Investment Act 2005.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM (supplementary rail only; not a primary settlement layer). New Zealand has no bespoke electronic-money-institution licensing category. A cross-border digital EMI must (i) register on the Financial Service Providers Register, (ii) become a reporting entity under the AML/CFT Act 2009 in the money-remittance or foreign-exchange-dealer category, and (iii) avoid the deposit-taking prohibition under the Deposit Takers Act 2023. In practice, Wise Business and Airwallex-type providers can support low-value disbursements, but tier-1 NZ banks will not permit an EMI to serve as the settlement or escrow counterparty for a High_Risk_Special_License multi-family office; all rail-critical balances must reside in regulated NZ bank accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Standard rail is a SWIFT MT103 from the Cook Islands IC's account to the NZ MFO's operational account at a tier-1 NZ bank. The payment must carry an ISO 20022 or MT103 purpose code mapping to the BPM6 classification: for a share-subscription contribution use 'EQUI' (equity capital); for an intragroup shareholder loan use 'LOAN'; for family settlement distributions use 'GIFT' or 'CLTR' (capital transfer) as appropriate. The receiving bank will require a board resolution, a certified register of the IC's directors and shareholders, a CRS/AEOI tax self-certification, an OIO approval letter where the funds will acquire sensitive NZ assets, and the NZ office's inward-remittance AML declaration. NZD proceeds settle through the RBNZ ESAS real-time gross settlement system; USD and EUR proceeds settle via SWIFT gpi through the NZ bank's correspondent network.Operational and family disbursements are cleared by SWIFT MT103 messages (or MT104/MT105 consolidated batch instructions) with beneficiary BIC and IBAN validation and explicit remittance advice describing each payment purpose, e.g. 'trust distribution' or 'custody fee'. Trade settlement with commercial counterparties uses documentary collections and commercial letters of credit: the NZ bank issues the LC by SWIFT MT700, typically at sight or up to 90 days usance, with an overseas confirming bank where required. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A contemporaneous Local File is expected where the aggregate value of the taxpayer's cross-border controlled transactions exceeds NZ$1 million per taxpayer per income year, or earlier where the transaction involves a high-risk arrangement or the taxpayer is otherwise a large enterprise. A Master File is required for multinational groups whose consolidated annual revenue exceeds the Country-by-Country Reporting threshold of NZ$1.3 billion (equivalent to EUR 750 million). Lower thresholds can apply for high-risk intercompany services or intangible transactions and for transactions involving NZ loss-making or zero-taxed entities.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not administer a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but applies OECD BEPS-aligned rules and its general anti-avoidance rule (s BG1 Income Tax Act 2007) to disregard arrangements lacking genuine commercial substance. A multi-family office using an International Company must have demonstrable substance: physical office in New Zealand, resident directors, locally employed professional staff, board decision-making in New Zealand, and accounting records maintained in New Zealand. Failure to maintain such substance will cause the entity to be treated as tax resident where central management and control lies, and may result in denial of treaty benefits, anti-avoidance reconstruction, or re-attribution of the MFO's service income to a New Zealand agency or permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False or Misleading Returns

Statutory sanction: Tax shortfall penalties: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 20% for unacceptable tax position, 100% for abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. Criminal conviction under s 143 Tax Administration Act 1994: imprisonment up to 5 years and/or a fine of up to NZ$250,000 for a body corporate and NZ$50,000 for an individual. The IRD may also assess use-of-money interest and obtain an Anton Piller order for records.

Director personal liability: Directors who knew or ought to have known of the false position are jointly and severally liable for the company's statutory shortfall penalties where they materially contributed to the return. Civil restitution may be ordered, and IRD can apply for a departure prohibition order preventing the director from leaving New Zealand. Reckless trading under s 135 Companies Act 1993 exposes directors to personal liability for company debts, including unpaid tax.
Transfer-Pricing Misstatement and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: IRD will recompute taxable income under the arm's-length principle using the most appropriate OECD method. Shortfall penalties apply: 20% for failing to take reasonable care, 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for intentional understatement. Interest is applied from the original due date until full payment. Cross-border financing can also be denied under thin capitalisation rules and the interest limitation rule for large MNE groups.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or implement a manipulative transfer-pricing policy may be subject to the same civil penalties as significant participants. The company veil may be pierced if the offshore entity is a mere instrumentality and the director caused the company to enter into sham transactions. Personal travel notifications and departure prohibitions may be issued in respect of unpaid tax liabilities of the controlled company.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / General Anti-Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Reconstruction under s BG1 and Part G Income Tax Act 2007: IRD may void the arrangement and attribute the income to the entity with actual economic substance; the shell company may be treated as resident in New Zealand based on central management and control; DTA benefits may be denied for lack of beneficial ownership or economic substance; shortfall penalties up to 100% and criminal penalties for evasion may apply if the arrangement is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for the company's tax shortfall under s 141 TAA if they knowingly participated in the tax avoidance arrangement. The court may lift the corporate veil and impose civil restitution against directors in their personal capacities. Directors may also be disqualified from acting as directors under s 383 Companies Act 1993 and may face travel bans through IRD enforcement measures.
AML/CFT Breach and Unlicensed Financial-Service Provision

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a reporting entity can face substantial civil penalties of up to NZ$5 million for serious non-compliance, with individual penalties up to NZ$500,000; knowingly making false or misleading AML/CFT information is a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment up to 2 years. The Financial Markets Authority may suspend, impose conditions on, or cancel the licence. Under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, providing unlicensed financial advice may attract fines up to NZ$1 million per breach.

Director personal liability: Directors are presumed to be responsible for ensuring the AML/CFT compliance programme is adequate. Individuals who knowingly direct or approve a breach may be personally prosecuted and barred from managing financial service companies. Courts can order compensation and civil restitution to investors. Travel bans may be imposed for unpaid penalties or in aid of enforcement of personal liability orders.
