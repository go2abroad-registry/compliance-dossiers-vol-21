Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. The Companies Act 1993 imposes no minimum issued share capital and no capital escrow or verification bank account is required; a company may validly incorporate with a single share issued at NZ$1.00 or any subscription price fixed by the directors. For non-cash consideration subscriptions, directors must certify solvency and comply with ss 68-77 distribution and allotment rules, including preparation of a certificate confirming the allotment will not render the company insolvent.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) approval of the fire evacuation scheme and Building Act 2004 Certificate of Compliance covering means of escape and active fire suppression; building consent authority structural load inspection certifying compliance with B1 Structure and B2 Durability of the New Zealand Building Code; WorkSafe New Zealand hazardous substances location certificate and plant registration for pressure equipment; occupational health clearance including ISO 14644-1 Class 8 cleanroom hygiene certification, MSDS-based chemical exposure monitoring, and electrical safety verification under the Electricity Act 1992.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake) — online portal myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir); New Zealand Customs Service (Customs / Ngā Aro o Aotearoa) for export declarations and customs processing.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident company first obtains a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) via the Companies Office registration under the Companies Act 1993. Following incorporation or registration as an overseas company, the entity must apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number and, where applicable, GST registration. The standard application path uses the IR596 Non-Resident Company Tax Registration form and the IR970 GST Registration form, submitted electronically through myIR or by paper. Non-resident companies carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand or making taxable supplies exceeding the registration threshold must hold an IRD number; the same IRD number serves as the GST identification number. No local physical presence is independently required for tax registration, but a tax agent or resident officer may be required for correspondence purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a New Zealand limited company (Ltd) is a separate taxable entity subject to the 28% corporate income tax rate on worldwide income if resident in New Zealand (with foreign tax credits and controlled-foreign-company attribution rules); a non-resident company is taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income. Territorial scope is worldwide for resident companies, with statutory exemptions for certain active foreign income and Australian-listed company exclusions. Tax-exempt zones: none; no income-tax special economic zones exist in New Zealand. A close-held company (five or fewer shareholders) may elect look-through company (LTC) treatment for transparent income-tax treatment to resident individual shareholders, but the entity remains liable for GST and PAYE obligations. — A non-resident company first obtains a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) via the Companies Office registration under the Companies Act 1993. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a New Zealand limited company (Ltd) is a separate taxable entity subject to the 28% corporate income tax rate on worldwide income if resident in New Zealand (with foreign tax credits and controlled-foreign-company attribution rules); a non-resident company is taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income. Territorial scope is worldwide for resident companies, with statutory exemptions for certain active foreign income and Australian-listed company exclusions. Tax-exempt zones: none; no income-tax special economic zones exist in New Zealand. A close-held company (five or fewer shareholders) may elect look-through company (LTC) treatment for transparent income-tax treatment to resident individual shareholders, but the entity remains liable for GST and PAYE obligations.

Available election forms

Available elections under the Income Tax Act 2007 include: (i) look-through company (LTC) election under subpart HG for eligible close-held companies; (ii) consolidated group election for wholly-owned corporate groups; (iii) portfolio investment entity (PIE) election for managed investment vehicles; (iv) Māori authority election; (v) grandfathered qualifying company election (closed to new companies formed after 2011). New Zealand has no US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification is statutory. New Zealand's hybrid-mismatch rules (subpart FH) are mandatory and non-elective, disallowing deductions or mandating inclusion for cross-border hybrid arrangements, with no general opt-out election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 annual taxable turnover (12-month rolling test) for mandatory registration; voluntary registration is available for persons making or intending to make taxable supplies; non-resident suppliers of remote services ('offshore supplier registration') are required to register in New Zealand when their total supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed NZD 60,000 per annum, while a GST-registered non-resident can claim zero-rating on qualifying exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods: zero-rating applies where the supplier supplies goods by way of sale and the goods are exported by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf (i) within 28 days after the supply, or (ii) within 12 months where the supplier is the exporter and the goods are entered for export; the supplier must retain credible evidence of export. Offshore services: zero-rating applies to services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the services are not supplied directly in connection with land or goods situated in New Zealand at the time of supply and the non-resident is not supplying the services to a GST-registered New Zealand business in respect of that supply. For an intraocular acrylic lens manufacturer, exports of finished medical devices to overseas distributors or healthcare institutions will ordinarily qualify for zero-rating if the contractual terms, physical export, and documentation evidence are consistent and contemporaneous.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUSDEC submitted via the New Zealand Trade Single Window / Customs electronic system)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange bank receipt evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration
  • Certificate of Origin where a preferential duty claim is made in the destination jurisdiction

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing clearance. E-invoicing is voluntary and interoperable through the Peppol network under the New Zealand e-Invoicing framework administered by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR on a one-monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly filing cycle depending on turnover; taxpayers must retain full source documentation (invoices, export evidence, bank records) for seven years. No fiscal data is transmitted to Inland Revenue at the moment of invoice issuance.

Inland Revenue audits export zero-rating claims strictly. If export documentation is incomplete, non-contemporaneous, or goods/services are re-characterised as standard-rated, the supplier is reassessed for GST at 15% on the full consideration, plus use-of-money interest from the date the GST became payable. Shortfall penalties apply on the tax shortfall at graduated rates: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for an unacceptable tax position taking a 'more than trivial' approach, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or fraudulent intent. Knowingly providing false export documents is a criminal offence under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the Crimes Act 1961, carrying fines of up to NZD 50,000 per offence and imprisonment. Refunds may be withheld pending verification, and clawbacks may reach back to the original return period with no limitation where fraudulent intent is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand subscribes to IMF Article VIII and maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the New Zealand dollar (NZD) on both the current account and the capital account. There are no exchange-control approvals for trade settlements, dividend repatriation, intercompany loans, or portfolio investment, subject only to prudential AML/CFT information requirements and the Reserve Bank's interest-rate framework. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is lawful via ordinary dividend or branch profit extraction, conditioned on the Companies Act 1993 solvency test and on applicable Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) at 15% (or an applicable reduced treaty rate); no central-bank conversion, consent, or surrender obligation attaches to such distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate export receipts into a domestic account. Exporters may freely hold invoiced proceeds in foreign-currency accounts maintained with New Zealand registered banks, operate multi-currency settlement accounts, and net or pool export receivables, provided the underlying transactions are duly documented and all payments conform to the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. Effective mandated surrender ratio: 0% (none).

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), the statutory central bank and prudential regulator of the New Zealand banking system; foreign exchange and capital-flow policy is coordinated with the Minister of Finance, and foreign investment screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 is administered by the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) within Land Information New Zealand (LINZ). New Zealand has no separate foreign-exchange-control administration because exchange controls were fully abolished.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) (e.g., Airwallex, Wise Business, and similar licensed non-bank payment service providers) may provide services in or into New Zealand only after registering on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Disputes Resolution) Act 2008, joining an approved dispute-resolution scheme, and being registered as reporting entities under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 with the Department of Internal Affairs, the Reserve Bank, or the Financial Markets Authority as supervisor. Such EMIs cannot accept deposits or arrange credit without Registered Bank status under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021; therefore, they are viable only as ancillary inbound/outbound payment rails, cross-border payroll, purchase-card settlements, and LC-receivables collection, while the primary treasury, escrow, and FX-trading accounts must remain with a licensed NZ trading bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity-capital or shareholder-loan injections into the NZ operating entity must be routed through a licensed New Zealand registered bank using the SWIFT MT103 format (or MT202/COV for large-value transfers) to the corporate account, quoting the beneficiary's New Zealand bank account number (bank, branch, and account components) and the relevant purpose-of-payment description (e.g., 'Foreign Direct Investment - Equity Capital' or 'Direct Investment - Intercompany Loan') per the New Zealand Bankers' Association purpose-code matrix. The remittance must be accompanied by the Overseas Investment Act 2005 assessment where a foreign person acquires securities or assets above the relevant threshold, and the NZ receiving bank will record the capital contribution for Balance of Payments / International Investment Position statistical purposes. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) (e.g., Airwallex, Wise Business, and similar licensed non-bank payment service providers) may provide services in or into New Zealand only after registering on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Disputes Resolution) Act 2008, joining an approved dispute-resolution scheme, and being registered as reporting entities under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 with the Department of Internal Affairs, the Reserve Bank, or the Financial Markets Authority as supervisor. Such EMIs cannot accept deposits or arrange credit without Registered Bank status under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021; therefore, they are viable only as ancillary inbound/outbound payment rails, cross-border payroll, purchase-card settlements, and LC-receivables collection, while the primary treasury, escrow, and FX-trading accounts must remain with a licensed NZ trading bank.
