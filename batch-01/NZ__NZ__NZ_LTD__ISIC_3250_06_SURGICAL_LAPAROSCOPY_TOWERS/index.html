Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; official online portal is myIR, accessible via https://www.ird.govt.nz. Customs export and import functions are administered separately by the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window, while IRD remains the competent authority for income tax and GST.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident shareholder must first incorporate the New Zealand limited company under the Companies Act 1993 through the New Zealand Companies Office and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). Upon incorporation, the company must apply to the IRD for a company IRD number (tax file number) using the IR742 form lodged via myIR or the Companies Office seamless online registration. The IRD will issue a company IRD number, open an income tax account, and issue an initial notice of tax obligations. If the company will carry on a taxable activity with annual turnover exceeding NZD 60,000, it must separately register for GST under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 using the online GST registration form in myIR or form IR356. A local New Zealand address for service must be maintained; non-resident directors and shareholders are permitted without restrictions on foreign ownership, but the company must still satisfy the registered office and incorporation requirements under New Zealand law.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A New Zealand limited company is a separate taxpayer resident in New Zealand; it is taxed at the corporate income tax rate of 28% on worldwide income. Non-resident-owned New Zealand companies are taxed on worldwide income, with offshore income potentially subject to the controlled foreign company (CFC) and foreign investment fund (FIF) regimes. New Zealand applies a worldwide/territorial hybrid scope: domestic-source income is always taxable and foreign-source income is taxable to residents unless an applicable exemption or CFC attribution rule applies. There is no special tax-exempt zone or offshore financial centre carve-out applicable to medical device manufacturers. Pass-through treatment is generally unavailable for a company with corporate shareholders; only an eligible closely-held company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status as described below.

Available election forms

A New Zealand resident company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 if it has five or fewer shareholders and all shareholders are individuals or eligible trustee shareholders; the company and its shareholders jointly elect LTC status in writing to IRD, after which the company's income, losses, and tax credits flow through to shareholders. This election is not available if any shareholder is another company or a non-eligible trust, which is often the case for non-resident corporate parents, so the default entity-level taxation will normally apply. New Zealand has no US-style check-the-box election for corporate classification. Hybrid mismatch rules under the OECD-aligned provisions in the Income Tax Act 2007 disallow deductions in respect of hybrid financial instruments and hybrid entities where there is a mismatch outcome, but no affirmative election is required. A New Zealand group of companies may elect to consolidate for tax purposes under subpart FG; each company remains a separately registered taxpayer but losses may be offset through the consolidated loss offset election. Provisional tax payment methods (standard, estimation, and GST-ratio methods) may be elected by the company to manage cash flow, and the company may elect to use the GST hybrid or payments basis in prescribed circumstances.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of gross taxable supplies in any 12-month period; registration is compulsory once the threshold is exceeded. Voluntary registration below the threshold is permitted, and once registered, an exporter of medical devices is entitled to claim input tax credits on purchases used in making zero-rated export supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for supplies made by a registered person in respect of goods physically exported from New Zealand, provided the supplier ships the goods and retains evidence that the goods left New Zealand. For goods sold by a New Zealand manufacturer to an overseas buyer on CIF or FOB terms, the supply is zero-rated if the goods are exported by the supplier pursuant to the contract of sale. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated if the non-resident recipient is outside New Zealand when the services are performed and the services are supplied to the recipient for use outside New Zealand, with specific statutory exclusions for services supplied directly in connection with land in New Zealand or movable property situated in New Zealand at the time of supply. For the manufacture of surgical laparoscopy towers, exported goods and separately invoiced installation, training, and remote software services performed outside New Zealand can qualify for zero-rating, provided the exporter maintains sufficient contractual and shipping documentation evidencing the actual offshore destination and foreign recipient. Zero-rating is not optional; if the statutory conditions are not met, the supply is standard-rated at 15%, and the supplier may incur a GST shortfall.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUS entry lodged via the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent Air Waybill for air cargo)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT/telex advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency consideration)

New Zealand does not mandate real-time transactional fiscal clearance or digital stamping. GST-registered persons must file GST returns (IR372) electronically through myIR on a one-monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly cycle; exports and zero-rated supplies are reported in the relevant GST return. E-invoicing operates on a voluntary Peppol-compatible framework: the New Zealand Government has actively adopted Peppol for business-to-government e-invoicing, and the IRD supports electronic invoicing, but there is no mandatory pre-clearance API or government fiscal server validation for B2B invoices. Exporters must still issue a valid tax invoice for taxable supplies; zero-rated export invoices must specifically state that the supply is zero-rated and include the supplier's IRD number and GST registration number.

The IRD may audit any zero-rated export claim and will scrutinise the documentary trail: the Customs export declaration, the bill of lading or master air waybill, the sales contract and invoice, and the foreign exchange receipt. New Zealand has no standalone statutory 180-day export proceeds repatriation rule; the 180-day benchmark in this dossier reflects a conservative bank documentary-compliance norm, but the actual invoicing terms should not extend unreasonably beyond payment terms, and foreign exchange repatriation controls are not imposed by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand for ordinary exports. If a zero-rating claim is unsupported, the supply is recharacterised as standard-rated, and the resulting GST shortfall is assessable together with use-of-money interest under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Shortfall penalties apply in tiered amounts: 20% for a lack of reasonable care, 40% for an unacceptable tax position, 100% for evasion or an arrangement designed to defeat the intent of the GST Act (increasing to 150% if obstruction occurred). Retrospective clawback will recover the entire input tax credits previously enjoyed on export inputs, plus interest and penalties, and the Commissioner may prosecute fraudulent zero-rating claims criminally under sections 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994, exposing the company and supervising officers to fines of up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment for up to five years, in addition to director liability and potential disqualification under the Companies Act 1993. Export records must be retained for at least seven years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised, free-floating currency regime with no residual exchange-control legislation. The New Zealand Dollar (NZD) is highly liquid and freely convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. As an IMF Article VIII member since 1982, New Zealand imposes no restrictions on payments and transfers for current international transactions, and no prior central-bank approval is required for profit repatriation, dividend streaming, intercompany loans, trade settlements, or equity injections. The only material constraints are: (i) the non-discriminatory screening of offshore investments under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 where a non-resident acquires significant business assets (NZ$100 million threshold for non-forestry assets) or sensitive land; and (ii) the non-resident dividend withholding tax (NRWT) regime, under which an unrestricted profit remittance is subject to NRWT at a treaty rate of 15% under most double-tax agreements (30% where no DTA applies), with imputation credits attachable to reduce the residual tax burden on fully imputed dividends. No FMA or RBNZ consent is required for a private company to allot shares to non-residents or to remit trade proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation, timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to New Zealand exporters. All exchange-control regulations were repealed in 1984. Export proceeds from surgical laparoscopy tower sales may be retained indefinitely in the entity's NZD or foreign-currency accounts, may be used directly to settle overseas supplier invoices without forced conversion, or may be converted at any time through a registered bank at freely market-determined exchange rates. The only continuing duties are ordinary tax record-keeping and transfer-pricing documentation obligations to Inland Revenue (IRD), plus any offshore bank-account and cross-border reporting obligations arising in the customer's own jurisdiction.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for operational multi-currency disbursements; Low for capital escrow and direct LC issuance. Offshore and domestic fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, OFX, Convera) are legally able to provide cross-border payment functionality in New Zealand as registered money remitters under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, supervised by the Department of Internal Affairs. They are not licensed deposit-takers under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's prudential regime (currently the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989 and, when fully in force, the Deposit Takers Act 2022), so they cannot host the capital-receipt escrow account, cannot issue commercial letters of credit, and do not offer the same creditor-priority and deposit-protection features as a registered bank. The optimal architecture is a licensed Tier-1 bank as the treasury backbone with one fintech EMI as a supplementary rails service for supplier payouts and multi-currency receipts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder's capital injection must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the entity's NZD operational account (or a dedicated escrow sub-account at the relationship bank), with field 70 (Remittance Information) populated as 'Share Capital Subscription - Board Resolution [date] - NZBN [number]'. The receiving bank's compliance function will require: (i) the final allotment of shares filed electronically with the New Zealand Companies Office within 20 working days; (ii) a certified board resolution and share-subscription agreement; (iii) the investor KYC package comprising passport, proof of residential address, source-of-wealth statement, and 6-12 months of investor bank statements; and (iv) confirmation that neither the investor nor the funding source originates from a sanctioned jurisdiction. Because New Zealand suppresses exchange controls, the Reserve Bank of New Zealand imposes no prior approval, no reporting licence, and no mandatory conversion step; funds become immediately available upon receipt and may be swept into USD, EUR, AUD, or CNY treasury sub-accounts in accordance with the approved FX hedge plan. The allotment must be reflected in the Companies Office register, and the board resolution must be retained to evidence the non-assessable nature of share capital for New Zealand income-tax purposes.Trade payments ride the same Tier-1 clearing rails. Telegraphic-transfer disbursements under supplier invoices are executed via SWIFT MT103 with purchase-order and invoice references, direct to the beneficiary's bank. For high-value component-kit and capital-equipment imports, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit is issued by the NZ relationship bank via SWIFT MT700 under UCP 600, with the credit incorporating the Medsafe / Therapeutic Products Act certificates, ISO 13485 documentation, commercial invoice, full set of clean on-board bills of lading, and destination-country import permits as required documents. The domestic settlement leg of both MT103 and MT700 structures settles same-day through the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESB), while the cross-border leg flows over SWIFT with MT202 COV cover payments for interbank credit. For recurring export receipts, the treasury blueprint recommends a 'sweep-and-hedge' ladder: received USD, AUD, and EUR flows are auto-swept weekly and hedged back into NZD via standing FX forwards rather than being left unhedged in Nostro suspense.Outright FX Forwards (NZD/USD, NZD/EUR, NZD/AUD, NZD/CNY) - permitted on a deliverable basis without Reserve Bank of New Zealand approval; executed under an IFX Master Agreement or ISDA 1998 FX Definitions with a NZ registered Tier-1 bank, with a strict underlying trade-exposure test.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) - permitted as hedge-only instruments where the underlying surgical-trade receivable or payable is denominated in a restricted or inconvertible currency (e.g., onshore CNY, INR, IDR, VND) and cannot be settled through the onshore deliverable interbank market.Cross-Currency Swaps (FX swaps and cross-currency interest rate swaps) - permitted for managing long-dated NZD/USD or NZD/EUR capital flows, matched funding of import payables, and the treasury hedge of the non-resident shareholder loan or equity position, subject to ISDA and internal credit-approval limits.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High for operational multi-currency disbursements; Low for capital escrow and direct LC issuance. Offshore and domestic fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, OFX, Convera) are legally able to provide cross-border payment functionality in New Zealand as registered money remitters under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, supervised by the Department of Internal Affairs. They are not licensed deposit-takers under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's prudential regime (currently the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989 and, when fully in force, the Deposit Takers Act 2022), so they cannot host the capital-receipt escrow account, cannot issue commercial letters of credit, and do not offer the same creditor-priority and deposit-protection features as a registered bank. The optimal architecture is a licensed Tier-1 bank as the treasury backbone with one fintech EMI as a supplementary rails service for supplier payouts and multi-currency receipts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory de minimis threshold is fixed in New Zealand for the preparation of Master File and Local File; Inland Revenue's Transfer Pricing Guidelines require contemporaneous documentation for all cross-border related-party transactions on a facts-and-circumstances basis, with practical audit focus on related-party dealings exceeding NZ$10 million annually. Mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) applies where the New Zealand group member belongs to a multinational group with consolidated global revenue of NZ$1.25 billion or more in the preceding fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to the United Arab Emirates. Economic substance is instead enforced through (a) the general anti-avoidance rule under section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, (b) tax residency based on incorporation or head-office/management location, (c) permanent establishment attribution under Article 7 of New Zealand's double tax agreements applying the authorised OECD approach, (d) the controlled-foreign-company and foreign-attribution regimes, and (e) thin-capitalisation rules restricting interest deductibility for non-resident-owned companies beyond a 75% NZ debt-to-asset safe harbour or the worldwide group's debt-to-asset ratio, whichever is higher. An NZ Ltd acting solely as a bare invoicing pass-through for foreign principals, without local manufacturing, R&D, warehousing, procurement, or properly empowered directors, cannot sustain treaty-benefit or transfer-pricing positions and will be recharacterised.
