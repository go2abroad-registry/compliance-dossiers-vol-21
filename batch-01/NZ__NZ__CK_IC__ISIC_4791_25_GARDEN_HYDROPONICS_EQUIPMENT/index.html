Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The New Zealand Companies Act 1993 requires a registered office in New Zealand, which may be the address of an agent if it is physically in New Zealand and not a PO Box. A virtual office or commercial mailbox with a street address may satisfy the Companies Office, but Inland Revenue and New Zealand registered banks generally reject a virtual mailbox address for GST registration and non-face-to-face account opening, particularly for online retail, because it cannot evidence commercial substance, management location, or stock holding. Recommendation is to secure a physical micro-lease or dedicated serviced office and conduct occasional staff or inventory presence; otherwise the proposed tax-exempt classification is at risk.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand; official taxpayer portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz; complementary registries: New Zealand Companies Office (NZBN) and New Zealand Customs Service for export controls.

E-government filing portal

A foreign International Company (CK_IC) must first determine whether it derives New Zealand-sourced supplies or income. If it does, the company obtains an IRD number by lodging Form IR742 for non-resident companies (paper) or applying through an authorised New Zealand tax agent via myIR; the application requires the certificate of incorporation, registered office address, directors and shareholders particulars, and a New Zealand address for service or tax agent appointment. If the company makes or intends to make taxable supplies in New Zealand exceeding NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, or elects voluntary registration, it completes the electronic GST registration in myIR (the same IRD number is then activated for GST); non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value imported goods register under the offshore supplier registration rules. The IRD number is issued by letter and is used for income tax, GST, and any PAYE obligations; no minimum capital or statutory licence is required for this e-commerce activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Primary) - companies are taxed at the 28% corporate rate; Territorial_Scope - non-resident companies are taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income, and the foreign IC's offshore income is not subject to New Zealand income tax; Pass_Through_Eligible - unavailable to a non-resident company (New Zealand's Look-Through Company regime is restricted to New Zealand-resident natural persons); Tax_Exempt_Zone - the CK_IC's offshore International Company status exempts its foreign income in its home jurisdiction but does not displace New Zealand GST, non-resident withholding tax, or income tax on New Zealand-sourced business profits.

Available election forms

New Zealand has no general check-the-box election. Statutory elections relevant to this dossier include: (i) voluntary GST registration under section 51 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for suppliers below the NZD 60,000 threshold; (ii) Look-Through Company election under the Income Tax Act 2007 for qualifying New Zealand-resident close-held companies (not applicable to CK_IC); (iii) consolidated group election for wholly-owned New Zealand-resident corporate groups; and (iv) the hybrid and branch mismatch rules (Subpart FH) apply automatically or by election to recharacterise hybrid entities and instruments in cross-border structures, preventing deduction/non-inclusion and double-deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (GST Act 1985, s 51). The same NZD 60,000 threshold applies to offshore suppliers of remote services and low-value imported goods (goods valued at NZD 1,000 or less) sold to New Zealand consumers; registration may be voluntary below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the GST Act 1985 if the supplier physically exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply and the goods are not subsequently returned, altered, or consumed in New Zealand. Services: zero-rated under section 11A where supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed and the services are not directly connected with New Zealand land or moveable property and are not physically performed in New Zealand for a person present in New Zealand. For the CK_IC e-commerce model, only genuine cross-border exports of hydroponics equipment leaving New Zealand customs territory qualify for 0% GST; supplies of remote/digital services and goods sold by an offshore supplier to New Zealand consumers are standard-rated at 15% under the offshore supplier regime.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Export Entry (electronic customs export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing physical export
  • Commercial Invoice / GST Tax Invoice stating zero-rated supply and supplier GST registration number
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Bank Remittance Advice / SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of foreign-currency sale proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based pre-clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime applies in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the international Peppol interoperability framework; GST returns and payments are submitted electronically via myIR; tax invoices must contain the statutory particulars required by section 24 of the GST Act 1985, but no government-approved clearance or digital stamp is required.

Inland Revenue cross-matches Customs export declarations, shipping manifests, and remittance records to verify zero-rating claims. If a zero-rated export is not substantiated - including failure to export within 28 days - output GST is clawed back at 15% with use-of-money interest from the original due date. Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, a shortfall arising from an abusive or fraudulent zero-rating position attracts civil shortfall penalties of 20% (not deliberate), 50% (deliberate but not concealed), 75% (deliberate and concealed), or 150% (evasion), plus criminal prosecution and director/manager personal liability in serious cases. For completeness, New Zealand's GST Act imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure reflected in this dossier is the standard documentary benchmark under international trade-finance and anti-money-laundering compliance, not a domestic tax-law requirement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand abolished exchange controls in 1984, accepts the IMF Article VIII obligations, and maintains a fully convertible, free-floating New Zealand dollar on both the current account and the capital account. The IC faces no exchange-control restriction on the conversion of trading receipts, dividend distributions, interest payments, or liquidation proceeds from New Zealand to the Cook Islands or any other offshore jurisdiction, and no prior central-bank approval is required for profit remittance. The only practical remittance hurdles are operational rather than regulatory: the designated beneficiary account must satisfy the bank's periodic KYC and sanction-screen re-verification, and all cross-border payments must carry an accurate Balance of Payments purpose classification. Inbound equity subscriptions and intercompany loans are freely credited, but all related-party financial arrangements must respect the arm's-length principle and the general anti-avoidance rule (section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007) and the transfer-pricing and financial-arrangement rules so that no payment can be recharacterised as a deemed New Zealand-source dividend, interest, or royalty. The Overseas Investment Act 2005 consent requirement arises only for acquisitions of sensitive land, fishing quota, or strategically significant assets; ordinary e-commerce treasury flows are outside that screening regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no time-limited repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The IC may lawfully retain one hundred percent of its Stripe, PayPal, and direct wire settlement receipts in USD, EUR, AUD, GBP, or NZD, in onshore or offshore accounts, indefinitely. The only binding compliance gates are: (i) prescribed-transaction reporting under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 for every cross-border wire transfer of NZD 1,000 or more; (ii) accurate declaration of settlement values in all applicable tax and GST filings; and (iii) retention of commercial documentation (invoices, shipping records, processor statements) sufficient for the bank's ongoing KYC, sanctions screening, and audit-trail reviews.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), the sole central bank and monetary authority of New Zealand; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for derivative conduct, licensing, and financial-market regulation; and the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) within Toitū Te Whenua Land Information New Zealand for foreign-investment screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High as a settlement and foreign-exchange liquidity pre-clearing rail, but Low as a long-term treasury vault. New Zealand does not maintain a dedicated electronic-money institution licensing category equivalent to the EU EMD2 regime; non-bank payment providers are instead regulated under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and any client-money holding must be supported by a trust arrangement with a licensed New Zealand registered bank. Offshore digital EMIs (including Airwallex, Currenxie, and Wise Platform) may lawfully provide the IC with NZD and USD settlement flow, receipt concentration from Stripe and PayPal, mid-market currency conversion, and supplier payouts to overseas manufacturers through New Zealand correspondent banks. The IC may therefore use an EMI as a high-velocity clearing layer, subject to a board-approved treasury policy capping un-cleared EMI balances at a documented operational maximum (recommended: the lower of 30 percent of monthly gross revenue or NZD 250,000) and mandating a one-business-day sweep of all settlement balances into the primary New Zealand registered bank. Holding investor capital, long-term operating reserves, or the escrowed equity funding inside an unlicensed EMI, or naming the EMI as the beneficiary of the Capital Escrow Account, is inconsistent with the Reserve Bank's prudential framework for deposit-taking institutions and with the client-money obligations under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and must be avoided.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High as a settlement and foreign-exchange liquidity pre-clearing rail, but Low as a long-term treasury vault. New Zealand does not maintain a dedicated electronic-money institution licensing category equivalent to the EU EMD2 regime; non-bank payment providers are instead regulated under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and any client-money holding must be supported by a trust arrangement with a licensed New Zealand registered bank. Offshore digital EMIs (including Airwallex, Currenxie, and Wise Platform) may lawfully provide the IC with NZD and USD settlement flow, receipt concentration from Stripe and PayPal, mid-market currency conversion, and supplier payouts to overseas manufacturers through New Zealand correspondent banks. The IC may therefore use an EMI as a high-velocity clearing layer, subject to a board-approved treasury policy capping un-cleared EMI balances at a documented operational maximum (recommended: the lower of 30 percent of monthly gross revenue or NZD 250,000) and mandating a one-business-day sweep of all settlement balances into the primary New Zealand registered bank. Holding investor capital, long-term operating reserves, or the escrowed equity funding inside an unlicensed EMI, or naming the EMI as the beneficiary of the Capital Escrow Account, is inconsistent with the Reserve Bank's prudential framework for deposit-taking institutions and with the client-money obligations under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and must be avoided.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory obligation to file Local File or Master File with the IRD. Inland Revenue may request contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where the aggregate amount of cross-border related-party transactions in a tax year exceeds NZ$2 million (goods/services) or NZ$500,000 (financing arrangements). Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting is required for multinational groups with annual group consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million (or an equivalent New Zealand dollar amount).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate Economic Substance Regulations in the style of the UAE or BVI. Instead, economic substance is a determinant of tax residence under s YD 2 Income Tax Act 2007: a company incorporated outside New Zealand is tax resident if it has its centre of management or control in New Zealand. The General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s BG 1) also denies tax benefits from arrangements lacking commercial substance. For the Cook Islands International Company, the IRD will disregard the separate existence of a shell company that is managed from New Zealand and has no office, staff, bank account, or board meetings in the Cook Islands. The company should maintain concrete evidence of director meetings, business records, and decision-making in the Cook Islands.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 150% of the tax shortfall under s 141B TAA 1994; criminal conviction under s 143A carries imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to NZ$50,000. The Commissioner may also pursue an additional civil penalty equal to the shortfall.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the evasion are personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties and may be prosecuted. They can be sentenced to imprisonment and banned for up to 10 years from managing a company under the Companies Act 1993.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The company is deemed New Zealand tax resident and is assessed on its worldwide income at 28%. In addition, a shortfall penalty of up to 100% (for tax avoidance) or 40% (for lack of reasonable care) may be imposed, plus statutory interest. If the arrangement is a sham, the IRD may reassess the underlying shareholders directly.

Director personal liability: Directors can be made personally liable for the company's unpaid taxes under s 148A TAA if they were 'knowing participants' in the sham. Such a determination pierces the corporate veil and may also result in director disqualification and criminal penalties for false statements.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may adjust the consideration to arm's length under s GC 13. Where a transfer pricing adjustment is made due to a failure to use the specified methods, a penalty of 20% of the resulting tax shortfall applies; a further penalty of up to 40% is imposed for tax avoidance. Interest runs on the adjusted amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed off a transfer pricing policy without proper independent benchmarking can be held personally liable for penalties if they acted without reasonable care. They may also be disqualified under company law for failing to exercise diligent management.
Non-Compliance with Withholding Obligations

Statutory sanction: Under s 140C, the company is liable for the unpaid NRWT plus a penalty of 10% of the undeclared amount (in addition to the tax), and a further initial non-payment penalty of 1% per month. Intentional failure to withhold is treated as tax evasion and attracts penalties of up to 150% of the shortfall.

Director personal liability: Section 148A makes directors personally liable for any NRWT, PAYE, or GST that the company collected or should have collected and failed to remit if the failure was intentional or caused by the director's attributable conduct. This civil liability bypasses the corporate veil.
AML/CFT Breach (Payment-Rail Related)

Statutory sanction: Civil liability up to NZ$1 million per contravention for a body corporate (s 95 AML/CFT Act) and, for criminal conviction, fines of up to NZ$500,000 for individuals and NZ$2 million for bodies corporate. The company may also be placed under supervision and forced to stop accepting payments.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are personally liable where the breach is 'knowing' or 'reckless', and they may be imprisoned for up to 2 years. The court may also order disqualification from acting as a director of any New Zealand company.
