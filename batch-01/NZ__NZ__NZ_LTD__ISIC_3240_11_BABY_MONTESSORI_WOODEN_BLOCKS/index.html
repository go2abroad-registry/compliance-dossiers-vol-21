Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue Te Tari Taake), Wellington, New Zealand; official online portal and filing interface: myIR Secure Online Services (https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir).

E-government filing portal

A New Zealand limited company obtains its IRD number (the statutory taxpayer identification number) automatically at incorporation through the Companies Office registration event; no separate TIN application is required for formation. A non-resident foreign entity not incorporated in New Zealand but deriving New Zealand-source income or carrying on a taxable activity must apply to Inland Revenue using the non-resident company IRD number application form (IR596) or, for natural persons, form IR742, and may submit the application online through myIR. Where the non-resident supplies goods or services in New Zealand and expects to exceed the NZ$60,000 GST registration threshold, the entity must separately register for GST through the myIR 'Register for GST' service, with registration effective from the date the taxable activity commences or the threshold is exceeded.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — an NZ limited company is a separate tax persona taxed at the 28% corporate rate on worldwide income (Income Tax Act 2007 subpart BC); New Zealand-resident companies are subject to worldwide taxation with the foreign non-attributable branch income exemption, while a non-resident company is taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an NZ limited company is a separate tax persona taxed at the 28% corporate rate on worldwide income (Income Tax Act 2007 subpart BC); New Zealand-resident companies are subject to worldwide taxation with the foreign non-attributable branch income exemption, while a non-resident company is taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income. The company is not a pass-through entity and files separate income tax returns (IR4) distinct from shareholders.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Statutory elections available to a qualifying close-held NZ limited company include: (1) look-through company (LTC) election under Income Tax Act 2007 ss HB 1–HB 4, which fiscally transparentises the company for income tax purposes while preserving corporate legal personality; (2) GST grouping election under Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 s 55, allowing commonly owned corporate groups to file a single GST return; and (3) hybrid mismatch neutralisation elections under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018, which re-characterise or deny deductions on cross-border instruments and entity payments to avoid double non-taxation. All elections are made by written notice to the Commissioner of Inland Revenue within prescribed time frames.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 — compulsory GST registration arises when the taxable supply turnover of the entity exceeds NZ$60,000 in any rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration is available to any person carrying on a taxable activity, including exporters wishing to recover input GST on manufacturing inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods is available under GST Act 1985 s 11 where the supplier exports the goods within 28 days of the time of supply, or delivers the goods to a recognised non-resident overseas customer for export and the goods are subsequently exported, and the supplier holds contemporaneous documentary evidence of export. Manufactured goods such as Montessori wooden blocks qualify when sold to non-resident buyers and exported from New Zealand. Offshore services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under GST Act 1985 s 11A where the recipient is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, the services are not connected with New Zealand land or tangible personal property, and the services are not physically performed in New Zealand by a person present in New Zealand; input tax on raw materials, beechwood turning, packaging, and freight may be refunded to the extent the supplies are attributable to zero-rated export activity.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export out of New Zealand

New Zealand does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the national Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 network administered by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE) in conjunction with Inland Revenue; registered businesses may transmit e-invoices and GST returns via IRD Gateway APIs and myIR digital services, while the fiscal authority relies on periodic self-assessment returns (GST101) rather than pre-clearance approval of individual invoices.

The Commissioner of Inland Revenue may reassess and claw back zero-rated output GST (imposing 15% GST on the full consideration) where the statutory export timing or evidentiary conditions fail, with use-of-money interest running from the original due date. Shortfall penalties under Tax Administration Act 1994 ss 141A–141D escalate from 20% (failure to take reasonable care) through 40% (gross carelessness) to 100% (abusive tax position) and 150% (evasion); knowingly fraudulent export zero-rating claims additionally expose the company and its officers to criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. The standard audit window is four years from the assessment date, extending to six years or beyond for evasion or serious non-disclosure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a freely floating and fully convertible currency following the repeal of the Exchange Control Act 1953 and liberalisation under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989. Both current-account transactions (export receivables, trade disbursements, royalties, freight, platform service fees) and capital-account transactions (equity subscription, shareholder loans, divestment proceeds, dividend remittances) are unrestricted by statute, with no central-bank approval, registration, quota, or taxation of currency conversion. Profit-remittance hurdles are limited to: (1) Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) at 30% on dividends, reduced to an effective 28% gross-up on fully imputed dividends and potentially lower under an applicable double-tax treaty; (2) Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) at 2% on non-resident interest if the borrower elects AIL-approved status in lieu of NRWT; (3) bank-level source-of-funds verification and suspicious-transaction reporting for any transfer above NZD 10,000 under the AML/CFT Act; and (4) the Overseas Investment Act 2005 consent requirement, which is applicable only where the funds acquire 25% or more of significant business assets exceeding the NZD 100 million screening threshold — inapplicable to this wooden-toy manufacturing entity. There is no profit-remittance cap, prior-authorisation moratorium, or exchange-rate band on NZD conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes zero mandatory export-proceeds surrender, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. An exporter may lawfully invoice in any currency, retain foreign-currency balances offshore, and convert at a commercially chosen date without RBNZ approval. The only binding indirect obligations are: (1) GST zero-rating substantiation — each export of goods must be supported by evidence of physical dispatch from New Zealand and the supplier must account for any exchange-rate conversion difference in the GST return period; (2) AML/CFT designated-transaction reporting — every inbound international wire above NZD 10,000 is automatically screened by the receiving bank and any unrelated or trade-document-deficient inflow is defensible only with matching export invoices and logistics proof; (3) income-tax translation — foreign-currency receivables and offshore balances must be translated to NZD at balance date under the Income Tax Act 2007, and any arrangement leaving export earnings indefinitely accumulated in a foreign subsidiary may trigger Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) attribution; and (4) balance-of-payments statistical reporting by the banking system, which carries no compliance burden on the exporter. Statutory conversion ratio: 0%. Statutory surrender deadline: none (open-ended retention permitted).

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua / RBNZ), acting as the central bank, prudential regulator, and operator of the wholesale Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS); the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) co-supervises over-the-counter derivative dealing conduct and market integrity; anti-money-laundering supervision of the banking sector is jointly exercised by the RBNZ, the FMA, and the Department of Internal Affairs under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act). No separate foreign-exchange-control administration or exchange-control licensing authority exists in New Zealand.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High as a supplementary settlement rail; Medium as a sole treasury provider. Legal constraints: Airwallex New Zealand Limited (FSP licence held) and Wise New Zealand Limited are registered financial service providers under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and may legally hold client money and offer payment services into NZD, but neither is a registered bank; they cannot provide direct membership of the RBNZ Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS), cannot issue cheque facilities, and are not covered by the RBNZ depositor-compensation regime applicable to registered banks. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Suppressed Turnover

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty up to 150% of the tax shortfall under Tax Administration Act 1994 s 141; criminal prosecution under s 143A with a fine of up to NZ$50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to five years; seizure of proceeds derived from the evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or authorise false returns are personally liable for penalties as individuals; Inland Revenue may collect unpaid tax debts from directors under the PAYE/GST recovery provisions; criminal conviction may trigger automatic disqualification under the Companies Act 1993.
Transfer Pricing Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to the arm's-length price; shortfall penalty of 20% for an unacceptable interpretation or 40% for a position lacking a reasonable basis; use-of-money interest on adjustments; potential consequential denial of imputation credits.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable where they authorised the pricing policy without a defensible transfer pricing study; fabrication of contemporaneous documentation is a criminal integrity offence with imprisonment exposure.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may cancel tax benefits under Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1, impose a 40% abusive-tax-position shortfall penalty, and disregard the company as the taxpayer, reallocating income to the real economic owners.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil recovery for reckless trading under Companies Act 1993 s 135, disqualification for up to five years, and personal liability for unpaid corporate tax where the shell structure was used to defeat creditors or the tax authority.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Non-Compliance (Profit Repatriation)

Statutory sanction: Unpaid NRWT becomes the company's liability; late payment penalties of 1% immediately and a further 4% after seven days; use-of-money interest; shortfall penalties up to 150% if deliberate non-deduction is established.

Director personal liability: Directors who are withholding agents are personally liable for NRWT not deducted and remitted; serious cases may lead to criminal prosecution, asset restraint orders, director disqualification, and imprisonment for up to five years; bail conditions in tax prosecutions impose effective travel restrictions.
AML / Record-Keeping and False Financial Declarations

Statutory sanction: Failure to keep adequate books and records is an offence carrying a fine of up to NZ$12,000; breaches of the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, where applicable, attract civil penalties up to NZ$200,000 for a natural person and NZ$2,000,000 for a company; termination of merchant accounts by payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally penalised for failure to implement and maintain customer due diligence controls; laundering the proceeds of crime under Crimes Act 1961 s 240 carries imprisonment for up to seven years, in addition to disqualification and confiscation orders.
