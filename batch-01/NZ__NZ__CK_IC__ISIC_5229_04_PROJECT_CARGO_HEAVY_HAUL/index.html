Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand (Te Tari Taake); secure taxpayer portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz. For export customs clearance, the competent adjunct authority is the New Zealand Customs Service (TRADEX / Trade Single Window) under the Customs and Excise Act 2018.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Confirm non-resident status of the CK_IC International Company (not incorporated in NZ; central management and control exercised offshore; no NZ permanent establishment). Step 2: If the entity derives NZ-sourced income or carries on a taxable activity in NZ, apply to the IRD for an IRD number (unique tax file number used for both income tax and GST) using Form IR742 (Application for IRD number for non-resident individual/company), accompanied by certified constitutional documents (certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, register of directors/shareholders, bank reference) for AML/CFT due diligence. Step 3: If the company is registered on the NZ Companies Register as an overseas company, first obtain an NZBN (New Zealand Business Number) from the Companies Office (Companies Act 1993) and quote it in the IR742. Step 4: Apply for GST registration in the same IR742 or via myIR, electing voluntary registration (GST Act 1985 s 51(3)) where turnover is below NZD 60,000, or compulsory registration if taxable supplies exceed the threshold; appoint a New Zealand GST agent and provide a NZ address for service if no physical presence exists. Step 5: The IRD will issue the GST registration number (same numeric as the IRD number) and the entity will file periodic GST returns (GST101) online. No separate VAT ID ledger exists in NZ.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: the CK_IC offshore International Company is classified as a non-resident foreign company outside the NZ corporate income tax base. NZ applies a source-based income tax to foreign-registered entities: all foreign-source project cargo freight, demurrage, deviation, and heavy-haul international logistics income derived offshore is exempt from NZ income tax, provided no NZ P.E. exists, no director meetings/management are held in NZ, and no NZ-source income is derived. Any NZ-domestic standard-rated GST activities (e.g., inland haul to port) are within the indirect tax net. Had the entity been NZ-resident, Entity_Level_Taxation at 28% with imputation credits would apply; the stated Tax_Exempt_Zone classification therefore requires strict substance documentation of offshore management and control.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity classification. Available statutory elections: (i) voluntary GST registration election (GST Act 1985 s 51(3)) below the NZD 60,000 threshold, enabling input refund recovery for non-residents with a taxable activity; (ii) GST return basis election — cash (payments) basis vs invoice basis (s 19, generally mandatory > NZD 2 million turnover); (iii) GST group registration election (s 55) for affiliated NZ entities to file a single consolidated GST return; (iv) export zero-rating election under s 11 for goods exported within 28 days and for offshore services to non-residents; (v) Look-Through Company election (Income Tax Act 2007 subpart HA) — only for NZ-resident closely held companies (max 5 shareholders) converting entity-level taxation to pass-through treatment; (vi) no hybrid mismatch election is generally available — NZ's hybrid mismatch integrity rules (subpart FH) instead deny deductions or impose inclusions automatically for cross-border dual-inclusion structures; structural changes creating hybrid entities must be disclosed to the IRD. The CK_IC, as a non-resident offshore trader, may most relevantly exercise the voluntary GST registration election and the s 11 export zero-rating positions; it cannot elect LTC treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (New Zealand dollars) of total taxable supplies in any 12-month period, including projected/turnover from intended activity, if the non-resident carries on a taxable activity in NZ. Registration is compulsory at or above this threshold; the non-resident may voluntarily register below it to recover input GST. No sub-national (state/city) sales tax threshold applies — NZ is a unitary national GST system.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% GST) for the CK_IC project cargo logistics business requires satisfaction of GST Act 1985 s 11 criteria: (i) goods — supply of goods exported from NZ by the supplier (or delivered as ship's stores) with physical export within 28 days of the time of supply, proven by a Customs Export Declaration and bill of lading/air waybill evidencing final destination outside NZ; (ii) services — supply to a non-resident customer at a location outside NZ at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not directly connected with NZ real property or moveable personal property, and are not received by the non-resident while in NZ in connection with a taxable activity carried on here; (iii) international transport — freight, charter, stevedoring, and handling services for the offshore leg of OOG/heavy-haul cargo movement to or from NZ are zero-rated as international transport; the NZ-domestic leg (depot/quay to the NZ port threshold) is standard-rated unless it forms part of an export through-transport contract and is separately disclosed as zero-rated international carriage. The exporter must retain the outward Customs Export Declaration, the bill of lading/waybill, commercial/project contract, foreign-currency or NZD settlement evidence, and a Letter of Credit/inward remittance certificate to demonstrate that the recipient is non-resident and consideration is attributable to the exported goods or offshore services; voluntary or compulsory GST registration is required to support the refund claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Customs Service electronic outward entry via TRADEX/Trade Single Window, with OOG cargo manifest) — statutory evidence of physical export.
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/breakbulk or multimodal through bill for out-of-gauge heavy lift cargo, issued to the non-resident project owner) or Air Waybill where airfreight is used.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign-currency settlement proof, or Letter of Credit documentary drawing evidence) — accepted by IRD audit as substantiating the export sale and the 180-day FX repatriation milestone.
  • Commercial invoice, project logistics services agreement and packing/flat-rack or breakbulk survey report evidencing the OOG scope and the non-resident counter-party.
  • Shipping/transport movement records (vessel stowage plan, sailing sheet, rail haul manifest) evidencing export within the 28-day statutory window.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no statutory pre-clearance API obligation for invoices in NZ. E-invoicing is voluntary and implemented via the Peppol network (NZ e-Invoicing interoperability standard); IRD accepts electronic invoices if authenticity and integrity are maintained, and records must be retained for 7 years. GST returns (GST101) are filed electronically through myIR or IRD-enabled accounting software; customs outward declarations are cleared through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window (TRADEX), while Inland Revenue receives export data by data exchange. For high-value OOG project export cargo, the exporter should also transmit the advance export/cargo manifest (ACH / Customs export entry) prior to vessel departure.

IRD audit verification of zero-rating and GST refund claims for non-resident exporters focuses on: physical export within the 28-day statutory window; genuineness and offshore location of the foreign recipient; absence of NZ-domestic service components; and, in bank-documented project cargo contracts, correspondence between the contract payment milestones (wire/Letter of Credit), inward remittance, and the declared consideration. Where goods are not exported within 28 days, the supply ceases to be zero-rated and the full consideration becomes standard-rated 15% output tax, assessed retrospectively with use-of-money interest. Incorrect/inflated refund claims expose the CK_IC to Tax Administration Act 1994 civil penalties and retrospective clawback: not taking reasonable care 20%; unacceptable tax position 20%; gross carelessness 40%; abusive tax position 100%; evasion 150% of the shortfall; plus late payment and use-of-money interest (underpayment rate approximately 10.7% per annum, adjusted quarterly). Criminal prosecution applies to fraudulent zero-rating or knowingly false export documentation — first offence fines up to NZD 50,000 and subsequent offences up to NZD 100,000 (and equivalent imprisonment for individuals), with director/agent personal liability in cases of aiding, abetting, or false GST refund applications. IRD assessment periods: generally four years (Tax Administration Act 1994 s 108), but no time bar for evasion or fraudulent/wilfully misleading returns, enabling an unlimited retrospective clawback. Additionally, the general anti-avoidance rule (Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1 / GA 1) may reconstruct the arrangement if the zero-rated offshore structure or the 180-day FX deferral is used to create GST timing or income-tax advantages without commercial substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a fully freely convertible G10 currency. Exchange controls were abolished in 1984, and neither the current account nor the capital account is subject to registration, licensing, quantitative limits, or prior approval for non-residents. Profit remittance from an International Company (IC) holding a New Zealand bank account is unrestricted: dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, and loan redemptions may be wired abroad without any exchange control consent, subject only to statutory tax withholding (Non-Resident Withholding Tax on interest and dividends, Approved Issuer Levy on eligible debt securities, and the thin-capitalisation rules in Part 4F of the Income Tax Act 2007). The only practical constraints are: (a) the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which requires consent for acquisitions of significant business assets (value threshold approximately NZD 100 million) or of sensitive land; (b) CRS/AEOI due diligence reporting by the New Zealand bank; and (c) AML/CFT source-of-funds verification performed by New Zealand reporting entities. For a project-cargo and heavy-haul logistics entity, no Overseas Investment Office consent is triggered by an ordinary equity injection or working capital advance, and the entity may freely hold NZD, USD, EUR, and other currencies and switch treasury positions without restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender or conversion rules exist in New Zealand law. There is no statutory timeline for repatriating export receipts and no mandatory conversion ratio into NZD. An Out-of-Gauge heavy industrial project cargo logistics provider may retain all offshore invoice settlement balances in the currencies received, may hold foreign currency accounts indefinitely, and may deploy funds externally without restriction. The only mandatory obligations are statistical in nature: exporters must respond to RBNZ balance-of-payments surveys when formally selected, and the IRD transfer pricing rules (Sections GC 6 to GC 14 of the Income Tax Act 2007) require arm's-length pricing on related-party cross-border receivables. None of these obligations require surrender, conversion, or repatriation of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) pursuant to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989, operating in coordination with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE) for AML/CFT supervision, and with the Toitū Te Whenua Overseas Investment Office for inbound foreign investment screening. New Zealand has no discrete statutory foreign exchange control regulator; the RBNZ exercises prudential oversight, monetary policy, and payment system settlement through the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium as an ancillary conduit; Low as a primary settlement bank. New Zealand law has no dedicated electronic money institution (EMI) licensing category. Offshore digital multi-currency platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Wise Platform, ClearJunction, Currencycloud) may lawfully serve the International Company only if they: (a) are registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008; (b) maintain an AML/CFT compliance programme supervised by a New Zealand supervisor; and (c) route all NZD settlement through a licensed New Zealand bank because they are not members of the RBNZ's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS). For an OOG project-cargo treasury, an offshore EMI can efficiently hold multi-currency master accounts and execute supplier payments in less-liquid corridors; however, the statutory protection of funds, insolvency recourse, and NZD clearing finality are materially inferior to a tier-1 New Zealand bank. The recommended architecture is a tier-1 New Zealand bank for NZD/ESAS final settlement, letter-of-credit issuance, and escrow, supplemented by one regulated EMI for foreign-currency pass-through in non-NZD supplier corridors, with all balances swept nightly back to the New Zealand bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the inward equity or funding wire: the contributor's bank initiates a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank covering) to the New Zealand bank's correspondent account (typically a New York or Singapore correspondent for USD, and a London or EU correspondent for EUR), which then credits the beneficiary International Company account in Wellington or Auckland in NZD via the RBNZ Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS). The credit instruction must reference the share-allotment resolution number and the Companies Office filing; the recommended ISO 20022 purpose code is EQUI (equity-related capital) with the remittance information field carrying the phrase 'Capital Contribution - Share Subscription per Board Resolution [date]'. For NZD amounts above the large-value threshold, the bank executes via ESAS wholesale high-value settlement; for sub-threshold amounts, the Bulk Electronic Clearing System (BECS) is used. The verification package must include: the original MT103; the SWIFT payment confirmation; the share register updated within 20 working days under Section 86 of the Companies Act 1993; the Return of Allotment (CSR) filed with the Companies Office within 20 working days; the allotment board resolution; an IRD Non-Resident Withholding Tax declaration confirming the contribution is equity rather than debt; and, where relevant, confirmation from the Toitū Te Whenua Overseas Investment Office that consent is not required because the transaction is an ordinary trading-company equity injection below the NZD 100 million significant-business-assets threshold.Trade settlements execute across the following rails: (1) SWIFT MT103/MT202 for milestone and vendor wires, cleared through the New Zealand bank's CHIPS/Fedwire or TARGET2 membership; (2) commercial documentary Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) for imported heavy-haul equipment (SPMT axle lines, mobile cranes, rigging gear) and for pre-shipment financing of out-of-gauge modules; (3) standby Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT760) and performance bank guarantees for the project bid and performance milestone obligations; and (4) domestic NZD payments to New Zealand subcontractors, port authorities, and rail operators via the Bulk Electronic Clearing System (BECS) for standard wires or the wholesale ESAS high-value stream where urgency and amount demand. Billing and reconciliation are denominated in the contract currency (usually USD or EUR), and the treasury uses a nightly sweep to concentrate balances into the Operational Account. Each outbound wire must carry a precise invoice and milestone reference together with Incoterms shipment evidence (bill of lading, air waybill, CMR) to survive audit review. The bank's Trade and Working Capital desk maintains, in parallel, the letter-of-credit register, discounting schedules, and freight-forwarding documentation in escrow.Outright FX forward contracts (vanilla deliverables) in NZD/USD and NZD/EUR for tenors up to 24 months, customised to project milestone dates and documented under a New Zealand bank FX Master AgreementNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) - expressly permitted for hedging the IC's USD flows against less-liquid currencies of the project-cargo corridors (IDR, VND, THB, KZT, INR) where offshore deliverability is unavailable; New Zealand banks transact these under an ISDA 2002 master agreement with a Credit Support AnnexCross-currency swaps (NZD/USD, EUR/USD) - permitted for matching the funding currency of the IC's capital and trade-finance facility against the revenue currency; requires ISDA 2002 master documentation with a standard credit support annexEuropean-style FX options - permitted for hedging contingent or tendered milestone cash flows; premiums paid upfront with a bilateral ISDA early-termination agreement; exotic barrier instruments are not permitted without prior treasury-committee resolutionFX swaps (swap deposits, spot-next/forward-next) - permitted for rolling short-term NZD/USD liquidity positions on the same ESAS settlement date without altering the underlying hedge book

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium as an ancillary conduit; Low as a primary settlement bank. New Zealand law has no dedicated electronic money institution (EMI) licensing category. Offshore digital multi-currency platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Wise Platform, ClearJunction, Currencycloud) may lawfully serve the International Company only if they: (a) are registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008; (b) maintain an AML/CFT compliance programme supervised by a New Zealand supervisor; and (c) route all NZD settlement through a licensed New Zealand bank because they are not members of the RBNZ's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS). For an OOG project-cargo treasury, an offshore EMI can efficiently hold multi-currency master accounts and execute supplier payments in less-liquid corridors; however, the statutory protection of funds, insolvency recourse, and NZD clearing finality are materially inferior to a tier-1 New Zealand bank. The recommended architecture is a tier-1 New Zealand bank for NZD/ESAS final settlement, letter-of-credit issuance, and escrow, supplemented by one regulated EMI for foreign-currency pass-through in non-NZD supplier corridors, with all balances swept nightly back to the New Zealand bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% – New Zealand standard corporate income tax rate (Income Tax Act 2007, Schedule 1, Part A).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% – statutory New Zealand non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on gross dividends paid to non-residents. Resident withholding tax (RWT) on dividends to New Zealand-resident shareholders is 33%, with a 28% corporate rate for inter-company distributions. Under most New Zealand double tax agreements, the treaty dividend rate is 15% for portfolio holdings and 5% for substantial shareholdings (typically 10% or more, varying by treaty).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$10 million – the threshold at which Inland Revenue expects contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File / Master File equivalent) for cross-border related-party transactions in an income year. Country-by-Country Reporting applies to New Zealand-headed multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of NZ$1.1 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate Gulf- or Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations. However, the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA 2007 s BG 1), CFC/FIF attribution rules, and the shareholder continuity and business continuity tests impose a de facto economic-substance requirement. A Tax_Exempt_Zone International Company must demonstrate genuine operational substance – real board control, decision-making, substantive books and records, and no central management and control exercised from New Zealand – otherwise the Commissioner may reclassify the entity as New Zealand-resident under s YD 2 and assess worldwide income at 28%, with shortfall penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misstatement

Statutory sanction: On conviction under Tax Administration Act 1994 s 143A: fine up to NZ$50,000 for an individual and NZ$100,000 for a body corporate, and/or imprisonment up to five years. Civil shortfall penalty of 100% of the tax shortfall, plus use-of-money interest and full recovery of unpaid tax by distress and attachment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct false returns face personal criminal liability, restitution orders, travel bans pending prosecution, and civil veil-piercing to recover diverted project-cargo profits from personal assets.
Sham Offshore Shell / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may disregard the entity or arrangement under s BG 1 and s GA 1 ITA 2007, reassess all global profits at 28% as if the entity were New Zealand-resident, disallow loss offsets, impose shortfall penalties up to 100% (or 40% for an abusive tax position), and trigger deregistration under the Companies Act 1993.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused or knowingly facilitated the sham may be personally assessed under Tax Administration Act 1994 s 150 for unpaid PAYE and GST, subjected to travel bans, disqualified from directorship for up to ten years, or convicted for aiding tax evasion, with civil asset tracing and recovery against personal estates.
Non-Arm's-Length Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue adjusts consideration to the arm's-length amount under s GC 7 ITA 2007, raising additional tax at 28%. Shortfall penalties of 20% (unacceptable tax position) up to 100% (evasion) may apply, together with use-of-money interest on underpaid tax. Deficient or non-existent transfer-pricing documentation triggers further documentation penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally engineered the pricing architecture are treated as parties to the avoidance arrangement and may be held personally liable for shortfall penalties where the company is insolvent; travel bans and restraint orders apply where evasion-level conduct is established.
AML/CFT Breach and Money Laundering Through Invoice-Led Remittance

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009: up to NZ$2,000,000 for a body corporate and NZ$200,000 for an individual per contravention. Criminal money-laundering conviction under Crimes Act 1961 s 243 carries imprisonment up to seven years and fines; asset forfeiture follows under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers face personal criminal exposure where they knew or were reckless about tainted funds, together with travel bans, asset freezing, civil recovery, director disqualification, and extradition risk in cross-border OOG logistics payment chains.
