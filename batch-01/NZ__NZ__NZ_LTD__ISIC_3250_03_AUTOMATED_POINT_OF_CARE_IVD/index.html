Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox may be legally accepted for address purposes provided the provider is physically situated in New Zealand and is authorized to accept service of process and legal notices on behalf of the company. However, for this specific industry (manufacture of microfluidic cartridges and immunoassay analyzers under ISIC 3250), the Inland Revenue Department and the New Zealand Companies Office expect a physical operating presence, such as a manufacturing, laboratory, or warehousing facility, due to the medical device regulatory regime and the goods and services tax (GST) registration requirements; banks providing corporate accounts typically reject a standalone virtual office as the sole registered office or principal place of business because they require a physical site visit, a local director's employment or operational address, and evidence of substantive activity. Therefore, a virtual office is permissible only as a supplementary registered-address service, not as the sole commercial substance; the ultimate beneficial owner must maintain a substantive physical place of operations in New Zealand or demonstrate a genuine local business purpose to satisfy the anti-avoidance provisions of the Income Tax Act 2007.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity to obtain a New Zealand IRD number (which serves as both income tax and GST registration number), the entity must submit an application to the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) using the appropriate form (e.g., IR742 for non-resident companies). The application requires: (1) certified copies of the certificate of incorporation and constitution, (2) details of all directors and shareholders, (3) a New Zealand address for service or an appointed agent's address, and (4) a signed non-resident company registration form. The IRD will issue an IRD number upon approval. For GST, the entity must separately register if its taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period, or voluntarily. Registration is completed online via myIR. If the entity does not have a permanent establishment in New Zealand, it may need to appoint a local tax agent, though this is not mandatory for registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from New Zealand are zero-rated for GST if they are exported within 28 days of the time of supply and the supplier holds evidence of export. Services supplied to non-residents who are outside New Zealand at the time of supply are zero-rated if the services are performed outside New Zealand or are directly connected with land or goods outside New Zealand. For IVD equipment manufacturing, exports of physical goods qualify for zero-rating upon compliance with export documentation requirements.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency

No mandatory e-invoicing; voluntary Peppol framework; no real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping.

If zero-rating is claimed without sufficient evidence, the supplier is liable for the GST amount plus late payment penalties and interest. Fraudulent or negligent claims may result in penalties up to 150% of the tax shortfall, and criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised current and capital account with no general foreign exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no mandatory conversion of export proceeds. The New Zealand dollar is freely convertible and trades on a floating exchange rate. Non-resident shareholding in a New Zealand limited company is permitted without exchange control approval, although inbound investment in sensitive land or significant business assets may trigger screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005. Profit remittances in the form of shareholder dividends may be paid without Central Bank approval, subject to company solvency tests under the Companies Act 1993, tax compliance under the Income Tax Act 2007, and usual banking due diligence. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not operate a rationing system for foreign currency, and both resident and non-resident entities may hold foreign currency accounts with registered banks. Nevertheless, cross-border transactions must satisfy AML/CFT Act 2009 obligations, balance-of-payments statistical reporting thresholds, and tax information exchange requirements. The practical hurdles are not regulatory convertibility restrictions but rather documentary evidence of trade substance, source of funds, beneficial ownership, and tax residence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender or repatriation timeline for export proceeds in New Zealand. Exporters may retain foreign currency receivables in bank accounts, settle invoices at commercial rates, and convert to NZD as needed. However, all cross-border receipts must be identifiable for AML/CFT and tax reporting purposes, and any foreign exchange transaction above ordinary thresholds must be supported by underlying trade or investment documentation.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) acting with the Financial Markets Authority and Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade for balance-of-payments reporting

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, and Wise Business have limited but viable utility for ancillary payment flows. New Zealand does not maintain an EU-style EMI licensing regime, and these firms are not RBNZ-registered banks; they cannot issue documentary letters of credit, confirm LCs, or provide full Treasury clearing via the Reserve Bank's Exchange Settlement Account System. They may establish partnership arrangements with licensed banks to offer NZD receipt accounts, but the IVD manufacturing entity's primary treasury, escrow, trade finance, and Fx risk management infrastructure should remain with a Tier-1 New Zealand registered bank. For medium-risk enhanced due diligence under AML/CFT, reliance on an unregulated non-bank fintech without direct ESAS access would be a compliance weakness and is not recommended as the primary clearing rail.

They may establish partnership arrangements with licensed banks to offer NZD receipt accounts, but the IVD manufacturing entity's primary treasury, escrow, trade finance, and Fx risk management infrastructure should remain with a Tier-1 New Zealand registered bank. For medium-risk enhanced due diligence under AML/CFT, reliance on an unregulated non-bank fintech without direct ESAS access would be a compliance weakness and is not recommended as the primary clearing rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No separate statutory de minimis threshold for filing Master File/Local File exists; Inland Revenue expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for any related-party cross-border transaction of material significance. Mandatory Country-by-Country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of NZ$1.2 billion or more, which in IRD practice is the operational threshold for Master File and Local File documentation for large groups.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply Economic Substance Regulations of the UAE type; however, the Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1 general anti-avoidance rule and s GA 1 reconstruction power, together with the transfer pricing provisions in s GC 13–GC 17, require that the New Zealand entity possess real managerial control, perform substantive manufacturing or distribution functions, bear economically significant risk, and have the actual assets and employees to generate the income reported.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Tax Shortfall

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty of 100% of the evaded tax, plus use-of-money interest from the due date of the unpaid tax; criminal prosecution under s 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994 carries a maximum fine of NZ$50,000 (or the equivalent of the benefit gained) and imprisonment for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors, and managing agents involved in or directing the evasion can be jointly prosecuted as parties to the offence; upon conviction they may face imprisonment, significant personal fines, director disqualification under the Companies Act 1993, and court-imposed travel bans or asset freezing orders.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Stripping Violation

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue can reprice the related-party transaction to the arm's-length result under s GC 13 and deny excess interest deductions under the thin capitalisation rules. The resulting shortfall penalty is 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% where the arrangement is an abusive tax position or constitutes tax avoidance; use-of-money interest accrues.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false transfer pricing documents or who cause the company to adopt a tax position without reasonable care can be personally liable for penalties to the extent they are knowingly involved; knowingly abetting evasion carries criminal sanctions including fines and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Substance Structure

Statutory sanction: The entire arrangement may be voided under s BG 1, with reconstruction of income and denial of treaty relief where the residence or ownership is artificial; the corporate tax shortfall is assessed at 28% and an abusive tax position penalty of 100% applies; CFC attribution may further impute undistributed offshore profits to the NZ shareholders.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for fraudulent misrepresentation in the incorporation or tax filings, disqualification for a term under s 383 of the Companies Act 1993, recovery of dissipated corporate assets via civil claw-release actions, and, where criminality is proven, imprisonment.
AML / Counter-Terrorism Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a body corporate can face a fine up to NZ$10 million or twice the value of the property involved, while an individual officer is liable on conviction to a fine up to NZ$300,000 and/or imprisonment for up to two years for serious or repeated violations.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior compliance officers can be prosecuted personally for failing to implement a compliant AML/CFT programme; unremitted penalty debt can result in director disqualification, sea/airport departure bans through enforcement warrants, and personal fiscal restitution orders.
