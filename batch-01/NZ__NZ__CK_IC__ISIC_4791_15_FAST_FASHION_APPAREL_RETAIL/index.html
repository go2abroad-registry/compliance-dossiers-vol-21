Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered office must be a physical address in New Zealand capable of accepting legal service; a PO Box or bare commercial mail-forwarding address is insufficient. For ISIC 4791 fast-fashion e-commerce, Inland Revenue may accept a genuine virtual office only when a licensed registered agent maintains a supervised physical location in New Zealand open during business hours, while major banks generally reject a mail-only virtual office for business account opening. A valid virtual office arrangement must be paired with a serviced office lease, physical presence records, e-commerce sales and inventory evidence, GST filings and UBO residential address disclosure; otherwise both IRD and banks will refuse commercial substance.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account rule applies. The Companies Act 1993 does not prescribe a floor for issued share capital. The initial capital is set at NZD 1.00 divided into one (1) ordinary share, paid in full at allotment as recorded in the board minutes and share register; subscriber funds are credited before the issue of the share certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Where a physical office or fulfilment warehouse is occupied: (i) building warrant of fitness (BWOF) with annual fire safety certification by a registered independent qualified person (IQP) and Form 12A compliance schedule lodgement with the territorial authority; (ii) compliance with the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 (HSWA), including a written hazard register, worker engagement, first-aid arrangements, and electrical certification of fit-out by an EWRB-licensed electrician; (iii) display of evacuation schemes and emergency lighting certification per the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017. For a pure home-office model, the territorial authority may require a minor home occupation certificate if customer traffic occurs; this does not apply to pure online fulfilment.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account rule applies. The Companies Act 1993 does not prescribe a floor for issued share capital. The initial capital is set at NZD 1.00 divided into one (1) ordinary share, paid in full at allotment as recorded in the board minutes and share register; subscriber funds are credited before the issue of the share certificate.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No dedicated industrial licensing board applies to electronic retail of apparel. Regulatory oversight is split among the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE) for consumer and workplace law, the relevant territorial authority (district or city council) for premises and signage, and Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) for fire safety.

Category: None statutorily mandated for an e-commerce-only retailer. A physical warehousing presence (if any) may require a Building Warrant of Fitness (BWOF) and an occupancy certificate under the Building Act 2004; no separate retail licence is required under New Zealand law.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; customer portal myIR at ird.govt.nz; supported by New Zealand Customs Service for border-related registrations and the Trade Single Window for customs declarations.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident or international company must obtain an IRD number (TIN) by registering through myIR using the non-resident company registration route or the approved non-individual registration form IR742. Required inputs include the certificate of incorporation or equivalent foreign registration evidence, registered office and principal place of business, director and ultimate beneficial ownership details, a New Zealand address for service or agent appointment if carrying on business in New Zealand, and simultaneous applications for GST registration and a Customs client code if importing/exporting. The IRD number is typically issued instantly online or within 10 working days for manual review; the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) is obtained from the Companies Office and is not an IRD number.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A non-resident or international company must obtain an IRD number (TIN) by registering through myIR using the non-resident company registration route or the approved non-individual registration form IR742. Required inputs include the certificate of incorporation or equivalent foreign registration evidence, registered office and principal place of business, director and ultimate beneficial ownership details, a New Zealand address for service or agent appointment if carrying on business in New Zealand, and simultaneous applications for GST registration and a Customs client code if importing/exporting. The IRD number is typically issued instantly online or within 10 working days for manual review; the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) is obtained from the Companies Office and is not an IRD number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available in New Zealand. A qualifying closely-held New Zealand company may elect look-through company (LTC) status under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, which provides pass-through treatment for income tax purposes. For an international company classified as Tax_Exempt_Zone, that status is recognized only to the extent the entity has no New Zealand taxable presence and no New Zealand-source income; if it creates a fixed establishment or carries on business in New Zealand, it is taxable on that New Zealand-source income and may be required to register under the Companies Act. Hybrid and branch mismatch rules contained in the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 may apply to align tax outcomes with counterpart jurisdictions and prevent double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in New Zealand in the preceding 12 months or expected to be supplied in the next 12 months; a separate NZD 60,000 threshold applies to offshore suppliers of remote services supplied to New Zealand-resident consumers, with collection through an approved marketplace or direct GST registration where applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods applies when a GST-registered supplier exports the goods or supplies them to a non-resident for export, provided the supplier or its agent removes the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply and the goods are actually exported within 12 months; certain services supplied to non-residents are also zero-rated when they are performed outside New Zealand, other than services connected with New Zealand land or services physically performed in New Zealand to an individual present in New Zealand. The supplier must obtain and retain sufficient export evidence within the statutory period to substantiate the 0% rate.

  • New Zealand Customs export declaration via the Trade Single Window/CUSR showing tariff classification, valuation, destination country, and departure date
  • Bill of lading or air waybill issued by the carrier evidencing export from New Zealand and the overseas consignee
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT/MT103 bank advice, or foreign currency receipt evidence matching the transaction
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing goods, quantities, prices, consignor, and consignee information
  • Supporting export entries, port or terminal handling records, vessel or aircraft departure records, and overseas customs entry evidence retained for 7 years

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping exists in New Zealand. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Peppol framework endorsed by IRD; GST returns are filed periodically through myIR without real-time transactional clearance. GST invoices must comply with the invoice content rules in the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, and e-invoices exchanged through the Peppol network are recognized as compliant invoices.

IRD and New Zealand Customs may jointly audit zero-rated export claims. If the required export evidence is not obtained within the statutory period, if goods fail to leave New Zealand within 28 days of supply, or if goods are later returned to New Zealand, the supplier must remit output GST at 15% and may be charged interest. Penalties include shortfall penalties of 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, and 150% for evasion or abusive tax positions; late filing penalties are also imposed. Fraudulent zero-rating arrangements, false invoicing, or refund manipulation may trigger criminal prosecution, NZ Customs penalties, retrospective GST adjustments extending to prior periods, and deregistration under the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar is fully convertible on both current account and capital account. New Zealand maintains no exchange controls and adheres to IMF Article VIII obligations, meaning profit remittances, dividends, capital returns, and intercompany payments may be freely transferred in any permitted currency. There are no statutory hurdles to converting NZD into USD, AUD, EUR, GBP, or major Asian currencies. Practical remittance hurdles arise only from anti-money-laundering and know-your-customer requirements, source-of-funds documentation, tax-residency filing expectations, and bank-level risk appetite toward tax-exempt or offshore International Companies. For the CK_IC fast-fashion e-commerce model, the treasury should maintain consolidated evidence of economic substance, an accurate UBO register, shareholder resolutions, and arm's-length transfer-pricing documentation to prevent bank-level delays on dividend distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no minimum percentage of export receipts that must be converted into New Zealand dollars. Exporters, including cross-border online retailers, may lawfully retain receipts in the currency of collection. For the CK_IC, all export proceeds should be documented with commercial invoices, parcel-level shipping and customs records, payment-acquirer settlement reports, and platform payout statements from Shopify Payments or Klarna. That documentation will support later outgoing remittances and satisfy bank-source-of-funds and AML/CFT checks.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), acting as the central bank and monetary authority for the Realm of New Zealand. There is no separate foreign-exchange control authority in New Zealand. Any licensed bank or financial institution operating in the Cook Islands, which is the incorporation nexus of the CK_IC International Company, is supervised locally by the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission under the Cook Islands Banking Act, with the Reserve Bank of New Zealand remaining the currency-issuing and monetary-policy authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for operational payment flows and treasury clearing, but Low as the sole capital-escrow and liquidity repository. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Platform, Airwallex, and similar payment-technology providers are not registered banks in New Zealand unless separately licensed under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand framework or the Deposit Takers Act regime. They may be used for money transmission, multicurrency settlement, and business payment accounts if they hold a New Zealand Financial Service Provider registration, are enrolled in an approved dispute-resolution scheme, and are reporting entities under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. The CK_IC may lawfully route e-commerce collections through such platforms, but must retain a primary New Zealand registered bank account for share capital, material tax payments, loan covenants, and hedging margin. A core legal constraint is that EMI balances are generally not protected by the New Zealand depositor compensation regime and may be subject to unilateral account freezes under sanctions or platform terms.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections should be routed through a fully compliant SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder or parent treasury to the CK_IC account at the chosen New Zealand bank. The remittance narrative must clearly state 'SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — CK_IC / NO GOODS OR SERVICES' to avoid classification as a trade payment. No New Zealand exchange-control approval is required, but the bank will require the beneficiary account number, SWIFT BIC, and, for USD payments, the US correspondent ABA/CHIPS details. The supporting verification bundle must include a certificate of incumbency, certified UBO register, board resolution approving share issuance, the subscription agreement, source-of-funds bank statements, and a trust-structure map if capital is routed through a family trust. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a standard 31 March balance date, the return is due by the 7th day of the seventh month following balance date (7 July) where no tax agent is engaged; with a tax agent extension, the filing date is 31 March of the following income year. Provisional tax is payable in three instalments: 28 August, 15 January and 7 May. Terminal tax is due 7 February after the 31 March balance date (non-tax-agent) or 7 April (tax-agent).
