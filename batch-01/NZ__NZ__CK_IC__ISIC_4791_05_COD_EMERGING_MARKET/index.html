Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, an operating arm of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), accessible through the New Zealand Register of Companies.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No 105), particularly sections 10-16 (Incorporation and Registration), sections 42-52 (Constitution), sections 125-143 (Directors' Duties), sections 154-156 (Share Capital and Issues of Shares), and sections 189-200 (Financial Reporting and Record-Keeping); supplemented by the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 for any offer of securities to the public.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NZCN1 – Application for Registration of a New Zealand Company (electronic lodgement via the New Zealand Companies Office online portal), together with the Company Constitution (if adopted) or default constitution under Schedule 1 of the Companies Act 1993, and signed consents of directors and shareholders.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand law imposes no minimum issued or paid-up share capital for a private limited company incorporated as an International Company (IC). The board may issue one or more shares for such consideration as the board determines adequate, including par-value-less shares, provided the share issue and allotment are recorded in the share register in accordance with sections 53-56 and 94-97 of the Companies Act 1993. There is no blocked escrow capital account requirement.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable at the incorporation stage. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand; online portal: myIR Secure Online Services (myIR.ird.govt.nz) for GST and income tax registration; New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS) for export declarations and border control.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity seeking to operate in New Zealand, first confirm whether an economic nexus exists: registration for Goods and Services Tax (GST) is mandatory from the effective date of registration if the entity is carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand and actual or projected turnover exceeds NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period. A non-resident entity may also voluntarily register for GST if it carries on a taxable activity, subject to IRD acceptance. For income tax purposes, a non-resident company not carrying on a New Zealand taxable activity is not ordinarily required to obtain an IRD number; if a fixed establishment or taxable activity arises, the company must register with the IRD. The online registration procedure is conducted in myIR Secure Online Services: establish a myIR login as a non-resident individual or use an IRD-approved agent; select the relevant registration type, such as GST registration; provide company constitutive documents, registered address, director details, country of tax residence, actual or expected turnover, commencement date, and, where a physical presence exists, the address of the New Zealand fixed establishment; nominate a New Zealand agent for service of notices if the entity has no place of business in New Zealand; submit the application. The IRD will issue an IRD number and GST registration number upon acceptance. No varying subnational tax registration is required because New Zealand has a unitary national taxation system with no provincial or state tax authorities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box election. A consolidated group election can be made under the Income Tax Act 2007 for wholly owned groups. For GST, registered persons may elect to be treated as a GST group under Part 6 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Hybrid mismatch rules, introduced by the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018, do not give the taxpayer an election but impose conformity adjustments if a hybrid or reversed hybrid gives rise to income non-recognition or double deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in the 12-month registration calculation period, including zero-rated exports and remote services supplied to New Zealand-resident consumers

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the goods are exported by the supplier in the course of the supplier's taxable activity, and the goods are exported before the time of supply or within 28 days after the time of supply. The exporter must retain evidence of export and is required by IRD practice to link each export supply to actual physical movement of the goods out of New Zealand. Supplies of qualifying offshore services to non-resident consumers can also be zero-rated if the services are provided to a non-resident person and are not physically performed in New Zealand, or if an exception applies; however, for a mail-order retail business with cash-on-delivery settlement, the primary zero-rating route is the physical export of goods and the availability of contemporaneous evidence of export, foreign receipt and settlement.

  • New Zealand Customs Service export declaration lodged through the Trade Single Window (NZCS) showing the sender, consignee, tariff classification, quantity, value and destination country
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the ocean or air carrier naming the New Zealand exporter as shipper
  • Commercial invoice and packing list generated for the export sale
  • Signed proof of delivery or logistics tracking report from the local fulfilment or courier partner for cash-on-delivery consignments
  • Receipt or settlement report from the cross-border payment aggregator Airwallex or the local logistics remittance rail evidencing the foreign-currency remittance and repatriation
  • SWIFT MT103 or equivalent bank transfer confirmation if Airwallex or local logistics rails are not used

No real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping is mandated by the IRD. GST-registered suppliers must issue tax invoices in the forms specified in sections 24, 24B, and 24D of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, retaining enough detail to verify output tax and zero-rating. The New Zealand e-Invoicing framework is a voluntary, PEPPOL-based network; there is no government API pre-approval system for invoice clearance. Export documentation must, however, be transmitted to the NZCS via the Trade Single Window.

If the IRD or NZCS finds that zero-rating was claimed on a supply that was not physically exported, or that the export evidence is deficient or fabricated, the IRD may issue a reassessment requiring the supplier to bring output GST to account at 15%, effective from the original time of supply, and may impose late payment interest under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Shortfall penalties apply for lack of reasonable care (20% of the resulting tax shortfall), an abusive tax position (50%), and evasion or fraudulent tax position (150%), with no time bar for assessments in cases involving fraud or tax evasion. False or misleading export declarations to NZCS can also be prosecuted under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, exposing the filer to substantial fines and potentially imprisonment, and the GST clawback is not limited to the original supplier if a knowingly false zero-rating arrangement is found.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible on both the current account and the capital account, reflecting New Zealand's standing commitment to OECD capital movement liberalization codes. There are no statutory or administrative hurdles to converting NZD into foreign currency, no surrender obligations, and no restrictions on the acquisition or holding of offshore foreign currency accounts by residents or non-residents. Profit and dividend remittances by non-resident shareholders are permitted without exchange control approval; the only constraints arise from AML/CFT verification, non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends sourced in New Zealand (commonly 15% under domestic law, reducible under double tax agreements), and the requirement that the International Company structure avoid deriving New Zealand-source income that would destroy its tax-exempt characterization. The residual practical hurdle is liquidity in exotic currency pairs, which transits through global FX centers, and the pricing impact of NZD as a small, commodity-linked currency traded largely against USD, AUD, EUR, and JPY.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline for the repatriation of export proceeds and prescribes no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters, including the International Company operating cross-border mail-order retail, may lawfully retain export receivables in foreign-currency settlement accounts at domestic or offshore banks, may net export proceeds against import payables under standard international banking practices, and may deploy proceeds directly to offshore logistics and supplier accounts without central bank notification. The only compliance obligations are indirect: (1) accurate declaration of trade transactions for Balance of Payments statistics where the value exceeds NZD 10,000 and the reporting bank is required to apply a purpose-of-payment code; and (2) full traceability of the use of funds under the AML/CFT Act, which obliges the reporting entity to verify the economic rationale of large or unusual cross-border receipts so that cash-on-delivery collections routed through logistics remittance partners can be reconstructed on demand.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), operating under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 as the sole monetary authority and prudential regulator for banks, insurers, and non-bank deposit takers. For foreign exchange policy, New Zealand maintains a fully liberalized, non-discretionary regime: no exchange control legislation exists, no central bank approval is required for cross-border payments, and the RBNZ does not administer surrender or repatriation mandates. Ancillary regulatory authority is exercised by the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) over registered financial service providers and by the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) as the supervisor of the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act), which governs transaction verification duties of banks, money service businesses, and payment service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility for operational disbursement rails, with material legal constraints for escrow and settlement tiering. Airwallex is registered on New Zealand's Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) as a non-deposit-taking payment service provider and is currently the recommended digital EMI for the target payment rails, enabling multi-currency wallet collection of cash-on-delivery proceeds, low-cost forex conversion, and local-currency payouts to logistics remittance partners. Wise (formerly TransferWise) and other offshore EMIs may operate into New Zealand only through partnership with licensed RBNZ banks or via an FSPR-registered intermediary; they are not authorized deposit takers and therefore cannot lawfully hold client funds as deposits in the New Zealand sense. Current-account services, capital-account escrow, trade finance products, and documentary credit issuance remain with regulated commercial banks; the blueprint therefore adopts a two-tier architecture: Tier 1 bank for statutory capital escrow, trade settlement, and liquidity, and Airwallex as the Tier 2 high-frequency clearing rail for COD receipts and logistics remittances.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Tax-exempt International Company status perceived as an offshore tax-haven vehicle with no economic substance

Root cause: Tax-exempt International Company status perceived as an offshore tax-haven vehicle with no economic substance

Defense evidence: (1) Certified certificate of incorporation and registered-agent statement proving valid existence in the Cook Islands; (2) complete register of directors, shareholders, and ultimate beneficial owners with passport copies certified by a notary; (3) legal opinion from a licensed Cook Islands counsel confirming the tax-exempt character and absence of bearer shares; (4) substantiation of active trade: supplier contracts, logistics agreements, and third-party e-commerce platform statements showing ongoing mail-order operations; (5) written explanation of the commercial purpose of the structure, including customer-country market access and settlement efficiency; (6) tax residency certificates of all ultimate beneficial owners from their respective revenue authorities; and (7) a commitment letter to provide semi-annual economic-substance declarations to the bank.
Cash-on-delivery business model flagging structuring, smurfing, or unlicensed money-service activity

Root cause: Cash-on-delivery business model flagging structuring, smurfing, or unlicensed money-service activity

Defense evidence: (1) AML/CTF policy manual of the entity acknowledging New Zealand supervisory standards and designating a compliance officer; (2) executed master service agreements with each local logistics and collection agent, including daily cash reconciliation and direct settlement obligations to the company or its processor; (3) anti-structuring procedures and transaction-monitoring thresholds embedded in the Airwallex wallet; (4) forward cash-flow forecasts and historic transaction volumes matching the COD model; (5) source-of-wealth evidence for opening inventory and working capital; (6) sample weekly remittance batches demonstrating end-to-end traceability of COD collections; and (7) confirmatory correspondence from Airwallex that its own NZ FSPR registration and AML/CFT obligations apply to the collection flow.
Opaque beneficial-ownership chain involving trusts or intermediate holding companies

Root cause: Opaque beneficial-ownership chain involving trusts or intermediate holding companies

Defense evidence: (1) Redacted trust deed or governing instrument identifying settlor, protector, and discretionary beneficiaries by class so as to permit risk rating while preserving confidentiality; (2) consolidated corporate-organization chart showing each intermediate entity with registration numbers and jurisdictions; (3) certified extract from the Companies Register of each intermediate entity naming directors and shareholders; (4) statutory declarations by each ultimate beneficial owner detailing source of funds for the capital contribution; (5) power-of-attorney matrix limiting signing authorities; (6) acknowledgment that beneficial-ownership information will be updated within 14 days of any change; and (7) a New Zealand legal practitioner's confirmation that the structure is not a prescribed trust or a vehicle for tax evasion.
High-velocity cross-border outbound payments to logistics remittance partners, triggering sanctions and counterparty screening failures

Root cause: High-velocity cross-border outbound payments to logistics remittance partners, triggering sanctions and counterparty screening failures

Defense evidence: (1) Complete and current list of approved logistics partners, courier aggregators, and local remittance agents with beneficial-ownership attestations; (2) sanctions and adverse-media screening results for every counterparty, updated on a rolling 30-day cycle; (3) narrative description of the settlement flow: inbound COD collections received by Airwallex, aggregated, then disbursed through SWIFT MT103 to supplier banks or local agents; (4) contractual prohibition on sub-delegation of collection duties without prior bank consent; (5) transaction threshold and velocity parameters to be encoded in the bank's monitoring system; and (6) quarterly attestation by the compliance officer that all remittance destinations have corresponding trade invoices or logistics fee invoices.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for initial share-capital injection: transfer from ultimate beneficial owner's personal or corporate account via SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pacs.008) to the Tier 1 bank Capital Escrow Account at ANZ or BNZ in Auckland, denominated in NZD or USD, with purpose code 'Capital contribution — direct investment in equity' mapped to the RBNZ Balance of Payments 'Direct Investment — Equity' classification. The bank requires the remitter to use a purpose-of-payment code reflecting the capital nature, a Board resolution of the International Company authorizing issuance of shares, the shareholder subscription agreement, and a source-of-funds attestation. Inbound remittances equal to or exceeding NZD 10,000 are reported by the receiving bank for statistical purposes; no RBNZ approval or foreign-investment screening is required because the entity does not control a New Zealand 'strategically important business' and no land or fishing quota is acquired. Multiple tranches are acceptable but must be individually referenced to the escrow account to avoid AML structuring flags; each tranche is released at the direction of the board signatories only after the bank verifies the share-registry update evidencing the issuance of corresponding shares.The primary clearing rail for supplier payments and inventory purchases is the SWIFT MT103 direct transfer from the Tier 1 operational account, with the beneficiary bank and the payer bank both operating under SWIFT membership; settlement currency recommended as CNY, USD, or EUR depending on the invoice currency to minimize conversion legs. For high-value or first-time-vendor transactions exceeding USD 50,000, the blueprint requires an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (documentary credit) issued by the NZ bank subject to UCP 600, with the beneficiary negotiating through its local confirming bank; this protects the International Company's cash-on-delivery margins by pre-qualifying suppliers. Airwallex operates as the Tier 2 high-frequency rail for local logistics remittances: COD collections are swept from the Airwallex wallet to the Tier 1 account daily, and logistics fee payouts are disbursed via Airwallex to the destination-country courier agents in local currency. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility for operational disbursement rails, with material legal constraints for escrow and settlement tiering. Airwallex is registered on New Zealand's Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) as a non-deposit-taking payment service provider and is currently the recommended digital EMI for the target payment rails, enabling multi-currency wallet collection of cash-on-delivery proceeds, low-cost forex conversion, and local-currency payouts to logistics remittance partners. Wise (formerly TransferWise) and other offshore EMIs may operate into New Zealand only through partnership with licensed RBNZ banks or via an FSPR-registered intermediary; they are not authorized deposit takers and therefore cannot lawfully hold client funds as deposits in the New Zealand sense. Current-account services, capital-account escrow, trade finance products, and documentary credit issuance remain with regulated commercial banks; the blueprint therefore adopts a two-tier architecture: Tier 1 bank for statutory capital escrow, trade settlement, and liquidity, and Airwallex as the Tier 2 high-frequency clearing rail for COD receipts and logistics remittances.
