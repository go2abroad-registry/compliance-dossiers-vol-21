Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital and no escrow requirement under New Zealand law. A company may incorporate with one share of no par value issued for NZ$1.00 (or unpaid/partly paid if permitted by the constitution). For an International Company operating in a tax-exempt offshore capacity, initial capital is usually the subscription monies for 100 ordinary shares at NZ$1.00 each, held in the corporate bank account; no statutory capital deposit is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ): evacuation scheme and fire safety certification for commercial premises under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017 and the Fire Safety and Evacuation Procedures Regulations 2006, including an annual evacuation trial for premises with more than 100 occupants. Building control: a building Warrant of Fitness (BWoF) and compliance schedule issued by the territorial authority under the Building Act 2004 for the leased office. Workplace safety: compliance with WorkSafe New Zealand requirements under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including a general risk assessment, emergency planning, workstation ergonomics, and in-service electrical safety testing to AS/NZS 3760.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital and no escrow requirement under New Zealand law. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) — the national tax administration of New Zealand; official online portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz; complementary business registration portal: New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) at https://www.business.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A non-resident International Company (ELF_CUSTOM_CK_IC) conducting exclusively offshore BIM/CAD engineering services without a New Zealand fixed establishment, New Zealand-sourced income, or GST nexus in New Zealand is generally outside the New Zealand tax net and is not required to obtain an IRD number. If the entity establishes a New Zealand branch, derives New Zealand-source income, supplies goods or services in New Zealand, or employs personnel in New Zealand, it must apply for an IRD number via myIR using form IR742 (for companies) or IR596 (for non-resident companies). The application must be accompanied by: (1) certificate of incorporation and constitutional documents; (2) register of directors and ultimate shareholders; (3) registered office and principal place of business address; (4) a New Zealand address for service (which may be a registered tax agent's address); and (5) evidence of a NZBN or business registration. For GST purposes, the same IRD number is used as the GST registration number; a non-resident must register through myIR when taxable supplies in New Zealand exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, including remote services supplied to New Zealand consumers. Voluntary GST registration is available to non-residents to claim input credits if the entity carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand. IRD typically processes registration within 5–10 business days; a New Zealand tax agent may file on the entity's behalf.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is statutory under the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Tax Administration Act 1994. A non-resident International Company with no New Zealand fixed establishment and no New Zealand-source income is effectively tax-exempt in New Zealand: foreign-sourced offshore income is not taxed, and no GST registration is required absent a New Zealand taxable activity. Relevant statutory elections available include: (1) GST cash-basis accounting election for businesses with taxable turnover below NZD 2 million per annum; (2) GST election to zero-rate land supplies where the recipient is a GST-registered person; (3) hybrid mismatch elections under subpart FE of the Income Tax Act 2007 to address hybrid entity or hybrid instrument mismatches; (4) branch equivalent tax elections for controlled foreign company attributions; and (5) group loss transfer elections available only to qualifying New Zealand-resident group companies. No separate 'tax-exempt zone' election exists; the tax-exempt outcome flows from the entity's non-resident status and absence of New Zealand-source activity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (annual taxable supplies in New Zealand over any 12-month period); non-resident suppliers of 'remote services' (including digital and engineering services) to New Zealand consumers also cross the mandatory registration threshold at NZD 60,000; voluntary registration is available for non-residents with a New Zealand business presence or who intend to claim input tax credits

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: GST zero-rating applies where goods are supplied for export and are physically exported from New Zealand within 28 days of supply (or such longer period as approved by Inland Revenue), provided the supplier holds contemporaneous export evidence and the recipient is a non-resident or the goods are delivered to a place outside New Zealand. Services: zero-rating applies to services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, unless the services are directly connected with New Zealand land, performed personally by a natural person physically present in New Zealand, or supplied in connection with moveable personal property situated in New Zealand. For offshore BIM/CAD structural drafting services (ISIC 7110 / NAICS 541330) delivered to a non-resident overseas client with no New Zealand land nexus and no performance of work in New Zealand, zero-rating is available where the supplier maintains a written contract, a GST tax invoice, evidence of the recipient's non-resident status, and payment evidence from outside New Zealand.

  • Customs Export Declaration (eCSD filed via the New Zealand Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/foreign exchange receipt evidencing payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial Invoice and GST tax invoice
  • Packing List
  • Independent evidence of export completion (carrier certificate, port authority confirmation, or inspection certificate)

New Zealand imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based electronic stamping, or continuous transaction controls. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates on the PEPPOL network through the New Zealand e-Invoicing framework; Inland Revenue does not pre-clear or digitally stamp tax invoices. Tax invoices, credit notes, and export substantiation records must be retained for 7 years. Where PEPPOL-format e-invoices are used, they must conform to the New Zealand e-Invoicing specification, but no government fiscal stamp or electronic clearance obligation exists.

Inland Revenue audits zero-rated export claims by cross-matching Customs export declarations against GST returns, bank receipts, and invoices. Where export documentation is deficient, goods are diverted back to New Zealand, or services do not meet the zero-rating tests, the zero-rating is reversed and GST at 15% is clawed back with use-of-money interest running from the original adjustment date. Fraudulent or deliberately false zero-rating claims attract shortfall penalties of 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, and up to 150% for an abusive tax position, plus criminal prosecution under sections 141 and 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994 with fines and imprisonment available for evasion. Export documentation must be retained for at least 7 years to support any GST zero-rating or refund claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand is an IMF Article VIII member, meaning the NZD is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There is no exchange-control restriction on current-account payments for CAD/BIM services, no requirement for central-bank allocation of foreign currency, and no restriction on the repatriation of capital, dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees. Practical remittance hurdles are documentary and tax-driven, not FX-approval-driven: the remitting bank must obtain a correct purpose code, evidence of the underlying transaction, and proof of withholding tax compliance where applicable. Non-resident dividend remittances are subject to Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) at the applicable treaty rate (commonly 15% under the double-tax agreement, 30% in the absence of treaty relief); interest remittances attract NRWT at up to 15% unless an IRD-approved treaty or approved issuer levy structure is used. Capital injections and full return of the same capital are free from tax and FX approval, provided the bank records the original source of funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters of engineering or BIM services may receive and retain USD, EUR, GBP, CNY, or any freely convertible currency in onshore or offshore foreign-currency accounts indefinitely. The only obligations are bank-level Balance of Payments International Transactions Reporting (ITR) for individual transactions above NZD 10,000 and ordinary anti-money-laundering verification of the exporter's underlying service contract. There is no requirement to convert export proceeds to NZD nor to liquidate foreign-currency balances by any date.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua) is the central bank and the statutory authority for monetary policy, prudential regulation, and the oversight of registered banks. New Zealand has no designated foreign-exchange-control agency other than the Reserve Bank acting through licensed registered banks, and no approval or reporting is required for the majority of FX transactions beyond transaction-level balance-of-payments statistical reporting undertaken by the banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Conditionally feasible as a receivables and payables payment rail, but not as a substitute for a licensed New Zealand bank for capital escrow, trade-finance issuance, or same-day NZD clearing. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Currencycloud may be used for foreign-currency disbursement and collection, provided the EMI is registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and, where applicable, holds an Australian AFSL/EMI equivalent accepted by the receiving bank. Legal constraints are material: an unregistered offshore EMI cannot open a settlement account in New Zealand, cannot issue commercial letters of credit, cannot provide a New Zealand domestic bank-account suffix, and may be blocked by tier-1 banks as an unacceptable route for capital-escrow or high-value settlement. Feasibility rating: B- (Conditionally Feasible) for operational treasury flows; D (Not Feasible) for regulated escrow, debt, and documentary-credit functions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant protocol for inbound capital injection is a SWIFT MT103 for the cash leg, or an MT202 for cover, to the New Zealand operating account clearly identified in the beneficiary field with the beneficiary bank's BIC. The remittance instruction must include a tailored purpose code aligned with the bank's New Zealand International Transactions Reporting (ITR) classification, typically 'Capital contribution' or 'Shareholder loan' for equity/debt injection and 'Professional consultancy services' for service prepayments. The narrative field (field 70) should state 'CAPITAL INJECTION - CK_IC - [Resolution Date]' or 'LOAN - CK_IC - [Facility Agreement Reference]'. The bank will verify the source of funds through the originator's bank and the corporate authority packet: board resolution, constitutional document, share register if equity, executed facility agreement if debt, UBO declaration, and source-of-wealth evidence for amounts above applicable due-diligence thresholds. For payments from a connected offshore entity, a transfer-pricing memorandum and an IRD withholding-tax position (for interest) must be annexed where the bank requires a filing-ready record. Escrowed capital should be held in a segregated Capital Escrow Account with contractual instruction that release occurs only against verified drawdown triggers approved by the board.The principal clearing rails for trade disbursements are: (1) SWIFT MT103 payments for clean contract settlement to subcontractors, software vendors, or external consultants; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP600 for high-value procurement, where the NZ bank issues a documentary credit to the seller's bank and makes payment against presented transport/acceptance documents; (3) Documentary collections (DP/DA) for supplier relationships requiring title documentation rather than an undertaking; (4) SWIFT gpi for end-to-end tracking of payments to high-risk corridors; (5) New Zealand's Electronic Settlement System (ESAS) for same-day NZD transfers to domestic beneficiaries; and (6) central bank real-time gross settlement rails where the counterparty bank is a direct Reserve Bank participant. For CAD/BIM outsourcing disbursements, an MT103 with purpose code 'Trade services' and the underlying subcontract number in field 70 is the preferred rail; commercial letters of credit are used only when the counterparty requires a formal payment undertaking.Plain-vanilla deliverable foreign-exchange forward contracts (USD/NZD, EUR/NZD, AUD/NZD, GBP/NZD, CNY/NZD) with a licensed New Zealand registered bank, with maturities of up to five years for construction or BIM delivery cycles.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies not freely deliverable in the onshore NZD market (e.g., certain emerging-market currencies), executed with an RBNZ-registered bank or an offshore bank with approved internal credit and collateralisation.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) where the New Zealand company receives or pays fixed/floating flows in different currencies to hedge long-term project financing or capitalised lease obligations.European-style FX options (call or put on USD, EUR, AUD, GBP, CNY) for contingent cash flows such as procurement bids or option-based construction milestones.FX vanilla swaps combining a spot exchange and a forward exchange to manage temporary liquidity needs when capital is injected in one currency and disbursed in another.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Conditionally feasible as a receivables and payables payment rail, but not as a substitute for a licensed New Zealand bank for capital escrow, trade-finance issuance, or same-day NZD clearing. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Currencycloud may be used for foreign-currency disbursement and collection, provided the EMI is registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and, where applicable, holds an Australian AFSL/EMI equivalent accepted by the receiving bank. Legal constraints are material: an unregistered offshore EMI cannot open a settlement account in New Zealand, cannot issue commercial letters of credit, cannot provide a New Zealand domestic bank-account suffix, and may be blocked by tier-1 banks as an unacceptable route for capital-escrow or high-value settlement. Feasibility rating: B- (Conditionally Feasible) for operational treasury flows; D (Not Feasible) for regulated escrow, debt, and documentary-credit functions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory NZD filing threshold for mandatory Local File or Master File exists in New Zealand. Inland Revenue applies the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines through subpart FG and expects contemporaneous arm's length documentation for all material cross-border related-party transactions. In practice, IRD audit attention is typically triggered where related-party sales or service fees exceed NZ$10 million in aggregate, but documentation must be able to support pricing at any level of materiality.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations of the Caribbean/UAE statutory form. Economic substance is enforced indirectly through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section BG 1), thin capitalisation rules, transfer pricing provisions, the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test, and the requirement that a company claiming New Zealand tax residence maintain its centre of effective management and real operational substance in New Zealand. A purported 'tax-exempt International Company' engaged in BIM and structural drafting without office premises, employees, or board control in New Zealand faces treaty denial, GAAR recharacterisation, and penalty exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax under the Tax Administration Act 1994; criminal prosecution with fines up to NZ$250,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years; seizure of company assets and management intervention by Inland Revenue.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in evasion face personal criminal conviction, restitution orders for the unpaid tax, Departure Prohibition Orders preventing travel from New Zealand, and civil liability for reckless trading under the Companies Act 1993.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the structure under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section BG 1); recomputation of taxable income on an arm's length basis; shortfall penalties of 40%–100% for abusive tax positions; potential strike-off of the company register.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for debts arising from reckless trading, ordered to restore company funds, subjected to travel bans, and disqualified from managing companies in New Zealand.
Transfer Pricing Fraud

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment by the Commissioner plus use-of-money interest; shortfall penalties ranging from 20% for an unacceptable tax position to 40% for gross carelessness; disallowance of non-compliant intercompany deductions.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing declarations or knowingly approve fraudulent intercompany pricing may be pursued personally for civil restitution of the tax shortfall and face criminal fraud charges.
AML and NRWT Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: The payer becomes personally liable for the un-withheld NRWT, plus shortfall penalties of up to 150%; AML/CFT Act offences attract fines up to NZ$2,000,000 per offence for entities and imprisonment for up to 7 years for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable for AML failures committed with their authority, consent, or connivance; statutory director penalties apply for unpaid NRWT under the Tax Administration Act; Departure Prohibition Orders may be issued against them.
