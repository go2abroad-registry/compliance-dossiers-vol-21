Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None — New Zealand imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital. A company may be incorporated with a single fully-paid ordinary share of nominal value NZ$1.00. No escrow deposit, capital commitment account, or bank verification account is required under the Companies Act 1993 (sections 12-15).

Funding & unlock path: No civil-defence clearance or occupational workplace licence is prescribed for remote cloud software operations. If a leased office is opened, the operator must obtain a Building Warrant of Fitness covering specified fire-safety and life-safety systems under the Building Act 2004, and must comply with the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including documented emergency planning. Service continuity planning aligned with the Civil Defence Emergency Management Act 2002 is additionally recommended to support micro-SaaS uptime commitments.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue; Te Tari Tāke), the national revenue authority of New Zealand, administered through the myIR secure online portal at www.ird.govt.nz. The New Zealand Customs Service (Tariff) separately governs export declarations and border clearance through the Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

(1) If the International Company is incorporated in New Zealand, an IRD number issues automatically upon Companies Office incorporation via the integrated IRD-registration process. (2) For a non-resident or foreign-incorporated company, submit Form IR742 'Application by a non-resident company for an IRD number' to Inland Revenue, either directly or through an approved New Zealand tax agent, together with the certificate of incorporation, registered office and principal place-of-business details, constitutional documents, and director/shareholder particulars. (3) GST registration is effected on the same IRD number by lodging a GST101A application or registering through myIR once taxable turnover reaches NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period or, alternatively, through voluntary registration. (4) No separate VAT identification number exists; the IRD number serves as the single statutory tax identification number for both income tax and GST purposes. (5) Processing typically concludes within 5–10 business days for non-resident applications lodged by an NZ agent, subject to anti-money-laundering verification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a New Zealand-incorporated International Company is ordinarily tax-resident in New Zealand and taxable at the entity level at the 28% corporate income tax rate on worldwide income. The tag 'Tax_Exempt_Zone' referenced in the entity structure metadata is not a statutory New Zealand tax category; New Zealand has no general offshore tax-exempt zone, and any such characterisation would be recharacterised under the general anti-avoidance rules in Part GA of the Income Tax Act 2007 and the associated tax-avoidance jurisprudence.

Available election forms

Look-Through Company (LTC) election under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007: a closely held New Zealand company may elect full fiscal transparency, causing profits and losses to be attributed to shareholders and removing entity-level income tax; New Zealand has no US-style 'check-the-box' election, but the LTC election is the functional New Zealand equivalent. Hybrid and branch mismatch rules in Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007 statutorily neutralise or deactivate elections that would otherwise create double non-taxation. Qualifying Company (QC) elections have been closed to new companies since 31 March 2011.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 — mandatory registration is triggered where the taxable supply of goods and services in New Zealand exceeds NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period (s 51 GSTA 1985); voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold, including for non-resident remote-services suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported tangible goods are zero-rated where the supplier proves the goods were exported from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply (s 11(1)(a)/(ca) GSTA 1985). For the SaaS service line, zero-rating applies to (i) B2B 'remote services' supplied to a non-resident recipient who receives the services outside New Zealand under an enforceable contract with the supplier and is not in New Zealand at the time of supply (s 11A(1)(k)–(l)); and (ii) B2C 'remote services' supplied over the internet or an electronic network to non-resident individuals who are outside New Zealand when the supply takes place (s 11A(1)(m)). The supply is zero-rated only where the supplier holds sufficient commercial and documentary evidence that the recipient's jurisdiction and location are foreign; supplies to New Zealand-resident consumers remain standard-rated, triggering mandatory non-resident GST registration if New Zealand consumer turnover exceeds NZD 60,000 per annum.

  • New Zealand Customs export declaration (lodged through the Customs Trade Single Window; outward entry with HS classification and quantity)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (the carrier-issued transport document evidencing physical export from New Zealand)
  • Tax invoice (or credit note) issued under ss 24/24G GSTA 1985 explicitly identifying the zero-rated supply and the zero-rating basis
  • Foreign currency sales contract, subscription, or order documentation identifying the non-resident counterparty or consumer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 bank proof evidencing external payment of the NZD or foreign-currency export consideration
  • Shipment or parcel carrier tracking and outward manifest match report evidencing cross-border transit

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, digital stamping, or government API clearance is imposed on New Zealand GST. Invoicing is governed by tax invoice rules (ss 24–24D GSTA 1985), and e-invoicing is voluntary under the Peppol interoperability framework adopted by Inland Revenue's e-Invoicing network. GST returns (GST101) are filed digitally through myIR on a six-monthly, two-monthly, or monthly cycle (monthly filing is mandatory where taxable turnover exceeds NZD 24 million per annum), with payment of GST due by the 28th day following the end of each taxable period.

Inland Revenue's GST export-audit programme scrutinises zero-rating claims for corroborative shipping and residency evidence; failure to substantiate triggers GST reassessment under s 78 GSTA 1985 (retrospective clawback plus use-of-money interest), shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 of 20% (failure to take reasonable care), 40% (unacceptable tax position), 100% (abusive tax position), or 150% (evasion), and criminal liability under s 143A for knowingly providing false GST returns, with fines up to NZD 50,000. Anti-fraud clawback extends to any refunds granted where the underlying export proceeds, including the 180-day repatriation evidence, are later reversed, repatriated onshore, or applied to New Zealand-resident consumption.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions, having accepted Article VIII obligations of the International Monetary Fund's Articles of Agreement. There are no exchange control laws, no prior central bank approval requirements, no capital repatriation restrictions, and no limitation on holding foreign currency accounts domestically or offshore. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a freely floating fiat currency with no official band. For the contracting International Company (IC) structured as a Tax-Exempt Zone entity with a Cook Islands legal origin and a New Zealand operational nexus, profit remittance to non-resident shareholders encounters zero statutory foreign-exchange hurdles, and the sole residual constraints arise from taxation rules (i.e., non-resident withholding tax on interest/dividends paid to foreign corporate shareholders, typically reduced to 0% under domestic law or an applicable double tax agreement where the recipient is a company) and bank-level documentary due diligence. The IC is generally outside the New Zealand tax net where central management and control are exercised offshore; nonetheless, capital subscription and revenue repatriation can be routed through New Zealand clearing systems without any prior Reserve Bank approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no export proceeds surrender obligations, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no statutory repatriation timelines. A New Zealand-resident or New Zealand-operating exporter, including the IC, may lawfully retain export receipts in foreign currency, maintain offshore collection accounts, delay conversion, or repatriate funds at the commercial discretion of the board. The only reporting touchpoints are (i) AML/CFT transaction monitoring by the receiving bank for cash transactions at or above NZD 10,000 and for wire transfers above NZD 1,000, and (ii) statistical data collection by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand for balance of payments reporting, neither of which requires any conversion or surrender of proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the national central bank exercising statutory oversight under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989, the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013, and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act), with delegated prudential supervision over registered banks, licensed insurers and non-bank lending institutions in New Zealand.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High. Global fintech EMIs and payment platforms (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, and the Merchant-of-Record rails Paddle and Lemon Squeezy expressly listed as target payment rails) are lawfully available to the IC for collection and payout purposes, subject to the bank-grade look-through AML/KYC policies of the underlying tier-1 acquiring banks. Legal constraints: (i) most digital EMIs decline tax-exempt offshore structures lacking demonstrable substance; (ii) the Cook Islands incorporation flag requires the EMI to verify the ultimate beneficial ownership and source of funds at the same standard as a regulated bank; (iii) NZD and AUD collection efficiency requires a local New Zealand settlement account, which EMIs may or may not provision; and (iv) where the EMI is not a New Zealand registered financial institution, the IC may be required by the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and the AML/CFT Act to appoint a local agent for service of process. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant path for an inbound equity or shareholder loan injection into the IC's New Zealand account is as follows: (i) the board passes a resolution approving the share subscription or shareholder loan, the account opening, and the signatory mandate; (ii) the remitting party sends a SWIFT MT103 with the narrative field stating 'Shareholder equity injection - subscription of Class A ordinary shares in [IC Name]' or 'Shareholder loan disbursement under facility agreement dated [date]'; (iii) the NZ receiving bank will not require a statutory purpose code but will internally classify the transaction as a capital account inflow for its regulatory reporting, and will screen the remitter and the underlying narrative against sanctions and PEP databases; (iv) the bank will verify the transaction against the account's declared purpose and may request the signed share certificates, updated register of members, and the source-of-funds documentation if the amount exceeds NZD 50,000 or is unusual for the account profile; (v) no Reserve Bank of New Zealand approval is required, and the funds become fully available for treasury use once the bank's internal post-transaction monitoring is satisfied. For an injected amount above NZD 250,000, a formal board minute and bank's Large Transaction Review form should be executed to preclude inadvertent structuring red flags.The operative clearing rails for the consumer micro-SaaS's outbound disbursements are: (i) SWIFT MT103 for all international transfers, including supplier payments to cloud hosting providers, software license fees to subcontractors, legal and accounting fees to New Zealand advisory firms, and periodic profit distributions to the non-resident shareholder; (ii) the New Zealand BECS (Bulk Electronic Clearing System) and the Reserve Bank's ESAS (Exchange Settlement Account System) real-time gross settlement rail for same-day NZD payments to domestic counterparties; (iii) SEPA (Single Euro Payments Area) for euro-denominated vendor payments from the bank's EUR sub-account, and the ACH/Fedwire networks for USD-denominated clearing; (iv) Commercial Letters of Credit are not a standard resource for SaaS royalty or subscription flows but remain a permissible and enforceable rail where an enterprise procurement contract expressly demands one — the IC may open a standby or documentary LC through its New Zealand bank for low-ticket sums backed by uncommitted facilities; and (v) the Merchant-of-Record platforms Paddle and Lemon Squeezy will settle net subscription revenue to the IC's designated NZD or USD account via SWIFT or local clearing, eliminating the need for the IC to maintain separate merchant acquiring accounts. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory New Zealand non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) rate on dividends paid to a non-resident company is 30%. This rate is reduced to 15% for fully imputed dividends paid to a non-resident company that holds 10% or more of the voting power in the dividend-paying company. New Zealand's dividend withholding tax regime also imposes a supplementary dividend election in limited cross-border investment arrangements; the standard DTA rates for portfolio dividends are typically 15%, and for substantial shareholdings (at least 10%), 5% or 15% depending on the treaty. The International Company (CK_IC) is presumed to be non-resident unless central management and control is in New Zealand; if non-resident, dividends from New Zealand payers are subject to NRWT at the applicable treaty rate, subject to entitlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The relevant threshold for mandatory New Zealand transfer pricing documentation: a taxpayer must prepare and retain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation if the taxpayer has international related-party transactions exceeding NZD 10 million in aggregate in an income year, or if related-party transactions in a common asset-intangible or financial category exceed NZD 10 million. For a micro-SaaS IC with annual global revenue below NZD 50 million and cross-border related-party transactions below NZD 10 million, no master file or local file is statutorily mandatory; however, if the IC has any cross-border related-party dealings exceeding NZD 4 million in aggregate, Inland Revenue may request documentation and the taxpayer must substantiate arm's length outcomes based on the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. The lacking contemporaneous documentation triggers reversals of burden of proof in tax disputes and potential documentation penalties. New Zealand does not prescribe a formal master file / local file regime under domestic law; compliance follows the OECD TP Guidance as applied through sections GC 1–GC 14 of the Income Tax Act 2007. For an IC deriving SaaS subscription income through a New Zealand resident related-party distributor or a platform, transfer pricing must be evidenced by a written intercompany agreement and comparable benchmarking, particularly where Capex and premium intangible returns are shared.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have a codified Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) in the same form as the UAE or BVI; however, statutory economic substance principles arise under: (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section BG 1, Income Tax Act 2007), (ii) the reconstruction powers under sections GB 1 and GC 1–GC 4i, and (iii) the tax residence tests under sections YD 2 and YD 5A, which deem a company resident in New Zealand if it is incorporated in New Zealand or if its central management and control is exercised in New Zealand, even if not incorporated in New Zealand. For a CK_IC International Company structured as a tax-exempt offshore vehicle, if it has no office, employees, decision-making substance, or independent administration in its own jurisdiction and its directors act from New Zealand, it will be treated as a New Zealand tax resident, causing worldwide income taxation at 28%. The economic substance doctrine requires that the IC's revenue, bank accounts, board decisions, and corporate records genuinely demonstrate that it is not a sham; otherwise Inland Revenue may recharacterise the arrangement and assess the New Zealand resident developer as the true taxpayer. The Interest Limitation Rule and the non-resident financial arrangement rules similarly deny deductions for interest paid to a related non-resident that lacks substance on excessive debt-funded acquisition structures. As the entity is a Low_Risk_Standard consumer micro-SaaS, the practical requirement is that the IC must maintain real economic substance in compliance with the underlying Cook Islands IC regime, including a registered agent, registered office, licensed trustee (if in a trust), and financial accounts, to avoid being classified as a shell or transparent vehicle.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994, an individual convicted of tax evasion is liable to imprisonment for up to 5 years and a fine up to NZD 50,000 (or twice the amount of tax evaded, whichever is greater). A company can be fined up to NZD 50,000 per offence; the court may order the payment of evaded tax plus interest and shortfall penalties. Civil shortfall penalties under section 141D: 100% of the shortfall for evasion, 50% for abusive tax position, 40% for a significant position without adequate disclosure, and 20% for an unacceptable tax position. Inland Revenue may raise amended assessments within the extended period (unlimited where evasion or fraud), seize assets, issue a bankruptcy notice, and prosecute through the Commissioner's criminal investigation unit. The Financial Transactions Reporting Act and the computer misuse legislation also apply if records are altered or deleted.

Director personal liability: Directors who aid, abet, direct, or connive in tax evasion are jointly and severally liable under section 140 of the Tax Administration Act 1994. Personal civil liabilities include the unpaid tax, shortfall penalties, and interest. Criminal prosecution of a director can result in imprisonment up to 5 years and personal fines of up to NZD 50,000; if the director is also a shadow director controlling the IC, the court may treat the IC as the director's alter ego and pierce the corporate veil. Directors may face travel restrictions through arrest warrants issued in criminal proceedings, and courts may impose restraining orders freezing personal assets and bank accounts under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.
Shell Company / Shallow Substance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue can apply the GAAR and reconstruction provisions to recharacterise the arrangement, deem the IC to be a New Zealand tax resident (central management and control test), and assess the entire worldwide income of the IC to New Zealand tax at 28%. Penalties under section 141A–141D for a tax avoidance arrangement: 50% of the shortfall for an abusive tax position, plus use-of-money interest. Additionally, failure to maintain proper accounting records is an offence under section 50 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the Financial Reporting Act 2013, attracting fines up to NZD 10,000 and potentially director liability. Under the Companies Act 1993, a shell company that fails to meet its obligations may be struck off the register, and any person knowingly concerned in the operation of the company for a fraudulent purpose can be personally liable for the debts of the company under section 320. The statutory anti-money laundering regime requires verification of UBO and source of funds, and a shell profile will be reported as suspicious to the New Zealand Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the IC to trade without a legitimate business purpose are liable for reckless trading under section 135 of the Companies Act 1993. The court may order the director to personally contribute to the company's assets for creditor losses, and in tax cases the corporate veil is pierced under the common law 'façade' principle where the company is used to evade existing obligations. Directors can be disqualified from managing companies for up to 5 years under the Companies Act 1993 for repeated offending, and in serious cases imprisonment up to 5 years under the Crimes Act 1961 for fraud offences. A director who signs tax returns while the entity is a sham may be personally liable for the tax shortfall under the strict liability offence in section 143B, with fines up to NZD 25,000 and, for persistent non-payment, imprisonment.
AML / Money Laundering Through Payment Processors

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, reporting entities that fail to conduct customer due diligence, report suspicious transactions, or implement risk-based compliance programmes are subject to civil penalties of up to NZD 200,000 for an individual and up to NZD 2 million for a body corporate. Criminal penalties for laundering the proceeds of crime under the Crimes Act 1961 and the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009 carry imprisonment for up to 7 years and fines up to $300,000. The court may order forfeiture of all property that is the proceeds of a serious crime. Additionally, merchant of record or payment processor contracts (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy) may be terminated for cause upon suspicion of AML breaches, causing immediate account closure, holdback of funds for 180 days, and contractual liability.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers of a reporting entity can be individually prosecuted for consenting to or knowingly being a party to money laundering under section 245 of the Crimes Act 1961; penalties include imprisonment for up to 7 years and large fines. Under the AML/CFT Act, the Financial Markets Authority can ban a person from being a senior manager or director of any financial institution for life in egregious cases. Where a director uses the IC as a vehicle to launder funds, the court may issue a pecuniary penalty order and restraint order over the director's personal property; directors face civil liability for the amount laundered through their bank accounts, and travel bans may be imposed as part of bail conditions in criminal proceedings.
