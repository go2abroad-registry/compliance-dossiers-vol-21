Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand; primary digital portal is myIR (secure online services at www.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident offshore entity (such as an International Company) must first determine whether it is tax resident in New Zealand. Under New Zealand domestic law, a company is tax resident if it is incorporated in New Zealand, has its head office in New Zealand, or its centre of management is in New Zealand. A foreign-incorporated International Company that does not meet these tests is non-resident. To obtain a statutory New Zealand TIN (IRD number) and, where required, a GST/VAT registration number, the foreign entity must submit Form IR596 ('Non-resident company IRD number application') to Inland Revenue, either online through the myIR portal or by paper submission, accompanied by authenticated copies of the certificate of incorporation, certificate of good standing, constitutional documents, directors' and shareholders' identification, and a certified statement confirming no New Zealand fixed establishment or residential management nexus. Upon issuance of the IRD number, if the entity intends to make taxable supplies in New Zealand or is required under the remote services rules to collect GST, it must file Form GST1A (or register online via myIR) selecting the 'non-resident supplier of remote services' category. The entity must also designate a New Zealand agent for service of notices if it has no physical presence. Registration is subject to Inland Revenue's anti-fraud verification and may take 5–15 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box election akin to the U.S. Treasury regulations. Instead, entity classification follows statutory residency tests and the Uniform Company Tax Code. For a non-resident International Company that is centrally managed and controlled outside New Zealand and has no New Zealand permanent establishment, no entity-level or flow-through election is required or available; the entity remains outside the New Zealand income tax net. However, New Zealand has targeted hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act 2007 (subpart FH) that deny deductions for certain hybrid financial instrument payments, imports of hybrid mismatch tax benefits, and reverse hybrid structures. Domestic close-held companies may elect to be taxable as Look-Through Companies (LTC) under sections HB 1–HB 7 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and, for limited partnerships, flow-through treatment applies by default. These domestic elections are not available to non-resident International Companies, which instead rely on application of the offshore entity exemption and applicable double tax agreements to remain tax-transparent or tax-neutral in New Zealand.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand is a full-currency-convertibility jurisdiction which accepts IMF Article VIII obligations. The New Zealand dollar is freely convertible for both current account and capital account transactions, including sales proceeds, dividends, interest, royalties, intercompany loan repayments and equity repatriation. There are no queues, quotas, conversion tiers or prior central-bank approvals for routine treasury movements. The only material constraints are: (i) anti-money laundering and countering financing of terrorism verification by banks; (ii) tax withholding obligations on income sourced in New Zealand; (iii) the Overseas Investment Act 2005 screening for sensitive land and significant business assets; and (iv) the obligation to make accurate Balance of Payments and CRS/FATCA-related declarations. Profit remittance from an eligible International Company is not subject to a remittance licence or export certificate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand abolished foreign-exchange controls in the 1980s and currently imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters and cross-border e-commerce sellers may retain receipts in foreign currency indefinitely, either in onshore foreign-currency bank accounts or in offshore accounts, and conversion to New Zealand dollars is optional. The relevant statutory and regulatory disciplines are evidentiary: export receipts must be traceable to underlying invoices and shipping or platform settlement records, banks must obtain complete originator and beneficiary information under the AML/CFT Act 2009, and tax returns must be prepared using the applicable foreign-exchange translation method.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ / Te Pūtea Matua) is the central bank and banking regulator; foreign-exchange administration is decentralised across RBNZ for banking and settlement system oversight, the Financial Markets Authority for financial market conduct and derivatives, and the Department of Internal Affairs for AML/CFT supervision. New Zealand maintains no centralised exchange-control office and imposes no general foreign-exchange licensing on ordinary corporate treasury operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High, but exclusively as an ancillary payment and FX execution rail, not as the principal custodial bank. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business may lawfully provide cross-border payment initiation and foreign-exchange conversion services to a New Zealand-resident account holder, provided they do not carry on deposit-taking business in New Zealand in breach of the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. Actual holding of customer deposits by a non-bank requires a registered bank licence or an exemption. The EMI must also comply with the AML/CFT Act 2009 if it has a New Zealand nexus or contracts with New Zealand customers, and client funds must be segregated and safeguarded. Execution-only FX payments are permissible; any advice or structured derivative issuance triggers the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013. Accordingly, Wise B2B is suitable for TikTok Shop payout collection, supplier disbursements, and FX conversion, while the capital escrow account and core treasury custody must remain with a licensed New Zealand commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection routing for the International Company utilises SWIFT MT103 for high-value wire transfers or ISO 20022 pain.001 instructions when initiated through an EMI or treasury platform. The payment narrative must expressly state 'Shareholder equity injection' or 'Shareholder loan advance' and reference the New Zealand operational account number and authorised beneficiary name. New Zealand has no mandatory domestic purpose-code regime, but the remitting bank may require a foreign-exchange or Balance-of-Payments purpose indicator such as 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital' or 'Other Investment - Loans', which must match the underlying board resolution and intercompany agreement. The receiving bank will verify that the beneficiary name corresponds exactly with KYC records, that signatories are authorised under a current board resolution, and that originator information accompanies the wire. The Overseas Investment Act 2005 only has relevance if the funding is used to acquire sensitive New Zealand land or sensitive business assets, which does not arise in the ordinary TikTok Shop retail model. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$10 million aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions; above this threshold the IRD expects contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation consistent with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. Below the threshold, arm's-length benchmarking records should still be maintained.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime in the form used by offshore financial centres. However, the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, the business-continuity test, and the resident-company central management and control rules impose a substantive economic test. A CK_IC International Company with no local offices, employees, or commercial functions attempting to book New Zealand social commerce profits as tax-exempt will be disregarded and the profits recharacterised to the economic substance owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under section 141 of the Tax Administration Act 1994; imprisonment up to 5 years; fines up to NZ$50,000; civil shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall; use-of-money interest from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or direct the evasion may be prosecuted personally. The corporate veil may be pierced to collect unpaid source deductions and withholding tax under the director liability provisions; asset freezing and court-imposed travel restrictions are possible under asset recovery and bail procedures.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: IRD adjusts taxable income under sections GC 13 to GC 17 of the Income Tax Act 2007; shortfall penalties range up to 100% for abusive tax positions and 150% for evasion; interest accrues on all adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false tax returns or knowingly approve mispriced related-party transactions face personal civil penalties and potential prosecution for aiding tax evasion; disqualification under the Companies Act 1994 may result.
Shell Company / General Anti-Avoidance

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner voids the tax benefit under section BG 1; revenue is recharacterised to the controlling New Zealand resident; shortfall penalties of 100% or 150% apply; foreign company registration may be struck off; treaty benefits are denied.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed personally if the company is a sham or was used as a mere instrument to avoid tax; courts may order restitution, disqualify directors for up to five years, and impose criminal sanctions for deliberate avoidance.
AML/CFT and Payment Rails Violations

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 of up to NZ$2 million per contravention for a body corporate; separate criminal liability for money laundering under the Crimes Act 1961.

Director personal liability: Directors, senior managers, and compliance officers can be prosecuted for knowingly permitting suspicious transactions; personal fines up to NZ$200,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years may be imposed, with related travel and occupational restrictions.
