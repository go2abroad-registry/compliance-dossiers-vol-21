Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. Under sections 37 and 38 of the Companies Act 1993, shares may be issued and paid for in money, property, or services, and may remain partly paid. No escrow account, government deposit, or capitalisation threshold is required for the incorporation or ongoing operation of an International Company limited by shares in New Zealand.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) compliance inspection is required if the Company occupies any physical commercial premises; a current Building Warrant of Fitness under the Building Act 2004 must be displayed for leased office space. For the digital-only publishing business, no hazardous-substance licence or civil-defence certification is triggered. Under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, WorkSafe NZ requires the Company to adopt a health and safety management system, conduct a workplace risk assessment, and ensure employee engagement in safety protocols; a clearance certificate is not normally issued, but the Company must register any workplace with FENZ if a scheduled substance is stored, which does not apply to cloud-based publishing. If office premises are used, the Company must maintain a Civil Defence emergency plan and fire-fighting equipment serviced in accordance with the FENZ Fire Safety and Evacuation of Buildings Regulations 2006.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital. Under sections 37 and 38 of the Companies Act 1993, shares may be issued and paid for in money, property, or services, and may remain partly paid. No escrow account, government deposit, or capitalisation threshold is required for the incorporation or ongoing operation of an International Company limited by shares in New Zealand.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. The available statutory elections for a non-resident digital publisher in New Zealand are: (1) voluntary GST registration where the NZD 60,000 threshold is not exceeded (s 51(3), Goods and Services Tax Act 1985), permitting input tax credit recovery at the 15% rate; (2) election to use a one-month taxable period for GST where the person is in a net refund position, while aggregation is mandatory for registered persons whose taxable supplies exceed NZD 24 million per annum (s 15, GST Act 1985); (3) GST group election to consolidate two or more NZ GST-registered entities into a single registration (s 55, GST Act 1985); (4) hybrid mismatch designatory elections under Part 5B of the Income Tax Act 2007 (inserted by the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018), allowing ordering elections or de-application of hybrid mismatch rules for structured arrangements involving offshore trust or controlled foreign company interests; (5) the Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, which is available only to NZ-resident close companies and is therefore unavailable to the CK_IC; and (6) election under the CFC and FIF rules to attribute income from a non-resident entity, applicable only if the CK_IC were to be re-characterised as an NZ-resident entity, which is not the intended position under the exempt status certificate. For the Tax_Exempt_Zone classification, the operative mechanism is the written election for exempt status filed with the Commissioner of Inland Revenue, accompanied by the legal opinion and constitutive documents supporting the International Company structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 for suppliers carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand (s 51, GST Act 1985); for non-resident offshore suppliers of remote services, the registration threshold is NZD 60,000 of remote services supplied to New Zealand-resident consumers in a 12-month period (s 54A, GST Act 1985). Digital store sales via Steam and PlayStation Store must be aggregated across the NZ market; registration is mandatory once the projected or actual 12-month turnover exceeds the threshold, and voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold. There is no separate low-value goods threshold for the CK_IC supplier, and the 15% GST applies to each NZ-resident consumer transaction from the first dollar once the entity is registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under s 11(1)(c) of the GST Act 1985 for goods physically exported by the supplier within 28 days of supply, and under s 11A for remote services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the services are not electronically supplied for direct use in New Zealand and do not relate directly to New Zealand land or moveable property. For PC and console game publishing via Steam and PlayStation Store: (i) digital-store sales to non-NZ-resident end users qualify as zero-rated remote services if the supplier retains robust evidence of non-resident status, including IP-country geolocation, payment-card billing country, storefront country settings, and device telemetry; (ii) physical exports of console discs, cartridges, or collector's editions qualify only if the goods are exported within 28 days and the supplier holds transport and customs documentation; (iii) platform-store configuration fees charged to overseas developers may qualify for zero-rating under s 11A(1)(k) (services supplied to a person not resident in New Zealand) unless the services relate directly to New Zealand assets or are performed in New Zealand. Sales to NZ-resident consumers are subject to the standard 15% GST rate and cannot be zero-rated, even where the consumer uses the game outside New Zealand, because the supply is made to a person in New Zealand in the course of an e-commerce transaction.

New Zealand imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no pre-clearance API regime for electronic invoices. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates on the Peppol interoperability framework administered by the New Zealand Government's e-Invoicing Service. Tax invoices must nevertheless satisfy s 24 of the GST Act 1985, including supplier name and IRD number, GST registration number, issue date, description of the supplies, GST-exclusive and GST-inclusive amounts, and, for zero-rated exports, a statement of zero-rating. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR or via IRD-approved gateway software using machine-to-machine APIs; however, these APIs transmit summary return data, not invoice-level clearance. For Steam and PlayStation Store publishing, the payment rails (Steam Valve Payout and PlayStation Store Payout) do not impose any New Zealand GST withholding; the CK_IC, as the electronic supplier, must self-assess and return GST on supplies to NZ consumers.

The Inland Revenue Department actively audits GST zero-rating through its GST refund and export verification programme, cross-matching New Zealand Customs Service export declarations against GST returns and bank inward remittance records by using the Business Intelligence and trade data reconciliation system. Where zero-rating is incorrectly claimed, the consequences include: (1) reinstatement of the full 15% GST as output tax on the supply, with the Commissioner reassessing the relevant taxable period retrospectively; (2) use-of-money interest (UOMI) on the unpaid tax at the IRD prescribed quarterly rate for underpayments; (3) shortfall penalties under Part 9 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 of 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for an unacceptable tax position, and 150% for evasion or an abusive tax position; (4) late payment penalties of 1% at the due date and a further 4% after seven days of unpaid GST; (5) for physical goods diverted or re-imported without complying with the 28-day export rule, the zero-rating is voided ab initio and the supplier must account for output tax even though the customer is a non-resident; (6) fraud or false documentation exposes the responsible persons to criminal liability under s 143A and s 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994 with fines of up to NZD 50,000 for a first offence and NZD 100,000 for subsequent offences, and imprisonment of up to five years for wilful tax evasion; and (7) the general reassessment period is four years, extended to seven years where the shortfall arises from evasion or fraud, allowing the Commissioner to claw back historically zero-rated export claims. Digital publishers must retain the complete zero-rating evidence pack for at least seven years after the end of the taxable period (s 24(6), GST Act 1985); absence of the mandated documentation automatically triggers reinstatement of output tax plus penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no restrictions on the purchase, sale, remittance, or retention of foreign exchange. Software licensing royalties, Steam/PlayStation store proceeds, dividends, and interest may be repatriated without FX-authorization hurdles. The only payable frictions are statutory tax compliance: non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at 15% on royalties absent a treaty reduction, approved issuer levy (AIL) on eligible interest, and resident withholding tax/international tax reporting. A profit-remittance 'hurdle' is therefore tax documentation (IRD tax compliance certificate, exemption certificate, double-tax-agreement residency certificate), not exchange control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, no minimum conversion ratio, and no deadline for repatriation or conversion. Exporters and digital-content sellers may hold USD, JPY, EUR, or other foreign currency proceeds in NZ or offshore accounts indefinitely. Banks nevertheless apply AML/CFT transaction reporting for cross-border inflows/outflows exceeding NZD 10,000, and the RBNZ may collect balance-of-payments statistical data on large direct, portfolio, and derivative cross-border positions.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua) is the apex monetary authority and prudential supervisor of registered banks. New Zealand does not operate a permit-based exchange-control authority; the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) separately screens sensitive foreign direct investment, and the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) regulates FX derivative dealing and swaps under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013. AML/CFT supervision is jointly exercised by RBNZ, FMA, and the Department of Internal Affairs under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity contributions should be routed as SWIFT MT103 wires from the parent shareholder's bank to the NZ bank account in NZD, with a clear remittance information field reading 'EQUITY CAPITAL INJECTION – CK_IC' and the IFM/BoP purpose code 'Capital contribution in cash' (BoP code 2100 under NZ statistical conventions).
