Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, an autonomous Crown entity operated by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), discharging the Registrar of Companies function through the New Zealand Companies Register (www.companiesoffice.govt.nz).

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), ss 10 (mode of incorporation), 12 (requirements for registration), 13 (no minimum paid-up capital), 20 (company name requirements), 117-118 (allotment and consideration for shares), 124 (registered office), 151-152 (disqualification and capacity of directors), 188 (meetings of directors), 190 (notice of registered office), 191 (change of registered office); Companies (Incorporation and Registration) Regulations 1993; Financial Reporting Act 2013; Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; Tax Administration Act 1994.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic 'Application for Registration of a Company' (Form NZCN/CO/01) filed through the online Companies Register portal, accompanied by the optional Company Constitution (if adopted), Consent to Act forms executed by every first director, an address for service, the registered office physical address (never a post office box), and the statement of share subscriptions/allotment.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 40 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists under the Companies Act 1993 (s 13). One or more shares must be allotted to at least one shareholder, but the payment of share consideration may be deferred or unpaid. There is no legal escrow account, deposit, or capital verification requirement imposed by the Companies Office; however, if a New Zealand bank is appointed as the merchant acquiring bank for the franchise, banking practice will require deposited trust capital to be evidenced by a subscription schedule.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake Inland Revenue), Wellington, New Zealand; online portal: myIR (secure Inland Revenue digital services portal)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains a statutory IRD number by applying through the Inland Revenue myIR portal or by submitting Form IR742 (Application for an IRD Number for a Non-Resident). The applicant must provide certified Certificate of Incorporation, entity constitutional documents, registered office and principal place of business, directors and ultimate beneficial ownership particulars, and confirmation of any New Zealand fixed establishment. If the entity undertakes a taxable activity in New Zealand or supplies remote services to New Zealand resident consumers, it must register for GST through myIR using Form IR402 or the online GST registration service; registration is compulsory once the NZD 60,000 rolling 12-month taxable supply threshold is exceeded or, for non-resident suppliers of remote services, before supplies are made where that threshold is likely to be exceeded. The IRD number serves as the New Zealand TIN and the GST registration number is the GST ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - Non-resident International Company not carrying on a New Zealand taxable activity and deriving no New Zealand-sourced income; such entity is outside the ordinary New Zealand resident corporate income tax base. Any New Zealand-sourced income or business carried on through a fixed establishment in New Zealand remains taxable, and the entity may be required to register as a non-resident GST supplier for imported remote services.

Available election forms

No United States-style check-the-box election is available in New Zealand. New Zealand adopts hybrid mismatch rules under subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 to neutralise tax avoidance through hybrid entities and instruments. A non-resident supplier may elect to register voluntarily for GST under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 even if below the compulsory threshold, subject to Inland Revenue approval and ongoing GST compliance obligations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (NZ$60,000) in any 12-month rolling period for taxable supplies made in the course of a New Zealand taxable activity; non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand consumers are registrable when their total supplies to New Zealand residents exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods are zero-rated where the supplier exports the goods to a place outside New Zealand before or within the statutory timeframe, generally within 28 days of supply, and retains appropriate commercial and customs evidence. Supplies of services are zero-rated when provided to a non-resident outside New Zealand and the supply does not relate to New Zealand land or goods physically present in New Zealand at the time of supply; certain international transport and related services also qualify. The supplier must hold proof of export, non-resident status of the recipient, and evidence that the consideration is received from offshore.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank repatriation/FX receipt advice evidencing settlement from the offshore buyer
  • Commercial Invoice and export sales contract confirming the zero-rated supply

New Zealand operates a Peppol-based e-Invoicing framework via the NZ e-Invoicing network; e-invoicing is voluntary, not mandated by law. There is no real-time fiscal clearance obligation, no government-approved digital stamping regime, and no mandatory tax authority API pre-validation for invoices. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR and use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties apply for non-compliance.

Inland Revenue may audit export zero-rating claims using customs data matching, bank remittance analysis, and commercial records. If goods are not physically exported within the statutory period or services are not genuinely supplied to a non-resident, the supply is re-characterised as taxable at 15% GST and the entire refund amount is clawed back, with use-of-money interest accruing from the original refund date. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 range from 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion; criminal prosecution may also follow for knowingly false GST returns or fraudulent claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible on both the current account and the capital account. New Zealand maintains no exchange-control approval, no centralised rationing of foreign currency, no restriction on opening and maintaining foreign-currency bank accounts, and no exchange ticket or liquidation system for trade proceeds. Profit, royalty, dividend, interest, and loan-capital remittances to non-residents are contractual matters rather than administrative FX licensing matters. Practical hurdles are limited to: (i) the transacting bank's due diligence under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009; (ii) New Zealand tax clearance, in particular non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at the relevant statutory rate under the Income Tax Act 2007 for dividends, interest, and royalties, the Approved Issuer Levy option for qualifying interest, and, where the International Company is tax-exempt in its home territory, the need for a letter of exemption or tax-residence certificate to support any claim for treaty benefits; (iii) the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's external statistical reporting for external financial claims and liabilities of NZD 1 million or more; and (iv) screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 only if the transaction will acquire sensitive land or significant business assets within the statutory thresholds. No foreign-exchange surrender or purchase-fixing requirement applies to any NZD transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. New Zealand has imposed no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no minimum repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio since the dismantling of exchange controls in 1984. The CK_IC licensor may hold export and royalty proceeds in any currency, convert at freely negotiated rates, and allocate funds between the New Zealand domestic pool and offshore multi-currency accounts without central-bank approval. The only residual requirements are: (a) anti-money-laundering cash declarations and reporting for the physical import or export of currency or bearer negotiable instruments equal to or above NZD 10,000 under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and associated regulations; (b) bank-level transaction monitoring and prescribed transaction reporting; and (c) Reserve Bank of New Zealand balance-of-payments and external-position statistical returns where aggregate external claims or liabilities equal or exceed NZD 1 million. None of these imposes conversion, retention, or surrender constraints on export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) is the central bank, monetary authority, and prudential regulator of the systemically important payment and settlement infrastructure. New Zealand does not maintain a classic foreign-exchange control administration; the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989, the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and the Overseas Investment Act 2005, administered through the Overseas Investment Office, set the limited regulatory perimeter. Payment clearing rails are supervised by the Reserve Bank through the Payments NZ owned settlement systems, including the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS), and FX derivative market conduct is regulated by the Financial Markets Authority.
