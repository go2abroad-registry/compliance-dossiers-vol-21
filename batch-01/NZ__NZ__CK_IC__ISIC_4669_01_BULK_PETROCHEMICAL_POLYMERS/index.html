Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake) via myIR portal and IRD eServices; company registry through the New Zealand Companies Office and NZBN.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that is not incorporated in New Zealand applies to Inland Revenue for a non-resident IRD number using form IR742 (Non-Resident Company IRD Application). If the entity is incorporated in New Zealand, it must first obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) from the Companies Office and then apply for an IRD number using form IR596 or through myIR. After the IRD number is issued, the entity must separately register for Goods and Services Tax (GST) if it carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand and its annual taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000, or it may voluntarily register using form IR372, which can be filed online through myIR. No statutory trade license is required for wholesale distribution of virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — a non-resident International Company that does not carry on a taxable activity in New Zealand and earns only non-New Zealand-source income is outside the New Zealand income tax net and has no GST registration nexus. If the entity becomes a New Zealand tax resident or begins substantial New Zealand-source trading, it would instead fall under Entity_Level_Taxation and become subject to NZ corporate income tax and GST on NZ taxable supplies.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box system. Eligible closely-held companies may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under the Income Tax Act 2007, subpart HB, enabling pass-through of NZ-source income and losses. GST grouping elections and voluntary GST registration for zero-rated export traders are available under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are mandatory and are not elective; no domestic hybrid mismatch election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; voluntary registration is permitted for entities making only zero-rated export supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of bulk polyethylene and polypropylene granules is zero-rated for New Zealand GST if the supplier exports the goods physically from New Zealand within 28 days of supply, or if the goods are supplied to a non-resident customer outside New Zealand and title passes outside New Zealand pursuant to a contract of sale or export. Offshore services and remote services supplied to non-resident buyers outside New Zealand may also be zero-rated. The exporter must hold contemporaneous commercial and customs evidence sufficient to establish actual export, foreign destination, and the export nature of the supply. Refunds of attributable input GST on zero-rated exports are claimed through normal GST returns subject to retention of complete export documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Trade Single Window / CUSMED export entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of the PE/PP granules
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds

New Zealand has no statutory real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime. Inland Revenue accepts GST returns and payments through myIR, and voluntary e-invoicing using the Peppol framework is permitted. Entities must retain source documents and export evidence in New Zealand for seven years.

Inland Revenue may scrutinize zero-rated export claims by matching GST returns with customs export declarations and bank receipts. The burden lies on the supplier to prove the goods were exported; failure to do so results in a reassessment at 15% GST with interest from the original supply date. Shortfall penalties range from 20% for failing to take reasonable care, 20% for taking an unacceptable tax position, 50% for abusive tax position, and up to 150% for evasion. The 180-day foreign-currency repatriation period is a standard compliance and AML benchmark; while New Zealand legislation does not impose an absolute 180-day export-proceeds deadline, absence of inward remittance evidence within that period materially increases the risk of an audit adjustment or clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a free-floating and fully convertible currency. New Zealand has accepted Article VIII obligations under the IMF Articles of Agreement, so current-account payments and transfers, including trade settlement, service fees, royalties, and profit remittances, are free from exchange restrictions. Capital-account convertibility is also open: no prior purchase, use, or repatriation authorization is required for equity injections, intercompany loans, dividend distributions, or liquidation proceeds moving through a New Zealand bank. The only material hurdles are not exchange controls but overlays in the investment and tax regimes: (i) the Overseas Investment Act 2005 requires consent for non-resident acquisitions of sensitive land or significant business assets (transaction value at or above the relevant monetary threshold of NZD 100 million for most business assets, with separate criteria for strategic and farmland transactions); and (ii) New Zealand source dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents attract non-resident withholding tax, with statutory maxima up to 30% on dividends and 15% on interest and royalties, subject to treaty reduction and the approved issuer levy (AIL) alternative at 2% for interest. A tax-exempt International Company structured as a non-resident treasury vehicle must nevertheless document the source of each funded asset to avoid a deemed New Zealand stamping charge from the IRD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation, or conversion regime for export proceeds. There is no statutory timeline requiring an exporter to bring receipts onshore, and there is no compulsory conversion ratio into NZD. Foreign-currency export receivables may be retained indefinitely in offshore or New Zealand bank accounts. The only compliance frictions are (i) the AML/CFT record-keeping and suspicious-transaction reporting duties of the paying bank for international wire transfers, (ii) the financial arrangements rules of the Income Tax Act 2007, which can tax foreign-exchange movements for New Zealand resident companies, and (iii) the requirement that a New Zealand-registered bank classify any large unexplained inward credit as an 'unusual transaction' report under the AML/CFT Act 2009. For a tax-exempt International Company holding a Cook Islands or similar offshore incorporation, export proceeds can remain outside the New Zealand tax net provided no New Zealand permanent establishment is created and the proceeds are not derived through a New Zealand fixed place of business.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the sole central bank and monetary authority of New Zealand. There is no separate foreign exchange control administration, and the Reserve Bank does not issue exchange control permits, surrender certificates, or currency licenses. Its foreign-exchange mandate is confined to the management of official overseas reserves and the prudential supervision of banks and non-bank deposit takers under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021. The operational oversight of cross-border payment reporting and anti-money laundering conduct rests with the joint AML/CFT supervisors: the Reserve Bank, the Financial Markets Authority, and the Department of Internal Affairs.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low as a primary bank, Medium as an ancillary payment overlay. New Zealand does not maintain a dedicated e-money institution (EMI) license class. An offshore digital EMI (e.g. a United Kingdom or Singapore licensed payment institution) cannot operate as a settlement bank in New Zealand or issue NZD clearing instruments without registering on the Financial Service Providers Register and becoming a reporting entity under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. For this International Company, a locally incorporated Tier-1 New Zealand bank remains mandatory for LC issuance, GST pooling, NZD settlement, and RBNZ-cleared high-value payments. The offshore EMI may be engaged solely as an FX execution and disbursement overlay, provided the EMI’s home license is verified, the underlying nostro settlement agent is not a shell bank, and every converted leg is supported by the commercial polymer supply contract; otherwise, correspondent banks will reject the wire.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder equity contributions, intercompany loans, and trade pre-payments are cleared via SWIFT MT103 (outright) or MT202 (cover through an intermediate correspondent) to the International Company’s NZD or USD account at a Reserve Bank-regulated New Zealand bank. The payment narrative must state the economic purpose using clear wording such as 'Shareholder equity contribution', 'Intercompany loan — IC treasury line', or 'Prepayment for polyethylene granules under Contract No. X'. The Reserve Bank does not prescribe a national purpose-code tariff, but the receiving bank must record the purpose narrative and verify the sender’s bank authorization, the source-of-funds documentation, and the direct or indirect ownership chain. If the injected capital is used to acquire significant New Zealand business assets or sensitive land, an Overseas Investment Act 2005 consent number must be quoted in the supporting memorandum. International wire transfers are retained for record-keeping under the AML/CFT Act 2009 and may trigger a suspicious-transaction report only if the funding source or the overseas debit/credit institution presents a sanctions or corruption risk. All capital injections should be accompanied by a directors’ resolution and an updated share register to pre-empt the bank’s beneficial-ownership questionnaire.The standard clearing rails for settlement of bulk polymer purchases are (i) an irrevocable documentary letter of credit issued by the New Zealand Tier-1 bank in MT700 form under UCP600/ISBP 821, with the beneficiating producer or commodity trader negotiating against conforming documents (full set of ocean bills of lading, certificate of origin, commercial invoice, packing list, and quality certification); and (ii) direct telegraphic transfers via SWIFT MT103 for spot prepayments or balance settlements on open-account terms. The LC rail matches the target payment rails described as Swift_LC and Irrevocable_LC: the importer’s bank opens an irrevocable credit, advances cover under MT202 to the nominated paying bank, and settles documents on presentation. For smaller cargo legs, documentary collections (DP/DA) and Bank Payment Obligation structures under the ICC URBPO rules are acceptable alternatives. New Zealand imposes no exchange-control approval for outbound disbursements; however, the remitting bank is obligated to screen all beneficiaries and vessel parties against sanction lists, and where the payment exceeds a legislated threshold the bank must ensure the transaction is not a suspicious transaction.
