Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Companies, New Zealand Companies Office, administered by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE).

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), ss 10-19 (registration and certificate of incorporation), ss 31-42 (constitution), s 50 (allotment of shares), s 128 (registered office), and ss 131-152 (directors); supplemented by the Financial Reporting Act 2013 and the Tax Administration Act 1994. New Zealand does not issue a separate 'International Company' charter, so CK_IC is incorporated as an ordinary company limited by shares, with the internal IC designation used solely for group classification.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of a Company (NZ Companies Office online incorporation application, incorporating statutory Form 2 information).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None - New Zealand imposes no minimum paid-up capital or statutory escrow requirement for a company limited by shares. Shares may be issued without par value and subscription consideration may consist of cash, property, or services pursuant to s 50 of the Companies Act 1993.

Funding & unlock path: Not required for a virtual or software-only office. If a physical premises is leased, a Building Warrant of Fitness (Form 12A/B) is required under the Building Act 2004 for fire safety and means of escape, and an evacuation scheme may be lodged with Fire and Emergency New Zealand under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017. WorkSafe New Zealand notification and a health and safety risk assessment are required under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 once employees are engaged.

Paid-in capital rule: None - New Zealand imposes no minimum paid-up capital or statutory escrow requirement for a company limited by shares. Shares may be issued without par value and subscription consideration may consist of cash, property, or services pursuant to s 50 of the Companies Act 1993.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) / Te Tari Taake; online filing and registration portal: myIR (https://myir.ird.govt.nz); export documentation interface via New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window (Tradelink).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident International Company (IC) must obtain an IRD number by lodging Form IR742 (Application for an IRD Number by a Non-Resident Company) or the general company application Form IR596, accompanied by certified certificate of incorporation, constitution, directors/shareholders register, and evidence of registered office; Inland Revenue issues a unique 8–9 digit IRD number. If the entity supplies 'remote services' (including EdTech subscription software) to NZ-resident consumers or carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand, it must separately register for GST using Form IR333 (GST Registration). The IC may also apply for Approved Issuer Status (Form IR483) to substitute the approved issuer levy for non-resident withholding tax on NZ-source interest. A non-resident without a NZ physical address must appoint a NZ tax agent to file via myIR; registration is completed online through myIR or by post to Inland Revenue, Wellington.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. The 'Tax_Exempt_Zone' classification reflects the IC's non-resident tax profile: it is subject to NZ income tax only on NZ-sourced income and is not taxable on foreign-sourced subscription revenue absent a NZ fixed establishment/permanent establishment. Available statutory mechanisms include: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election (Income Tax Act 2007 ss HA 10–HA 20) for qualifying NZ close companies, conferring fiscal transparency; (2) GST group election (Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 s 55), allowing grouped members to file a single GST return; (3) Approved Issuer Status (AIS) election to secure the reduced 2% approved issuer levy rather than non-resident withholding tax on NZ-source interest; (4) substituted accounting period election; (5) CFC attribution elections and branch-equivalent adjustments under the controlled foreign company rules; (6) hybrid mismatch rules (Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007) impose mandatory aligning adjustments and do not provide elective hybrid-mismatch preferences.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 turnover threshold for domestic and non-resident suppliers carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand (GST Act 1985 s 51); zero (nil) registration threshold for non-resident suppliers of 'remote services' (cross-border digital services, including EdTech subscription apps distributed via Apple App Store and Google Play) to NZ-resident consumers under the remote-services regime, and for non-resident suppliers of low-value imported goods under the cross-border B2C rules — mandatory registration from the first NZ dollar of such supplies; voluntary registration permitted below thresholds where land transactions or zero-rated export supplies exist.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under s 11(1)(a) of the GST Act 1985 where the supplier removes the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply (extendable to 60 days or longer at the Commissioner's discretion, and up to 2 years in limited circumstances) and retains sufficient evidence of export. Services are zero-rated under s 11(1)(m) where supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, unless the services relate directly to land situated in NZ, movable personal property situated in NZ at the time of supply, or an obligation to refrain from carrying on a taxable activity in NZ. EdTech subscription services (automated tutoring software) supplied by a NZ-established entity to non-resident customers qualify for zero-rating where the recipient is non-resident and outside New Zealand and no NZ land or goods nexus exists; conversely, a non-resident supplier supplying the same services into New Zealand consumers is subject to NZ GST at 15% under the remote-services registration model. Input tax credits may be claimed on GST returns and excess credits are refunded by Inland Revenue, ordinarily within 15 working days of return processing.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged via New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window (Tradelink)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer evidence evidencing FX repatriation
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Signed recipient declaration (for zero-rated services) confirming non-resident status and overseas location
  • GST return worksheet (GST101 or electronic equivalent) recording zero-rated supplies
  • Evidence of export receipt in the overseas destination (delivery confirmation or foreign customs entry)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, fiscal API, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping regime exists in New Zealand. e-Invoicing is voluntary and operates under the Peppol framework; GST returns are filed electronically via myIR on a monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly filing cycle depending on turnover; invoices must satisfy the GST invoice content requirements under s 24 of the GST Act 1985, but individual sales transactions are not subject to government pre-clearance.

Inland Revenue audits zero-rating substantiation documents, cross-checking customs export data, bank FX records, and counterparty residency. Where zero-rating is claimed without valid documentary evidence, the supply is recharacterised as standard-rated and the taxpayer faces a shortfall penalty of 20% of the resulting GST under s 141B of the Tax Administration Act 1994, plus use-of-money interest on the outstanding amount (currently approximately 8–10% per annum). Fraudulent evasion or intentional misstatement attracts a 150% penalty under s 141E and criminal prosecution under ss 143A and 148 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, with imprisonment available in serious cases. The statutory time bar for reassessment is 4 years, but no time bar applies in cases of fraud or evasion, enabling retrospective clawback. GST-registered persons who improperly claim input tax credits face de-registration and recovery of all incorrect refunds with penalties and interest; exported goods not removed from New Zealand within the statutory deadline are recharacterised as taxable supplies, forcing GST reversal with interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the IMF Article VIII. No exchange controls or restrictions exist for the repatriation of profits, dividends, capital, loan repayments, or trade-related payments. Companies may freely convert NZD to and from foreign currencies through licensed banks. EdTech subscription revenues received in foreign currencies may be retained in foreign currency accounts or converted without any surrender requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts in New Zealand. Exporters may retain all earnings in foreign currency, hold them in offshore accounts, or convert them at commercial rates. The only compliance obligations are standard tax reporting under the Income Tax Act 2007 and record-keeping obligations under AML/CFT regulations.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for conduct of financial services

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs (e-money institutions) such as Wise, Airwallex, or Payoneer are viable as supplementary payment processors for inbound subscription collections and low-value disbursements, but they are not regulated as banks in New Zealand and cannot provide a primary corporate settlement account. Their legal status is not recognised as an authorised deposit-taking institution by the RBNZ. They may be used for operational convenience, subject to the entity's own treasury risk policy, but core treasury and FX settlement must be conducted through an RBNZ-licensed registered bank to ensure regulatory certainty and access to local clearing systems.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from shareholders, including trust beneficiaries or parent entities, must be routed via SWIFT MT103 for straightforward wire transfers. The remitter must specify a clear purpose code such as 'Capital Contribution' or 'Intercompany Financing'. The receiving New Zealand bank will verify the source of funds and may require a source-of-funds declaration, supporting loan or equity documents, and confirmation that the funds originate from a compliant AML/CFT jurisdiction. New Zealand does not impose exchange controls on inward remittances. Funds can be received in NZD or held in a foreign currency account. For high-value transfers, banks may require pre-notification and enhanced due diligence.Outbound trade disbursements for procurement, royalties, dividends, or operational payments are executed via SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers, MT202 for bank-to-bank transfers, or commercial letters of credit (LC) for documentary trade transactions. For EdTech, the primary outbound payments are likely intercompany royalties, platform app store commissions, and software vendor payments. New Zealand banks offer API-based batch payments through the Bulk Electronic Clearing System (BECS) for domestic rails, and SWIFT for cross-border.
