Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office (Companies Register), Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), 15 Stout Street, Wellington 6011; online filing platform: companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (NZ), ss 10, 12, 13, 16, 22, 39, 42, 44, 53, 117, 122, 128, 129, 151 and 199-202 (registration, constitution, share capital, registered office, appointment and residence of directors); Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, Part 5 (offer of securities and product disclosure statement); Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, ss 12-19; Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, ss 31-33; Companies (Beneficial Ownership Information) Amendment Act 2023; Income Tax Act 2007, ss BD 1(5), CV 13 and the foreign investment fund rules.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NZCN1 - Application for Registration of a Company under s 12(1)(a) of the Companies Act 1993, together with the company constitution (s 16), director consent forms NZCN5, and the beneficial ownership declaration; upon registration the Registrar issues a Certificate of Incorporation and an NZ Business Number (NZBN).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the Companies Act 1993; a single share may be issued under s 39. To evidence commercial substance, the founding subscriber subscribes for 100,000 ordinary shares at NZD 0.10 nominal value each, being aggregate paid-up capital of NZD 10,000. No statutory escrow arises at incorporation; however, for a listed SPAC, the gross IPO proceeds are contractually segregated and held in a regulated NZ-bank trust escrow account administered by a licensed escrow trustee, in accordance with the offer constitution, the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, and NZX Listing Rules, with release permitted only for qualifying acquisition consideration, pro-rata redemption, or investor return on liquidation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety and Evacuation Scheme approval from Fire and Emergency New Zealand for the registered office floor; an annual Building Warrant of Fitness (BWOF) with Form 12A inspection certificate issued by an Independent Qualified Person (IQP) for the leased premises, verifying structural load capacity and seismic compliance per NZS 1170.5:2004; WorkSafe New Zealand clearance covering the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 workplace policy, electrical equipment test certificates under AS/NZS 3760:2010, first-aid provision and kit audit, and a smoke-free workplace policy; storage of hard-copy escrow and governance records in a class-1 fire-rated cabinet for the entire blank-check period.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake, IRD), operating through the myIR online tax portal (myIR.ird.govt.nz), with supporting registration functions at the New Zealand Companies Office (NZBN) and export/import controls at the New Zealand Customs Service.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity structured as an International Company (SPAC/blank-check acquisition vehicle) in a Tax_Exempt_Zone model: (1) register the vehicle with the New Zealand Companies Office to obtain a New Zealand Business Number if a local shell presence is established; (2) if the vehicle will be treated as a non-resident company deriving no New Zealand-sourced income, no IRD registration is mandatory; (3) upon creating a New Zealand taxable activity, applying for an IRD number for non-resident companies involving completion and submission of the IR596 Application for Registration, together with the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, directors'/beneficial owners' declarations, and the registered foreign tax identification number; (4) separately apply for GST registration through myIR on form IR356, selecting the non-resident taxation division and providing details of anticipated imports, supplies, and a New Zealand resident agent if required; (5) obtain a Customs client code and Trade Single Window (TSW) access for motor vehicle/export declaration obligations; (6) maintain a permanent tax file in myIR for filing NZ corporate tax, GST returns, withholding tax, and annual international disclose forms if the exempt status is lost or a New Zealand permanent establishment is established.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone. The International Company (IC) is a non-resident offshore vehicle classified outside New Zealand's ordinary entity-level taxation for foreign-source income; it is not subject to New Zealand income tax on its worldwide income while it remains in the exempt zone, provided it does not derive New Zealand-sourced gross income through a New Zealand permanent establishment, agency, or taxable activity. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone. The International Company (IC) is a non-resident offshore vehicle classified outside New Zealand's ordinary entity-level taxation for foreign-source income; it is not subject to New Zealand income tax on its worldwide income while it remains in the exempt zone, provided it does not derive New Zealand-sourced gross income through a New Zealand permanent establishment, agency, or taxable activity. On any New Zealand-source taxable income, the entity becomes subject to entity-level income taxation at the standard corporate rate and to non-resident withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Statutory elections and mechanisms available include: (a) election to be a Look-Through Company (LTC) for eligible closely held companies so income/losses flow to shareholders; (b) GST grouping election for associated suppliers; (c) consolidated group election under subpart IG of the Income Tax Act 2007; (d) election under section 104 of the GST Act to zero-rate a supply of a going concern or land; (e) thin-capitalisation and hybrid-mismatch rules are mandatory, not elective, though taxpayers may elect to apply the de minimis exclusion in certain cross-border hybrid arrangements; (f) New Zealand foreign trust election allowing qualifying trusts to pay a foreign-source income exit/foreign income rate as applicable. For a SPAC vehicle, no hybrid check-the-box election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of annual taxable supplies made in New Zealand during any 12-month period. Non-resident suppliers of remote services and electronic marketplace supplies to New Zealand consumers must register for GST when their taxable supplies to New Zealand exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period. Voluntary GST registration is permitted below this threshold, including for refund-oriented registrations where a non-resident is carrying on a taxable activity of a continuing and permanent nature in New Zealand.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating eligibility under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 applies to: (a) supplies of goods that are exported from New Zealand to a destination outside New Zealand before any use in New Zealand under a contract of sale, or removed to a foreign destination within the statutory period; (b) supplies of services that are materially supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, and where the services are not supplied through a fixed establishment in New Zealand, with limited exceptions for property situated in New Zealand and services that produce a direct benefit to New Zealand land; (c) supplies to a New Zealand customs area for later export through a bonded area, subject to documentary conditions. For the SPAC vehicle, the issue, transfer, and redemption of shares and securities are exempt rather than zero-rated; management fees, advisory fees, and escrow/custody services supplied to a non-resident principal from outside New Zealand may qualify for zero-rating if the statutory non-residence and supply-location tests are fully documented.

  • New Zealand Customs Export Declaration lodged through the Trade Single Window (CusCAT) or equivalent outward declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing carriage of the goods to a destination outside New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank standing/foreign exchange credit statement proving export proceeds were received in New Zealand within the statutory repatriation period
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin (if applicable), and, for service zero-rating, a certified non-resident status and place-of-performance evidence file

New Zealand has no mandatory real-time electronic fiscal clearance, no digital stamping obligation, and no government-mandated invoice-authorisation API. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and uses the Peppol PINT/New Zealand e-Invoicing framework. GST returns are filed periodically through myIR, and exports/imports are declared to New Zealand Customs through the Trade Single Window, which performs cargo risk assessment and tariff/revenue collection functions. A New Zealand GST-registered supplier must issue tax invoices showing GST number and either 'zero-rated' or 'GST included' status, but no pre-clearance from IRD is required.

Inland Revenue may reassess a zero-rated export supply retrospectively and claw back 15% GST if the statutory export conditions are not met or if the documentation packet is incomplete. Civil shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply on the resulting GST shortfall: 20% for failure to take reasonable care/unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness or an abusive tax position, and 150% for fraudulent evasion. Use-of-money interest accrues from the date the GST became due. Criminal liability for evading GST can include fines of up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment of up to two years. The ordinary time bar is four years from the filing date, but there is no time limit where the taxpayer has engaged in evasion or obstructive conduct.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised currency and capital account regime. The New Zealand Dollar is freely convertible for current and capital account transactions; there are no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on the repatriation of dividends, interest, or capital proceeds by non-residents. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand retains emergency statutory powers only under the Foreign Exchange Control Regulations 1981 that could be activated in a national crisis, but no such controls are in force for SPAC treasury operations. Profit remittance and capital flows for a non-resident SPAC are unrestricted, subject only to standard tax compliance, AML/CFT verification, and overseas investment screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no specified timeline for the repatriation of export proceeds. Exporters and trading vehicles may retain export receipts in foreign currency, convert freely, or hold them in any approved licensed bank. The only relevant requirements are reporting obligations under the Income Tax Act 2007, the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and the Reserve Bank's statistical data collection. A SPAC acting as a blank-check acquisition vehicle is not subject to any special export proceeds regime.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand – Te Pūtea Matua, together with the Financial Markets Authority for market conduct and AML/CFT supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (electronic money institutions) that are not registered banks in New Zealand have limited viability for SPAC treasury functions. New Zealand law reserves the licensed banking business for registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and the Banking Prudential Requirements. A non-bank may provide payment services only if licensed as a financial service provider and AML/CFT-reporting entity under the FSP Act and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. Offshore EMIs typically cannot operate dedicated corporate trust/escrow accounts, issue bank letters of credit, or offer documentary trade settlement without a New Zealand subsidiary and an approved designated business group AR. For a medium-risk enhanced SPAC, an offshore EMI is rated 'Low viability' for primary account residency, and 'Medium viability' only as a supplementary collection/distribution rail in jurisdictions where the EMI holds a valid e-money licence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections must route exclusively through licensed bank channels using SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border wires. In New Zealand, all transactions are cleared via the New Zealand Exchange Settlement System (ESIS) for NZD and via correspondent banks for USD/EUR/JPY. For SPAC proceeds, the route is underwritten via a dedicated Capital Escrow Account held at a tier-1 registered bank, using BOP purpose codes: for direct investment and SPAC share subscription, use code 6B (Equity Capital) or code 6D (Investment by/in third funds). The remittance instruction must include the SPAC's NZBN, the final recipient account number, and the legal reference 'Injection of share capital into blank-check acquisition vehicle'. The bank will require proof of the underlying subscription agreement, a passport-verified investor list, and post-migration AML/CFT screening. No central bank approval is required, but the receiving bank must file a suspicious transaction report if any chapter IV exemption applies.Outbound payments for trade settlements and acquisition disbursements are effected primarily through SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for plain payments, MT202 for bank-to-bank funds transfers, and a Commercial Documentary Letter of Credit (MT700) for conditional payment against trade documents. For a SPAC release of funds from escrow, the bank trustee will require a release instruction executed by all directors, a signed acquisition agreement, board minutes evidencing the M&A approval, and proof of target due diligence. Payments to New Zealand resident suppliers are cleared via the NZ ECB/2FA deferred clearing system with finality on T+1, while cross-border outflows settle over SWIFT with a same-day or T+1 value date depending on the correspondent corridors. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border related-party transactions exceeding NZ$10 million in an income year trigger the mandatory New Zealand Local File requirement; a Master File is required when the multinational group has aggregate revenue exceeding NZ$50 million in a 12-month period.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not impose Economic Substance Regulations in the Gulf/OECD sense, but the Income Tax Act 2007 applies the general anti-avoidance rule (section BG 1) and the Commissioner may recharacterise artificial or contrived arrangements lacking commercial substance. A company incorporated in New Zealand, or managed and controlled from New Zealand, is treated as tax-resident and taxed on worldwide income; treaty benefits are subject to beneficial ownership and principal purpose / limitation-of-benefit clauses.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax avoided for evasion; criminal prosecution may result in fines of up to NZ$50,000 and imprisonment for up to five years; all assessments are payable with use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or authorise evasion are personally liable for civil penalties, may be subject to criminal prosecution, and may have departure prohibition orders and asset restraint orders issued against them by the Commissioner.
Shell Company Abuse / Sham Structure

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may disregard the corporate form, treat the company as New Zealand resident, reassess all income at 28%, and impose shortfall penalties of 20-150% plus interest. Sham trust arrangements can be voided under general law.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell company can be personally liable under the Companies Act 1993 for allowing the company to trade while insolvent; the court may order civil restitution, disqualification from managing companies, and in serious cases impose custodial sentences.
AML/CFT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 up to NZ$1 million for companies and NZ$200,000 for individuals; criminal penalties and enforceable undertakings may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who knowingly fail to maintain AML/CFT compliance programs are individually liable; they may be subject to personal fines, prohibition orders and, in the case of enabling money laundering, criminal prosecution.
Withholding Tax and NRWT Failure

Statutory sanction: The payer is personally assessed for the unrecovered NRWT/AIL, plus late payment penalties and use-of-money interest. Shortfall penalties for deliberate failure can reach 150% of the unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who are knowingly involved in the non-payment of withholding tax can be jointly and severally liable under the Tax Administration Act 1994, and the Commissioner can recover unpaid amounts directly from director assets.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Unsupported transfer pricing adjustments are assessed as additional corporate income tax; shortfall penalties of 40% apply for an unacceptable transfer pricing position, increasing to 100% or 150% where reasonable care is absent or evasion is established.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve deficient transfer pricing documentation without reasonable grounds can be subject to personal penalty exposure where the company cannot pay; the Commissioner may disqualify them and issue personal liability notices.
