Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake) – sole national tax authority; official taxpayer portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz (secure online services for corporate entities, GST, PAYE, and customs-linked GST information is not managed by this portal; customs matters sit with New Zealand Customs Service at https://www.customs.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A foreign company that is not carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand may apply for an IRD number using form IR742 (Application for an IRD number for a company) submitted to Inland Revenue, with certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitution, register of directors/shareholders, and a New Zealand address for service (a New Zealand tax agent or resident agent is advisable where no fixed place of business exists). The statutory IRD number is the single tax identification number used for income tax, GST, PAYE, and FBT. Where the company becomes liable to register for GST (taxable supplies exceeding NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, or voluntarily), the same IRD number is endorsed for GST via form IR356 or through myIR; the resulting GST registration number is the IRD number suffixed with 'GS' for administrative purposes. Non-resident companies with no New Zealand physical presence must also complete a Non-Resident Land Withholding Tax declaration only where applicable; for EOR payroll operations, registration as an approved payroll intermediary and compliance with Schedule 3 of the Income Tax Act 2007 (PAYE) requires the IRD number and an Employer Schedule filing obligation through myIR.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – New Zealand-resident companies are taxed at 28% corporate income tax on worldwide income; there is no statutory Tax_Exempt_Zone or offshore 'International Company' exempt regime in New Zealand. The metadata tag 'Tax_Exempt_Zone' is not recognised under the Income Tax Act 2007; non-resident companies are taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income and on income attributable to a New Zealand permanent establishment. Certain passive income exemptions and the active business exemption for CFCs (section EX 20B) reduce the effective burden on offshore active income of resident companies, but entity-level taxation remains the default.

Available election forms

Available statutory elections under New Zealand law: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 – maximum five shareholders, fiscally transparent for income tax while remaining a separate legal entity; (2) Limited Partnership treatment under the Limited Partnerships Act 2008 – generally fiscally transparent unless central management and control is exercised in New Zealand, causing the partnership to be treated as a company; (3) CFC active income exemption (section EX 20B) – electing to exempt active attributable CFC income from New Zealand tax; (4) GST grouping election under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 to treat related entities as a single group; (5) GST cash basis / payments basis election under section 89 of the GST Act for smaller entities; (6) hybrid mismatch elections under Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007, which are compliance-driven and generally disallow mismatches rather than creating elective deferrals; (7) foreign tax credit election under subpart LJ (annual definitive election and elimination election). New Zealand does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (NZD 120,000 threshold applies to non-profit bodies). Non-resident suppliers of remote services and digital services (including B2C and some B2B supplies) to New Zealand consumers are required to register and return GST where their annual supplies to New Zealand recipients exceed NZD 60,000, regardless of where the supplier is located. Voluntary registration is permitted where the entity carries on a taxable activity, with a minimum 2-year registration period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating to 0% GST applies under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 to: (1) goods exported by the supplier from New Zealand, where the goods are exported within 28 days of the time of supply (or such longer period as the Commissioner allows); (2) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time services are performed, provided the services are not supplied directly in connection with land in New Zealand, not supplied in connection with goods in New Zealand for the purpose of manufacturing or processing, not supplied to a non-resident who is physically present in New Zealand (with limited exceptions), and not supplied for the purpose of a taxable activity carried on in New Zealand by the recipient; (3) remote services supplied to a non-resident under section 11A when the recipient is outside New Zealand and the services are 'remote services' within the statutory definition; (4) certain supplies of internationally transported goods and services ancillary to international transport. For a Global EOR / Staffing Agency, payroll administration and cross-border contractor management services supplied to an offshore client can be zero-rated only where the recipient is outside New Zealand and does not acquire the services for a taxable activity conducted in New Zealand; services physically performed in New Zealand by employees working in New Zealand (e.g., back-office, domestic placement, local payroll under a New Zealand taxable activity of the recipient) are standard-rated at 15%. Where a GST-registered entity makes zero-rated supplies, input tax credits may be claimed under section 20 and refunds issued on the standard 2-monthly or monthly filing cycle, subject to the Commissioner's right to withhold refunds pending verification.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls, and no digital stamping regime in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Peppol network (New Zealand Peppol Authority under the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment); Inland Revenue accepts electronic invoices and GST returns filed via myIR under the standard GST101/ITA electronic channel. There is no government pre-approval of individual invoices, no API-based invoice clearance obligation, and no national e-invoice clearinghouse; businesses may use commercial invoicing software subject to record-keeping requirements under section 75 of the GST Act 1985 (retention of sufficient records for 7 years).

False or unsupported zero-rating claims attract reassessment with tax shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: up to 150% of the tax shortfall for evasion (with reductions for voluntary disclosure to 75% before audit notification and 95% after), late payment penalties of 1% per month (escalating to 1.5% per month after six months), and use-of-money interest on underpayments calculated daily from the due date. Retrospective clawback of GST refunds is available to Inland Revenue within 4 years for non-fraudulent errors and without any time limit where evasion or fraudulent GST returns are involved (section 108 TAA). Criminal exposure includes: section 143B of the TAA (filing a false GST return) – maximum fine NZD 50,000 on first conviction and NZD 100,000 on subsequent conviction; section 143A TAA (evasion of assessment) – maximum fine NZD 50,000, imprisonment of up to 5 years, or both; and Crimes Act 1961 dishonest-use-of-document offences carrying up to 7 years' imprisonment. Under section 149 of the TAA, directors and officers may be held personally jointly liable for GST debts of a company in circumstances involving carelessness, reckless trading, or asset stripping. Inland Revenue's export zero-rating audit program focuses on retained evidence (export entries, shipping documents, FX remittance records, and non-resident recipient status) and will reassess misclassified supplies at 15% GST with penalties and interest where substantiation is deficient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account; there are no exchange-control regulations restricting the purchase, sale, or transfer of foreign currency, and no surrender requirements have applied since the comprehensive liberalisation of 1984. Current-account items (service fees, payroll, contractor payments, management charges) are freely transmissible without approval. Capital-account items (equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, loan repayments, profit remittances) are likewise unrestricted, provided the underlying documentation is available for bank-level AML/CFT and tax-compliance review. Profit remittance hurdles are exclusively tax-driven: (i) Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) at a default 30% on dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents, reducible to 15% or lower under New Zealand's network of double tax agreements; (ii) Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) of 2% where an offshore lender elects the levy in lieu of NRWT on interest; (iii) the need for CRS and FATCA self-certification forms to establish tax residence; and (iv) potential thin-capitalisation and transfer-pricing adjustments under the Income Tax Act 2007 if intercompany funding is not commercially structured. No currency convertibility bottleneck exists for the International Company treasury.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no Central Bank order requiring exporters or cross-border service providers to convert foreign-currency receipts into NZD. Accordingly, the Global EOR and contractor payroll platform may retain EUR, USD, GBP, or AUD invoices in dedicated Foreign Currency Accounts (FCAs) held at registered New Zealand banks and may disburse those balances directly for offshore payroll and vendor settlements without mandatory repatriation. The only obligations are indirect: (i) banks must report international funds-transfer instructions of NZD 10,000 or more to the New Zealand Police Financial Intelligence Unit under the AML/CFT Act 2009 framework; (ii) the company must declare attributable foreign income and CFC interests, if any, under the Income Tax Act 2007; and (iii) the company must maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation where cross-border service revenue is received from related parties. No conversion timeline or quota applies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), established and governed by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, which superseded the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989. The RBNZ is the central bank of New Zealand and performs the functions of monetary policy, prudential regulation and supervision of registered banks, and management of foreign reserves. New Zealand operates a free-float exchange rate regime; the RBNZ does not administer foreign exchange controls, issue import/export licenses, or require ex-ante approval of specific cross-border payments. Certain non-resident investments in significant business assets or sensitive land are subject to screening by the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) under the Overseas Investment Act 2005; however, ordinary treasury operations such as capital contribution, dividend distribution, trade settlement, and contractual payment flows do not require regulatory authorisation. The Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) jointly supervise compliance with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, which directly governs bank onboarding and wire-transfer due diligence.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are feasible as supplementary payment rails, not as primary banking substitutes. Wise New Zealand Limited (Financial Service Provider No. 367121) and Airwallex New Zealand Limited are registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register and are subject to the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 as money remitters. Their material legal constraints are: (i) they are not registered banks under the Banking Act 1982 and therefore cannot accept deposit liabilities the way registered banks do; (ii) client funds must be held in segregated safeguarding accounts at licensed partner banks, creating a custody-chain relationship that may frustrate perfect payment finality; (iii) they cannot directly settle through the Exchange Settlement Account System (NZESIF) operated by the RBNZ; and (iv) transaction thresholds trigger travel-rule information sharing and enhanced due diligence. Feasibility rating: Medium-High for low-value, high-frequency cross-border contractor payouts below NZD 50,000 per transaction in corridors well-served by Wise or Airwallex; Low for capital escrow, large intercompany liquidity buffers, or any activity requiring a regulatory bank guarantee or documentary letter of credit. The ultimate treasury structure must always embed at least one tier-1 registered bank as anchor liquidity provider.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholder equity injections should be routed through SWIFT MT103 (for amounts below the bank's value threshold) or SWIFT MT202/202COV (interbank covers), using ISO 20022 purpose code ECEQ (equity capital) or CAPT (capital transfer). The remittance narrative must cite the relevant board/shareholder resolution approving the allotment and must include the company registration number and legal-entity identifier. New Zealand imposes no exchange-control filing or prior consent; nevertheless, banks automatically transmit international funds-transfer instructions of NZD 10,000 or more to the New Zealand Police Financial Intelligence Unit, and the account-opening bank must verify that the payer is not a sanctioned person. For injections exceeding NZD 100 million or transactions involving sensitive land, the company must first complete an Overseas Investment Act 2005 assessment. The funds may be received into a dedicated Capital Escrow Account held at a tier-1 bank, with release effected only after reconciliation against the share-allotment register maintained under the Companies Act 1993. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs are feasible as supplementary payment rails, not as primary banking substitutes. Wise New Zealand Limited (Financial Service Provider No. 367121) and Airwallex New Zealand Limited are registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register and are subject to the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 as money remitters. Their material legal constraints are: (i) they are not registered banks under the Banking Act 1982 and therefore cannot accept deposit liabilities the way registered banks do; (ii) client funds must be held in segregated safeguarding accounts at licensed partner banks, creating a custody-chain relationship that may frustrate perfect payment finality; (iii) they cannot directly settle through the Exchange Settlement Account System (NZESIF) operated by the RBNZ; and (iv) transaction thresholds trigger travel-rule information sharing and enhanced due diligence. Feasibility rating: Medium-High for low-value, high-frequency cross-border contractor payouts below NZD 50,000 per transaction in corridors well-served by Wise or Airwallex; Low for capital escrow, large intercompany liquidity buffers, or any activity requiring a regulatory bank guarantee or documentary letter of credit.
