Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (Te Tari Taake), operating under the Tax Administration Act 1994; primary online portal: myIR (secure taxpayer portal for IRD number registration, GST registration, GST return filing, and export zero-rating records).

E-government filing portal

A NZ-incorporated Limited Company (NZ_LTD) is automatically registered with Inland Revenue upon incorporation under the Companies Act 1993 and receives a unique 9-digit IRD number (tax identification number) without separate application. A foreign-incorporated entity carrying on business in New Zealand must apply for an IRD number using Form IR742 (Company IRD number application) or via the myIR 'Register for an IRD number' workflow, providing the certificate of incorporation, registered office address, director/shareholder particulars, and NZ tax agent appointment if applicable. The same IRD number is then used for GST registration, which is completed online in myIR (or by filing Form GST 101A for non-resident GST registration) when actual or expected taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 per 12-month rolling period, or voluntarily at any lower level. Exporters must additionally obtain a New Zealand Customs client code (importer/exporter code) from the Customs Trade Single Window to file Customs Export Declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with Territorial Scope: NZ_LTD is a resident company taxed at the corporate income tax rate of 28% on worldwide income under the Income Tax Act 2007. Non-resident companies are taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income. The default status is a separate entity-level taxpayer; pass-through treatment is not available by default but a closely held eligible company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under Subpart HB, with the 28% corporate tax base replaced by shareholder-level taxation.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not employ the United States check-the-box regime. Statutory elections available to NZ_LTD include: (i) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under ss HB 1-HB 16 Income Tax Act 2007 where the company has 5 or fewer owners and no corporate LTC shareholder; (ii) GST group registration election under s 55 Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 permitting related companies to file consolidated GST returns; (iii) imputation credit account election – NZ resident companies generally must maintain an imputation credit account, but a company with wholly non-resident shareholders may elect to be a non-imputation company; and (iv) hybrid mismatch election/ordering adjustments under ss FH 1-FH 11 Income Tax Act 2007 to neutralise hybrid instrument and hybrid entity mismatches consistent with OECD BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 – mandatory GST registration once taxable supplies made in New Zealand exceed NZD 60,000 (GST-exclusive) in any 12-month rolling period. Non-resident suppliers of remote services (electronically supplied services) and low-value imported goods are subject to the same NZD 60,000 threshold based on taxable supplies to New Zealand consumers; voluntary registration below the threshold is permitted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: exported goods are zero-rated under s 11(2)(b) GST Act 1985 where the supplier exports (or makes arrangement for export of) the goods within 28 days after the time of supply and the supplier holds credible export evidence (customs export approval, bill of lading, air waybill, or equivalent transport evidence). Services: internationally supplied services are zero-rated under s 11A where the services are supplied to a non-resident (or a resident outside NZ) and are not services supplied in New Zealand, not connected with NZ land or improvements, not NZ holiday accommodation, and not excluded transport services. For manufacturing of action figure mecha model kits (ISIC 3240) exported to offshore buyers, zero-rating applies to the sale of the physical goods and, if separately invoiced, to exports of intangible or engineering services to non-residents, subject to the s 11A exceptions.

  • New Zealand Customs Export Declaration (Customs Export Entry filed in the Trade Single Window with approval status)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of the action figure and mecha model kits from New Zealand within 28 days of supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or original SWIFT/bank transfer statement evidencing receipt of foreign-currency proceeds
  • Zero-rated tax/commercial invoice (s 24 GST Act 1985 compliant)
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (NZ Certificate of Origin / Form NZ) supporting the export consignment

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, tax digital stamping, or mandatory Government API invoice clearance exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary, using the Pan-European Public Procurement On-Line (Peppol) framework operated under the TXNZ Peppol Authority, interoperable with Australia's trans-Tasman e-invoicing network. Inland Revenue mandatorily requires electronic filing of GST returns through myIR (or approved tax-agent software) and requires registered persons to keep all tax invoices, credit notes, debit notes, export evidence, and underlying accounting records for 7 years under s 75 GST Act 1985 and s 22 Tax Administration Act 1994.

IRD audits export zero-rating claims strictly: if the goods are not physically exported within 28 days, or the supplier fails to hold the statutory export evidence within the required timeframe, the supply is recharacterised as taxable and GST at 15% is assessed retrospectively with use-of-money interest (Part 7 Tax Administration Act 1994). Late payment penalties accrue at an initial 1%, an additional 4% after 7 days, and incremental 1% per month while unpaid. Shortfall penalties under s 141A-141M TAA 1994 apply: 20% for failing to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for knowing tax shortfall, and 150% for evasion. Fraudulent zero-rating documentation attracts clawback of all refunded GST, promoter penalties, court fines, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates one of the most liberalised currency regimes in the OECD. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible across both current-account and capital-account transactions with zero exchange-control approval requirements, zero transaction licensing, zero quantitative restrictions, and zero surrender obligations for a standard ISIC 3240 action-figure manufacturing company owned by non-residents. Equity injections, shareholder loans, trade payments, royalties, and profit remittances may flow freely at market-determined exchange rates. The only relevant fiscal frictions are non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at 15% on dividends, and potentially reduced rates under applicable double-tax agreements; the Overseas Investment Act 2015 applies only to sensitive land or significant business assets generally above the NZD 100 million screening threshold for non-consenting overseas persons and will not capture a standard injection-moulding manufacturing operation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandated repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters of ISIC 3240 goods may retain all foreign-currency export receipts in foreign-currency bank accounts (FCAs), convert at any time at market rates, or use the receipts directly for offshore procurement of resin, steel coil, tooling, and logistics. The only compliance obligations are statistical and prudential: accurate declaration of export values on New Zealand Customs Service export entries, standard AML/CFT customer due diligence on counterparties, and fair-value/translation accounting of foreign-currency balances under NZ IAS 21.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the nation's central bank and prudential regulator under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Acts 1989 and 2021, acting jointly with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) on market conduct and with the Department of Internal Affairs as supervisor under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 for the banking industry.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary collection/payment rail for B2C and e-commerce settlement (e.g. PayPal, Stripe, Checkout.com); Low as a primary substitute for regulated corporate banking. Legal constraints: any financial-service provider carrying on business in New Zealand must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and comply with the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. An offshore digital EMI without New Zealand incorporation cannot open a domestic transactional account for the NZ entity; however, the NZ entity may procure collection services through a locally incorporated and FSPR-registered payment provider, provided that all EMI flows are swept to the primary operational account at a registered bank and are fully reconcilable for IRD and audit purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder-loan capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from the overseas shareholder's bank account to the NZ entity's operational or capital-escrow account at a registered New Zealand bank. The remittance must carry a purpose-of-payment code consistent with New Zealand's Balance of Payments direct-investment classification, commonly expressed as "Equity Capital — Direct Investment" or the correspondent-bank code "901/Equity", alongside a clear narrative referencing the shareholder resolution and capital-contribution schedule. The receiving bank will verify the remitter against the company's share register, screen against sanctions and PEP databases, and may request the underlying board resolution, the shareholder's certificate of incorporation, and the passport of the signing officer. No RBNZ exchange-control approval is required; funds may be remitted in NZD or any freely convertible currency. To prevent shortfall, remit with 'OUR' (payer-pays-all-charges) instructions, and retain the MT103 confirmation, bank credit advice, and shareholder-resolution minutes as permanent record for AML/CFT audit, future dividend-repatriation substantiation, and NZ accounting basis records.Trade disbursements will utilise two complementary clearing rails. First, SWIFT MT103 direct transfers for recurring operating imports — ABS resin pellets, steel coil, packaging, freight, and logistics services — settled in the respective currency through the NZ bank's correspondent network (CHIPS/Fedwire for USD, TARGET2 for EUR, BOJ-Net for JPY) or in NZD through the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS), which provides real-time gross settlement. Second, irrevocable documentary letters of credit governed by UCP600 for high-value capital equipment and precision mould/die acquisitions, where the overseas supplier demands a bank payment undertaking conditioned on presentation of a clean bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin. Every outbound disbursement must be supported by the underlying purchase order, supplier pro-forma invoice, New Zealand Customs import-entry filing, and, where the transaction involves currency conversion, an FX deal confirmation, so that the bank's regulatory screening, IRD transfer-pricing documentation, and GST input-credit substantiation are seamlessly satisfied.Delivered outright spot and forward foreign-exchange contracts (NZD/USD, NZD/EUR, NZD/JPY, NZD/AUD) for firm import settlement and export-receipt conversion, with tenors of up to 12-24 months and no FX-authorisation requirement under New Zealand lawNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on NZD traded with global bank FX desks where counterparties require USD or EUR settlement and where NZD liquidity or counterparty credit considerations favour a non-deliverable structure; permissible because New Zealand maintains full convertibility and imposes no hedging restrictionCross-currency interest-rate swaps (NZD/USD, NZD/EUR) to align multi-year equipment-finance liabilities or to swap USD-denominated export cash flows into NZD funding streams under standard ISDA/CSA documentationPlain-vanilla European FX options (purchased, delta-hedged) to protect the treasury against adverse NZD movements on long-dated contracted receivables or major capital-expenditure commitmentsFX limit orders, same-day conversion lines, and forward-rate agreements executed on the electronic FX trading platforms of registered New Zealand banks for day-to-day liquidity and working-capital management

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends: 30% statutory rate for non-resident companies and individuals, subject to reduction under an applicable double tax agreement (DTA). The non-resident must be the beneficial owner and provide a valid certificate of residence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand does not legislate a fixed monetary threshold for contemporaneous TP documentation; practical documentation is expected where cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZ$10 million in aggregate annual value. Inland Revenue can require documentation for any transaction. Master File and Local File mandatory under OECD BEPS Action 13 principles for multinational groups meeting: (i) group consolidated revenue ≥ NZ$1.1 billion, and (ii) New Zealand entity revenue ≥ NZ$50 million (subject to revision); in any case, documentation must be prepared for significant related-party transactions to avoid penalties.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand's Income Tax Act 2007 contains a general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1) that disregards arrangements lacking commercial substance. Transfer pricing rules require the economic substance of transactions to reflect actual functions, assets, and risks. For a New Zealand manufacturing company producing action figure mecha model kits, economic substance requires real production facilities, employees, and decision-making in New Zealand. Interest limitation rules (2021) deny excessive interest deductions for multinational groups with low substance relative to assets/expenditure. No separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) exist, but the OECD principal purpose test (PPT) applies under DTAs, and shell arrangements are void for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty of 150% of the tax evaded. Criminal conviction under the Tax Administration Act 1994: imprisonment up to 5 years, fine up to NZ$50,000 (individual), NZ$250,000 (company). Confiscation of proceeds under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009. Interest and late payment penalties also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or authorise evasion are personally liable for the company's tax debt under the Companies Act 1993 s 300 and Tax Administration Act s 149A for PAYE/NRWT/GST. Directors may face criminal liability, fines, imprisonment, travel bans, asset restraint orders, and disqualification as directors (up to 5 years).
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s BG 1) may void the arrangement and reattribute income to the New Zealand company. Transfer pricing adjustments are made under s GC 6-GC 14; shortfall penalties of 20%-40% for unacceptable tax positions, 100% for evasion. Interest limitation rules may disallow excessive debt deductions. The company may also be struck off under the Companies Act.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for tax shortfalls if they signed incorrect returns. The corporate veil is pierced where the company is a mere sham. Directors may face disqualification and liability for unpaid Crown debts; travel bans may be imposed to enforce tax collection.
AML/CTF Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, civil penalties up to NZ$800,000 for a company and NZ$200,000 for an individual; criminal penalties up to NZ$5 million for a body corporate and NZ$2 million and/or 2 years imprisonment for an individual. The Financial Market Authority and NZ Police can impose infringement notices, asset freezes, and forfeiture orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who directed or acquiesced in AML/CFT breaches may be personally liable for penalties. Money laundering offences under the Crimes Act 1961 carry up to 7 years imprisonment. Directors face disqualification and asset confiscation.
Cross-Border Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS)

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustments to arm's length consideration, denial of deductions, recharacterisation of arrangements. Penalties for TP non-compliance: 20% for an unacceptable TP position, 40% for gross carelessness, and a 50% shortfall penalty for failure to prepare/maintain TP documentation by the statutory deadline. Additional civil penalties and interest. The CFC rules may attribute income back to New Zealand shareholders.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the tax shortfall if they knowingly signed false returns or directed the TP scheme. Where the company becomes insolvent from penalties, directors can face personal liability for Crown debt under the Companies Act. Criminal liability for evasion where deliberate.
Imputation and Supplementary Dividend Manipulation

Statutory sanction: NRWT becomes due at 30% (or treaty rate) on gross dividends. Imputation debit penalties apply under s OB 61 of the Income Tax Act (NZ$1,000 penalty for debit balances not cleared). Hybrid mismatch rules (ss FH 1-FH 11) neutralise double non-taxation. Shortfall penalties of 20%-150% apply. The Companies Act s 55 requires solvency before distributions; breach results in personal liability to repay.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise a dividend while the company is insolvent are personally liable to the company for the amount distributed. Directors may be personally liable for unremitted NRWT under s 149A. In cases of intentional tax fraud, directors face prosecution, imprisonment, and penalties.
