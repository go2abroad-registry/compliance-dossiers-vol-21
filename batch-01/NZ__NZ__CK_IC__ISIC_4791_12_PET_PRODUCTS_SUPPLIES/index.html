Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. At least one share of any class must be subscribed and allotted to the incorporating shareholder upon registration, and shares may be partly paid or unpaid. Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: where physical business premises are occupied, a Building Warrant of Fitness (BWOF) must be current under the Building Act 2004, and an approved fire evacuation scheme must be registered with Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) under s.68 of the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake), Wellington, New Zealand; taxpayer portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must apply to the IRD for a New Zealand IRD number, which is the statutory taxpayer identification number; there is no separate VAT/GST registration number. An eligible International Company applying from outside New Zealand should use Form IR742 (IRD number application for a non-resident company) and provide certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director/shareholder particulars, a New Zealand contact or registered office, and confirmation of non-residence where relevant. After obtaining an IRD number, the entity registers for GST electronically through myIR using the GST registration process. Foreign companies also registered on the New Zealand Companies Register as an overseas company must provide their NZ Company Number. The same IRD number is used for income tax, GST, and other tax obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not apply a United States-style check-the-box election. Available elections include the election to become a Look-Through Company (LTC) for qualifying New Zealand-resident companies, election for Māori authority tax treatment, and certain GST treatment elections. Hybrid mismatch rules apply under New Zealand tax law to recharacterise or deny deductions for hybrid entities and instruments. For an International Company treated as a non-resident and not deriving New Zealand-source income, no entity classification election is required; if it otherwise carries on business in New Zealand, entity-level income tax applies to New Zealand-source income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (annual taxable supplies in the last 12 months or expected next 12 months; the threshold also applies to offshore suppliers of remote services and digital goods supplied to New Zealand consumers; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST-registered supplier may zero-rate supplies of goods that are physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier within 28 days, including e-commerce sales of pet products to non-resident customers. Zero-rating also applies to certain services supplied to non-residents, including remotely supplied services received outside New Zealand, provided the recipient is outside New Zealand and the supply is not connected with land or goods situated in New Zealand. The supplier must hold contemporaneous documentary evidence proving export, delivery, and payment from the foreign customer.

  • NZ Customs electronic export declaration (export entry or export manifest)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list itemising the PET pet supplies and pet care devices
  • Inward remittance certificate or New Zealand bank statement proving export proceeds received and FX repatriated within the 180-day window
  • GST tax invoice for the zero-rated sale and any transport or logistics contracts

New Zealand does not operate a real-time tax clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime. GST tax invoices must contain the prescribed particulars, and GST-registered persons must file periodic GST returns through myIR. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates on the Peppol New Zealand network; there is no mandatory government fiscal stamping, digital approval, or per-invoice IRD clearance requirement. Records, including export evidence, must be retained for seven years.

The IRD may audit zero-rated export claims and require proof of physical export, genuine offshore supply, and repatriation of proceeds. If substantiation fails, the supply is reassessed at 15% GST, input credits are clawed back, and use-of-money interest runs from the original return date. Civil shortfall penalties range from 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and up to 150% for evasion or an abusive tax position. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims are prosecutable under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and may attract criminal fines and imprisonment. Reassessment and clawback may extend to prior periods, and deliberate falsification of export documentation may also trigger Customs offences.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full and free convertibility on both current and capital account. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a free-floating, freely tradeable currency with no quantitative exchange controls, no central bank permission requirements for inbound or outbound payments, and no obstacles to the repatriation of profits, dividends, royalties, interest, or liquidation proceeds. Since the abolition of exchange controls in 1984, neither the Reserve Bank of New Zealand nor the Treasury imposes approval gates or surrender quotas. The only compliance frictions are generic: AML/CFT reporting under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (including prescribed transaction reports for cash over NZD 10,000), and the potential application of Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) or Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) on outbound interest and dividend flows, which are assessable under tax law rather than FX regulation. The subject International Company, classified as Tax_Exempt_Zone, will enjoy unrestricted capital mobility through the standard banking system, provided its beneficial ownership documentation and source-of-funds evidence satisfy bank due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no mandatory surrender, no forced repatriation timing, and no statutory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds in NZD or any foreign currency may be retained indefinitely in offshore accounts or held in New Zealand foreign-currency bank accounts. There are no Reserve Bank directions requiring settlement within a specific number of days and no percentage-based conversion obligations. The only applicable obligation is evidentiary: export documentation (commercial invoices, packing lists, customs declarations) must be retained for AML/CFT and tax audit purposes, and any transaction involving physical cash movement above NZD 10,000 must be reported to the New Zealand Financial Intelligence Unit. For the subject e-commerce retail operation, payment settlement from Stripe and PayPal into NZD or USD accounts is unrestricted and fully retainable.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua), operating solely as the central bank and monetary authority; foreign exchange policy is liberalized and administered by the Bank under the non-statutory framework of New Zealand's no-exchange-control regime, with the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) exercising prudential oversight only for 'sensitive' asset acquisitions and the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) supervising FX derivative providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for non-resident-owned e-commerce entities. New Zealand-licensed financial technology providers and Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) that hold a Financial Service Provider (FSP) registration and are supervised by the FMA, or are registered as money remitters under the AML/CFT Act 2009, are legally viable. Providers such as Airwallex (NZ FSP), Wise New Zealand, and Payoneer can issue multi-currency settlement accounts with virtual IBANs/NZ bank account numbers, enabling seamless Stripe/PayPal sweeps. Legal constraints: (i) the EMI must maintain a local anti-money laundering compliance officer; (ii) the International Company must pass beneficial-ownership disclosure equivalent to Tier-1 bank standards; (iii) EMI accounts typically do not provide credit facilities, letters of credit, or interest-bearing treasury products; (iv) some EMIs decline jurisdictions with offshore trust structures unless the trust deed and source of wealth are exhaustively documented. For the subject low-risk e-commerce profile, a hybrid structure of an NZ Tier-1 parent operational account plus a global EMI (e.g., Airwallex) for cross-border supplier disbursement is the most efficient configuration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital subscription from the offshore parent or shareholder must be executed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank cover) into the New Zealand operational account held at a Tier-1 bank. Compliant protocol: (i) obtain a board resolution authorizing the capital contribution and specifying whether it is equity injection or subordinated shareholder loan; (ii) if equity, make entries in the company's share register and, for NZ company law purposes, advise Companies Office of any issue of shares to non-resident persons; (iii) the remitting bank must use a purpose code or payment description explicitly stating 'Share Capital Subscription' or 'Capital Injection - ISIC 4791 E-Commerce'; (iv) the receiving bank will credit the NZD account in real-time via the ESAS settlement system; (v) if the amount exceeds NZD 50,000, the receiving bank will request supporting evidence including the board resolution, source-of-funds statement, and UBO identification; (vi) no OIO consent is required because an e-commerce retail business does not constitute 'sensitive land', 'significant business assets', or fishing quota, unless the relevant 25% or more ownership threshold is crossed by a non-resident; (vii) for amounts in USD or AUD, the bank may auto-convert at the prevailing interbank rate or route through the multi-currency account, subject to the exchange rate and the bank's market-marker spread. A receipt confirmation (MT910) should be reconciled against the company's internal cash ledger.Clearing rails for trade settlements are: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments where the beneficiary is known and the amount is below LC thresholds, with the reference field containing the invoice number and purchase order ID to satisfy audit trail; (2) Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) issued via SWIFT MT700 for high-value or first-time supplier relationships, with payment upon presentation of compliant shipping documents; (3) Documentary Collections (MT400/MT422) for trusted Asian suppliers seeking lower costs than LC; (4) for payment processor disbursements, Stripe and PayPal settlement in NZD will be initiated by the processors via SWIFT or via their local New Zealand clearing arrangements (BECS/ESAS) into the designated bank account, with a settlement delay of typically 2-7 banking days; (5) intra-NZD government and tax payments, if any, will clear via BECS and Inland Revenue's payment channels; and (6) supplier payments to New Zealand domestic vendors will use the Bulk Electronic Clearing System (BECS) / PayTo rail. For FX conversion associated with outbound disbursements, the bank's treasury desk executes at the NZD spot rate; no surrender or conversion approval is required. Any payment to a jurisdiction under an OFAC/UN sanctions embargo will trigger an automated block and compliance referral, so the supplier master list must be screened against the NZ Sanctions Register and OFAC SDN list.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for non-resident-owned e-commerce entities. New Zealand-licensed financial technology providers and Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) that hold a Financial Service Provider (FSP) registration and are supervised by the FMA, or are registered as money remitters under the AML/CFT Act 2009, are legally viable. Providers such as Airwallex (NZ FSP), Wise New Zealand, and Payoneer can issue multi-currency settlement accounts with virtual IBANs/NZ bank account numbers, enabling seamless Stripe/PayPal sweeps. Legal constraints: (i) the EMI must maintain a local anti-money laundering compliance officer; (ii) the International Company must pass beneficial-ownership disclosure equivalent to Tier-1 bank standards; (iii) EMI accounts typically do not provide credit facilities, letters of credit, or interest-bearing treasury products; (iv) some EMIs decline jurisdictions with offshore trust structures unless the trust deed and source of wealth are exhaustively documented. For the subject low-risk e-commerce profile, a hybrid structure of an NZ Tier-1 parent operational account plus a global EMI (e.g., Airwallex) for cross-border supplier disbursement is the most efficient configuration.
