Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (Te Tari Taake) — New Zealand's national tax administrator; taxpayer portal is myIR (myIR.ird.govt.nz).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident or newly incorporated company that commences business activity in New Zealand must obtain an IRD number before filing income tax and/or GST returns. Procedure: (1) determine the form of entity and the activity to be conducted; (2) submit the applicable registration application — IRD Form IR596 (for non-resident individuals and special companies) or the online company registration via the New Zealand Companies Office and myIR for locally incorporated International Companies; (3) obtain an IRD number, which simultaneously serves as the GST identification number where GST registration is elected or required; (4) where trading turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed NZD 60,000 per 12-month period, complete GST registration on Form GST101A or through myIR; (5) submit supporting identity and corporate governance documentation (certificate of incorporation, director identification, registered office details, and foreign ownership details); (6) appoint a New Zealand tax agent or registered address for service if no local director is available. The IRD number issuance is generally completed within 5–10 business days of a complete application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box election; classification follows the Income Tax Act 2007 and applicable double-tax agreements. Available statutory elections include: (i) Look-Through Company (LTC) election for close-held companies to achieve fiscal transparency; (ii) Qualifying Company (QC) election with a separate entity-level rate; (iii) GST payment-basis election (invoice basis vs. payments basis) for cash-flow timing; (iv) GST grouping election under GST Act 1985; and (v) hybrid-mismatch election or consent-based arrangement adjustments under Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007 to neutralise deductible/non-inclusion mismatches for cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 annual turnover: a person must register for GST once supplies made in New Zealand in a 12-month period exceed or are expected to exceed NZD 60,000; supplies to non-residents outside NZ are disregarded for the registration threshold test.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (a) goods physically removed from New Zealand by the supplier or recipient within 28 days of the time of supply; (b) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply where the services are not physically supplied to a person (or performed) in New Zealand and are not directly connected with real property situated in New Zealand; (c) international transportation and associated services; and (d) supplies of certain intellectual property rights and digital services to non-residents where the performance-based and land-based carve-outs do not apply. For the video-matching app scenario, the digital app services supplied to users outside NZ qualify for zero-rating where the offshore recipient is outside New Zealand and the services are not consumed in NZ; services consumed by individuals present in New Zealand remain standard-rated at 15%.

  • NZ Customs Export Entry / Customs Export Declaration (approved export entry under the Customs and Excise Act 2018)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal of goods from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer advice evidencing settlement from the offshore buyer
  • Executed supply contract with the non-resident customer including the non-resident status of the counter-party
  • Service delivery records/logs demonstrating that the services are performed offshore or supplied to a recipient outside New Zealand
  • Evidence of the non-resident’s jurisdiction of residence (e.g., foreign trade registry or tax certificate)

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control. Electronic invoice issuance is optional but increasingly standard; Inland Revenue supports voluntary Peppol (E-invoicing) interoperability for B2B and B2G transactions. GST-registered persons invoicing NZD 60,000 or more annually in a taxable activity must file GST returns and maintain electronic records; large businesses are expected to use myIR online lodgement, but there is no government approval or pre-clearance requirement for individual tax invoices. Tax records must be retained for seven years.

Zero-rating substantiation is subject to Inland Revenue audit under the Tax Administration Act 1994, with a four-year time bar (extendable for evasion or fraud). Wrongly claimed zero-rating may be clawed back with use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for unconscionable tax avoidance or abusive tax position, and up to 150% of the tax shortfall for evasion or intentional misstatement. Offence-level penalties may also apply for knowingly creating false documentation in support of an export zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully free-floating and freely convertible currency under a deregulated financial system; since the March 1985 float there has been no exchange-control licensing regime, no capital-account approval queue, and no restriction on non-resident ownership of NZD or foreign-currency accounts. Current-account transactions (ISIC 6311 cross-border telephony and video-match subscription receivables) and capital-account transactions (equity injections, loans, and repatriation) are unrestricted, subject only to tax compliance, notably non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at 15% on dividends, interest, and royalties, treaty-reduced where a Double Tax Agreement applies, and to standard Balance of Payments statistical reporting by the receiving bank. The only material structural hurdle is the Overseas Investment Act 2005: a non-resident acquiring sensitive New Zealand land or significant business assets above the statutory thresholds (e.g., NZD 100M for non-OECD investors and NZD 200M for OECD investors) requires consent from the Overseas Investment Office; a treasury holding only bank deposits, trade receivables, and intellectual-property contracts will not trigger consent. For the tax-exempt CK_IC, the practical convertibility risk is bank de-risking rather than legal convertibility restriction; therefore, the treasury strategy must anchor on a Tier-1 New Zealand bank with a dedicated offshore-corporate client segment and a bilateral FX dealing line.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist in New Zealand. There is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate export or service proceeds; the residual exchange-control powers of the Reserve Bank of New Zealand are emergency-only and are not exercisable in normal economic conditions. The CK_IC may lawfully retain 100% of its USD, Apple IAP, and Paymentwall receivables in foreign-currency settlement accounts, select the timing and counterparty for conversion into NZD, and maintain multi-currency balances as natural hedging. The only compliance incidents are (i) AML/CFT suspicious-transaction and large-cash reporting to the New Zealand Police Financial Intelligence Unit, (ii) the receiving bank's obligation to report the economic purpose of each inward credit to the RBNZ Balance of Payments survey using the BPM6 'computer services/information services' classification corresponding to ISIC 6311 / NAICS 519130, and (iii) daily transaction-monitoring obligations under the bank's customer risk-rating for a Medium_Risk_Enhanced profile.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) as the sole monetary authority, prudential regulator of registered banks, and operator of the ESAS real-time gross settlement system; jointly with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) exercising AML/CFT market-conduct supervision over financial service providers in New Zealand. For the CK_IC (a Cook Islands International Company), registration integrity is supervised by the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission, and all NZD-denominated treasury clearing, foreign-exchange settlement, and bank prudential oversight flow through the Reserve Bank of New Zealand.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as an auxiliary collection/payout rail, LOW as a primary treasury bank. New Zealand has no dedicated e-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime; a non-bank operator must register on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the FSP Act 2008, comply with the AML/CFT Act 2009 as a reporting entity, and cannot access the ESAS/NZClear settlement systems without a commercial-bank sponsor. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Currenxie, and Paymentwall Payouts can open business accounts for CK_IC and provide USD/EUR/GBP virtual ledgers, but the following legal constraints apply: (i) funds held with an EMI are not legal tender or protected deposits in New Zealand; (ii) EMIs generally decline tax-exempt offshore international companies without a substantive OECD nexus; (iii) sub-ledger positions at an EMI must be disclosed to the principal clearing bank, which will treat them as counterparty credit exposure; and (iv) direct consumer deposits into a NZ account would constitute unlawful deposit-taking outside the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. Therefore, the CK_IC treasury architecture must use a Tier-1 New Zealand bank as the settlement anchor and EMIs only for niche purpose-specific rails such as rapid creator payouts in Southeast Asian markets.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Founding and growth equity from non-resident shareholders or venture investors is injected via SWIFT MT103 from the remitter's bank to the CK_IC's ANZ or Westpac NZD/USD capital account. The remitter must complete the bank's inward-purpose declaration using the BPM6 classification 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital', and the receiving bank will file the corresponding Balance of Payments statistical return with the Reserve Bank of New Zealand. To avoid unavoidable-wealth flags, the IC must pre-approve each subscribing shareholder on its bank profile and attach: an executed board resolution allotting shares, an updated shareholders' register, the signed subscription agreement, and a funds-origin statement comprising six months of bank statements from the investor. If the investor is domiciled in a high-risk jurisdiction, the compliant routing is a two-hop transfer: first into the IC's Singapore/HK treasury collection account at a white-list bank, and second an onward MT103 carrying the full originator and beneficiary trail into New Zealand; the NZ clearing bank will require a narrative field stating 'Equity capital - no goods/services - ISIC 6311 video-match app' to prevent a trade-mismatch auto-suspend. No OIO consent is required while the injected capital is deployed as working capital, software-development spend, or financial assets; however, if the IC later acquires 25% or more of a New Zealand business above the Overseas Investment Act thresholds, the acquisition must receive OIO consent prior to settlement. USD tranches may be converted at the bank's dealing desk at interbank mid-minus-spread, with a booked forward where the deployment date is known and a swap where timing is uncertain.The CK_IC treasury disburses along the following clearing rails: (1) Corporate payments via SWIFT MT103/MT202 for IP licensing, cloud-bandwidth, legal, accounting, and director fees, each tagged with an internal treasury reference (e.g., BANDWIDTH-AMZN-001) and backed by the underlying contract retained in the corporate treasury management ledger. (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) and documentary collections under URC 522 only where the IC is importing physical IT hardware, procuring server equipment, or acquiring intellectual property under a bank-assured escrow; for the core ISIC 6311 video-match service, an LC is not appropriate and repeated LC use without underlying transport documents would be a red flag, so the default is MT103. (3) Mass payout rails for revenue-share, content-moderation, developer, and influencer payments: the IC aggregates payouts through Paymentwall's payout API or through a file-based BNZ/ANZ bulk-credit product; NZD-denominated payouts clear via Payments NZ BECS (Bulk Electronic Clearing System) on a D+1 cycle, while foreign-currency payouts clear via SWIFT on a D+2 international cycle. (4) Intercompany funding movements between the NZ treasury account and the CK_IC's EMI sub-ledgers are executed as MT103 transfers with purpose code 'Intercompany loan - treasury centralisation' and are subject to automatic EDD prompts above NZD 10,000-equivalent. All outbound disbursements are screened against sanctions and adverse-media lists; beneficiaries showing aggregate flows above NZD 10,000-equivalent per rolling 30-day period trigger enhanced due diligence and contract verification.Deliverable FX forwards (tenors up to 12 months) in NZD/USD, NZD/AUD, NZD/EUR, and NZD/GBP, executed under ISDA/CSA or the bank's master FX terms, aligning with Apple's 21-day and 45-day App Store settlement cycles and Paymentwall's T+7/T+14 cycles to hedge subscription revenue repatriation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in PHP, VND, IDR, THB, INR, and BRL for collection proceeds from Southeast Asian and Latin American user cohorts that are ultimately settled in USD but expose the treasury to local-currency conversion variance.Cross-currency swaps (XCS) converting a portion of the USD subscription float into NZD at matched tenors, funding the NZ-resident payroll, corporate-services administration, and cloud-hosting obligations without repetitive spot conversion.Vanilla FX call and put options purchased from the ANZ/Westpac dealing desks, with capped premiums, to protect budget rates for strategic M&A or capex in foreign currencies, such as acquiring a 49% interest in a Philippine development studio or purchasing EU-based data-centre hardware.Natural hedging through a multi-currency pooling and notional-pooling arrangement (e.g., ANZ Multi-Currency Account or Westpac Global Currency Account), allowing the IC to net USD, NZD, AUD, and EUR ledgers internally and to reduce the notional size and cost of outright derivative transactions.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as an auxiliary collection/payout rail, LOW as a primary treasury bank. New Zealand has no dedicated e-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime; a non-bank operator must register on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the FSP Act 2008, comply with the AML/CFT Act 2009 as a reporting entity, and cannot access the ESAS/NZClear settlement systems without a commercial-bank sponsor. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Currenxie, and Paymentwall Payouts can open business accounts for CK_IC and provide USD/EUR/GBP virtual ledgers, but the following legal constraints apply: (i) funds held with an EMI are not legal tender or protected deposits in New Zealand; (ii) EMIs generally decline tax-exempt offshore international companies without a substantive OECD nexus; (iii) sub-ledger positions at an EMI must be disclosed to the principal clearing bank, which will treat them as counterparty credit exposure; and (iv) direct consumer deposits into a NZ account would constitute unlawful deposit-taking outside the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. Therefore, the CK_IC treasury architecture must use a Tier-1 New Zealand bank as the settlement anchor and EMIs only for niche purpose-specific rails such as rapid creator payouts in Southeast Asian markets.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% statutory NRWT on dividends paid by a New Zealand-resident company to non-resident shareholders. The rate may be reduced to 15% for fully imputed dividends under domestic rules and further reduced under applicable DTAs, e.g. 15% under the OECD/NZ standard treaty, 5% where the recipient is a company holding at least 10% of the voting power, and 0% in certain jurisdictions for 80%+ corporate owners.
