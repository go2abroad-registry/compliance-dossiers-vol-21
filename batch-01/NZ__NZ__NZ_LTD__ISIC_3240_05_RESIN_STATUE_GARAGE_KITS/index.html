Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue; Te Tari Taake), the national tax authority of New Zealand. Statutory functions and filing are administered through the official portal www.ird.govt.nz, with all tax registrations, GST returns, and secure communications effected through the myIR (myIR Secure Online Services) platform. Company incorporation and the issue of the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) are administered by the New Zealand Companies Office under the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE).

E-government filing portal

1) The NZ_LTD is incorporated under the Companies Act 1993 via the Companies Office online register; the incorporation application automatically issues the NZBN and typically simultaneously registers the company for an IRD number and GST where the taxable-activity declarations are completed. 2) If the company has already been incorporated or was formed without tax registrations, the procuring officer must file IRD Form IR596 (Company IRD Number Application) through the myIR portal; the IRD number is generally issued within 5-10 business days, with an IRD number confirmation letter delivered to the registered addresses. 3) GST registration is compulsory once the value of taxable supplies exceeds NZ$60,000 in any 12-month period; registration is executed via the myIR portal (or the Companies Office new-company workflow) using Form IR356, with the effective GST registration date aligned to the start of the taxable activity or the date of incorporation if the company elects voluntary registration. 4) For a foreign company operating in New Zealand through a branch, the entity must first apply for registration as an overseas company with the Companies Office under Part 18 of the Companies Act 1993 and then obtain an IRD number by filing Form IR596 with certified incorporation documents, registered-office particulars, and a New Zealand service address. For a NZ-incorporated limited company with non-resident shareholders, the company is automatically a tax resident of New Zealand and is not subject to residency elections at incorporation; all statutory tax filings must be made in the IRD number of the NZ_LTD.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — The NZ Limited Company is a separate legal and taxable person. Under s YA 2 of the Income Tax Act 2007, a company incorporated in New Zealand is tax resident in New Zealand and is taxed at the 28% corporate rate on its worldwide income. There is no territorial limitation for resident companies; foreign income is taxable in New Zealand with foreign tax credits available under the foreign tax credit rules, and the full imputation credit system applies to resident and non-resident shareholders to prevent economic double taxation. New Zealand operates a unitary national tax system without state or provincial income taxes. The standard entity-level model is not a pass-through; however, eligible closely held companies may elect Look-Through Company treatment as described below.

Available election forms

1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007: an eligible closely held company (maximum 5 qualifying shareholders, each a natural person, eligible trust or another LTC) may elect full pass-through taxation of its income, expenses, losses, and tax credits to shareholders; the election is binding for 5 years and requires shareholder consent on each qualifying share, without prejudice to GST registration which remains at company level. 2) Non-Resident Company election under s HB 1: a company incorporated in New Zealand that is managed and controlled outside New Zealand may elect to be treated as a non-resident company for income tax purposes, so that only income with a New Zealand source is subject to tax in New Zealand. 3) Hybrid mismatch rules (subparts FA-FH of the Income Tax Act 2007) are mandatory and non-elective; New Zealand companies involved in structured hybrid arrangements must apply neutralizing adjustments to deny deductions or include income in line with BEPS Action 2. 4) GST Grouping election (s 55, Goods and Services Tax Act 1985) and GST Joint Venture election (s 57) allow the NZ_LTD and associated companies to be treated as single GST-registered persons for input tax and output tax purposes. 5) Dividend imputation and non-resident withholding tax elections, including the foreign dividend exemption and available subscribed capital elections, apply where the NZ_LTD has non-resident shareholders receiving distributions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZ$60,000 — compulsory GST registration applies to every person carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand where the value of taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any 12-month period exceeds NZ$60,000 (excluding GST). The NZ_LTD is required to register from commencement of its taxable activity once this turnover threshold is reached; voluntary registration below the threshold is permitted and is often elected where input tax credits on establishment costs are material.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under s 11(1) of the GST Act 1985, the supply of goods is zero-rated where the supplier has exported the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the date of supply. For the NZ_LTD's polyresin vacuum-cast and hand-painted limited-edition statues, zero-rating requires that the statues are delivered to a non-resident purchaser or to the purchaser's overseas designee and physically leave New Zealand within the 28-day window; legal title and risk must pass to the overseas buyer under the agreed Incoterms. The supply is not zero-rated if the goods remain in New Zealand, are not exported within 28 days, or are consumed in New Zealand. Exported statue supplies must be recorded as zero-rated in the GST return, and the underlying purchase order (received via Preorder_Deposit_Stripe or PayPal), the export invoice, and the carrier's evidence must demonstrate the overseas destination of each physical consignment. Invoices may be issued in foreign currency for export transactions; for GST return purposes the foreign-currency amount is converted to New Zealand dollars using the IRD's prescribed exchange rate. Services supplied to non-residents outside New Zealand at the time of supply are also zero-rated under s 11A, subject to exceptions for services connected with land, goods, or taxable activities physically performed in New Zealand. The use of Stripe or PayPal payment rails does not by itself alter the export zero-rating analysis, but complete payment and shipping records are essential to satisfy audit evidence requirements.

  • NZ Customs Export Declaration (electronic lodgment via the Trade Single Window, message set IE515)
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing shipment from a New Zealand port or airport
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank foreign-exchange settlement advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds (New Zealand has no statutory exchange-control mandate; this evidence serves as the export-payment verification)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (overseas consignee address, Incoterms, description of the resin statues and unit values)
  • Proof of physical export within 28 days of supply (port of loading certificate, container tracking report, sailing or flight departure confirmation, and Customs clearance certificate)
  • GST zero-rated export schedule and reconciliation supporting the relevant period filed in myIR

There is no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no compulsory digital stamping, and no tax-authority clearance-before-issue model in New Zealand. The IRD operates a self-assessment compliance system with periodic GST returns filed through myIR (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly accounting periods). Electronic invoicing (e-invoicing) is voluntary in the private sector, with the Government promoting the PEPPOL e-invoicing standard for B2B and B2G interoperability; invoices to New Zealand government departments must be transmitted via the Peppol network, but non-government trade can lawfully use paper, PDF, or any other format that satisfies the content requirements of s 24 of the GST Act 1985 (supplier name and GST number, invoice date and number, customer name and address, description of the goods, GST-exclusive amount, GST amount and total consideration). The New Zealand Customs Service requires electronic export declarations in the Trade Single Window portal, which serves as the operational clearance mechanism for exported resin statue goods; GST refunds on export supplies are effected through the ordinary GST return system with IRD payment of net refunds typically made within 10-15 working days.

The zero-rating status is retrospective: if goods are not physically exported within 28 days of supply, or are diverted to the New Zealand market, the supplier must return GST at 15% on the supply price and file an adjustment in the relevant GST period. Wrongful zero-rating triggers the shortfall penalty provisions of Part 9 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, together with use-of-money interest: 20% of the tax shortfall for failure to exercise reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion, with criminal liability for tax evasion arising under s 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994 and s 260 of the Crimes Act 1961. The IRD and the New Zealand Customs Service operate joint data-matching of export declarations, freight forwarder records, bill-of-lading data, and banking and payment settlement flows; fully documented and auditable export dossiers are required. Retrospective adjustments and GST clawbacks may be assessed within the 4-year limitation period of s 108 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, with no time limit where evasion or fraudulent intent is established. The 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline reflected in this schema is not a statutory requirement in New Zealand, as the country's exchange controls were abolished in 1984; however, prolonged non-receipt of export proceeds beyond 180 days may be treated as evidence that the export transaction was not genuine and may lead to reassessment of the zero-rating, with the anti-avoidance rule in s 76 of the GST Act 1985 authorizing the Commissioner of Inland Revenue to adjust the GST consequences of any arrangement that defeats the object and purpose of the zero-rating rules.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully open capital account and a freely convertible New Zealand dollar under the IMF Article VIII obligations. Both current account and capital account transactions are unrestricted, with no foreign exchange controls, no requirement for central bank approval or no permitted threshold on cross-border capital flows. Profit remittance via dividends, management fees, royalty and intercompany loan repayments to non-resident shareholders is permitted, subject to standard tax withholding at source (e.g. Non-Resident Withholding Tax on dividends and Approved Issuer Levy on interest) and to transfer pricing and beneficial ownership documentation. The practical hurdles are not exchange control but rather: (i) bank-level KYC/AML diligence on the origin of inbound capital, (ii) the application of the overseas investment screening rules if a substantial asset or shareholding is acquired, and (iii) the statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations on banks. There is no separate profit remittance licence or fee.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand does not impose mandatory export proceeds surrender, conversion ratios, or any statutory timeline for repatriation of export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in offshore or onshore foreign currency accounts, settle in any currency agreed with the buyer, and convert at quotable market rates. The only ancillary compliance obligations are (i) ensuring the underlying export transaction is accurately declared to the New Zealand Customs Service for statistical and tariff purposes, (ii) reflecting the transaction in the exporter’s financial statements and tax returns where applicable, and (iii) preserving underlying commercial invoices, logistics documents and settlement records to support bank KYC/AML checks and any future queries from the Reserve Bank or the New Zealand Customs Service.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) as the central monetary and prudential authority; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the Department of Internal Affairs as co-supervisors of the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AMLCFT); the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) under the Ministry for Business, Innovation and Employment as the administrator of New Zealand’s overseas investment screening regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium. New Zealand does not have a standalone electronic money institution (EMI) licence. A non-bank fintech may provide payment services and hold settlement funds only if it is registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and, where applicable, holds a money service business registration that is subject to AMLCFT oversight. Additionally, if the fintech accepts deposits or lends, it must be registered as a bank under the Banking Prudential Supervision Act 1989. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g. Wise Platform, Airwallex, Currencycloud-style aggregators) can provide NZD-denominated virtual accounts, payment disbursements and collection services via a white-label partnership with a licensed New Zealand bank, but they cannot accept deposits or act as the principal holding bank for a commercial entity without a locally registered NZ entity and a local banking partner. Therefore, for the NZ_LTD treasury, an offshore digital EMI is viable as an auxiliary cross-border payment and disbursement rail, but the primary liquidity clearing and escrow relationship must sit with a licensed Tier 1 commercial bank to ensure transactional integrity, credit support and regulatory coverage.

Offshore digital EMIs (e.g. Wise Platform, Airwallex, Currencycloud-style aggregators) can provide NZD-denominated virtual accounts, payment disbursements and collection services via a white-label partnership with a licensed New Zealand bank, but they cannot accept deposits or act as the principal holding bank for a commercial entity without a locally registered NZ entity and a local banking partner. Therefore, for the NZ_LTD treasury, an offshore digital EMI is viable as an auxiliary cross-border payment and disbursement rail, but the primary liquidity clearing and escrow relationship must sit with a licensed Tier 1 commercial bank to ensure transactional integrity, credit support and regulatory coverage.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing documentation (equivalent to Local File and Master File) is required when the taxpayer belongs to a multinational group with global group revenue exceeding NZ$1.2 billion (approx. EUR 750 million) AND cross-border related-party transactions in NZ aggregate NZ$10 million or more in an income year. Taxpayers below this threshold are still expected to maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing evidence under s GC 13 and may be required to produce it within 30 days on IRD notice.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not impose an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime analogous to offshore financial centres. However, general anti-avoidance (s BG 1), thin capitalisation (s FE 1), and loss continuity rules (s IA 5) effectively require real economic substance. A NZ shell company with no employees, office, or decision-making, interposed to repatriate profits under DTA rates or to access NZ imputation credits, is vulnerable to IRD reconstruction, loss disallowance and penalty taxes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty under TAA s 141: 150% of the tax shortfall for evasion (100% for abusive tax position). Criminal prosecution under TAA s 143B: fine up to NZ$250,000 for a company (NZ$50,000 for an individual) and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. Use-of-money interest at 8.35% p.a. compounds daily on all unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be made personally liable under TAA s 149 for unpaid tax if they knowingly caused the evasion; director liability also arises under Companies Act 1993 s 135 (reckless trading) and s 136 (insolvent trading). The Commissioner may issue a departure prohibition order under TAA s 157, preventing a director from leaving New Zealand until tax debts are settled.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: General anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1) allows the Commissioner to reconstruct the arrangement and deny the tax benefit; loss carryforward denied under s IA 5; abusive tax position penalty of 100% of the shortfall. The company may be struck off under the Companies Act 1993 for failure to maintain a registered office or agent; banks and payment processors (Stripe/PayPal) may terminate accounts upon integrity review.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign falsely misleading returns commit an offence under TAA s 143A (knowingly providing false information) - fines up to NZ$50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. Directors may also incur civil liability for insolvent trading under Companies Act 1993 s 135/136 and personal liability for GST or PAYE collected but not remitted under TAA s 149.
AML / CFT and Payment Processor Integrity

Statutory sanction: Although the resin statue seller is not itself a reporting entity under the New Zealand AML/CFT Act 2009, knowingly dealing with the proceeds of crime constitutes money laundering under the Crimes Act 1961 s 243: imprisonment up to 7 years for individuals and fines for bodies corporate. The Crown may restrain and forfeit assets under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009, and payment processors may permanently blacklist the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors can be prosecuted for money laundering if they knew the funds were criminal proceeds, and personal civil penalties may arise under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2014 if preorders are materially misrepresented. Directors may also face travel bans, departure prohibition orders, and in serious cases simultaneous tax evasion and money laundering charges with cumulative imprisonment.
