A virtual office is permissible as a registered office under the Companies Act 1993 only if it is a physical New Zealand street address at which a person authorized by the company is available to accept service of process and official documents; a post office box is not acceptable. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue Te Tari Tāke), Wellington, New Zealand; official digital portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned New Zealand limited company must obtain an IRD number (tax file number). Where the company is incorporated through the New Zealand Companies Office and indicates a NZ income tax or GST obligation at incorporation, an IRD number is issued automatically; otherwise the company or its non-resident agent must apply via myIR or paper form IR742 (Non-Resident Company Registration), submitting the certificate of incorporation, constitution, particulars of directors and resident agent, and beneficial ownership documentation. The IRD number is generally issued within 5–10 business days. If any 12-month turnover exceeds NZD 60,000 or payroll is paid, the company must simultaneously register for GST and PAYE. A foreign company carrying on business through a branch in New Zealand may also be issued an IRD number under the registered foreign-company regime.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a New Zealand resident limited company is taxed on its worldwide income at the corporate rate; no territorial election applies; pass-through treatment is unavailable unless a valid Look-Through Company election is made; no tax-exempt zone treatment is available within New Zealand

Available election forms

Eligible close companies with 5 or fewer shareholders and no corporate shareholder may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) treatment under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007; corporate groups may elect consolidated group treatment under subpart IG; qualifying companies may elect dividend imputation credit account status; hybrid mismatch rules apply under New Zealand BEPS measures, but no US-style check-the-box election is available; a branch exemption election applies only to certain foreign attributed income under s DN 2

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 — GST registration is compulsory when taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period. Non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value imported goods, including Etsy marketplace sellers and Stripe-processed supplies to New Zealand consumers, must register under the offshore-supplier GST rules when NZ-supplied goods and services exceed NZD 60,000 per annum.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST-registered New Zealand supplier zero-rates exported goods under s 11(1)(a) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the goods are exported by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf within 28 days of supply. Zero-rating also applies under s 11 to services performed outside New Zealand or supplied to a non-resident for use outside New Zealand, and to deemed exports, provided the supplier holds documentary evidence of physical export or foreign use. Die-struck brass cloisonné enamel lapel pins sold through Etsy to a non-resident customer and shipped to an overseas address qualify for zero-rating, subject to production of export evidence such as carrier stamping or track-and-trace confirmation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Export Declaration / ICS Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Exchange Receipt / bank transfer credit advice evidencing settlement in foreign currency from the non-resident purchaser
  • Commercial invoice with HS/ISIC classification and unit values
  • Packing list and shipping manifest
  • Carrier stamping confirmation / track-and-trace export proof from NZ Post, FedEx, DHL or other freight forwarder
  • Contract or purchase order evidencing overseas sale terms (EXW, FOB, CIF)

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based transaction stamping, pre-clearance e-invoicing mandate, or digital stamping regime exists in New Zealand. GST returns are filed electronically via myIR using forms GST-101, GST-102B, or GST-103. New Zealand has adopted the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 framework as the national e-invoicing standard, and e-invoicing is mandatory for suppliers transacting with New Zealand government agencies. Inland Revenue provides open application programming interfaces (APIs) for integrated GST return lodgement, but there is no continuous transaction control (CTC) or clearance regime requiring government pre-approval of invoices.

Inland Revenue audits zero-rated export claims and may re-characterise them as taxable supplies if the statutory documentary evidence bundle is incomplete or the goods or services are not in fact exported. Consequences include retrospective GST reassessment, clawback of credits and refunds already paid, shortfall penalties under s 141 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 (25% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and up to 100% for evasion), use-of-money interest on unpaid or over-claimed amounts, and, in cases of serial export fraud, criminal prosecution and cancellation of GST registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a freely floating and fully convertible currency regime on both the current account and the capital account. There are no exchange control approvals, no mandatory conversion ratios, no profit-remittance permits, and no restrictions on the repatriation of dividends, branch profits, royalties, interest, or share capital to non-resident shareholders. The only statutory gateway constraints are: (i) the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which screens 'sensitive' land and 'significant business assets' transactions (inapplicable to ordinary trading of hard enamel lapel pins); and (ii) the Inland Revenue (IRD) non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) and thin capitalisation compliance for outward remittances. In practice, the de facto prior restraint on convertibility is not government control but bank-level AML/CFT and sanctions screening. Current-account transactions (export receipts from Etsy and Stripe) are wholly liberalised. Profit remittance hurdles are effectively zero for a fully compliant NZ limited company with a clean tax clearance certificate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand abolished all statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements, compulsory conversion timelines, and exchange-control repatriation quotas on the floating of the NZD in December 1984. A New Zealand exporter may lawfully hold 100% of its export receipts in foreign currency (USD, AUD, EUR) or convert them to NZD at any time. The only residual compliance obligations are: (i) correct recognition of export revenue in the financial statements in accordance with NZ IFRS 15; (ii) foreign-currency translation and taxation of gains/losses under the Financial Arrangements Rules (Income Tax Act 2007, subpart EW); and (iii) transaction-reporting thresholds under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (wire transfers at or above NZD 1,000 in certain circumstances).

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) in conjunction with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA), the Ministry of Business, Innovation & Employment (MBIE), and the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) — Exchange control authority administered under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989 and the Overseas Investment Act 2005

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH as an auxiliary payment-settlement channel for e-commerce receipts (Etsy Direct Pay, Stripe, Shopify Payments), but MEDIUM as the sole primary operating account. Legal constraints: Offshore digital EMI providers holding only a foreign e-money licence (e.g., FCA-authorised in the UK or MAS-authorised in Singapore) are not registered banks in New Zealand under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989 and cannot take deposits in the course of carrying on business in NZ unless admitted to the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and holding a Money Services Business (MSB) registration with the Department of Internal Affairs. The Crown's 'depositor compensation scheme' protection only applies to licensed banks. To maintain tax and corporate integrity with the Inland Revenue, the Companies Office, and the FSPR, the entity should always hold at least one fallback operating account at a New Zealand-incorporated registered bank for IRD payments, company registration, and statutory-seizure compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing of an inbound equity injection from a foreign shareholder requires a SWIFT MT103 wire to the entity's Capital Escrow Account at a Tier-1 New Zealand bank. The beneficiary account must be designated as 'Capital Subscription Account' and the payment details field must state: 'Share capital contribution' together with the shareholder's name and the corresponding Companies Office shareholder resolution authorising the issue or allotment of shares. Although New Zealand has no statutory purpose-code regime, banks use a de facto classification matrix: (i) code 'K' for share capital; (ii) code 'L' for shareholder loans; and (iii) code 'C' for trade/current payments. For the bank's automated monitoring, the remitter should include the ISO 20022 purpose code 'CDQC' or 'CNCB' (capital contribution). The transaction must be cross-referenced with the Companies Office share allotment filing, the IRD shareholder return, and if the parent is non-resident, the bank will typically require a board resolution and an exemption from, or acknowledgment of, non-resident withholding tax on any subsequent dividends. For amounts exceeding NZD 50,000 equivalent, the bank may ask for an evidence pack including the source of funds statement and the incorporation documents of the remitting entity.Outbound trade disbursements for the purchase of raw materials (metal blanks, cloisonné enamels, packaging) and for refunds to Etsy/Stripe buyers should be cleared via: (i) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer for all standard transactions, especially where the supplier holds a foreign bank account; (ii) Commercial Documentary Letter of Credit (MT700 series) for purchase orders above NZD 50,000 requiring transport documents, a certificate of origin, and a clean bill of lading; (iii) NZD domestic interbank clearings through the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) for local suppliers; and (iv) the establishment of multi-currency accounts (NZD, USD, AUD, EUR) at the domestic bank to avoid double conversion of Stripe/Etsy receipts. For USD outgoing wires, the NZ bank will route through Fedwire or CHIPS via its US correspondent network. Import settlement for a NZD/USD forward contract should make use of the bank's FX conversion at the forward rate, followed by the MT103, ensuring the FX reference is included in the underlying payment instruction.Outright deliverable FX forward contracts (NZD/USD, NZD/AUD, NZD/EUR) with tenors from 1 week to 24 months for hedging large trade orders from Etsy/Stripe payoutsFX swaps (tomorrow-next and spot-forward combinations) to convert USD-proceeds cash flows without impacting liquidityCross-currency basis swaps for matching a foreign-currency receivable asset against a foreign-currency payable, where the NZ entity has both USD and AUD cash flowsVanilla FX options (call or put on NZD, with USD, AUD, or EUR as the counter-leg) for flexible hedging of uncertain high-volume seasonal salesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are legally permitted in New Zealand but commercially unnecessary for the NZD, which is fully deliverable and has a liquid onshore forward market; NDFs may be used only where the underlying settlement currency belongs to a jurisdiction with exchange controls that preclude physical delivery

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH as an auxiliary payment-settlement channel for e-commerce receipts (Etsy Direct Pay, Stripe, Shopify Payments), but MEDIUM as the sole primary operating account. Legal constraints: Offshore digital EMI providers holding only a foreign e-money licence (e.g., FCA-authorised in the UK or MAS-authorised in Singapore) are not registered banks in New Zealand under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989 and cannot take deposits in the course of carrying on business in NZ unless admitted to the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and holding a Money Services Business (MSB) registration with the Department of Internal Affairs. The Crown's 'depositor compensation scheme' protection only applies to licensed banks. To maintain tax and corporate integrity with the Inland Revenue, the Companies Office, and the FSPR, the entity should always hold at least one fallback operating account at a New Zealand-incorporated registered bank for IRD payments, company registration, and statutory-seizure compliance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions triggers the expectation of contemporaneous Local File-style transfer pricing documentation; multinational groups with New Zealand operations and consolidated global revenue exceeding EUR 750 million (approximately NZD 1.3 billion) must additionally prepare Master File and Country-by-Country Report.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have separate Economic Substance Regulations as in offshore financial centres, but substance is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule (Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1), the voidable arrangement provisions (s GB 1), thin capitalisation rules, non-resident controlled company rules, and the requirement that any tax-residency, transfer-pricing, or head-office cost-allocation claim be supported by actual decision-making, employees, premises, contracts, and commercial rationale in New Zealand.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Conviction under Tax Administration Act 1994 s 143A or s 143B may attract a fine up to NZD 50,000 for first offence and up to NZD 100,000 for subsequent offences, plus imprisonment for a term up to 5 years; the company may also be ordered to pay the full tax shortfall plus shortfall penalties up to 150%.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally participated in or directed the evasion may be prosecuted as parties to the offence, face the same fines and imprisonment, and may be barred from managing a company under the Companies Act 1993; civil restitution of diverted corporate funds may also be ordered.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: The Registrar of Companies may initiate compulsory strike-off under the Companies Act 1993, deregister the entity, and forfeit its assets to the Crown; the company's income may be recharacterised by Inland Revenue as derived by a shadow associate or the controller, with penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for debts and tax if they allow the company to trade while insolvent, fail to keep proper accounting records, or permit the company to be used to defeat tax; the High Court may impose a management ban of up to 10 years and order compensation for creditors.
AML/CFT

Statutory sanction: Austrac-style penalties are not applicable; under the AML/CFT Act 2009, non-compliance with due diligence, suspicious transaction reporting, or prescribed transaction reports may attract civil fines of up to NZD 200,000 for individuals and NZD 2 million for companies per contravention, plus criminal sanctions for knowingly false statements.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and directors can be personally fined and may face criminal liability for willful non-compliance; a director who facilitates money laundering through the company's accounts commits an offence under the Crimes Act 1961 and may be imprisoned for up to 7 years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Arm's-Length Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may adjust the taxable income to an arm's-length amount under Income Tax Act 2007 ss GC 13-GC 15, impose a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the resulting tax shortfall, and apply late payment interest; the tax shortfall itself remains payable without restructure.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation or knowingly allow uncommercial related party pricing may incur personal liability for penalties, and if the arrangement is found to be tax avoidance, the anti-avoidance provisions can reach the controlling shareholder personally, including piercing the corporate veil for restitution, asset freezing orders, and travel bans pending criminal investigation.
