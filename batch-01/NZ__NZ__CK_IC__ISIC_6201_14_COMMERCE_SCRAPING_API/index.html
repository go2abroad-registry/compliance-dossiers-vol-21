Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake), New Zealand Government — official national tax authority and portal: https://www.ird.govt.nz (myIR Secure portal for registrations, GST returns and taxpayer profiles). Customs-side export controls are administered by the New Zealand Customs Service (https://www.customs.govt.nz), which supports GST export documentation and border clearance.

E-government filing portal

The statutory route for a foreign-incorporated International Company (CK_IC) to obtain a tax identity number is: (1) Inland Revenue issues an IRD number (non-resident company tax file number) on application using form IR596, supported by the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, board/shareholder resolutions, director identity documents, and proof of foreign residence or tax residence; (2) once the IRD number is received, the entity activates a myIR Secure account and applies for Goods and Services Tax (GST) registration through the myIR portal (form GST101A) either mandatorily, if the 60,000 NZD threshold is exceeded, or voluntarily; (3) where the company is incorporated under the Companies Act 1993 in New Zealand, registration with the Companies Office automatically transmits particulars to Inland Revenue, which assigns an IRD number and New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) without further application; (4) a CK_IC with no New Zealand fixed establishment, no New Zealand-resident management and no New Zealand-sourced income is not required to file New Zealand income tax returns, but is required to hold an IRD number and to file GST returns where New Zealand GST registration applies; (5) all registrations, returns and payments are made electronically through myIR or approved tax-agent software.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The CK_IC International Company is classified for New Zealand tax purposes as a non-resident company operating entirely offshore; it has no New Zealand source revenue and no permanent establishment in New Zealand, therefore the 28% New Zealand corporate income tax does not apply. The New Zealand income tax system is not a territorial system for resident companies (residents are taxed on worldwide income), so the Tax_Exempt_Zone classification is conditional on non-residence under the place-of-incorporation and central-management-and-control test, together with the absence of New Zealand source income. Digital API and web-scraping subscription fees sourced from non-resident customers are not income derived from New Zealand and fall outside New Zealand income tax.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not maintain a check-the-box election for corporations. The applicable statutory elections and options for this entity are limited to: (1) voluntary GST registration under section 51 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for suppliers below the 60,000 NZD threshold; (2) GST group registration under section 55 of the GST Act 1985 aggregating related companies into one GST registered group; (3) simplified non-resident supplier GST registration for remote digital services supplied into New Zealand consumers; (4) optional taxable-supply-information methods in the form of e-invoices that satisfy GST invoice requirements; (5) in income tax, New Zealand has no elective pass-through classification for International Companies — look-through company elections under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 are reserved exclusively for closely held resident companies and are unavailable to a non-resident International Company; (6) no hybrid-mismatch or entity classification election is available for this entity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates as an IMF Article 8 jurisdiction with fully liberalised current and capital accounts. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible in the global interbank market with no exchange controls, no prior-approval mechanism, and no documentation requirements for cross-border settlement. Capital-account transactions — including the injection of equity into the CK_IC International Company, intercompany loans, and acquisition of New Zealand digital-asset infrastructure — are lawful without RBNZ approval except where the Overseas Investment Act 2005 notification threshold applies to sensitive land, fishing quota, or 25%+ control of significant business assets. Current-account items (API subscription fees, scraping-software royalties, data-gateway disbursements, and profit remittances) may be freely converted into any currency. The only substantive hurdles to profit remittance are tax-based rather than exchange-based: (i) non-resident withholding tax on dividends (15% under applicable DTA, 30% otherwise); (ii) Approved Issuer Levy for eligible interest; (iii) transfer-pricing rules under Part 8 of the Income Tax Act 2007; and (iv) thin-capitalisation interest-limitation rules that constrain debt-funded repatriation. Payments in or relating to USDC are convertible to NZD provided the conversion is executed via an FMA-supervised exchange and the receiving bank is satisfied the arrangement does not operate as an unregistered designated settlement system under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2022.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no export proceeds surrender or repatriation obligation in New Zealand. The statutory regime does not impose a timeline for bringing export receipts onshore, nor does it mandate any conversion ratio for foreign currency earned from ISIC 6201 digital-commerce activities. The CK_IC may retain USD, EUR, GBP, and USDC indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts at an RBNZ-registered bank or within segregated licensed-custody wallets, subject only to New Zealand tax valuation: the company must recognise foreign-currency balances at fair value under NZ IFRS 9 and account for any FX gains under the Income Tax Act 2007 (reporting on an accruals basis unless a functional-currency exemption has been obtained from the IRD). The reporting constraints that replace surrender rules are: (a) notifiable transaction reports to the New Zealand Police Financial Intelligence Unit for cash or digital-asset transactions above NZD 10,000; (b) bank international-transaction reporting to Statistics New Zealand for any receipt above NZD 100,000; and (c) IRD disclosure of offshore direct-investment balances above NZD 1 million. Where the exporter chooses to convert, settlement is executed through a licensed FX dealer at prevailing interbank rates without approval.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the central bank and prudential supervisor of all registered banks; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) exercises co-supervision over non-bank financial institutions and AML/CFT reporting entities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for ancillary treasury agility, but legally constrained for primary banking. New Zealand permits foreign digital EMIs (Airwallex, Payoneer, Wise Platform, and similar providers) to serve the CK_IC through AML/CFT-registered financial service providers supervised by the Financial Markets Authority. Such EMIs provide multi-currency virtual IBANs, SWIFT receiving rails, mass-payout APIs, and instantly convertible USD/NZD/GBP/EUR pockets, making them strong candidates for Stripe-Aggregator payouts and low-value supplier disbursements. They are not registered banks and cannot take deposits, issue credit, or access the RBNZ ESAS settlement system without becoming clearing participants, so an RBNZ-registered bank must remain the settlement anchor. Specific legal constraints: (a) the EMI must hold an FMA registration number and appear on the Financial Service Providers Register; (b) an offshore EMI that is not NZ-registered can service the IC only for foreign-currency accounts, not NZD bank accounts; (c) crypto-to-fiat conversion inside an EMI may be rejected under bank de-risking policies, requiring use of a licensed NZ crypto/stablecoin exchange as an intermediate step; and (d) cross-border EMIs are subject to the Financial Market Infrastructures Act 2021 designation powers should their transaction volumes exceed systemic thresholds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High for ancillary treasury agility, but legally constrained for primary banking. New Zealand permits foreign digital EMIs (Airwallex, Payoneer, Wise Platform, and similar providers) to serve the CK_IC through AML/CFT-registered financial service providers supervised by the Financial Markets Authority. Such EMIs provide multi-currency virtual IBANs, SWIFT receiving rails, mass-payout APIs, and instantly convertible USD/NZD/GBP/EUR pockets, making them strong candidates for Stripe-Aggregator payouts and low-value supplier disbursements. They are not registered banks and cannot take deposits, issue credit, or access the RBNZ ESAS settlement system without becoming clearing participants, so an RBNZ-registered bank must remain the settlement anchor. Specific legal constraints: (a) the EMI must hold an FMA registration number and appear on the Financial Service Providers Register; (b) an offshore EMI that is not NZ-registered can service the IC only for foreign-currency accounts, not NZD bank accounts; (c) crypto-to-fiat conversion inside an EMI may be rejected under bank de-risking policies, requiring use of a licensed NZ crypto/stablecoin exchange as an intermediate step; and (d) cross-border EMIs are subject to the Financial Market Infrastructures Act 2021 designation powers should their transaction volumes exceed systemic thresholds.
