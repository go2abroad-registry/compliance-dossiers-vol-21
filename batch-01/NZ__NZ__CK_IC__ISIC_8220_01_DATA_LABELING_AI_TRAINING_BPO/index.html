Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office (Registrar of Companies), administered within the Service Delivery Branch of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE); electronic lodgement via the Online Services portal at www.companiesoffice.govt.nz, integrated with the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) register.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No. 105), as amended by the Companies Amendment Act 2014; core sections: ss 10-11 (execution of documents and electronic signatures), ss 12-13 (mode of incorporation), s 16 (name), s 17 (certificate of incorporation), ss 33-40 (names and 'limited' suffix), ss 42-45 (registered office), ss 46-60 (registry obligations), ss 128-141 (shareholders and board powers), ss 151-161 (directors' duties), ss 161-180 (officers), ss 189-200 (accounting records), ss 311-320 (offences and compliance). Supplementary: Financial Reporting Act 2013, Income Tax Act 2007, and the Companies (Fees) Regulations 2012. The 'International Company' designation is a contractual and administrative label customarily used for a company limited by shares carrying on an offshore business scope; there is no separate International Company statute in New Zealand.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NZ1 (Incorporation Application), lodged electronically through the Companies Office Online 'Incorporate a Company' workflow; the same filing generates the NZBN registration record.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 10 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow requirement. The Company may be incorporated with fully or partly paid shares issued in any currency, including a single ordinary share of NZD 1.00 subscribed on incorporation. The Companies Office does not verify capital contribution and no proof-of-funds filing is required at formation; capital contributions are recorded in the share register and financial statements.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) is satisfied by obtaining a building warrant of fitness (BWOF) from the territorial authority for the leased office, including an annual fire-protection systems inspection under the Building (Specified Systems, Change the Use, and Earthquake-prone Buildings) Regulations 2005. Structural load certifications are required at lease-fit-out stage for any raised-floor or server-rack installation, with a chartered structural engineer's PS1/PS4 certificate. WorkSafe New Zealand clearance is obtained through the adoption of the Health and Safety at Work (General Risk and Workplace Management) Regulations 2016, a signed workplace health and safety policy, first-aid and ergonomic compliance, and an electrical wiring certificate under the Electricity (Safety) Regulations 2010.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Tāake), Wellington, New Zealand; official taxpayer self-service portal: myIR at https://myir.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

For the CK_IC (International Company, non-resident owned, tax classification model Tax_Exempt_Zone), the foreign entity must first obtain a New Zealand IRD number — the single consolidated statutory taxpayer identifier used for income tax, GST, PAYE, and other tax lines. The non-resident company lodges Form IR742 (IRD Number Application – Non-Resident Company) with Inland Revenue, attaching certified certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered-office evidence, and a disclosure of any New Zealand business activities or fixed establishment. Upon issuance of the IRD number, GST registration (where required) is completed electronically via myIR using Form IR372 (GST Registration – Non-Individuals); the GST registration is reflected as a suffix on the same IRD number and an effective date is fixed by the Commissioner. If the CK_IC is a non-resident supplier with no New Zealand fixed establishment, it must nonetheless register for GST when its supplies of remote services to New Zealand consumers exceed NZ$60,000 in a 12-month period; all registration notifications, approval notices, and banking details must be retained as permanent compliance records.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the CK_IC International Company is structured as an offshore tax-exempt entity; under New Zealand's territorial tax framework, New Zealand income tax is limited to New Zealand-sourced income, and offshore data-labeling and AI-training service income will fall outside the New Zealand tax base unless the entity maintains a fixed establishment or carries on substantial business activities in New Zealand.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in New Zealand. Available statutory elections include: (i) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, permitting eligible closely held New Zealand companies to be fiscally transparent; (ii) GST elections for cash basis, payments basis, and the zero-rating of land supplies under section 11(1)(mb) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; (iii) the branch equivalent tax account (BETA) streaming election for non-resident companies operating through a New Zealand branch; and (iv) ordering and interpretive elections relating to the hybrid mismatch rules in Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007, which operate as mechanical anti-avoidance disallowances rather than elective tax regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZ$60,000 (registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed NZ$60,000 in any rolling 12-month period or are reasonably expected to exceed that amount within 12 months; non-resident suppliers of remote or digital services to New Zealand consumers apply the same NZ$60,000 threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST is available for (i) exported goods supplied and physically exported from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply, supported by customs evidence; and (ii) services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the services are not performed in direct connection with land or tangible personal property situated in New Zealand. For the ISIC 8220 AI data-labeling, computer-vision annotation, and RLHF curriculum-data services supplied by the CK_IC to offshore clients, the supply is zero-rated when the overseas client is outside New Zealand at the time the service is performed or supplied and the service does not relate to New Zealand land or New Zealand-situate goods. If the recipient is GST-registered in New Zealand or the supply is part of a New Zealand taxable activity, standard 15% GST applies unless another zero-rating provision is engaged.

  • New Zealand Customs export declaration (Trade Single Window / TECC submission) evidencing export clearance
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, as applicable to the delivery mode
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or equivalent bank wire/foreign-exchange settlement confirmation evidencing receipt of payment from the overseas buyer
  • Executed master services agreement and purchase orders describing the AI annotation and RLHF data-curation deliverables
  • GST tax invoice marked 'Zero-Rated' showing supplier and recipient names, respective GST/IRD numbers, and the statutory zero-rating basis

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital tax-stamping, or API-based invoice validation mandate applies in New Zealand. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Peppol network (eInvoicingNZ). A GST-registered business may issue either standard tax invoices or buyer-created tax invoices without prior government approval; invoices must satisfy the statutory content requirements of section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, including supplier and recipient identification, GST registration numbers, date of issue, description of goods or services, and GST amount or a clear 'zero-rated' notation where applicable.

The Commissioner of Inland Revenue may, under the Tax Administration Act 1994, reassess and claw back zero-rated supplies within four years from the end of the relevant assessment/return period, with no time limit in cases of fraud, evasion, or other dishonest conduct. Upon an adverse audit outcome, output tax is re-assessed at 15% GST on the full supply value, with late payment penalties assessed at 1% immediately after the due date, an additional 4% within the first month, and incremental monthly penalties thereafter up to a statutory cumulative cap of 25% of the unpaid tax. Shortfall penalties apply separately: 20% for failing to take reasonable care, 20% for an unacceptable tax position (increasing to 40% for significant shortfalls), 100% for an abusive tax position, and up to 150% for evasion. Use-of-money interest runs from the original due date to the date of full payment. Deficient or missing export documentation creates a rebuttable presumption that the supply is domestic, triggers retrospective reversal of GST refunds previously credited, and disqualifies the taxpayer from future zero-rating until documentary systems are remediated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully convertible currency under a free-floating exchange rate regime. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions, with no exchange control restrictions imposed by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 or the Overseas Investment Act 1973 (as amended). Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repatriation by non-resident shareholders are freely permitted, subject only to standard corporate law solvency tests, tax clearance obligations under the Income Tax Act 2007 (e.g., dividend withholding tax and non-resident withholding tax), and the Financial Markets Authority's disclosure rules where applicable. The only residual hurdles are anti-money laundering (AML) verification under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act), which apply to regulated financial institutions processing remittances, and specific reporting or consent requirements for certain overseas investments in sensitive land or significant business assets under the Overseas Investment Act. No surrender or conversion obligations apply to export proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in New Zealand for exporters to surrender export proceeds to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand or any other authority within any prescribed timeline. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts domiciled with New Zealand registered banks or offshore banks, converted at commercial rates, or held as working capital, at the discretion of the exporter. All foreign exchange transactions are conducted through registered banks and licensed non-bank foreign exchange dealers operating under the AML/CFT Act, which require standard transaction monitoring and source-of-funds verification but impose no mandatory conversion ratio. Under the Financial Reporting Act and tax legislation, exporters must maintain accurate accounting records for all export proceeds, and any gain or loss on foreign exchange is recognised in accordance with New Zealand International Financial Reporting Standards (NZ IFRS) and the Income Tax Act 2007.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High. New Zealand does not prohibit the use of offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) services for payment processing, provided the EMI is licensed in a recognised jurisdiction (e.g., UK FCA, Singapore MAS, or EU EEA) and the underlying services do not constitute regulated financial services in New Zealand without appropriate registration. The AML/CFT Act requires that any person carrying on a financial services business in New Zealand, including money or value transfer services, be registered with the Department of Internal Affairs and comply with AML/CFT obligations. An offshore EMI operating without a New Zealand registration may not solicit New Zealand residents or provide services to New Zealand-based entities unless an exemption applies; however, a New Zealand incorporated company may use a non-resident EMI's payout rails (e.g., PayPal Business, Wise) for receiving international payments from clients abroad, provided the EMI is not acting as a New Zealand deposit taker or lender. Directors should confirm that the EMI's terms of service permit New Zealand-registered business customers and that the entity's transactional profile remains within the EMI's acceptable-use policy. Legal constraints include restrictions on holding client funds in New Zealand without an authorised deposit-taking institution licence, and the requirement to report suspicious transactions to the New Zealand Financial Intelligence Unit. For mass PayPal payouts and Wise settlement, viability is assessed as High, subject to identity verification and beneficial ownership disclosure.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Inbound capital injection from related party or shareholder without economic justification

Root cause: Inbound capital injection from related party or shareholder without economic justification

Defense evidence: Shareholder resolution approving the capital injection; board minutes referencing working capital needs and payment of international staff and software vendors; executed loan or equity subscription documents; source-of-wealth statement for the funding party supported by bank reference letters and tax records; invoice or statement of works from contracted data-labelling platform providers; and an internal cash-flow forecast demonstrating no money-laundering or layering purpose.
Frequent cross-border payments to jurisdictions perceived as high-risk or involving digital-asset exposure

Root cause: Frequent cross-border payments to jurisdictions perceived as high-risk or involving digital-asset exposure

Defense evidence: AML/CFT risk assessment and internal compliance manual; due-diligence file on each counterparty including incorporation proof, beneficial ownership, and professional references; transaction trail reconciling FX conversion to client invoices; payroll records for data annotators and freelancers with tax withholding evidence; and a sworn affidavit from the compliance officer confirming no digital-asset or unregulated gambling exposure.
PayPal mass payouts and Wise transfers flagged as potential alternative-remittance or money-service business activity

Root cause: PayPal mass payouts and Wise transfers flagged as potential alternative-remittance or money-service business activity

Defense evidence: Commercial explanation that payments represent settlement of employee wages and freelance annotator fees under ISIC 8220 services; notarised client contract describing data-labelling output and payment schedule; screenshot or exported statement from PayPal and Wise showing matching invoice references; list of recipients with national identification or passport verification; and evidence that the entity is not offering money-transfer services to the public or holding third-party funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank to the New Zealand IC's NZD or USD operating account. Standard purpose codes should reference capital contribution, with instructions including the shareholder identification and a reference to the board-approved capital plan. Common SWIFT purpose codes for inbound investment to New Zealand include 'EC03' (foreign direct investment equity capital) or 'EC04' (loans from foreign direct investor), depending on the funding instrument. The receiving bank will require the underlying subscription or loan agreement and may issue an email confirmation to the AML compliance team. Additional verification steps include: (i) bank-to-bank KYC due-diligence on the remitter; (ii) completion of the bank's beneficiary declaration form stating the nature and source of funds; (iii) provision of the shareholder resolution and capital injection evidence; (iv) registration of the foreign investment under the Overseas Investment Act if the capital is applied to acquiring sensitive New Zealand assets or significant business assets (unlikely for a data-labelling BPO unless office real estate is acquired); and (v) for amounts exceeding NZD 10,000 in cash or where suspicious, filing of an AML/CFT prescribed transaction report. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High. New Zealand does not prohibit the use of offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) services for payment processing, provided the EMI is licensed in a recognised jurisdiction (e.g., UK FCA, Singapore MAS, or EU EEA) and the underlying services do not constitute regulated financial services in New Zealand without appropriate registration. The AML/CFT Act requires that any person carrying on a financial services business in New Zealand, including money or value transfer services, be registered with the Department of Internal Affairs and comply with AML/CFT obligations. An offshore EMI operating without a New Zealand registration may not solicit New Zealand residents or provide services to New Zealand-based entities unless an exemption applies; however, a New Zealand incorporated company may use a non-resident EMI's payout rails (e.g., PayPal Business, Wise) for receiving international payments from clients abroad, provided the EMI is not acting as a New Zealand deposit taker or lender. Directors should confirm that the EMI's terms of service permit New Zealand-registered business customers and that the entity's transactional profile remains within the EMI's acceptable-use policy. Legal constraints include restrictions on holding client funds in New Zealand without an authorised deposit-taking institution licence, and the requirement to report suspicious transactions to the New Zealand Financial Intelligence Unit. For mass PayPal payouts and Wise settlement, viability is assessed as High, subject to identity verification and beneficial ownership disclosure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and Local File are mandatory for multinational groups whose consolidated global revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750 million (approximately NZ$1.3 billion) and that have covered cross-border related-party transactions with New Zealand; the IRD also expects contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for material cross-border dealings generally above NZ$5 million aggregate per income year, and for low-value services a specific arm's-length return is required.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; instead, economic substance is tested through tax-residence rules (central management and control under ITA 2007 s YC 2), permanent-establishment principles in DTAs, and the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1). An International Company (CK_IC) claiming tax-exempt offshore status must demonstrate board control and commercial activity in a foreign jurisdiction; failure to do so results in NZ resident taxation on worldwide income and penalty exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax (TAA 1994 s 141E); criminal prosecution under s 143B with fines up to NZ$100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; compounding interest on all underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or direct revenue diversion are joint offenders; personal criminal fines, imprisonment, departure prohibition orders (s 157 TAA), asset restitution, and disqualification under Companies Act 1993 s 385.
Shell Company / Letterbox Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Commissioner reassessment as NZ tax resident under s YC 2; worldwide income subjected to 28% corporate tax; 100% abusive tax position penalty (s 141D); denial of treaty benefits; cancellation of any offshore exempt status.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly operating the sham face personal liability for assessed tax on a common-law agency basis, civil penalties for culpable participation, travel restrictions, and director bans; criminal exposure where evasion intent is proven.
Transfer Pricing Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length reallocation of consideration under s GC 13; shortfall penalty of 20% (unacceptable tax position) rising to 100% (abusive) where documentation is absent or deficient; use-of-money interest on assessed shortfall.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign tax returns knowing that transfer prices are not arm's length are personally liable for penalties for knowingly taking an unacceptable tax position; director disqualification and possible prosecution for tax evasion.
Unremitted Non-Resident Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: Payer remains liable as the withholding agent for the full 30% NRWT (or 15% royalty/interest NRWT), plus late filing and late payment penalties accruing at 1% per month; the gross distribution is grossed up for assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes under TAA 1994 s 174 where the company defaults; IRD may pursue directors immediately, issue departure prohibition orders, and disqualify directors of any company with persistent non-compliance.
AML/CFT Deficiencies in Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to NZ$2 million per breach for a body corporate under the AML/CFT Act 2009 (ss 87-89); criminal penalties for deliberate non-compliance; IRD may also penalise hidden taxable revenue derived through unreported foreign payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers personally liable for failing to maintain an AML/CFT compliance programme; individual fines up to NZ$200,000 and up to 2 years' imprisonment, plus director disqualification under s 385 Companies Act 1993.
DTA / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Treaty relief denied, NRWT recaptured at the full statutory rate (30% dividends / 15% interest and royalties); shortfall penalties of 20%-150% for false or misleading withholding information; interest accrual from original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors making false treaty declarations to IRD and foreign authorities are individually liable for penalties, subject to criminal perjury-type charges, departure prohibition orders, and disqualification; civil restitution can be levied on personal assets.
