No escrow arrangement, capital deposit account, or bank confirmation of paid-up capital is required under New Zealand law.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake), Wellington, New Zealand; taxpayer portal: myIR (https://ird.govt.nz). Business entity registration infrastructure also includes the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) Register (https://www.nzbn.govt.nz) and the New Zealand Companies Office for foreign company/branch registration.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident or foreign-incorporated International Company: (1) obtain an NZBN from the NZBN Register, including registration as an overseas company if a local branch or legal person is created; (2) apply to the Inland Revenue Department for a New Zealand IRD number (tax file number) using Form IR742 (non-resident company/non-resident tax registration), accompanied by certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, board resolution, director and controller details, foreign tax identification number, registered office/principal place of business information and a declaration of expected New Zealand tax nexus; (3) where taxable supplies will exceed the GST threshold, register for GST through myIR or Form GST-101A; the GST registration uses the same IRD number and no separate VAT registration number label is issued. A fully offshore tax-exempt International Company with no New Zealand taxable activity is not ordinarily required to register, but an IRD number may be obtained for withholding tax reporting if New Zealand-source payments will be made.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A corporate International Company is treated as an entity-level taxpayer to the extent it derives New Zealand-source income through a fixed establishment or carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand. No general tax-exempt-zone election exists under New Zealand domestic law; any tax-exempt status must arise from the non-resident/offshore international-company regime. For controlled foreign company attribution, the statutory active income exclusion may be available; for hybrid mismatch arrangements, elections and timing adjustments may be required under the Taxation (Neutralising Hybrid Mismatch Arrangements) Act 2018, and binding rulings or private rulings are available under Part VA of the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (a) supplies of goods physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier or by the purchaser before the goods are used in New Zealand; (b) services supplied to a non-resident outside New Zealand where the services are not supplied to a New Zealand resident and not performed in connection with goods situated in New Zealand at the time the services are performed; and (c) certain international transport, telecommunications and supplies to inbound/outbound non-residents. Programmatic advertising, ad exchange and SSP services supplied to foreign counterparties will be zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident and the services are effectively consumed outside New Zealand; if the recipient is a New Zealand GST-registered business making taxable supplies, the supply may be zero-rated by way of the recipient's reverse-charge election rather than the supplier's export evidence. The exporter must hold contemporaneous documentary proof of export or non-resident performance.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged with the New Zealand Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing physical export movement from New Zealand and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and programmatic service agreement evidencing the offshore recipient and non-resident performance
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, Swift MT103 payment instruction or bank statement proving foreign currency consideration repatriated to a New Zealand bank account within the statutory period
  • GST return schedules, foreign exchange conversion records, ad-delivery/SSP logs, and foreign counterparty due diligence records

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory continuous transaction controls. E-invoicing is voluntary; the New Zealand GST Act 1985 permits electronic tax invoices provided they contain all prescribed particulars, including supplier name and GST number, date, description of supply, consideration, GST amount and zero-rated or exempt-supply indicators. New Zealand supports the Peppol PINT/UBL standard through the NZBN Peppol Authority, but there is no statutory obligation to use it or to clear invoices through an IRD API. Taxpayers must retain source documentation; the IRD may require read-only access to accounting systems during audit, but no automated fiscal clearance occurs for programmatic advertising or SSP supplies.

Incorrect zero-rating, fraudulent export claims, or failure to substantiate export/repatriation within the 180-day deadline will result in reversal of the zero rate and output GST at 15%, together with late payment penalties of up to 100% of the unpaid amount, use-of-money interest, and shortfall penalties of 20% for an unacceptable tax position, increasing to 150% for evasion. Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, deliberate tax evasion may be prosecuted criminally and can result in fines up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment up to five years; directors and controlling shareholders may be held personally liable. The IRD and New Zealand Customs Service jointly audit export-facing platforms; retrospective clawbacks may include the full 15% GST, penalties, and compound interest where the documentation bundle is incomplete or the foreign sales proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalized capital and current account regime with a freely floating New Zealand dollar (NZD). The NZD is fully convertible for both current and capital account purposes; there are no residual surrender or conversion obligations and no central bank approval requirement for repatriation of profits, dividends, royalties, or intercompany settlements. Profit remittance is subject only to standard non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at treaty-reduced rates on outbound dividends, interest, and royalties, plus standard documentation requirements under the Income Tax Act 2007. For an International Company with exempt-zone tax treatment, the assessable risk is substance, not exchange control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in New Zealand. Exporters and cross-border digital service providers may retain foreign currency proceeds offshore or in New Zealand foreign-currency accounts indefinitely, and may convert freely at market rates. The only applicable obligations are statistical reporting to Statistics New Zealand and record-keeping under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act), including prescribed transaction reports for cash transactions at or above NZD 10,000 and wire-transfer record thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), with foreign direct investment screening administered by the Overseas Investment Office at Toitū Te Whenua / Land Information New Zealand under the Overseas Investment Act 2005; New Zealand maintains no comprehensive foreign exchange control statute.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material structural constraints. An offshore digital EMI may service the New Zealand entity only if the EMI is registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers Act 2008, maintains membership in an approved dispute-resolution scheme, and complies with the AML/CFT Act when carrying on business in New Zealand. An unregulated offshore EMI without FSPR registration is not an acceptable sole treasury rail for an NZ-incorporated International Company and will generate adverse audit opinions, bank refusal of downstream correspondent wires, and regulator sanction risk. Where used, the EMI should be limited to settlement facilitation while the primary custody and operating accounts remain at a tier-1 NZ bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed exclusively through New Zealand registered banks via SWIFT MT103 wires using standard transaction purpose codes: 'EQUITY' for paid-in share capital, 'LOAN' for shareholder advances, and 'DEBT' for intercompany treasury flows. No Reserve Bank of New Zealand exchange-control approval or conversion permit is required for a non-bank entity. The receiving bank performs sanctions screening, source-of-funds verification, and AML/CFT wire-format record-keeping. For share capital injections, a bank confirmation of the inward funds may be required for Companies Office solvency certification and to evidence the capital escrow release condition.Cross-border trade settlements to advertising partners, media sellers, and inventory suppliers are cleared primarily via SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer messages denominated in USD, EUR, or NZD with same-day treasury execution from multicurrency accounts. Domestic NZD disbursements clear through the Bulk Electronic Clearing System (BECS)/ACH rails. For larger or structured settlements, Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 and documentary collections are available from the tier-1 banks, though they are uncommon for programmatic ad-tech flows. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% (flat company tax rate under Income Tax Act 2007, s BC 18)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% standard non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends paid to non-residents; reduced to 15% for fully imputed dividends. Lower treaty rates may apply, commonly 15% under most comprehensive double tax agreements and 5% under certain treaties (e.g., Australia for significant shareholdings).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a standard balance date of 31 March, the IR4 return is due by 7 July following the balance date. For a non-standard balance date, the due date is the 7th day of the 4th month after the balance date (e.g., 30 June balance date due 7 October). A tax agent extension may extend the due date for a limited period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing documentation (Local File / Master File equivalent) must be prepared and retained where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions with non-resident associated enterprises exceeds NZ$10 million in the relevant income year; country-by-country (CbC) reporting is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of NZ$1 billion or more, aligned with the OECD Pillar One/Two thresholds.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to zero-tax offshore centres, but substantive business presence is a core requirement through the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA 2007 s BG1), the place-of-effective-management test, and transfer pricing rules. An International Company structured as a pure mailbox or SSP/DSP conduit with no New Zealand office, no local employees, no strategic board decisions taken in New Zealand, and no alignment of profit with actual risk-taking and commercial activity will be disregarded or recharacterised, with all income reallocated to the jurisdiction where the real economic functions, assets, and risks sit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abusive Tax Position

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the unpaid tax for evasion or an abusive tax position, plus late payment penalties and use-of-money interest. Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994 s 143B: on conviction, a fine of up to NZ$50,000 for an individual and up to NZ$200,000 for a company, and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. Repeat offenders face higher fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly file false returns or cause tax avoidance are personally liable for shortfall penalties; IRD may issue a departure prohibition order under the Tax Administration Act preventing the director from leaving New Zealand while tax debts exist. Directors may also be sued for civil restitution for amounts diverted from the company, disqualified from managing companies under the Companies Act 1994, and, upon criminal conviction, imprisoned as principals or parties to the evasion.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the arrangement under the general anti-avoidance rule; transfer pricing adjustments increasing New Zealand taxable income; denial of treaty benefits if the residence claim is a sham or the entity is not beneficial owner; penalties of up to 150% for gross carelessness or abusive tax position; cancellation of tax registration and potential strike-off under the Companies Act 1994 for no real business.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the company's unpaid PAYE, GST, and certain penalties under director penalty notice rules; for income tax, personal liability attaches where the director has caused the company to evade tax or has assisted in a sham arrangement. Courts may pierce the corporate veil to order civil restitution to corporate creditors and IRD; directors can face travel bans, disqualification, and criminal prosecution.
AML / Money Laundering and Unlicensed Payment Routing

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a reporting entity that fails to conduct adequate customer due diligence, collection, or verification controls, or that knowingly facilitates money laundering, is liable on conviction to large fines: up to NZ$2 million for a body corporate and NZ$300,000 for an individual, plus potential imprisonment of up to 2 years. Non-reporting evasion of the AML/CFT regime carries corresponding criminal penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors, AML compliance officers, and senior managers are personally liable if they knowingly allow tainted funds to move through the platform. Penalties include personal fines up to NZ$300,000, imprisonment up to 2 years, civil forfeiture orders, extradition and travel bans, and disqualification from holding directorships. Company directors cannot shelter behind corporate immunity where they were the controlling mind of the money laundering operation.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Cross-Border Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under Part IV of the Income Tax Act 2007, added tax, shortfall penalties up to 150% for evasion-like behaviour or 40% for gross carelessness, and daily late payment interest. If the adjustment creates an understatement of taxable income exceeding NZ$100,000, the taxpayer may be subject to a penalty of 100% of the tax shortfall for an abusive tax position.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate transfer pricing disclosure certificates or cause the company to enter into structured arrangements to defeat the arm's length principle can be personally liable for penalties and interest. In severe cases, directors may be criminally prosecuted for evasion of tax, banned from leaving New Zealand under departure prohibition orders, and required to personally reimburse the company for tax penalties and losses through civil proceedings.
