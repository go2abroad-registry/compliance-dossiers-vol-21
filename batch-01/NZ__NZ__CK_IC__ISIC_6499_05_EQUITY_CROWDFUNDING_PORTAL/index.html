Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

New Zealand imposes no statutory minimum paid-up share capital for incorporation; the Companies Act 1993 requires only that each initial shareholder subscribes at least one share and that the total issued share capital is stated in the incorporation application and initial annual return. No escrow account or capital deposit is required at the Companies Office stage. However, under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 (FMC Act) licensing criteria, the applicant crowdfunding portal must demonstrate minimum operational net tangible assets of NZD 200,000 (approximately USD 126,000) or maintain a professional indemnity insurance policy or a trust/custody arrangement as prescribed by FMA conditions, which must be evidenced at the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) licensing stage, not at incorporation.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident International Company: not tax-resident in New Zealand; New Zealand income tax applies only to New Zealand-source income under the territorial rule; an offshore equity crowdfunding portal without a New Zealand branch, permanent establishment, or New Zealand-source intermediary commission is currently outside the New Zealand income tax net, and the entity is not a New Zealand resident taxpayer).

Available election forms

No United States-style check-the-box election exists under New Zealand law; companies are entity-level taxpayers by default. The hybrid mismatch rules in subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 operate by default to neutralise certain cross-border hybrid arrangements, with limited administrative elections and documentation requirements available before the Commissioner of Inland Revenue. No general election is available to convert a non-resident International Company into a pass-through vehicle for New Zealand income tax purposes; entity classification follows New Zealand domestic law and the entity's actual tax-residence status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (NZ$60,000) of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; the same NZD 60,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of remote services and digital products to New Zealand consumers; voluntary registration is permitted where taxable supplies are made in New Zealand and the threshold is not exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supplier has documentary evidence that the supply qualifies as an export: (a) goods physically removed from New Zealand within the prescribed statutory timeframe, provided the supplier holds shipping/customs evidence showing export; (b) services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the services are not performed in connection with New Zealand land and are not otherwise supplied under a contract that gives the supplier a reasonable expectation of use in New Zealand; (c) certain cross-border business-to-business remote and digital services supplied to non-resident recipients where the recipient is not physically in New Zealand. For an equity crowdfunding portal, platform, escrow, and administrative services supplied to non-resident issuers and non-resident investors can qualify for zero-rating where the portal's performance, management, and supply chain are offshore, the recipients are non-resident at the time of supply, and no New Zealand land or physical services are involved.

New Zealand does not mandate real-time invoice clearance by the tax authority, does not require fiscal digital stamping, and does not currently mandate an electronic invoicing API for all taxpayers. GST invoices must contain the prescribed statutory particulars, including seller name and GST registration number, invoice number and date, description of the supply, amount of consideration, and GST charged. Electronic invoices, including those transmitted via the voluntary Peppol network, are valid, but there is no pre-clearance or transaction-level approval procedure by the IRD; periodic GST returns and digital records are submitted through myIR.

The IRD subjects zero-rated export claims to strict documentary scrutiny, particularly for offshore digital platforms and financial intermediaries. Where a zero-rating claim is unsupported or fraudulent, the IRD may reassess the supply at the standard 15% GST rate, deny input tax credits, impose use-of-money interest, and apply civil shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994, including up to 150% of the tax shortfall for evasion or similar conduct. Repeat or deliberate conduct can also result in criminal prosecution, significant fines, imprisonment, director and corporate liability, cancellation of pending refunds, retrospective clawback of claimed refunds, and removal from the GST registered persons register.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility of the NZD on both current and capital account. The legal distinction between current-account and capital-account items is immaterial for FX control purposes because no exchange-control approval, currency rationing, or registration of cross-border contracts applies. Non-resident shareholders, trust settlors, and lenders may freely convert foreign currency into NZD for equity injections, shareholder advances, inter-company loans, and working capital; the crowdfunding portal may freely remit dividends, interest, royalties, service fees, platform commissions, escrow refunds, and redemption proceeds abroad without Overseas Investment Office consent or Reserve Bank permission. Practical friction points are limited to (i) the AML/CFT Act 2009 wire-transfer originator and beneficiary information duties; (ii) non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) obligations on dividends and interest remitted to non-residents, which require Inland Revenue form IR67 or a valid certificate while the platform applies the correct treaty rate under an applicable double-tax agreement; and (iii) the Part 6 client-money rules of the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, which prohibit commingling the platform's operational funds with investor funds held in the qualifying escrow trust account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. There is no statutory timeline within which export proceeds, service fees, platform commissions, or other foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated to New Zealand, and there is no prescribed conversion ratio requiring proceeds to be exchanged into NZD. A licensed crowdfunding portal and its foreign issuers may retain receipts in offshore foreign-currency accounts or in approved NZ foreign-currency deposit accounts at Reserve Bank of New Zealand registered banks. The only residual duties are (i) accurate statutory financial reporting of foreign-currency balances under the Companies Act 1993 and applicable financial reporting standards; (ii) compliance with the AML/CFT Act 2009 rules on transmitting originator information for all cross-border wires and making suspicious-transaction reports where required; and (iii) where the non-resident parent trust or International Company benefits from a tax-exempt-zone classification, continued adherence to the evidentiary transparency conditions of the foreign-trust or international-company regime so that bank-account documentation remains accurate, current, and legally enforceable.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the sole central bank and currency-issuing authority for the NZD, prudential supervisor of registered banks, and the AML/CFT supervisor applicable to NZ banking institutions. New Zealand does not operate a dedicated foreign-exchange-control administration; the Exchange Control Regulations were fully abolished in 1984, so no central-bank approval, surrender, or registration is required for cross-border payments. Adjacent functional supervision is distributed among the Financial Markets Authority (FMA, licensing of the equity crowdfunding intermediary under Part 6 of the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013), the Department of Internal Affairs (AML/CFT supervision of non-bank financial institutions and reporting entities), the New Zealand Police Financial Intelligence Unit (suspicious transaction reporting), and Inland Revenue (non-resident withholding tax and thin-capitalisation enforcement). The Overseas Investment Office administers foreign-investment screening limited to sensitive land and significant business assets and is irrelevant to ordinary treasury flows of a digital crowdfunding platform.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Overall viability is LOW as the primary escrow and settlement rail; MEDIUM as an auxiliary FX, payroll, or disbursement utility. Legal constraints: under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Disputes Resolution) Act 2008, no person may provide financial services — including money transfer, FX dealing, or payment services — in New Zealand without being registered on the Financial Service Providers Register. Offshore digital EMIs without NZ FSP registration cannot lawfully open NZD transaction accounts for the platform or hold client funds without breaching the FMC Act and the FSP Act. Locally licensed EMIs such as Airwallex NZ can provide multi-currency accounts, but they cannot satisfy the FMA client-money obligation, which requires all equity-crowdfunding investor proceeds to be paid into a single qualifying trust account maintained with an RBNZ-registered bank or licensed non-bank deposit taker. Therefore the statutory escrow rail must sit at a tier-1 commercial bank; fintech EMIs are confined to corporate cards, FX conversion, payroll execution, and sub-account automation that does not touch investor funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound investment capital into the New Zealand platform should arrive via SWIFT MT103 single credit transfer, or via an ESAS-participant same-day credit push where both banks are New Zealand clearing members. Recommended wire narrative: CONTRIBUTION TO CAPITAL — EQUITY CROWDFUNDING PLATFORM [NZBN] — NON-RESIDENT SHAREHOLDER [NAME] — OPERATIONAL ACCOUNT REFERENCE. The beneficiary account must be the operational non-escrow NZD account; investor client funds must never be routed to the operating account. New Zealand does not use a mandatory Asian-style purpose-code list; banks rely on the SWIFT Remittance Information field, so the narrative must unambiguously convey the capital nature of the transfer, whether equity injection, shareholder loan, or subordinated working-capital facility. During onboarding the bank registers the ultimate remitting entity, and every subsequent wire should originate from the same remitter and carry the same capital reference to avoid compliance holds. Overseas Investment Office consent is not required for a digital crowdfunding intermediary because the business involves no sensitive land and does not approach the NZD 100 million significant-business-asset screening threshold; nevertheless the platform should provide the bank with a standing written confirmation that no sensitive land or over-threshold asset exposure exists. To prevent clearing delays, the remitter should provide in advance a board resolution authorising the capital contribution, the executed subscription or loan agreement, and individual identification and beneficial-ownership documents for the remitting entity.Layered clearing-rail architecture: (1) Domestic issuer disbursements — equity-crowdfunding proceeds payable to New Zealand-resident issuers are cleared through Payments NZ BECS, the Bulk Electronic Clearing System, as same-day or next-day NZD credit pushes to the issuer's nominated account; large-value payments above the bank's cutoff are routed through the RBNZ ESAS real-time gross settlement system for finality. Each disbursement must reference the offer register code and the milestone-release certificate, satisfying the FMC client-money rule that funds are released only when the offer closes successfully and the minimum subscription target is met. (2) Cross-border wires to offshore issuers, suppliers, or advisors — executed via SWIFT MT103 with complete originator and beneficiary identifiers, the ISIN or offer reference, and the purpose narrative EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION PROCEEDS OR PLATFORM COMMISSION UNDER FMC LICENCE NUMBER; the platform must maintain a watchlist of all foreign issuers and perform pre-send sanctions screening because correspondent banks at each intermediary may review the transaction. (3) Commercial letters of credit — where the portal intermediates asset-backed or trade-finance offerings, LCs must be opened through the tier-1 bank's trade-finance desk under UCP600, with the escrow trust account designated as the claiming party and transport documents or service-delivery evidence attached to each presentation; the portal must not create standalone payment undertakings outside the bank's LC and SBLC facilities, as any such undertaking would constitute unlicensed financial-service activity and a breach of the client-money rules. (4) Refund and cancellation flows — investor refunds are made by BECS reversal within ten business days of cancellation and must always be paid from the escrow account, never from the operational account. All rails must support a daily cash ledger reconciled to the trust account in accordance with FMA Part 6 record-keeping standards.Deliverable OTC forward FX contracts (NZD/USD, NZD/AUD, NZD/EUR, NZD/GBP, and NZD/CNY) executed with a tier-1 New Zealand bank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement — the standard instrument for hedging anticipated platform-commission income, escrow-currency exposure, and foreign-currency issuer obligations.FX swaps (spot and forward combination legs) used to manage dual-currency escrow timing mismatches between the investor contribution currency and the issuer disbursement currency.Cross-currency swaps for long-dated foreign-currency funding of the platform's working capital where the borrowing is denominated in USD, GBP, or EUR and operating revenue is NZD-denominated.Vanilla currency options (purchased calls and puts) obtained from the New Zealand bank to cap the downside of large issuer-denominated receivables without creating margin-call liquidity risk.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) — technically permissible, but neither standard nor necessary for the NZD because the currency is fully deliverable offshore; where an NDF referencing NZD is nonetheless executed with an offshore counterparty, it will be economically equivalent to a deliverable forward and subject to RBNZ prudential margin and collateral rules if a New Zealand bank or its branch is a counterparty, and to the Financial Market Infrastructures Act 2021 clearing obligations where any applicable clearing threshold is exceeded.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Overall viability is LOW as the primary escrow and settlement rail; MEDIUM as an auxiliary FX, payroll, or disbursement utility. Legal constraints: under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Disputes Resolution) Act 2008, no person may provide financial services — including money transfer, FX dealing, or payment services — in New Zealand without being registered on the Financial Service Providers Register. Offshore digital EMIs without NZ FSP registration cannot lawfully open NZD transaction accounts for the platform or hold client funds without breaching the FMC Act and the FSP Act. Locally licensed EMIs such as Airwallex NZ can provide multi-currency accounts, but they cannot satisfy the FMA client-money obligation, which requires all equity-crowdfunding investor proceeds to be paid into a single qualifying trust account maintained with an RBNZ-registered bank or licensed non-bank deposit taker. Therefore the statutory escrow rail must sit at a tier-1 commercial bank; fintech EMIs are confined to corporate cards, FX conversion, payroll execution, and sub-account automation that does not touch investor funds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand has not implemented statutory documentation threshold amounts (NZD figures) requiring automatic Local File or Master File preparation; instead, an exemption from contemporaneous documentation applies where the aggregate value of cross-border associated-party transactions is NZD 3 million or less, or the taxpayer is a 'non-active' investor. A Master File and Local File must be prepared and provided to Inland Revenue within 30 days if requested, where the group exceeds NZD 100 million in global turnover and cross-border related-party dealings exceed NZD 10 million, subject to the commissioner's guidelines. All documentation must support an arm's length result for transactions exceeding materiality thresholds set under the OECD transfer pricing guidelines adopted by the Income Tax Act 2007.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand applies general business substance principles. There is no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code as in the UAE; however, under the tax avoidance general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) in s BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and the interest limitation rules, a company lacking commercial substance with arrangements whose sole or main purpose is tax avoidance will be recharacterized. Crowdfunding portal licensees must maintain genuine operational substance, including registered office, local directors, legal/compliance staffing, and transaction governance, to avoid denial of tax treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) in NZ's DTAs.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Penal tax of 150% of the tax shortfall under the Tax Administration Act 1994, plus civil penalties up to NZ$250,000 for false returns; criminal conviction for tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to NZ$50,000 per offence. In severe cases, Inland Revenue may seek liquidation of the company.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit evasion or are knowingly party to a false return can be held personally liable for the company's tax shortfall under the Companies Act 1993 and tax recovery provisions. Directors may face disqualification, personal restitution orders, travel bans by court order, and imprisonment. The corporate veil is pierced where the company is used as a façade for deliberate tax wrongdoing.
AML/CFT Breach by Equity Crowdfunding Portal (High Risk Special License)

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a supervised entity that fails to meet AML/CFT obligations faces a civil penalty of up to NZ$2 million for a body corporate for each non-compliance event; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) may revoke the crowdfunding license and order remediation costs. Criminal sanctions of up to 2 years imprisonment and fines of up to NZ$200,000 for individuals apply for willful misconduct.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are subject to statutory duties under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013; civil liability for pecuniary penalties of up to NZ$1 million per director for reckless or dishonest conduct is possible. The FMA may issue banning orders preventing individuals from managing financial services companies; personal liability for investor losses may arise for misstatements in crowdfunding offer documents.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The FMA may treat the licence as ineffective and cancel registration; Inland Revenue may apply the GAAR to reallocate income and deny treaty benefits under the PPT. The company's directors may be liable for breach of section 137 of the Companies Act 1993 (reckless trading). Fines up to NZ$200,000 and revocation of the certificate of incorporation under the Companies Office protocols may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors must ensure the company carries on business in a manner that does not create a substantial risk of serious loss to creditors; violation exposes directors to personal liability for debts and restitution orders, plus travel bans and mandatory appearance orders in Inland Revenue investigations. Persistent non-residency of actual decision-makers could render the company managed and controlled in New Zealand, subjecting it to full tax residence and worldwide taxation.
Unauthorized Crowdfunding Activity / Unlicensed Financial Intermediation

Statutory sanction: Offences under the FMCA carry criminal penalties of up to NZ$300,000 for an individual and NZ$2.5 million for a body corporate, plus civil pecuniary penalties of up to NZ$1 million for an individual and NZ$5 million for a body corporate. Orders for restitution to investors, injunctions, and public warning notices may be issued by the FMA.

Director personal liability: The court may order civil penalties against every director involved in the contravention; directors are personally liable for such penalties unless they can prove they took all reasonable steps to prevent the breach. They may be subject to banning orders and compensation orders in favor of defrauded investors.
Profit Repatriation / NRWT Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty of 40% for an unacceptable tax position or 150% for evasion, plus interest (use of money interest) on unpaid NRWT. The company as withholding agent is personally liable for all tax not deducted, and late-filed returns invite late filing penalties for every period on the IR4S/RA schedule.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the company's failure to deduct and pay NRWT under a director penalty notice regime; the Commissioner of Inland Revenue may issue a notice requiring payment within 30 days, after which personal recovery action, including bankruptcy proceedings and asset restraining orders, may be pursued.
