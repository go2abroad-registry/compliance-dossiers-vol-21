Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand — operational portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/; login via myIR secure online services); business registration integrated through NZBN (https://www.nzbn.govt.nz/) and the Companies Office.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity (e.g., an International Company registered under the Cook Islands International Companies Act) seeking New Zealand tax registration must first determine whether it has New Zealand tax residence or a New Zealand source of income. A non-resident company carrying on business in New Zealand or deriving New Zealand-sourced income must register with Inland Revenue using the IRD ‘Non-resident company registration’ procedure: obtain an NZBN if required, complete IRD application form IR596 (or equivalent online application via myIR for non-residents), provide certified constituent documents, proof of foreign tax residence, and details of the New Zealand agent or fixed establishment, and receive a New Zealand IRD number. If the entity is deemed resident under New Zealand domestic law (incorporation in NZ, head office in NZ, or control and management in NZ), registration as a resident company with an IRD number and NZBN is mandatory. For GST purposes, a non-resident must separately register under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 if taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 per 12-month period or if the entity is a ‘remote services’ supplier with significant New Zealand consumer supplies; registration is effected through myIR and produces a GST registration number distinct from the IRD number. Any pure equity holding SPV that has no New Zealand banking, payroll, or trading activity may nonetheless require an IRD number for withholding tax compliance, dividend declarations, or tax residency certificate applications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Under New Zealand law there is no direct analog to the U.S. check-the-box election; entity classification follows the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Companies Act 1993. A foreign ‘International Company’ (Cook Islands IC) that is centrally managed and controlled outside New Zealand and that derives only foreign-source passive dividend income is treated as non-resident and generally outside the New Zealand tax net. A non-resident company with a New Zealand fixed establishment may elect to file on a branch basis or, if incorporated in New Zealand, must file as a resident company. Where hybrid mismatch rules apply under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018, an ‘election’ may be required to align the treatment of a foreign hybrid entity in accordance with the OECD BEPS Action 2 rules; New Zealand does not offer a general check-the-box regime for foreign disregarded entities, but a non-resident entity may seek a binding ruling from IRD regarding its New Zealand tax classification and permanent establishment exposure. For GST purposes, a non-resident can elect to register even below the registration threshold in certain circumstances, and groups of companies may elect GST group registration. For thin capitalisation and interest apportionment, no elective de minimis applies beyond statutory safe harbours.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 taxable supplies in any 12-month period; a non-resident ‘remote services’ supplier has a separate registration obligation for supplies of remote services to New Zealand resident consumers if total supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in 12 months; a ‘redesigned GST on low-value imported goods’ registration can be required for non-resident suppliers of low-value goods (goods valued at or below NZD 1,000) to New Zealand consumers when turnover exceeds the NZD 60,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under New Zealand GST law, the supply of goods or services is zero-rated when the supplier is a GST-registered person and the supply qualifies as an ‘export of goods’ (goods physically exported from New Zealand within 28 days of supply, or within 12 months if supplied under a hire-purchase or conditional sale agreement), a supply of ‘services to a non-resident’ where the services are supplied to a person who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed and the benefit of the services is received outside New Zealand (with exceptions for services connected with New Zealand land, goods situated in New Zealand, or work performed on New Zealand goods), or a supply of a ‘travel’ or ‘international transport’ service. A pure equity holding SPV that only holds shares and receives dividends will not ordinarily have export supplies; however, if it makes management or treasury services to non-resident group companies, it must demonstrate that the recipient is non-resident and the benefit is received outside New Zealand; intra-group services performed by a New Zealand entity for an offshore parent may still be subject to GST at 15% if the services are physically performed in New Zealand and the benefit is received in New Zealand, unless zero-rating applies under section 11A(1)(k) for services supplied to a non-resident who is not in New Zealand at the time and the supply is not connected with New Zealand land or goods.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (ocean/air transport document evidencing physical export from New Zealand)

New Zealand has no mandatory real-time e-invoicing clearance regime or fiscal stamping requirement. Inland Revenue operates a voluntary PEPPOL-based e-invoicing network; businesses may voluntarily adopt e-invoicing using the New Zealand Peppol Authority standards. GST returns are filed electronically via myIR with payment and filing obligations generally monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly depending on turnover and registration choice. There is no pre-clearance API requirement for individual invoices, but taxpayers must retain transactional records in New Zealand or accessible electronically, and IRD may require digital access under the Tax Administration Act 1994. A pure equity holding SPV with no supplies has no GST registration obligation and no e-invoicing duty, unless it makes exempt financial supplies in New Zealand (in which case input tax recovery is generally denied).

IRD and New Zealand Customs may conduct joint audits of zero-rated export transactions. If the exporter cannot substantiate the physical export or the non-resident status of the recipient, the zero-rating is reversed and GST becomes payable at 15% with use-of-money interest from the original due date. Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, an unsubstantiated claim may attract shortfall penalties: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for hidden or abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or scam; criminal prosecution is possible for knowingly filing a false GST return. For goods, exporters must also comply with the Customs and Excise Act 2018; false export declarations carry fines and potential forfeiture of goods. Retrospective clawback periods generally extend four years from the filing date, or up to seven years (or no limit) in cases of fraud, evasion, or significant or repeated shortfall penalties under sections 108 and 141 of the Tax Administration Act 1994. Where the exporter is a pure equity holding SPV structurally incapable of making taxable supplies, any fraudulent zero-rating claim would be treated as a sham and struck down, with joint and several liability of directors and group companies under anti-avoidance provisions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full capital account convertibility under the OECD Codes of Liberalisation. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely floating and fully convertible for current and capital account transactions. Profit remittance by non-resident holding companies is unrestricted provided that the underlying transaction is bona fide, tax residency is established, and no sanctions designations apply. Dividends and capital distributions may be repatriated without exchange control approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement exists under New Zealand law to surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign currency earnings into NZD within any prescribed timeline. Export receipts may be retained in Foreign Currency Accounts (FCAs) as permitted by the relevant banks subject to commercial terms.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) and Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for market conduct; overseas portfolio and direct investment transactions are generally liberalized under the Overseas Investment Act 2005.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Moderate feasibility with substantial legal structuring. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may provide NZD virtual account issuance, but they are not registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989. They are regulated in their home jurisdictions and must partner with NZ prudentially regulated banks for local settlement. Inbound capital injections to a licensed trust structure and outbound dividend distributions should be routed through an NZ-incorporated ADI to preserve bare trust validity, segregation of trust assets, and compliance with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. The SPV may use EMIs solely for ancillary expense payments and minor foreign currency hedging if the EMI is registered on the FMA's licensed financial service provider register.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection into the Cook Islands International Company's NZ operational account should be routed via SWIFT MT103 / MT202 for high-value transfers with a clear purpose code (e.g., 'DIRIN - Direct investment', 'CFIN - Company finance', or 'Subscribed capital contribution'). The bank will require the underlying subscription agreement, board resolutions, and anti-money laundering checks. If funds traverse a bank in another jurisdiction (e.g., Hong Kong or Singapore), all routing must include the beneficial owner/payer details in compliance with FATF Recommendation 16. Capital injections for the purpose of subscribing for shares of the subsidiary (e.g., New Zealand HoldCo as the actual asset-owning entity) must be characterized as an intercompany loan or equity contribution, and proper NZ thin-capitalization and foreign-invested entity rules under the Income Tax Act 2007 must be analyzed before funds move. For any overseas investment in New Zealand farmland or sensitive assets, the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) pathway must be considered: consent may be required if the ultimate trust/company is not a New Zealand resident or has non-resident shareholders.Outbound trade disbursements from the SPV should be structured as either SWIFT MT103 (wire) or a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) for trade-related transactions. Because the SPV is a passive holding entity, the bulk of outbound flows will be dividend distributions, loan repayments, or re-injection of capital into subsidiary operations. MT103 payment instructions must include strict purpose codes (e.g., 'DIVI', 'LOAN', 'INTE', 'TREA'). Correspondent banks should be pre-agreed to ensure no freezing in transit. For any genuine trade flows (e.g., agricultural/food technology supply chain), an LC with a confirmed advising bank is ideal to guarantee goods documentation and payment security. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 3,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border related-party controlled transactions (goods, services, or financing) triggering contemporaneous Local File or Master File under New Zealand transfer pricing practice; where the CK_IC has nil transactions because it is a pure passive holder of subsidiary shares, a written nil transfer-pricing position is recommended.

Economic substance scope: Cook Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to all International Companies performing relevant activities. A pure equity holding SPV is a Low Risk Pure Equity Holding Company. It must: (i) file an annual Economic Substance Return (ESR-1); (ii) conduct its board meetings and core equity management functions in the Cook Islands; (iii) maintain an adequate registered office, local employee capacity, and accounting records in the Cook Islands; (iv) have no non-active or taxable income outside the described equity holding; and (v) satisfy the reduced economic substance test only. Non-compliance exposes the company to penalties up to NZD 50,000, deregistration, financial information exchange, and possible criminal liability of the registered agent and directors.
