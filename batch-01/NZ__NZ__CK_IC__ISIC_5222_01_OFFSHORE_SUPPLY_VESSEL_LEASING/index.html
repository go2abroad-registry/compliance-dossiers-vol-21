Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

New Zealand law does not prescribe a minimum paid-up capital and does not require capital to be held in escrow. The company must have at least one issued share, commonly 100 ordinary shares; shares may be issued unpaid or partly paid, and fully paid status is established by board resolution and by the statement of capital filed with the Companies Office. No paid-up capital escrow or banked capital verification is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) fire safety scheme approval for shore offices and stores; MOSS vessel survey covering structural load, stability and safe manning; Worksafe New Zealand notification and workplace safety compliance under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 for crew and shore personnel; international or New Zealand-approved STCW certification for officers and crew; and vessel civil defence readiness including emergency towage, oil spill contingency plans and firefighting equipment certification.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue Tāke / Te Tari Tāke), New Zealand Government; statutory portal: myIR, accessible at https://www.ird.govt.nz. For the associated Cook Islands International Company, the registrar is the Cook Islands Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC), and tax-exempt status is evidenced by a certificate from the Cook Islands Ministry of Finance and Economic Management (Revenue Management Division). For all New Zealand income tax and GST purposes, Inland Revenue is the sole competent national tax authority.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity, including an International Company incorporated in a tax-exempt zone, must obtain a New Zealand IRD number before incurring any NZ tax obligations. Procedure: (1) submit the prescribed application (form IR742 for companies, or IR596 for non-resident individuals) together with the certified certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, registered office certificate, worldwide ultimate parent details, and a completed tax residence and permanent establishment disclosure schedule; (2) where a taxable activity is carried on in New Zealand, the IRD number functions as the GST registration number, and compulsory GST registration arises when annual taxable turnover in New Zealand exceeds or is expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, effected through myIR; (3) for a purely offshore charter operation with no New Zealand-source income and no New Zealand fixed establishment, no IRD or GST registration is required, but a formal letter confirming non-residence and absence of a permanent establishment may be requested from Inland Revenue; (4) the International Company must retain the current Cook Islands FSC annual certificate and the exemption ruling in its statutory records to substantiate its Tax_Exempt_Zone status in all NZ tax correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime. The relevant statutory elections and mechanisms are: (i) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, available only to close-held companies satisfying the ownership composition tests and in practice unavailable to an International Company with non-resident corporate shareholders; (ii) Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) attribution elections for New Zealand-resident shareholders, including the de minimis exemption and the active-income exemption from attributable CFC income; (iii) Foreign Investment Fund (FIF) method elections (comparative value, fair dividend rate, or cost method) where applicable to NZ-resident shareholders; (iv) hybrid and branch mismatch neutrality elections and notification obligations under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018; (v) election to apply the New Zealand-Cook Islands double taxation agreement to allocate business profits away from New Zealand in the absence of a permanent establishment; and (vi) for the International Company itself, the election of complete tax exemption under the Cook Islands International Companies Act 1981-82, which is a foreign-law status recognised by Inland Revenue only where management and control are not exercised from New Zealand.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000: compulsory registration when annual taxable supplies made in New Zealand exceed, or are reasonably expected to exceed, NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period (GST Act 1985, sections 51 and 52). Voluntary registration is available to any person carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand. Non-resident suppliers of remote services, digital services, or services physically performed in New Zealand by a vessel operator (e.g., time-charter operations within New Zealand waters) may be subject to mandatory registration under the remote-services rules where supplies are made to non-registered consumers in New Zealand.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for services, including time-charter, bareboat charter, and leasing of offshore platform support or supply vessels, supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, where the services are performed outside New Zealand (GST Act 1985, section 11(4)). This applies when the chartered vessel is deployed beyond the 12-nautical-mile New Zealand territorial sea and the charterer has no New Zealand fixed establishment. Additional statutory bases include: (i) services supplied in connection with movable personal property (the vessel) situated outside New Zealand when the services are performed; and (ii) services performed for a non-resident that are not physically rendered in New Zealand and are integral to the vessel's offshore operations. Zero-rating is denied where the recipient is a New Zealand resident; where the non-resident recipient is present in New Zealand at the time of supply; or where the services relate to land or goods, including the vessel, located in New Zealand at the time of supply. A vessel chartered for operations within New Zealand waters is therefore generally subject to GST at 15% unless the charterer is a non-resident outside New Zealand and the vessel is not in New Zealand at the time of supply.

  • New Zealand Customs Service export declaration (Trade Single Window / CusMod entry) or certification of non-New-Zealand movements for international charter itineraries
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill evidencing movement of the vessel, or vessel registry and AIS/GPS itinerary log demonstrating operation outside New Zealand
  • Charter Party Agreement (Barecon or time-charter form) identifying the parties, hire amount, charter period, trade area, and non-resident charterer status
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT advice evidencing receipt of hire proceeds in a permitted foreign currency
  • Non-resident status certificate, including a tax residence certificate issued by the charterer's jurisdiction and an IRS Form W-8BEN-E style declaration (where applicable)
  • Vessel nationality or registry certificate and navigation records demonstrating deployment exclusively beyond the 12-nautical-mile territorial sea

New Zealand imposes no continuous transaction control (CTC), real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping requirement. E-invoicing through the Peppol framework is voluntary and coordinated by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE). GST returns must be filed electronically through the myIR portal (form GST101 electronically, or IR372 for paper-based filers). Tax invoices must contain all prescribed particulars, including supplier and recipient identity, invoice date, description of supply, GST-exclusive value, GST amount, and the applicable zero-rating basis. Digital tax records must be retained for at least seven years.

Zero-rated export claims are a high-risk audit focus for Inland Revenue, which applies risk algorithms targeting repeated refunds, related-party charter arrangements, round-tripping of hire proceeds, and vessels returning to New Zealand waters. Where zero-rating is incorrectly claimed, the full charter hire is assessed to GST at 15% together with use-of-money interest from the due date of each affected GST return. Shortfall penalties apply under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% of the tax shortfall for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% or criminal prosecution for evasion, with fines and imprisonment of up to five years. Where the vessel is subsequently used within New Zealand and the supplier knew or ought to have known of the intended New Zealand use, a retrospective clawback extends to all prior GST periods, the supplier's GST registration may be cancelled, and the supplier may be prohibited from making zero-rated supplies for a period of up to five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates an open, fully convertible capital and current account with no bilaterally or unilaterally imposed licenses, approvals, quotas, or prior-deposit requirements on the New Zealand dollar. Current account transactions — charter hire, towage, offshore platform supply fees, vessel bareboat lease consideration, marine insurance premiums, crew wages, and third-party disbursement reimbursements — are freely remittable through any Registered Bank (defined under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989) and are not subject to any convertibility certification. Capital account items, including share subscription consideration, subordinated shareholder loans, and capital repatriation on liquidation, are equally convertible; the only gate is the Overseas Investment Act threshold for 'significant business assets' (NZ$100 million for investors from OECD/CPTPP jurisdictions, NZ$200 million for non-OECD/CPTPP investors) and a separate NZ$100 million 'strategic assets' test for military/dual-use technology, media, or critical national infrastructure. The ISIC 5222 offshore marine charter trade does not fall within any sensitive strategic class. Profit remittance channels are unencumbered, but the payer must withhold Non-resident Withholding Tax (NRWT): 15% on fully imputed dividends (30% on unimputed dividends), 15% on interest (reducible to 0% under the Approved Issuer Levy at 2% per annum for registered securities, or to 10% under applicable double taxation agreements), and 5% on royalties. Return of contributed capital is a non-taxable capital repayment and may be remitted on presentation of a share cancellation or capital reduction resolution under the Companies Act 1993; no Central Bank permission or FX conversion certificate is required. The IC is fully entitled to hold USD, EUR, JPY, and AUD operating account balances with no aggregate cap imposed by regulation, although the RBNZ requires banks to report aggregate foreign-currency positional data for balance-of-payments compilation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation window, or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts or offshore service income. The IC may lawfully retain 100% of charter hire and vessel lease payments in a foreign-currency bank account with an NZ Registered Bank, or convert the proceeds to NZD at any commercial rate, at its sole discretion. The only 'surrender-like' discipline is statistical: under the Statistics Act 2022 and the RBNZ/Statistics New Zealand Balance of Payments survey, receipts above NZ$1,000,000 in a calendar month from non-residents must be classified in the monthly International Transactions form using the OECD BPM6 code for marine transport services (code 310 / 320), and must be reported within 15 business days of month-end. There is no minimum export proceeds conversion or any required transfer to a designated account. The operative compliance timeline is therefore not a surrender rule but a banking verification rule: for a first inbound charter-hire swipe, the paying bank will perform a 5-business-day 'clearing verification' hold on amounts above NZ$10,000 where the underlying charter party, bill of lading, and International Safety Management (ISM) certificate are required; subsequent receipts for recognised counterparties clear T+1. Under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act 1996, the bank must report to the NZ Police Financial Intelligence Unit any suspicion of proceeds of crime without a threshold, and any cash transaction above NZ$10,000, but this creates no restriction on the IC's retention or use of foreign exchange.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) is the sole monetary and prudential supervisory authority under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021. Foreign exchange administration is deliberately liberalised: New Zealand observes IMF Article VIII status and maintains no dedicated exchange-control authority. The Overseas Investment Office (OIO), an operational unit of Land Information New Zealand, administers foreign direct investment screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 and the Overseas Investment Regulations 2019, covering sensitive land, significant business assets, fishing quota, and strategic assets. AML/CFT supervision of the banking and treasury sector is exercised jointly by the RBNZ, the Financial Markets Authority (FMA), and the Department of Internal Affairs under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary capital escrow and statutory maritime client-funds; MEDIUM for ancillary operational disbursements. Offshore digital EMIs and non-bank payment institutions (including Airwallex, Wise Business, WorldFirst, and PayPal Treasury) are not Registered Banks under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989 and cannot hold the statutory trust designation required for maritime liabilities under s. 386 of the Maritime Transport Act 1994 or for crew-wage protection under the Maritime Labour Convention 2006. Any EMI servicing the CK_IC must be registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and must appoint a New Zealand AML/CFT compliance officer under the AML/CFT Act 2009. The EMI may lawfully support sub-ledgers for bunker payments, port dues, and ship-chandler settlements, provided the EMI maintains its own settlement account at an Australian-owned NZ Registered Bank and the IC's master treasury ledger is the primary NZ bank account. The IC must not permit the EMI to act as the receiving institution for share-subscription capital or as the paying agent for crew wages, because the resulting audit trail would fail the RBNZ's prudential expectation that all non-resident-origin treasury flows are traceable to a prudentially supervised legal entity in New Zealand.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 (cross-border related-party transactions, including vessel charter hire, fuel, crew services and loan balances, exceeding this aggregate annual threshold require contemporaneous Local File and Master File, or equivalent OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation). Below this threshold, the Inland Revenue may still require documentation where a pricing divergence exceeds NZD 1 million in taxable income or NZD 100,000 in tax.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply the Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the general anti-avoidance rule in Part GA of the Income Tax Act 2007 and the judicial sham doctrine require an arrangement to have genuine commercial substance. A Cook Islands International Company that is interposed solely to hold legal title over an offshore supply vessel while all commercial decision-making, chartering, crew payroll and risk control is exercised by New Zealand personnel can be disregarded for New Zealand tax purposes. The income will then be re-attributed to the substance-bearing New Zealand entity and reassessed at the 28% corporate rate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: A civil shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax is imposed under sections 141B and 141E of the Tax Administration Act 1994, plus use-of-money interest. Criminal prosecution under section 143A carries fines of up to NZD 100,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years for wilful evasion; fraudulent tax evasion under section 144A carries fines of up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Where fraud or a sham is proven, the corporate veil will be pierced. Directors are personally liable for the company's PAYE/GST and vessel-charter tax debts under sections 172D to 172H of the Tax Administration Act 1994, may be subjected to travel bans under the Serious Fraud Office Act 1990, and may be sentenced to imprisonment where they knowingly participate in or direct the evasion.
Sham Offshore Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The arrangement may be void for tax purposes under Part GA of the Income Tax Act 2007; the Commissioner is entitled to reconstruct the income as taxable in New Zealand, apply the 28% corporate rate, impose an abusive tax position shortfall penalty of 100% of the resulting tax, and deny the participation exemption or foreign loss offsets claimed by the non-resident entity.

Director personal liability: Directors approving the sham charter documentation are jointly and severally liable for the reconstructed New Zealand tax under the Companies Act 1993 where the company is unable to pay, may be prohibited from managing a company for up to 5 years, and may face criminal liability if they knowingly assisted in the tax avoidance.
AML/CFT and International Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a body corporate may be fined up to NZD 4 million and individuals up to NZD 200,000 for each non-compliance. Breaches involving designated sanctioned persons under the Sanctions Act 2021 carry penalties of up to NZD 1 million and imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly authorise or cause the AML/CFT failure are subject to separate criminal liability, including pecuniary penalties and imprisonment. The Serious Fraud Office can also obtain departure prohibition orders to prevent a director from leaving New Zealand during the investigation.
