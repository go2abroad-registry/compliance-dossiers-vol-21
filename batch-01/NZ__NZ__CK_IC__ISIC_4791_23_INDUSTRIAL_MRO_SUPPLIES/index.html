Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under the Companies Act 1993, the registered office must be a physical street address in New Zealand and cannot be a P.O. Box. A virtual office with a dedicated physical New Zealand street address, mail forwarding, and registered-agent service is technically permissible for incorporation. However, New Zealand banks under AML/CFT customer due diligence ordinarily require a verifiable operating location or a substantive management relationship and may reject a pure commercial-mailbox profile for an ISIC 4791 B2B MRO e-commerce business. Inland Revenue may also scrutinize alleged tax residency and GST registration where there is no local operational substance. For this low-risk industry, a virtual office is acceptable if supported by a written office-service agreement, local directorship, and credible records evidencing management and control in New Zealand.

New Zealand Companies Office (Companies Register), a business unit of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), operating the online register at https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz and supporting the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) scheme.

Governing statute

The Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), principally Sections 15–20 (incorporation and registration), Section 26 (constitution), Sections 128–130 (registered office and address for service), Sections 152–156 (director requirements and board proceedings), and Part VII (financial reporting); supplemented by the Companies (Registration) Regulations 1994 and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) framework. The entity is constituted as a New Zealand-incorporated International Company (IC) with limited liability, designed for non-resident ownership and intended to qualify for Tax Exempt Zone treatment under the Income Tax Act 2007 with respect to foreign-sourced income of the company for non-resident shareholders.

Application for Registration of a Company (Form 2) submitted electronically on the NZ Companies Register portal, together with the Company Constitution (or the statutory default rules of Table 1 of the Companies Act 1993) and the Consents to Act as Director (Form 3) for each proposed director.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (Te Tari Taake), New Zealand Government. Online portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir/)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident or tax-exempt international company seeking to establish a tax presence in New Zealand must first obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) via the New Zealand Companies Office if it carries on business in New Zealand, and then apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number. The standard application path for a foreign-incorporated or offshore-registered entity is Form IR742 (Registration for income tax and GST for non-resident companies) or the online non-resident company registration through myIR. The applicant must provide certified constitutional documents, board resolutions confirming the New Zealand agent or place of business, proof of registered office and director details, and a completed IRD number application. IRD numbers are generally issued within 5–10 working days. GST registration, if required or elected, is effected on the same IRD number through myIR or Form GST1A.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime. The CK_IC, as an International Company under the applicable tax-exempt offshore regime, is automatically treated as a tax-exempt non-resident entity for New Zealand tax purposes: it is not subject to New Zealand income tax on foreign-source income and is not assessed on income derived outside New Zealand, provided it is not managed and controlled in New Zealand. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exports applies to supplies of goods that are exported from New Zealand by the supplier, or supplied and delivered directly to a place outside New Zealand. For B2B electronic-catalog sales of industrial MRO hardware, the supply will be zero-rated if the supplier retains evidence that the goods left New Zealand within 12 months of the supply and that the customer is outside New Zealand. Services supplied to non-residents who are outside New Zealand at the time of supply and that are supplied in respect of goods situated outside New Zealand may also be zero-rated. The zero-rating is available only where the supplier has obtained the export documentation prescribed by Inland Revenue and the New Zealand Customs Service.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Export Entry (Cargo Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the ocean or air carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of funds in foreign currency from the overseas purchaser
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Delivery confirmation or signed proof of arrival at the overseas destination
  • Copy of the customer's order and record of the offshore shipping terms

New Zealand has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. GST returns and tax invoices are filed electronically through myIR or approved software. Use of the New Zealand Peppol e-Invoicing network is voluntary. Inland Revenue API services permit software providers to transmit GST returns and supporting data, but there is no government pre-clearance of invoices before issue.

Inland Revenue and the New Zealand Customs Service closely audit exported-supply claims. If the exporter cannot substantiate actual export or if goods are returned to New Zealand or diverted to domestic use, the zero-rating is retrospectively reversed and the supplier becomes liable for output GST at 15% on the original consideration, plus use-of-money interest. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% to 150% for evasion or fraudulent GST claims. Directors may be held personally liable for GST clawback and penalties in sham-export arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised and freely convertible currency regime under the Currency Act 1983 and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, with no exchange-control legislation applicable to current-account or capital-account transactions. The New Zealand Dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both the onshore and offshore markets, and there are no restrictions on the repatriation of dividends, branch profits, loan repayments, royalties, or invested capital. Non-resident foreign-currency accounts (USD, EUR, AUD, GBP, CNH, JPY) are routinely permitted without central-bank approval. Profit remittance from a New Zealand operational subsidiary or branch is subject only to Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) at treaty rates (typically 15% on dividends, 10% on interest, 5% on royalties under the NZ–Cook Islands double tax arrangements) and standard AML/CFT source-of-funds verification; no quantitative or temporal restrictions apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in New Zealand to surrender, deposit, or convert export proceeds into NZD. Exporters are free to retain foreign-currency receipts in any authorised foreign-currency account or to remit funds globally without central-bank conversion directives. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand and Stats NZ require only statistical reporting of cross-border merchandise trade (Overseas Merchandise Trade dataset) once cumulative values exceed the monthly reporting threshold, and all inward/outward international wire transfers are subject to the transaction-monitoring under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. No mandatory conversion ratio, time limit, or exchange-rate obligation is imposed on the export sale of industrial MRO supplies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — New Zealand's central bank and prudential regulator; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) jointly supervise AML/CFT obligations for banks, money remitters, and financial service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for supplementary clearing rails, NOT a replacement for a Tier 1 transactional account. Off-shore digital financial institutions and EMIs — including Wise (originally NZ-founded), Airwallex, and Revolut Business — can provide efficient cross-border collection/payout channels for the B2B MRO e-commerce platform, including virtual USD/EUR receivables accounts and discounted supplier wire pricing. Legal constraints: (i) any EMI that performs money-remittance services in New Zealand must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and must appoint a NZ compliance officer under the AML/CFT Act 2009; (ii) an off-shore EMI without a NZ-licensed entity must rely on its home-country passporting or on a NZ money-changers licence, and settlement of merchant proceeds is normally restricted to a fully onboarded NZ bank account in the same legal name; (iii) the Cook Islands International Company (CK_IC) will be subject to full beneficial-ownership disclosure and source-of-funds verification by the EMI, and any threshold-based transaction limits may trigger enhanced due diligence, especially for high-velocity card-plus-wire flows.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for supplementary clearing rails, NOT a replacement for a Tier 1 transactional account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand Inland Revenue's administrative transfer pricing documentation threshold: an aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeding NZD 10 million per income year triggers the expectation that a Local File be prepared and retained. A Master File is expected where the multinational group's total consolidated revenue exceeds NZD 100 million, and Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue exceeding approximately NZD 1.13 billion. Despite these administrative thresholds, the Tax Administration Act 1994 s 51(4)(c) requires every taxpayer to retain sufficient transfer pricing records to enable the Commissioner to verify that related-party dealings are on an arm's length basis, even where no contemporaneous Local File is prepared.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) analogous to those of offshore financial centres; however, the general anti-avoidance rule in ITA 2007 s BG 1, the purpose test in s GA 1, the sham doctrine and the central management and control test operate to deny tax advantages where an entity lacks commercial substance. A Cook Islands IC holding MRO e-commerce income without a real office, employees, decision-making autonomy or direct operational function will be challenged. The explicit tax-exempt classification of the Cook Islands IC does not extend to New Zealand-source income, and if the IC is managed and controlled from New Zealand it will be treated as a New Zealand-resident company taxable on its worldwide income at 28%.
