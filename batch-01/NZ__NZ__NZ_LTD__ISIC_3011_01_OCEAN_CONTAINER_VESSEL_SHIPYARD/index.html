Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand, online portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity incorporated or registering to carry on business in New Zealand as a limited company must first be registered on the New Zealand Companies Register via the Companies Office (NZBN issuance mandatory). With the NZBN, the company applies to Inland Revenue for a New Zealand tax file number (IRD number) and simultaneously registers for Goods and Services Tax (GST), pay-as-you-earn (PAYE), and other relevant tax types. Application may be made through the myIR portal using the NZBN, Companies Office details, and a New Zealand registered office address. Non-resident directors or shareholders require a New Zealand address for service and standard identity verification documentation. No annual minimum capital is required for a limited company.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand has no check-the-box election equivalent for standalone companies; a New Zealand limited company is a separate tax resident entity subject to company tax on worldwide income if incorporated in New Zealand, with available group loss offset elections and consolidated group elections under subpart IG of the Income Tax Act 2007. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections FH 1 to FH 11 apply with an elective deemed-ordering mechanism for dual-inclusion income; limited partnerships and look-through companies may elect look-through treatment, but a standard NZ Ltd remains at entity level with no transparent election for general tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 actual or expected taxable supplies in any 12-month period; non-residents not carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand may register voluntarily to claim input tax credits, subject to the physical-non-resident business-to-business registration rules under section 54B of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported goods where the supplier removes them from New Zealand or supplies them on a ship or aircraft engaged in international carrying on, as supplied to a GST-registered recipient outside New Zealand for goods exported within 28 days or within such extended period as the provider allows; services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of performance and the services are not supplied in connection with New Zealand land or moveable personal property. For shipbuilding, a supply to a non-resident of a newly manufactured ocean-going commercial vessel is zero-rated as exported goods where the vessel is delivered outside New Zealand territorial waters and ISIC 3011 shipyard substantiation must evidence economic destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent ship delivery certificate
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / FX receipt evidence

No real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoice clearance mandate is in operation for domestic GST supplies. Electronic invoicing (e-invoicing) is permitted under the Peppol BIS 3.0 framework; buyers and sellers may use accredited Peppol service providers. GST returns are filed electronically via myIR or intermediary software under pay-return frequency (six-monthly, two-monthly, or monthly based on turnover). No digital stamping, continuous transaction controls, or tax authority pre-clearance API is required.

The Inland Revenue Department may require production of export documentation and shipping records to substantiate zero-rated treatment. Where zero-rating is claimed without genuine export or where documentary requirements are not met, the amount becomes taxable at 15% GST with use-of-money interest and late payment penalties; knowingly making a false GST return may attract shortfall penalties up to 150% of the tax shortfall, and intentional evasion may incur criminal prosecution and fines up to NZD 50,000 for an individual or NZD 250,000 for a company, plus disgorgement or reaffirmation of tax liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand Dollar (NZD) is a freely convertible currency operating under a floating exchange-rate regime with no exchange control legislation. Since the abolition of exchange controls in 1984, New Zealand has imposed no restrictions on current account or capital account convertibility, and no prior approval or licence is required to purchase foreign currency, remit dividends, repatriate paid-up capital, pay intercompany debt, or settle cross-border trade obligations. Profit remittance is subject only to general tax compliance: non-resident withholding tax of 15 percent on dividends, or the approved issuer levy of 2 percent on interest obligations, in each case subject to applicable double-tax-agreement relief. A separate screening regime under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 applies to foreign investment in sensitive land, significant business assets, and fishing quota; a shipyard leasehold or freehold landholding may trigger Overseas Investment Office consent, but such screening is not a currency-control mechanism and does not restrict convertibility once consent is obtained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory exporter surrender or repatriation obligations in New Zealand. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 confers no power to compel the conversion of export receipts into NZD, and no prescribed timeline or minimum mandatory conversion ratio exists. A commercial shipyard may lawfully retain foreign-currency export proceeds offshore or onshore, deploy them to fund imported equipment and subcontractor payments, or convert them to NZD at commercial rates, subject only to its internal treasury policy and the balance-of-payments statistical reporting obligations of its banking provider.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ), exercising statutory authority pursuant to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 (as amended). RBNZ is the licensing and prudential supervision authority for registered banks and non-bank deposit takers, and together with the Financial Markets Authority and the Department of Internal Affairs, supervises reporting entities under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 for AML/CFT compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium viability as an operational and FX payment enhancement, Low viability as a substitute for regulated commercial banking infrastructure. Digital payment and cross-border EMI providers operating in New Zealand, including Airwallex, Wise Platform, OFX, and Convera, may lawfully service a New Zealand corporate if registered as Financial Service Providers under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, admitted to an approved dispute-resolution scheme, and compliant with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 as reporting entities. No EMI is licensed as a registered bank by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand; therefore EMIs cannot issue Commercial Letters of Credit, hold client escrow funds in a bankruptcy-remote custodial structure, or provide committed borrowing facilities. For a medium-risk shipyard treasury handling large USD-denominated vessel installments, an EMI should be used only for niche operational disbursements and spot FX efficiency, while capital escrow, trade settlement, and liquidity lines remain with a registered commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward equity capital must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 for ordinary customer credit transfers or SWIFT MT202 COV for bank-to-bank cover payments, directed to the New Zealand operating account or the capital escrow account held with a registered commercial bank. The ordering customer should be the direct foreign parent or the disclosed beneficial owner, and the payment narrative must state equity subscription or share capital contribution with a cross-reference to the board resolution and share-register entry. New Zealand banks capture transaction purpose for balance-of-payments statistics, so the remitter should classify the funds under the BPM6 direct investment in enterprises equity category. Where the shipyard land is sensitive under the Overseas Investment Act 2005, evidence of consent from the Overseas Investment Office must be provided to the bank before funds are applied. No exchange-control approval is required for conversion of the incoming USD or other currency into NZD, and conversion may be executed at same-day market rates without restriction.Approved clearing rails are as follows: (i) standard subcontractor, materials, classification-society, and expatriate payroll payments via SWIFT MT103 with UETR tracking across the SWIFT gpi network; (ii) Commercial Letter of Credit settlements issued via SWIFT MT700 with MT707 amendments and MT720 transferability where intermediate equipment suppliers are integrated; (iii) documentary collections via MT400 and MT412 for lower-value equipment shipments; (iv) Demand Guarantee and Advance Payment Bond issuance via SWIFT MT760 to shipowner beneficiaries; (v) domestic New Zealand legs cleared through the New Zealand systemically important payment system operated by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand, permitting same-day settlement of NZD; and (vi) USD-denominated streams settled through nostro accounts at the major New Zealand banks' offshore USD hubs, enabling T+1 gross settlement without forced NZD conversion.Outright and vanilla European-style NZD/USD foreign-exchange forwards executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a bank FX Master Agreement, supported by genuine commercial exposure documented in the shipbuilding installment schedule.Foreign-exchange swaps and FX rollovers used to manage timing mismatches between NZD operating funding and USD contract receipts without creating speculative open positions.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CIRS) converting NZD floating-rate shipyard borrowing into USD or alternative-currency fixed or floating obligations aligned to the vessel construction program.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) settled in NZD, permitted only where the underlying exposure is denominated in a non-deliverable or restricted currency of a counterparty jurisdiction and a genuine payable or receivable is evidenced; such instruments are lawful for New Zealand corporates when transacted with registered banks under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013.FX options and option-combination strategies, including collars, participating forwards, and zero-premium structures, employed to cap conversion cost on future USD installment receipts while preserving upside participation, subject to a board-approved hedging policy.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Total income (turnover, interest, dividends, etc.) Report all New Zealand-sourced income; foreign income may be exempt under DTA or domestic law. Late filing penalty of NZD 250 for each month or part month (up to NZD 1,000); late payment penalty of 1% per month plus interest on unpaid tax.
Box 2 Total expenses (deductible business costs) Claim only expenses directly incurred in deriving assessable income; ensure arm's length pricing for related-party transactions. Late filing penalty of NZD 250 for each month or part month (up to NZD 1,000); late payment penalty of 1% per month plus interest on unpaid tax.
Box 3 Net taxable income (Box 1 minus Box 2) Apply any available tax losses carried forward; ensure continuity of ownership rules are met. Late filing penalty of NZD 250 for each month or part month (up to NZD 1,000); late payment penalty of 1% per month plus interest on unpaid tax.
Box 4 Tax payable (28% of Box 3) Offset any imputation credits or foreign tax credits; provisional tax payments already made are credited. Late filing penalty of NZD 250 for each month or part month (up to NZD 1,000); late payment penalty of 1% per month plus interest on unpaid tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 100 million for Master File and Local File; NZD 1.1 billion for Country-by-Country Report.

Economic substance scope: No specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) exist in New Zealand, but general anti-avoidance rules (Part G of the Income Tax Act 2007) and treaty abuse provisions require that the entity has genuine commercial substance and that transactions are not structured primarily for tax avoidance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall; criminal prosecution with fines up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participate in evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reassessment of income, and imposition of shortfall penalties up to 100% of the tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if the company is deemed a sham; potential disqualification as a director.
AML/CFT Violation

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to NZD 2 million for companies; criminal penalties up to NZD 300,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for AML/CFT breaches if they were knowingly involved; travel bans and restrictions on business activities may apply.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, shortfall penalty of 20% to 40% of the tax shortfall, and interest on unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they were aware of the mispricing and did not take corrective action; potential criminal liability for fraudulent transfer pricing.
