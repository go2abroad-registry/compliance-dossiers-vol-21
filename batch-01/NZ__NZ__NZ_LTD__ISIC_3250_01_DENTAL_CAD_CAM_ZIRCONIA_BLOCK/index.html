Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow capital account are required under New Zealand law. A limited liability company may be incorporated with one share and zero nominal paid capital. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue), Government of New Zealand; electronic portal: myIR, https://www.ird.govt.nz/myIR

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident person forming NZ Ltd: (1) incorporate the company with the Registrar of Companies at the New Zealand Companies Office and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) and certificate of incorporation; (2) apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number (the national tax identification number) through the myIR business registration channel, stating the entity is a New Zealand limited company and identifying directors, shareholders, registered office and underlying control relationships; (3) using the same IRD number, register for GST under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and select a filing period; (4) register as an employer for PAYE if any New Zealand employees are engaged, and consider FBT for non-cash benefits; (5) open a New Zealand bank account in the company's name to facilitate export proceeds and tax payments. A non-resident company without a fixed New Zealand place of business may still register for GST using the IRD number; no local director or residency requirement is imposed for tax ID issuance, but the Companies Office requires a registered office in New Zealand and must record directors and shareholders in the public register.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign non-resident person forming NZ Ltd: (1) incorporate the company with the Registrar of Companies at the New Zealand Companies Office and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) and certificate of incorporation; (2) apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number (the national tax identification number) through the myIR business registration channel, stating the entity is a New Zealand limited company and identifying directors, shareholders, registered office and underlying control relationships; (3) using the same IRD number, register for GST under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and select a filing period; (4) register as an employer for PAYE if any New Zealand employees are engaged, and consider FBT for non-cash benefits; (5) open a New Zealand bank account in the company's name to facilitate export proceeds and tax payments. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for entity-level classification; New Zealand does not offer check-the-box or comparable classification elections. The NZ limited company is per se a company taxable as a separate entity. Voluntary GST registration is available below the NZD 60,000 threshold, and hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act 2007 may require a taxpayer to self-disclose mismatches, but no optional fiscal classification election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; mandatory registration when actual or expected turnover exceeds NZD 60,000, with voluntary registration permitted below the threshold. The same threshold applies to non-resident suppliers supplying goods or services to New Zealand unless specific remote-services rules apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods qualifies for 0% GST if the NZ supplier physically exports the goods from New Zealand and retains sufficient evidence of export as required by Inland Revenue under section 11(1)(a) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. The dental CAD/CAM zirconia blocks must be shipped by the manufacturer to a foreign customer and title must pass outside New Zealand. Services supplied directly to a non-resident outside New Zealand, including digital or offshore services, may also be zero-rated under the appropriate section. Supplies cannot be zero-rated merely on the basis of a foreign purchaser's address; actual export and offshore payment are essential.

  • New Zealand Customs Service export declaration (CUSDEC) identifying the exporter, consignee, goods classification and destination
  • Original or authenticated Bill of Lading / sea waybill or air waybill evidencing physical departure of the goods from a New Zealand port or airport
  • Commercial invoice issued by NZ Ltd in the name of the overseas buyer showing zero-rated GST treatment and including the supplier IRD number and NZBN
  • Packing list and cargo manifest identifying the dental CAD/CAM zirconia blocks by quantity and weight
  • Bank foreign-currency receipt / SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer confirmation or New Zealand bank credit advice showing the offshore buyer remitted settlement to the NZ company bank account

New Zealand has no real-time fiscal clearance system, mandatory e-invoicing, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime. E-invoicing via the PEPPOL network is voluntary and supported by Inland Revenue. GST invoices may be paper or electronic and must include the words 'tax invoice', seller's name and IRD number, recipient's name and address, date of issue, description of goods or services, amount excluding GST, GST amount, and total. For zero-rated export supplies the invoice may state the supply is zero-rated rather than show a GST amount; the supplier must retain all supporting export evidence.

Inland Revenue may audit export zero-rating claims and claw back the 15% GST if the exporter fails to produce documentary evidence that goods left New Zealand and that export proceeds were repatriated. A false or unsupported zero-rating claim results in an assessment of GST at 15% on the supply, late payment interest, and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for lack of reasonable care or unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% for evasion or abuse. Knowingly issuing false GST records can also attract criminal sanctions. Exporters should retain the full evidence bundle for at least seven years after the tax return period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully open and convertible capital account for ordinary business transactions. The New Zealand dollar is a free-floating currency under IMF Article VIII status, which prohibits exchange restrictions on current international transactions and discriminatory currency practices. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand is the national monetary authority and prudential supervisor; it does not issue foreign-exchange permits, does not operate a currency surrender regime, and does not restrict conversion of NZD for legitimate trade or investment. Inbound equity contributions, intercompany loans, trade receivables and dividend remittances can be converted freely at prevailing market rates through any authorised bank. There is no mandatory sell-down of export receipts, and no deadline for converting hard currency back to NZD. Because there is no FX surrender, an NZ manufacturing company can retain USD, EUR or CNY balances to fund future raw-material imports. The only real profit-remittance hurdles are corporate-law and tax-law driven: directors must sign solvency certificates for any dividend distribution, and a non-resident shareholder will incur non-resident withholding tax on dividends, statutorily 30 percent but usually reduced to 10 to 15 percent under tax treaties. The bank also expects clean evidence of source funds, a country-of-origin narrative, and complete supporting contracts to avoid unintended AML escalation. Capital account restrictions under the Overseas Investment Act apply only to sensitive land or significant business assets, not to a dental technology manufacturing shell or trading subsidiary.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirements apply. New Zealand does not mandate the repatriation or conversion of export proceeds into NZD. There is no statutory timeline for converting foreign currency receipts, and the operator may retain 100 percent of its export revenue in a foreign-currency account with a New Zealand bank. For proceeds over NZD 10,000 in cash, a border cash report is required under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009; for wire transfers, the transaction narrative must identify the underlying dental block export invoice so that the receiving bank can apply normal customer due diligence.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

In-depth feasibility rating is 6 out of 10 for ancillary payment use and 3 out of 10 as the primary treasury host. An offshore digital EMI is not legally barred from servicing a New Zealand entity, but it must be registered as a reporting entity under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 if it operates inside New Zealand, and if it operates purely offshore, a New Zealand Tier-1 bank will classify it as a high-risk non-bank financial institution. An EMI cannot offer NZD deposit-taking, trade finance, documentary letters of credit, or access to Reserve Bank of New Zealand settlement channels unless it holds a New Zealand banking or deposit-taking licence. The NZ manufacturing entity still needs a Tier-1 bank for statutory capital verification, audit confirmations, and documentary trade. An EMI is therefore best used for low-value cross-border supplier payments after enhanced due diligence, and never for the primary equity escrow or final FX clearing rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand Inland Revenue expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZD 10 million, or where related-party dealings involve significant risk of mispricing, including intercompany purchases of zirconia raw materials, manufacturing services, sales commission, royalty and financing arrangements.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the general anti-avoidance rule in the Income Tax Act 2007 and transfer pricing rules require that the entity undertaking the transaction has real operational substance in New Zealand. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for a dental block manufacturer include procurement of raw material, CAD/CAM software control, milling and sintering, quality control, inventory management, invoicing, and risk assumption. A shell company with no employees, no premises, and no local decision-making will be recharacterised and exposed to reconstructed pricing and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Administration Act 1994 s 141, a shortfall penalty equal to 150% of the tax shortfall applies for evasion or similar default. Criminal prosecution can be brought under s 143A and the Crimes Act 1961, exposing fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced; directors who authorise false returns become jointly and severally liable for tax and penalties, may have assets frozen by IRD enforcement, may be placed on border alert or travel restriction lists, and may be personally imprisoned for tax fraud.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1 and s GB 1, Inland Revenue can reconstruct the arrangement and allocate profits to the New Zealand entity. Abusive tax avoidance shortfall penalties of 100% under Tax Administration Act 1994 s 141D may apply. Companies Office may strike off the company and prohibit the directors from managing companies under the Companies Act 1993.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the company to operate with zero substance face personal civil liability for unpaid company taxes, equitable damages for reckless trading, director banning, asset clawback actions, and potential travel restrictions pending resolution of tax and creditor claims.
AML/CFT Breach and Facilitated Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a body corporate may be subject to criminal penalties up to NZD 2 million for serious AML/CFT breaches; individual officers and directors may be fined up to NZD 200,000. Money laundering under Crimes Act 1961 s 243 can result in imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for AML/CFT compliance may be personally fined, disqualified from acting as directors, held civilly liable for losses caused by failing to monitor suspicious transactions, subjected to international mutual legal assistance travel bans, and imprisoned upon conviction for money laundering.
