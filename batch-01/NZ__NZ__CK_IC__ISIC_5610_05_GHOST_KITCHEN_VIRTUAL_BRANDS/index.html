Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office (NZCO), a business unit of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE); online portal at companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), specifically sections 12 (incorporation by registration), 15 (constitution), 16 (manner of incorporation), 23 (share capital and classes of shares), 37 (financial assistance for acquisition of shares, where applicable), and 122 (appointment of directors). Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake), Government of New Zealand; principal statutes: Income Tax Act 2007, Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, Tax Administration Act 1994; primary digital portal: myIR (www.ird.govt.nz); export customs formalities are separately administered by the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated International Company (CK_IC) that has no fixed establishment in New Zealand must apply to the IRD for a non-resident IRD number (the statutory 8- or 9-digit tax file number) using Form IR742, accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, full board and ownership particulars, AML/CFT beneficial-ownership information, and evidence of tax residence in the jurisdiction of incorporation; the IRD number is the single taxpayer identifier for income tax, GST and PAYE; GST registration is made on Form IR356 and is mandatory once taxable supplies made in or from New Zealand exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period; a non-resident may also be obliged to register for offshore remote-services GST from the first dollar where supplying remote services to New Zealand consumers; registration must be completed before the first taxable period return falls due; engagement of a NZ-resident agent for service of process and the use of licensed tax agents for myIR filing are strongly recommended.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the CK_IC International Company is classified as a non-resident, non-dual-resident corporate vehicle with no New Zealand fixed establishment or permanent establishment; it is accordingly outside the New Zealand income-tax base for all foreign-sourced income and is taxable only on income having a New Zealand source (standard corporate rate 28% under the Income Tax Act 2007); the label denotes the model classification for this non-residence-driven exemption and does not imply any statutory freeport or territorial tax-incentive zone within New Zealand.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; available statutory elections include: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 for closely held NZ-resident companies electing full pass-through tax treatment; (2) the irrevocable non-residence election inherent in the IC template where central management and control remain offshore; (3) New Zealand Foreign Trust (NZFT) election under the Tax Administration Act 1994, exempting the trustee from NZ income tax on foreign-sourced income of a non-resident settlor; (4) Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) election to reduce non-resident withholding tax on interest paid to offshore lenders; (5) hybrid-mismatch countermeasures imposed by Subpart FM of the Income Tax Act 2007 (deduction/no-inclusion, double-deduction and imported-mismatch rules), which eliminate any elective benefit from hybrid instruments or hybrid entities; no dual-consolidation or optional transparency other than LTC is available to the CK_IC structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 per rolling 12-month period for persons carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand (section 51, GST Act 1985); voluntary registration is permitted below this threshold; non-resident suppliers of remote services (electronically supplied services) to NZ consumers must register from the first dollar; non-resident suppliers of low-value goods (goods valued NZD 1,000 and below) must register only where supplies to NZ consumers exceed NZD 60,000 per annum; for the ghost-kitchen model, domestic NZ food and beverage sales through UberEats and DoorDash payouts are taxable supplies subject to the standard NZD 60,000 threshold unless the sales are exported.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% GST is available under section 11 of the GST Act 1985 for: (a) goods physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier where title passes outside NZ; (b) services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of performance, subject to exclusions for services connected with NZ land, services physically performed on NZ goods, and services supplied to a non-resident physically present in NZ for enjoyment in NZ; and (c) services supplied in a taxable activity that form part of a through-out chain to an offshore principal; for the ISIC 5610 ghost-kitchen/virtual-brand scope: local meal sales fulfilled by a NZ kitchen and paid via UberEats or DoorDash for NZ consumers are standard-rated at 15% GST; international shared-service fees, brand-licence fees, virtual-brand platform commissions, and management or IT back-office fees recharged to an offshore brand owner are zero-rated where the recipient is non-resident and outside NZ at the time of supply; cross-border dispatched meal kits sold to foreign buyers qualify for zero-rating only where contemporaneous export documentation substantiates physical delivery outside New Zealand.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUS-EX) filed via the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window
  • Original Bill of Lading (ocean) or Master and House Airwaybills (air) evidencing physical departure from New Zealand
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List identifying the non-resident buyer, Incoterms, HS classification and quantity
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank Foreign Exchange Credit Advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the IC's bank account
  • Certificate of Origin (NZ-China FTA, RCEP, CPTPP or NZ-EU FTA) where preferential tariff origin is claimed
  • Proof of arrival or delivery at the foreign destination (port discharge report, foreign customs entry, or third-party delivery confirmation) supporting the export GST claim

No real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance API, digital stamping, or tax-authority invoice authorisation mandate exists in New Zealand; e-invoicing is voluntary through the IRD's PEPPOL-based e-Invoicing Service (Peppol BIS 3.0 / UBL 2.1 standards); statutory tax invoices must be issued within 28 days of the supply, contain the compulsory particulars listed in section 24 of the GST Act 1985 (supplier name, IRD number, GST-exclusive amount, GST amount, date, nature of supply, and a statement that GST is included where the total exceeds NZD 1,000), and must be retained electronically for 7 years; GST returns are filed electronically through myIR or EDI channels; the IRD does not operate a stamping or clearance node on the PEPPOL network.

IRD audit scrutiny of zero-rating claims is document-intensive and substance-based; failure to substantiate the export chain results in reassessment of output GST at 15% for every affected taxable period, with shortfall penalties under section 141 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 of 20% (failure to take reasonable care), 40% (unacceptable tax position), or 150% (abusive tax position or evasion), plus use-of-money interest accruing daily on unpaid tax; fraudulent zero-rating by a ghost-kitchen/virtual-brand entity is a criminal offence risking fines up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years, in addition to retrospective clawback of all GST refunds and input credits from the date of the original assessment; the 180-day figure in this dossier is the prudential documentary benchmark for inward remittance — New Zealand's GST Act imposes no statutory FX-repatriation term, and a genuine unpaid export debt beyond 180 days will not by itself void zero-rating if the debt remains commercially real, arms-length and fully substantiated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility for NZD under a free-floating exchange-rate regime (no IMF Article XIV restrictions). No prior central-bank approval is required for inbound capital subscription, trade financing, profit repatriation, dividends, or foreign-currency advances to related parties. The practical friction points are exclusively prudential and AML/CFT-based: bank counterparty due diligence, source-of-funds substantiation, SWIFT payment references, and the tax withholding regime (non-resident withholding tax on interest/dividends). Profit remittance is unfettered once the New Zealand branch/company satisfies IRD return and RWT/NRWT obligations; a New Zealand Inland Revenue (IRD) registration and an NZBN are essential for account opening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil statutory surrender or mandatory conversion obligation in New Zealand. Exporters (including delivery-platform settlement receipts from UberEats and DoorDash) may hold USD/AUD/EUR receivables indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts without any prescribed conversion ratio or repatriation timeline. No RBNZ approval threshold has been set for retention of export proceeds; the only reporting requirement is statistical data reported to Statistics NZ on a voluntary/aggregate basis for balance-of-payments compilation, and the automatic exchange of information for CRS/FATCA tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) / Te Pūtea Matua, with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) sharing AML/CFT supervision; no administered foreign-exchange control authority exists in New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a supplementary FX/collection corridor, but LOW as a primary treasury bank. Under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989, only RBNZ-registered banks may conduct the business of banking (including accepting on-demand deposits) in New Zealand; a non-bank fintech/EMI must either partner with a registered bank (pass-through segregated accounts) or restrict itself to licensed money-changer/MSB activities regulated under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act). Cross-border EMIs with an NZ money-service-business license and a licensed trustee arrangement, e.g., Airwallex NZ or Wise Payments NZ, may accept platform settlement from UberEats/DoorDash subject to the platform's own payout-plus/USD rails, but treasury policy should treat the EMI as a transitory clearing node only, with end-of-day sweeps to the Tier-1 NZ bank account. Critical legal constraints: (i) EMI client funds must be held in a designated trust account and are not covered by the RBNZ depositor compensation scheme; (ii) EMI account applications by Cook Islands International Companies are typically declined because the EMT is unable to verify the ultimate beneficial owner at the required AML/CFT standard; (iii) any accumulated settlement must be swept before the EMI's daily balance cap to avoid an unintended 'non-bank borrowing' characterization.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million aggregate cross-border related-party controlled transactions in an income year trigger the mandatory contemporaneous New Zealand transfer pricing documentation (Local File/Master File). OECD Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue over NZD 1.14 billion (EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not execute Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the IRD applies BEPS-consistent substance requirements through controlled foreign company (CFC) rules, the general anti-avoidance rule (Income Tax Act 2007, s BG 1 and Part FE), and the Limitation of Benefits/Principal Purpose Test under NZ DTAs. An IC labelled 'Tax-Exempt Zone' will be disregarded for treaty/tax purposes if it lacks physical presence, payroll, local board meetings, and real business decision-making. The ghost-kitchen operator must maintain board minutes evidencing operational control over franchise food-preparation contracts, delivery-platform agreements, and profit repatriation; office or kitchen premises; a local New Zealand bank account; and functional employees who manage order flow, supplier contracts, and compliance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Concealment of Turnover

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty up to 150% of the tax shortfall plus late payment penalties and use-of-money interest. Criminal prosecution under Tax Administration Act 1994, s 143A may result in judgment fines up to NZ$50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years, plus forfeiture of profits.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false IR4 returns or participate in concealment may be personally prosecuted, have the corporate veil pierced under Companies Act 1993 ss 382-385, be disqualified from managing companies for up to 5 years, be subject to travel restrictions in IRD asset-preservation cases, and be jointly and severally liable for unpaid PAYE/GST and shortfall penalties.
Shell Company / Artificial Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation of the entire arrangement under s BG 1 and s GA 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007; denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; re-allocation of profits to New Zealand; TP adjustment plus a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the resulting additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the company to operate without economic substance may be jointly liable for the adjusted corporate tax debt where the company is unable to pay. The Court may order restitution, impose a civil penalty, and disqualify the director from managing any company in New Zealand.
Anti-Money Laundering / AML-CFT Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: AML/CFT civil penalties of up to NZ$4 million for a body corporate and NZ$1 million for individual directors, plus criminal liability for knowingly laundering proceeds, imprisonment up to 7 years, confiscation of assets, and public enforcement publication.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are personally liable for a failure to implement and maintain AML/CFT programmes; culpable conduct may lead to imprisonment up to 7 years, disqualification, and civil costs, and the Court may order disclosure of personal assets for confiscation.
Withholding Tax and Imputation Credit Mismatch

Statutory sanction: The payer company is assessed for the entire unwithheld NRWT, plus a non-declaration penalty of up to NZ$1,000, a 150% shortfall penalty where the failure is intentional, and late payment penalties over the full unpaid amount.

Director personal liability: The director is jointly and severally liable for unpaid NRWT and penalties to the extent they knowingly directed or participated in the dividend/royalty distribution, and may be barred from leaving New Zealand pending payment, with seizure of personal assets under tax recovery provisions.
