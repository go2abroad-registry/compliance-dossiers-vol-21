Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under the Companies Act 1993. A New Zealand company may be incorporated with one or more shares having no par value; subscribers may agree to pay nominal or zero consideration. No statutory escrow account is required, but any subscription funds received must be held by the directors and applied in accordance with the first board resolution and the company's constitution.

Funding & unlock path: For the business premises: obtain or verify fire safety certification and an evacuation scheme under the Fire Safety and Evacuation Procedures Regulations 2018 administered by Fire and Emergency New Zealand; secure a building warrant of fitness for the leased commercial space; and comply with WorkSafe New Zealand health and safety duties under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including workstation ergonomics and PPE-free software development activities.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Tauhake) – New Zealand Inland Revenue; official online portal: myIR (https://myir.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident International Company (CK_IC) first obtains an IRD number for tax purposes. For GST registration, the company files Form IR356 (Registration for Goods and Services Tax) via myIR or paper; a non-resident without a place of business in New Zealand must appoint an NZ-resident agent and file a written appointment together with the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered office address, director details, and a description of the taxable activity. For an IRD number without GST, the company files Form IR596 (Non-resident Company IRD Number Application) supported by the certificate of incorporation and evidence of the non-resident status. A non-resident IC that has no New Zealand permanent establishment and derives no New Zealand-sourced income is not required to file NZ income tax returns; however, GST registration becomes mandatory from 1 April 2025 for non-resident suppliers of remote services (including gaming anti-cheat middleware) supplied to New Zealand consumers, irrespective of turnover.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope; Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident classification) – the CK_IC is liable to NZ tax only on New Zealand-sourced income under ss YD 4 and YD 8 of the Income Tax Act 2007; offshore business profits, software licensing royalties, and anti-cheat middleware licence fees derived from non-resident customers are outside the NZ tax base and effectively exempt, provided no NZ permanent establishment exists.

Available election forms

New Zealand has no US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections relevant to an International Company: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election for closely held NZ-resident companies (generally unavailable to a non-resident IC); (2) branch exemption election for foreign PEs under s DNHS of the Income Tax Act 2007; (3) optional GST registration election for non-residents making supplies below the statutory threshold; (4) attribution election for offshore portfolio share investments under the FDR rules; (5) hybrid mismatch provisions under subpart FH are mandatory and override any elective treatment, with no elective opt-out; and (6) a deemed-residence election for a company incorporated outside NZ is not permitted where head office and central management remain in the Cook Islands or other offshore jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies made in New Zealand over any 12-month period for domestic goods and services; from 1 April 2025, non-resident suppliers of remote services (imported digital services, including gaming anti-cheat middleware and SaaS) have a nil registration threshold and must register for GST regardless of turnover; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold for suppliers with a taxable activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for 0% GST where they are physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier or delivered to a carrier for export within 28 days of the supply, or supplied to a non-resident who exports the goods. Services qualify for 0% GST when supplied to a non-resident outside New Zealand and the services are not physically performed in New Zealand, or when supplied to a GST-registered resident business for its taxable activity (B2B zero-rating). Gaming anti-cheat middleware supplied by way of software licence, SaaS, or integrity security services to non-resident gaming operators qualifies for zero-rating where the place of supply is offshore and the benefit is consumed outside New Zealand. The supplier must hold contemporaneous documentary evidence of export, non-residence, and offshore consumption at the time the return is filed.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged via the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window (ECMS/Tradelink), including the exporter code and Customs Entry Number
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing loading at a New Zealand port/airport and delivery to a foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list identifying the exported goods, software deliverables or hardware, and the foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank advice evidencing full settlement of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Signed contract or licence agreement for the gaming anti-cheat engine confirming the foreign counterparty, place of supply, and offshore consumption
  • Certificate of Non-Residence or IRD letter confirming the supplier's status for zero-rating of services

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping regime exists in New Zealand. Inland Revenue requires GST returns and relevant schedules to be filed electronically via myIR. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Peppol network, with phased B2G e-invoicing mandates planned but not yet operative. Tax invoices must comply with s 24 of the GST Act 1985, include the supplier's IRD/GST registration number, the date, description, quantity, and amount of consideration, and be issued within 28 days of supply. No government pre-clearance or API-based clearance of invoices is required.

Zero-rating claims are scrutinised by Inland Revenue under s 11 of the GST Act 1985. Where export documentation is defective, the supply is recharacterised as a domestic supply, or the recipient is found to be a New Zealand resident, the 15% GST is assessed as a tax shortfall together with use-of-money interest (prescribed rate ~8.14% per annum) and shortfall penalties escalate by culpability: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion, with criminal prosecution available. Wrongly refunded GST is clawed back retrospectively, refund claims are subject to a 4-year statutory time bar, and directors of the International Company may incur personal liability for GST debts under s 149 of the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full current and capital account convertibility under its statutory framework and is an IMF Article VIII member. There are no exchange-control restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, interest, royalties, loan repayments, or equity injection proceeds. The New Zealand dollar is freely floating and may be purchased or sold onshore and offshore without exchange-control approval; the only relevant compliance gate is the AML/CFT and sanctions assessment performed by the receiving bank. Foreign investment screening applies only to sensitive assets under the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which is not applicable to the conduct of a software anti-cheat middleware business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline within which export receipts must be repatriated. An International Company may receive foreign-currency service fees for ISIC 6201 anti-cheat software and retain those proceeds in a New Zealand foreign-currency account or an approved offshore account, convert them at market rates, or apply them directly to outbound trade disbursements without prior Reserve Bank approval.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand – Te Pūtea Matua

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, subject to clear legal constraints. New Zealand does not issue an offshore digital EMI licence. A non-bank provider must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and must comply with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. An offshore EMI may provide multi-currency transaction processing, cards, and FX conversion, but it cannot independently access New Zealand's local retail clearing rails and will require a sponsor bank. For a tax-exempt International Company, an offshore EMI is acceptable for operational disbursements and subscription collections, but it must be supported by a principal New Zealand bank account for escrow, settling, and documentary trade transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit
