Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) of the Republic of Panama. Official electronic portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa, providing the e-RUC, e-filing, electronic invoice validation, and taxpayer management systems.

E-government filing portal

For a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima: (1) duly incorporate before a Panama notary and register the corporate charter with the Public Registry; (2) file the Aviso de Operación (Notice of Business Operation) at the DGI within 30 days of commencing commercial activity; (3) obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), which serves simultaneously for income tax and ITBMS; (4) for a branch of a non-Panamanian corporation, deposit an apostilled certificate of incorporation, by-laws, and board resolution appointing a local agent; (5) activate the online account in the DGI Sede Electrónica using a digital signature certificate issued by an authorized certifying authority; (6) request the applicable invoice series and taxpayer e-credentials. Non-resident shareholders must submit passport copies or a foreign tax identification number for the transparency register. No minimum capital or local residency of shareholders is statutorily required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope; Entity_Level_Taxation. A Panamanian S.A. is a separate juridical person subject to entity-level corporate income tax at 25% only on income derived from Panamanian-source activities and assets; all foreign-source income received by the S.A. is outside the Panamanian tax net. The entity is not a pass-through vehicle and has no check-the-box analogue to disregarded LLC status for Panamanian tax purposes.

Available election forms

Panama has no U.S.-style check-the-box election and no general hybrid-mismatch election. Available statutory elections include: (i) the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) special regime under Law 41 of 2007, subject to meeting headcount, payroll, and regional-export requirements, replacing ordinary corporate income tax with an annual fixed tax; (ii) exclusive territorial fiscal regimes applicable to companies physically operating in the Panama Pacifico and City of Knowledge designated areas; (iii) the Simplified Tax Regime (REST) for small taxpayers whose annual gross revenue is within the statutory cap; and (iv) automatic territorial-source treatment for foreign-derived income, provided the taxpayer maintains documentary proof that the income was generated outside Panama and invoiced to non-domiciled counterparties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 0 — Panama imposes no general de minimis turnover threshold for ITBMS registration. Every person or entity engaged in taxable commercial activity in Panama must register for ITBMS from the first taxable transaction. Small taxpayers whose annual gross income does not exceed the statutory simplified-regime limit may elect the REST regime, but this does not eliminate the basic RUC/ITBMS registration requirement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating at 0% ITBMS is available for: (i) tangible goods physically exported from Panama to a foreign destination, provided the goods leave Panamanian customs territory under a valid export declaration; and (ii) services sold to a non-resident or non-domiciled customer where the benefit, use, and consumption occur entirely outside Panama. Eligibility requires that the invoice identifies the RUC of the Panamanian exporter, quotes ITBMS at 0%, and that the exporter maintains direct evidence of the foreign sale, the payment inflow, and the export movement. For a ghost kitchen or virtual-brand operator, true cross-border catering or delivery to non-Panamanian recipients abroad may qualify, whereas local delivery orders from platforms such as UberEats or DoorDash are domestic supplies subject to the applicable Panamanian ITBMS rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduanas / Export Manifest DU)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing physical departure from Panama
  • Commercial Invoice (Factura Comercial) quoting RUC and 0% ITBMS
  • Packing List (Lista de Empaque)
  • Freight forwarder certificate or shipping document showing final foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice demonstrating FX repatriation
  • Service contract and certificate of non-residence of the foreign buyer (for service exports)
  • Container or seal number records in the name of the Panamanian exporter (for goods exports)

Panama operates a mandatory electronic invoicing framework under the DGI 'Facturación Electrónica' system. All RUC-registered taxpayers must issue fiscal invoices in electronic form. The DGI authorises chronological invoice series; invoices must be transmitted to the DGI via the Sede Electrónica or an approved API connector. For each transaction, the system performs digital validation and returns a fiscal authorization code/QR stamp. Real-time fiscal clearance is mandatory for large taxpayers and is being phased in for all registered businesses. Credit notes and debit notes must be processed through the same electronic clearance loop, and the original XML/PDF electronic file must be retained for the statutory retention period.

DGI maintains a dedicated export zero-rating audit program that cross-checks the National Customs Authority export registries, bank inward-remittance records, and the exporter's RUC filings. If a zero-rated invoice lacks the mandatory customs or remittance support, DGI will recharacterise the supply as taxable domestically and assess the omitted 7% (or applicable special-rate) ITBMS. The exporter may then be subject to a 100% administrative surcharge on the under-declared tax, statutory monthly interest, and fines of up to 200% of the omitted tax for intentional fraud. Fraudulent export schemes carry criminal tax-evasion liability under Panamanian law with potential imprisonment, forfeiture of refunded VAT/ITBMS credits, and retroactive clawback for the statutory assessment window, which is extended in cases of false documentation or deliberate concealment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama has a fully dollarized financial system with no exchange controls; the US Dollar is sole legal tender. Both capital and current account transactions are freely convertible, with no quantitative restrictions on the conversion, repatriation, or remittance of profits. Legal and judicial protections ensure prompt transferability of funds. Practical hurdles are limited to satisfying AML/KYC requirements and documentary evidence of the underlying transaction. Dividends attached to bearer shares are disallowed, and profits remitted may be subject to a 10% dividend tax (or applicable tax treaty relief), but no convertibility or surrender restrictions exist.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory obligation exists for exporters to surrender export proceeds or convert foreign currency receipts into local currency. Because Panama's monetary system adopts the US Dollar, all exported proceeds may be retained in USD or any other foreign currency, freely deposited, held, or remitted. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no timeline for repatriation, and no central bank control.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Superintendency of Banks of Panama); Ministry of Economy and Finance (Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas) — Panama has no central bank and uses the US Dollar as legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium feasibility. Panama does not currently grant deposit-taking authorizations to pure digital EMIs; non-bank fintech companies must operate under a banking license or as a licensed payment processor. Offshore EMIs cannot perform banking or deposit-taking activities in Panama and cannot hold client funds in Panama without a local license. As an alternative, ghost kitchen operators may establish a Panama corporate account in a tier-1 bank for local currency (USD) operations, while using an offshore fintech (e.g., a US or EU EMI) for international disbursements if compliant with local AML regulations.
