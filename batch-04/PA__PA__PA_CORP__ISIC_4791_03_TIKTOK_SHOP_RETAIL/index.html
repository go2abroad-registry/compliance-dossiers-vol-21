Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Ley No. 32 de 26 de febrero de 1927 (General Law of Sociedades Anónimas), as amended, Articles 1, 2, 5, 12-18, 46, and 52-70; implemented by the Regulations of the Registro Público, Agreement No. 2 of 19 October 2012.

Formation / constitutional filings

Escritura Pública de Constitución de Sociedad Anónima (notarized public deed of incorporation), alternatively a Pacto Social inscribed in the Mercantile Registry of Panama.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The General Law requires a minimum authorized capital of USD 10,000, but there is no paid-up or escrow account requirement; capital may be subscribed without immediate payment and non-resident ownership is unrestricted.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Benemérito Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá (fire load and extinguisher inspection); structural load inspection certificate issued by a licensed engineer and accepted by the Municipality; workplace occupational health clearance from the Ministerio de Salud (MINSA) where employees are physically present; compliance with workplace risk prevention rules of the Ministerio de Trabajo y Desarrollo Laboral (MITRADEL).

Paid-in capital rule: The General Law requires a minimum authorized capital of USD 10,000, but there is no paid-up or escrow account requirement; capital may be subscribed without immediate payment and non-resident ownership is unrestricted.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); official electronic portal: Sede Electrónica DGI / 'e-Tax 2.0' at www.dgi.gob.pa with authorized notaries and certified public accountants acting as registered intermediaries.

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors must: (1) incorporate a Sociedad Anónima via notary and register with the Registro Público; (2) appoint a resident agent (agente residente) and legal representative; (3) obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación or business license; (4) file the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) application with DGI, attaching certified copy of the public deed, appointment of directors, identity documents, and evidence of the registered office; (5) obtain the digital signature credential and RUC certificate; (6) activate the e-Tax account for electronic filing of income tax, ITBMS, and dividend tax returns. The entire process takes 5-10 business days; non-resident shareholders are permitted without Panamanian tax residency implications.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope. A Panama S.A. is an entity-level taxpayer under the Impuesto Sobre la Renta (ISR) but is taxed only on Panama-source income at the headline corporate rate of 25%; foreign-source income is generally outside the taxable base provided the taxpayer maintains adequate substance and the income does not qualify as Panama-source under the Código Fiscal. Dividend distributions are subject to the 10% dividend tax (or 5% for dividends paid out of taxed profits) and a 4% complementary tax on dividends from non-taxed profits. Pass-through treatment is not available for S.A. entities; the entity classification follows the corporate form and cannot be elected away. — Foreign investors must: (1) incorporate a Sociedad Anónima via notary and register with the Registro Público; (2) appoint a resident agent (agente residente) and legal representative; (3) obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación or business license; (4) file the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) application with DGI, attaching certified copy of the public deed, appointment of directors, identity documents, and evidence of the registered office; (5) obtain the digital signature credential and RUC certificate; (6) activate the e-Tax account for electronic filing of income tax, ITBMS, and dividend tax returns. The entire process takes 5-10 business days; non-resident shareholders are permitted without Panamanian tax residency implications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope. A Panama S.A. is an entity-level taxpayer under the Impuesto Sobre la Renta (ISR) but is taxed only on Panama-source income at the headline corporate rate of 25%; foreign-source income is generally outside the taxable base provided the taxpayer maintains adequate substance and the income does not qualify as Panama-source under the Código Fiscal. Dividend distributions are subject to the 10% dividend tax (or 5% for dividends paid out of taxed profits) and a 4% complementary tax on dividends from non-taxed profits. Pass-through treatment is not available for S.A. entities; the entity classification follows the corporate form and cannot be elected away.

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification regime. An S.A. is mandatorily taxed as a corporation. Available statutory elections and regimes include: (a) the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41/2007 granting significant ISR and ITBMS exemptions for qualifying regional headquarters; (b) special economic zones such as Panama Pacifico and the City of Knowledge that substitute local taxes for zone-specific fees; (c) optional accrual vs. cash accounting for small taxpayers under DGI conditions; (d) the simplified 'pequeño contribuyente' regime, although only natural persons below the income cap may use it; and (e) group assessment/consolidation is not generally available. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized; Panama's income tax law follows a strict territorial source rule rather than an international classification mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for ITBMS; any S.A. carrying out taxable acts (sales of goods, rendering of services, or imports) in Panama must register with the DGI before commencing operations, irrespective of revenue volume. An optional simplified regime exists for natural persons with gross annual income at or below B/.36,000 (equivalent to USD 36,000), taxed at 2% on gross receipts; this option is not available to Sociedad Anónima corporations. For the TikTok Shop retail scope, the commercial platform operator and the Panamanian seller entity are each independently subject to ITBMS registration if they perform taxable supplies in Panama; cross-border digital platform sales to Panamanian consumers are subject to withholding at the payment gateway level.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered ITBMS taxpayer may apply the 0% rate to: (a) physical exports of goods when the merchandise leaves Panamanian customs territory under a valid customs export declaration; (b) services rendered to non-resident clients when the services are executed, used, or exploited entirely abroad, including digital services and platform advertisements sold to foreign beneficiaries; and (c) supplies to operators of Panama Pacifico and SEM-licensed entities where applicable. The zero-rating is a technical exemption, not an input credit refund mechanism; the exporter must segregate input ITBMS attributable to export sales, claim related input credits proportionally, and recover net credits either by offset against output ITBMS or by filing a refund application with DGI. For TikTok Shop retail, each consumer sale delivered to a foreign address must be supported by proof of export, and sales into Panama are taxable at 7%; the platform's marketplace facilitator obligations are governed by DGI criteria on deemed ITBMS registration for digital platforms.

  • DUA - Declaración Única Aduanera (electronic customs export declaration endorsed by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas de Panamá with date and exit port),
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading (maritime), Air Waybill (air), or Carta de Porte/CMR (land), evidencing physical exit of goods from Panama, and
  • Commercial zero-rated invoice electronically accepted by DGI, plus the SWIFT MT103/MT202 payment confirmation from a Panamanian bank or the inward remittance certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) issued by the receiving local bank showing the export proceeds credited to the seller's Panamanian account; supplementary packing lists and insurance certificates are recommended for audit defense.

Mandatory electronic invoicing ('Facturación Electrónica') is phased in by DGI for all RUC holders, with large taxpayers already obligated. Each invoice must be generated as structured XML or JSON, digitally signed using the taxpayer's cryptographic certificate, and transmitted in real time to the DGI e-Tax platform for automated validation and acceptance/rejection response (Resultado de Aceptación y Rechazo). Accepted invoices receive a fiscal authorization code and a QR code that must be printed or displayed. ERP application-to-application (API) integration is required for high-volume sellers such as e-commerce and social commerce operators; fiscal printers remain accepted for physical point-of-sale equivalence. Delivery notes, credit notes, debit notes, and export invoices must all flow through the same electronic clearance infrastructure.

DGI conducts targeted audits of export zero-rating claims by cross-checking the electronic customs SIS system, banking records, the e-Tax invoice clearance feed, and carrier manifests. If the export documentation bundle is incomplete, untimely, or inconsistent, DGI retroactively recharacterizes the sale as a taxable domestic supply and assesses the 7% ITBMS with monthly interest and surcharges, and fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the omitted tax depending on the degree of fault. Deliberate fabrication of export documents or false invoicing constitutes criminal tax fraud (defraudación fiscal) under the Código Penal, punishable by imprisonment, disqualification from acting as a RUC holder, and publication of the sanction; the statute of limitations for assessment is generally three years, extended to six years where returns are omitted or false, with no limitation period for fraudulent documents in aggravated cases. Repatriation within the 180-day reference period is the DGI's administrative benchmark; longer terms require documented open-account commercial terms and may attract TP and fictive-benefit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility without capital controls. USD is legal tender and the Balboa (PAB) is a nominal 1:1 accounting unit minted only as coins. Current-account and capital-account flows, including TikTok Shop sales revenue and profit remittances, may be moved freely in USD without central bank approval. Remittance of dividends is subject only to the 10% dividend withholding tax and standard bank AML/CFT source-of-funds verification under Law 23 of 2015. No foreign-exchange authorization or documentary clearance is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panama imposes no export proceeds surrender, compulsory conversion, repatriation timeline, or settlement-in-local-currency obligation on e-commerce exporters. The only compliance layer is commercial: Panamanian banks must preserve transaction records and can request evidence (order ledgers, payout statements, supplier invoices) for anti-money-laundering reporting under Law 23 of 2015 and SBP due-diligence rules.

Central bank supervision: No central bank or dedicated foreign-exchange administration exists in Panama. The Republic operates under full US dollarization; the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panama (SBP) supervises and regulates all banking licensees, while Banco Nacional de Panama acts solely as fiscal agent for government accounts. No authority issues Balboa paper currency or administers exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Medium-High as treasury overlay, but not as primary settlement venue. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise B2B, Airwallex, Payoneer) may open USD accounts through US/EU/UK partner banks; however, they are not SBP-licensed banks and cannot provide a Panamanian clearing branch. Their USD rails settle via US ACH/Fedwire and are legally acceptable for collection of TikTok Shop payouts provided the operating S.A. maintains a corresponding SBP-supervised Panamanian bank account for DGI tax payments, statutory record retention, and AML audit trail. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% on dividends paid out of profits already subject to the 25% corporate income tax (Fiscal Code Art. 733); 10% on distributions made out of exempt or foreign-source profits and on dividends the payer cannot substantiate as taxed. Dividend distributions by a standard S.A. are additionally preceded by the 1.4% 'advance dividend tax' mechanism only where the company elects the optional accelerated payment regime; the standard rule is 5%/10% at the moment of constructive distribution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.3,000,000 (~USD 3,000,000) of annual gross revenue triggers the mandatory TP Local File and the 745-A annex; intra-group transactions (e.g., content fees, commissions, license payments) exceeding B/.1,000,000 in the fiscal year also require full documentation. A Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above B/.50,000,000; the Country-by-Country Report applies to groups with consolidated revenue of USD 750,000,000 (Ley 45 de 2017 and Reg. 201-7108).

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regime (Law 254 of 2020, implemented by Executive Decree and DGI Resolution) applies to relevant entities carrying on relevant activities. A TikTok Shop retail S.A. performing distribution, online retail and service-center functions is classified as a 'distribution and service center' relevant activity. It must demonstrate: (i) direction and management in Panama; (ii) adequate local premises and qualified employees; (iii) core income-generating activities performed within Panamanian territory; and (iv) an annual sworn substance report filed via the SUIRE platform. Failure to comply carries fines from B/.10,000 up to B/.1,000,000 and publication on the DGI non-cooperative list; persistence of a pure shell structure also justifies the DGI's re-characterization of the territorial tax exclusion and exposure to the full 25% tax on the entire cross-border profit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Tributaria)

Statutory sanction: Fiscal Code Arts. 762-763: surcharge of 200% of the omitted tax plus 2% monthly interest and inflationary adjustment of the base. Penal Code Art. 278-A (Law 49 of 2019): imprisonment of 2-5 years, aggravated to 5-10 years where the amount defrauded exceeds B/.100,000 or false documents are used; fine equal to the amount evaded; corporate fine between B/.100,000 and B/.1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and the legal representative who sign or approve a false return face direct criminal conviction, precautionary travel bans, attachment of personal assets, disqualification from acting as administrators, and personal civil restitution of the omitted tax jointly with the company under veil-piercing principles.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Breach

Statutory sanction: Law 254 of 2020: B/.10,000-B/.100,000 for late or incomplete filing; false declaration B/.20,000-B/.100,000; failure to meet the substance test B/.100,000-B/.1,000,000; cancellation of the corporate registration and publication on the EU/OECD blacklist for shell-company activity.

Director personal liability: The Resident Agent and each director are jointly and severally exposed to the fines; the Penal Code criminalizes the use of fictitious companies (sociedad ficticia) with imprisonment; travel bans and the suspension of professional licences apply while the proceedings are pending.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of B/.10,000 to B/.1,000,000 under Law 23 of 2015; money-laundering offense under the Penal Code (Arts. 254 et seq.): imprisonment of 5-10 years and fines up to B/.5,000,000 for the legal entity; in rem forfeiture/extinción de dominio over the proceeds and the connected bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or implement the transfers are personally subject to money-laundering prosecution, extradition within the Inter-American mutual-assistance framework, travel bans, freezing of personal assets, and no corporate veil protection for criminal conduct.
