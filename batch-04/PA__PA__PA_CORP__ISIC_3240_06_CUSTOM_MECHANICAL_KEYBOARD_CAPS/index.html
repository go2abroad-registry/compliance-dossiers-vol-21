Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Sociedad Anónima. In practice the deed commonly declares authorized capital of at least USD 10,000 (e.g., 10,000 shares at USD 1.00 par value) with only a portion subscribed and paid. No escrow account or deposit verification is required before inscription. Bearer shares are phased out; registered (nominative) shares are the standard.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministry of Economy and Finance; taxpayer portal VUE/e-Tax (https://www.vue.gob.pa, https://etax.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

Foreign companies must register with the DGI via the Ventanilla Única Electrónica (VUE) to obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC). The process requires: (1) a power of attorney granted to a Panamanian licensed attorney/resident agent; (2) notarized and apostilled or consularized corporate and beneficial-ownership documents of the foreign parent/shareholders; (3) the Company incorporation certificate issued by the Registro Público and municipal 'Aviso de Operación'; (4) submission of the Articles of Incorporation, board resolution appointing local officers, and a sworn statement on estimated Panama-source income. Upon approval, the DGI issues the RUC and tax registration certificates, generally within 5–10 business days after complete submission.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign companies must register with the DGI via the Ventanilla Única Electrónica (VUE) to obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC). The process requires: (1) a power of attorney granted to a Panamanian licensed attorney/resident agent; (2) notarized and apostilled or consularized corporate and beneficial-ownership documents of the foreign parent/shareholders; (3) the Company incorporation certificate issued by the Registro Público and municipal 'Aviso de Operación'; (4) submission of the Articles of Incorporation, board resolution appointing local officers, and a sworn statement on estimated Panama-source income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under Panamanian law. A Sociedad Anónima is mandatorily taxed at the entity level on Panama-source income only. Limited optional regimes include the Multinational Company Headquarters (SEM) regime and the simplified small-taxpayer ITBMS regime for entities with annual gross receipts up to B/.36,000.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory ITBMS registration for all persons engaged in taxable business activities in Panama irrespective of revenue; however, micro-businesses with annual gross revenue equal to or below B/.36,000 may elect the simplified ITBMS regime (Régimen Simplificado). A RUC and 'Aviso de Operación' are required before commencing any commercial activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS taxes at 0% the export of tangible goods, provided the seller is a registered taxpayer and retains documentation evidencing that the goods physically left Panamanian customs territory. Services supplied to foreign recipients and effectively used/consumed outside Panama are also zero-rated if registered as 'exportador de servicios' with the DGI and hold a valid 'Aviso de Exportación de Servicios' (Law 27 of 2018). Goods must be shipped from Panama under a valid customs export declaration within 30 days of invoicing; sales to local free zones may apply special VAT suspension rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Mercancías, DUA) duly stamped by the Dirección Nacional de Aduanas
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing physical departure from Panamanian territory
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order referencing the ITBMS export exemption
  • Inward Remittance Certificate/Proof of payment from Banco Nacional or commercial bank showing receipt of foreign-currency proceeds from abroad
  • Transportation/freight insurance documents and warehouse exit confirmation
  • DGI export registration certificate and annual ITBMS export declaration

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) through the DGI e-Tax platform for all registered taxpayers, phased in by taxpayer category and annual revenue. Invoices must be digitally signed (XML with PKCS#7 digital certificate) and transmitted to the DGI in real time for online validation, with authorization codes and QR codes for fiscal control. The system requires API-based submission for enterprises using ERP or billing software; full e-invoicing compliance is expected for all businesses, including e-commerce sellers, no later than 2025 for SMEs.

The DGI and Customs may audit export documentation and levy penalties for unjustified 0% rating: recovery of unpaid ITBMS plus surcharge (10% per month, capped at 100%), fines under the Fiscal Code ranging from B/.500 to B/.20,000 or 100%–300% of tax evaded, and criminal sanctions for tax fraud, including imprisonment and disqualification of the taxpayer. Retroactive clawback of improperly granted refunds and exclusion from future zero-rating for up to 5 years also apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility on both current and capital account, with zero exchange-control formalities, no preferential rate tiers, no transfer taxes on FX conversion, and no repatriation licensing. Practical hurdles are confined to AML/CFT verification rather than exchange regulation: (i) each inbound USD credit to a Panamanian S.A. bank account must carry underlying-transaction evidence (sales invoice, customs declaration, PSP settlement report) under Law 23 of 2015; (ii) profit remittances to foreign shareholders follow standard Panamanian corporate mechanics, with the dividend-withholding framework (generally a 10% dividend tax on distributions sourced from Panamanian-taxable income and credited against underlying ISR) requiring bespoke tax counsel because the company's foreign-sale income is territorial and may stand outside that base; (iii) Stripe and Shopify Payments do not yet provide native merchant registration in Panama, so the practical architecture is a two-hop inflow: card acquirer settles to a US or Hong Kong merchant-of-record account, which then sweeps USD by SWIFT into the Panamanian S.A. operational account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion rules apply. Because the USD is the circulating medium, there is no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation deadline, and no DGI declaration for export proceeds. Funds may remain indefinitely in foreign PSP or settlement accounts. If repatriated to the Panama banking system, the receiving bank requires a documentary file evidencing the commercial reality of the transaction, including commercial invoices, customs export declarations (documento único de aduana or XML), bill-of-lading or courier proof, and the aggregate PSP settlement statement; absence of those documents may result in a five-to-fifteen-day funds-on-hold period under SBP monitoring practices.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama maintains no central bank of issue. United States dollars circulate as legal tender alongside fractional Balboa (PAB) coinage pegged at 1:1. Prudential banking supervision and the licensing of banks and trust companies are vested exclusively in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) under Decree-Law 2 of 1971, as amended by Decree-Law 9 of 1998 and Law 23 of 2015. Foreign-exchange dealing is treated as ordinary regulated banking activity requiring no central-bank authorisation; the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) has no FX-control competence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound flows fall into three categories. (a) Equity capital injection: SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder account with beneficiary name matching the S.A. exactly and concept field reading 'Aporte de capital social'; the file package comprises notarised board minutes approving the capital increase, updated share registry, apostilled identity documents, and source-of-funds bank statements of the contributing shareholder; the capital increase must be notarised and filed with the Registro Público within thirty days. (b) Shareholder loan: MT103 with concept 'Préstamo de accionista', supported by a signed interest-bearing loan agreement and corporate borrower resolution; no Registro Público filing is mandatory, but the bank requires a credit file showing repayment capacity and the loan must be recorded in the company's books at arm's-length interest. (c) Operational sales settlements: two-hop route in which Stripe/Shopify payouts settle to the US or Hong Kong merchant-of-record account and are swept same-day via MT103 into the Panama account with the description 'Ingresos por ventas de mercadería - keyboard keycaps'; each significant sweep must be accompanied by the corresponding PSP settlement report. (i) Standard USD T/T disbursement via SWIFT MT103 from the Panama account through Citibank or JP Morgan correspondent legs, with typical value in one to three business days. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Medium. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (flat corporate income tax, Impuesto Sobre la Renta, on net taxable Panamanian-source income; no provincial or municipal income surtax).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory dividend withholding tax on dividends paid out of Panamanian-source taxed profits; no Panama dividend withholding on distributions sourced from exempt foreign-source profits. The 10% withholding is applied to the gross dividend declared by the S.A.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 300,000 of annual aggregate related-party transactions or USD 3,000,000 of annual gross income (Law 43 of 2009, Executive Decree 176 of 2010, as amended), triggering mandatory contemporaneous Local File/Form 194 documentation; the Panama S.A. should also prepare a Master File if it is part of a multinational group with consolidated gross revenue above the national threshold.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 254 of 2021, Executive Decree 713 of 2022) apply to entities conducting relevant activities in Panama, including holding, distribution and service centers, intellectual property, headquarters, and shipping. A custom keycap injection-molding S.A. with a physical Panamanian plant, local employees, utility contracts, and substantive procurement decisions in Panama satisfies substance as a legitimate manufacturing business. By contrast, an S.A. merely interposed as the invoicing agent for keycaps drop-shipped from Asia must demonstrate ceteris paribus that its staff, premises, and expenditure in Panama are proportionate; otherwise DGI and the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF) may reclassify the entity as a shell, deny the foreign-source income exclusion, and revoke its tax-residency certificate for treaty purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion (Defraudación Tributaria) - Fiscal Code Art. 695 et seq.; Código Penal Art. 318

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the evaded tax, moratory interest at 1% per month, statutory surcharges, and administrative fines. Criminal conviction for tax fraud carries imprisonment of typically 2 to 5 years, and the court may order payment of the full evaded amount plus damages where the evaded tax exceeds B/.10,000.00.

Director personal liability: Signing a false Form 193 exposes directors and legal representatives to joint and several civil liability for the S.A.'s unpaid taxes. Criminal prosecution may trigger preventive detention (travel bans), freezing of personal and corporate bank accounts, and disqualification from holding corporate offices in Panama.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure (Law 254 of 2021; Exec. Decree 713 of 2022)

Statutory sanction: DGI may assess administrative penalties under the ESR regime, suspend the Aviso de Operación, deny the issuance of a Certificate of Tax Residency, and recommend deregistration of the S.A. from the Registro Público. Persistent inactivity may lead to corporate dissolution at the registry's initiative.

Director personal liability: If the S.A. is a mere façade, courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold shareholders and directors personally liable to the State and creditors. Directors may also be flagged by the UAF for domestic and international financial advisory actions, resulting in asset freezes, inability to open bank accounts, and personal fines.
AML / Money Laundering (Law 23 of 2015; UAF Regulations)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines against the legal entity of up to B/.1,000,000.00, cancellation of licenses, and confiscation of proceeds. Criminal money-laundering conviction under Panama's Código Penal and Law 23 of 2015 carries imprisonment of 5 to 12 years, with enhanced penalties for organized involvement.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face personal criminal prosecution, travel restrictions, and preventive arrest during investigation. The S.A.'s bank accounts may be frozen without prior notice, and directors may be debarred from serving as officers of any Panamanian licensed entity.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (Law 43 of 2009; Law 52 of 2014)

Statutory sanction: DGI may adjust the S.A.'s taxable income to the arm's-length result, disallow the deduction of undocumented related-party payments, impose a 10% surcharge on the deficiency, and levy fines of up to B/.10,000.00 for non-filing or materially defective Form 194; the assessment is enforceable by garnishment of bank accounts and receivables.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false transfer-pricing statements risk criminal prosecution for making false declarations before a public official. The disallowance may render the entity insolvent, allowing creditors and the tax authority to pursue restitution from directors for breach of fiduciary duty, with accompanying travel bans and potential imprisonment.
