Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama. Electronic filing and registration portal: Sede Electrónica E-Tax (https://etax.mef.gob.pa).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first incorporate and register the Panama corporation in the Public Registry (Registro Público) through a licensed attorney and resident agent. Thereafter, file the Registration of Tax Identification (Solicitud de Registro Único de Contribuyentes - RUC) with DGI, submitting certified constitutional documents, valid identity documents of directors and shareholders, the registered office certificate, and a notarized power of attorney for the resident agent. Once the RUC is issued, register for the ITBMS (VAT) and, if applicable, as an employer with the Social Security and Labor authorities. Applicants may use the E-Tax portal or attend the DGI 'Módulo de Atención al Contribuyente' in Panama City. Foreign-source income taxation status must be disclosed using DGI form/formats for territorial income assessment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not employ a check-the-box entity classification election. A Panama S.A. may elect the Small Taxpayer Simplified Regime (SIS) upon registration if annual gross revenue does not exceed USD 36,000, substituting general CIT and ITBMS obligations with a fixed monthly fee. Qualifying companies may elect the Multinational Company Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007 for regional coordination services. With DGI authorization, commonly controlled corporate groups may elect consolidated income tax assessment as an economic group for Panamanian-source income, and taxpayers may elect tax-neutral reorganization treatment for share exchanges and mergers when conditions under the Fiscal Code are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold in USD: any person engaged in taxable transfer of movable goods or provision of services in Panama must register as an ITBMS taxpayer. A simplified SIS threshold exists at USD 36,000 of annual gross revenue, subject to full compliance requirements above this amount.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for exports of tangible goods (including portable solar generators and solar panels) provided the goods physically leave Panama under a customs export declaration and are sold to a foreign customer outside the Panamanian customs territory. Services are zero-rated when the recipient is resident abroad and the benefit is consumed outside Panama. The seller must maintain an audit trail demonstrating delivery terms (FOB/EXW), title passage, foreign destination, and actual export completion; online e-commerce exports must be evidenced by international courier/Waybill records and payment settlement from foreign bank/card networks. Local sales to Panamanian customers are not zero-rated and remain subject to 7% ITBMS.

  • Declaración Única de Exportación (DUS) / Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (master and house) evidencing foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • DGI zero-rated electronic invoice (Factura Electrónica con tasa 0%)
  • Foreign payment settlement evidence (e.g., Stripe payout statement, SWIFT MT103, card scheme settlement report)

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) under DGI resolutions for large taxpayers and progressively for all businesses. Taxpayers must use an authorized e-invoicing operator or the DGI E-Tax system; invoices must carry a valid DGI-issued digital signature, include a QR code, and be transmitted to DGI in real-time (or within 24 hours) through the Sede Electrónica's API. Retail sales through online payment rails must issue e-invoices or cash-register equivalents with fiscal control numbers; digital stamping occurs at issuance, and DGI requires immediate reconciliation of invoice tax output with ITBMS returns.

Fraudulent zero-rating claims subject the taxpayer to full ITBMS clawback plus compound interest (statutory annual rate of 12% or proportional monthly surcharge of 1.5%), administrative fines of up to 200% of the tax evaded, closure of establishment, and criminal liability for tax evasion under Panama's Fiscal Code. The DGI can also disqualify the exporter's digital signature and impose monetary sanctions per uninvoiced transaction; wrongful input VAT credits are subject to immediate recovery plus penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized; both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible without exchange-control authorization or licensing. Profit remittance is unrestricted: dividends, shareholder loans, and capital repatriation may be wired abroad at will. The only friction is taxation: dividends sourced from Panama taxable profits bear a 10% withholding tax generally creditable against the corporate dividend advance; distributions from untaxed foreign-source profits attract a 20% withholding rate. The bank-debit tax (ITBM) of 0.25% applies only to debits, not to inward remittances or credit balances. No approval from any FX authority is required for outward profit transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Exporters may freely retain, deploy, or remit USD. The only relevant obligation is documentary: exporters must retain the underlying commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, insurance certificate, and customs declaration for the SBP-mandated five-year record retention period and must make such records available for AML/CFT examination on request.

Central bank supervision: No issuing central bank exists in Panama. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the prudential and AML/CFT regulator for banks and financial institutions; Banco Nacional de Panamá (BNP) acts as the State's fiscal agent and clearing-house bank. The US Dollar is full legal tender under Law 84 of 1941, with the Panamanian balboa circulating only as coins at parity, producing a fully dollarized system without discretionary monetary policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. Panama does not issue a standalone electronic-money institution (EMI) license; cross-border payment settlement must be executed through SBP-licensed commercial banks or SBP-licensed money remitters (casas de remesa). Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Wise Business, or Dukascopy are not authorized deposit-taking institutions in Panama; use of such accounts as principal treasury vehicles creates unlicensed-money-transmission red flags and severe enhanced due diligence. For the target merchant rails, Stripe does not list Panama as a supported market as of this blueprint; therefore, the Panama-domiciled S.A. cannot receive Stripe or Affirm BNPL settlements directly. The viable structure is a US or EEA merchant-of-record or payment-facilitator intermediary (for example, a US LLC onboarded to Stripe with Affirm as a payment method) that nets settlements to the Panamanian operational account via SWIFT/ACH. This settlement-flow chain must be fully documented to the Panamanian bank to satisfy source-of-funds and business-model review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders may inject equity or subordinated shareholder loans from abroad via SWIFT MT103 or correspondent telegraphic transfer without central bank or SBP approval. Recommended SWIFT reference codes: OECD purpose code 'D.3' for equity subscription and 'C.2' for intercompany loans, entered in field 70 with the remitter's bank identified in field 50K/52A. Funds are credited at parity in USD without conversion charge or FX authorization. The bank will require a certified board resolution approving the capital increase or loan and a definitive share-subscription statement; there is no statutory minimum capital, but Tier-1 banks expect initial inflows of USD 25,000–100,000 to evidence funding integrity. Inward amounts are exempt from ITBM debit tax; only subsequent withdrawals will be debited.Clearing rails for trade settlements include: (1) SWIFT MT103 priority payments for direct USD disbursements to suppliers in the PRC, the US, and other markets; (2) commercial documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for established supplier relationships, routed through the Panamanian bank as presenting bank; (3) irrevocable sight or term letters of credit confirmed by a Hong Kong or PRC advising bank for high-value inventory replenishment of solar panels and battery products; (4) US ACH/Fedwire transfers via the bank's New York correspondent for US logistics, warehousing, and Stripe payout net settlements; (5) Panama local clearing through the Cámara de Compensación operated by Banco Nacional de Panamá for local operating expenses; and (6) CNY-cleared payments through the offshore CNH market or CIPS/SWIFT channel when suppliers require RMB settlement. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium feasibility. Panama does not issue a standalone electronic-money institution (EMI) license; cross-border payment settlement must be executed through SBP-licensed commercial banks or SBP-licensed money remitters (casas de remesa). Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Wise Business, or Dukascopy are not authorized deposit-taking institutions in Panama; use of such accounts as principal treasury vehicles creates unlicensed-money-transmission red flags and severe enhanced due diligence. For the target merchant rails, Stripe does not list Panama as a supported market as of this blueprint; therefore, the Panama-domiciled S.A. cannot receive Stripe or Affirm BNPL settlements directly. The viable structure is a US or EEA merchant-of-record or payment-facilitator intermediary (for example, a US LLC onboarded to Stripe with Affirm as a payment method) that nets settlements to the Panamanian operational account via SWIFT/ACH. This settlement-flow chain must be fully documented to the Panamanian bank to satisfy source-of-funds and business-model review.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Balboa (B/.) at parity with USD; mandatory Local File and Master File required when the taxpayer's related-party transactions exceed B/. 3,000,000 during the fiscal year, or when gross revenue and total assets both exceed B/. 3,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama implemented Economic Substance Regulations (Law 8 of 2018; Cabinet Resolution 3-2019), requiring companies engaged in intellectual property, distribution, financing, and service activities to maintain physical presence, full-time employees, and board decision-making in Panama. A solar-portable-power online retailer that keeps a Panama warehouse, locally manages inventory, and performs actual commercial functions will satisfy economic substance; a mere letterbox company claiming foreign-source income faces reclassification and penal sanctions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under Panama's Tax Procedure Law (Código Fiscal) and Law 5 of 2005: deficiency assessment plus 50% fraud surcharge, criminal fine of B/.10,000–B/.50,000, and imprisonment up to five (5) years for responsible persons.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations or permit controlled funds to circulate exclusively abroad are jointly and severally liable for the omitted tax; courts may pierce the corporate veil, attach personal assets, impose travel bans, and issue Interpol red notices.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of profits as Panama-source income with a 25% corporate tax charge, penalties up to 200% of tax due, cancellation of the company's tax ID, and strike-off from the Public Registry under the economic substance compliance procedure.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally responsible for tax debts if the company was managed without substance and in violation of the ESR; civil restitution, denial of exit permits, and disqualification from holding directorships in Panama are primary sanctions.
AML / Structuring Violation

Statutory sanction: Breach of Panama Law 23 of 2015 (AML/CFT) and FATF recommendations: fine from B/.1,000 to B/.1,000,000, mandatory filing of suspicious transaction reports with the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF), and possible suspension of the merchant banking APIs used for payment processing.

Director personal liability: AML compliance officers and directors can face criminal prosecution and imprisonment of two (2) to five (5) years; personal bank guarantees, director disqualification, and asset freezing may be ordered even before conviction.
Dividend Withholding Failure

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax becomes immediately payable, plus a 10% non-filing surcharge and monthly interest; DGI can block the company's tax account and deny tax clearance certificates until the liability is settled.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly executed or authorized the distribution incur personal liability equal to the withheld tax and related fines; travel restrictions and embargo of personal property can be applied during assessment.
