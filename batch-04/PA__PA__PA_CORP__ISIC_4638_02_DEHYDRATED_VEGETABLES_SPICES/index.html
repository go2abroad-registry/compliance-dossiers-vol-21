Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; official digital portal: https://www.dgi.gob.pa (taxpayer e-services, RUC registration, and mandatory electronic-invoicing gateway).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) must obtain the statutory tax identifier, the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes), from the DGI in accordance with the Código Fiscal and DGI regulations. Procedure: (1) appoint and maintain a licensed resident agent and a registered office in Panama; (2) inscribe the public deed of incorporation with the Registro Público; (3) file DGI Form 0116 (Solicitud de RUC) together with the Aviso de Operación and supporting documents, including apostilled/legalized incorporation instruments, the registered agent's certificate, and identification of directors and beneficial owner; (4) upon approval, obtain the RUC certificate and activate the DGI electronic-invoicing account. A branch of a foreign corporation follows the same procedure with additional home-country corporate documents and parent-board resolutions. No minimum capital or local shareholder residency is required; the RUC serves as the unified tax ID and is used for ITBMS registration and customs export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

None. Panama does not maintain a check-the-box regime or hybrid-mismatch election. A Panamanian S.A. is a juridical person taxed at entity level on Panama-source income only; foreign-source income is outside the charge, and pass-through treatment is unavailable. The sole elective option relevant to small operators is the Régimen Especial Simplificado, a fixed monthly quota covering ISR and ITBMS for taxpayers with annual gross receipts up to B/. 40,000; it does not reclassify the entity type.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Nil general turnover threshold: any person or entity habitually carrying on taxable transfers of movable goods or taxable services within Panamanian territory must register for RUC and ITBMS from the first transaction. The optional small-taxpayer simplified regime applies only up to B/. 40,000 of annual gross turnover; exceeding that ceiling triggers full ITBMS registration and compliance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% ITBMS) applies to the sale and physical exportation of goods from Panamanian customs territory to a foreign destination, including deemed exports to the Zona Libre de Colón and authorized export-processing zones, provided the exporter is the registered seller, title and risk pass to the foreign buyer outside Panama, and the goods leave through an authorized Panamanian customs port under a lodged export declaration. Offshore services supplied to non-residents qualify as zero-rated only when the service is effectively consumed outside Panama. The zero-rated transaction preserves the exporter's input ITBMS credits, which may be offset against output tax or recovered via a DGI refund where a credit balance arises.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduana – DUA) duly lodged in the Panamanian customs single-window platform
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing loading at a Panamanian port or airport and foreign discharge port
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin when required by the buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) or SWIFT/TT credit advice establishing collection of export proceeds into a Panamanian bank account
  • Insurance certificate and terminal or warehouse exit records evidencing physical departure

Panama requires electronic invoicing for all ITBMS-registered taxpayers, including exporters. Invoices must be generated through the DGI electronic-invoicing system or an authorized fiscal service provider, transmitted to the DGI in real time (XML schema) before delivery to the buyer, and validated with a DGI-assigned unique fiscal code and QR digital stamp printed on the invoice. Credit notes, debit notes, and export-related documents must also pass through the same clearance environment. Issuers must retain complete invoice data for at least five years and may not issue uncleared paper invoices.

DGI and the National Customs Authority jointly audit zero-rating and refund claims. Missing documentation, absence of proof of physical export, or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day DGI administrative benchmark may cause recharacterization of the sale as a domestic taxable transaction at the 7% ITBMS rate, automatic clawback of credited or refunded input tax, statutory interest and surcharges, and fines of up to 100% of the omitted tax. False export declarations constitute tax fraud, exposing the legal representative and directors to criminal prosecution under the Penal Code, potential imprisonment, and suspension of the RUC or customs exporter registration; the review and clawback window runs for five fiscal years from the year in which the claim was made.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama maintains unrestricted convertibility across both current and capital accounts. Since USD is legal tender, there is no exchange control law, no central bank FX authorization system, and no mandatory conversion requirement. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, shareholder capital liquidation, interest payments, and trade settlement can be freely executed in USD. The only practical friction is bank-level due diligence: clearing of dividends above USD 100,000 generally requires a certified solvency certificate (Paz y Salvo) from the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) and evidence that the territorial-source income has been properly reported.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rules exist in Panama. Exporters of dehydrated vegetables and spices must retain 100% of USD export receipts in any SBP-licensed bank or offshore account, without any statutory timeline to sell, transfer, or convert proceeds. Documentary obligations follow the banking AML framework, requiring a commercial invoice, bill of lading, export declaration (Declaración de Exportación) filed with the customs authority, and quality/phytosanitary certificates where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as the primary banking and foreign exchange intermediation regulator; there is no formal central bank issuing domestic currency. Panama operates under full dollarization with the US Dollar (USD) as legal tender and the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) as a non-issued, 1:1 denomination coin. The Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) exercises AML/CFT supervision over bank and non-bank financial institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Panama does not have a European-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime; the SBP's Fintech Hub permits sandbox pilots but grants no passportable EMI license. Offshore digital EMIs (Mercury, Brex, Wise Business, Airwallex) typically reject Panamanian corporate entities because of correspondent banking risk and the absence of a Panamanian payment system license for those platforms. Some international EMIs (e.g., Swiss/EU-based Dukascopy, certain UK EMI entities with B2B SaaS rails) may onboard the S.A., but will impose restricted monthly throughput, require third-party KYC confirmation, and are not suitable as the primary clearing venue for letter-of-credit trade. The SWIFT-correspondent rail of an SBP-licensed Tier 1 bank remains the legally and operationally preferred route.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Incoming shareholder capital contributions must be routed as SWIFT MT103 or MT202 credits denominated in USD to the operational account. The wire narrative must clearly state "Aporte de Capital" (Capital Subscription), referencing the corporate RUC number (DGI taxpayer ID), the date and number of the board/shareholder resolution authorizing the injection, and the number of subscribed shares. Because Panama is dollarized, no FX conversion or central bank purpose code is required. For injections above USD 50,000, the receiving bank will request supporting documentation (resolution, subscription registry, source of funds) within 48 hours; for amounts exceeding USD 500,000, a sworn source-of-wealth statement, corporate balance sheet, and DGI solvency certificate are commonly required. Deposits from unrelated third parties are highly discouraged; all funding must originate from the UBO or a clearly disclosed corporate shareholder with a matching name in the KYC file.Trade settlements for both supplier payments and customer collections are executed through SWIFT MT103 (outward payments to garlic and chili growers in China, India, and Spain) and through Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit issued to suppliers via MT700. For the export side, the Panamanian bank will advise and negotiate export LCs presented by the buyer's bank, subject to the Standard Documentary Credit Practice (UCP 600) and ISBP 745. Disbursements must be accompanied by the purchase contract, commercial invoice, full packing list, evidence of shipment (B/L or AWB), and, for the dehydrated food sector, a phytosanitary certificate and Certificate of Origin (where required). The Panamanian bank acts as issuing, advising, or negotiating bank while relying on its global correspondent network (typically Bank of America, JPMorgan, Citibank, or Bank of New York Mellon) for settlement in USD/EUR; intra-day liquidity is available when the company maintains clean collateral (cash margin or standby LCs) for the LC issuance facility.USD/CNH Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) with offshore CNY fixing (suitable for garlic/seasoning ingredient sourcing from China)USD/EUR Deliverable Forwards (covering sales to EU clients or payments to Spanish/Italian suppliers of dehydrated chili)USD/INR NDF Forwards (for cumin/pepper sourcing from India)Cross-Currency Basis Swaps (USD/CNH and USD/EUR) for term mismatches on LC financingFX Swaps (Swap-in/Swap-out) for short-term USD/EUR liquidity repositioningCommercial Bank Flexible Forwards (window forwards) only via SBP-licensed banks' international treasury desks, subject to IFRS hedge documentation and board-approved treasury policy

Panama does not have a European-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime; the SBP's Fintech Hub permits sandbox pilots but grants no passportable EMI license. Offshore digital EMIs (Mercury, Brex, Wise Business, Airwallex) typically reject Panamanian corporate entities because of correspondent banking risk and the absence of a Panamanian payment system license for those platforms. Some international EMIs (e.g., Swiss/EU-based Dukascopy, certain UK EMI entities with B2B SaaS rails) may onboard the S.A., but will impose restricted monthly throughput, require third-party KYC confirmation, and are not suitable as the primary clearing venue for letter-of-credit trade. The SWIFT-correspondent rail of an SBP-licensed Tier 1 bank remains the legally and operationally preferred route.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 1,000,000 (balboa pegged 1:1 to USD) annual gross income threshold; taxpayers exceeding this threshold with cross-border related-party transactions must file Formulario 707 and maintain local transfer pricing documentation. A master file is expected for multinational groups with worldwide consolidated revenue exceeding USD 10,000,000 or where related-party transactions involve low-tax jurisdictions.

Economic substance scope: Panama applies economic substance expectations under Law 76 of 2019 for Panamanian tax-resident entities carrying on relevant activities, including distribution and service-center operations. A dehydrated spices exporter must maintain a physical office or warehouse in Panama, local management capacity, employment records, board decisions, bank accounts, and actual operational expenditures proportionate to the activity. A pure pass-through vehicle with no local presence is vulnerable to being characterized as a shell entity, with fines, loss of territorial treatment, and potential deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Omission of Panamanian-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of two to five years, fines of up to 100% of the evaded tax, monthly interest of 1%, and a 10%-style late payment surcharge. The DGI may also levy administrative fines up to USD 5,000 and re-assess the taxpayer for five fiscal years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, civil damages, and criminal penalties. Courts may pierce the corporate veil, impose asset freezes, issue travel restrictions, and order imprisonment of responsible directors.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration / Missing TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: DGI discretionary adjustment of taxable income, imposition of an administrative fine of up to USD 5,000, and penalties computed on the resulting underpaid tax. Where false documentation is submitted, criminal tax fraud sanctions apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and the legal representative are personally answerable for the accuracy of the TP return; deliberate manipulation of intercompany margins can result in criminal fines, retirement fund disqualification, and imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Panama may impose substantial administrative penalties under the economic substance rules, deny the territorial income classification, treat the entity as fiscally transparent or non-compliant, and ultimately strike it off the public registry. The company can also lose access to banking and trade finance facilities.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if the corporate form is disregarded as a sham. Personal exposure includes civil restitution, travel bans, and criminal prosecution for using a shell structure to facilitate tax evasion or money laundering.
AML Failure / Trade-Based Money Laundering Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015, fines up to USD 500,000 and liability for disqualification from regulated activities; trade-based money laundering is a criminal offense carrying prison terms from two to ten years. The UAF may order freezing of bank accounts and seizure of the exported goods or equivalent assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or deliberately ignore laundering indicators are personally liable under criminal AML laws; they face asset seizure, travel restrictions, bans on holding directorship positions, and imprisonment. The corporate legal veil will not protect individuals actively involved in illicit trade schemes.
Dividend and Bearer Share Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The DGI can re-classify the distribution as a taxable dividend and assess withholding tax, surcharges, and interest; bearer share violations carry fines under corporate law and may result in the suspension or nullification of shareholder rights.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for ensuring withholding is remitted to DGI; failure to do so creates a collectible obligation against the company and its officers. Intentional misclassification of dividends can lead to civil restitution and criminal tax evasion charges, with imprisonment for the responsible individuals.
