Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministry of Economy and Finance of Panama; electronic services through the SIRI portal (siri.mef.gob.pa) and the integrated customs platform SIGA/VUPA.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Sociedad Anónima must first be registered in the Public Registry of Panama with a resident agent and registered office, and obtain the relevant municipal aviso de operación where required. Thereafter, the company must apply for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) at the DGI, submitting the public-registry certificate, nominee/legal representative appointment, local fiscal-domicile evidence, powers of attorney for the responsible representative, and passport copies of the ultimate beneficial owners/directors. RUC serves for both income tax and ITBMS/VAT identification. Foreign entities without a Panamanian fixed establishment may still be registered for specific withholding and export-zero-rating obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope; the Sociedad Anónima is a per-se corporation, not pass-through, and Panamanian tax is imposed on Panama-source income only. Foreign-source income, including offshore aviation-parts trading where title passes outside Panama and no Panama fixed establishment is involved, generally falls outside the taxable base.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch entity election is available in Panama. Entity classification follows the legal form of the Sociedad Anónima. Limited elections exist for special regimes such as free-trade-zone (Zona Franca) licensing or the Multinational Company Headquarters (SEM) regime, and an eligible small taxpayer may elect simplified income tax treatment, but an S.A. remains a corporate taxpayer for ITBMS purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 0.00 — no turnover de minimis applies to a Panamanian S.A. engaged in commercial transfers or imports; ITBMS registration is mandatory before the first taxable supply or import. Natural-person small taxpayers may use a simplified fixed-payment regime, but this does not alter mandatory registration for a corporate entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% ITBMS rate is available for (i) goods physically exported from Panama to a foreign buyer, (ii) services rendered entirely outside Panama or services supplied for direct use abroad, and (iii) sales to duty-free zones and supplies for international maritime/aircraft operations, including commercial aircraft rotables and surplus parts destined for foreign airlines or international carriers. Eligibility requires the seller to be a registered ITBMS taxpayer, customs export formalities to be completed in the taxpayer's name, and the goods to leave Panamanian territory within the period stated in the export declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration accepted in SIGA/VUPA
  • Certificate of Export / exit validation issued by the Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the foreign consignee
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT credit advice, or bank payment evidence in the seller's name
  • ITBMS zero-rated invoice including the export legend and DGI electronic authorization
  • Acquisition/Title documentation evidencing the aircraft parts and export value where applicable

Panama mandates electronic invoicing for active taxpayers. Invoices must be issued as Documento Tributario Electrónico (DTE) XML through the DGI's free e-invoice portal or an authorized PSFE service provider. The DGI SIRI system performs near-real-time validation and issues a fiscal authorization code; digital stamping is represented by the DGI validation code and QR. Export zero-rated invoices, customs export declarations, and supporting transport documents must be cross-matched through SIGA/VUPA at the time of shipment.

DGI and the Customs Authority may audit export files and cross-check the customs declaration, transport documents, and banking remittance. If zero-rating is claimed without proven physical export, with false values, or where goods are re-introduced into Panamanian commerce, the entire ITBMS is clawed back at 7% plus statutory late-payment recargos. The Código Fiscal and Customs Law 112 of 2017 impose omitted-tax surcharges, fines of up to B/. 50,000 or more for false customs declarations, potential criminal liability for customs evasion/contraband, publication of tax defaulters, and the temporary suspension or cancellation of the RUC for repeat offenders.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Panamanian balboa (PAB) is a fractional denomination of the U.S. dollar at a fixed 1:1 rate, creating a fully convertible, unified currency zone. There are no controls on current or capital account transactions, no exchange-rate bands, and no government approval for inward or outward remittances. Profit remittances, dividends, royalties, loan repayments, and capital repatriation are unrestricted. Panama applies territorial taxation; non-Panamanian source income is outside the domestic tax net, and foreign shareholders may repatriate Panamanian-source profits after the 10% dividend withholding tax (subject to double-tax-treaty relief, if applicable). No pre-clearance or exchange-control permit is required for any cross-border capital movement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to surrender foreign currency. A Panamanian exporter may retain 100% of export receipts in any freely convertible currency at any bank, local or offshore. Export earnings may remain offshore indefinitely, provided the territorial tax return accurately identifies Panamanian-source revenue and correctly excludes offshore receipts from taxable income.

Central bank supervision: Panama maintains no statutory central bank; the U.S. dollar circulates as legal tender under the Monetary Agreement with the United States. Banking supervision is vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), while Banco Nacional de Panamá carries out fiscal-agency and clearing-house functions. No foreign exchange administration exists because the monetary standard is full dollarization.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility is Low-to-Medium. Panama has no bespoke e-money institution (EMI) licensing regime analogous to the EU; fintech activities generally require a banking license, a 'Sociedad Financiera' license, or Sandbox authorisation from the SBP. Nonresident offshore EMIs (for example, Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) may be used for liquidity pooling and cross-border collections without a Panamanian license, but local banks will typically reject remittance counterparties that cannot provide complete KYC and beneficial-owner data. Key constraints: (i) EMIs are not recognised as banking counterparties eligible for custody of aircraft-escrow proceeds under SBP guidance; (ii) any EMI receiving funds from a Panamanian account must furnish an AML/CFT compliance statement and UBO disclosure; (iii) the SBP's transaction reporting threshold (USD 10,000 equivalent under Law 23 of 2015) applies equally to bank and EMI channels. The recommended approach is dual-rail: maintain a licensed Panama bank account for settlement and use an FATF-equivalent supervised EMI only for low-value payouts, with the EMI added as a sub-account at the Panamanian bank after prior approval of the compliance officer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections from foreign shareholders must be routed by SWIFT MT103 with the remitter's true name, a source-of-funds statement, and field 70 specifying 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / PAID-IN CAPITAL'. Funds must be credited to the Panama S.A.'s USD operational account at a licensed bank. No pre-registration, central-bank approval, or exchange-control licence is required. Retain the following compliance file: (i) board or shareholder resolution approving the capital injection; (ii) shareholder registry update; (iii) bank-issued credit notification; (iv) UBO declarations when the injection originates from a trust or treasury entity. If the single injection exceeds USD 1,000,000, expect enhanced due diligence requiring a comprehensive source-of-wealth summary and audited financial statements of the remitting parent or beneficial owner. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Panama Transfer Pricing regime (Law 8 of 2010 and Decree 28 of 2012) requires preparation of a Local File and, where applicable, a Master File when the aggregate value of related-party transactions entered into by the Panamanian taxpayer equals or exceeds B/.1,000,000.00 (US$1,000,000, as the Balboa is pegged to the US dollar). A Master File is also required when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenues exceed B/.300,000,000.00. For taxpayers not meeting these thresholds, a simplified Declaration of Transactions with Related Parties is still required annually.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Ley 254 of 2020) and implementing rules impose reporting and substance requirements on tax-resident entities carrying on 'relevant activities', which include distribution and service centres. For a surplus commercial aircraft parts distributor that is tax-resident in Panama, at least one local director, physical premises in Panama, or adequate local employees and expenditure proportionate to the profits must be maintained and reported annually via the ‘Aviso de Sustancia Económica’. If the Sociedad Anónima is managed from abroad, compensates its directors outside Panama, and has no Panamanian-source operating activity, it is considered a non-resident for ESR purposes and has no filing obligation. However, claiming tax-residency under a DTA while lacking economic substance will trigger ESR penalties and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under Article 216-A of the Penal Code is punishable with imprisonment of three to five years and a fine equal to two to four times the amount of tax evaded. The DGI may also impose administrative surcharges of up to 20% plus interest on the unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false returns or direct treasury routing schemes are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's tax debt. The DGI may apply civil seizure against personal assets, and a criminal conviction can result in a travel ban and disqualification from holding office in Panamanian companies.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Failure to meet Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 254 of 2020) results in fines of up to B/.100,000 for inadequate substance, separate fines for failure to file the annual substance notice, and, in severe or repeat cases, cancellation of the company's registration and denial of the certificate of tax residence.

Director personal liability: Directors and the appointed resident agent are personally liable for false declarations of substance. Penalties include personal fines, suspension or permanent revocation of the resident agent license, and criminal sanctions for signing fraudulent statements. A director who is removed may also be barred from entering Panama pending tax proceedings.
AML – Money Laundering and Unregulated Payment Flows

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015 and the Penal Code, money laundering offences carry imprisonment of five to ten years, asset forfeiture, and fines of up to B/.1,000,000. Administrative AML fines for non-reporting subjects reach B/.500,000.00 per violation.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and any beneficial owner who knowingly uses shell entities or nominee vehicles for laundering or sanctions evasion face criminal prosecution, travel bans, extradition eligibility, and civil liability up to the full value of assets laundered. Individual directors may also be disqualified from management of any Panamanian regulated entity.
