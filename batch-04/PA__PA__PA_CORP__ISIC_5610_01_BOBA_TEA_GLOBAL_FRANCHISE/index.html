Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Autoridad Nacional de Ingresos Públicos (ANIP) – formerly Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; official portal: https://www.anip.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must (1) engage a duly licensed Panamanian lawyer and register as a Panama branch or reproduce its incorporation or foreign company registration with the Public Registry; (2) file the public deed, bylaws or constitutive documents, and evidence of appointment of a resident agent and legal representative with the ANIP; (3) obtain a permanent/non-resident RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) through the ANIP electronic portal; (4) issue an 'Aviso de Operación' or aviso de inscripción to commence activities; (5) obtain a digital signature certificate for electronic filings; and (6) register as an ITBMS taxpayer, if applicable. The complete procedure generally takes 2–5 business days after presentation of apostilled and Spanish-translated documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a US-style check-the-box system. A Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) is an entity-level taxpayer subject to corporate income tax only on Panama-source income. Available statutory elections include the 'Pequeño Contribuyente' (small taxpayer) regime for annual gross income up to B/.150,000, optional grouping or consolidated filing for qualifying economic groups, elections regarding transferable tax-loss carryforwards, and the CFC/anti-hybrid elections under Law 23 of 2020 implementing BEPS Action 2 and Action 3. No fiscal transparency election is allowed for a commercial S.A.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic turnover threshold triggers ITBMS registration; every person or legal entity that carries out taxable transfers of goods or services in Panama, or imports goods, must register with ANIP and obtain an RUC from the first taxable transaction. A reduced compliance regime ('Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente') is available for annual gross income not exceeding B/.150,000 (Balboas), but registration remains mandatory once taxable activity begins.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods are zero-rated (0% ITBMS) when the goods are physically removed from Panama under a valid customs export declaration and the exporter retains evidence of title transfer to a foreign buyer. Services, including franchise royalties and software or supply-chain management services, are outside the Panamanian ITBMS scope if the recipient is non-resident, the service is wholly performed outside Panama, or the benefit is enjoyed abroad. The supplier must be registered, file export statements, and maintain contemporaneous documentation proving the economic activity took place outside Panama. Input ITBMS directly attributable to zero-rated or non-taxed exports is creditable, and excess credits may be offset against other taxes or refunded following ANIP verification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera – DUA)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Currency Wire Receipt from a Panamanian bank demonstrating receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Export contract, purchase order, and proof of foreign buyer identity
  • ITBMS Export Certificate or ANIP export-supporting declaration (when required)

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica – FE) for registered taxpayers on a phased schedule. Invoices must be transmitted in real time to ANIP through the authorized electronic invoicing platform or via ANIP-approved API/Web Services, and each invoice must receive a tax-authority validation code and a QR code. No invoice may be legally issued without the ANIP digital authentication; non-validated invoices are not deductible or creditable. The system contemplates four control modes: online transmission, offline contingency, mobile/portable facturador, and ERP integration.

Fraudulent or inadequately documented export zero-rating claims are subject to immediate rejection, retrospective clawback of any refunded ITBMS, monetary indexation, and interest accruing from the date of the original refund. Administrative penalties may reach up to 200% of the tax understated or refunded, and criminal tax fraud convictions carry imprisonment of 2 to 5 years under Panamanian law. Failure to repatriate foreign sales proceeds within 180 days may cause the foreign revenue to be recharacterized as taxable Panama-source income and invalidate the zero-rating substantiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy using the United States Dollar (USD) as legal tender; both current account and capital account transactions are freely convertible with no restrictions on inbound or outbound transfers; profit remittances require no special governmental approval beyond standard bank compliance documentation; the absence of a central bank means no domestic exchange controls exist, and repatriation of dividends, royalties, and franchise fees is unrestricted when supported by proper corporate and tax documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratios apply to export proceeds in Panama; exporters may freely retain, disburse, or repatriate all foreign-currency receipts without any obligation to convert into local currency; the only administrative requirement is accurate reporting to the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) for territorial tax compliance and compliance with anti-money laundering (AML) transaction reporting thresholds under the Superintendencia de Bancos.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as the primary banking regulator, with Banco Nacional de Panamá as the state-owned financial agent but not a central bank capable of issuing domestic currency; fiscal and monetary policy coordination through the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate feasibility; Panama does not have a dedicated e-money institution (EMI) licensing framework comparable to the EU, but fintechs may operate under the general non-bank financial services regime through the SBP's fintech law (Article 3 of the Decree Law 2 of 2018); unlicensed offshore digital EMIs cannot directly hold client funds or provide payment services in Panama without partnering with a licensed local bank; however, digital EMIs can process cross-border payments using correspondent banking and open virtual accounts through Panama's SWIFT-connected banking system; the viability rating for using an offshore EMI as a primary treasury vehicle is 'Low' due to strict AML correspondent banking due-diligence and the risk of de-risking by international partners, while an EMI with a local SBP license or a bank-backed fintech arrangement is rated 'Medium'.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant capital inflows from foreign franchise investors should be routed via SWIFT MT103 messages to a Panamanian commercial bank using standardized purpose codes, commonly 'FDI' (foreign direct investment) or 'Genesis' codes under PAN liquidity reporting, with supporting capital contribution agreements, share purchase documents, and bank confirmation of the exact business purpose; all inbound funds must be sourced from the identified investor's bank account and be free of any connection to high-risk jurisdictions; for a Panama S.A., capital injections can be reflected as paid-in capital or subordinated shareholders' loans; the bank requires the completion of the 'Oringo' (Foreign Exchange and Capital Transactions) form only if the transaction exceeds US$10,000 for AML reporting, or if the bank's internal controls set a lower threshold; the routing must avoid cash or precious-metals couriers and use direct correspondent banks of the Panama bank (typically in the US or Europe) to maintain regulatory continuity.Outbound trade settlements for syrup, packaging, and equipment purchases must be executed through SWIFT MT103 wires from the Panamanian operational account, accompanied by commercial invoices, packing lists, and customs declarations; for higher-value or highly regular supply purchases, a commercial documentary letter of credit (LC, UCP 600) may be established at a Panamanian bank, with the beneficiary bank and the shipping documentation (bill of lading, commercial invoice, certificate of origin) specified in the LC terms; all disbursements above the bank's due-diligence threshold require transaction-level screening against OFAC sanction lists and FATF watchlists; recommended rails are the SWIFT gpi network for rapid and traceable payments, and the local ACH (ACH Panama) for settlements to domestic suppliers and franchise employees.No local FX forward products are typically needed owing to full dollarization, but Panamanian banks and international broker-dealers may offer cross-currency swaps and FX forwards for non-USD foreign exchange risk (e.g., exposure to CNY or EUR in franchise royalty or syrup purchases); such derivatives are permissible under SBP license rules and are executed through a bank-approved master agreement (ISDA) to be documented as part of treasury policy.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not prohibited but are rarely used in Panama because the USD is legal tender, making currency risk primarily secondary to trade-partner currencies; banks will permit NDFs on minor currencies if the underlying commercial transaction is proven.Currency swaps (cross-currency interest-rate swaps) may be arranged with foreign counterparty banks for multi-year franchise revenue hedging, subject to credit-risk limits and intra-committee approval; Panamanian banks do not require special regulatory authorization for hedging derivatives, but transaction reporting to the SBP under the derivatives statistics mandate applies for notional amounts exceeding USD 1 million.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderate feasibility; Panama does not have a dedicated e-money institution (EMI) licensing framework comparable to the EU, but fintechs may operate under the general non-bank financial services regime through the SBP's fintech law (Article 3 of the Decree Law 2 of 2018); unlicensed offshore digital EMIs cannot directly hold client funds or provide payment services in Panama without partnering with a licensed local bank; however, digital EMIs can process cross-border payments using correspondent banking and open virtual accounts through Panama's SWIFT-connected banking system; the viability rating for using an offshore EMI as a primary treasury vehicle is 'Low' due to strict AML correspondent banking due-diligence and the risk of de-risking by international partners, while an EMI with a local SBP license or a bank-backed fintech arrangement is rated 'Medium'.

Disclose exclusively in the informational annex and preserve evidence of foreign situs: foreign IP registrations, sub-license agreements, and bills of lading; misclassifying Panama-source income as foreign-source is a criminal fraud red flag. Line 4 - Pérdidas compensables Operating loss carryforward applied against current-year Panama-source taxable income. Utilisation limited to five fiscal years; losses must derive exclusively from Panama-source activities and cannot offset foreign-source profit. Apply the 25% statutory rate; the CAIR 2% gross-receipts minimum tax floor has been suspended by successive fiscal laws and must be revalidated for the specific filing year. Reconcile prepared advance payment vouchers and any Panama WHT suffered; excess credits are recoverable through the formal DGI refund procedure.
