Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); electronic services portal SERTRACEN/SICE (dgi.gob.pa) and Registro Único de Empresas (RUES); customs and export-related registrations with the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Sociedad Anónima: (1) incorporate by public deed before a Panamanian notary and file with the Registro Público de Panamá; (2) appoint a resident agent to obtain the corporate certificate and legalized copy; (3) apply for the municipal Aviso de Operación through the RUES or the competent municipality; (4) obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) via SERTRACEN, presenting the Public Registry deed, notarized board resolutions appointing the legal representative, and the Aviso de Operación; (5) enrol as an ITBMS taxpayer; and (6) register as an exporter at ANA, including a customs code and digital signature for issuing Declaraciones Únicas Aduaneras. No minimum capital, local residence, or local employment requirement applies; non-resident ownership is unrestricted.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — entity-level taxation on Panamanian-source income only (Código Fiscal situs rules). Foreign-source income, capital gains, dividends, and interest are excluded from the Panamanian tax base, but income may be recharacterized as Panamanian-source where a Panamanian entity lacks economic substance or acts as a mere intermediary (Law 254 of 2021 and related DGI substance rules). — For a non-resident-owned Sociedad Anónima: (1) incorporate by public deed before a Panamanian notary and file with the Registro Público de Panamá; (2) appoint a resident agent to obtain the corporate certificate and legalized copy; (3) apply for the municipal Aviso de Operación through the RUES or the competent municipality; (4) obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) via SERTRACEN, presenting the Public Registry deed, notarized board resolutions appointing the legal representative, and the Aviso de Operación; (5) enrol as an ITBMS taxpayer; and (6) register as an exporter at ANA, including a customs code and digital signature for issuing Declaraciones Únicas Aduaneras. No minimum capital, local residence, or local employment requirement applies; non-resident ownership is unrestricted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — entity-level taxation on Panamanian-source income only (Código Fiscal situs rules). Foreign-source income, capital gains, dividends, and interest are excluded from the Panamanian tax base, but income may be recharacterized as Panamanian-source where a Panamanian entity lacks economic substance or acts as a mere intermediary (Law 254 of 2021 and related DGI substance rules).

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Panamanian law. Available statutory elections for a locally operative S.A. include: (i) the Régimen de Tributación Alternativa (RTA), permitting a flat 10% income tax on net Panamanian-source taxable income subject to a minimum alternative tax of 4.67% of gross Panamanian-source income, in lieu of the standard 25% corporate income tax; and (ii) special incentive regimes applicable to re-export operations, such as Zona Libre de Colón, Área Económica Especial del Pacífico, and SEM (Sede de Empresa Multinacional) regimes. Panama does not offer fiscal-transparency, hybrid-mismatch, or partnership basis-adjustment elections for S.A. entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 (Panamanian Balboa, at parity with USD) of annual gross sales of taxable goods or services; mandatory registration applies regardless of turnover for commercial, industrial, import, or export activities, since customs clearance and export zero-rating require an active ITBMS registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for the 0% ITBMS rate under Article 1057-A of the Código Fiscal where the goods physically leave Panamanian customs territory within 90 days of invoicing, or are delivered into a bonded/free-zone regime for subsequent export. Services provided to non-residents and exploited entirely outside Panama are also zero-rated. For the wholesale physical charter of prilled urea and potassium chloride, zero-rating requires a genuine commodity sale to a non-resident buyer, shipment from Panama via an international carrier, and transfer of title overseas consistent with the agreed Incoterms. The exporter must be an active ITBMS taxpayer; input VAT on local purchases is creditable against zero-rated export turnovers, and excess credits are refundable in cash upon DGI resolution.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera - DUA, duly accepted and confirmed by ANA)
  • Bill of Lading (clean negotiable ocean or air bill naming the Panamanian exporter as shipper and a foreign consignee)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank receipt or certificate evidencing collection of the export proceeds into a Panamanian bank account or an exporter-owned foreign bank account)

Mandatory under Panama's phased fiscal electronic invoicing mandate (Facturación Electrónica — SICE). Taxpayers must issue invoices exclusively through DGI-authorized technological providers, transmit invoice data to the DGI in real time for validation and approval before issuance to the recipient, and include a DGI-generated QR/code and qualified electronic signature. Export invoices are subject to the same real-time fiscal clearance; paper-based or non-cleared invoices cannot support ITBMS credits or export zero-rating. Non-compliance triggers fines, suspension of the taxpayer's authorization, and disallowance of input deductions.

The DGI and ANA jointly audit 0%-rated export claims, with particular focus on commodity re-invoicing chains, circular shipments, and delayed foreign-exchange repatriation. Failure to substantiate the physical exit of goods or to repatriate proceeds within the mandatory 180-day period causes the transaction to be reclassified as a taxable domestic sale, resulting in retroactive ITBMS at 7%, a recalculation surcharge of 25%–75% of the tax involved, statutory moratorium interest of 1% per month, and denial of related refund credits. Fraudulent or fictitious export documentation activates criminal tax-fraud penalties, extends the ordinary five-year prescription to ten years for retrospective clawback, and may result in suspension or cancellation of the exporter's ANA registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized economy with no separate national currency; both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible and no Central Bank authorization is required for cross-border wiring, capital injection, or profit repatriation. Remittance constraints are limited to regulatory reporting: financial institutions must report cash or electronic transactions equal to or exceeding USD 10,000 to the UAF, and all onboarding requires FATCA/CRS self-certifications. Dividends distributed from Panamanian-source profits carry a 10% withholding tax, whereas dividends derived from foreign-source income fall outside the territorial tax net, making profit remittance to non-resident shareholders essentially frictionless.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline; exporters may retain and disburse USD proceeds freely because USD is legal tender. The trader must nevertheless preserve a complete export/invoice file per shipment to satisfy OFAC screening and bank trade-file due-diligence queries, and all receipts over USD 10,000 automatically generate UAF monitoring reports.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank and no domestic currency issuance. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panama (SBP) exercises prudential supervision over banks, payment systems, and fintech licensees; the Unidad de Analisis Financiero (UAF) administers AML/CFT reporting under Law 23 of 2015; the Ministerio de Economia y Finanzas (MEF) oversees fiscal policy under full dollarization. The US Dollar is the sole legal tender and no exchange-control authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Panama does not recognize the EU/UK Electronic Money Institution (EMI) concept; the SBP regulates payment-service providers under a fintech sandbox framework (Acuerdo SBP 0001-2021, as amended) and requires local licensing. Offshore digital EMIs cannot independently maintain operational USD accounts for a Panamanian S.A. unless they establish a licensed local branch or partner with a locally chartered bank; therefore the viable treasury design is to use tier-1 licensed Panamanian banks as the primary payment spine and to restrict EMI usage to ancillary FX-conversion or collection services through a licensed local processor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The parent or shareholder wires USD directly to the S.A.'s US Dollar account at the chosen Panamanian bank via SWIFT MT103; the payment detail field must state 'Aporte de capital / Capital Contribution' and must be preceded by the notarized board resolution authorizing the share issuance, the share subscription ledger entry, and the source-of-funds file. All transfers equal to or exceeding USD 10,000 generate UAF reporting, and the beneficiary bank will typically apply a 3-10 business-day verification hold pending OFAC screening and compliance clearance. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends distributed out of Panamanian-source income (applied as a definitive withholding tax). No withholding applies on distributions of previously taxed profits, and no withholding is levied on dividends sourced exclusively from foreign-source income.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months after the fiscal year-end. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the filing and payment deadline is March 31 of the following year. Panama does not impose mandatory quarterly income tax declarations on corporations; however, quarterly estimated advance payments are triggered when the prior year's tax exceeded USD 200,000. The return must be accompanied by the sworn statement of the income tax and the audited financial statements if gross revenue exceeds USD 750,000 or capital exceeds USD 1,500,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB 1,500,000 (Panama Balboa, at par with USD) in related-party transactions during the fiscal year. Above this threshold, the taxpayer must prepare and file a statutory transfer pricing declaration (Declaración Informativa de Precios de Transferencia, Formulario 4) and maintain contemporaneous Local File and Master File. Transactions below the threshold are not exempt from the arm's-length principle; they require only reasonable documentation and disclosure in the annual income tax return.

Economic substance scope: Panama maintains full Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) under Executive Decree No. 56 of December 2019, applicable to legal persons engaged in prescribed 'relevant activities'. Wholesale physical commodity trade is not per se a prescribed relevant activity under the ESR list unless the entity also carries on shipping, logistics, headquarters, financing, or intellectual-property activities as revenue-generating activities. The fertilizer-trading S.A. must nevertheless demonstrate substantive commercial presence through its registered agent, local office or appropriately located management, real employees, and adequate bank accounts, especially if it also conducts chartering of vessels or holds maritime registry benefits. Failure to satisfy economic substance in an applicable activity subjects the entity to reporting sanctions, fines up to USD 2,000,000, deregistration, and disqualification of the resident agent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty under the Código Fiscal: imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, plus a fine equivalent to 50% to 100% of the tax evaded. In aggravated cases with falsified documents, the fine may rise to triple the evaded tax and the prison term may reach 10 years. Administrative surcharges and monthly interest also accrue.

Director personal liability: Directors, president, and resident agent who sign false returns may be jointly and severally liable for the tax, surcharges, and fines. The comptroller's court and criminal courts may order travel bans, attachment of personal assets, and disqualification from holding office in any Panamanian corporation; custodial sentences are enforceable for directors who knowingly execute sham export transactions.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance (ESR) regime and the ongoing beneficial-ownership transparency rules, the entity may be declared inactive, struck from the Registro Público, or subjected to a fine of up to USD 1,000,000 for ESR non-compliance, plus annual penalty of USD 100,000 for continued non-compliance. The Resident Agent may be fined USD 1,000 to USD 10,000 and the corporation may be denied the issuance of tax receipts and immigration endorsements.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines for aggravated non-compliance and possible criminal liability for misuse of corporate form. A court may pierce the veil, ordering the directors to repay all transaction proceeds into the tax arrears, and may impose travel restrictions and a prohibition on managing other Panamanian legal entities.
AML

Statutory sanction: Panama's AML regime (Law 23 of 2015, as amended) imposes fines on the legal entity from USD 10,000 to USD 2,000,000, suspension of operations up to one year, and cancellation of the license where applicable. The Financial Analysis Unit (Unidad de Análisis Financiero) may also order asset freezing and referral to the Public Ministry for money-laundering prosecution under the Criminal Code, with principal money laundering penalties of 5 to 12 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Officers executing or approving the trade-based money-laundering scheme face individual criminal prosecution, seizure of personal and corporate assets, travel bans, and imprisonment of 5 to 12 years. Directors may also be held civilly liable to defrauded creditors and the tax administration for the full amount laundered.
Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: DGI may make an adjustment to the arm's-length price, assessing the full difference as taxable income at 25%, plus a penalty of 25% to 100% of the additional tax assessed, and monthly interest. Failure to file the TP information return carries a separate fine of up to USD 25,000 per infraction.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the income tax return incorporating the transfer-pricing adjustment are jointly liable for the additional tax, penalties, and interest if the DGI demonstrates fraudulent intent or reckless disregard. Criminal charges for fiscal fraud may attach when the TP manipulation exceeded the 50% threshold of the tax evaded.
Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: The DGI requires the payer to remit the withheld amount plus a surcharge of 10% if paid within two months after the deadline and higher surcharges thereafter, and a fine equal to 50% of the withheld tax when it was not declared. The withheld amount is not deductible, and the payer becomes personally liable as a withholding agent.

Director personal liability: Directors and the chief financial officer are personally liable for the un-withheld tax and penalties; criminal liability for defrauding the treasury may attach where the dividend distribution was deliberately mislabeled as a foreign-source distribution or as a shareholder loan without commercial substance.
