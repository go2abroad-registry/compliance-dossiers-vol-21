Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Panamanian regulatory framework provides unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange-control laws, no licensing requirements for the purchase or sale of foreign currency, and no restrictions on profit remittances or dividend distributions. Repatriation of earnings is subject only to the territoriality principle under Panamanian tax law and to standard anti-money-laundering (AML) recordkeeping obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement exists in Panama. Exporters are entitled to retain and dispose of export receipts in any currency on the open market, including U.S. dollars and other freely convertible currencies. There are no mandatory conversion ratios, mandatory timelines, or central-bank reporting obligations for trade proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank or dedicated foreign exchange administration. Monetary oversight and financial system regulation are exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance; the United States dollar is the official legal tender and reserve instrument.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Panama does not operate a comprehensive electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime. Offshore digital EMIs are not permitted to take deposits or provide banking services without a full banking license issued by the SBP. A restricted financial institution or money-transfer license is possible as a payments-only vehicle, but credit extension and capital escrow functions remain unavailable; a regulated Panamanian bank is therefore required for this wholesale petrochemical treasury structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections are remitted by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank transfers) in U.S. dollars directly to the corporate operating and escrow accounts. Panama imposes no exchange-control approval or central-bank remittance code; however, banks require the descriptive field to state 'capital contribution for PA_CORP Sociedad Anónima' and require a board capitalization resolution plus a clear source-of-funds declaration for compliance with the SBP’s General Resolution No. 006-2017 AML rules.Trade settlements are executed mainly through SWIFT MT103 direct transfers for commercial payments and through irrevocable documentary Letters of Credit transmitted via SWIFT MT700. The standard route for bulk petrochemical polymer purchases includes a confirmation bank outside Panama, with the Panamanian corporate account opening the LC under a cross-border revolving facility and each drawing backed by a commercial invoice, bill of lading, inspection certificate, and cargo insurance documents. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 2,000,000 (Panama Balboa at par to USD) aggregate related-party cross-border transactions in the fiscal year triggers mandatory Local File documentation. A consolidated group with global revenue exceeding B/. 50,000,000 triggers Master File preparation for the Panamanian S.A.

Economic substance scope: Panama applies an economic substance doctrine derived from Law 70 of 2017 and implementing regulations of DGI and the Ministry of Economy and Finance. The doctrine obliges a Panamanian S.A. engaged in cross-border petrochemical trading to demonstrate in each tax year the presence of real local management, adequate personnel, and physical premises in Panama. Comprehensive board minutes, local negotiation files, and warehouse/freight documentation are required to substantiate the business substance and to defend the territorial tax exemption from being recharacterized as shell activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Territoriality Claim

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of the full corporate income tax at 25%; penalty equal to 100% of the tax evaded; monthly interest surcharge of 1.5%; criminal indictment for tax fraud resulting in fines up to USD 100,000 and confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved the false declaration are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax. Personal criminal exposure includes imprisonment for 3 to 8 years, prohibition from serving as director of any Panamanian entity, and a travel ban constraining departure from Panamanian soil pending resolution.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Cancellation of the corporate registry registration and commercial license; administrative fine up to B/. 50,000; forfeiture of the benefit of the territorial tax exemption for all fiscal years in which substance is absent; mandatory closure by the Public Registry.

Director personal liability: Directors and registered agents may be declared personally liable for corporate debts and taxes if the company is deemed a sham; they face disqualification from forming new Panamanian companies for up to 5 years, restitution claims from commercial creditors, and judicial travel restrictions where criminal misuse of corporate form is established.
Money Laundering / AML-CFT Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Panama’s Criminal Code and Anti-Money Laundering Law, the corporate vehicle faces asset seizure and confiscation, fines of up to ten times the laundered amount, and mandatory regulatory delisting. The S.A. can lose its ability to operate within the port logistics and petrochemical free-zone sectors.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face imprisonment of 5 to 10 years, personal forfeiture of assets traced to the laundering scheme, and an entry ban for non-resident officers who enabled the conduits. The Superintendency can also impose personal travel bans while investigations are pending.
Transfer Pricing / Misinvoicing of Petrochemical Exports

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment increasing the Panamanian tax base; secondary adjustment treating the artificial price difference as a constructive dividend distribution subject to 10% WHT; penalty equal to 60% of the TP adjustment for willful noncompliance; separate documentation penalty of B/. 25,000 per missing TP file.

Director personal liability: Directors who executed or authorized the misinvoicing are personally exposed to criminal charges for filing false tax documents and commercial fraud. Upon conviction they face imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, cancellation of their legal capacity to act as importers/exporters, and inclusion in the DGI public list of non-compliant taxpayers.
