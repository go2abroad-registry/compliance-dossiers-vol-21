Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Panamá; centralized portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa (electronic services: ETax / SIFE / RUC registry).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or its duly appointed Panama resident fiscal representative must register the entity in the Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC) before the DGI. Required filings include: (i) certificate of incorporation and current bylaws attested from the Public Registry (Registro Público de Panamá); (ii) notarized power of attorney appointing a resident legal representative; (iii) identification documents of directors/shareholders; (iv) physical address declaration for the Panama registered office; and (v) if applicable, a DG freight forwarding license. The DGI issues a NIT/RUC certificate; no minimum paid-up capital is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope (entity-level taxation on Panama-source income only). A Panamanian Sociedad Anónima is a juridical person subject to corporate income tax (ISR); foreign-source income and capital gains arising outside Panama are outside the territorial taxation net. It is not a pass-through entity; no entity-exempt status applies unless a special export-processing-zone license is separately granted. — A foreign entity or its duly appointed Panama resident fiscal representative must register the entity in the Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC) before the DGI. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope (entity-level taxation on Panama-source income only). A Panamanian Sociedad Anónima is a juridical person subject to corporate income tax (ISR); foreign-source income and capital gains arising outside Panama are outside the territorial taxation net. It is not a pass-through entity; no entity-exempt status applies unless a special export-processing-zone license is separately granted.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch/mandatory disclosure elections are available under Panamanian tax law for an S.A. The only administratively recognized status election is the 'pequeño contribuyente' simplified regime for taxpayers with annual gross turnover up to B/.36,000; that election affects filing frequency and ITBMS settlement, but does not change the S.A.'s entity-level corporate tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 (equivalent to USD 36,000, Panamanian balboa pegged 1:1 to US dollar) of annual gross taxable sales/services; below this level an entity may register as a pequeño contribuyente or, if taxable operations exceed it, must register as a full ITBMS taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of movable goods and, where qualifying, offshore services are subject to ITBMS at 0%, provided the seller is an active RUC holder, the goods are physically shipped from Panama to a foreign destination, or the service is supplied to a non-resident recipient and used wholly outside Panama. For DG freight forwarding, the 0% applies only to services directly connected to the export movement and supported by the export transport documentation; ancillary local services (e.g., local storage or domestic haulage) remain taxable at 7%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / DUE) filed with the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) and electronic validation in SISCOM
  • Bill of Lading or original Air Waybill issued by the carrier showing Panama as port/place of shipment and foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice endorsed 'EXPORTACIÓN - ITBMS 0%' with the DGI-authorized electronic invoice code
  • Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods (IMO/multi-modal) evidencing the UN-classified dangerous goods shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit evidence from a Panama-licensed bank showing transfer of export proceeds
  • Insurance certificate/packing list only where required by ANA risk assessment

Mandatory electronic invoicing applies to all active RUC holders through DGI's Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (SIFE). Invoice documents must be transmitted and validated by DGI in near-real time; paper invoices require DGI-authorized fiscal printing/QR seal. Each export invoice must carry the DGI authorized electronic invoice code and no ITBMS suspended amount. Failed or out-of-band invoices are inadmissible for customs and ITBMS credit purposes.

Export zero-rating is aggressively audited by the DGI and ANA. If the export documentation is found false, incomplete, or non-repatriated proceeds are involved, the exporter is required to reinstate the 7% ITBMS plus interest from the date of invoice, a surcharge of up to 200% of the omitted tax in fraud cases, and administrative fines, with potential criminal liability for customs smuggling and tax evasion under the Código Fiscal and Código Penal. Prior-year zero-rating credits may be retroactively reversed within the statute of limitations (generally 5 years).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de facto dollarization eliminates local currency convertibility risk: both current and capital account transactions are freely convertible, with zero surrender, prior-authorization, or repatriation requirements. Dividend/profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted but are operationally subject to the 10% dividend withholding tax, the distributable-dividends declaration platform, and the SBP-required source-of-funds and OFAC/sanctions-compliant USD clearing corridor.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on DG forwarding export receipts. Proceeds may be retained in USD or any foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts; reporting is limited to electronic invoicing (e-Factura), ITBMS tax filings, and standard corporate ISR returns.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP — Superintendency of Banks of Panama); Panama has no traditional central bank. The US Dollar circulates as full legal tender under the 1904 Currency Law, and the SBP is the de facto banking and foreign-exchange supervisory authority, with Banco Nacional de Panamá acting as the state clearing and government collection agent.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Medium (constrained). Panama's Fintech Law No. 70 of 2021 and SBP innovation regulations permit licensed local fintech and EMI-type virtual accounts, but offshore digital EMIs without an SBP banking license cannot serve as settlement-grade treasury rails (no LC issuance, no escrow, no correspondent USD clearing). Such EMIs are viable only for ancillary vendor payouts, petty-cash floats, and low-volume disbursements; any high-value DG forwarding settlement must route through an SBP-licensed commercial bank to avoid correspondent de-risking and FATF 'nested account' refusals.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is via SWIFT MT103/MT103 GPI same-day USD clearing from the foreign shareholder's bank to the Panama receiving bank, with field 70 noting 'Capital Contribution — Share Subscription — DG Forwarding OpCo'. No FX conversion occurs because of full dollarization. The receiving bank mandates pre-funding submission of stamped board/shareholder minutes, capitalization schedule, source-of-funds questionnaires, and sanctions screening on both parties; wires arriving without matched corporate documentation are placed in suspense and may trigger EDD.Primary rails are SWIFT MT103 for general DG trade payables and MT103 GPI for time-critical settlements, supplemented by commercial documentary letters of credit (usance or at-sight) issued under ICC UCP600 and INCOTERMS 2020 for high-value or high-risk chemical purchases. Standby letters of credit support DG tender guarantees. Late filing: fine of B/. 50 per month or fraction (cap B/. 500), plus 5% monthly surcharge on the tax due (cap 50%), plus legal interest; submission of false data: penalty up to 100% of the omitted tax and criminal tax-fraud prosecution (2–5 years imprisonment).
Casilla 141 Costos directos e indirectos: costs incurred to generate the taxable Panama-source revenue. Non-resident filers may deduct only costs that are necessary, evidenced by electronic F-20 invoices, and consistent with transfer pricing for intercompany freight and logistics charges. Late filing: fine of B/. 50 per month or fraction (cap B/. 500), plus 5% monthly surcharge on the tax due (cap 50%), plus legal interest; submission of false data: penalty up to 100% of the omitted tax and criminal tax-fraud prosecution (2–5 years imprisonment).
Casilla 149 Renta neta gravable: Panama-source gross revenue less attributable costs and deductions. Non-resident filers should reconcile this figure with the quarterly anticipo base; a mismatch triggers automatic DGI cross-checks. Late filing: fine of B/. 50 per month or fraction (cap B/. 500), plus 5% monthly surcharge on the tax due (cap 50%), plus legal interest; submission of false data: penalty up to 100% of the omitted tax and criminal tax-fraud prosecution (2–5 years imprisonment).
Casilla 154 Impuesto sobre la renta determinado: 25% multiplying factor applied to the net taxable base. Non-resident filers must verify that no territorial exemption is improperly applied to Panama-source profits before computing the 25% rate. Late filing: fine of B/. 50 per month or fraction (cap B/. 500), plus 5% monthly surcharge on the tax due (cap 50%), plus legal interest; submission of false data: penalty up to 100% of the omitted tax and criminal tax-fraud prosecution (2–5 years imprisonment).
Casilla 155 Créditos por anticipos y retenciones: credits for quarterly Form 307 prepayments and third-party withholding receipts. Non-resident filers must attach the stamped/payment evidence of the four 2% anticipos; missing anticipos trigger recargo assessments. Late filing: fine of B/. 50 per month or fraction (cap B/. 500), plus 5% monthly surcharge on the tax due (cap 50%), plus legal interest; submission of false data: penalty up to 100% of the omitted tax and criminal tax-fraud prosecution (2–5 years imprisonment).
Casilla 160 Saldo por pagar o crédito a favor: final settlement or carryable credit. Non-resident filers must pay the balance through the DGI payment gateway or authorized banks; a refund claim is subject to DGI audit. Late filing: fine of B/. 50 per month or fraction (cap B/. 500), plus 5% monthly surcharge on the tax due (cap 50%), plus legal interest; submission of false data: penalty up to 100% of the omitted tax and criminal tax-fraud prosecution (2–5 years imprisonment).
Casilla 101 Identificación del contribuyente (RUC). Non-resident filers must file even with a nil balance; RUC must remain active. Non-payment/underpayment of quarterly anticipos: 5% monthly surcharge on the unpaid amount (cap 50%) plus legal interest; failure to file: B/. 50 monthly fine (cap B/. 500); repeated non-compliance triggers immediate full annual settlement and consolidated debt enforcement against the entity and its assets.
Casilla 402 Trimestre fiscal: the calendar quarter being declared. Non-resident filers must match the quarter to the annual fiscal period registered with DGI. Non-payment/underpayment of quarterly anticipos: 5% monthly surcharge on the unpaid amount (cap 50%) plus legal interest; failure to file: B/. 50 monthly fine (cap B/. 500); repeated non-compliance triggers immediate full annual settlement and consolidated debt enforcement against the entity and its assets.
Casilla 403 Ingresos brutos gravados del trimestre: Panama-source gross revenue for the quarter. Non-resident filers should use the same territorial allocation methodology as the annual Form 301 to avoid inconsistencies. Non-payment/underpayment of quarterly anticipos: 5% monthly surcharge on the unpaid amount (cap 50%) plus legal interest; failure to file: B/. 50 monthly fine (cap B/. 500); repeated non-compliance triggers immediate full annual settlement and consolidated debt enforcement against the entity and its assets.
Casilla 404 Anticipo a pagar: 2% of the gross taxable revenue line (or the substitute tax-computation method chosen by the taxpayer). Non-resident filers must compute the 2% strictly on Panama-source revenue; foreign-source amounts are excluded only where the allocation is defensible. Non-payment/underpayment of quarterly anticipos: 5% monthly surcharge on the unpaid amount (cap 50%) plus legal interest; failure to file: B/. 50 monthly fine (cap B/. 500); repeated non-compliance triggers immediate full annual settlement and consolidated debt enforcement against the entity and its assets.
Casilla 405 Total a pagar: settlement of the quarter's anticipo with bank or gateway stamp. Non-resident filers must preserve the payment receipt for the Form 301 credit schedule. Non-payment/underpayment of quarterly anticipos: 5% monthly surcharge on the unpaid amount (cap 50%) plus legal interest; failure to file: B/. 50 monthly fine (cap B/. 500); repeated non-compliance triggers immediate full annual settlement and consolidated debt enforcement against the entity and its assets.
