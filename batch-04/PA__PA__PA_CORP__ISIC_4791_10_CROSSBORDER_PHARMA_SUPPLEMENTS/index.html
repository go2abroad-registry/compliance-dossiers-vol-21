Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas — official e-services portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa and https://pita.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

For a foreign or local entity, the statutory step is to obtain a Panama RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente). A Panama Sociedad Anónima is first registered with the Public Registry; then the company must submit the RUC registration application on Form DGI-01 via the PITA portal or a licensed tax representative, attaching the notary certified public-registry inscription (Ficha), corporate documents, proof of registered agent and fiscal domicile, and appointee identification. Foreign entities without a Panama subsidiary are also eligible to apply: they must appoint a resident legal representative, file a notarized power of attorney, provide a certificate of good standing from the foreign jurisdiction, and declare the commencement of taxable activities in Panama. Upon approval, the DGI issues a permanent 10-digit RUC number which serves for income tax, ITBMS, and social security registry payments.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

None. A Panama S.A. is a distinct juridical person subject to entity-level taxation exclusively on Panamanian-source income. No check-the-box or pass-through election exists under domestic law; a Panama corporation cannot be disregarded for Panamanian tax purposes. The only administrative election available is consolidated income-tax filing for a corporate group of directly or indirectly owned Panama corporations with DGI authorization; hybrid-mismatch or entity-classification elections are not contemplated in Panamanian law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold. Any person habitually selling taxable goods or services in Panama is required to register for ITBMS before commencing operations. Non-resident digital service suppliers selling to Panamanian consumers are also required to register as ITBMS taxpayers from the first taxable supply, without a de minimis exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Panama are outside the scope of ITBMS or, where treated as a taxable export, are granted a 0% rate. The exporter must prove that legal title passed to a non-resident buyer and that the goods exited Panama through an authorised customs regime. Offshore services (including digital sale-platform operations, logistics and freight) are zero-rated where the service is rendered to a non-resident recipient or consumed entirely abroad. The exporter must present the customs export declaration, evidence of shipment, and proof that the net purchase price was received in the Panamanian financial system within the statutory repatriation deadline in order to claim the zero rate and the corresponding input ITBMS credit or refund.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduanas – DUA)
  • Bill of Lading or Flight Waybill evidencing physical departure from Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) or corresponding SWIFT / bank payment confirmation
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales contract issued by the Panama S.A.
  • Packing list and required nutritional/sanitary certificates for the nutraceutical shipment

Panama operates a phased mandatory electronic-invoicing regime known as Facturación Electrónica. All VAT-registered businesses must issue XML invoices through DGI-authorised software, transmit the XML document in near-real-time to the DGI validation platform (PITA / API service), and affix a DGI-generated digital stamp with a validation code and QR code. The invoice is legally cleared only after DGI certifies the code; approved e-invoice issuers may use REST/SOAP APIs or the DGI web portal. Bulk retail invoices require the same validation or an authorised simplified electronic receipt fallback.

The DGI cross-references every zero-rated sale with SIECAA customs exit records, the electronic invoice, the shipping manifest and banking remittance data. A zero-rating assertion is automatically rejected if any of the required export documents is missing or if the foreign currency consideration is not repatriated within 180 days; the full 7% ITBMS is then assessed as if the sale were domestic. Administrative penalties include late-payment surcharges, interest from the due date, and fines for incorrect invoice data; fraudulent export or refund claims are prosecuted as tax evasion with criminal penalties of imprisonment and fines, and all input VAT credits or refunds are retrospectively clawed back together with accrued interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Panamanian Balboa is a legal-tender currency unit at a fixed 1:1 parity with the U.S. dollar, which is the circulating medium of exchange. There are no exchange-control restrictions, no current-account or capital-account convertibility impediments, and no administrative hurdles on profit, dividend, royalty, or interest remittance to non-resident shareholders. Remittances may be freely completed in U.S. dollars; however, regulated banks must apply current AML/CFT surveillance, client due-diligence standards, and reporting thresholds established by the Financial Analysis Unit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no export-proceeds surrender regime. Exporters may retain 100 percent of export receipts indefinitely in U.S.-dollar accounts, with no mandatory conversion, repatriation timeline, or surrender ratio. All export payments remain subject to standard banking record-keeping and suspicious-transaction reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: No orthodox central bank is constituted in Panama. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá serves as the primary banking and financial regulator, while monetary policy is effectively set by the U.S. Federal Reserve under Panama's full-dollarization framework; the Banco Nacional de Panamá operates as a state-owned commercial bank, not a monetary authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Panama has introduced a licensed framework for financial-technology companies under the supervision of the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá. Offshore digital EMI service providers without a Panamanian banking or financial-technology license cannot lawfully take deposits, issue payment accounts, or hold customer funds in Panama. They may be used only as unregulated software or gateway facilitators, with all settlement activity conducted through a licensed local bank. For a high-risk online nutraceuticals merchant, reliance on an offshore EMI is strongly discouraged due to merchant-acquirer and AML de-risking exposure; a fully licensed Panamanian account with a commercial bank or a licensed local fintech is the viable structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital contributions or shareholder loans must be transmitted in U.S. dollars through SWIFT MT103 messages directly to the newly opened Panamanian corporate operating account. There is no statutory Panamanian FX purpose-code classification; banks follow accepted SWIFT narrative conventions and may request an explicit purpose text such as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION FOR PA_CORP S.A.' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN PROCEEDS'. The clearing infrastructure consists of the U.S. dollar interbank clearing system in Panama, local ACH same-day clearings, and international SWIFT connections. Within three months after fiscal year-end. For a calendar-year taxpayer (January 1 - December 31), the annual return must be filed no later than March 31 of the following year, unless the DGI officially extends the deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File must be prepared where related-party transactions exceed B/.1,000,000 (USD-equivalent) in a fiscal year; a Master File becomes mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds B/.100,000,000. An informative transfer pricing return is required when related-party transactions exceed B/.250,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama Economic Substance Regulations under Law 254 of 2020 apply to resident juridical entities engaged in relevant activities, including distribution and service centers, financing, leasing, shipping, headquarters, and high-risk IP/intangibles. An e-commerce supplement seller operating through a Panama S.A. must maintain a physical office in Panama, have adequate full-time employees, incur proportionate expenditure, and make key decisions in Panama; otherwise the entity risks classification as a shell and deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Omission of Income

Statutory sanction: Fine equal to approximately 100% of the omitted tax, plus 3% monthly interest and default surcharges; criminal conviction for tax evasion carries imprisonment and disqualification from serving as a corporate officer.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize diversion of sales or false returns may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and fines; authorities may pierce the corporate veil, attach personal assets, impose travel bans, and seize passports.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of B/.1,000 to B/.10,000 per fiscal year, forced deregistration from the Mercantile Registry, disqualification of the resident agent, and loss of good standing for banking, licensing, and tax purposes.

Director personal liability: Directors and resident agents who knowingly operate a shell face personal fines, loss of professional license, and criminal money-laundering exposure where the structure is used to conceal assets or evade tax.
AML / Unlicensed Nutraceuticals

Statutory sanction: Seizure and destruction of non-compliant supplements, license revocation, administrative fines up to B/.1,000,000 under AML Law 23 of 2015, and criminal money laundering penalties of 2 to 10 years imprisonment plus forfeiture of accounts and inventory.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be prosecuted individually for knowingly processing proceeds of unlicensed health products; personal bank accounts may be frozen, and travel restrictions or extradition requests may apply.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DGI transfer-pricing recharacterization and upward adjustment to taxable income; monetary penalty of 2% to 10% of the improperly valued transaction plus monthly interest; public registration of defaulting taxpayers; criminal liability for submission of false transfer pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false TP declarations are personally liable for the tax adjustment to the extent the contrived value constitutes a disguised dividend; travel bans and up to 5 years disqualification from managing Panamanian companies may be imposed.
