Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office is permitted for general corporate registry purposes (registered agent service, mailing address, and virtual office arrangements) under Panama law; a registered agent must have a physical Panamanian address. However, for a cross-border payment gateway company operating under ISIC 6619, the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) and the UAF require a substantive local operational presence or a qualifying foreign jurisdiction model. Pure virtual office (shared workspace without dedicated staff and director meetings) will not satisfy the SBP's licensing expectations if the entity becomes a licensed PSP; for a non-licensed B2B payment gateway entity that merely holds contracts and uses foreign processing partners, a virtual office can satisfy merely the corporate registered agent requirement, but the company should maintain a registered agent address distinct from a shell-mail concept, with a physical presence capable of receiving inspections. Acquiring a banking license or PSP authorization under Title III of Legislative Decree 2 of 2008 (SBP) is generally not feasible with only a virtual office; physical office and local compliance officer (Oficial de Cumplimiento) presence are mandatory for a licensed PSP.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; the Articles customarily state a nominal issued capital of US$10,000 divided into 10,000 registered shares of US$1.00 par value. Paid-in capital may be zero or contributed in kind, with no escrow, deposit, or capital duty requirement at incorporation. An annual franchise tax applies: US$300 for societies with net assets exceeding US$150,000 and US$150 for smaller societies (Law 181 of 2021).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; electronic services portal: Sede Electrónica DGI (https://www.dgi.gob.pa), with taxpayer self-service portal for RUC administration and returns.

E-government filing portal

A foreign payment-service provider must form or register a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) through a licensed resident agent, register the company with the Public Registry, and then apply for the unified tax identifier number (RUC - Registro Único de Contribuyentes) at the DGI. The application requires the notarized and registered public deed, corporate bylaws, appointment of the resident agent, certified list of directors and shareholders, local fiscal address, and any banking/AML references required for the high-risk PSP licence. The RUC integrates corporate income tax, ITBMS, withholding tax, and municipal tax obligations. Non-resident ownership is permitted and there is no minimum paid-in capital.

The application requires the notarized and registered public deed, corporate bylaws, appointment of the resident agent, certified list of directors and shareholders, local fiscal address, and any banking/AML references required for the high-risk PSP licence. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box election and does not provide hybrid mismatch entity classification elections. A Panamanian S.A. is taxed on Panama-source income only; foreign-source income is outside the corporate income tax base. Available administrative elections include voluntary or mandatory registration under the small-taxpayer ITBMS regime for persons with annual gross income at or below B/.36,000 and election of the DGI-authorized e-invoicing model (FEL - Facturación Electrónica en Línea). Certain qualifying companies in Panama's special regimes (e.g., SEM, City of Knowledge) may elect special tax treatment. No domestic hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 (36,000 Panamanian balboas / US dollars) in annual taxable turnover. Taxpayers with annual gross income above this threshold must register for ITBMS; taxpayers below the threshold may register voluntarily or, if otherwise carrying on a taxable activity, may qualify as small taxpayers (pequeño contribuyente).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under ITBMS is available for exports of goods (actual shipment out of Panama) and for exports of services, including financial/payment processing services, where the service is rendered to a non-resident recipient, is effectively used or exploited outside Panama, and is not attributable to a Panama permanent establishment. For a cross-border payment gateway, qualifying services include merchant acquiring, clearing and settlement, transaction switching, and related processing services supplied to foreign financial institutions or foreign merchants, provided the recipient is established abroad and the consideration is paid from non-Panama sources. Written contracts, invoices, bank settlement records, and if required, a DGI export-of-services declaration, must evidence the non-Panama use.

  • Customs export declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera - DUA) for physical goods
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, or other transport document proving physical export
  • Commercial invoice and export contract evidencing the cross-border transaction
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit statement showing payment received from a foreign account
  • DGI export-of-services documentary file where services are zero-rated, including service contracts, performance evidence, and confirmation of foreign use/benefit

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica en Línea - FEL) applies to ITBMS-registered taxpayers, phased in under Law 49 of 2018, based on taxpayer size and activity. Invoices and credit/debit notes must be issued as structured XML documents through a DGI-authorized technology provider or the DGI's own platform, transmitted electronically to the DGI for immediate validation, and must carry a unique invoice number, QR code, and DGI security code. The payment gateway, as a high-volume automated B2B service provider, must integrate its systems with the DGI through API/Web services for near-real-time submission and receipt of the DGI acceptance response.

Panama's DGI applies strict audit scrutiny to zero-rated export transactions. Absence of the required export documentation or false claims trigger retroactive re-characterization of the zero-rated sale as subject to ITBMS at 7%, plus monthly default surcharges (up to 1.5% per month), correction interest, and administrative fines ranging from B/.500 to B/.50,000 or a percentage of the omitted tax (up to 100%-300%) depending on the offence. Fraudulent issuance of export invoices, forged customs declarations, or use of offshore routing to disguise Panamanian consumption, may additionally constitute tax fraud under the Código Fiscal and Law 49 of 2018, with criminal penalties including monetary fines and incarceration of responsible directors, corporate officers, or resident agents. The DGI's general statute of limitation for tax assessments is up to five years, extended where the taxpayer fails to register or files false returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current- and capital-account convertibility. The SBP neither clears nor licences exchange transactions; capital injections, share redemptions, dividend distributions, intercompany loans, royalty payments and profit remittances may all be effected freely in USD without surrender obligation or official approval. Income-tax frictions are minimal under the territorial system: foreign shareholders of a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima generally face no Panamanian withholding on dividends paid from Panama-source profits. However, the Ley 254 de 2022 tax reform widened the income base for entities conducting cross-border payment activities; dividend distributions made from profits attributable to newly taxable non-Panama income may attract a 5% dividend withholding tax, and the treasury should therefore maintain segregated maps of Panama-source versus foreign-source profits to preserve clear remittance economics.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio in Panama because there is no central bank monetising foreign exchange. Exporters and service providers, including a payment gateway exporting payment services, may retain 100% of receivables in USD or any convertible currency onshore or offshore, subject only to normal commercial bank AML/CFT large-value declaration filings and, where applicable, tax information filings with the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI). Trade-statistics obligations are limited to customs declarations filed through the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas and do not impose any FX repatriation or conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central issuing monetary authority by constitutional design. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) performs banking supervision and acts as the de facto monetary regulator of the financial system; the Banco Nacional de Panamá operates as the Government's fiscal agent, clearing and settlement bank, and final-credit guarantor for public-sector balances. The national currency, the Balboa (PAB), is fixed at par 1:1 with the US dollar (USD) under Law 84 of 1904; no Panamanian paper or coinage circulates and USD is legal tender throughout the Republic. Consequently, foreign-exchange policy is a passive function of USD liquidity and the domestic banking system rather than an actively managed control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium, legally constrained. Decreto-Ley 9 de 1998 reserves deposit-taking and funds intermediation to SBP-licensed entities; an offshore electronic-money institution (EMI) has no licensing pathway in Panama and cannot lawfully constitute the gateway's regulated clearing bank. An EMI may only be used as an ancillary acquirer or payout facilitator abroad; client balances and gateway settlement must remain booked at an SBP-licensed institution to preserve fund segregation and bankruptcy remoteness.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing: (i) subscribe capital by wire to the Sociedad Anónima's escrow account at Banco Nacional de Panamá or a designated Tier-1 bank using SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) with a full 70-character narrative citing the investment purpose, the corporate tax ID (RUC) and the SBP PSP licence application number; (ii) supply the source-of-funds file and beneficial-ownership registry excerpt so the receiving bank may complete its UAF filing without blocking; (iii) instruct funding in USD directly so that no FX conversion leg exists; (iv) release the escrow balance to the operating account only after licence approval and bank acceptance; (v) register the investment with the DGI for territorial-tax classification.Settlement rails available: SWIFT MT103 for standard merchant payouts; MT202 COV for interbank cover payments arising from the gateway's host bank; commercial letters of credit (UCP600 / ISBP745) issued via SWIFT MT700 for trade-instrument relationships with acquiring sponsors; direct Fedwire settlement through a US correspondent bank (e.g., Banco Nacional de Panamá's New York clearing account) for high-value USD finality; domestic low-value clearing through Panama's electronic payment system (SIPE) operated by Banco Nacional de Panamá; and the domestic high-value real-time gross-settlement (RTGS) infrastructure for interbank liquidity transfers. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Medium, legally constrained. Decreto-Ley 9 de 1998 reserves deposit-taking and funds intermediation to SBP-licensed entities; an offshore electronic-money institution (EMI) has no licensing pathway in Panama and cannot lawfully constitute the gateway's regulated clearing bank. An EMI may only be used as an ancillary acquirer or payout facilitator abroad; client balances and gateway settlement must remain booked at an SBP-licensed institution to preserve fund segregation and bankruptcy remoteness.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.3,000,000 (Panamanian balboa, at parity with US dollar) aggregate annual related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation; taxpayers with annual gross revenue exceeding B/.10,000,000 are also required to maintain contemporaneous documentation and may be called to present it within 45 days of DGI request.

Economic substance scope: Panama maintains Economic Substance Regulations under Law 254 of 2021 and related Cabinet Decrees. A Sociedad Anónima operating as a cross-border payment gateway performing operational direction, clearing, settlement and contracting activity in Panama must demonstrate local physical presence, resident management, adequate employees and expenditure proportional to the revenue booked in Panama. Entities with no real substance and only formal registration are treated as shell companies, denied tax residency benefits and exposed to regulatory cancellation. The annual economic substance declaration must be filed with the DGI; failure to comply renders the corporate shell presumption applicable.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Payment of the evaded tax plus monthly surcharge and default interest; administrative fine equal to 100% of the evaded tax; criminal prosecution under the Código Penal carrying imprisonment of two to six years; cancellation of business licenses and RUC suspension.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or direct the revenue misclassification are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and may face criminal imprisonment, judicial travel bans, asset freezes and disqualification from acting as officers of any Panama company.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the Economic Substance Regulations of up to B/.1,000,000; denial of tax residency status; retroactive disallowance of territorial benefits; judicial liquidation of the company and cancellation of the corporate registration.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced when the S.A. is used for tax evasion; directors and nominees become personally liable for the tax and penalties, are subject to civil restitution of distributed assets and may be forbidden from leaving Panama pending criminal investigation.
Unlicensed Cross-Border Payment Activity and Money Laundering (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Cease-and-desist and regulatory closure; financial penalty up to B/.5,000,000 or 5% of annual transaction volume; criminal asset forfeiture; imprisonment of up to fifteen years under Panama's money laundering statutes; loss of visa and immigration benefits for foreign principals.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face personal criminal liability for consenting to or benefiting from unregulated payment flows, preventive detention, travel bans, seizure of personal property, freezing of accounts and long-term disqualification from regulated financial activity.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Pricing Default

Statutory sanction: DGI may make a transfer pricing adjustment and tax the recharacterized Panama-source income at 25%; administrative penalty of up to 10% of the undocumented related-party transaction amount; assessment of the unpaid tax, surcharges and interest; criminal liability for willful omission in the annual return.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign intercompany arrangements lacking arm's-length substance are personally liable for the resultant tax and penalties; courts may issue civil restitution orders and travel bans against directors who repatriate funds out of Panama without proper economic consideration or documentation.
