Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Panama imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Sociedad Anónima. The articles may declare a nominal authorized capital (customarily US$10,000 divided into 100 shares) but no escrow account, deposit, or full payment is required at incorporation. Shares may be issued for consideration deemed sufficient by the board, subject to general creditor-protection rules.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas, Panama; e-portal: Sede Electrónica T@S (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

For the Panamanian Sociedad Anónima, a licensed resident agent files DGI Form RUC via Sede Electrónica, attaching certified copy of Public Registry entry, articles of incorporation, ID of directors/legal representative, and proof of fiscal address. The DGI then assigns the RUC/NIT and issues an ITBMS account. For a foreign entity without Panamanian establishment, registration is required only upon deriving Panama-source income or completing taxable transactions; a legal representative must be appointed and a local address maintained.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box or similar election exists; all S.A.s are subject to entity-level corporate income tax. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis threshold: all persons performing taxable supplies in Panama must register as ITBMS taxpayers from the first supply; the small-taxpayer simplified regime applies to qualifying operations with annual gross receipts up to B/.36,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Sales of tangible goods qualify for ITBMS 0% when ownership is transferred to a non-Panamanian resident and goods are exported to a third country from Panama, evidenced by customs exit. Offshore services for non-resident use outside Panama also qualify. The distributor of passive electronic components may zero-rate FOB/CIF/FCA export transactions to foreign distributors/end-users if the Panama-origin shipment and full payment prove real export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA) - Declaración Única de Aduana de Exportación
  • Commercial invoice (original in Spanish or bilingual) describing HS code 8532/8533 goods
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing Panama port/airport of export
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit confirmation (SWIFT/transfer receipt) for the export proceeds
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (if requested)

Panama mandates electronic issuance through DGI-approved billing systems (Facturación Electrónica). Invoices are transmitted in XML/JSON via API to the Sede Electrónica, validated in real time, and assigned a fiscal authorization number. Digital stamping and periodic clearance reporting (RIT, IRE) apply to active ITBMS taxpayers. From 2024, large and medium taxpayers cannot use non-electronic invoices.

Unsupported or sham zero-rating is void ab initio; the DGI claws back 7% ITBMS, plus statutory interest and surcharges of 50% to 100% of the tax for misdeclaration. Recurring fraud triggers criminal liability, suspension of RUC, and personal liability for directors under Panama's Tax Code and Customs Law. Exporter must maintain complete files for 5 to 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions; Panama operates a fully dollarized economy (no national fiat currency), so there is no legal distinction between resident and non-resident convertibility. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject only to standard tax withholding (e.g., 10% dividend tax under territorial taxation rules) and compliance with anti-money laundering documentation for cross-border wires.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There are no statutory timeframes, no mandatory surrender requirements, and no minimum conversion ratios for export receipts because US dollars are the domestic legal tender. Exporters may retain and disburse proceeds freely in USD or any other currency without central bank reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Banco Nacional de Panamá (state-owned monetary agent) in coordination with the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Panama's banking supervision authority); no autonomous central bank, US dollar is legal tender under Law 84 of 1904 and Decree Law 9 of 1998

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Panama's licensing framework under the Superintendency of Banks permits digital banks and fintech payment entities, but offshore digital EMIs must obtain a local authorization to offer accounts to Panamanian corporates. Offshore EMIs cannot clear through the local ACH/SWIFT infrastructure without a Panamanian banking partner. Legal constraints include the lack of a dedicated digital asset licensing regime and the requirement to maintain a physical compliance office in Panama for regulated banking activities.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (Impuesto Sobre la Renta, ISR, charged on net taxable income from Panamanian-source activities; wholesale distribution of passive electronic components is within the territorial net unless the inventory is wholly held and traded abroad)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% on dividends paid out of profits that were already subject to the 25% corporate tax; 10% on dividends paid from untaxed or exempt profits (e.g., extraterritorial income without substance). An additional 5% 'complementary dividend tax' arises at the corporate level on profits not distributed within 12 months of fiscal year-end.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months after fiscal-year end; calendar-year corporations must file no later than 31 March of the following year. Fiscal-year companies must file by the last day of the third month after the fiscal-year close.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 2,000,000 (equivalent to PAB 2,000,000) of aggregate related-party transactions in a fiscal year triggers mandatory preparation and filing of the Transfer Pricing Report (Local File) and the related-party disclosure annex (Form 552/580-TP). Transactions below the threshold require only an intercompany policy statement and arm's-length justification in the tax file.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Requirements (ESR; territorial-reform legislation, including Ley 254 de 2021 and the DGI substance regulations) apply to a PA_CORP S.A. deriving income from 'relevant activities' such as distribution, service-centre operations, and logistics. A passive electronic-components distributor must maintain in Panama: (i) a physical office or storage facility, (ii) adequate full-time employees making distribution and inventory decisions, (iii) local bank accounts and books, and (iv) an annual substance report filed with the DGI. Failure to meet ESR exposes the company to characterization as a shell and to the re-attribution of income to the non-resident principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Invoicing Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax-fraud penalties under Panama's Penal Tax Regime: imprisonment of 3 to 6 years and a fine of up to 10 times the evaded tax; DGI also imposes the 2% monthly surcharge, terminates fiscal solvency, and publishes the taxpayer in the public delinquency register.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve fraudulent returns are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and face civil restitution orders; the DGI may obtain travel bans on directors and legal representatives until the debt is secured.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to USD 10,000 under the ESR/dissolution procedures; the DGI may disregard the entity and confiscate its tax benefits, and the Public Registry may dissolve the company for failing to maintain a resident agent or file accounting records. The company loses its territorial tax defence, and all income is re-characterized as Panamanian-source.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under the alter-ego doctrine, making the directors personally liable for unpaid corporate tax and third-party debts; directors may also be prohibited from incorporating new Panamanian companies for up to 5 years.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: AML Law 23 of 2015 and the Beneficial Owner Law: administrative fines up to USD 1,000,000 per violation, criminal sanctions for predicate money-laundering offences (imprisonment up to 12 years), and revocation of the resident agent's licence. The DGI may also impose a 30% withholding assessment on unrevealed shareholders.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil restitution of laundered amounts, confiscation of personal assets, travel bans, and imprisonment if they knowingly executed the transactions; compliance officers and registered agents are subject to the same enforcement.
