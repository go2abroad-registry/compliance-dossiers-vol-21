Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); official portal: Sistema Integrado de Información Tributaria (SITIO) - https://sitio.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) obtains the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) through SITIO: (1) complete incorporation at the Registro Público de Panamá and obtain the company Ficha; (2) appoint a resident agent and a local fiscal representative with power of attorney; (3) file the RUC application in SITIO declaring CIIU activity code 5621 (food service activities for transport) and estimated gross receipts; (4) attach the certificate of incorporation, corporate document amendments, and proof of Panamanian operational address; (5) receive an immediate provisional RUC and final RUC after DGI documentary validation, normally within 5-10 business days; (6) open the mandatory ITBMS tax account in the same registration process and apply for exporter status; (7) activate SITIO e-filing credentials and obtain electronic-invoicing (FEL) authorization. Non-resident ownership is expressly permitted and no paid-in minimum capital is required, although an active local operational address must be evidenced for the catering licence and DGI records.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Panamanian law; the S.A. is mandatorily taxed at entity level at 25% corporate ISR on Panamanian-source income only. Available statutory elections: (1) consolidated group income-tax return for 100%-related Panamanian companies with DGI pre-approval under Código Fiscal Article 699-A; (2) micro-business simplified regime for annual gross receipts up to B/.36,000, generally unsuitable for a commercial airline catering kitchen; (3) Multinational Headquarters (SEM) and International Financial Center (IFC) licensing frameworks for qualifying exclusively offshore-source income; (4) contractual tax-stability and incentive regimes where expressly granted by law for qualifying investments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover exemption for ITBMS: any person commercially selling goods or rendering services in Panama must register at commencement of activities. A simplified small-taxpayer option exists for annual gross receipts up to B/.36,000; a full-scale catering kitchen exceeds that threshold and must maintain standard ITBMS registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the ITBMS territorial framework, the 0% export rate applies to (i) goods physically leaving Panamanian customs territory and (ii) services performed in Panama that are supplied to non-resident persons and exclusively utilised outside Panama. An airport in-flight catering kitchen may zero-rate ramp deliveries of meals and provisions to foreign-flag airlines operating international routes when title passes to the airline at the aircraft-side delivery point and the goods are consumed outside Panamanian territory. Domestic-leg supplies on Panama-to-Panama flights and sales to airport retail concessions are local supplies taxable at the standard 7% ITBMS.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) filed electronically through SICE (Sistema Integrado de Comercio Electrónico) evidencing physical exit from Panamanian customs territory
  • Bill of Lading (Conocimiento de Embarque) or, for bonded aircraft-ramp deliveries, the signed Catering Delivery Receipt/Flight Manifest countersigned by the airline's authorized ground representative and cross-referenced to the international flight itinerary
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT/MT103 or ACH credit confirmation) showing settlement of the export invoice in the Panamanian S.A.'s local USD account
  • Commercial invoice annotated 'ITBMS 0% - Exportación de Alimentos a Bordo' with the corresponding airline purchase order
  • Aircraft and flight documentation evidencing the international route for each supplied service leg

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica Panamá - FEP) governed by DGI Resolution No. 201-1579 of 2019 and implementation Resolution No. 201-2422 of 2021, phased in by annual gross-income brackets from 2021 through the final universal wave in 2023-2024. Taxpayers must issue invoices through an authorized technology provider or the DGI FEP web client and transmit each invoice in real time to SITIO; the DGI validates the transaction and returns a unique electronic authorization (FEL) number that must appear with a QR code on the invoice. Non-compliant invoices deny purchasers the input-credit entitlement and trigger seller penalties.

The DGI may reclassify zero-rated export supplies as local taxable supplies and assess 7% ITBMS with statutory moratory interest from the original due date, and may simultaneously claw back all input ITBMS credits claimed in respect of such supplies. Under the Código Fiscal as reformed by Law 70 of 2019, fraudulent export claims are penalized at 100%-300% of the omitted tax plus monetary fines, and serious cases may be prosecuted as tax crimes carrying imprisonment. False customs export declarations also attract separate aduanero penalties and retrospective reassessments. The State retains an extended limitation period to reassess and collect in cases of fraudulent export documentation. Because Panama uses the US dollar as legal tender, no statutory FX surrender rule applies; the 180-day repatriation-evidence benchmark is enforced in DGI practice as the contemporaneous substantiation standard for preserving export zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

No foreign exchange control regime exists. Panama operates a fully dollarized economy (balboa at 1:1 USD). Both current and capital account transactions are freely convertible; profit remittance, dividends, and loan payments face no FX approval hurdles. Only Panamanian-source income is taxable under territorial rules; dividend distributions paid by a Panama S.A. from foreign-source income are not subject to Panamanian tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists for export/airline catering receipts. All USD-denominated export proceeds may be retained in any bank account, free of surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) (Panama has no issuing central bank; the US dollar is legal tender and the National Bank of Panama acts as fiscal agent for government financial services).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Moderate feasibility: Panama permits foreign fintech/EMI accounts only for payment processing, not as a primary treasury settlement rail. Legal constraint: non-bank payment service providers must be licensed by SBP for local money movement; offshore digital EMIs (e.g. Wise/Revolut) generally cannot provide a Panama S.A. with a domestic Panamanian account and are not treated as regulated deposit-taking institutions for escrow requirements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on net taxable income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends distributed by a Panamanian S.A. to both resident and non-resident shareholders out of Panamanian-source taxable profits.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filed no later than 31 March of the calendar year immediately following the fiscal year-end; for an approved non-calendar fiscal year, within three months after the actual fiscal year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Line 1a / Gross Receipts Total gross revenue from airport bonded catering, ramp vehicle deliveries, airline meal uplift, and ancillary kitchen services performed in Panama. A non-resident-owned Panamanian S.A. must report only Panama-source catering revenue. Invoices billed to a foreign airline for services physically rendered and delivered inside Panama must be included; genuine foreign-source revenue, if any, is excluded with documentary evidence. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of approximately 0.5% of the final tax due, capped at 50%, plus a late-filing fine ranging from USD 50 to USD 10,000 depending on gross receipts. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 1,000,000 in annual related-party transactions triggers the mandatory Local File and transfer pricing affidavit; group-wide revenue above USD 10,000,000 triggers the Master File. Transactions with entities resident in tax-haven or no-tax jurisdictions can trigger documentation at a lower administrative threshold.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations under Law 70 of 2019 apply to relevant companies and relevant activities. In-flight catering performed through a physical airport kitchen is a substantive local business: the S.A. avoids any shell-company issue by maintaining premises and kitchen equipment, employing kitchen and dispatch staff, making contracts and operational decisions in Panama, and incurring commensurate local payroll and expenditures. If instead the entity is used solely as a contractual pass-through for offshore group cuisine suppliers, the ESR failure and artificial construction doctrine will apply.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Income Tax Declaration

Statutory sanction: Civil surcharge of at least 25% to 50% of the evaded tax, interest on arrears, and criminal penalties under Panamanian tax and criminal law including imprisonment from 2 to 5 years for fraudulent tax declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct false tax returns are personally and jointly liable for restitution, tax penalties, and criminal fines. Panama courts may pierce the corporate veil, impose travel bans, and order civil damages to the National Treasury.
Shell Company / Artificial Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations penalties of up to USD 1,000,000, cancellation of the RUC tax identification and commercial registry, DGI deemed-profit assessments based on gross receipts, and referral for criminal fraud investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent may be held personally liable for false substance declarations, disbarred from practicing in Panama, subjected to exit bans, and exposed to extradition requests in egregious structuring cases.
AML / Money Laundering Through Airport Bonded Logistics

Statutory sanction: Money laundering penalties under Panamanian law carry imprisonment of 5 to 12 years, fines up to substantial statutory maxima, asset forfeiture, administrative sanctions by the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF), and criminal shutdown of the catering operation.

Director personal liability: Directors, executives, and beneficial owners are individually prosecutable. Personal travel bans, passport revocation, restrictions on banking activity, and criminal confiscation apply. Nominee directors who sign AML affidavits are not shielded by the corporate form.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: DGI transfer pricing adjustment to taxable income, a penalty of up to 25% of the adjustment, late-filing fines of up to USD 50,000, and potential loss of deductibility of the intercompany expense.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify incomplete or fraudulent TP documentation may be held jointly liable for additional tax and penalties. In flagrant cases, travel restrictions and criminal sanctions for financial fraud may apply.
