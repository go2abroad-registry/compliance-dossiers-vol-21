Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); taxpayer portal: E-Tax 2.0 and SIFA e-invoicing platform; customs authority: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA), electronic customs portal SISEA/VUCEA.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Sociedad Anónima secures its statutory TIN/VAT registration through the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) maintained by DGI. STEP 1: incorporate via a licensed Panamanian attorney, file the estatutos with the Registro Público de Panamá, and protocolize the deed before a Notario Público; STEP 2: appoint the mandatory local resident agent and registered office; STEP 3: file the RUC registration with DGI, obtain the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), and enroll for the SIFA/E-Tax electronic signature (firma electrónica); STEP 4: register for ITBMS under the economic activity ISIC 4669 and, where the trading entity performs customs operations, register with ANA as exporter; STEP 5: for a fully offshore trading operation, maintain annual corporate records, minutes, and the informativa return to preserve territorial-source classification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box entity election exists under Panamanian law; the S.A. is an entity-level taxpayer on Panamanian-source income only. Elective regimes available: SEM multinational headquarters incentives (Law 41/2007) with optional legal-stability agreement (convenio de estabilidad jurídica) executed with MEF; Panama Pacifico special economic zone tax incentives (Law 41/2004); Colon Free Zone operating license; and look-through reporting for local partnerships and trusts (fideicomisos) whose income is attributed to partners or beneficiaries. Effective 1 January 2025, Law 254/2023 introduces CFC and hybrid-mismatch anti-avoidance rules that may tax certain offshore passive income realized by Panamanian residents.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory monetary threshold exists: ITBMS registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply of goods or services effected in Panamanian territory or benefiting a Panamanian recipient. Export-only traders register as exporters under the RUC to access the 0% rate and input-VAT credit mechanics despite having no domestic taxable turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% export rate applies under Article 1057 of the Código Fiscal (introduced by Law 6 of 2005) where tangible goods, including granular elemental sulfur, are physically exported from Panamanian territory and title passes to a foreign buyer, or where services are performed entirely outside Panama or are rendered to non-residents and paid from abroad. The exporter must be the registered supplier of record, must issue its commercial invoice at 0% ITBMS, and must evidence the foreign destination of the cargo through customs, transport, banking, and insurance documentation. For offshore brokerage transactions where goods never enter Panamanian territory, the 0% treatment is sustained by demonstrating that the supply, logistics, and payment cycle are foreign-source; otherwise the DGI may recharacterize the income or supply as Panamanian-source.

  • DUI (Documento Único de Aduana) export declaration filed through ANA SISEA
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or equivalent marine cargo document naming the foreign port of discharge
  • Commercial invoice issued at 0% ITBMS with export terms (CIF/FOB) and ISIC 4669 commodity description
  • Inward remittance certificate (bank MT103 or credit advice) evidencing receipt of proceeds from the foreign buyer

Panama mandates electronic invoicing through the DGI SIFA (Sistema de Facturación Automatizada) integrated with the E-Tax 2.0 portal. Invoices are transmitted as XML via SIFA web services/API with prompt DGI validation and assignment of an authorization code; physical counterparts bear a digital stamp or tax authorization number (constancia de autorización). During connectivity failure taxpayers may issue under the contingency regime and upload the batch within the legally permitted period. Export invoices must be clearly marked as exports with 0% ITBMS, must be reported in the periodic ITBMS return and in the ANA export declaration, and must reconcile to the customs DUI and shipping documents.

Panama's territorial tax rules do not impose a general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation window; the 180-day figure is adopted in this dossier as the standard commercial evidentiary cycle within which the inward remittance certificate should be produced to substantiate the 0% ITBMS and foreign-source income position. Where DGI or ANA detects false export filings, sham transit, artificial intermediary structures, or domestic sales disguised as exports, the DGI may reassess 7% ITBMS together with monthly surcharges (recargo) up to 100% of the tax, statutory interest at rates up to 12% per annum, and administrative fines for fiscal and customs infringements. Criminal liability for fraudulent export zero-rating and aggravated tax evasion arises under Law 70 of 2019 with imprisonment of 3 to 6 years, and the DGI's retrospective reassessment period extends from the ordinary 4-year term to 6 years where returns were not filed or fraud is established, with no limitation in cases of serious documented fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized, open economy with complete currency convertibility. There is no separate legal tender or domestic fiat currency; therefore, capital and current account convertibility are unrestricted. Profits, dividends, principal and interest payments, and trade settlement amounts may be freely repatriated in USD without prior approval, but all cross-border transfers remain subject to bank-level AML/CFT and OFAC compliance screening. No invisibles payment restrictions or profit remittance hurdles apply to a sulfur mineral ore trading company incorporated in Panama.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no export proceeds surrender or conversion requirement in Panama. Exporters are not required to repatriate, convert, or surrender foreign exchange to any central authority. No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists because Panamanian law treats USD receipts as domestic currency. The only practical requirement is that banks may request underlying trade documentation, such as invoices, bills of lading, and contracts, to satisfy their own customer due diligence and anti-money laundering obligations.

Central bank supervision: No central bank exists in Panama. The banking and financial supervision authority is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). Foreign exchange administration is ineffective because the U.S. dollar is the official legal tender under Decree Law No. 111 of 1904, and the Banco Nacional de Panamá operates as a state-owned commercial bank handling public funds, not as a monetary authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Panama does not recognize foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) as licensed deposit-taking entities. A foreign fintech or offshore EMI cannot independently maintain corporate funds or provide clearing services in Panama unless it operates through a regulated Panamanian bank under the supervision of the SBP. International EMIs may provide payment initiation, FX conversion, or API-based clearing rails but must settle through a local Tier 1 correspondent bank account. The preferred compliant approach is to use a direct account with a Panamanian bank and integrate the EMI as a non-custodial payment interface, subject to the bank’s approval of the EMI’s KYC and sanctions infrastructure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use a standard SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the Panama entity’s USD-denominated account at its Tier 1 local bank. The wire must identify the recipient as the exact legal name of the Sociedad Anónima and its account number. The payment reference should state 'Capital Contribution under Shareholder Agreement' or 'Shareholder Loan Agreement No. [reference]' to provide clear audit trail. No purpose code is required due to the absence of FX controls, but the remitter should be ready to provide, through its own bank, a copy of the Panama entity’s certificate of incorporation, board resolution authorizing the account, and source of funds documentation. The receiving Panamanian bank performs OFAC and customer screening as part of its SBP-mandated EDD; the remitter should ensure its end-point is not on any sanctions lists. To minimize clearing friction, use an approved correspondent bank routing via the receiving bank’s head office in Panama City.For direct supplier payments, use SWIFT MT103 via the Panamanian bank’s correspondent network, referencing the invoice number and contracting entity. For structured sulfur ore shipments, use commercial letters of credit governed by ICC UCP 600; issue via SWIFT MT700, with amendments sent via MT707. Standby LC instruments can be issued through MT760 for performance or advance payment guarantees. All these rails are compliant with Panama’s banking law and are subject to internal AML review based on the transaction narrative and underlying documents.
