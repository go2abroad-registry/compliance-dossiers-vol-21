Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists under Panamanian law. Customary drafting sets authorized capital at US$10,000, divided into 100 registered common shares of US$100 par value each. Payment may be deferred in whole or in part under Art. 34 of Ley 32 de 1927. No escrow account, bank deposit, or evidence of capital payment is required at formation.

Funding & unlock path: Benemérito Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá - mandato de inspección técnica contra incendios (fire safety inspection) and issuance of the Certificado de Prevención de Incendios for the registered office. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); official e-filing and registration portal: SISTEMA T@X (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Sociedad Anónima, the resident agent (agente residente) must apply for the Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC) through the DGI's SISTEMA T@X portal. Required filings comprise the notarized and apostilled articles of incorporation and Registro Público corporate excerpt, designation of the Panamanian fiscal domicile and resident agent, certified copy of legal representative identification documents, and a notarized power of attorney; a provisional RUC is issued immediately upon filing, with the definitive RUC following DGI verification. The same RUC serves as the ITBMS registration identifier and is mandatory for export operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; the S.A. is automatically a separate entity-level taxpayer taxed only on Panama-source income. Available elections include: (i) the simplified small-taxpayer ITBMS regime for annual gross receipts up to B/.300,000 (US$300,000); (ii) consolidated group filing for related entities under Article 691 of the Código Fiscal; (iii) the Multinational Company Headquarters (SEM) regime for qualifying regional coordination centers; and (iv) hybrid-mismatch alignment rules introduced by Law 254 of 2021 transposing OECD BEPS recommendations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General ITBMS regime: mandatory registration for any person carrying out taxable transactions in Panama, with no minimum turnover threshold; simplified small-taxpayer regime available for annual gross receipts not exceeding B/.300,000 (US$300,000); exporters and brokers under ISIC 4631 must register in the general regime to claim input credits and zero-rating.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods (including bulk soybeans and yellow corn) and exports of services are subject to ITBMS at 0%. Eligibility requires the exporter to hold a valid RUC registered for export activity, to evidence that the goods physically left Panamanian customs territory within the statutory timeframe, and to evidence that the buyer/consignee is a non-resident. For brokerage services (ISIC 4631) rendered to offshore counterparties, the service must be performed, utilized, and economically consumed outside Panama; export invoices must be issued under the DGI-approved export invoice series. Input ITBMS paid on export-related purchases may be credited against output tax and, where a credit balance arises, recovered through the fiscal credit refund procedure.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) filed electronically through VUCE/SIGA
  • Ocean Bill of Lading evidencing the physical shipment of bulk grain to the foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT bank credit advice evidencing collection of the export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice issued under the DGI-approved export invoice series with CSF and zero-rated designation
  • Charter party, bill of lading, and warehouse receipts substantiating the underlying commodity brokerage transaction

Panama's electronic invoicing mandate (Facturación Electrónica) requires all registered taxpayers to issue invoices through DGI-certified billing software or the T@X web portal; each invoice must be transmitted in real time to the DGI before issuance, validated with a unique Código de Seguridad Fiscal (CSF), stamped with a QR code, and archived electronically. Export transactions must additionally be declared through the VUCE/SIGA customs single window using the export invoice series; failure to pre-clear invoices triggers automatic rejection and daily fines under Código Fiscal sanctions.

The DGI cross-references VUCE/SIGA customs declarations, shipping manifests, and bank AML records to verify zero-rated export claims. Unjustified or fraudulent zero-rating is clawed back as ITBMS due at 7% plus surcharges (recargo) of up to 10% of the tax, monthly late interest, and fines ranging from B/.500 to B/.50,000 under Código Fiscal Articles 709–726. Serious misdeclaration is prosecuted as criminal tax fraud under the Código Penal, carrying imprisonment sanctions; all export documentation and audit records must be retained for no less than five years following the filing deadline and throughout any pending audit or judicial proceedings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized. The Panamanian balboa is a nominal currency unit exchanged at a fixed 1:1 rate with the US dollar, which is legal tender. There are no exchange controls governing capital account or current account transactions, and no prior central-bank authorization is required for profit remittances, dividend distributions, or capital repatriation. The only practical hurdles are corporate and tax compliance requirements, especially the 5% dividend tax, corporate income tax clearance certificates, and AML/CFT onboarding due diligence imposed by licensed Panamanian banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion rules apply to export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in USD or any currency, held in Panamanian bank accounts, or disbursed abroad without exchange-control approval. There is no statutory conversion timeline. Exporters must only preserve underlying documentary evidence of the commercial transaction, including invoices, bills of lading, and payment instructions, to satisfy tax, AML, and SBP supervisory requirements.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank or foreign exchange administration. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) regulates banking and trust activities. Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the fiscal agent of the State and participates in public-sector clearing and settlement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a principal treasury rail, Moderate as an auxiliary cross-border payment gateway. Panama does not recognize non-bank electronic money institutions as deposit-taking entities; an unlicensed EMI cannot legally maintain customer balances or provide Panamanian clearing account functionality. Offshore digital EMIs may be used only for ancillary collection or disbursement on top of a fully licensed Panamanian or correspondent banking account, subject to SBP guidance on money laundering prevention, US-dollar net settlement, and customer-fund safeguarding.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from a disclosed beneficial owner or clearly related corporate entity whose name appears in the final onboarding dossier. The payment narrative must state a precise commercial concept, such as 'Capital Contribution', 'Share Subscription', or 'Paid-in Capital', in the corresponding SWIFT field. The receiving Panamanian bank will require the subscription minutes, board resolution, source-of-funds documentation, and AML questionnaires. Beneficiary credentials must include the exact corporate legal name, account number, and bank SWIFT code. No deposits should arrive from unrelated third parties or unregulated crypto-fintech platforms.Outbound settlements for bulk grain, soybeans, and yellow corn are primarily effected through SWIFT MT103 direct wires to suppliers, with US-dollar correspondent clearing via Fedwire or CHIPS for USD-denominated contracts. For structured agricultural trades, an irrevocable transferable letter of credit issued by the Panamanian bank and advised/confirmed by the supplier's bank under UCP 600 is the preferred documentary clearing rail.
