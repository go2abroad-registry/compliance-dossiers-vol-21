Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) – General Revenue Directorate of the Ministry of Economy and Finance of the Republic of Panama. Operational portal: SISTEMA TAX (DGI Digital Services) at dgi.mef.gob.pa and the SIVE electronic invoicing platform.

E-government filing portal

For a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) beneficially owned by non-residents, the statutory TIN/RUC is procured after (1) incorporation is recorded in the Registro Público de Panamá, and (2) a Panama-licensed resident agent has been appointed. The resident agent files with the DGI an application for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), submits the corporate charter and articles of incorporation, files an 'Aviso de Operación' (business opening notice), obtains the corporate digital signature certificate, and registers the authorized invoicing system. Non-resident beneficial owners are not required to appear in person; a notarized power of attorney and passport copies are sufficient. The DGI issues a 'Certificado de Registro Único de Contribuyente' containing the RUC number used for income tax, ITBMS, and customs purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope: corporate income tax is imposed only on income generated from Panamanian-source operations; foreign-source income (including revenue from offshore exports where the sale occurs abroad) is not subject to Panamanian corporate income tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: corporate income tax is imposed only on income generated from Panamanian-source operations; foreign-source income (including revenue from offshore exports where the sale occurs abroad) is not subject to Panamanian corporate income tax. For ITBMS (indirect tax), the entity is a regular taxable person if it carries out taxable transfers, imports, or taxable services in Panama, regardless of territorial-source classification.

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' or deemed pass-through entity election. Available statutory elections are limited to: (i) the simplified small-taxpayer regime (Pequeño Contribuyente) for gross annual receipts under the legal threshold; (ii) the optional calculation of ITBMS liability on the basis of input/output tax only rather than requiring monthly advances, subject to DGI approval; and (iii) elections under export incentive or free-zone laws (e.g., Ley 41 de 2007) for approved exporters. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized under the Panamanian Tax Code.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No fixed general economic-turnover threshold triggers ITBMS registration; any person habitually engaging in taxable imports, transfers of movable property, or taxable services in Panama must obtain a RUC and register for ITBMS. A simplified 'small taxpayer' regime is available for businesses with gross annual receipts of up to PAB 150,000 (USD 150,000).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods, including electronic patient monitoring equipment, are zero-rated for ITBMS purposes when such goods physically leave Panamanian customs territory, ownership passes to a non-resident buyer, and the goods are not used or consumed in Panama. Offshore services, including telemetry data processing or calibration services for foreign buyers, are zero-rated when the direct beneficiary is a non-resident and the service is effectively not consumed in Panama. The taxpayer must hold an active RUC with the relevant export activity classification and must be registered in the DGI exporter registry.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Mercancías / DUA – Declaración Única Aduanera) accepted by the Panama Customs Authority (ANA), including the Electronic Export Number (EAE)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, airway, or land manifest) clearly evidencing dispatch from a Panamanian port or warehouse to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice stating the transaction value in USD and showing the applicable 0% ITBMS treatment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) issued by a Panamanian bank demonstrating successful repatriation and deposit of export proceeds in a local bank account
  • Certificate of Origin and shipping/insurance documentation evidencing the physical movement of goods to the non-resident buyer

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) under DGI regulations. Invoices must be generated through a DGI-authorized electronic invoicing system or the DGI's SIVE platform, and invoice numbering must be pre-authorized by the DGI (Certificado de Autorización de Impresión for physical/electronic ranges). Taxpayers transmit invoice XML data to the DGI through the prescribed web services using a valid digital certificate, and receive an electronic acknowledgement identifying each accepted batch. Real-time fiscal clearance applies to manufacturers and other high-volume taxpayers; for exporters, the 0% export invoices are cross-validated against the customs declaration system.

The DGI and the Panama Customs Authority (ANA) jointly audit export zero-rating claims by reconciling the customs export declaration, the ITBMS return, and the inward remittance certificate. If any export claim is unsupported or documents are found fraudulent, the 0% treatment is clawed back with statutory interest from the date the tax would otherwise have been due. Additional penalties include fines up to 50% of the omitted tax under Código Fiscal Article 784, criminal liability for falsifying export documentation, disqualification from future zero-rating, and, where the recipient is actually a Panamanian resident, an automatic reclassification as territorial-source taxable revenue subject to income tax and ITBMS penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama's monetary regime is fully dollarized, so there is no local fiat currency and no foreign-exchange control authority. Capital-account and current-account convertibility are unrestricted. Profit and dividend remittances are freely transferable, but banks require the remitting entity to be in good tax standing (Paz y Salvo) and to provide the underlying corporate resolution. For outbound payments to non-cooperative jurisdictions, the payer must observe Panama's tax transparency rules, which may render expenses non-deductible if the beneficiary lacks economic substance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panamanian law imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no conversion ratio. The exporter may retain USD or any other convertible currency freely in SBP-regulated bank accounts.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama has no central bank issuing local currency; the United States dollar is legal tender. Banking and payment-system supervision is exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). The State-owned Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent of the Republic, while the Ministry of Economy and Finance supervises tax-clearance requirements relevant to outbound remittances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating is Medium only for ancillary collection and payment passthrough. Offshore digital EMI accounts are not legally recognized as bank accounts in Panama; a foreign fintech/EMI cannot debit or credit local accounts without a license from the SBP. Panama's fintech sandbox allows supervised electronic-money institutions, but for high-value ECG equipment transactions, import LC issuances and export collections must clear through an SBP-regulated commercial bank. Any inbound funds routed to the S.A. through an unlicensed overseas EMI will likely be rejected or frozen pending enhanced due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital contributions from the foreign parent must be remitted in USD via SWIFT MT103, or via Fedwire from the parent's U.S. correspondent where applicable. The SWIFT Field 70 narrative must state 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN' to permit accurate classification by the receiving bank. The bank will require the S.A.'s board resolution authorizing the capital increase or intercompany loan, an updated shareholder register, a source-of-wealth statement for the remitter, a negative PEP declaration, and, if a loan, a signed loan agreement with market-rate interest terms. Panama has no exchange-control registration, but the receiving bank must complete internal beneficial-ownership verification before crediting the funds.Trade disbursements for component purchases and calibration supplies use USD-denominated SWIFT MT103 wire transfers or documentary letters of credit issued under UCP600. For standby and commercial LC flows, the Panamanian bank issues MT700, amendments via MT707, and settlement through MT752/MT754 with the confirming bank. USD clearing is effected through nostro accounts at New York correspondent banks via CHIPS or Fedwire; same-day local dollar clearing may use Panama's Automated Clearing House where both parties hold accounts in SBP-regulated banks.
