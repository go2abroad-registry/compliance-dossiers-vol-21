Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is required under Law 32 of 1927; the articles may fix a nominal capital (e.g. USD 10,000) at the incorporators' discretion. There is no escrow, pre-funding, or capital levy condition precedent to registration.

Funding & unlock path: Each operating property must obtain the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá fire safety certificate, municipal structural and occupancy inspection, and SINAPROC evacuation/emergency compliance where applicable. The REIT's own office must register with MITRADEL for occupational health and safety and obtain the workplace clearance before employees are stationed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); tax administration and filing portal: DGI e-Tax electronic services platform and the Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (SFE) portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Sociedad Anónima must: (1) incorporate through a licensed resident agent and register the corporate deed with the Registro Público de Panamá; (2) maintain a registered office in Panama and obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación; (3) appoint a local fiscal representative (representante fiscal) and file the Formulario de Solicitud de Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) with the DGI, attaching the certificate of incorporation, bylaws, shareholder and officer records, and proof of registered address; (4) upon issuance of the RUC/NIT, complete the ITBMS (VAT) registration and, where applicable, employer registration; and (5) activate the DGI e-Tax digital certificate and SFE electronic-invoicing enrolment. Non-resident shareholders are expressly permitted and no local shareholding is required; the resident agent and fiscal representative remain the statutory service and filing nexus.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope — A foreign-owned Sociedad Anónima must: (1) incorporate through a licensed resident agent and register the corporate deed with the Registro Público de Panamá; (2) maintain a registered office in Panama and obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación; (3) appoint a local fiscal representative (representante fiscal) and file the Formulario de Solicitud de Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) with the DGI, attaching the certificate of incorporation, bylaws, shareholder and officer records, and proof of registered address; (4) upon issuance of the RUC/NIT, complete the ITBMS (VAT) registration and, where applicable, employer registration; and (5) activate the DGI e-Tax digital certificate and SFE electronic-invoicing enrolment. Non-resident shareholders are expressly permitted and no local shareholding is required; the resident agent and fiscal representative remain the statutory service and filing nexus.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' or elective pass-through regime; every Sociedad Anónima is an entity-level taxpayer, liable only on Panamanian-source income. Available statutory elections include: (a) the territorial-source taxation default, with complete exclusion of foreign-source income; (b) optional consolidated income tax filing for economically linked companies subject to prior DGI authorization; (c) the simplified small-taxpayer quota method for ITBMS where annual turnover is within statutory limits; and (d) the special capital-market regimes for SMV-regulated investment funds, trusts, and real-estate investment vehicles, which may achieve tax-neutral distribution treatment upon fulfillment of licensing and public-registry conditions. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized under the Código Fiscal; transfer-pricing documentation follows BEPS-aligned rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Panama imposes no monetary exemption threshold: any person habitually carrying on taxable commercial activity in Panamanian territory must obtain a RUC and register as an ITBMS collector upon commencement of operations. Taxpayers with gross annual taxable turnover not exceeding B/.36,000 (Balboas, pegged 1:1 to the US dollar) may elect the simplified small-taxpayer quota regime; above that threshold, full monthly ITBMS return filing, SFE electronic invoicing, and input-tax credit accounting are mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% ITBMS rate applies where: (i) tangible goods are physically exported from Panama under customs supervision; (ii) services qualify as offshore services, being rendered to a beneficiary not domiciled in Panama and not consumed or commercially exploited within Panamanian territory; and (iii) the exporter is a registered ITBMS taxpayer issuing valid electronic invoices. For this REIT entity, rental income from real estate situated in Panama does NOT qualify for export zero-rating, because the situs of the property constitutes Panamanian-source consumption; zero-rating remains available only for cross-border advisory or management services to non-resident investors and for tangible goods exported abroad. Input ITBMS attributable to zero-rated transactions may be offset or recovered as saldos a favor through the DGI refund mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) filed and cleared through the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) electronic module
  • Commercial invoice, in Spanish or English, expressly stating the ITBMS zero-rated (0%) nature of the transaction
  • Original Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical departure from Panamanian territory
  • Packing list and, where applicable, certificate of origin
  • Bank credit advice or Inward Remittance Certificate proving collection of the export proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • For offshore services: signed cross-border service contract and tax-residence certificate of the non-resident recipient

All active ITBMS taxpayers must issue electronic invoices through DGI-certified accounting software or the SFE web portal. Each invoice is transmitted to the DGI in near-real time, validated, and returned with a unique digital stamp and QR code (código de validación) that must appear on the invoice delivered to the buyer; unvalidated invoices are not tax-deductible by the recipient. The e-invoicing mandate is phased by taxpayer category under successive DGI resolutions, with full rejection-and-correction workflows integrated into the e-Tax environment.

Panama is dollarized and maintains no separate exchange-control repatriation regime, so the 180-day period represents the DGI-accepted evidentiary window for crediting export proceeds to a Panamanian bank account; the bank credit advice is the controlling proof. The DGI, in coordination with the ANA, may retrospectively reopen export zero-rating claims for up to six years. Fraudulent, backdated, or materially defective export documentation triggers: (i) re-levy of ITBMS at the 7% standard rate; (ii) statutory late-payment surcharge and interest; (iii) administrative fines generally ranging from one to three times the omitted tax; (iv) clawback (inadmissibility) of related input credits; and (v) criminal tax-fraud liability for the legal representatives and fiscal representative, with imprisonment of two to five years under the Código Fiscal and Law 8 of 2010, as amended. Missing ANA clearance or inward-remittance evidence defeats the zero-rating claim and converts the transaction into an uncollected taxable supply at the standard rate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized monetary system: the US Dollar is legal tender for all banking, clearing, and contractual obligations, and the Balboa exists only as sub-dollar coinage at a fixed 1:1 parity. Convertibility is total and unrestricted for both current account transactions (rents, service fees, interest, lease income) and capital account transactions (equity injections, redemption of participations, repatriation of sale proceeds). There are no exchange-rate bands, no prior FX licensing requirements, and no foreign-exchange control statute. Profit remittance hurdles are exclusively fiscal rather than FX-related: dividends distributed by a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima out of Panamanian-source taxable income bear a 10% dividend tax, which may be withheld by the corporation or paid as a prepayment, and remittances of taxable dividend amounts require production of DGI payment certifications. Distributions made from foreign-source income under Panama's territorial tax principle are exempt from dividend taxation. No additional FX approval is required; the REIT may remit USD directly through any SBP-licensed bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panama imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters, cross-border service providers, and REITs receiving foreign-source consideration (including sale proceeds from overseas properties and non-Panamanian rental income) may freely retain, convert, or disburse USD, EUR, or other convertible currencies without governmental intervention. There is no prescribed repatriation timeline and no obligation to report foreign receipts to the DGI except under standard AML/CFT reporting rules, namely UAF currency transaction reports for transactions at or above USD 10,000 and any suspicious-activity reporting.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) — banking regulator and de facto monetary authority under Panama's full-dollarization regime; Banco Nacional de Panamá — fiscal agent and state-owned settlement bank; Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) — fiscal and monetary policy coordination. Panama has no currency-issuing central bank; the United States Federal Reserve System serves as the effective provider of base money.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating for principal treasury operations: LOW. Legal constraints: (i) foreign offshore EMIs hold no Panamanian banking license and cannot lawfully receive core operating deposits from a Panama-domiciled REIT without breaching banking intermediation restrictions under Decree Law 9 of 1998; (ii) the SBP authorizes payment services exclusively through locally incorporated payment entities under its fintech regulatory framework (SBP Agreement 004-2022), and a foreign EMI lacks membership in Panama's ACH clearing house and direct access to the local SWIFT correspondent network; (iii) funds held at a foreign EMI remain outside SBP prudential supervision and Panama's deposit-protection coverage; (iv) for escrow-linked REIT capital commitments, counterparties and settling banks will not treat EMI account statements as proof of cleared funds. Ancillary viability: MEDIUM — a foreign EMI may be employed solely for tenant pre-collection in niche cross-border corridors on the condition that balances are swept daily to the local tier-1 operational account; such arrangements must be disclosed in the REIT's AML/CFT compliance program and carry elevated money-laundering risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing relies on direct SWIFT MT103 credits to the REIT's operational account at a tier-1 SBP-licensed bank. Panama maintains no statutory purpose-code system; remitters should nevertheless state in SWIFT Field 70: 'Equity contribution to REIT capital base — Panamanian territorial exemption', referencing the shareholder number and REIT registration file. Verification protocols: (i) currency-transaction reporting to the UAF for any credit of USD 10,000 or more; (ii) enhanced due diligence requiring source-of-funds evidence for credits above USD 30,000; (iii) notarized contribution agreements or funds-comfort letters for credits above USD 500,000; and (iv) confirmation that the originating institution is licensed in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction. The receiving bank must also verify authorized signatories and corporate-sealing against its mandate records prior to crediting.Outbound settlements for property purchases, construction disbursements, and income distributions execute predominantly via SWIFT MT103 through each Panamanian bank's New York correspondent (CHIPS), reflecting Panama's USD-based clearing infrastructure; large-value local payments may settle intraday through the national automated clearing house (Cámara de Compensación Automatizada). Construction and development disbursements are routed under irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued per UCP 600 by the tier-1 Panamanian bank against drawdown certificates, invoices, and lien waivers. Escrow disbursements follow the conditions precedent of the purchase contract and are released only upon confirmation of title transfer at the Registro Público. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 2,000,000 in aggregate related-party transactions in a fiscal year; a Local File is required when related-party revenue exceeds this threshold. A Master File is required when the Panama entity belongs to a multinational group with consolidated worldwide turnover exceeding USD 50 million. The REIT must also file Form 210 (Related-Party Transactions Information Return) with the DGI for any covered transaction.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 78 of 2019) apply to any entity undertaking 'relevant activities', including a Real Estate Investment Trust holding Panamanian property. The REIT must show: (i) management and direction in Panama, (ii) adequate physical premises, (iii) qualified employees and payroll, and (iv) core income-generating activity in Panama. A full ESR information return is due within twelve months of fiscal year-end; failure to satisfy ESR creates a conclusive presumption of shell status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to 200% of evaded tax, monthly surcharges, and criminal penalties of 2 to 5 years imprisonment under Law 70 of 2019; the tax liability becomes enforceable against all group assets.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution where they signed the false return; the DGI may impose travel bans when the unpaid tax exceeds USD 10,000, and criminal proceedings carry imprisonment.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: ESR non-compliance penalties up to USD 50,000 for false filings; the DGI can dissolve the entity and disallow treaty withholding benefits; profits may be reclassified and taxed at 25% plus 50% surcharge.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for shell misuse; directors may be disqualified from forming new Panamanian entities, their shares frozen, and they may face 3 to 5 years imprisonment for breach of Economic Substance Regulations.
Anti-Money Laundering and Asset Forfeiture

Statutory sanction: Fines under Law 23 of 2015 of up to USD 5,000,000; criminal money-laundering sanctions from 5 to 15 years imprisonment; assets linked to illicit activity may be confiscated.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are criminally responsible for willful failure to apply AML controls; travel bans and asset freezes are imposed, and the corporate veil is pierced to the extent of illicit proceeds.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Fatal Red Flags

Statutory sanction: The DGI may reassess taxable income using the arm's-length standard, apply a 25% tax on the adjustment, charge a 10% penalty, and impose monthly interest from the original due date; criminal referral for a false statement is possible.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the transactional documents with knowledge of the non-arm's-length price are jointly liable for the additional tax and penalty; civil suits for restitution by minority shareholders may also pierce the veil.
