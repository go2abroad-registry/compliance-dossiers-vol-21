Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá (Public Registry of Panama), Sección de Personas Jurídicas / Registro de Sociedades, operating jointly with the PANAMÁ EMPRENDE Ventanilla Única (MICI-DGI-Municipality inter-institutional window).

Governing statute

Ley General de Sociedades, Law No. 32 of 1927, as amended (including Law No. 25 of 2010 and Law No. 56 of 2018), Articles 1-6 (formation, minimum capital, subscription and payment), Article 17 (resident agent), Articles 30-35 (board of directors and officers); Acuerdo General No. 178 of 2018 of the Registro Público de Panamá; Executive Decree No. 165 of 2019 (PANAMÁ EMPRENDE one-stop incorporation procedure).

Formation / constitutional filings

Escritura Pública de Constitución (public deed executed before a Panamanian Notary Public) or, for the online path, the Certificado de Constitución de Sociedad Anónima generated by the PANAMÁ EMPRENDE 'Línea S.A.' electronic system; both instruments require inscription at the Registro Público.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; e-Tributario portal (https://e-tributario.dgi.gob.pa).

E-government filing portal

A foreign shareholder incorporates a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima by public deed before a notary and registers it in the Public Registry. Before starting any Panamanian-source activity, the company must submit an Aviso de Operación through a resident agent to DGI and obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) via the e-Tributario portal. The S.A. then registers for ITBMS if it carries on taxable supplies of goods or services in Panama. Non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the S.A. shares; a resident agent must maintain local corporate records. For export or GDP pharma freight logistics, the company must also register as an exporter with Panama Customs and, if applicable, obtain approval under a special regime such as SEM or a free-zone license.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope. A Panama S.A. is a separate taxable corporation, not a pass-through entity. Income derived from Panamanian source is taxable; foreign-source income received by the S.A. is generally outside Panamanian income tax. Panama does not provide U.S.-style check-the-box pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Panama. Panamanian law does not provide hybrid entity classification elections. Available elective special regimes include Multinational Headquarters (SEM) under Law 41 of 2007 and Law 8 of 2010 for qualifying regional service and logistics management operations, Colon Free Zone licensing, and special tax stabilization contracts for export-oriented logistics projects. Such elections require prior approval by DGI or the pertinent Panamanian authority.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general zero-turnover threshold; any company carrying on commercial or logistics activity in Panama must obtain a RUC and register for ITBMS. Natural-person microbusinesses may use a simplified small-taxpayer regime below limits determined by DGI, but this does not apply to a PA_CORP Sociedad Anónima engaged in GDP pharma freight.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services from Panama may be zero-rated if the goods are physically exported or the services are rendered for the direct benefit of a non-resident and consumed abroad. For GDP pharma freight and logistics, the services must relate to cargo whose final destination is outside Panama, be incidental to international transit, or qualify as offshore services under DGI criteria. The supplier must be ITBMS-registered and issue an electronic invoice identifying the foreign client or exporter and the non-Panama destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Documento Único de Aduana / export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/ACH credit confirmation
  • International commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • RUC/ITBMS registration certificate
  • Proof of final cargo destination outside Panama
  • Copy of the international logistics contract for services zero-rating

Mandatory electronic invoicing under DGI's Factura Electrónica regime. Registered taxpayers must transmit invoices to DGI in structured XML format through an authorized service provider or the DGI online system, obtain a DGI fiscal authorization code for each invoice set, and maintain digital records. Invoices must be issued immediately upon each supply; DGI may clear or reject in near real time for large taxpayers. There is no separate physical stamping; the DGI authorization code and digital stamp serve as fiscal clearance.

DGI may require positive documentary proof that goods left Panamanian territory or services were consumed abroad. Missing, false, or fraudulent export documentation triggers reassessment of ITBMS at 7% on the export price, statutory interest, surcharges, administrative fines up to 200% of unpaid tax, cancellation of electronic invoicing authorization and RUC, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud. The tax authority's clawback and review period is generally five years; fraudulent suppression may extend the limitation period. Panama has no exchange controls and therefore no statutory FX repatriation deadline, but banking documentation must corroborate the remittance for refund claims and audit validation.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized monetary system under which the balboa is a nominal unit at strict parity with the US dollar. There are no exchange controls on the current or capital account. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, capital repatriation, and payment for services require only standard corporate and tax filing compliance, including, where applicable, the 10% Panamanian dividend withholding tax on net taxable earnings. No central bank currency conversion authorization is required, and no balance-of-payments approval gate applies to a Sociedad Anónima conducting GDP pharma freight activities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no export proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion requirement. Because the US dollar is the functional currency of the Republic, export proceeds and service fees, including pharmaceutical logistics and freight revenue, may be retained in USD indefinitely, held in any approved currency, or fully repatriated without time-specific conversion windows.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Panama Banking Superintendent) as the prudential bank regulator, with Banco Nacional de Panamá serving as the fiscal agent of the Republic. Panama has no traditional central bank issuing domestic currency; the US dollar is legal tender and circulates freely.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs have low direct viability as primary treasury counterparties. Panama does not recognize an EU-style EMI license; any entity performing payment intermediation must hold a Superintendencia de Bancos license, a trust company license, or a 'Pago Electrónico' authorization under Ley 1 de 2022. A foreign EMI may operate only by partnering with a licensed Panamanian bank or finance company, and such partnerships are subject to mandatory KYC look-through and to the bank's own AML program. Using an unlicensed EMI would expose the GDP freight entity to aggravated due diligence and potential regulatory adverse action; therefore EMIs are recommended only as supplementary receivables concentrators where the banking partner has a clear controlled-network agreement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the Panamanian S.A. must be wire-transferred via SWIFT MT103 to the beneficiary account at a Superintendencia de Bancos-licensed bank. The payment narrative must expressly state 'Capital Injection – Sociedad Anónima' and include the corporate tax ID (RUC) and escrow reference. Panamanian banks apply the Law 32 AML threshold for cash declarations and require a source-of-funds questionnaire for any deposit exceeding USD 10,000. For extranet vendor injections, a signed escrow letter and corporate resolution must accompany the MT103. Panama does not use IMF-style purpose codes, but the bank's internal transaction form will require a purpose designation; recommended codes are 'Shareholder Capital Contribution', 'Shareholder Loan', or 'Trade Receivables Funding'.Outbound disbursements for GDP pharma freight settle via SWIFT MT103 for supplier payments, via MT700/MT701 documentary letters of credit for high-value pharmaceutical cargo, and via Direct ACH for recurring low-value disbursements within permitted corridors. All outbound payments must be mapped to an underlying airway bill, commercial invoice, and the GDP distribution certificate where applicable. For customs duties and MINSA-regulated fees, the bank may require prior presentation of the import permit and a legalized cost breakdown. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File are required when aggregate related-party transactions exceed USD 500,000 per fiscal year or annual gross revenue exceeds USD 3,000,000. Panama uses the USD as legal tender, and B/. figures are equivalent to USD.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Law 57 of 2019, as amended, imposes Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) on entities carrying on relevant activities. A GDP clinical freight and logistics operation will normally be classified as a 'Distribution and Service Centre' relevant activity and must demonstrate physical presence, adequate employees in Panama, local management and an annual ESR declaration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Surcharge of up to 200% of omitted tax, monthly default interest, administrative embargo of bank accounts, and criminal prosecution under Panama's Tax Code and related anti-evasion laws

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for civil restitution; criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment, prohibition from corporate management, and travel bans
Transfer Pricing Disregard

Statutory sanction: Primary tax adjustment, recharacterization of revenue, secondary withholding assessments, default interest, and administrative fines for documentation failures

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for adjusted taxes if they knowingly signed false TP declarations; potential criminal liability for abusive transfer pricing in serious cases
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR fines up to thousands of dollars per violation, compulsory deregistration of the S.A. in the Public Registry, and exchange of information notifications to foreign jurisdictions

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined for false ESR filings, barred from serving as directors, and subjected to travel restrictions under AML and tax enforcement actions
GDP / AML Criminal Liability

Statutory sanction: Revocation of GDP authorization, customs and health penalties, financial intelligence referrals, freezing orders, asset seizure and fines under Panama's AML regime

Director personal liability: Directors can be charged personally for trafficking counterfeit or non-conforming pharma goods; custodial sentences, travel bans and civil restitution may be imposed on senior management
