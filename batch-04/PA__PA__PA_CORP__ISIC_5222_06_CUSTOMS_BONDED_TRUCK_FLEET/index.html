Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under Panamanian law; the deed must state an authorized capital (commonly US$10,000 divided into common shares). Capital contributions may remain unpaid until called, and no escrow or bank deposit obligation is imposed at incorporation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direccion General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economia y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; taxpayer portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa; electronic filing and e-invoicing platform: SITeD and the DGI Facturacion Electronica portal. Customs administration is separate, under the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA), operating the SICE single window for export and bonded transit declarations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Sociedad Anonima (S.A.) at the Registro Publico de Panama through a licensed resident agent (abogado idoneo) and obtain the Public Registry certificate (testimonio) with the registered corporate bylaws and appointment of officers. Step 2: File the municipal Aviso de Operacion (business notice) with the Alcaldia of the operational domicile and obtain the municipal operating license/patente. Step 3: Procure from the government electronic signature authority (AIG) a certified digital signature (Firma Digital) for the legal representative, required for all DGI filings. Step 4: Submit the RUC (Registro Unico de Contribuyente) application at DGI presenting the Public Registry attestation, Aviso de Operacion, proof of fiscal domicile, notarized and apostilled powers of attorney, and identification of the legal representative; the RUC certificate is generally issued within 5-10 business days. Step 5: Register the ITBMS general-regime status, enroll in the DGI electronic invoicing program, and register the employer payroll planilla with the DGI. Step 6 (foreign entities without a Panamanian incorporation): a non-resident entity must appoint a resident fiscal agent, register a branch or permanent establishment at the Registro Publico, and obtain a local RUC through the same DGI procedure; the RUC is the exclusive statutory tax identity for issuing invoices and filing returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: the S.A. is a Panamanian resident taxpayer taxed at entity level on Panama-source income only; corporate income tax (ISR) applies at 15% on the first B/. 50,000 of net taxable income and 25% on the excess; foreign-source income and capital gains realized abroad are outside the Panamanian tax net; dividends paid from Panama-source profits are subject to a 10% withholding tax (5% for shares listed on the Bolsa de Valores de Panama); there are no CFC rules, no worldwide consolidation, and no general thin-capitalization limitation.

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box election or a BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch election, and hybrid mismatch rules have not been enacted. Available statutory elections: (1) the small-taxpayer simplified regime (REPEC) - an S.A. with annual gross revenue not exceeding B/. 36,000 may elect a monthly presumptive quota in lieu of regular ISR and ITBMS filing obligations; (2) the reduced 5% dividend withholding rate election for S.A. issuers with shares admitted to trading on the Bolsa de Valores de Panama; (3) the free-zone or bonded-operator election allowing suspension of ITBMS on qualifying intra-zone and re-export movements where the operator holds an ANA or Ministerio de Comercio license; and (4) the accelerated depreciation election for commercial vehicle and GPS fleet assets under DGI guidelines, requiring a written accounting policy filed with the annual ISR return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General regime: no de minimis threshold - any person habitually transferring movable goods or providing services in Panama, including a non-resident acting through a Panamanian permanent establishment, must register for ITBMS from the first taxable operation. Simplified REPEC small-taxpayer threshold: cumulative annual gross revenue up to B/. 36,000 (Balboas 36,000, equal to USD 36,000 under the fixed 1:1 peg) permits election of the monthly presumptive quota regime replacing regular ITBMS/ISR declarations. The bonded truck fleet operator providing international airport feeder services is in the general regime and must file monthly ITBMS returns.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The bonded truck fleet operator may claim 0% ITBMS on qualifying export feeder services when the cargo is in customs bonded transit to an international flight and the commercial risk and contracting party are non-resident. The exporter of record must hold the RUC and issue the invoice with the DGI-validated electronic fiscal code indicating ITBMS 0%. The claim must be substantiated with customs and commercial documentation showing the physical exit of goods from the national customs territory within a reasonable time after invoicing.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaracion de Exportacion) filed through the ANA SICE single window, bearing the electronic acknowledgment and export stamp of the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas.
  • Customs Transit Declaration and carrier road manifest (Hoja de Ruta / Manifiesto de Carga Terrestre) evidencing the regulated bonded movement of the sealed unit from origin to the departure airport.
  • Master or house Air Waybill (MAWB/HAWB) or multimodal transport waybill issued to the non-resident principal, evidencing the international carriage of the consignment.
  • Commercial invoice issued at 0% ITBMS with the DGI-validated fiscal number, describing the ISIC 5222 road-feeder service and referencing the related AWB and customs declaration.
  • GPS seal tracking report and delivery receipt (carta de porte) demonstrating uninterrupted bonded custody from collection to aircraft-side delivery.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank credit advice showing receipt of USD funds from the non-resident counterparty within the 180-day benchmark.
  • Packing list and consignment security/SSL certificate, plus, upon DGI audit request, the DGI export certification form and the carrier's ANA bonded operator license.

Electronic invoicing (Facturacion Electronica) is mandatory for general-regime taxpayers, phased in since 2021 and currently required for all RUC holders issuing commercial documents. Permitted issuance channels: (a) the DGI web portal (eFactura) using the holder's certified digital signature, (b) DGI-approved fiscal POS equipment or fiscal printers, and (c) machine-to-machine API (M2M/ERP) integration directly from the carrier's transport management system. Each invoice in XML/CII format must be transmitted to DGI for real-time automated validation before the fiscal copy is delivered; the DGI returns an acceptance token (respuesta de validacion) and a sequential fiscal authorization number that must be reproduced on the customer copy, including a machine-readable QR element. Taxpayers must batch-report daily sales, preserve the DGI-validated XML for at least 7 years, and reconcile e-invoice data with monthly ITBMS returns. Export declarations are separately cleared through the ANA SICE single window, which interfaces with the DGI for export VAT substantiation.

DGI may audit ITBMS positions within a 5-year limitation period. Where 0% export treatment is applied without valid SICE export declarations, matched air waybills, and foreign remittance evidence, DGI will reverse the zero-rating, assess the unpaid 7% ITBMS, charge moratory interest, and impose administrative fines ranging from 25% to 100% of the omitted tax, or up to 200% for repeated or fraudulent conduct. Refunds already claimed are clawed back with interest and penalty. False attestation of exportation (including fabricated waybills, sham transit declarations, or simulated off-shore contracts) constitutes tax fraud and may trigger criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment under the Codigo Penal, alongside administrative disqualification. ANA may additionally suspend or revoke the bonded truck fleet operator's customs license and its Operador Economico Autorizado (AEO) status, barring the entity from the bonded transit regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized monetary system with no local paper currency. Current and capital account items are freely convertible; there are no exchange controls, no capital gains repatriation restrictions, and no obligatory surrender of export proceeds. Profit remittance is subject only to ordinary corporate income tax if sourced to Panama income and withholding tax for distributions to non-resident shareholders at applicable treaty or statutory rate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists under Panamanian law. Export receipts may be retained in any currency, although business must be conducted in U.S. dollars for local clearing and SBP-regulated bank settlement.

Central bank supervision: No Panamanian central bank exists; the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) acts as the prudential banking supervisor. The U.S. dollar is legal tender under Law 84 of 1946 and Decree Law 14 of 1973.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium: Panama does not currently issue EU-style e-money institution (EMI) licenses, but SBP-regulated 'fintech' service corporations may operate under the Panama Financial Innovation Law (Law 42 of 2023) and obtain a 'Servicios Financieros' license for payments and digital wallets. Non-regulated offshore EMIs cannot clear Panamanian ACH or U.S. dollar point-of-sale transactions without a sponsoring local bank and are viewed as unlicensed financial institutions by SBP for AML/CFT purposes.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (top marginal rate; progressive scale applied to net taxable income before the 10% dividend tax: 0% on the first USD 50,000; 15% on the band from USD 50,001 to USD 1,500,000; 25% above USD 1,500,000)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends paid out of Panamanian-source taxable profits (Art. 731-A, Código Fiscal). The 10% applies to distributions to both residents and non-residents; dividends sourced from foreign-source income of the Panama S.A. are exempt. No branch remittance tax applies to non-resident shareholders, but the dividend WHT is due at the moment the dividend is declared or effectively paid, whichever occurs first.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 250,000 (aggregate annual related-party transactions in the fiscal year). If the Customs Bonded Truck Fleet entity enters into intercompany arrangements (e.g., transport fees with a foreign airline group member, management fees, intercompany loans, or warehouse subleases) exceeding USD 250,000, it must prepare and file the Local File (Form 750) and, where relevant, the Master File, in accordance with Executive Decree No. 56 of 2018, as amended by Executive Decree No. 44 of 2020. Penalties for failure: USD 1,000–10,000 per infraction plus TP recharacterization.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not apply EU-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) comparable to BVI or Cayman; however, the territorial tax system and GAAR (Law 38 of 2017, effective FY 2019) require real commercial substance. A Panama S.A. operating the bonded truck fleet must maintain: (i) its principal decision-making office in Panama; (ii) payroll, social security (CSS) registrations, and at least locally employed dispatchers/drivers; (iii) physical custody of the GPS-sealed trucks and trailers registered before the Transit Authority (ATTT) and Customs (ANA); (iv) signed customs bond agreements and warehouse lease contracts; (v) minutes proving that management decisions are made in Panama. Failure to sustain substance triggers: (a) CAIR minimum tax as a punitive alternative charge; (b) denial of intercompany deductions under GAAR; (c) reclassification of the S.A. as a tax-resident conduit, exposing non-resident shareholders to Panamanian taxation; and (d) FATF/AML scrutiny under Law 23 of 2015 for trader-as-shell risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal) and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil fine up to 200% of the evaded tax, monthly surcharges of 5% (cap 25%), legal interest, and clawback of the foreign-source exemption for the manipulated period. Criminal: imprisonment of 2 to 5 years under Art. 815-A of the Código Fiscal and Art. 254 of the Código Penal for aggravated fraud; fines of up to USD 250,000; administrative blacklisting before the DGI and ANA.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the return or knowingly direct the concealment are personally and severally liable for the unpaid ISR, WHT, surcharges, and interest via piercing of the corporate veil. The DGI may impose travel bans (alerta migratoria), freeze bank accounts, and order civil restitution. Foreign nominee directors face the same imprisonment exposure under Panamanian law and possible extradition under the Central American extradition conventions.
AML/CFT – Shell Company and Beneficial Ownership Violations (Law 23 of 2015, Law 52 of 2016, FATF)

Statutory sanction: Money laundering offences under Art. 254 of the Código Penal: imprisonment of 5 to 15 years and fines of up to USD 5 million; administrative sanctions under Law 23 of 2015 of USD 10,000–1,000,000 per breach; automatic forfeiture of the bonded warehouse licence and the GPS-sealed fleet; if the S.A. is adjudicated a shell, dissolution of the company may be ordered by the Public Registry.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners with knowledge or reckless disregard face personal criminal indictment. The Superintendency of Banks and the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF) may impose travel bans, asset freezing, and a permanent prohibition from serving as directors in the Panamanian financial system. Civil restitution is joint and several with the entity.
Transfer Pricing and Withholding Tax Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment with recomputed tax at 25%, plus 5% monthly surcharge up to 25% and legal interest; Form 750 penalties of USD 1,000–10,000 per infraction; the DGI may disregard the transaction and recharacterize it as a dividend, triggering additional 10% dividend WHT and 20% penalty on the misapplied credit. Continued failure can escalate to criminal tax fraud charges.

Director personal liability: The managing director, CFO, and any independent director who approved or facilitated the non-arm's-length payments are jointly and severally liable for the shortfall. If the foreign parent directed the arrangement, Panamanian directors remain the enforcement target; travel restrictions and disqualification from corporate boards apply under the DGI's compliance enforcement powers.
Customs, Licensing, and Economic Substance Breach (ANA and ATTT)

Statutory sanction: Customs fines of up to 10 times the commercial value of the merchandise moved in breach (Ley 12 de 2018, Customs Law); cancellation of the bonded carrier licence; public registry dissolution if the S.A. fails the annual fee and permanent agent requirements; CAIR minimum tax recomputation at 1% of net assets for every year lacking substance. In aggravated smuggling cases, penalties reach 3 to 7 years' imprisonment and confiscation of vehicles.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unauthorized release of bonded cargo; ANA can issue travel bans against the legal representative and order the seizure of the fleet; criminal liability for smuggling under Art. 258 of the Código Penal attaches to directors, customs brokers, and beneficial owners deemed to have control, with civil tort liability for the full customs debt.
