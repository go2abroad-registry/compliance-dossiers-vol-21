Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; e-filing and registration portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI at https://sede.dgi.gob.pa; customs counterpart: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) operating the SIUCA/VUPE clearance and documentation platform.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Sociedad Anónima operating a specialty tea lounge: (1) Appoint a resident legal agent (abogado idóneo) and maintain the registered office; (2) Inscribe the corporation in the Registro Público de Panamá; (3) File the Aviso de Operación with the local municipality (Alcaldía) covering the commercial premises; (4) Submit the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) application via the Sede Electrónica de la DGI using the RUC registration form, attaching the notarized public deed (testimonio de la escritura pública), the legal-agent certificate of idoneidad, and the municipal aviso de operación; (5) Receive the RUC resolution and obtain the digital signature certificate (Certificado de Firma Electrónica) required to access DGI e-services and electronic invoicing; (6) Register as an employer with the Caja de Seguro Social (CSS) before hiring staff. The DGI assigns the RUC and activates ITBMS/ISR obligations within approximately 5-10 business days; no minimum capital requirement and no local shareholding conditions apply to the S.A.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope (Panama-source income only). The Sociedad Anónima is a separate juridical person taxed on net Panama-source income at the 25% corporate rate, with a 15% rate applicable to the first B/. 50,000 of taxable income for entities whose annual net taxable income does not exceed B/. 1,500,000 (Law 43 of 2016). Non-resident shareholders are not subject to Panama withholding tax on dividends derived from Panama-source profits. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope (Panama-source income only). The Sociedad Anónima is a separate juridical person taxed on net Panama-source income at the 25% corporate rate, with a 15% rate applicable to the first B/. 50,000 of taxable income for entities whose annual net taxable income does not exceed B/. 1,500,000 (Law 43 of 2016). Non-resident shareholders are not subject to Panama withholding tax on dividends derived from Panama-source profits. The S.A. has no pass-through analogue in Panama and is not eligible for partnership-style flow-through treatment.

Available election forms

(a) Optional inclusion of foreign-source income in the Panamanian tax base under Art. 694-E of the Código Tributario by paying an additional 5% tax on net foreign-source income, thereby activating the foreign tax credit mechanism; (b) Election to adopt the SEM multinational-headquarters regime (Law 41 of 2007) for qualifying intra-group service operations, generally unavailable to a retail tea lounge; (c) Election of the simplified small-taxpayer regime (RTS, Law 33 of 2019) for gross annual turnover not exceeding B/. 36,000, replacing standard ITBMS and ISR with a presumptive 3% tax on gross revenue; (d) Panama has not enacted check-the-box legislation or OECD hybrid-mismatch (BEPS Action 2) entity elections; hybrid outcomes are mitigated by the strict territorial source rules and the absence of participation exemptions on foreign dividends.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis threshold for ordinary registration: any person habitually carrying out taxable transfers of movable goods or services in Panama must register for ITBMS before commencing operations (Art. 56, Law 8 of 2010). An optional simplified regime (RTS) applies only to businesses with gross annual sales not exceeding B/. 36,000, substituting the standard 7% ITBMS with a presumptive 3% single tax; a tea salon projecting turnover above B/. 36,000 is mandatorily subject to the ordinary 7% ITBMS registration and filing obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Art. 65-67, Law 8 of 2010 to: (i) definitive export of movable goods, such as packaged specialty tea physically shipped abroad and evidenced by an approved customs export declaration; (ii) services rendered from Panama to non-resident beneficiaries where the economic use and benefit occurs entirely outside Panama, as limited by ITBMS regulations; and (iii) supplies to free zones, multinational headquarters, and diplomatic missions under special regimes. On-premise tea service, lounge sales to domestic consumers, and takeaway retail within Panama remain taxable at the standard 7% rate. Only off-premise export transactions qualify for the 0% rate, generating a right to credit and refund of ITBMS borne on related inputs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) filed in SIUCA and cleared by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) through the VUPE single window
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the goods from Panamanian customs territory
  • Commercial invoice containing full goods description, quantities, and stated export value
  • Bank remittance or payment receipt (comprobante de transferencia) evidencing receipt of funds from the non-resident buyer
  • Packing list and, where applicable, sanitary or phytosanitary export permits for tea products
  • Proof of the exporter's active RUC and ITBMS registration status on the date of export

Mandatory electronic invoicing under the Sistema de Facturación Electrónica de Panamá (SIFEP) operated by the DGI: the taxpayer must obtain prior digital authorization and transmit each invoice (XML format, RSA digital signature) to the Sede Electrónica de la DGI in real time for validation; DGI clearance generates a unique authorization code and QR/digital stamp printed on the invoice. A simplified e-invoice (factura simplificada) is authorized for transactions up to B/. 200 at the point of sale. POS and credit-card payment software must integrate with DGI web services (SIFEP-WS API); non-compliant systems are blocked from obtaining invoice authorization codes and expose the taxpayer to automatic reassessment and fines.

DGI may reliquidate (retroactively claw back) zero-rated treatment wherever the export documentation, title transfer, or payment-flow evidence fails to prove final destination outside Panama, restoring ITBMS at 7% plus monthly surcharge interest on the recaptured tax. Administrative fines under the Código Tributario for defective invoicing or fraudulent refund claims range from B/. 1,000 to B/. 50,000; knowingly false export declarations that produce an undue credit or refund constitute fiscal fraud punishable under Ley 13 de 2018 with imprisonment of 3 to 7 years and monetary penalties of no less than 25% of the evaded tax. The exporter bears the evidentiary burden, and the DGI may examine the transaction throughout the statutory audit window of 5 years from the tax filing due date. Panama has no exchange-control laws, so the 180-day repatriation period operates as the evidentiary benchmark of a genuinely commercial export payment rather than a statutory currency-control deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized; both capital and current account convertibility are unrestricted with no central bank exchange-control approval required for profit remittances. Dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders may attract Panama dividend advance tax at rates between 5% and 10% depending on the applicable corporate tax regime, but no foreign-exchange license or surrender obligation arises.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. Exporters may hold, use, and remit foreign-currency proceeds freely; the U.S. dollar is the national currency, creating a structurally zero-reserve, zero-surrender environment.

Central bank supervision: No central bank exists; banking supervision is vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá and the U.S. dollar serves as legal tender

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. Panama does not recognize 'electronic money institution' as a distinct regulatory license category; holding client funds, issuing payment instruments, or facilitating remittances may trigger regulated banking-activity rules. Offshore digital EMIs cannot lawfully operate in Panama without a local banking or financial license, a registered agent, physical compliance presence, and full AML/CFT integration with the Superintendencia de Bancos.

10% final withholding tax on Panama-source dividends distributed by a Panamanian S.A.; dividends paid out of foreign-source profits of an S.A. may be excluded from Panamanian withholding under the territorial source rule.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) is required in Panama where aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed US$2,000,000 (B/.2,000,000) in the taxable year or where the DGI specifically requests supporting analysis; a Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for multinational groups exceeding Panama's consolidated revenue threshold under Panama's law implementing BEPS.

Economic substance scope: For a local high-end tea salon, no separate Economic Substance Regulations filing applies; however, Panama's anti-avoidance rules require real and measurable economic substance, including licensed operating premises, personnel, inventory, management, and expenditure in Panama. The S.A. must maintain its registered office, resident agent, and auditable records. Artificial arrangements structured to hide beneficial ownership or shift profits to foreign entities risk DGI recharacterization and AML intervention.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative: 50% to 100% of the evaded tax as penalty, plus statutory interest and late-filing surcharges; criminal: fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years for deliberate tax fraud under the Panamanian Criminal and Fiscal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the false return are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficit and penalties; courts may order civil restitution, asset embargoes, travel bans, and disqualification, and the corporate veil may be pierced in serious fraud.
Shell Company / Sham Substance and Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: The DGI may disregard the corporate form, reclassify foreign-source claims as Panama-source, apply 25% tax to rebuilt income, and impose penalties for omission; the corporation may also be struck off the Public Registry.

Director personal liability: Directors, resident agents, and administrators may face criminal responsibility for aggravated tax fraud, money laundering, and false documentation; travel bans and confiscation of S.A. assets can result.
