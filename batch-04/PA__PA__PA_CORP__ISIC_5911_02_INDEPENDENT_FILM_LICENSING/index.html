Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; official institutional portal: https://www.dgi.gob.pa; primary transactional e-filing portal: Sede Electrónica (https://sede.mef.gob.pa); electronic invoicing clearance via the SGT (Sistema de Gestión Tributaria) API integrated into the Sede Electrónica.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) before the Registro Público de Panamá; a resident agent (abogado idóneo) must be appointed and minimum capital of USD 10,000 is stated in the by-laws (no paid-in capital requirement). Step 2: After inscription in the Public Registry, file the tax registration application with the DGI to obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente, 10-digit legal-entity code, format 8 digits - 2 digits - 6 digits, e.g., 1234567-1-234567). Step 3: Submit notarized and registry-stamped corporate documents (certificate of incorporation, by-laws, appointment of officers), identification of the legal representative, proof of fiscal domicile in Panama (typically an operating office address or registered agent office), and, if applicable, the power of attorney for the resident agent. Step 4: DGI assigns the RUC and simultaneously enrolls the company for corporate income tax (ISR), ITBMS (VAT), and the quarterly/informative regimes (including the electronic invoicing credential). Step 5: Obtain the Aviso de Operación (business operating notice) from the Alcaldía Municipal for the economic activity; for a pure film-licensing holding company with no local exploitation, the DGI may register the entity as an 'agente retenedor' only. Step 6: Activate the Sede Electrónica account with digital signature (Firma Digital) and register the bank account for potential ITBMS refunds. A foreign-incorporated entity without Panamanian registration may not obtain a Panamanian RUC; the S.A. must be locally incorporated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope / No_Pass_Through_Eligibility. A Panamanian S.A. is a juridical person taxed at the entity level under the territorial source regime (Código Fiscal, Art. 694): only income economically produced, derived, or rendered within Panamanian territory is subject to corporate income tax at 25% (standard rate); foreign-source income (including royalties from film distribution and licensing earned from non-resident licensees where the exploitation occurs abroad) is exempt. No elective pass-through treatment exists (no check-the-box); Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable by default, although the Panama Pacifico Special Economic Area (SEM) and City of Knowledge (Know-How) free-zone regimes offer exemptions only if the company is physically established and authorized there, which is not part of the standard film-licensing setup.

Available election forms

Panama has no check-the-box, partnership pass-through, or hybrid mismatch election for a standard corporation. Available statutory elections: (1) Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente (simplified small-taxpayer regime) for gross annual income ≤ USD 36,000, allowing a 3.5% ITBMS rate and simplified income tax; (2) optional credit for deemed fixed costs when computing ISR on Panama-source services; (3) election to deduct interest subject to the 3:1 thin-capitalization debt-to-equity ratio (Código Fiscal, Law 8-2010); (4) accelerated depreciation election for certain film equipment; (5) under Law 41 of 2007 (CEDE - Certificado de Exportación), exporters of services — including international film licensing — can apply for export credit certificates (equivalent to 2.5% of export value subject to limits) usable against internal taxes; (6) no hybrid mismatch election exists, and Panama does not apply OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid rules beyond the general territorial source test.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 36,000 annual gross turnover (B/.36,000) — this amount establishes eligibility for the optional small-taxpayer simplified ITBMS regime (3.5% rate) only; there is no general turnover threshold for regular ITBMS registration. Registration is legally triggered by the habitual performance of taxable acts within Panamanian territory (Art. 758 Código Fiscal) or by the importation of taxable goods into Panama, regardless of revenue level. A non-resident film licensor is deemed to have a Panama ITBMS nexus if the licensed content is commercially exploited or the licensee distributes within Panama; licensing to foreign distributors with exploitation solely abroad creates no Panama ITBMS nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are subject to ITBMS at 0% (zero-rated/exempt with credit recovery) under Código Fiscal Art. 763(9). Eligibility criteria for a Panamanian film-licensing S.A.: (i) the film copyright or distribution right must be licensed to a non-resident counterparty and the film must be exploited, exhibited, or distributed exclusively (or principally) outside Panama; (ii) the export transaction must be evidenced by an international written contract (license agreement) and delivery of the licensed deliverables (master copies, digital files, documentation) to the foreign licensee to a destination abroad; (iii) the consideration must be paid from abroad in freely convertible currency; and (iv) the Panamanian company must not render related services physically performed inside Panama. If any exhibition or exploitation occurs in Panama, that portion is subject to 7% ITBMS. For services, the DGI applies the 'beneficiary/place of use' test: the service is exported when the recipient is abroad and the benefit is realized outside Panamanian territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / DUE lodged via the SIGA customs system; service exports require the Service Export Declaration issued under DGI regulations)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (for physical delivery of film masters; for digital-only delivery, the licensed delivery protocol and server-path evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit advice confirming SWIFT wire / Letter of Credit settlement from the foreign licensee)
  • License Agreement (contract detailing IP grant, territory, royalty rate, and termination; notarized and optionally registered with the Registro Público)
  • Film Copyright Registry extract or chain-of-title documentation (to prove beneficial ownership and licensing authority)
  • Export invoice (Factura Comercial/Export Invoice) with ITBMS 0% line item and the DGI e-invoice authorization code

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica) under DGI Resolution No. 214-2019 (as amended) through the Sede Electrónica / SGT platform. Taxpayers whose gross annual income exceeded USD 50,000 in the preceding fiscal year, and all Gran Contribuyente (large taxpayer) account holders, must issue e-invoices requiring real-time fiscal validation via the DGI API (SGT web services). Each approved invoice is digitally stamped with an authorized 'Código de Verificación' (verification code) and QR code. The invoice data package must include issuer RUC, fiscal series prefix assigned by DGI, ITBMS rate code, and invoice hash. Fiscal printers (impresoras fiscales) remain lawful only for taxpayers below the USD 50,000 threshold during the transition period; their use requires prior DGI homologation. Failure to adopt e-invoicing triggers fines under Art. 798 Código Fiscal (up to USD 10,000) and disallowance of input VAT credits.

The DGI audits export zero-rating through cross-verification of the e-invoice metadata, SIGA customs export records, and bank remittance data; a 'no-payment, no-export' documentary chain is required within 180 days of the export invoice. For fraudulent zero-rating claims: (i) the DGI may immediately reassess the omitted ITBMS at 7% plus default interest and apply a surcharge of 50% (minor infraction) to 200% (aggravated fraud) of the unpaid tax under Art. 797 Código Fiscal; (ii) administrative fines up to USD 10,000 per non-compliant invoice (Art. 798); (iii) criminal tax fraud (fraude fiscal) under Law 76 of 2019 and Decree Law 170 of 1993 is punishable with imprisonment of up to 5 years for the legal representative and abettors; (iv) retrospective clawback may be asserted against the open statute of limitations of 6 fiscal years (Art. 787 Código Fiscal). Additionally, misuse of the CEDE export certificate regime can trigger forfeiture of certificates and criminal charges under the customs code. Taxpayers bear the burden of proof: every element of the export bundle must be contemporaneous and in Spanish or duly translated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a full-dollarization regime (and proximal monetary union with the US financial system), meaning the USD circulates as legal tender and is freely convertible for all transaction purposes, both on current account and capital account. There are no exchange controls, no restrictions on the buying or selling of foreign currency, and no prior approval requirements for inbound or outbound fund transfers. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, royalty remittances, and liquidation proceeds may be freely transferred abroad without withholding or bureaucratic approval, subject solely to standard anti-money laundering (AML) and know-your-customer (KYC) compliance by the correspondent banks. Because Panama uses the USD, there is no local foreign exchange rate risk for USD-denominated film licensing contracts; settlement, invoicing, and cash accounting may be executed wholly in USD. Capital account openness is statutory; no distinction is made between resident and non-resident accounts regarding convertibility, though the financial information transparency regime (Law 23 of 2015) requires beneficial ownership and tax information disclosure to the authorities on a risk-based basis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for repatriation of export receipts. Earnings from international film copyright distribution and licensing may be retained offshore or brought into Panamanian bank accounts in USD freely. In accordance with the territorial tax system, export-derived income is generally outside Panamanian income tax unless the economic activity is deemed to be performed in Panama, but banking compliance still requires that all receipts be credited to a declared account and be traceable. The applicable rule is 0% mandatory surrender, 0 days repatriation deadline, and full freedom to hold proceeds in any currency (effectively USD).

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama does not maintain a traditional central bank with monetary policy authority. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) acts as the bank licensing, supervision, and regulatory authority. The Banco Nacional de Panamá (BNP) performs limited clearing, fiduciary, and fiscal-agent functions for the State; the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) oversees fiscal policy. Panama is a fully dollarized economy with the US Dollar (USD) as legal tender and no independent currency issuance or monetary policy framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for purely unlicensed offshore EMI digital platforms; MEDIUM for licensed Fintech entities in Panama. Panama has no standalone 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) licensing category; entities that issue digital payment instruments or process payment transactions are regulated under the SBP's payment systems and financial technology regulations. Offshore digital EMIs that are not licensed in Panama may not maintain accounts for Panamanian corporations without breaching the general banking monopoly; inbound wire payments to an unlicensed EMI will be treated as non-banking counterparty risk and may fail strict KYC, sanctions screening, and source-of-funds due diligence. A viable alternative for independent film licensing collections is to use a locally licensed bank account as the principal treasury account and, if required, connect to international payment Fintechs only as front-end collection rails that immediately settle into the domestic USD bank account. Cross-border EMI accounts may also present risks under Panama's Law 23 beneficial ownership disclosure and CFT (counter-terrorism financing) legal framework; therefore, the use of unlicensed offshore EMI channels should be restricted to non-core, low-value receipts and always routed onward to the primary bank.

Defense evidence: Master service agreements with each platform, consolidated royalty reports and settlement schedules, periodic accounting reconciliation statements, signed declarations from the company's compliance officer, and a maintained client ledger mapping every inbound payment to a specific film title and licensing agreement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection routing for a Panamanian S.A. independent film production and licensing entity should follow the SWIFT wire protocol using MT103 (high-value corporate-to-corporate transfer) or SWIFT MT202 (interbank transfer) from the investing shareholder. The initial share capital contribution must be justified in the corporate bank's KYC file; despite no statutory minimum capital, a capital injection of a commercially credible amount (e.g., US$50,000–US$500,000) is recommended. The sending bank must be a fintech or bank in an OECD-listed or other fully compliant jurisdiction, satisfying the Panamanian bank's correspondent due diligence. Purpose codes should be provided in the wire instructions according to BOP reporting categories (if bank asks) and to the shareholder's outbound bank: equivalent to IMF Balance of Payments codes, typically 'Direct investment – Equity capital injection' (using local code A.1 or USA Fedwire approach code 840-A.35), or 'Additional paid-in capital'. The Panamanian receiving bank will rely on the client's declaration, the corporate deed, and the source-of-funds letters. For capital injections in excess of US$10,000, the bank is required to file a Currency Transaction Report (CTR) or similar transaction report; the beneficial ownership filing with the Fintech Registry is also triggered. The following wire protocol applies: (a) wirefuging mandates: Beneficiary bank receives in USD; no conversion; (b) all instruction fields must match the account mandate exactly, including the Panamanian S.A. full legal name, bank, ABA or SWIFT BIC, account number, and statement purpose; (c) the remitter's name must match the registered shareholder or a duly notified 'third-party remitter' under a written shareholders' resolution; (d) bank will conduct a 24-hour verification hold on high-risk of identity mismatch; (e) an escrow account, if used for capital contributions, should be at the same bank under the same legal entity name and clearly designated 'Capital Escrow Account' in the account title to avoid commingling with operating flows.Outbound disbursements for international film trade and licensing settlements may be executed using the following clearing rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers to pay production house advances, distributor guarantees, legal fees, trademark and copyright registration fees, and royalty remittances to licensors; (b) Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) issued by the Panamanian corporate bank for acquisitions of major film catalogues, when the seller requires documentary credit security; such LC may be payable against presentation of an invoice, certificate of title, bill of lading, or other transport document (if distribution of physical media), and insurance certificates; (c) SWIFT MT101 / RTGS via Banco Nacional de Panamá for high-value domestic settlement; (d) ACH/SEPA rails only when dealing with EU-based counterparties through a European clearing partner, but settlement ultimately must be reflected in the USD account; (e) debit card or online payment rail for low-value platform subscription payments, only if the bank supports such transactions; for all outbound wires exceeding US$10,000, the bank requires a signed payment instruction under dual or single mandate, invoice documentation, and a description of the underlying licensing transaction for record-keeping. For film distribution, outbound trade disbursements should be strictly segregated by acquisition and distribution streams: advance payments against future royalties are typically classified as 'prepaid license fees' and must carry the corresponding invoice; royalty disbursements to sub-distributors must be accompanied by a Royalty Statement and be routed to the same beneficiary account as registered in the KYC file. The use of Commercial LC is highly beneficial for acquiring a film library from a foreign studio: the LC can be denominated in USD, with shipment/documentation dates tied to the delivery of DCP master files, subtitles, trailer, and worldwide distribution rights.FX Forward (outright deliverable) contracted with a Panamanian bank or a licensed international bank counterparty, involving conversion of USD into a non-USD currency (e.g., EUR, GBP) at a specified future date, permissible for hedging confirmed film licensing revenue or acquisition payments denominated in foreign currency.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) may be entered into with offshore banks for emerging-market currencies (e.g., Mexican Peso, Brazilian Real) where local deliverable forward markets are inaccessible to Panamanian non-bank counterparties; settlement is in USD on net differential.Cross-currency swap (USD for EUR/GBP/JPY) available for longer-duration film licensing contracts where both interest rate and FX risk need to be hedged; must be executed under an ISDA Master Agreement with a scale-eligible bank and subject to the bank's credit approval.Plain vanilla FX options (zero-premium collars and European-style call/put options) are permissible to cap adverse currency movement on a guaranteed foreign currency advance, provided the corporate bank offers a treasury derivatives desk and the entity passes suitability/appropriateness testing.Spot FX transactions for immediate settlement may be used for operating liquidity conversions and for payment of foreign subcontractors, with no statutory limitations.Interest rate and exchange-traded currency futures are generally outside Panamanian banking markets and would require onboarding with a US or EU broker; such products are permissible under Panama's territorial legal framework but are not physically available within the local banking clearing rails.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for purely unlicensed offshore EMI digital platforms; MEDIUM for licensed Fintech entities in Panama. Panama has no standalone 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) licensing category; entities that issue digital payment instruments or process payment transactions are regulated under the SBP's payment systems and financial technology regulations. Offshore digital EMIs that are not licensed in Panama may not maintain accounts for Panamanian corporations without breaching the general banking monopoly; inbound wire payments to an unlicensed EMI will be treated as non-banking counterparty risk and may fail strict KYC, sanctions screening, and source-of-funds due diligence. A viable alternative for independent film licensing collections is to use a locally licensed bank account as the principal treasury account and, if required, connect to international payment Fintechs only as front-end collection rails that immediately settle into the domestic USD bank account. Cross-border EMI accounts may also present risks under Panama's Law 23 beneficial ownership disclosure and CFT (counter-terrorism financing) legal framework; therefore, the use of unlicensed offshore EMI channels should be restricted to non-core, low-value receipts and always routed onward to the primary bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 3,000,000 of annual gross revenue triggers mandatory Local File, Master File, and Transfer Pricing Return under Law 98 of 2010 and Executive Decree 28 of 2012; related-party transactions below USD 1,000,000 per counterparty may reduce documentation burden.

Economic substance scope: Law 254 of 2021 Economic Substance Regulations apply because copyright licensing is a relevant activity; the S.A. must evidence a physical Panama office, local board meetings, resident directors, and local accounting records and must file an annual ESR declaration; thin substance creates a presumption of artificiality and triggers CRS/FATCA information exchange.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Tributaria)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to the defrauded amount; 10% late-payment surcharge plus 1.5% monthly interest; asset seizure; prosecution under Articles 848-849 of the Código Fiscal and Law 31 of 2012.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally civilly liable where fraud is proven; travel bans and preventive embargoes on personal assets; imprisonment from 2 to 5 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR fines up to USD 100,000 per year; cancellation of the Aviso de Operación; strike-off from the Public Registry; adverse Global Forum peer review and international blacklisting.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent are jointly fined and disqualified from licensed activity; foreign directors face follow-on reassessments in their home jurisdictions after Panama information exchange.
Money Laundering (Ley 23 de 2015)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to USD 5,000,000; forfeiture of the S.A.'s bank accounts; termination of correspondent banking relationships; placement on international AML risk lists.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face criminal conviction with imprisonment of 5 to 12 years; personal travel bans; confiscation of personal property; permanent disqualification from holding Panamanian directorships.
Withholding Tax Default on Outbound Payments

Statutory sanction: Solidary liability of the S.A. for grossed-up amounts; 10% surcharge and 1.5% monthly interest; disallowance of the underlying expense deduction in the S.A.'s own return.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for withheld taxes diverted to corporate operations; aggravated cases prosecuted as misappropriation (peculado) with prison terms of 3 to 10 years.
