Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), with the DGI Sede Electrónica at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa/ and the SUNTRAIS fiscal-web portal at https://suntrais.mef.gob.pa/

E-government filing portal

For a Panama Sociedad Anónima: (1) execute the public deed of incorporation and record it with the Registro Público de Panamá; (2) file the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) application with the DGI through the Sede Electrónica using the digital notarial certificate, articles of incorporation, appointment of the resident legal representative, and proof of registered address; (3) the issued RUC serves as the ITBMS/VAT identification number. Foreign entities not carrying on business in Panama are generally not required to obtain a Panamanian tax ID for in-country transfers, but if they have a permanent establishment, local agency, or Panama-source taxable activity, they must register for a RUC and obtain a Panama ITBMS certificate through the same electronic channel; appointing a fiscal representative may be required for e-invoicing compliance. For the active PA_CORP exporter, the standard path is RUC and ITBMS registration before the first export shipment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not have a check-the-box equivalent and does not offer domestic hybrid-mismatch entity elections. The Sociedad Anónima is a per se taxable corporation. Administrative or non-classification elections available include the selection of the applicable export-incentive framework if the entity qualifies, election to use DGI-authorized SUNTRAIS electronic invoicing schema, and application for special free-zone treatment in territories such as Panamá Pacífico or Colon Free Zone, none of which change the default territorial entity-level classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic threshold in US Dollar/Panamanian Balboa. Any person engaged, with or without regularity, in the taxable transfer of movable goods or provision of services in Panama must register as an ITBMS taxpayer; exporters may register immediately even if they will only issue zero-rated export invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% ITBMS rate applies to goods physically exported from Panama through a customs office or consigned to a free zone, where the exporter proves the goods left the territory within the statutory period and the sale is not simulated; and to services performed in Panama for the exclusive benefit of a recipient outside Panama or entirely consumed abroad. The exporter must be registered, keep independent export accounting, and hold the export documentation described below.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduanas – DUA)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill) evidencing physical exit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT credit receipt evidencing overseas remittance
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the DUA and e-invoice
  • ITBMS export reconciliation worksheet (formulario de exportación) linking input credits to zero-rated supplies

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) is administered by the DGI through SUNTRAIS. Invoices must be generated in the Cadecos-compatible XML fiscal schema, serialized by the DGI, digitally signed, and transmitted to the DGI in near-real time; SUNTRAIS returns a fiscal authorization/'afectación' that must be embedded in the invoice and QR-bearing PDF representation. API integration or DGI-approved billing system integration is mandatory for all economic operators, including exporters, with real-time reporting of issuance, cancellation, and credit-note flows.

DGI and the Panama Customs Authority (ANA) will cross-check the DUA, transport documents, and remittance evidence. Missing or fraudulent export documents reverse the zero-rating, causing ITBMS at 7% to be assessed on the gross export value, plus monthly statutory interest and late-payment surcharges. False export declarations constitute tax fraud and smuggling, subjecting the exporter to fines from 50% up to 500% of the evaded tax, criminal penalties, seizure of goods, and debarment from fiscal privileges. ITBMS credit refunds previously granted on exported inputs are clawed back with interest, and the assessor may reopen the export cycles under the six-year limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. The Panamanian Balboa (PAB) exists solely as fractional coinage pegged 1:1 to USD and the U.S. dollar circulates freely as legal tender. There are no capital controls, prior exchange-authorization requirements, or profit-repatriation hurdles; dividends, royalties, and loan repayments may be remitted without restriction, subject only to bank-level OFAC/KYC screening and standard anti-money-laundering source-of-funds scrutiny. Panama holds IMF Article VIII status, confirming the absence of exchange restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no mandatory surrender ratio, conversion obligation, repatriation timeline, or retention quota for export receipts. Exporters may retain all proceeds in USD in any domestic or foreign account, and no foreign-exchange surrender declaration is required by the SBP or the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI).

Central bank supervision: Panama has no formally constituted central bank. Banking and financial-system oversight is vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) under Decreto Ley No. 2 de 2008; banknote issuance is effectively delegated to the U.S. Federal Reserve because the U.S. dollar is legal tender (Ley de Moneda, 1904). Banco Nacional de Panamá performs limited fiscal-agency functions but does not exercise monetary authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Panama has no standalone e-money institution (EMI) license under Decreto Ley No. 2 de 2008; the SBP fintech innovation hub and regulatory sandbox accept only locally chartered entities sponsored by a licensed bank. An offshore digital EMI (e.g., UK FCA or EU-licensed) may operate only as an unlicensed technology or software provider and may not hold Panamanian customer funds; the workable structure is an account-sponsorship / Banking-as-a-Service arrangement with a fully licensed Panamanian bank, with the licensed bank retaining custody and AML/CFT responsibility.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: shareholder remits USD via SWIFT MT103 to the beneficiary account (e.g., Banco General or Banistmo) with Field 70 structured as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – SHARE SUBSCRIPTION [DATE]/[RESOLUTION NO.]'. Beneficiary bank compliance requires: (i) certified copy of the capital-increase resolution registered in the Public Registry; (ii) updated share registry (libro de acciones); (iii) RUC and commercial license; (iv) source-of-funds declaration signed by the remitting shareholder; and (v) beneficial-owner identification per SBP CDD rules. The transaction is coded by the receiving bank as 'Inversión Directa' or 'Aporte de Capital'; no exchange license or central-bank prior approval is required under the free-dollar regime.
