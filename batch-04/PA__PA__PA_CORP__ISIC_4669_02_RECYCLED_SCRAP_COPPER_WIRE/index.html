No minimum paid-up capital is required for a Panama Sociedad Anónima. The corporation may be formed with capital declared in the deed and may issue shares without par value. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) – Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); digital e-service portal: Sede Electrónica DGI (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

For a Panama Sociedad Anónima, the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) serves as the sole statutory TIN and VAT ID. To procure it, the company must finalize Public Registry incorporation, maintain a Panama resident agent (registered agent), and file the DGI registration package via Sede Electrónica: certified articles/charter and public deed excerpt, resident-agent consent, notarized identification of directors and legal representative, sworn statement of business address, and declaration of intended economic activity (ISIC 4669). DGI issues a provisional RUC and activates the ITBMS taxpayer file simultaneously. A foreign entity without local incorporation may nevertheless obtain an RUC for withholding or permanent-establishment purposes, but the standard Panama export-trader vehicle is the local S.A.

Default tax classification

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None – every person habitually effecting taxable transfers of movable goods or rendering taxable services in Panama must register for ITBMS; there is no monetary threshold. A simplified invoicing regime exists for small taxpayers with gross annual turnover up to B/.36,000, but such status does not excuse registration for an export-trading business and cannot be used to zero-rate exported scrap metals.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to tangible movable goods, including shredded non-ferrous scrap and recycled copper wire, sold to a non-resident purchaser, definitively exported from Panamanian customs territory, and documented by a valid customs export declaration. The goods must actually depart Panama within the regulatory window and title/risk must pass outside Panama. Ancillary services directly connected to the export (e.g., international freight and logistics) may also be zero-rated where the effective use and benefit take place outside Panama.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación de Mercancías) accepted by the Panama National Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading (or alternative international transport document evidencing physical departure)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the recycled copper wire scrap
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-issued Swift MT103 / payment credit evidence from the foreign buyer

Mandatory electronic invoicing applies to all active RUC taxpayers under the DGI 'Facturación Electrónica' program (Res. 201-0625/2020 and subsequent implementing rules). Invoices must be transmitted to the DGI validation platform in near-real time, receive a unique digital authorization code, and be displayed with a QR code. For exports, the electronic invoice is reconciled against the electronic customs export declaration; no physical fiscal stamp is required.

DGI and Customs may retroactively deny the zero-rating where export substantiation is incomplete, the goods are not shipped out within the statutory period, or payment is not collected within the 180-day evidentiary window. Reclassification triggers ITBMS at 7% on the gross invoice value, statutory interest from the supply date, and an administrative surcharge of up to 100% of the tax per Código Fiscal penalty rules. False, forged, or anticipatory export declarations constitute customs fraud and smuggling, subject to monetary fines, cancellation or retroactive clawback of input VAT credits and prior export-tax benefits, and criminal sanctions under Panamanian customs laws.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Panamanian economy is effectively 100% dollarized and operates under full, unrestricted currency convertibility. There are no capital controls, no current-account or capital-account distinctions, no exchange permits, no import/export FX licensing, and no repatriation restrictions. Profit remittance and dividend distribution do not require central-bank approval; the only practical hurdles are documentary: banks require AML/CFT evidence under Ley 23 de 2015 (FATF-aligned), proof of the beneficial-owner registration at the DGI Beneficial Owner Registry, and compliance with the territorial-source Income Tax rules (ISLR) and the dividend withholding regime under the Código Fiscal. A remitting bank may also insist on a DGI tax-clearance certificate (Paz y Salvo) before executing high-value dividend or capital outflows. Because Panama has no central-bank lender of last resort, USD systemic liquidity depends on the commercial banks' NOSTRO balances held in New York correspondent institutions; nonetheless, capital account convertibility is unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no timeline within which exporters must repatriate foreign currency. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in USD-denominated accounts, whether onshore in Panama or offshore, and may be freely applied to foreign supplier payments or domestic disbursements. The only obligations are documentary and fiscal: the exporter must file export declarations with the Dirección General de Aduanas (DGA – General Directorate of Customs) and register export invoices with the DGI for statistical and income-tax purposes; the collecting bank must obtain underlying trade documentation (commercial invoice, bill of lading, customs declaration) to satisfy UAF record-keeping requirements. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%. Statutory surrender timeline: not applicable.

Central bank supervision: No central bank exists in the Republic of Panama. The national monetary system is fully dollarized, with the US Dollar as de facto legal tender and the Panamanian Balboa as the nominal accounting unit at a fixed 1:1 parity. Bank licensing, prudential supervision, and systemic oversight are vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP – Superintendency of Banks of Panama). Anti-money laundering supervision and financial-intelligence functions are exercised by the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF – Financial Analysis Unit). The Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF – Ministry of Economy and Finance) through its Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI – General Directorate of Revenue) administers tax compliance; the Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the fiscal agent of the State. There is no Foreign-exchange Administration, no exchange-control authority, and no central-bank intervention mechanism.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium for primary treasury rails. Panama has no comprehensive EMI licensing regime that would allow an offshore digital EMI or e-money institution to act as a local deposit-taker or operate an independent Panamanian settlement account. A Panamanian S.A. may establish an account with a reputable foreign EMI (EU/UK-licensed) as a peripheral collection channel, but the SBP-regulated banking system remains the only acceptable core clearing rail for this medium-risk commodity profile. Offline EMIs generally cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, cannot execute UCP 600 negotiation, and are frequently constrained by correspondents refusing to clear commodity-scrap flows. Practical assessment: viable for small pre-payments from familiar buyers, not viable as the primary MT103/Documentary-LC rail; the S.A. should designate a Tier-1 Panamanian bank as the treasury counterparty of record.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection is routed via the US wire-compatible S.W.I.F.T. MT103 from the shareholder's bank through a New York clearing correspondent (JP Morgan, Citibank, Bank of America, or Wells Fargo) to the Panama bank's USD NOSTRO, final-credited to the S.A.'s operational account or an optional Capital Subscription Escrow ledger. Panama does not operate a statutory 'FX purpose-code' system; however, the remittance narrative field must expressly state 'Aporte de capital / Suscripción de acciones – Sociedad Anónima [name], RUC [number]' to permit the receiving bank's compliance team to classify the funds as non-trade equity proceeds. The S.A. must be prepared to submit the board resolution authorizing share issuance, the updated share-registry ledger, DGI Beneficial Owner certificate, RUC certificate, and source-of-funds documentation for capital injections above USD 500,000. Because there are no exchange controls, the injection may be held in USD without conversion, and there is no obligation to channel through Banco Nacional. MT700 issuance/confirmation, MT707 amendments, and MT760 standby support where required. The seller presents the full shipping dossier against the LC: original marine bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of weight, independent laboratory assay for copper content, DGA export declaration, certificate of origin, insurance policy, and radioactive contamination declaration. Upon honored negotiation, the bank credits the exporter by S.W.I.F.T. MT103/MT202 to its operational account. For open-account or cash-against-documents trade, direct S.W.I.F.T. MT103 telegraphic transfer is executed against the same documentary set. USD clearing for all Panama-based settlement occurs through the commercial bank's NOSTRO in the United States via Fedwire/CHIPS; no local FX conversion is required because the USD is the domestic monetary unit. Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Boxes 1-3 (Identification) RUC number, legal name, fiscal closing date and economic activity (ISIC 4669) of the S.A. Provide the S.A.'s Panamanian RUC; no foreign shareholder personal data is required on the return. Late filing: 5% of the tax due or USD 50-5,000 fixed fine (Law 76 of 2019); late payment: 1.5% per month on the unpaid tax, capped at 30%; failure to file after notice can suspend licences and trigger criminal prosecution for persistent non-compliance.
Line 1a - Panama-source gross income Gross sales, consignment commissions and export-related proceeds arising from purchase/resale and delivery of scrap copper wire where negotiation, custody or title transfer occurs in Panama. For the S.A. as taxpayer; direct Panama-source sales by a non-resident are separately taxable and trigger registration obligations. Late filing: 5% of the tax due or USD 50-5,000 fixed fine (Law 76 of 2019); late payment: 1.5% per month on the unpaid tax, capped at 30%; failure to file after notice can suspend licences and trigger criminal prosecution for persistent non-compliance.
Line 1b - Panama-source services Commission income, handling, tolling and demurrage fees invoiced to foreign consignors for services performed in Panamanian port/customs territory. Non-resident contractors receiving such fees from the S.A. are subject to 12.5% final withholding by the S.A. Late filing: 5% of the tax due or USD 50-5,000 fixed fine (Law 76 of 2019); late payment: 1.5% per month on the unpaid tax, capped at 30%; failure to file after notice can suspend licences and trigger criminal prosecution for persistent non-compliance.
Line 2 - Foreign-source / non-taxable income Offshore consignment resale margins on copper scrap that never touches Panamanian soil, with management and control exercised abroad. Must be excluded only if contracts, shipment documents and board minutes prove the extraterritorial sourcing; DGI presumes Panama sourcing when the S.A. negotiates from Panama. Late filing: 5% of the tax due or USD 50-5,000 fixed fine (Law 76 of 2019); late payment: 1.5% per month on the unpaid tax, capped at 30%; failure to file after notice can suspend licences and trigger criminal prosecution for persistent non-compliance.
Lines 3-4 - Costs and deductible expenses Cost of scrap, international logistics, inspection/weight certificates, bank LC/commission charges and documentary export duties apportioned against Panama-source revenue. Support with purchase orders, Bills of Lading and Swift/LC statements; disallowable costs (fines, personal expenses, non-withheld payments to non-residents) are added back. Late filing: 5% of the tax due or USD 50-5,000 fixed fine (Law 76 of 2019); late payment: 1.5% per month on the unpaid tax, capped at 30%; failure to file after notice can suspend licences and trigger criminal prosecution for persistent non-compliance.
Line 5 - Net taxable income Result after statutory add-backs and prior-year NOL deductions (5-year window), before applying the rate schedule. N/A for direct non-resident shareholders; NOLs earned by the S.A. cannot be transferred to shareholders. Late filing: 5% of the tax due or USD 50-5,000 fixed fine (Law 76 of 2019); late payment: 1.5% per month on the unpaid tax, capped at 30%; failure to file after notice can suspend licences and trigger criminal prosecution for persistent non-compliance.
Line 6 - Tax determination and CAIR alternative Apply 15%/20%/25% brackets; compute 1.5% CAIR on gross taxable revenue and pay the higher; subtract advance payments made in June/December of the prior period. Withholding at source does not appear on the S.A.'s line 6; do not offset foreign tax credits against Panama CIT. Late filing: 5% of the tax due or USD 50-5,000 fixed fine (Law 76 of 2019); late payment: 1.5% per month on the unpaid tax, capped at 30%; failure to file after notice can suspend licences and trigger criminal prosecution for persistent non-compliance.
Line 7 - Payments, credits and balance Credit for advance payments, any withholding suffered on Panama-source income, and treaty credits allowed by DTA; balance is due by wire transfer at filing. Only the S.A. claims credits; dividend withholding paid by the S.A. on distributions is a shareholder-level tax, not a credit to the S.A. Late filing: 5% of the tax due or USD 50-5,000 fixed fine (Law 76 of 2019); late payment: 1.5% per month on the unpaid tax, capped at 30%; failure to file after notice can suspend licences and trigger criminal prosecution for persistent non-compliance.
