Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Ley 32 de 1927 (General Law on Corporations), as amended, Arts. 2, 4, 5, 9-12, 49-52; implementing rules: Reglamento General del Registro Público, Acuerdo N° 3 de 2015, Titulo IV (Sociedades Mercantiles).

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Authorized capital must be at least USD 10,000 (Art. 5, Ley 32 de 1927). No mandatory initial paid-up escrow deposit: only one or more shares must be subscribed at formation, with the deed stating the subscription and payment pattern; the balance may be called per the by-laws. Public-offering escrow and treasury rules do not apply to a private wholesale S.A.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certificate issued by the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá (inspection of egress, extinguishing system, electrical room, and stored electronics clearance); municipal structural load clearance for high-density racking and server storage; occupational health and safety work-site approval from MITRADEL (Ministerio de Trabajo y Desarrollo Laboral), including ESD-protective flooring inspection, electrical panel lockout/tagout, and emergency-response evacuation drill records for the Panama City premises.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No national industrial licence is triggered for non-manufacturing wholesale distribution of solid-state drives and server processors; sector registration for reference and verification is available with the Ministerio de Comercio e Industrias (MICI) — Dirección General de Industrias, and municipal Aviso de Operación from the Municipio de Panamá remains mandatory for physical premises.

Category: Aviso de Operación de Actividad Comercial No Industrial (Municipal Business Operation Notice) — no manufacturing or commodity-specific national operating permit is required for ISIC 4651 spot semiconductor trading, provided the company does not engage in local assembly, programming, or hazardous-waste handling.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); operations portal: SIME (Sistema Integrado de Mejoramiento de la Recaudación) at https://sime.mef.gob.pa; official site: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

For the Panamanian S.A.: (1) file the Escritura Pública / incorporation certificate registered at the Registro Público (Sección Mercantil) and the Certificado de Nombramiento de Directores; (2) execute a notarized power of attorney (poder) to a Panamanian resident agent/fiscal representative; (3) register online via SIME to obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes); (4) obtain digital signature (Firma Electrónica) certified by the Autoridad Nacional para la Innovación Gubernamental (AIG) for electronic filings and e-invoicing; (5) request CUFIN invoice authorization before issuing commercial invoices. Non-resident entities without a Panamanian establishment appoint a fiscal representative of repute to open the RUC file and must provide identity documents (passport, apostilled incorporation for foreign companies, foreign VAT/tax ID if any).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation plus Territorial_Scope: the Sociedad Anónima is a juridical person taxed at entity level under a territorial tax base (Panama-source income only; foreign-source income is outside the taxable base regardless of remittance); no pass-through treatment for S.A. shareholders; dividends and remittances of foreign-source earnings to non-resident shareholders are not subject to Panamanian withholding; the entity may qualify as a 'contribuyente especial' (large taxpayer) for DGI monitoring; the Zona Libre de Colón / SEM regimes are available as tax-exempt-zone alternatives only if the trading operations are physically relocated into a qualifying free zone or corporate-services regime.

Available election forms

Panama has no US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch partnership election; the corporate income tax 'Alternativa' under Código Fiscal Art. 699-A allows a large taxpayer (gross income > B/.1,500,000) to elect, annually, the greater of 25% of net taxable income (territorial base) or 2% of gross taxable income, with 0% effective rate on foreign-source income; ITBMS accrual vs. cash-basis (causación) election is available for small taxpayers and for those under the simplified ITBMS regime; the Multinational Headquarters (SEM, Law 41 of 2007) election grants income-tax and ITBMS incentives after committing minimum local investment of B/.200,000 and expanding the scope to regional services; the 'CINEM' or free-zone (Zona Libre de Colón) concession election is available if the warehouse and logistics function is moved into the free zone.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 (Panamanian balboa pegged 1:1 to USD) of annual gross turnover in taxable transfers/services; registration is compulsory from the first day of operations for taxpayers whose projected twelve-month revenue exceeds the threshold and for all importers of record; taxpayers supplying solely zero-rated exports may nevertheless register voluntarily for ITBMS input-credit refunds; non-resident digital service suppliers have an equivalent registration obligation through the SIME portal.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS 0% rate applies to (i) tangible goods (SSD drives and server processors) physically exported from Panamanian customs territory under an export declaration filed by a licensed customs broker, and (ii) services rendered to non-residents where the benefit is realized outside Panama and the services are not connected with Panama-located immovable property or goods; the sale must be made by an ITBMS-registered Panamanian S.A.; in the current back-to-back spot-trading model where goods move directly between third countries without entering Panama, the transaction is foreign-source income outside the ITBMS tax base rather than zero-rated, and the correct formal record is an offshore income schedule with supporting contracts and transport documents; where a genuine Panamanian export occurs, exporter must retain full documentary chain to preserve refund of input ITBMS credits.

  • Documento Único de Aduana (DUA) – electronic export declaration processed through the SENAVIC / Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) customs platform (SICA), signed by a licensed Panamanian customs broker (agente aduanero)
  • Bill of Lading (marine B/L) or Airway Bill (AWB) evidencing shipment from a Panamanian port/airport with the Panamanian S.A. named as shipper/exporter and the foreign buyer as consignee
  • Commercial export invoice in the DGI e-invoice format bearing the CUFIN and QR code, describing the SSD/CPU chips and stating ITBMS 0%
  • Packing list and any applicable certificate of origin or electronic export document issued by the customs authority
  • Inward remittance certificate / bank statement (comprobante bancario de ingreso de divisas) showing the export proceeds credited to the Panamanian S.A.'s USD bank account within the repatriation deadline
  • Contracts, purchase orders, and broker confirmations evidencing open-market arm's length pricing for the spot semiconductor trade

Electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) is compulsory for taxpayers in phase-in groups, including large taxpayers and all newly incorporated commercial companies with active RUC; invoices must be pre-authorized by the DGI through the issuance of a CUFIN (Código Único de Facturación) in the SIME portal or certified invoicing software; each invoice XML with the CUFIN and a digital signature (Firma Electrónica) must be transmitted to the DGI platform within the regulatory timeframe (no later than 24 hours after issue for online generation mode); failure to transmit results in the invoice being deemed unauthorized; exports must embed CUFIN + QR code in the paper/electronic export invoice; batch authorization (bloque) is available for high-volume authorized printers.

DGI audits zero-rated exporters using data-matching against customs export records and bank remittance systems; statute of limitations is generally 5 years, extended where returns were not filed or fraud is alleged; exporters must keep supporting documents for 7 years (10 years for special regimes). False zero-rating claims trigger: reassessment of ITBMS at 7% on the invoice value, disallowance and clawback of input ITBMS credits claimed via refunds, late-payment surcharge of 10% per month or fraction of month (cap at 100% of the tax), and fines from B/.500 to B/.50,000 plus personal liability of directors; criminal tax fraud penalties (prisión) apply under Código Fiscal Art. 760 and Código Penal when documentary fraud or customs false declarations are proven; customs penalties up to 3x the value of the merchandise and delisting of the customs broker may also be imposed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility. Panama is a fully dollarized jurisdiction: it imposes no exchange controls, no restrictions on the conversion of US dollars, and no limitations on profit repatriation, dividend distribution, loan repayment or capital injection for corporate entities. The Panamanian balboa serves only as fractional coinage. Corporate treasury can freely hold, transfer and redeem USD/other currencies through regulated banks, subject solely to AML/KYC, sanctions, and beneficial ownership filing requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama does not mandate surrender, conversion, or repatriation of export proceeds within any timeline. Receipts from wholesale sales of IT hardware and semiconductor components may be retained in the bank account of the Panamanian corporate entity or used offshore without a statutory conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank and does not issue paper currency. The official monetary authority for banking regulation is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Superintendency of Banks of Panama), while the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) oversees AML/CFT enforcement and the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) coordinates economic policy. The US Dollar is legal tender and the Balboa (PAB) circulates solely as a sub-unit at parity with USD.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Panama does not yet maintain a comprehensive stand-alone electronic money institution (EMI) regime comparable to the EU or UK. Offshore digital banks and EMI platforms that are not licensed or recognized by the Superintendency of Banks of Panama can only be used on a cautionary basis; they are considered high-risk third-party payment service providers. For the SSD/CPU wholesale trade, a licensed Panamanian commercial bank account remains the legally safest operational and escrow rail. Foreign fintech EMIs may be used as supplementary receivables vehicles only if they facilitate settlement in USD/PA, are covered by the bank's counterparty due diligence, and the account-holding company maintains auditable records.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions are routed via SWIFT GPI MT103 transfers from the shareholder's bank to the Panamanian corporate account using the SWIFT sender reference and payment narrative 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / PA CORP'. The remitter obtains a purpose code consistent with the payment system in use (most commercial banks use 'G200 Investment of non-resident abroad', or its local equivalent) and attaches corporate documents: board resolution, capital subscription agreement, certificate of share issuance, and passport copy of the beneficial owner, all notarized/apostilled. The receiving bank verifies the source of funds and records the inflow as subscribed paid-in capital in the corporate records.Outbound settlement for SSD/CPU purchases uses (i) direct SWIFT MT103 transfers to suppliers, displaying invoice references and incoterms; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit (LC) under UCP600 with deferred or sight payment terms, issued by the buyer's US-correspondent bank or a Panamanian bank, guaranteeing payment against shipping documents; and (iii) escrow inspection wires under an escrow agreement, releasing only after freight-forwarder inspection and proof-of-delivery documentation. For high-value orders, a standby LC can secure performance while the main payment runs as MT103. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 5,000,000 gross annual intercompany transactions. Mandatory Local File (Informe Local) and Master File (Informe Maestro) filing with DGI when threshold is met.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Act (Law 254 of 2022, as amended; Decree 946) applies to 'distribution and service centers' and 'sale of goods to related entities'. The SSD/CPU wholesale S.A. is within scope if it sells goods to related enterprises. Requirements: (1) physical office in Panama; (2) adequate staff performing core income-generating activities; (3) local management and accounting; (4) board meetings in Panama at least once a year; (5) annual ESR filing via Form 951/ESR Report. Non-compliance: administrative fines up to USD 1,000,000 and potential RUC/registration cancellation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: 20–50% of omitted tax plus monthly interest; criminal referral under the Tax Procedure Code; imprisonment for 2–6 years; RUC suspension and closure of business premises.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and resident agents signing false returns are jointly liable for deficiency, civil restitution, fines, travel bans, and imprisonment; court-ordered corporate veil piercing where company is a mere alter ego.
Shell Company / Insufficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR fines up to USD 1,000,000; loss of treaty benefits; retroactive revocation of territorial tax privileges; strike-off from the Public Registry; disqualification of the resident agent.

Director personal liability: Personal administrative fines (USD 10,000–100,000) for certifying false ESR reports; civil liability for unpaid taxes; criminal prosecution for fraud where deliberate misrepresentation is proven; travel bans and asset attachments.
AML Breaches (Notario de Lavado de Activos / Cártel de Divisas)

Statutory sanction: Fines per Law 23 of 2015: USD 10,000 to USD 5,000,000; asset freezing; criminal money laundering penalties of 5–12 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors, MLRO/compliance officer, and shareholders are personally liable for AML fines up to USD 250,000 each; imprisonment up to 12 years; travel bans; extradition; veil piercing where the S.A. functions as an instrumentality for criminal profit sheltering.
Transfer Pricing Violations (Ajustes por Precios de Transferencia)

Statutory sanction: DGI re-assessment of income to arm's length margin; underpayment surcharge: 30–50% of additional tax; TP documentation failure fine: 1% of related-party transactions, capped at USD 100,000; plus monthly interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve transactions without reasonable economic basis or sign false TP reports may be jointly and severally liable; civil restitution; criminal liability for falsification of documents; measure of corporate veil penetration where upstream shareholders direct the pricing scheme.
Unlawful Use of Nominees / Undisclosed Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Fines under Law 70 of 2019 up to 50,000 USD for each infraction; cancellation of registration; prohibition to operate; criminal liability up to 5 years for false statements.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and the official resident agent are liable for unauthorized acts; criminal penalties for false declarations; civil liability for all unpaid taxes and damages; travel bans and restrictions on leaving Panama are possible within ongoing criminal investigation.
