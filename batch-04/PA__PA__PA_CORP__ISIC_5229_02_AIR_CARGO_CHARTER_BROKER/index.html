Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office address is permissible as the statutory registered office during corporate formation, because Panama only requires a local registered agent address for the public registry. However, for an operating air cargo charter broker, the Civil Aviation Authority (Autoridad Aeronáutica Civil) and the General Directorate of Revenue will expect a physical place of business, professional staff, and management presence in Panama; a pure virtual office cannot be the economic substance and management-control seat for licensing or tax purposes.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá – Dirección General de Personas Jurídicas (Public Registry of Panama, Legal Entities Department), Corregimiento de Ancón, Panama City.

Governing statute

Panama General Corporation Law: Law No. 32 of 1927, as amended by Law 5 of 2018 and related Executive Decrees; codified in Book II of the Panama Commercial Code. Core corporate provisions include Articles 1 through 106 governing formation, capital, shares, directors, and resident agent requirements; additional registration procedures are governed by the Registry Regulation (Acuerdo No. 7 of 19 July 2019 as amended).

Formation / constitutional filings

Escritura de Constitución (Deed of Incorporation) / Pacto Social, embodied in a public instrument issued by a Panamanian Notary and submitted as Testimonio for inscription in the Public Registry.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 47 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow deposit requirement. The Articles may set a nominal authorized capital, but at least one share must be issued and fully paid-in, in any lawful currency, prior to inscription; US$1.00 or a nominal par value of B/.1.00 is acceptable. No capital verification or bank block certificate is required at formation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas — Official portal: e-Tax (https://etax.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first file an Aviso de Operación with the competent Municipalidad, attach notarized constitutional documents and proof of a resident legal representative, and then register with the DGI through the e-Tax portal to obtain the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC). Non-resident entities without a permanent establishment may register for limited ITBMS and income tax obligations and must appoint a local taxpayer agent for service of process.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign entity must first file an Aviso de Operación with the competent Municipalidad, attach notarized constitutional documents and proof of a resident legal representative, and then register with the DGI through the e-Tax portal to obtain the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC). Non-resident entities without a permanent establishment may register for limited ITBMS and income tax obligations and must appoint a local taxpayer agent for service of process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not have U.S.-style check-the-box elections or a hybrid mismatch election regime. A Sociedad Anónima is treated as an entity-level taxpayer under the Código Fiscal. Elections available include consolidated group taxation for affiliated companies (Article 708 Código Fiscal), the special Sociedad Holding regime for foreign-source dividend and capital gains exemption, and tax stability agreements with the DGI for qualifying operations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold for ITBMS registration; any person carrying out taxable activities in Panama must obtain an RUC. A simplified small-taxpayer regime applies to annual gross turnover up to B/.36,000 (approximately USD 36,000), but registration with DGI remains mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (i) tangible goods physically exported from Panama; and (ii) services supplied to non-residents and entirely used or enjoyed outside Panama, including international air cargo charter and freight forwarding services. The taxpayer must demonstrate with documentary evidence that the service benefit is consummated abroad and that the counterparty is a foreign non-resident. Incidental charges for cross-border transportation are zero-rated when the shipment is outbound and evidenced by an export declaration.

  • Declaración Única de Aduana (DUDA) / Customs Export Declaration
  • Air Waybill (AWB) or Bill of Lading (B/L)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT receipt proving payment from an offshore bank account
  • Service contract or charter agreement showing the foreign recipient and place of performance (for export services)

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing through the DGI-approved Facturación Electrónica platform. Invoices must be transmitted in XML UBL 2.2 signed with a digital certificate. The DGI immediately validates each invoice and returns a fiscal acceptance seal/code ('Sello de Aceptación' or 'Código de Aceptación'). Ragistering taxpayers must integrate via REST/JSON APIs; physical fiscal stamping is replaced by DGI digital stamping and QR-code validation.

The DGI may retroactively disallow zero-rating and assess ITBMS at 7% plus monthly late interest of 1% per month and penalties up to 300% of the omitted tax. Fraudulent claims, false customs declarations, sham offshore service arrangements, or unrepatriated export proceeds may additionally trigger criminal sanctions under the Código Fiscal, cancellation of the RUC, and disqualification from future export zero-rating for up to two years. The ordinary limitation period is five years, extending to ten years for unreported or fraudulent transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility in both current and capital accounts. Panama imposes no exchange controls, no currency board restrictions, no mandatory surrender, and no prior central bank authorization for inbound or outbound USD transfers. The operational constraints are exclusively prudential: SBP-regulated KYC/AML documentation, UAF (Unidad de Análisis Financiero) suspicious transaction reporting, OFAC screening, and FATF-compliance expectations following Panama's removal from the grey list in October 2023. Dividends and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders of a Panama S.A. are free of source tax when derived from foreign-source income, and transfer of USD abroad through licensed banks is unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender regime, no statutory repatriation deadline, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies to Panama-incorporated cargo charter brokers. All revenues denominated in USD are held freely in USD operational accounts. Banks nevertheless require the underlying commercial documentation for each inbound MT103 under SBP KYC Regulation 8-B-2015, including the invoice, air waybill or charter party, and the exporter service code declaration for statistical reporting; none of these trigger conversion or retention obligations.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank. Monetary and regulatory authority is split between the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), which supervises all licensed banks and enforces AML/CFT rules, and Banco Nacional de Panamá, which acts as fiscal agent for the State. The Panamanian Balboa is not issued by any bank; US Federal Reserve Notes are legal tender for all transactions pursuant to Ley 84 de 1904 and the Codigo Fiscal, making the system fully dollarized.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low for a Panama S.A. in the MSC 4 air cargo charter sector. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Wise Business) may onboard a Panama S.A. provided digital KYC, apostilled registry, and beneficial ownership declarations are submitted, but they are not licensed by the SBP and carry enhanced counterparty risk with domestic banks. Local Tier-1 banks will generally treat EMI-originated funds as high-risk layering channels, require redundant source-of-funds evidence, and may reject returns from the same EMI. EMI rails are thus viable only for auxiliary operational balances below USD 50,000 per month; the primary treasury architecture must run through SBP-licensed banks and their US correspondent accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injection shall be remitted by the shareholder via SWIFT MT103 to the Panama S.A.'s USD account at an SBP-licensed bank, with field 70 coded 'CNA — Capital Subscription' or 'Loans to Shareholder' as applicable; the bank's New York correspondent ABA (e.g., Bank of New York Mellon or Citibank) credits the Panama account. Required pre-validation documents are: certified copy of the incorporated S.A. from the Registro Público, board resolution approving the account and capital increase, utility-bill proof of address for the shareholder, and notarized ultimate-beneficial-owner declaration. No FX approval is required because the receiving jurisdiction is the US dollar. Commercial inbound payments for charter brokerage commissions must be identified as 'Miscellaneous Commercial Payments' with the broker's invoice, itinerary, and air waybill attached, and must comply with the UAF's transactional reporting thresholds.Outbound settlements from Panama will be cleared over SWIFT MT103 for international remittances (aircraft owner hire, sub-charter operators, European fuel suppliers) and over Fedwire/CHIPS via the bank's US correspondents for US counterparties and FBO fuel payments. Letters of credit for charter performance deposits or aircraft lease collateral are opened through the bank's trade desk using SWIFT MT700 with USD availability confirmed by the Panamanian issuer's US correspondent; MT202 COV is used for interbank reimbursements above USD 500,000. All charter disbursements require a signed charter party, a commercial invoice, and a security-cleared counterparty list; records must be retained for five years under SBP regulation.
