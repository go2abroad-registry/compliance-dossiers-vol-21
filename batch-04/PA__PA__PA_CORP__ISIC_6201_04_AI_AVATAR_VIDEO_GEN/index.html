Applicable AML statute: Panama's AML/CFT statutory framework comprises: (i) Ley No. 23 de 27 de abril de 2015, as amended, which adopts preventive measures against money laundering and terrorism financing, creates the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF), imposes customer due diligence, record-keeping and suspicious-transaction reporting on obliged parties including lawyers acting as resident agents and trust providers, and regulates politically exposed persons (PEPs); (ii) Ley No. 129 de 30 de octubre de 2020 and implementing Decreto Ejecutivo No. 125 de 19 de agosto de 2021, which establish the mandatory Beneficiario Final (UBO) register filed electronically with the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) for every Panamanian legal entity; and (iii) FATF/GAFILAT Recommendations implemented through UAF resolutions and sectoral AML rules of the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) and the Superintendencia del Mercado de Valores (SMV).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; taxpayer web portal available at https://www.dgi.gob.pa/; electronic services accessible via the DGI virtual office 'e-Tax'.

E-government filing portal

A Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) must obtain a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) from the DGI before commencing taxable activities. The procedure for a foreign-owned entity is: (i) incorporate the S.A. via a Panamanian registered agent and register the corporation with the Sección Mercantil of the Registro Público de Panamá; (ii) file the Aviso de Operación (business operation notice) through the DGI e-Tax platform, providing proof of registered agent, corporate existence, and a Panamanian fiscal address; (iii) appoint a local legal representative or resident agent for tax domicile purposes if the directors and shareholders are non-resident; (iv) receive the provisional RUC certificate; (v) register for ITBMS as an 'agente retenedor' if applicable; and (vi) complete any complementary DGI inspection or documentary verification to obtain a definitive RUC. The RUC must be quoted on all invoices, contracts, customs declarations, and tax returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama operates a territorial tax system; no general check-the-box or pass-through election exists for S.A. corporations. A Panamanian entity is taxed at corporate level only on Panama-source income, while foreign-source income may be excluded by election under Article 760 of the Código Fiscal, subject to substantiation of foreign source and payment. No hybrid mismatch election is generally available. Specialized regimes permitting elective total or partial tax exemption include Law 41 of 2004 (Panama Pacifico Special Economic Zone) and Law 25 of 2010 (Headquarters Company regime), which may be applicable to qualifying AIGC exporters; these are separate administrative elections, not check-the-box classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold generally exempts a business from ITBMS registration; any entrepreneur, corporation, or individual undertaking taxable operations in Panama (including digital services with local consumption nexus) must register with the DGI and obtain ITBMS tax identification from the first taxable event. However, small taxpayers with annual gross income not exceeding approximately USD 36,000 may elect the Régimen Simplificado de Pequeños Contribuyentes, replacing the full ITBMS with a simplified quota or 3% tax on gross receipts where applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of tangible goods qualifies for a 0% ITBMS rate if the goods physically leave Panamanian customs territory and are proven exported. Export of services, including AI video generation and software-as-a-service through cloud platforms, qualifies for 0% ITBMS if (i) the recipient is a non-resident entity or individual physically located outside Panama, (ii) the service is performed or generated for use completely outside Panama, (iii) the economic benefit occurs abroad, and (iv) the invoice is issued with the zero-rate code and accompanied by documentary evidence of the offshore nature of the transaction. Panama sources services according to the place of consumption or economic use; offshore AIGC video generation for foreign clients may be zero-rated, but services downloaded or accessed by Panamanian customers are subject to standard 7% ITBMS.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SISPA electronic filing acceptance or PAN/PEDL for goods)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (for physical exports)
  • Commercial Invoice and export price list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (from Panamanian bank) or SWIFT/transaction receipt proving foreign payment for services
  • Executed service contract specifying offshore recipient and place of performance

Panama has a mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) framework for all taxpayers. Taxpayers must issue electronic invoices using the DGI web portal or an authorized EDI (Electronic Data Interchange) software provider, and each invoice must include a digital signature (Firma Electrónica), a QR code with verification link, the seller's RUC, the ITBMS rate applicable, and a sequential number. Real-time fiscal clearance is not yet universally implemented; taxpayers transmit invoice batches to the DGI on a periodic basis (typically within 24 hours to a few days depending on taxpayer category). Some goods subject to excise taxes may require digital stamps (pólizas fiscales) or DGI pre-approval before physical movement.

The DGI audits export zero-rating claims retrospectively, especially for service exports and software transactions. If documentary substantiation is incomplete or the transaction is recharacterized as domestic consumption, the DGI claws back the zero-rated ITBMS input credits, assesses the 7% ITBMS with interest (legal rate), and imposes statutory penalties under the Código Fiscal (Articles 730-762). Fraudulent or benchmarked claims attract penalties ranging from 100% to 300% of the evaded tax, plus possible criminal tax sanctions and suspension of the RUC. The ordinary statute of limitations for assessment is 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud, false accounting, or non-declaration. Any zero-rating benefit improperly retained must be repaid in full before issuance of a no-due certificate, and the involvement of a registered agent does not shield the corporation from clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Currency convertibility is unrestricted because Panama is a fully dollarized jurisdiction and uses the U.S. dollar as domestic legal tender. Both capital account and current account transactions are freely convertible and no exchange control authorization is required for the import or export of funds. For an AI video-generation SaaS company (ISIC 6201), profit remittance and dividend distributions are treated as Panama-source income only to the extent derived from local activities under the territorial tax model; non-Panama-source revenue is outside the territorial tax base, and remittances of dividends from Panama-source profits are subject to the 5% (or 10% for certain unlicensed entities) dividend tax through tax withholding, but not to FX conversion or exchange-license hurdles. The practical impediments are compliance-driven: OFAC sanctions screening, FATCA, CRS, and SBP-mandated AML/KYC due diligence on beneficial owners and counterparties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation in Panama to surrender export proceeds to the government, deposit them in a specified bank, or convert them into local currency as Panamanian law does not maintain a central bank or an exchange-control regime. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 0%; export receipts may be retained in USD, freely remitted, reinvested, or disbursed without any timeline restriction. Receipts from the export of AI software services, subscription licenses, and virtual avatar video generation are not subject to invoicing, surrender, or repurchase requirements, although the exporter must retain standard commercial documentation (contracts, invoices, and bank SWIFT records) for tax and AML compliance purposes.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no statutory central bank or monetary authority. Banking supervision and AML/CFT regulation are vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) manages fiscal policy and public debt. The U.S. dollar is fully legal tender under Ley 24 de 1920 (reformed by Decreto Ley 9 de 1992), and the Republic of Panamá maintains no independent monetary issuance or foreign exchange reserve authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for use of offshore digital payment institutions (e-money institutions / EMI-style processor banks) as treasury rails, but with important legal constraints. Panama does not maintain a dedicated EMI or electronic-money license under SBP law; offshore EMIs cannot independently operate a local settlement account unless they are licensed as an international banking licensee or hold an SBP-authorized payment-provider arrangement with a local tier-1 bank. For an AI video SaaS platform, a practical structure is to use a licensed Panama commercial bank as the primary USD clearing account and contract with a supervised digital payment provider (e.g., Stripe, Paddle, or Luxembourg/UK EMIs) solely for customer card-acquisition, with daily or weekly sweeps into the Panama bank account. Legal blockers include SBP AML rules under Ley 23 de 2015, the prohibition on unlicensed deposit-taking from the public, and the requirement that the final treasury settlement account reside with a licensed bank in Panama or an acceptable OFAC-compliant jurisdiction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed as a standard international wire transfer in U.S. dollars through the global SWIFT system; no central-bank declaration or exchange-control permit is required. Appropriate MT103 field usage includes ordering customer '50K' as the positive payee company S.A., field 52 for the U.S.-dollar sender bank, and field 56/57 for the Panama beneficiary bank. The economic purpose is identified as 'Equity Contribution' or 'Capital Investment – Subscription of Shares' in the wire instruction and concurrently filed with the local bank as part of the corporate KYC file. Beneficiary bank verification requires receipt of the Panama Public Registry certificate, board resolution authorising account opening, director and UBO identification, and the source-of-funds statement from the sending investor. Compliance screening for OFAC, FATCA, and CRS must be completed, but there is no Panamanian approval timeline beyond the standard 24-72 hour bank settlement window.For outbound disbursements, Panama S.A. treasury functions use SWIFT MT103 in USD for recurring trade settlements, SaaS subscription payouts, and contractor payments. For high-value deliverables or enterprise sales of avatar video generation services, the standard commercial clearing rail is a documentary letter of credit opened at a Panama tier-1 bank and transmitted via SWIFT MT700/MT799 to the beneficiary's confirming bank, with invoice, bill of lading where applicable, or customer acceptance certificate. SE-credit payments are generally not used because Panama has no local clearing code except for the national low-value automated clearing house (ACH) operated by Banco Nacional de Panamá, which is reserved for domestic-only payments. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB 250,000 (approximately USD 250,000) in aggregate related-party transactions per fiscal year triggers mandatory Local File preparation and filing of the DGI transfer pricing annex; Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated annual turnover exceeding USD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Panama Economic Substance Regulations (Law 254 of 2019) apply to corporations engaged in relevant activities, including AI software licensing, service centers, distribution, and IP exploitation. The AI avatar video generation S.A. must demonstrate economic substance in Panama: physical office, employees with decision-making capacity, board meetings held in Panama, and local accounting and operational records. If the entity merely licenses foreign-developed AI code and collects subscription revenue without local substance, the DGI may recharacterize it as a shell entity and subject it to 25% tax on deemed Panama-source income, fines, and potential nullification of its tax-exempt status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Fine equal to 25% of the tax evaded plus the unpaid tax, accrued interest at 10% per annum, and criminal penalties of 2 to 5 years imprisonment and fines up to three times the evaded amount; DGI may also freeze bank accounts and reject subsequent filings.

Director personal liability: Panama courts may pierce the corporate veil for fraud or evasion, imposing civil restitution on directors, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment for the officers who signed false returns.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Law 254 of 2019, fines of up to USD 50,000 per year per relevant activity, plus cancellation of the commercial license and reclassification of the entity's income as taxable Panama-source income at 25%; the DGI may further impose monthly defaults of USD 5,000 for missing substance records.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent are jointly and severally liable for penalties and can be disqualified from acting as officers; the tax and commercial authorities may impose travel bans and personal fines on directors who knowingly operated the shell.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines of USD 100,000 to USD 1,000,000 per violation, mandatory reporting to the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF), freezing of company bank accounts, and potential revocation of the corporate charter for serious breaches.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face criminal prosecution under Panama's AML statute, with imprisonment of 2 to 10 years, personal civil fines, and travel restrictions; bankruptcy/disqualification orders are common in serious AML violations.
Transfer Pricing & Cross-Border Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Mandatory transfer pricing adjustment to arm's-length conditions, additional tax assessed at 25% plus underpayment interest at 10% per annum, documentation penalties of USD 5,000 to USD 10,000 per unfiled requirement, and an additional penalty of up to 25% on the understatement.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute related-party agreements or sign the transfer pricing annex are exposed to personal liability for deliberate misstatements; criminal fraud sanctions and personal fines may be imposed when the TP arrangement is an orchestrated tax evasion scheme.
