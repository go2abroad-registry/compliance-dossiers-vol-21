Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas, Panama; official electronic portal: E-Tax 2.0 at https://etax.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first formalize its Panama Sociedad Anónima before the Registro Público through a resident registered agent, then physically/logically apply to the DGI for a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) using the E-Tax 2.0 portal. The application requires apostilled articles of incorporation, the registered business license, identity documents of directors and officers, proof of the registered agent appointment, and a physical Panama registered office address. The RUC serves as the unified tax identification number for income tax (ISR), ITBMS, and customs obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch elections exist under Panamanian law. A qualifying small taxpayer may elect the Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente (simplified taxation) if annual gross turnover does not exceed the statutory threshold. Standard Sociedades Anónimas may otherwise elect to be governed by the standard territorial income tax regime and ITBMS monthly/quarterly filing rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general statutory turnover threshold for ITBMS registration; all persons engaged in taxable transfers of movable goods or provision of services in Panama must obtain a RUC. A simplified small-taxpayer regime is available for annual gross receipts up to B/. 200,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods from Panama customs territory to a non-resident buyer are zero-rated at 0% ITBMS, provided the goods physically leave Panama and the export declaration is filed and validated by Panama Customs. Services supplied to a non-resident for use wholly outside Panama, international transport services, and supplies of returned goods moving to/from bonded free zones may also qualify for zero-rating. Input ITBMS attributable to zero-rated export transactions may be claimed as a credit or refundable tax balance (saldo a favor).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) validated through Panama Customs / SICE electronic platform
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the returned merchandise from Panama customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or banking evidence of receipt of export proceeds from abroad

Panama mandates fiscal electronic invoicing for active ITBMS taxpayers. Invoices, credit notes, and debit notes must be transmitted in real time to the DGI through the E-Tax 2.0 portal or an authorized electronic billing provider (PAFE), with DGI validation generating a unique digital clearing key/clave de acceso. Certified software providers must integrate via DGI-approved APIs; each document receives an electronic stamp/sello digital before issuance to the counterparty.

The DGI and Panama Customs (ANA) may audit zero-rated export claims within the five-year statute of limitations. Unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating triggers immediate clawback of the VAT benefit, collection of unpaid ITBMS at 10%, plus monthly interest and surcharge penalties, and administrative fines of 100% to 200% of the omitted tax. Falsified export documentation or fictitious export declarations carry criminal liability under Panama's Penal Code, disqualification from simplified regimes, and revocation of the exporter's RUC.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized: the U.S. dollar is legal tender and the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) exists only as a nominal unit with USD parity, minted purely as coinage. There are no capital account or current account controls, no exchange approval authorities, no FX quotas, and no restrictions on the conversion of USD into any foreign currency. Profit remittance abroad by a Sociedad Anónima is unrestricted under Decree Law No. 9 of 1998. The only friction is tax-driven: dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders attract a 10% withholding tax and interest paid offshore to non-bank lenders is subject to a 10% withholding, exigible by the bank/Panama payer under the territorial-tax regime. No residual currency-convertibility hurdle exists for reverse-logistics trade settlements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Panama imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory repatriation deadline, and no conversion ratio for export receipts or cross-border service income. Invoices relating to return-merchandise logistics and quality-grading services can be collected directly in USD into any Panamanian or foreign bank account without a central bank filing, customs certification, or balance-of-payments declaration. The absence of such rules means the reverse-logistics treasury model can net receipts and refunds in USD with full freedom; AML record-keeping obligations under Law 23 of 2015 still require support for the underlying transaction.

Central bank supervision: No traditional central bank exists in Panama. Banking supervision is vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) under Decree Law No. 9 of 1998 (Código Bancario); the Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent of the Republic and operator of the domestic clearing house; the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) manages fiscal policy. Monetary and FX policy are de facto conducted by the U.S. Federal Reserve given full dollarization.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High with legal constraints. Panama has no standalone electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime; Law 70 of 2019 established a finite fintech regulatory sandbox overseen by the SBP, under which a fintech may obtain a temporary certificate to test payment services, but deposit-taking remains the exclusive province of licensed banks. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Wise Business, Currencycloud, Visa Direct capabilities) are lawful as unregulated agents only if they do not book deposits in Panama; the Panamanian S.A. may lawfully open a pooled-virtually-held USD account with such an EMI, route goods-refunds and merchant settlement flows, and initiate cross-border disbursements, since the EMI's underlying settlement runs through the EMI's own licensed bank (the EMI is acting as payment-initiator and agent). The main constraints are: (a) EMI funds are exposed to counterparty and uninsured-offshore risk; (b) the EMI cannot provide local Panamanian clearing (per-local Deposito ACH) for domestic payroll or vendor disbursement; (c) SBP KYC expectations require that the Panamanian company provide source-of-wealth documentation on the funds in the EMI account as if it were a local account. Recommended treasury architecture: a local US$ operational account at an SBP-regulated Tier-1 bank for in-country liquidity and escrow/compliance credibility, supplemented by an EMI rail solely for cross-border parcel-reversal refunds and low-ticket e-commerce payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire-transfer protocol: the non-resident shareholder instructs its international bank to issue an SWIFT MT103 (Customer Transfer) payable to the Panama S.A.'s U.S.-dollar account at the local bank; the remittance information field (Field 70) must carry the official purpose narrative 'Capital contribution under Board Resolution dated [date]; deemed-up transfer pursuant to the company register subscription agreement'. The Panama entity then files the internal share-register entry and marks the bank credit as maximum equity. For large tranches, the verification dossier is a: certified copy of the Board Resolution authorizing the share issuance; a subscription certificate with outstanding shares; the escrow agreement between the Panama bank and the shareholder (if a capital-escrow account is used); and proof of the foreign bank's KYC basic identification. Alternative routing through a U.S. money-center correspondent (e.g., via CHIPS) is equally compliant; no Panamanian investment registration or central-bank authorization is required. If transactions involve passenger/exchange or cash-receipt risk, a bank reference letter from the issuer-outside bank must accompany first-time wires.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103/MT103+ for supplier payments and reverse-logistics vendor invoices in USD; settlement occurs over the U.S. Fedwire/CHIPS rails with Panamanian bank correspondents; (2) local vendor payroll and ACH in-country disbursement run through the Panama Interbank Clearing System operated under the Banco Nacional de Panamá (same-day ACH for high-frequency refunds); (3) for documented trade-finance operations involving returned-goods liquidation among a Panamanian S.A. and a foreign buyer, an Irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the Panamanian Tier-1 bank via SWIFT MT700 (with an advising bank in the exporter's jurisdiction) is the recommended instrument; (4) export collections under documentary sight or term drafts via MT400/MT410 from the negotiating bank, suitable when the customer accepts the goods after quality-grading; (5) cross-border e-commerce chargeback refunds can be batched through the Fintech-EMI aggregator rail (Airwallex) to minimize per-transaction SWIFT fees. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends distributed to shareholders; payable via the Form 745 (annual) or the 'Dividendos' form whenever a distribution is made. No additional tax applies to non-resident shareholders on Panamanian-source dividends.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months after the fiscal year-end, generally March 31 for calendar-year taxpayers; payments are due March 31 and forms are due no later than June 15 (extensions apply for electronic filing).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 – General Identification Taxpayer identification number (RUC), corporate name, fiscal year-end, and address. For a Panama S.A. owned by non-residents, the resident agent’s details must be listed. The RUC is the entity’s 12/15-digit ID; foreign shareholders are not separately listed. Late filing penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction thereof, up to a maximum of 50% of the tax due. Interest accrues at the statutory rate (currently 1% per month). Surcharge of 10% if the taxpayer fails to file within the deadline without reasonable cause.
Box 2 – Income (Ingresos Brutos) Gross revenue from Panamanian-source activities, including reverse logistics handling fees, storage fees, and service charges. Only report Panama-source revenue. Revenue from cross-border logistics where the services are rendered outside Panama may be excluded under territoriality, subject to supporting documentation. Late filing penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction thereof, up to a maximum of 50% of the tax due. Interest accrues at the statutory rate (currently 1% per month). Surcharge of 10% if the taxpayer fails to file within the deadline without reasonable cause.
Box 3 – Cost of Sales and Deductions Direct costs, freight, warehousing, labor, and other deductible expenses attributable to producing Panamanian-source revenue. Expenses must be supported by invoices and properly accrued. Costs incurred with foreign related parties require transfer pricing documentation if above thresholds. Late filing penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction thereof, up to a maximum of 50% of the tax due. Interest accrues at the statutory rate (currently 1% per month). Surcharge of 10% if the taxpayer fails to file within the deadline without reasonable cause.
Box 4 – Net Taxable Income (Renta Neta Gravable) Gross income less allowable deductions and any carried-forward losses (up to 5 years). For non-resident-owned S.A., this must reflect only the territorial net income. Losses from foreign-source activities are not deductible. Late filing penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction thereof, up to a maximum of 50% of the tax due. Interest accrues at the statutory rate (currently 1% per month). Surcharge of 10% if the taxpayer fails to file within the deadline without reasonable cause.
Box 5 – Tax Payable (Impuesto a Pagar) 25% of net taxable income. Credit is given for advance payments and any withholding taxes already detained. Non-resident shareholders do not directly file this; the S.A. remits the corporate tax. No additional branch tax applies to the S.A. itself. Late filing penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction thereof, up to a maximum of 50% of the tax due. Interest accrues at the statutory rate (currently 1% per month). Surcharge of 10% if the taxpayer fails to file within the deadline without reasonable cause.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are required when transactions with related parties exceed USD 2,000,000 (2 million Balboas) per fiscal year, or when the taxpayer applies for specific tax rulings. Also, if the taxpayer is a multinational enterprise (MNE) group member, a CbCR notification is required if consolidated revenue ≥ USD 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Panama applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for entities in Panama having tax residence. For a logistics corporation conducting cross-border reverse logistics, ESR requires that core income-generating activities are performed in Panama by qualified personnel and that the entity maintains adequate physical presence, employees, and records. The low-risk standard applies, but annual ES return submission is mandatory for all entities (even zero-revenue entities). Failure to file carries penalties of up to USD 50,000.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Tributaria)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: imprisonment of 2 to 5 years; monetary fine equal to the evaded tax plus 100% surcharge; administrative fine of up to USD 50,000. The DGI may also revoke the RUC and order the closure of the business.

Director personal liability:
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Failure to comply with Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) carries penalties of up to USD 50,000 per year; the entity may be struck off the corporate registry, and the resident agent may be disqualified. The entity may lose its corporate tax benefits and be subject to reassessment.

Director personal liability:
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to USD 1,000,000 for non-compliance with AML regulations; for bearer shares, criminal penalties including imprisonment from 2 to 4 years. The Superintendency of Non-Financial Entities (SBNEF) can suspend the license.

Director personal liability:
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty: up to 2% of the related-party transaction value, with a minimum of USD 10,000; income adjustments of understated profit are taxed at 25% with interest and surcharges.

Director personal liability:
Withholding Tax Violations

Statutory sanction: The company is personally liable for the withheld amount plus interest; fines of 50% to 100% of the unpaid tax; criminal liability for the responsible officers in cases of willful non-remittance.

Director personal liability:
Director and Officer Personal Liability

Statutory sanction: Personal civil liability for taxes, penalties, and interest; judicial orders freezing assets; travel bans; and imprisonment of 2 to 6 years for criminal tax fraud. Panama courts may pierce the corporate veil under the General Corporation Law when the S.A. is used to commit fraud or conceal unlawful activity.
