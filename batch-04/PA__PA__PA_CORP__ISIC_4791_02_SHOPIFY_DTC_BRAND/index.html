Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá (Public Registry of Panama) – Sección Mercantil y de Comercio (Ficha Mercantil Registry)

Governing statute

Ley 32 de 26 de febrero de 1927 (General Corporations Law of Panama), as amended; primarily Articles 1-10 (formation and registration), 13-35 (share capital, classes of shares, bearer shares), 40-56 (directors and officers), and 59-70 (registered agent and domicile), together with the Regulations of the Registro Público de Panamá (Ficha Mercantil system) and the Codificación Administrativa del Registro Público.

Formation / constitutional filings

Escritura Pública de Constitución (Public Deed of Incorporation) executed before a Panamanian Notary Public, submitted as the certified Testimonio for inscription in the Ficha Mercantil; alternatively incorporable by private document with legalized signatures under Law 32 conditions.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow deposit requirement under Ley 32 de 1927. The Articles of Incorporation must declare the authorized capital (commonly USD 10,000), and at least one share must be subscribed at formation; the full consideration may remain unpaid and callable at the Board's discretion.

Funding & unlock path: Not required for a virtual storefront with no premises or employees. Contingently, upon opening any physical office or warehouse: annual Certificado de Seguridad Contra Incendios from the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá; structural and use-of-soil clearance from the Dirección de Ingeniería Municipal; and workplace safety registration with the Ministerio de Trabajo y Desarrollo Laboral (MITRADEL) under the occupational health and safety regime once employees are engaged.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow deposit requirement under Ley 32 de 1927. The Articles of Incorporation must declare the authorized capital (commonly USD 10,000), and at least one share must be subscribed at formation; the full consideration may remain unpaid and callable at the Board's discretion.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for the pure DTC e-commerce scope under ISIC 4791. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas de Panamá – official web portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign‑owned Panama Sociedad Anónima obtains a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) by: (1) incorporating and registering the S.A. with the Public Registry of Panama; (2) appointing a resident legal representative; (3) completing the online pre‑registration in the DGI/SIT platform (Sistema Integrado Tributario); (4) submitting the public deed, corporate papers, proof of registered address, and identification of the qualified resident representative; and (5) receiving a provisional or definitive RUC. The same RUC is used for income tax and ITBMS. If the entity is a non‑resident company without a permanent establishment, the DGI assigns a non‑resident RUC (RUC‑PN) for electronic invoicing and digital‑services VAT compliance.

If the entity is a non‑resident company without a permanent establishment, the DGI assigns a non‑resident RUC (RUC‑PN) for electronic invoicing and digital‑services VAT compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — a Sociedad Anónima is entity‑level taxed; only Panama‑source income is subject to corporate income tax; foreign‑source income received by a Panama S.A. is generally outside net taxable base if not attributable to a Panama permanent establishment; shareholders are not taxed on undistributed earnings; the entity is not a pass‑through.

Available election forms

Panama does not provide US‑style check‑the‑box elections. Relevant elections include: (i) the election to be treated as a small taxpayer under the Régimen Especial de Pequeño Contribuyente if gross income does not exceed US$36,000 per year; (ii) the election to issue electronic invoices through a DGI‑authorized private biller or through the DGI’s own electronic billing system; (iii) no domestic hybrid‑mismatch or entity‑classification election is codified; therefore no hybrid election is available under Panamanian law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ITBMS registration is mandatory with no general economic threshold for the supply of taxable goods or services. Any person, including an e‑commerce seller, must obtain a RUC and register for ITBMS before commencing business in Panama. A small‑taxpayer simplified regime may apply for annual gross income up to US$36,000, but the taxpayer remains required to register and to account for ITBMS where taxable supplies in Panama are made.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating at 0% applies to goods exported from Panama, including DTC brand goods sold through Shopify and shipped to customers abroad, when: (a) the goods leave Panamanian customs territory under an export declaration; (b) the commercial invoice is issued to a foreign customer and states ‘export/sale for export’ and 0% ITBMS; (c) shipping documents (bill of lading, air waybill, or courier tracking record) evidence departure; and (d) title/risk passes outside Panama in accordance with Incoterms. For services, zero rating applies when the service is rendered to a non‑resident foreign customer and is not used within Panama; the contract and payment evidence must demonstrate foreign utilization.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Mercancías — DUA / TICA) accepted by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or courier consignment tracking documentation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Bank Credit Evidence from PayPal/Stripe/Shopify Payments showing receipt of export proceeds from abroad
  • Commercial invoice marked ‘Export’ and ‘Zero Rated ITBMS’
  • Purchase order, contract, or other evidence of the foreign buyer and place of destination

Panama has a phased mandatory electronic‑invoicing program. Taxpayers must issue electronic fiscal invoices (comprobantes fiscales electrónicos) through the DGI’s SIT portal or through DGI‑approved private billing software. The systems transmit invoice data to the DGI in real time, obtain a digital fiscal folio and validation number, and apply a digital stamp/QR to accepted invoices. Export invoices must be explicitly marked as resolutions/export operations, must include the export declaration reference and zero‑rated ITBMS indication, and must comply with the DGI’s data format (XML / DAF equivalents).

The DGI and customs authorities may deny zero rating, re‑assess ITBMS at 7%, and impose monetary fines of up to 100% of the tax attempted to be avoided, plus monthly surcharge interest (2% per month on the tax due) and administrative penalties. Fraudulent export zero‑rating claims expose the taxpayer to criminal tax fraud sanctions, suspension or cancellation of export privileges, denial of export refunds, and joint and several liability for the responsible legal representative. Auditor review closely examines the customs declaration, shipping documents, foreign payment remittance, and the timing of the FX repatriation; failure to maintain a complete export file within the 180‑day remittance period creates a rebuttable presumption of domestic sale and loss of zero rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama maintains complete current-account and capital-account convertibility under its fully dollarized monetary regime. There is no exchange-control legislation, no central-bank transfer authorization, no system of import/export currency licensing, and no restriction or barrier on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital liquidation applicable to a Sociedad Anonima engaged in foreign-source DTC e-commerce (ISIC 4791). Under the territorial tax system, digital retail revenue derived from customers outside Panama is foreign-source income and is exempt from Panamanian corporate income tax and from dividend tax when distributed to non-resident shareholders. Pre-remittance compliance is limited to filing the sworn annual income tax return when thresholds apply, payment of the annual franchise fee (currently B/.300), maintenance of a registered agent, and, where intercompany flows exist, adequate transfer-pricing documentation. No bank or government approval is required to repatriate profits or to convert funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation, conversion, or time-bound settlement of export proceeds. The S.A. may freely receive and hold 100% of USD export receipts in a Panamanian commercial account, maintain balances offshore, or apply funds directly to suppliers, inventory, logistics, and operating expenses without quantitative or temporal constraints. There is no central bank register of export proceeds and no balance-of-payments compulsory settlement form equivalent to surrender obligations found in other Latin American regimes.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama does not operate a conventional central bank with discretionary monetary policy. Monetary, regulatory, and payment-system functions are distributed among (i) the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panama (SBP), the statutory supervisor of banks, finance companies, and financial groups; (ii) Banco Nacional de Panama, the State fiscal agent and operator of the national clearing infrastructure; and (iii) the Ministerio de Economia y Finanzas, which coordinates external credit and macro-financial policy. The U.S. dollar is full legal tender and serves as the effective circulating currency; the Panamanian balboa (PAB) exists only as a unit of equal parity and is not independently issued in bulk.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: Conditional - Lawful only through a licensed-partner architecture. Panama's General Banking Law (Legislative Decree 2 of 2008, as amended) reserves deposit-taking and regulated payment intermediation for banks licensed by the SBP. A pure offshore digital EMI without a Panamanian banking license cannot lawfully hold funds as a treasury leg for a Panama-domiciled S.A.; however, it may operate as a front-end payment gateway if ultimate settlement passes through an SBP-licensed bank or an SBP-authorized fintech licensee. The recommended viable structure is: Shopify Payments, PayPal, and Stripe settlement flows are directed to a Tier-1 Panamanian operational account, while any EMI is used only for disbursement execution, reconciliation analytics, or local card acquiring under the licensed bank's umbrella. The S.A. must maintain at least one Panama operational account to document beneficial ownership, mitigate correspondent-bank de-risking, and provide a clear audit trail for foreign payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Panama does not administer a statutory FX purpose-code registry, so inward remittances must carry a precise narrative instruction in the SWIFT MT103 or MT202. Recommended routing chain: originating bank in the jurisdiction of the shareholder -> U.S. correspondent aggregate USD clearing bank -> Panamanian beneficiary bank -> S.A. operational account. Wire format: MT103 with field 50K naming the remitter entity, field 59 naming the Panama S.A. beneficiary exactly as registered, and field 72 reading 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN UNDER INTERCOMPANY AGREEMENT'. The receiving bank classifies the flow internally for balance-of-payments and AML purposes; supporting documents must include the notarized shareholder or board resolution, the intercompany loan agreement if applicable, the full beneficial-ownership chart, and a corporate reference letter. For platform treasury top-ups from Shopify, PayPal, or Stripe, funds should be initiated from the platform's payout bank to the designated Panamanian account with unique transaction reference codes tied to the merchant ID to avoid mismatch flags.Clearing-rail matrix for a Panama DTC brand: (i) supplier payments to foreign manufacturers: SWIFT MT103 with trade-invoice description; no Panamanian regulatory approval is required; (ii) letter-of-credit procurement for batch inventory: irrevocable documentary credit issued by the Panamanian bank and advised or confirmed through a U.S. or EU confirming bank; (iii) card-network disbursals: use Shopify Payments, PayPal, and Stripe scheduled payout files to deposit directly into the Panamanian operational account via USD ACH or international SWIFT; (iv) intra-group deployment: MT202 cover or internal sweep to a related account within the same banking group. Domestic same-day movement of USD balances is executed through the interbank clearing infrastructure operated under the fiscal agency of Banco Nacional de Panama, while all cross-border USD settlement routes through the SWIFT network and U.S. correspondent clearing. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% territorial corporate income tax (Impuesto Sobre la Renta – ISR) applied only to Panama-source income; non-Panama-source income is outside the tax net.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding tax on dividends paid from profits that have already been subject to Panamanian corporate income tax; 20% tax on dividends paid out of non-taxed, exempt, or foreign-source profits; no withholding tax on repayment of capital or shareholder loan principal.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if annual consolidated gross revenue exceeds USD 12,000,000 or the aggregate value of related-party transactions exceeds USD 1,000,000; Master File required if the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds USD 50,000,000; threshold calculated in Panamanian Balboa (official currency USD equivalent).

Economic substance scope: Panama applies Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) to entities carrying on defined relevant activities, including distribution of goods and IP exploitation; a DTC e-commerce brand engaged in Panama-source internet retail must demonstrate real physical presence (licensed premises, full-time employees with decision-making control, bank accounts, accounting and tax records) in Panama; failure satisfies statutory substance may lead to revocation of tax residence certificate and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Tributaria)

Statutory sanction: Administrative: fine up to 200% of the evaded tax, plus monthly surcharges, interest, and an asset freeze order; Criminal: imprisonment of 2 to 5 years for the responsible natural persons.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and beneficial owners may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, penal surcharges, and civil damages; travel bans and precautionary incarceration can be issued pending investigation; conviction triggers disqualification from holding board positions.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Restitution of all underpaid taxes, retroactive reassessment for up to 7 years, revocation of the entity's tax registration and RUC, cancellation of the DTA residence certificate; monetary penalties under the Fiscal Procedure Code of USD 1,000 to USD 25,000 per period.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is permitted where the corporation is an alter ego; directors can be assessed personally for 100% of unpaid tax liabilities; civil restoration and injunctions against property disposal are common; serious non-compliance can trigger arrest warrants and extradition in cases of transnational fraud.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Breach

Statutory sanction: Penalties under Executive Decree 326/2013 and Law 23/2015 range from USD 50,000 to USD 5,000,000 depending on severity; the corporate entity may be dissolved; collection of criminal proceeds is subject to asset forfeiture; statutory fine up to 20% of the unreported transaction amount.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face criminal liability of up to 10 years' imprisonment for structuring transactions to conceal beneficial ownership; personal travel bans and preventive attachment of personal assets are routinely enforced; professional disqualification and cancellation of fiduciary licenses follow felony conviction.
False Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment to arm's-length profit, additional tax at 25%, surcharge of 5% per month, plus an accuracy penalty of 50% of the adjusted tax; denial of deductions for payments made to non-residents without valid transfer pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: Board members who signed or were aware of the fictitious agreements are personally liable for the deficiency and statutory penalties; criminal referral for tax evasion is made if artificial construction is proved; judicial authorities may issue directors and shareholders travel bans and seizure orders over personal assets.
