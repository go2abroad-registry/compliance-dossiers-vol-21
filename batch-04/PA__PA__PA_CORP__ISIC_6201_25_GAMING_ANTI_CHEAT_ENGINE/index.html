Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama. Official portal: DGI e-Tax (dgi.gob.pa) and the DGI electronic invoicing / Facturación Electrónica platform.

E-government filing portal

A Panama Sociedad Anónima must be incorporated and recorded in the Registro Público with a resident agent and registered office. Following incorporation, the resident agent submits the public deed, corporate charter, registered-office evidence, and beneficial-ownership information to the DGI Registry to obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente). The RUC is the single tax identity used for income tax, ITBMS, employer obligations, and digital invoicing. Foreign shareholders and directors may be non-residents; no minimum capital or local economic substance condition applies for RUC issuance, although the DGI may require a local fiscal address and an active registered agent. A foreign entity with no Panama-source activity is not automatically required to obtain a RUC, but the company should register before issuing taxable supplies in Panama or maintaining a local payroll.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide US-style check-the-box entity classification elections. A Panama S.A. is automatically a separate corporate taxpayer subject to entity-level taxation on Panama-source income under the territorial tax system; it cannot be treated as pass-through or disregarded. No hybrid mismatch or reverse-hybrid election is available. The only applicable simplification election is the 'pequeño contribuyente' small-taxpayer regime, which may affect ITBMS filing frequency and accounting burden below the relevant DGI small-taxpayer turnover threshold without altering corporate tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 0 — there is no general economic-turnover exemption from ITBMS registration; any person carrying out taxable transfers of goods, imports, or taxable services in Panama must register for the RUC/ITBMS. The DGI small-taxpayer regime, applicable to taxpayers with gross annual receipts below the ministerial threshold periodically updated by DGI resolution, affects simplified filing and invoicing procedures only, not the duty to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the ITBMS applies to actual exports of goods that leave Panama and to services sold to non-resident recipients whose economic use and benefit occur entirely outside Panama. For the video game kernel-level anti-cheat middleware, zero-rating is claimable where the licensed software, anti-cheat updates, and SaaS/remote monitoring are delivered to foreign gaming publishers or platform operators, the servers and users are outside Panama, and no Panamanian customer receives the benefit. The exporter must be registered for ITBMS, must report export invoices through the electronic invoicing system under the export classification, and must maintain written contracts, technical documentation, user-location evidence, and banking records sufficiently demonstrating foreign-source export consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / electronic customs manifest)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exit of goods where applicable
  • Commercial invoice, export contract, and purchase order identifying the non-resident buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank movement/payment evidence evidencing foreign-source export proceeds

Panama mandates electronic invoicing through the DGI Facturación Electrónica platform. Taxpayers must transmit invoice data in real time through DGI-approved software or API integration; the DGI validates the electronic invoice and returns a unique authorization code and digital fiscal stamp before the invoice is legally issued to the client. The e-invoice must contain the supplier's and buyer's RUC, invoice series, product/service code, ITBMS rate including 0% export classification, and a QR code/digital seal. Fiscal printers are being phased out in favour of API-connected e-invoicing for all VAT-equivalent taxpayers.

The DGI may audit zero-rated export transactions and claw back any ITBMS refunds previously granted if the export documentation is insufficient, the services are deemed consumed in Panama, or the transaction is considered simulated. The typical reassessment includes 7% ITBMS on improperly zero-rated invoices, late-payment interest, and administrative penalties that may reach up to 100% of the omitted tax. Fraudulent zero-rating claims, forged export documents, or artificially structured foreign invoices also expose the entity, its directors, legal representatives, and resident agent to criminal tax-fraud penalties. The DGI generally may reassess and claw back within a five-year statute of limitations, which is extended for fraud or non-filing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility. The US Dollar circulates as official legal tender with the locally minted Balboa coin pegged 1:1. There are no exchange permits, prior licensing requirements, repatriation controls, or capital account restrictions on dividends, intercompany loans, royalties, or direct investment inflows. Profit remittance is subject only to territorial income tax mechanics: dividends paid by a Panamanian S.A. generally carry a 10% withholding tax when distributed, reduced to 5% if the underlying profits have already borne Panamanian corporate income tax; interest and royalties paid abroad are subject to a 12.5% withholding tax absent an applicable treaty exemption. These are fiscal obligations, not FX convertibility barriers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory surrender, forced conversion, holding period, or repatriation timeline for export receivables. Software licensing receipts may be retained indefinitely in any SBP-licensed bank in USD or other currencies. Documentary compliance under the AML framework (Ley 23 de 2015) requires the exporter to preserve underlying contracts, invoices, and wire confirmations, but no conversion ratio or Central Bank deposit obligation applies.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as licensing and prudential banking regulator; Banco Nacional de Panamá as fiscal agent of the Republic; Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) as fiscal and monetary policy coordinator. Panama has no conventional central bank issuing a domestic currency; the US Dollar is legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for unlicensed offshore digital EMIs operating as primary treasury rail. Panama's Ley de Innovación Financiera (2024) authorizes SBP-supervised fintech licenses and a regulatory sandbox; however, an offshore EMI (Estonian, Lithuanian, Hong Kong, or UK licensed) is not deemed a bank under Panamanian law and may not lawfully take deposits or offer corporate clearing services from within Panama. It may be used only as a secondary settlement conduit for low-value SaaS subscription receipts, subject to the debtor-side jurisdiction's own licensing regime. For the PA_CORP treasury, tier-1 licensed banks are mandatory for capital escrow, operational liquidity, and trade settlement; fintech rails are feasible only as complementary receivables collection points with immediate onward settlement to a licensed Panamanian account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. USD 5,000,000 (Panama Balboas at 1:1 with USD) of gross annual income triggers contemporaneous Transfer Pricing documentation for related-party transactions; Panama S.A. must also file the annual TP informative annex (Form 748/Anexo de Precios de Transferencia) when group-related revenue exceeds the threshold.

Economic substance scope: Panama’s Economic Substance Regulations (Law 254 of 2021, Executive Decree 9/2022) apply to all legal entities performing 'relevant activities'. Software licensing and development services are a 'service business' for ESR purposes. The S.A. must prove and report: (a) core income-generating activity performed in Panama; (b) an adequate number of local employees and physical premises; (c) board meetings and management decisions in Panama; and (d) operating expenses commensurate with revenue. An anti-cheat engine business that functions solely as a shell or passive IP-holding vehicle without local substance will fail ESR.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: DGI imposes 50% to 100% penalty on the omitted tax plus 1.5% monthly default interest; Panama Criminal Code and Law 8 of 2010 treat intentional evasion as a financial crime punishable by 3 to 6 years imprisonment; assets and bank accounts may be seized via administrative enforcement.

Director personal liability: Directors, resident agents, and beneficial owners who sign false returns or orchestrate sham foreign income are jointly liable for the evaded tax, face travel bans and embargoes, and may be prosecuted personally; professional licenses of involved lawyers/accountants may be suspended.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Fraud

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties include fixed fines from B/.10,000 to B/.50,000, cancellation of the RUC, forced dissolution of the entity, and automatic information exchange with OECD/EU jurisdictions; the corporate veil may be lifted under Panama’s anti-abuse doctrine.

Director personal liability: The named Panama director may be fined personally for non-compliance with ESR, barred from serving on any Panama company for up to 5 years, and subjected to travel restrictions if tax debts arise from the shell structure.
Dividend Withholding Tax Abuse

Statutory sanction: The paying S.A. is liable for the un-withheld dividend tax, plus the 10% surcharge and monthly 1.5% interest; professional enablers may be sanctioned for facilitating unlawful non-taxation.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or approve distributions without satisfying withholding obligations are personally and severally liable to the DGI for the resulting deficiency; bank directors can face travel bans and personal seizure of assets when corporate assets are insufficient.
AML/CFT Violation in Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Law 23 of 2015, as amended, authorizes fines up to B/.5,000,000 per violation against the corporate; compliance officers and beneficial owners may be fined separately; criminal money laundering charges carry penalties of 5 to 15 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and resident agents who knowingly process gaming-derived proceeds from unlawful operations can be charged as principals in money laundering; travel bans, asset freezing, and imprisonment apply; directors are disqualified from future financial services functions in Panama.
