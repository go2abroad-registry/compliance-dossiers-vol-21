Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); official taxpayer portal: e-Tax 3.0 (https://etax.dgi.gob.pa; https://www.dgi.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Panama Sociedad Anónima must, through its resident registered agent, file the Aviso de Inscripción (DGI registration form) via the e-Tax portal within 30 days of incorporation at the Registro Público. The DGI issues the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), which serves as the unified TIN and automatic ITBMS enrollment. Required documentary set: certificate of incorporation/public deed, registered agent appointment certificate, PAN/passport of directors and officer, proof of registered address in Panama, and opening balance sheet. Processing time is typically 3 to 10 business days; the RUC must appear on all invoices, customs declarations, and tax returns.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign-owned Panama Sociedad Anónima must, through its resident registered agent, file the Aviso de Inscripción (DGI registration form) via the e-Tax portal within 30 days of incorporation at the Registro Público. The DGI issues the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), which serves as the unified TIN and automatic ITBMS enrollment. Required documentary set: certificate of incorporation/public deed, registered agent appointment certificate, PAN/passport of directors and officer, proof of registered address in Panama, and opening balance sheet. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or entity pass-through election exists for a Panama S.A.; the sociedad anónima is taxed at the entity level on Panama-source income only. Available elections: (i) Pequeño Contribuyente simplified regime election for annual gross income up to PAB 36,000, substituting monthly ITBMS and advance income tax with a reduced annual gross-receipts tax; (ii) election to treat a Panama branch as a permanent establishment of the foreign head office; (iii) election to carry forward ITBMS input credits versus claiming a cash refund; (iv) accelerated depreciation election for qualifying electronic equipment; and (v) Multinational Headquarters (SEM) status under Law 41 of 2007, generally not applicable to pure e-commerce retail. Hybrid-mismatch elections are not recognized under Panamanian law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Zero general de minimis threshold: any person or juridical entity, including a foreign-owned S.A., making taxable supplies of goods or services in Panama or importing goods must obtain the RUC and register for ITBMS with the DGI before commencing activities. An optional small-taxpayer threshold of PAB 36,000 annual gross income allows election of the simplified substitute regime in lieu of full ITBMS filing obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An ITBMS 0% rate applies to (i) tangible goods physically exported from Panama by the seller, (ii) services rendered by a Panamanian taxpayer to a non-resident client for use entirely outside Panama, and (iii) qualifying free-zone and multitransit export transactions. An online consumer-electronics retailer can zero-rate cross-border sales only if the goods are shipped abroad, risk and title pass to the foreign customer at exit, and the export is evidenced by Panama customs documents. Domestic deliveries, free-zone deliveries for local consumption, and goods sold to tourists in Panama for local use remain subject to the 7% standard rate. The exporter must retain the full documentation bundle to substantiate the 0% rate and the corresponding input ITBMS credit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Panama SISCO / VUPE Declaración de Exportación)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Panamanian territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit voucher demonstrating receipt of export proceeds in a Panama-licensed bank

Panama mandates electronic invoicing under the DGI Facturación Electrónica (FAS) system, phased in since 2022. Online retailers must issue e-invoices through DGI-certified billing software or the free e-Tax web/mobile FAS module, transmit invoice data in XML/JSON to the DGI in real time for fiscal clearance, and display the DGI-assigned QR code, digital stamp (Código de Registro FAS), and access key on each invoice. E-credit notes, e-debit notes, and electronic receipts follow the same prior-authorization framework. Domestic B2C and B2B sales require clearance; export invoices are covered by the zero-rating export evidence rules rather than local clearance.

The DGI may retroactively audit export substantiation within the six-year tax prescription period. Incomplete or false evidence triggers reclassification of the supply as a taxable domestic sale, assessment of unpaid ITBMS at 7%, monthly late-payment interest at 1%, and fines ranging from PAB 500 to PAB 10,000 for invoice and customs documentation violations. Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating constitutes tax evasion (defraudación fiscal) under the Código Fiscal, punishable by imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, in addition to clawback of input ITBMS credits and surcharges of up to 100% of the tax evaded; foreign directors may be held jointly and severally liable for the assessed amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized jurisdiction with total capital and current account convertibility. There are no exchange controls, no central bank transfer approval, and no restriction on profit remittance. Dividends, royalties, and service payments may be freely transferred abroad subject only to documentary compliance by the bank and applicable tax filing requirements, not prior administrative authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama does not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export receipts. Export proceeds in USD may be retained offshore or domestically in unrestricted amounts.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); Panama has no traditional central bank and the U.S. dollar serves as legal tender, with the Balboa pegged 1:1 solely as a fractional currency unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium but restricted as a primary treasury rail. Panama permits supervised fintech payment processors, but foreign offshore EMIs cannot hold Panamanian corporate funds as a substitute for a SBP-licensed bank account. Such EMIs are acceptable only as settlement collection channels for retail e-commerce, subject to the underlying funds being swept to a local Panama bank account under explicit AML/CFT oversight.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% flat ISR (Impuesto sobre la Renta) on Panama-source taxable income, under Panama's territorial tax system (Código Fiscal, Title I, Chapter I).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% statutory withholding tax on dividends or profit distributions paid by a Panamanian S.A. out of Panama-source profits that have borne the 25% ISR; a 10% final withholding rate applies to distributions out of profits that were not taxed in Panama (territorial/exempt foreign-source income) unless the company has previously paid the 10% complementary dividend tax. A compliant electronics retail S.A. distributing audited locally-sourced profits will normally use the 5% rate and issue Form 150-D to the shareholder.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual Form 140 must be filed within three months after the fiscal year-end; for a calendar-year S.A., the final deadline is March 31 of the following tax period. Payment must accompany the return. Extensions are not automatic; DGI may grant a written 1-month extension only upon justified application.

Non-resident online sellers without a Panama permanent establishment do not file Form 140 solely for distance sales. For the Panamanian S.A., include only Panama-situs sales; export sales to foreign customers should be excluded and recorded in the foreign-source income schedule. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File/Formulario 170-TP is triggered when consolidated related-party transactions (cross-border or domestic) exceed USD/B/. 250,000 in a fiscal year; if the Panamanian entity's annual gross income exceeds USD/B/. 3 million, TP documentation is also required regardless of transaction value. Master File must be prepared when the ultimate parent's global consolidated revenue exceeds USD/B/. 20 million. Filing and penalties follow the OECD-aligned Decree 63/2016 rules; these thresholds are in Balboas (PAB), the legal currency pegged 1:1 to the United States dollar.

Economic substance scope: Panama Law 70 of 2019 and Executive Decree 286 of 2019 impose explicit economic substance requirements on an S.A. that performs any "relevant activity," which includes distribution, logistics, service provision, financing, and IP holding. The present online retail of consumer electronics and smart gadgets is a distribution/relevant activity, so the S.A. must maintain: a filing address and real office/warehouse facilities in Panama; full-time qualified employees (admin, logistics, compliance) proportionate to volume; board and operational decisions made in Panama; local bank accounts in Panama that support invoicing; and annual ESR reporting to the DGI. The use of exclusive nominee directors, virtual-only operations, or foreign outsourced fulfillment without local control will trigger a denial of substance. The doctrine also applies to a "pure equity-holding" parent of the retail subsidiary; a definitive waiver is available only for truly dormant companies whose sole assets are shares and that file the negative ESR affirmation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion/False ISR Return

Statutory sanction: Panama's tax code and Penal Code punish the evasion with a penalty of three times the evaded tax, plus late-payment surcharge and interest; the responsible natural person may be sentenced to 2-6 years of imprisonment, with a warrant of embargo on corporate bank accounts and competing commercial credit lines. DGI may administratively close the fiscal registration and issue a certificate of insolvency.

Director personal liability:
Shell Company / ESR Failure

Statutory sanction: Breach of Law 70/Decree 286 exposes the company to an annual economic substance failure penalty of USD/B/. 10,000 up to USD/B/. 1,000,000 or 0.5% of gross income if higher; the DGI may refuse to issue or renew the operation's Aviso de Operación, nullify tax receipts, and apply for judicial dissolution of the S.A. as a fictitious entity.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who certify false substance reports may be personally fined and banned from acting as director in Panama; directors may be subject to restitution of the penalties if they knowingly used a shell to evade ESR and TP rules. Travel bans per Ley 40 may be imposed for unpaid fiscal penalties.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DGI will adjust taxable income to the arm's length standard, assess the 25% ISR difference, impose an additional penalty of 10%-30% of the adjustment, add unpaid-amount interest, and apply a late-filing sanction of at least B/. 1,000. If the TP documentation is fabricated, criminal liability for fraud follows; a final administrative determination may be published in official registries and attached to the S.A.'s compliance file.

Director personal liability: Directors and the CFO cannot shield behind the corporate entity when they sign inaccurate Form 170-TP; Panama courts may pierce the veil, hold individuals jointly and severally for the additional tax, and order precautionary embargo of personal assets, passports and exit permits.
AML / Money Laundering through Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Under Panama Law 23 of 2015 and the AML Criminal Code, a juridical person may be fined up to USD/B/. 1,000,000 per violation; a license or operating permit may be revoked, and financial institutions may terminate correspondent banking. Criminal prosecution for money laundering carries 5-12 years of imprisonment for the natural-person operators and sequestration of assets used in the predicate offense.

Director personal liability: Senior management may be charged criminally without requiring a prior corporate conviction; civil seizure of personal property, travel bans under Código Procesal Penal, and disqualification from future directorship in Panama are the standard consequences.
Unpaid Dividend Withholding / Constructive Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The S.A. is personally liable for the unpaid 5% or 10% dividend withholding tax, plus the late-filing surcharge of 5% per month (cap 50%) and statutory interest; DGI may order an embargo of the S.A.'s receivable bank accounts, levy on inventory, and impose a fixed fine for failure to issue withholding forms. In egregious cases, the amount is also gravamen for fraud since the administration is the unpaid creditor.

Director personal liability: In Panama, directors who are responsible for distributing dividends without making the tax retention act as de facto statutory withholding agents and may be jointly and severally liable for the withheld tax, with additional personal fines and an exit-bar travel restriction until the debt is paid.
