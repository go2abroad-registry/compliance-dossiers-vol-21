Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; unitary national tax administration with no sub-national income tax or VAT authorities. Electronic services are centralized in the DGI e-Tax portal (Sistema T@x, integrated with SIGMEF) and the DGI Electronic Invoicing platform (Facturación Electrónica); customs matters are administered by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) in coordination with DGI for export substantiation.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the S.A. before a Panamanian notary and register the public deed with the Registro Público de Panamá; appoint a licensed resident agent (agente residente) and maintain a registered office in Panama. No minimum paid-in capital is required. Step 2: File the Aviso de Operación with the DGI (business-license aviso integrated under Law 254 of 2019) to obtain the fiscal identity (Registro Único de Contribuyente, RUC), which for juridical persons corresponds to the corporate cédula jurídica assigned at incorporation. Step 3: Submit the DGI taxpayer registration form through the DGI portal or through the resident agent, declaring the fiscal domicile, statutory officers, and the ISIC 6430 activity; obtain the RUC registration certificate and register for ITBMS if any taxable commercial supply will be carried out. Step 4: Obtain the FEA digital signature certificate (Firma Electrónica Avanzada) enabling e-filing, electronic invoicing, and Customs SDA system access. Step 5 (charitable election): file with DGI the constitutive documents and internal regulations demonstrating the non-profit, philanthropic or scientific purpose and the non-distribution constraint, and apply for the resolucion de calificacion as entidad sin fines de lucro (tax-exempt qualification). Equivalent RUC/aviso registration applies where the vehicle is structured as a Fundacion de Interes Privado under Law 25 of 1995. Foreign entities operating through a Panamanian branch additionally register the sucursal under the parent RUC after inscribing the branch deed in the Registro Publico. The RUC is kept in good standing by payment of the flat annual franchise tax (tasa única) and by filing annual informational returns even when no tax is due.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not recognize check-the-box or partnership pass-through elections; an S.A. is a per se entity-level taxpayer (25% ISR on Panamanian-source income only). Under territoriality, foreign-source endowment income, foreign capital gains, and offshore grants are automatically outside the tax base and require no election. Available statutory elections and regimes: (i) DGI non-profit qualification under Article 6(f) of the Codigo Fiscal, conferring ISR exemption on institutional income (donations, grants, endowment earnings) for charitable, educational, scientific, or social-assistance entities whose statutes prohibit profit distribution; (ii) reorganization into or registration as a Fundacion de Interes Privado under Law 25 of 1995 with fixed annual taxation and confidentiality rules, subject to RUC/DGI registration; (iii) ITBMS simplified small-taxpayer regime (Regimen Simplificado de Tributacion) for annual gross receipts up to USD 36,000; (iv) Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007 for qualifying regional control or service functions, not applicable to a pure charitable endowment; (v) free-zone customs/ITBMS regimes in the Colón Free Zone, Panama Pacifico, and similar areas. Hybrid-mismatch and fiscal-transparency elections are not available under Panamanian law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Standard ITBMS registration is required for all legal entities from the commencement of taxable commercial activity in Panama, with no statutory de minimis turnover threshold for corporations. An optional simplified small-taxpayer (pequeno contribuyente) regime applies where annual gross receipts do not exceed USD 36,000. A charitable S.A. or foundation performing only exempt grant-making and endowment management, with no taxable supplies, must obtain from the DGI an ITBMS exclusion certificate (certificado de exclusion) to avoid mandatory registration; donations and grants are outside the scope of ITBMS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated (0% ITBMS) where the goods physically depart Panamanian customs territory and title passes to a non-resident, evidenced by the customs export declaration and transport documents. Cross-border services qualify for zero-rating when performed for a non-resident recipient, wholly used abroad, and not connected with Panamanian real property; offshore asset-management, advisory, and grant-coordination services for foreign charitable donors fall within this category, while bare endowment holding and grant distributions are outside ITBMS scope for lack of a taxable supply. The exporter must issue an electronic invoice reflecting 0% ITBMS and retain the full documentary trail; input ITBMS on export-related purchases is creditable and recoverable through monthly offset or export refund (devolucion) under DGI rules using supplier export-certificates. Panama levies no VAT on exports and maintains no exchange-control repatriation requirement (the U.S. dollar is legal tender); the 180-day benchmark reflects interbank and DGI audit expectations for documentary collection closure and evidenced receipt of proceeds from abroad.

  • Panama Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / Documento Único de Aduana, DUA) filed via the Customs SDA system
  • Signed Bill of Lading (Conocimiento de Embarque) or Air Waybill evidencing physical exit from Panamanian territory
  • DGI electronic export invoice at 0% ITBMS bearing the fiscal validation code and QR
  • Export sales contract or service agreement with the foreign recipient
  • Inward remittance certificate (comprobante de transferencia bancaria / certificado de ingreso de divisas) evidencing collection from abroad
  • Packing list and certificate of origin, where applicable

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electronica) applies to all taxpayers under Law 254 of 2019 and DGI technical resolutions. Invoices must be generated through the DGI electronic-invoicing platform or certified private software connected to DGI web-service APIs; each invoice is transmitted to the DGI and validated online before or at issuance, receiving a unique fiscal folio, authentication code (código de seguridad), and QR code (prior-validation model). Export invoices, credit/debit notes, and purchase receipts must use the same digital channel, and access requires the taxpayer's FEA digital certificate. There is no physical fiscal stamping; the DGI online validation operates as fiscal clearance, and ITBMS returns are filed electronically on a monthly basis through the T@x portal.

ITBMS export claims are subject to ex-post documentary audit by DGI and the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas. Where the goods are not exported or the service is not consumed abroad within the required period, the supply is recharacterized as domestic, triggering retroactive assessment of 7% ITBMS, statutory moratory interest, surcharges, and fines computed on the tax at issue. False or simulated export documentation constitutes tax fraud under the Codigo Fiscal: consequences include administrative fines, suspension or revocation of the RUC, closure of the establishment for up to 20 working days under Law 254 of 2019, loss of the non-profit qualification with retroactive recapture of exempt income, and, for willful evasion, criminal liability with imprisonment. The charitable S.A. or foundation must therefore retain export and foreign-income documentation for the statutory prescription period (generally five years) and, for grantees, must evidence non-profit use of funds to preserve the DGI qualification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy with unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account; there are no currency controls, no dual exchange rates, and no impediments to profit remittance or philanthropic grant distribution; territorial taxation further shields offshore-source charitable endowments from local income tax, and cross-border contributions may be received and disbursed freely in USD or any mutually agreed foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no mandatory timeline, no surrender threshold, and no conversion ratio for export receipts; exporters and licensed private interest foundations may retain all proceeds in USD or exchange them at market rates through SBP-regulated commercial banks.

Central bank supervision: No central bank exists in Panama; the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) governs bank licensing and AML/CFT compliance, while the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) administers external financial policy and public debt; the U.S. dollar serves as official legal tender with Federal Reserve monetary anchor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. Foreign digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) do not possess Panamanian banking licenses and cannot independently maintain demand deposit accounts; they may provide payment initiation or card-clearing services only through a licensed local commercial bank in a correspondent-facilitator model. Acceptance by Panamanian banks is discretionary and depends on the applicant's source of funds documentation and the bank's correspondent banking appetite for non-resident private charitable foundations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (Panama CIT flat rate on net Panamanian-source income; 0% effective on exempt private interest foundation income under Law 25 of 1995; territoriality excludes foreign-source income from the tax base)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding tax on dividends distributed from Panamanian taxable profits; 0% on dividends originating from foreign-source accumulated profits. An additional 10% corporate-level 'dividend tax' is payable at distribution when exempt profits are distributed without having borne effective corporate income tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before 31 March of the year following the fiscal year end; if the fiscal year ends on a date other than 31 December, the return is due within three months after fiscal year end. Nil returns are required for inactive Sociedades Anónimas.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on the DGI collection calendar dates for the fiscal period, generally within two months after the end of each calendar quarter (30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 (Rentas gravadas de fuente panameña) All gross income derived from Panamanian sources, including management fees, local rental income, domestic interest and services performed in Panama. A non-resident-owned S.A. must include only Panama-source income; foreign endowment proceeds, offshore dividends and overseas capital gains are excluded under territoriality. Late filing penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction thereof, capped at 100%; monthly arrears interest at the statutory rate; DGI may issue compulsory collection notices, freeze banking attachments, and criminally prosecute wilful non-filing.
Box 2 (Rentas exentas) Statutory exemptions, including private interest foundation income, non-profit grants recognized by the DGI, and any legislated tax holiday amounts. Claim only exemptions supported by a DGI ruling or Law 25/1995 foundation status; attach the foundation certificate and charitable-purpose charter. Late filing penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction thereof, capped at 100%; monthly arrears interest at the statutory rate; DGI may issue compulsory collection notices, freeze banking attachments, and criminally prosecute wilful non-filing.
Box 3 (Total de ingresos brutos) Arithmetic sum of Box 1 and Box 2, representing total gross income reported on the legal entity's books. Reconciling with the audited financial statement is mandatory; if no Panama activity is conducted, report zero in Box 3 and file a nil return. Late filing penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction thereof, capped at 100%; monthly arrears interest at the statutory rate; DGI may issue compulsory collection notices, freeze banking attachments, and criminally prosecute wilful non-filing.
Box 4 (Costos y gastos deducibles) Deductible expenses wholly incurred for the production of Panamanian taxable income, including local wages, rent, professional services and depreciation. Foreign overheads and intra-group charges are deductible only if substantiated, incurred in Panama, and priced at arm's length; keep contracts, invoices and board resolutions available for DGI inspection. Late filing penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction thereof, capped at 100%; monthly arrears interest at the statutory rate; DGI may issue compulsory collection notices, freeze banking attachments, and criminally prosecute wilful non-filing.
Box 5 (Renta neta gravable) Taxable net income after deducting Box 4 from Box 1. Losses from Panamanian sources may offset future income for five fiscal years. Do not offset foreign losses against Panama-source income; if Box 5 is negative, the loss carryforward must be separately tracked in a tax memorandum. Late filing penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction thereof, capped at 100%; monthly arrears interest at the statutory rate; DGI may issue compulsory collection notices, freeze banking attachments, and criminally prosecute wilful non-filing.
Box 6 (Impuesto causado) Corporate income tax computed by applying the 25% rate to Box 5, or the non-profit preferential rate when a DGI exemption ruling is valid. A foreign-resident shareholder is not taxed on the S.A.'s undistributed profits; the S.A. itself is the taxpayer. Late filing penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction thereof, capped at 100%; monthly arrears interest at the statutory rate; DGI may issue compulsory collection notices, freeze banking attachments, and criminally prosecute wilful non-filing.
Box 7 (Créditos, retenciones y pagos a cuenta) Advance payments, withholding tax credits, dividends-advance credits and other recognized prepayments recovered by the entity. Only Panamanian withholding credits may be claimed; no credit is granted for foreign taxes paid, in line with territorial income tax principles. Late filing penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction thereof, capped at 100%; monthly arrears interest at the statutory rate; DGI may issue compulsory collection notices, freeze banking attachments, and criminally prosecute wilful non-filing.
Box 8 (Impuesto neto a pagar o saldo a favor) Net tax due after applying Box 7 credits to Box 6; if negative, the result is a refundable or creditable balance. Any balance due must be paid by the statutory deadline; refund requests must be accompanied by supporting certificates and tax residency documents for non-resident claims. Late filing penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction thereof, capped at 100%; monthly arrears interest at the statutory rate; DGI may issue compulsory collection notices, freeze banking attachments, and criminally prosecute wilful non-filing.
Line A (Ingresos brutos del trimestre) Actual gross Panamanian-source receipts realized during the quarter. If the foundation earns no Panamanian income, file the quarterly form as zero or request a formal filing exemption where DGI policy permits. Late filing and late payment surcharges apply monthly until regularized; failure to file quarterly advances can invalidate the issuance of a tax solvency certificate (Paz y Salvo) and block public registrations or dividend distributions.
Line B (Renta neta estimada) Estimated net taxable income for the quarter using the most recent Form 714 data plus trending adjustments. Estimate must not include foreign-source income; calculations must be consistent with the annual territorial tax base. Late filing and late payment surcharges apply monthly until regularized; failure to file quarterly advances can invalidate the issuance of a tax solvency certificate (Paz y Salvo) and block public registrations or dividend distributions.
Line C (Créditos de trimestres anteriores) Cumulative quarterly advance payments already made for the same fiscal year. Retain the DGI payment receipts for each quarterly installment. Late filing and late payment surcharges apply monthly until regularized; failure to file quarterly advances can invalidate the issuance of a tax solvency certificate (Paz y Salvo) and block public registrations or dividend distributions.
Line D (Cuota a pagar) Net quarterly instalment due after deducting prior credits. Payment must be made in Panamanian Balboas or US Dollars through an approved banking network; use the entity's registered ''tax ID'' (RUC). Late filing and late payment surcharges apply monthly until regularized; failure to file quarterly advances can invalidate the issuance of a tax solvency certificate (Paz y Salvo) and block public registrations or dividend distributions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. (Balboa) thresholds: Local File required when controlled cross-border transactions exceed B/.1,000,000 in the fiscal year; Master File required when the multinational group has consolidated revenue exceeding B/.50,000,000 and related-party transactions exceed B/.1,000,000. Annual Transfer Pricing Informative Return (Form 792) is due by 30 June of the following fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) under Law 254 of 2021 apply to relevant activities including financing, leasing, distribution, shipping and investment management. A charitable endowment manager is subject to ESR if it provides financing or investment management services to related parties. The entity must maintain a registered office in Panama, hold local board meetings, keep minutes of charitable grant decisions, staff appropriately, and retain accounting records at the registered agent. Deficient substance will disqualify tax residency claims, trigger exchange of information, and expose the entity to administrative dissolution risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Evasion Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to B/.100,000 and imprisonment from 3 to 5 years; civil fraud penalty of 30% of omitted tax plus arrears interest; DGI may cancel all statutory exemptions and enforce immediate collection through asset attachment.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency, interest and civil penalties; a criminal conviction may trigger travel bans, passport revocation, freezing of personal assets, and disqualification from serving as directors of any Panamanian entity.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under Law 254 of 2021, fines from B/.1,000 to B/.100,000; cancellation of tax-resident status; removal from the beneficial ownership register; and forced dissolution in cases of complete absence of economic substance.

Director personal liability: Each listed director and the resident agent may be personally fined, barred from incorporating new Panama companies for five years, and ordered to make civil restitution where the shell structure facilitated tax evasion or fraudulent grants.
Money Laundering and AML Violations (Law 23 of 2015)

Statutory sanction: Money laundering penalty under the Criminal Code is 5 to 10 years imprisonment for individuals; corporate fines can reach B/.1,000,000; all tainted funds and connected assets are subject to forfeiture; revocation of the foundation's license and inclusion on the national financial intelligence and OFAC sanctions list are automatic outcomes.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorize suspicious transfers or who administer the foundation as alter ego of the settlor may be prosecuted personally, subjected to travel bans and extradition, and be required to restore misappropriated charitable assets.
Dividend Tax Evasion and Unlawful Distribution

Statutory sanction: Monthly surcharge of 5% on the unreported dividend tax up to 100% of the under-withheld amount; disallowance of deductible fees; the company is flagged as a habitual defaulter and blocked from obtaining tax solvency certificates.

Director personal liability: Officers who ordered distribution without withholding are civilly liable for the full withholding amount, interest and penalties; where signatures are used to misrepresent a dividend as a grant, directors face criminal fraud or conspiracy charges and a personal travel ban may be imposed.
