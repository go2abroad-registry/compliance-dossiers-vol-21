Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or passthrough election exists for S.A.s; the entity is a per-se taxpayer subject to 25% ISR on Panamanian-source income only, while foreign-source income is exempt. Available elections: (i) simplified small-taxpayer regime (Régimen de Pequeños Contribuyentes) for annual gross revenue up to B/.200,000; (ii) the statutory dividend mechanism whereby the 10% dividend withholding tax may be absorbed/credited by the corporation rather than withheld at distribution; (iii) designation of a fiscal representative for the annual Tasa Única franchise tax (USD 300) compliance of non-actively-trading corporations. Hybrid-mismatch restrictions under Law 254 of 2021 (effective fiscal year 2022) are mandatory and cannot be elected out of.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.200,000 (PAB, at parity with USD) of annual gross revenue is the small-taxpayer simplified regime threshold; there is no general de minimis exemption below this amount — RUC and ITBMS registration are mandatory from the first taxable sale or service performed in Panama.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: permanent physical export from Panama evidenced by the DUA (Declaración Única Aduanera) endorsed by ANA, with ownership and risk transferred to a foreign buyer. Services, including fintech underwriting and platform services: zero-rating applies where the beneficiary is non-domiciled in Panama and the benefit or use occurs abroad; the underlying interest on micro-loans remains outside ITBMS, while commissions and fees qualify at 0% if exported. The exporter must hold a valid RUC, e-invoicing authorization and ANA exporter registration; a refund of related input ITBMS is available through the periodic ITBMS declaration with full documentary substantiation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA) filed and endorsed in ANA's SIGA system
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill/CMR) evidencing movement from Panamanian territory
  • Commercial invoice plus zero-rated ITBMS export e-invoice with DGI verification code
  • Inward remittance evidence (SWIFT/bank credit advice) and underlying export contract
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (where preferential tariff treatment is claimed)

Mandatory electronic invoicing through the Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (SFE): 'facturación en línea' requires real-time transmission of each invoice to the DGI via accredited API/CFD software before issuance, with DGI validation returning a unique verification code and QR; 'facturación diferida' (offline) is permitted only for authorized small taxpayers with batch upload within 72 hours. E-invoices must include the RUC, DGI-assigned authorization number and digital signature; non-compliant invoices are ineffective for the input-credit and export-refund rules. The phased rollout concluded with full mandatory implementation for all registered taxpayers in fiscal year 2024.

DGI performs cross-matching of SFE e-invoices, SIGA customs exits and bank remittance data. Unsubstantiated 0% claims trigger: (i) retroactive assessment of 7% ITBMS with a monthly surcharge of 1.5% and default interest (Código Fiscal Arts. 707/725); (ii) fines up to 200% of the evaded tax and invalidation of input-credit refunds, with clawbacks for up to 5 fiscal years (10 years where fraud is shown); (iii) criminal tax-fraud exposure under Código Penal Art. 261-A with imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, increased for amounts above B/.150,000; and (iv) suspension or removal from the ANA exporter registry and revocation of e-invoicing authorization. Directors and resident agents are jointly liable for false certifications.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Panamanian balboa is a purely nominal accounting unit pegged at 1:1 to the U.S. dollar, and only U.S. dollar banknotes and coins are in circulation. Panama maintains full and unrestricted convertibility for both current and capital account transactions; there are no exchange-control approvals, no surrender requirements, and no balance-of-payment restrictions. Capital contributions, profit remittances, dividend distributions, loan repayments, and contract payments may be freely effected in U.S. dollars or any other convertible currency. The only practical hurdles are bank-level AML/KYC verification and statutory recordkeeping under Law No. 254 of 2021 and SBP regulations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama does not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export proceeds. Exporters and financial institutions may retain export receipts in any currency or convert them freely. For treasury operational prudence, inbound export proceeds must be identified using underlying commercial documentation such as commercial invoices, bills of lading, and customs declarations, but no percentage must be converted into local currency. This lending platform has no export-receipt exposure, so the rules are inapplicable.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Superintendency of Banks of Panama, SBP); Banco Nacional de Panamá (BNP) acts as the fiscal agent of the Republic of Panama. Panama has no central monetary authority; the U.S. dollar circulates as legal tender under the currency board-like bimetallic regime established by the 1904 Monetary Convention and Decree-Law No. 10 of 1922.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) cannot obtain a standalone EMI or banking license from the SBP. A foreign EMI may be used only as a payment-collection or card-issuance agent for an SBP-licensed Panamanian bank, and only if it is licensed in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction and entered into a sponsor-bank agreement with an SBP-regulated institution. Direct participation in Panama's local clearing and settlement infrastructure is not available to unlicensed EMIs. Feasibility rating: Low for direct onboarding; Medium for a sponsored banking partnership with a local licensed bank serving as settlement sponsor.

Within three months after the close of the corporate fiscal year. For a December 31 year-end, the deadline is March 31 of the following year. Companies not closed in December must file within three months of the annual closing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB 8,000,000 (USD 8,000,000) of gross income in the immediately preceding fiscal year, or PAB 2,000,000 (USD 2,000,000) of aggregate controlled transactions in the tax year, triggers the obligation to prepare and submit the Master File and Local File (Form 741). A Country-by-Country report is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue equal to or greater than PAB/USD 750 million. Controlled transactions with entities resident in jurisdictions subject to a no/low tax regime are within scope regardless of the above thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 116 of 2022) impose a substantive economic-presence requirement on entities carrying out 'relevant activities'. Financing and leasing, which includes consumer micro-credit and peer-to-peer lending intermediation, is expressly relevant. The platform must conduct its underwriting activities, credit risk management, and lending operations through a local office in Panama, with local employees (full-time) and local management and board meetings held in Panama. Mere registration or invoicing from a registered agent is insufficient. Failure to satisfy the ESR test exposes the taxpayer to annual penalties (up to PAB 50,000), potential loss of territorial-source exemption, and adverse recharacterization as a shell company of interest to foreign tax authorities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Omitting Panama-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Fiscal Code Articles 1027 and following: criminal tax evasion penalty of two to ten times the evaded tax; administrative adjustment plus 25% revaluation penalty; interest at 10% per annum; and for aggravated or repeated evasion, closure of the Panamanian business unit and forfeiture of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false tax returns or approve transfer of income abroad may be held personally liable for the unpaid tax and may face imprisonment from three to eight years. The Panamanian Tax Administration may block bank accounts and invoke the 'guilty taxpayer' list resulting in travel restrictions.
Unlicensed Money Transmission and Lending

Statutory sanction: Under the SBP Licensing Law (Ley 21 de 2017 and amendments), unlicensed lending financial services can trigger fines of up to USD 1,000,000, suspension of activity, and revocation of any related license. Money laundering predicate offenses carry penalties of up to five to eight years of imprisonment; AML breaches separately fine the institution up to USD 500,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and the compliance officer face personal fines (up to USD 50,000) and imprisonment (up to five years) for willful AML non-compliance. Directors may also be disqualified from serving as licensed managers or officers in Panama.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Violation of Law 116 of 2022: annual ESR non-compliance fee of PAB 50,000 for each relevant activity; escalation to administrative sanction up to PAB 100,000; automatic de-registration and/or loss of commercial registry registration after continuous default; possible nullification of tax exempt status and reclassification of all foreign income as Panama-source income.

Director personal liability: Resident agent and directors who sign false ESR declarations are subject to criminal sanctions for false testimony and commercial fraud; directors can be held jointly and severally liable for the company's outstanding fines and can be banned from acting as resident agents in future.
Improper Profit Repatriation and Dividend Withholding Default

Statutory sanction: DGI will charge the payer the un-collected tax plus a 50% surcharge, late payment interest, and an additional 20% tax on distributions made from profits never subjected to Panamanian income tax. In repeated violations, the company can be placed on the DGI debtors list and blocked from public contracts and state transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the distribution resolution are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding tax and fines; the foreign parent may be denied all tax treaty benefits and thereby subject to a 20% final withholding on the entire distribution. Expatriate directors may face exit visa denials or travel bans.
Aggressive Transfer Pricing / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Application of Article 762-F, Fiscal Code; DGI can adjust taxable income upward, deny the claimed deduction, and impose a 25% surcharge on the adjusted amount, plus interest. Non-filing of the required TP documentation triggers a fixed fine of USD 10,000 for each omitted transaction group. In egregious settings, adjustments are combined with evasion penalties of up to 100% of the unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and CFOs who knowingly sign false TP reports are personally liable for the adjusted tax; criminal prosecution for conspiracy to evade tax can result in imprisonment up to six years. If the company becomes insolvent because of tax adjustment, punitive civil liability is extended to the individuals participating in the intercompany circular transactions.
