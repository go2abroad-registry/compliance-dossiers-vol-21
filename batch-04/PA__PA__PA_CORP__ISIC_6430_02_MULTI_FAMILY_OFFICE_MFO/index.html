Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Panama. Official portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Sociedad Anónima, the statutory tax identification number is the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC). A foreign entity must (i) appoint a resident legal representative or agent in Panama; (ii) obtain a certificate of incorporation and public deed from the Registro Público de Panamá; (iii) complete the DGI pre-registration and request a DGI appointment, submitting certified copies of the incorporation documents and the legal representative's identification; and (iv) receive the Certificado de Registro Único de Contribuyente evidencing activation of tax obligations. RUC activation is required before engaging in taxable activities and before applying for ITBMS registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — For a foreign-owned Sociedad Anónima, the statutory tax identification number is the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC). A foreign entity must (i) appoint a resident legal representative or agent in Panama; (ii) obtain a certificate of incorporation and public deed from the Registro Público de Panamá; (iii) complete the DGI pre-registration and request a DGI appointment, submitting certified copies of the incorporation documents and the legal representative's identification; and (iv) receive the Certificado de Registro Único de Contribuyente evidencing activation of tax obligations. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not maintain a US-style check-the-box entity classification election. Available statutory elections are limited to (i) consolidated income tax filing for economic groups meeting common-control and DGI authorization requirements, and (ii) the simplified cash-basis/small-taxpayer method for taxpayers within statutory turnover thresholds. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized as separate statutory elections; Panama applies its territorial source rules without elective hybrid regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary exemption threshold is prescribed for ITBMS registration; any person habitually engaged in the sale of goods or rendering of services in Panama must register for RUC/ITBMS. A simplified quarterly filing regime applies to small taxpayers with annual gross taxable turnover not exceeding B/.150,000 (Panamanian Balboa, at par to USD), but this is a filing simplification, not a registration exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under ITBMS is available for (i) tangible goods physically exported from Panama, provided the export is completed within the statutory period and supported by customs exit documentation; and (ii) services rendered to non-resident recipients that are effectively used or exploited wholly outside Panama, including qualifying offshore services. The taxpayer must maintain the original export declaration, transport documents, billing evidence, and contemporaneous proof of receipt and use abroad to substantiate the 0% rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera / DUA) with DGI validation stamp for ITBMS zero-rating
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificación Bancaria de Ingreso de Divisas) or equivalent bank card/receipt showing completion of foreign exchange receipt

Panama has phased in mandatory electronic invoicing through the DGI's Sistema de Facturación Electrónica de Panamá (SFEP). Taxpayers transmit structured XML/UBL invoices through DGI-approved electronic invoicing service providers; the DGI performs automatic real-time validation of tax data, issuance codes, and arithmetic consistency, returning a unique validation/auth code. Digital stamping (QR code or seal with validation status) is required on invoices for transactions with other taxpayers. Integration via API is mandatory for taxpayers with approved ERP/accounting systems, and the obligation applies by DGI resolution based on taxpayer category and turnover.

Fraudulent export zero-rating claims are subject to intensive DGI audit review. Upon disallowance, the taxpayer is retrospectively assessed ITBMS at 7% on the invoice value plus default interest and surcharges, and may incur fines of up to 150% of the omitted tax. Where patterns of deliberate misrepresentation are found, criminal tax penalties may apply, including imprisonment and debarment from the taxpayer's RUC, electronic invoicing accreditation, and VAT refund programs. The normal tax statute of limitations may be extended or suspended in cases of fraud or incomplete documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama has full and unrestricted convertibility for both capital and current account transactions. The Panamanian balboa is legal tender only as a token representation of the US dollar, redeemable at a fixed 1:1 parity. Profit remittance is freely permitted without exchange-control approval; no central bank authorization, no prior import/export license for funds, and no repatriation restriction applies. The only constraints are statutory AML/CFT record-keeping, tax reporting obligations, and the need for the MFO to hold valid financial services licensing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement. Exporters and service providers, including a licensed multi-family office, may retain 100% of USD export proceeds and deploy them freely. No conversion mandate, no mandatory surrender timeline, and no central-bank filing are applicable. Banking records must nevertheless evidence the commercial or investment source of funds for AML purposes.

Central bank supervision: None — Panama is a dollarized jurisdiction with no central bank. Banking and financial supervision is exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); Banco Nacional de Panamá operates as the State fiscal agent and settlement bank but holds no monetary policy authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low — Panama does not recognize a dedicated digital EMI license comparable to an EEA Electronic Money Institution. Offshore fintech EMIs cannot access Panamanian automated clearing rails, cannot act as licensed custodians or settlement providers for a regulated multi-family office, and generally will be treated as collection-only tools by SBP-supervised institutions. Any digital bank wishing to operate in Panama must be a licensed bank under Decree Law 52 of 2008; an unlicensed EMI cannot open a treasury or escrow account for the MFO in Panama.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions are received as USD wires via SWIFT MT103 through correspondent banks in the United States, cleared over CHIPS or Fedwire, and credited at the Panamanian bank. The remittance instruction must include the MFO's corporate name, bank account number, and a purpose description such as 'Capital Subscription' or 'Foreign Direct Investment in Panama.' The local bank will require a board resolution approving the capital injection, certified UBO documentation, source-of-funds evidence, and the underlying investment management mandate. No local currency conversion occurs because Panama physically circulates the US dollar.Outbound trade and investment disbursements are executed through SWIFT MT103 messages using USD clearing via CHIPS/Fedwire in the New York corridor. Commercial trade disbursements may be structured as irrevocable documentary letters of credit issued by an SBP-licensed Panamanian bank under ICC UCP 600, with settlement through the issuing bank's USD correspondent network. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory dividend withholding tax on cash dividends paid out of Panamanian-source profits; 5% for dividends paid from profits subject to the SEM preferential regime or out of exempt foreign-source earnings when properly ring-fenced; bearer-share distributions are subject to an additional 4% surcharge where bearer shares remain unreformed.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months following the fiscal year-end (March 15 for calendar-year taxpayers); extension available to June 15 upon submission of a provisional return and payment of estimated tax.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 – Renta Bruta (Gross Income) Total Panamanian-source gross revenue and any taxable foreign-source revenue attributable to a permanent establishment in Panama. Report only Panama-source revenue from advisory fees, management fees, or custody-linked services rendered to Panama clients or generated by activities performed in Panama. Foreign-source fee income beneficially derived from non-resident private family office clients is excluded under territorial taxation, provided no Panamanian permanent establishment exists and no deduction is claimed for related foreign costs. Late filing penalties: minimum of USD 500 and maximum of USD 20,000 for corporations, plus a monthly surcharge of 10% of the tax due, capped at 100% of the tax due. Failure to pay triggers interest at 10% per annum. Additional sanctions include deregistration of the taxpayer ID (RUC) and criminal tax evasion liability for intentional non-filing.
Box 2 – Ingresos exentos (Exempt Income) Income exempt under the territorial system, including foreign-source portfolio advisory fees, dividends from non-Panamanian issuers, and interest from foreign securities. Clearly identify foreign-source fee income and attach a tax reconciliation statement. Do not include such amounts in taxable income; maintain underlying client contracts and source-country evidence to support the exemption. Late filing penalties: minimum of USD 500 and maximum of USD 20,000 for corporations, plus a monthly surcharge of 10% of the tax due, capped at 100% of the tax due. Failure to pay triggers interest at 10% per annum. Additional sanctions include deregistration of the taxpayer ID (RUC) and criminal tax evasion liability for intentional non-filing.
Box 3 – Costos y deducciones (Costs and Deductions) Allowable deductions strictly attributable to the generation of Panamanian-source income, including salaries, rent, professional fees, depreciation, and financial expenses paid to Panamanian vendors or for services rendered in Panama. Only expenses economically connected to Panama-source revenue are deductible. Costs incurred abroad for servicing non-resident families may not be deducted against domestic-source income. Intercompany management fees must satisfy arm's length conditions under transfer pricing rules. Late filing penalties: minimum of USD 500 and maximum of USD 20,000 for corporations, plus a monthly surcharge of 10% of the tax due, capped at 100% of the tax due. Failure to pay triggers interest at 10% per annum. Additional sanctions include deregistration of the taxpayer ID (RUC) and criminal tax evasion liability for intentional non-filing.
Box 4 – Renta neta gravable (Taxable Net Income) Gross taxable income less allowable deductions before tax credits; the base for the 25% corporate rate. Compute using statutory accounting adjusted to Panama tax principles. Losses may be carried forward 5 years but only against Panama-source taxable profits. Late filing penalties: minimum of USD 500 and maximum of USD 20,000 for corporations, plus a monthly surcharge of 10% of the tax due, capped at 100% of the tax due. Failure to pay triggers interest at 10% per annum. Additional sanctions include deregistration of the taxpayer ID (RUC) and criminal tax evasion liability for intentional non-filing.
Box 5 – Impuesto sobre la renta (Income Tax Payable) 25% of taxable net income, subject to any SEM reduced-rate benefit or withholding tax credits. Apply reduced rate only where the SEM/Sede Empresarial regime certificate is current. Otherwise apply the full 25% statutory rate. Late filing penalties: minimum of USD 500 and maximum of USD 20,000 for corporations, plus a monthly surcharge of 10% of the tax due, capped at 100% of the tax due. Failure to pay triggers interest at 10% per annum. Additional sanctions include deregistration of the taxpayer ID (RUC) and criminal tax evasion liability for intentional non-filing.
Box 6 – Créditos y retenciones (Credits and Withholdings) Advance income tax payments (anticipos), withholding tax credits, and tax paid abroad on Panama-source income if a foreign tax credit treaty or unilateral credit applies. Attach Form 2A and certificates of withholding issued by Panamanian payers. No foreign tax credit is available on exempt foreign-source income. Late filing penalties: minimum of USD 500 and maximum of USD 20,000 for corporations, plus a monthly surcharge of 10% of the tax due, capped at 100% of the tax due. Failure to pay triggers interest at 10% per annum. Additional sanctions include deregistration of the taxpayer ID (RUC) and criminal tax evasion liability for intentional non-filing.
Box 7 – Impuesto a pagar o saldo a favor (Tax Due or Credit Balance) Final balance due to the DGI or refundable/offsettable balance. Verify on-time payment via the TAN system before deadline. A minimal fee, zero-tax return is still mandatory for active family office S.A.s; failure to file imposes penalties even when no tax is due. Late filing penalties: minimum of USD 500 and maximum of USD 20,000 for corporations, plus a monthly surcharge of 10% of the tax due, capped at 100% of the tax due. Failure to pay triggers interest at 10% per annum. Additional sanctions include deregistration of the taxpayer ID (RUC) and criminal tax evasion liability for intentional non-filing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panama annually adjusts transfer pricing thresholds. For FY 2025, taxpayers are required to prepare and maintain the Local File and Master File when consolidated gross revenues or intercompany transactions exceed USD 3,000,000 (the threshold is B/.3,000,000, pegged at 1:1 to the USD). Related-party transactions with non-resident affiliates in tax havens must be documented regardless of threshold.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regime (ESR) applies under Legislative Decree No. 3 of 2023 and DGI Resolution 4500/2023. Multi-Family Offices conducting portfolio advisory and discretionary management are 'relevant activities', requiring the entity to maintain economic presence in Panama: physical office, qualified local directors/employees with appropriate seniority, board meetings and strategic decisions in Panama, and adequate operating expenditure proportionate to income. The MFO must elect the regulated license regime and file an annual Economic Substance Return (Form ES-1) alongside the income tax return. Pure foreign-income relaxation does not remove ESR duties; non-compliance triggers B/.100,000 to B/.1,000,000 fines and potential strike-off and revocation of the FIDU/AML license.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Tax Criminal Code: imprisonment from 2 to 5 years, plus fines equal to 25% to 200% of the evaded tax; permanent deregistration from the RUC and prohibition from obtaining the SEM license.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes if they signed false returns or directed diverted fee flows. Civil restitution orders and asset freezes are typical; foreign travel bans (solicitud de impedimento de salida) may be issued during criminal investigation. Conviction can trigger extradition risk in DTA/MLAT partner states.
Shell Company / Paper Entity

Statutory sanction: Strike-off under the General Companies Law; cancellation of the SEM certificate and the FIDU license; ESR penalty from B/.100,000 to B/.1,000,000; DGI may recharacterize foreign-source income as Panama-source and assess 25% tax plus interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unjust enrichment and fraudulent conveyance under Panamanian law if they knowingly dissolved or stripped the company while it retained tax obligations. Economic-substance non-compliance is increasingly treated as a management omission, triggering civil suits by affected beneficiaries and potential criminal fraud charges.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Fines up to B/.1,000,000 under the AML institutional regime; suspension or revocation of the SIA license for the family office; criminal money-laundering penalties under Law 23 of 2015 with imprisonment of 5 to 10 years for wilful managers.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers, directors, and senior managers face personal criminal liability for AML protocol failures; personal travel bans and freezing of personal bank accounts routinely accompany investigations. Beneficiaries may pierce the corporate veil to recover losses caused by director gross negligence in custody and wire oversight.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DGI may adjust the taxable base, disallow deductions, and apply a 25% tax plus a penal surcharge of 50% to 150% of the underpaid tax. Late filing of the informative TP return triggers a fine of B/.10,000 to B/.50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign intercompany agreements without supporting benchmarking are personally liable for the resulting deficiency if the corporate entity is insolvent. Tax administration may lift the corporate veil to impose personal liability where the MFO structure is found to be an alter ego of the founding family.
Dividend / Repatriation WHT Evasion

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent penalties equal to the unpaid WHT plus 10% monthly interest; fines from B/.5,000 to B/.50,000; criminal prosecution for theft of public funds where the evasion exceeds B/.100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize a dividend distribution without remitting the withholding tax are personally liable for the full amount withheld. Travel bans and personal asset seizure are available against the resident agent and each director for failure to settle the WHT obligation.
