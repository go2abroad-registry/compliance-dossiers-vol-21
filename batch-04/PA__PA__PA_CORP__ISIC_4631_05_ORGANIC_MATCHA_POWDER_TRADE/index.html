Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; taxpayer portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa) and Sistema de Gestión de Ingresos.

E-government filing portal

Any Panamanian Sociedad Anónima, including 100% foreign-owned entities, must obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) before commencing commercial operations. Procedure: (1) register the S.A. with the Registro Público de Panamá and file the municipal Aviso de Operación with the relevant Alcaldía (e.g., Municipio de Panamá); (2) appoint a resident legal representative and declare a fiscal domicile in Panama through a public deed or private document; (3) submit to the DGI the public incorporation deed, legalization of foreign shareholder/director documents, passport copies, and proof of fiscal address; (4) a domestic bank account and minimum capital deposit are recommended for the corporate record (no legal minimum capital exists); (5) the DGI issues the RUC certificate, which simultaneously enrolls the entity for income tax (ISR), ITBMS, and withholding obligations. Processing time: approximately 5–10 business days. Foreign-source-only trading entities may register as 'exportador' or remain passive RUC holders where no local taxable activity occurs.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope — the Panamanian S.A. is a taxable entity under the classical system; Panamanian ISR (Impuesto sobre la Renta) applies only to Panama-source income. Foreign-source income (procurement and resale of matcha powder entirely outside Panama, with title, risk, and payment located abroad) is exempt from ISR and dividends paid from such income are not subject to Panamanian withholding. Exception: under Law 22 of 2010, the entity loses territoriality and becomes taxable on worldwide income if 50% or more of its shares are owned by Panamanian residents or 50% or more of its gross income derives from transactions with Panamanian residents. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope — the Panamanian S.A. is a taxable entity under the classical system; Panamanian ISR (Impuesto sobre la Renta) applies only to Panama-source income. Foreign-source income (procurement and resale of matcha powder entirely outside Panama, with title, risk, and payment located abroad) is exempt from ISR and dividends paid from such income are not subject to Panamanian withholding. Exception: under Law 22 of 2010, the entity loses territoriality and becomes taxable on worldwide income if 50% or more of its shares are owned by Panamanian residents or 50% or more of its gross income derives from transactions with Panamanian residents. No pass-through default exists; the optional transparency regime may apply.

Available election forms

Panama does not employ U.S.-style check-the-box classification; the following statutory elections are available: (a) Optional Fiscal Transparency Election (Ley 22 de 2010, Art. 4) — an entity deriving 80%+ foreign-source income may elect transparent treatment, attributing income to shareholders and enabling foreign tax credit preservation; (b) SEM / Multinational Headquarters Regime (Ley 41 de 2007) — for regional headquarters providing services to related parties, granting 0% tax on qualifying services; (c) Zona Libre de Colón operator license — goods physically entering the free zone benefit from a separate territorial exemption regime; (d) Panama-Pacifico and Ciudad del Saber special tax incentive elections; (e) Small Taxpayer Fixed-Fee Scheme (Ley 42 de 2014) for annual turnover at or below USD 36,000.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis registration threshold — every person engaged in taxable transfers of goods or services in Panamanian territory must register for ITBMS prior to commencement. Small taxpayers with annual gross sales up to USD 36,000 (approx.) may elect the fixed monthly quota regime of Law 42 of 2014. An offshore matcha trading vehicle whose goods never enter Panamanian customs territory is not engaged in taxable local transfers and is not required to collect ITBMS, although RUC enrolment remains advisable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are subject to ITBMS at 0%. For the Matcha Powder Distribution model, three factual patterns support zero-rating: (a) direct physical export from Panama — requires valid DEX customs exit and transport documents; (b) transit through Zona Libre de Colón — requires ZLC entry/exit documentation evidencing that goods were not sold for domestic consumption; (c) pure offshore drop-shipment (goods shipped from origin country directly to the non-resident buyer, never entering Panama) — supported by placement of contract, risk and title outside Panama, foreign-source payment, and absence of any local taxable event; this pattern simultaneously safeguards territorial ISR treatment. The zero-rated invoice and ITBMS credit refunds are available for related local logistics, warehousing, and professional costs.

  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (including through B/L for offshore transshipment)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice from the non-resident buyer

Panama mandates electronic invoicing via the SFEP (Sistema de Facturación Electrónica de Panamá). All ITBMS-registered taxpayers must issue standardized XML e-invoices authenticated by the taxpayer's digital signature (Firma Electrónica), transmit invoices to the DGI platform in real time (or same-day batch transmission) through Sede Electrónica or certified billing software, obtain a computerized DGI acknowledgment (Acuse de Recibo), and print a QR code with authenticity verification. Pre-printed prefixed paper invoices are restricted to small taxpayers using simplified tiquete machines or the fixed-quota regime. Export invoices must be flagged 'Exportación' at 0% ITBMS and reference the supporting customs document.

Panama is fully dollarized with no exchange controls and no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure reflects standard documentary-export banking and DGI audit expectation for settlement of foreign receivables. Penalties for abusive export zero-rating or sham foreign-sourcing structures: DGI may retroactively claw back zero-rated/refunded ITBMS with monthly surcharges (1.5% per month on the unpaid tax) and compensatory interest; fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the suppressed tax under Código Fiscal Art. 751 and Law 6 of 2005; criminal tax fraud referral with imprisonment of 2 to 5 years (Código Fiscal Arts. 762–767); and retroactive requalification of foreign-source income as Panama-source under Law 22 of 2010 with assessment up to the 4-year statute of limitations (extended to 6 years where no return was filed). Export refund claims are subject to 100% documentary verification by the DGI before payment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama maintains full, unconditional dollarization with no exchange-control regime. The U.S. dollar serves as circulating medium, unit of account, and legal tender in unlimited amounts; the Balboa is a subsidiary denomination at a fixed 1:1 parity. Panama is an IMF Article VIII member and imposes no restrictions on current international transactions, no capital-account controls, no central-bank currency permits, and no investment or profit-repatriation authorization. Dividends, branch remittances, and trade proceeds may be freely transferred in USD without approval from the SBP, the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF), or Panama's Directorate General of Revenue (DGI). The sole practical convertibility or remittance hurdle is the commercial bank's internal AML/KYC clearance under Law 23 of 2015 and the beneficial-ownership transparency requirements of Law 254 of 2021.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panama imposes no surrender requirement, no compulsory conversion ratio, no domiciliation/repatriation timeline, and no identification requirement for export proceeds. Because USD is legal tender, export receipts of organic matcha powder from Panamanian territory (or, in the case of pass-through trade, non-Panamanian source income of a Panama S.A.) are retained freely in USD. Customs filings through the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) single-window system serve statistical, tariff, and trade-compliance purposes only and do not affect FX convertibility or treasury liquidity.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama has no issuing central bank. Banking, AML/CFT, and prudential supervision is exercised exclusively by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) under Decree-Law 2 of 2008 and Law 23 of 2015. Banco Nacional de Panamá acts exclusively as fiscal agent of the State and manager of public funds, without monetary authority powers. Domestic currency issuance is limited to Balboa-denominated coins (PAB, minted by the U.S. Mint), while the U.S. dollar (USD) circulates as full legal tender under the country's de jure policy of dollarization adopted via the Currency Law of 1904.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. Panama does not maintain a dedicated electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime; no entity may passport an offshore EMI license (e.g., UK FCA, EU PSD2 EMI) into Panamanian territory. A Panama S.A. trading matcha may technically open a multi-currency wallet at an unregulated neobank or foreign EMI, but such rails are not compliant primary settlement infrastructure, are frequently rejected by Japanese counterparties and correspondent banks, and generate avoidable AML red flags under Law 23 of 2015. Recommended posture: use a licensed Panamanian bank with full correspondent network for primary settlement; use Fed-wired Treasury-grade fintechs only for information-gathering or disbursement in jurisdictions with local EMI licensing (e.g., UK, Singapore) where the Panama S.A. has established a valid local branch or agent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders inject USD capital via SWIFT MT103 (or FEDWIRE/CHIPS from U.S. correspondent banks) credited to the Panama S.A.'s operational or escrow USD account at a Panama City bank. The SWIFT field-70 payment reference must state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – SHARE SUBSCRIPTION – ISIC 4631 – COMMERCIAL TRADING'. No SBP, DGI, or foreign-exchange prior approval is required because Panama has no exchange controls; however, the bank will verify sender identity matches the registered shareholder, may provisionally segregate the funds in a capital escrow account until the KYC file is completed, and must record the injection in the corporate share ledger. Commissions and dividends may later be repatriated to shareholders as non-Panamanian-source income subject solely to the territorial tax rules and any applicable dividend-withholding under the Panama Income Tax Code.Primary clearing rail for trade settlements is outbound SWIFT MT103 in USD from the Panama S.A. bank to the Japanese beneficiary's bank (e.g., MUFG Bank, SMBC, Mizuho), with FX conversion to JPY executed either by the Panamanian bank's international desk or at the receiving correspondent. For large, first-time, or credit-sensitive supplier relationships, utilize an irrevocable, confirmed commercial Letter of Credit (LC) issued through the Panama bank's trade desk (with a U.S. or Japanese confirming bank) governed by UCP 600, payable against presentation of commercial invoice, phytosanitary certificate, certificate of origin, weight/packing list, and ocean or air transport documents. Documentary collections (D/P or D/A) are suitable for repeat suppliers. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Medium. Panama does not maintain a dedicated electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime; no entity may passport an offshore EMI license (e.g., UK FCA, EU PSD2 EMI) into Panamanian territory. A Panama S.A. trading matcha may technically open a multi-currency wallet at an unregulated neobank or foreign EMI, but such rails are not compliant primary settlement infrastructure, are frequently rejected by Japanese counterparties and correspondent banks, and generate avoidable AML red flags under Law 23 of 2015. Recommended posture: use a licensed Panamanian bank with full correspondent network for primary settlement; use Fed-wired Treasury-grade fintechs only for information-gathering or disbursement in jurisdictions with local EMI licensing (e.g., UK, Singapore) where the Panama S.A. has established a valid local branch or agent.
