Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); official portal: Sede Electrónica e-Tax (https://etax.mef.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A Panamanian Sociedad Anónima must obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) through the DGI e-Tax portal after registration of the public deed (Escritura Pública) in the Registro Público de Panamá and appointment of a resident agent (agente residente). The RUC for a legal entity follows the format 3-XXX-XXXXXX-X. The initial application simultaneously triggers ITBMS registration and enrolment in the electronic invoicing system (SFEP). No minimum capital or physical local office is required; non-resident ownership is unrestricted. Foreign vendors with no local presence but with Panama domestic sales (including e-commerce via Shopify/Stripe) require RUC registration through the same portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with Territorial_Scope: corporate income tax at 25% applies solely to Panama-source income; foreign-source income is not subject to Panamanian corporate tax.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists. Available elections: (i) simplified 'Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente' for enterprises with annual gross income not exceeding PAB 300,000; (ii) Sede de Empresa Multinacional (SEM) licence under Law 41/2007 for qualified multinational export services, granting tax and immigration incentives; (iii) option to prepare the taxable basis under IFRS as accepted by DGI; (iv) election for definitive withholding on certain cross-border payments (retention regimes) under the Código Fiscal.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de-minimis threshold: every person habitually transferring taxable goods or services in Panama must register for ITBMS upon obtaining a RUC. A lower simplified-regime threshold of PAB 300,000 in annual gross income applies for classification as 'Pequeño Contribuyente', subject to a fixed monthly quota instead of standard 7% filing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods (including designer blind-box toys) and qualifying offshore services are subject to ITBMS at 0% pursuant to Código Fiscal Articles 1082-H and 1082-I. The applicable conditions are: (a) sale concluded with a non-resident buyer; (b) physical departure of the goods from Panamanian territory evidenced by a DUDA export declaration; and (c) receipt of the export proceeds from abroad. ITBMS paid on inputs attributable to such export sales generates a creditable surplus (saldo a favor) refundable or offsettable against other tax liabilities.

  • DUDA export customs declaration (Formulario Único de Aduanas) approved by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exit from Panamanian territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Remesa del Exterior) issued by the Panamanian receiving bank
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales contract/purchase order
  • Export certificate and packaging list for customs verification where applicable

Mandatory SFEP (Sistema de Facturación Electrónica de Panamá) under DGI Resolution 201-3804 of 2021. Taxpayers transmit invoices in real time via the e-Tax API; the DGI authorizes each invoice by returning a unique authorization code and embedded QR code. Large taxpayers have been mandatory since January 2022; all other registered taxpayers are phased into mandatory electronic issuance. Failure to obtain fiscal clearance nullifies the invoice for ITBMS input-credit purposes.

DGI audit teams cross-check the DUDA, bills of lading, and bank remittance certificates against the ITBMS return. If export proceeds are not repatriated and credited in Panama within 180 days, or if the export documentation is inconsistent, the transaction is re-characterized as a domestic taxable sale and assessed at 7% ITBMS, plus a 10% monthly surcharge under Article 716, default interest of 1.5% per month (or current legal DGI rate), and, in cases of fraud or fictitious exports, fines of up to 100% of the omitted tax under Article 760 and potential criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Law 1/2021 with imprisonment of 1 to 5 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and effective convertibility without capital controls. Current account and capital account transactions are equally freely convertible because the monetary base is USD itself; the PAB is nominal and counterside to the USD. Profit remittance and dividend distributions face no exchange-control barrier; a territorial-tax entity withholding outbound dividends to non-resident shareholders is generally exempt provided the beneficiary is not a Panamanian-sourced activity and formal documentation is filed. Anti-money-laundering (AML) Bank Secrecy Law obligations may delay large wire transfers until the underlying corporate beneficial-owner structure is disclosed to the bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirement. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no export-receipt deposit obligation. Export proceeds in USD may be retained offshore, used for import settlement, or repatriated at the exporter's discretion. Administrative reporting applies only to cash transactions above USD 10,000 and to the SBP/UAF (Unidad de Análisis Financiero) threshold filings, not to FX conversion.

Central bank supervision: No central bank exists in the Republic of Panama. The anchor currency is the US Dollar (USD) as legal tender under Decree-Law of 1904; the Balboa (PAB) exists solely as divisional coins pegged 1:1. Banking and FX oversight is exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), while macroeconomic policy and SOE fiscal agency rest with the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF). There is no Foreign Exchange Administration; exchange operations are unregulated free-market activities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Moderate feasibility, with structured constraints. Panama's legal framework (Law 15 of 2019 and SBP regulations on electronic payment systems) permits licensed 'Sociedades de Emisión y Medios de Pago Electrónicos' (EMIs) to issue e-money and payment instruments in USD. For the designer-toy merchant, platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, dLocal, and Banking-as-a-Service rails via local banking partners can serve as collection and disbursement adjuncts; however, they should not replace a Tier-1 commercial bank. Key constraints: (i) settlement to a Panamanian corporate account requires a local partner bank; (ii) UAF and SBP transaction monitoring applies to e-money flows and cross-border settlement (over USD 10,000 triggers enhanced reports); (iii) onboarding of a holding S.A. holding electronically signed board resolutions is accepted by leading EMIs but generally requires a passport, apostilled incorporation certificate, and a UBO declaration. A full treasury reliance on an offshore EMI without a local bank account is legally imprudent and operationally fragile.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (general corporate rate, Art. 699 Código Fiscal as amended by Law 8 of 2010); graduated micro scale: 15% on the first B/.50,000 of net taxable income for fiscally small companies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% final withholding on dividends and profit distributions paid out of profits that have been taxed at the corporate level (Art. 733-734 Código Fiscal); 10% where distributions derive from exempt or foreign-source funds; additional advance-dividend WHT is prepaid at the corporate level (5% for taxed profits / 10% exempt profits) and imputed as a credit against the 5%-10% definitive tax on the shareholder.

Within three months after the close of each fiscal year; a calendar-year company must file no later than 31 March of the following year; up to two 3-month extensions may be sought with prior DGI approval but tax must be provisionally paid when extensions are granted.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.2,000,000 (USD 2 million, fixed Balboa parity) in aggregate related-party cross-border transactions during a fiscal year triggers mandatory (a) Form 425 annual transfer pricing return (due 30 June following the fiscal close) and (b) preparation and surrender on demand of the Local File (Informe Local de Precios de Transferencia) and Master File (Informe Maestro); transactions between the S.A. and its foreign licensor parent for the 'DEADLY BLIND BOX' artwork IP, procurement of vinyl resin and bulk freight are the relevant lines.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not impose a UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulation; the doctrine enters Panamanian law through (1) Art. 694 territorial sourcing (profits must arise from actual panameño-based operations), (2) the SEM/HQ regime (Law 41/2007, Decree 3/2016) requiring genuine office, local payroll and senior management decisions in-country, (3) Law 47 of 2013 prohibiting anonymous bearer shares - all bearer shares must be immobilized and beneficial ownership filed in the Shareholders' Register at the Public Registry, and (4) the OECD Inclusive Framework commitment that forces DGI to deny treaty/regime advantages to a shell. A rotocasting/injection workshop S.A. that can show physical meters, utility bills, form 401 payroll declarations and Customs export records satisfies the doctrine; an S.A. acting purely as a Shopify invoicing pass-through with no inventory or staff is recharacterized as wholly artificial and loses the territorial exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Defraudación Fiscal

Statutory sanction: Criminal defraudación fiscal (Código Fiscal Arts. 712-C to 712-E as amended by Law 76 of 2019): prison term of 2 to 5 years and pecuniary fine of twice to ten times the tax evaded, plus reinstated tax and non-waivable interest at 1.5% monthly; DGI also files the 'Lista de Contribuyentes en Mora' blocking import/export screening and VAT (ITBMS) licences.

Director personal liability: Directors or legal representatives who sign or consent to the false Form 401 are deemed handlers of the evasion (Código Fiscal Art. 712-G): joint and several civil liability for the corporate debt; levying preventive embargo of personal accounts, a 180-day travel ban pending indictment, and extradition if the director is a foreign national. The Public Registry may cancel the S.A.'s licence upon DGI certification.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: For Law 47 of 2013 bearer-share breaches: fine from B/.5,000 to B/.50,000 and the shares become unenforceable; commercial operations may be suspended by the Superintendencia de Empresas (SEP?); tax consequences: elimination of territorial exclusion, reclassification of all global sales as Panamanian (25% ISR) and automatic 10% dividend WHT on distributions from reclassified funds.

Director personal liability: Veil piercing under the fraud-in-the-law doctrine (Reforma de la Ley 5 de 2009?): directors jointly liable for the full tax difference; the DGI can embargo personal assets without court order when the S.A. is reduced to an alter ego; directors are flagged in the UAF database, and during criminal proceedings the Travel Judge (Juzgado de Garantías) may order an arrest warrant for the beneficial owner (non-punishable shell scheme) if linked to AML predicate proceeds.
Anti-Money Laundering / FINANCING OF INTELLECTUAL-PROPERTY TRADING (AML Case)

Statutory sanction: Law 23 of 2015 (as reformed by Law 70 of 2019) criminalizes money laundering with 5 to 12 years' imprisonment (sg. 15 years for organized crime facilitators), extinción de dominio (forfeiture) of all traced proceeds, corporate/institutional fines up to B/.5 million, and cancellation of the company's licence to operate; the S.A. also loses the territorial exception because laundered funds are constructively Panama-source.

Director personal liability: Under the 2019 reforms, a director who manages, finances or conceals the operation of a laundering scheme is a 'autore de lavado' — no corporate veil available: preventive detention, restraining orders over passports, freezing of linked commercial accounts, and disqualification from holding positions in any regulated Panamanian entity for 10 years.
Withholding Tax and Dividend Distribution Defaults

Statutory sanction: The unpaid WHT becomes an immediate personal statutory debt of the S.A. (Art. 733); surcharge of 100% of the deficiency (Art. 744? — actually 'recargo del 100%' applies to withheld amounts never declared) plus 1.5% monthly interest, publication in the 'morosidad' register, and possible reconduction of the shareholders to the 35% patrimonial tax regime on deemed distributions.

Director personal liability: Corporate officers are personally liable when they were the withholding agents in fact: joint and several liability for the withheld amounts, penalty, interest and procedural costs; failure to comply triggers a personal 'visa bar' via the DGI-Migración interlock, meaning the responsible director cannot travel on Panamanian passports or renew work permits until full settlement is secured.
