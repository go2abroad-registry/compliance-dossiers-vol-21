Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office address is accepted for mailing operations and client-facing communications, but every Panamanian S.A. must permanently maintain a registered office address at the premises of a licensed resident agent (abogado o fiduciario). Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; principal e-portal 'TACA' and e-invoicing platform at https://etax.mef.gob.pa; institutional information at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned Panamanian S.A. must first be incorporated in the Registro Público de Panamá and obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación. Thereafter, the entity, through an authorized public accountant, files the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) application with the DGI using Formulario DGI-RUC, submitted via the e-Tax/TACA portal or at a DGI taxpayer service center. Required exhibits: certified copy of the public deed of incorporation, filed articles of incorporation and bylaws, certificate of the registered agent and legal representative, evidence of local domicile, municipal operation notice, and passport copies of officers/shareholders. A non-resident shareholder is permitted; the DGI issues the 11-digit RUC within approximately 10-15 business days. For non-resident digital platform operators without a Panamanian establishment, the DGI additionally operates a special electronic registration procedure under the digital services rules (Proveedor de Servicios Digitales no establecido) that issues a dedicated ITBMS payer ID without requiring an in-country fiscal representative for income tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope. A Panamanian S.A. is per se a taxable corporation subject to the 25% flat corporate income tax (ISR) solely on Panamanian-source income; foreign-source income, including SaaS export revenue from the SEO analytics tool, is outside the taxable base. No pass-through or transparent treatment is available; the entity is not eligible for check-the-box classification.

Available election forms

Panama has no US-style check-the-box election; the S.A. is statutorily a taxable corporation. Available voluntary elections include: (i) Sede de Empresa Multinacional (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007 for qualifying multinational operational headquarters claiming special tax incentives; (ii) the simplified small-taxpayer ITBMS regime (Régimen de Pequeños Contribuyentes) available to entities with annual gross receipts up to PAB 300,000; (iii) opting to be an authorized electronic invoicing issuer (Proveedor de Facturación Electrónica) under DGI administrative authorizations; and (iv) for hybrid mismatch planning, Panama does not apply OECD hybrid rules, but tax treaty/credit elections may be relevant only for source-country withholding positions. The entity may also elect to constitute itself as an SEM or remain under the general territorial regime without further filing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover exemption for persons habitually engaged in taxable activities; ITBMS registration is obligatory upon commencement of operations. However, taxpayers with annual gross income up to PAB 300,000 (USD 300,000) may elect the simplified small-taxpayer ITBMS regime (percentage-of-sales basis), while those exceeding PAB 300,000 must register under the general regime with full input-output credit mechanics. Non-resident digital service suppliers must register for ITBMS without any threshold when supplying digital services (including SaaS analytics tools) to non-business Panamanian consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of services, including digital content, data analytics, and SaaS tools, are zero-rated for ITBMS under the territorial principle of Law 8 of 2010 (Article 3 and 56-A) and Decree 124 of 2010. To claim 0%, the SEO analytics service must be rendered to a recipient domiciled outside Panama, the benefit or economic use of the service must occur entirely abroad, and the consideration must be payable by a non-resident. The DGI recharacterizes as domestic any digital service whose use is effectively exploited in Panama (including via Panamanian-resident users); therefore, the exporter must maintain a written declaration of foreign domicile and end-use documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera – DUA) and its DGI export settlement; for digital SaaS services the DUA is replaced by the electronic 'Factura de Exportación de Servicios' submitted in the DGI SFE module with export transaction code.
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (applicable only to tangible goods; not required for the pure digital SEO analytics tool, but must be retained if any parity or hardware is physically exported).
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / payment processor settlement report (Stripe, Paddle) evidencing collection of the export invoice in a foreign payment stream and the foreign-domiciled payer.
  • Signed service agreement with the non-resident client, clearly stating the cross-border nature, place of use, and ITBMS 0% treatment.
  • Certificate of tax residence or equivalent non-residence evidence of the foreign client (e.g., foreign VAT/GST registration number or non-resident declaration).
  • DGI export invoice (Factura de Exportación) bearing the DGI authorization code and QR, together with proof of filing of the monthly ITBMS return declaring the zero-rated turnover.

Panama mandates electronic invoicing through the DGI's Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (SFE). Every invoice, credit note, debit note, and export document (Facturación de Exportación) must be transmitted in real time to the DGI via the public JSON/XSD API or the free web/mobile issuer module before delivery to the customer. The DGI validates and returns an approval code ('Código de Seguridad de Facturación Electrónica') and a QR code that must be printed on the invoice; no invoice is legally valid for ITBMS credit or sales without such digital fiscal clearance. The issuer must assign a Series and Correlative Number per issuance point, use the DGI classification of 'Tipo de Operación 02' for export services, and retain a 'Documento de Facturación Electrónica (DFE)' record for at least 5 years. Offline issuance is permitted only for the web-light module under defined DGI conditions, and all records must synchronize within the prescribed calendar timeline.

The DGI cross-references e-invoicing data, banking records of the Panamanian S.A., and international payment processor reports to verify that the reported zero-rated exports are truly collected from foreign clients. If the entity fails to evidence receipt of export proceeds, or the service is found to be effectively used in Panama, the DGI retroactively recharacterizes the sale as taxable domestically at the relevant ITBMS rate together with monthly default interest, statutory surcharges, and fines. Under the Código Fiscal (Articles 713 et seq., as reinforced by Law 70 of 2019), tax fraud penalties range from 25% to 200% of the omitted tax, with criminal liability for fraudulent zero-rating claims carrying imprisonment up to five years; false electronic invoicing or unauthorized analog invoicing attracts additional administrative fines between PAB 50 and PAB 5,000 per document, and the DGI may suspend the exporter's taxpayer ID and authorization codes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both capital and current accounts. The U.S. Dollar is legal tender under Ley 72 de 1944 and circulates freely. There are no exchange controls restricting profit remittance, dividend repatriation, or capital outflows for a Panama Sociedad Anónima. Non-resident shareholders may distribute dividends without FX approval, subject only to standard corporate and dividend tax obligations under Panama's territorial tax regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender requirements and no mandatory conversion timelines. Exporters and service providers may retain all foreign-currency revenue in any commercial account, convert freely, or reinvest offshore without any central-bank oversight.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) – Panama has no classic central bank, and the SBP is the primary banking and foreign-exchange supervisory authority; monetary policy is effectively set by the U.S. Federal Reserve as the U.S. Dollar is legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. Panama grants no passporting rights to foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs); an offshore EMI can only provide services as a correspondent payment processor if it maintains a local bank settlement account at a licensed Panamanian bank. The SBP does not recognize a general 'EMI sandbox' for non-resident fintechs, so a physical local presence (or branch relationship) is legally necessary for full deposit-taking or account-issuance. As a third-level middleware partner, an EMI may image invoices and initiate wire transfers via SWIFT, but the actual Panamanian bank account must remain with a locally licensed institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection is executed via SWIFT MT103 / MT202 in U.S. Dollars. The shareholder's bank shall use a purpose code indicating 'capital contribution' (e.g., standard ISO 20022 remittance context or bilateral instruction). For a Panama S.A., the receiving bank requires a notarized-board-resolution authorizing the injerto/equity, a completed source-of-funds form, and a sworn statement that the funds are not related to illicit activities. There is no pre-approval from the SBP for inbound USD; the bank's internal compliance team validates the UBO and the exact legal-entity name before crediting the operational or escrow account.For trade settlements, SWIFT MT103 remains the primary cross-border clearing rail. For outbound SaaS vendor subscriptions, dedicated card-rail payouts (Mastercard/Visa business credit cards) are common, while commercial Letters of Credit are reserved for high-value physical-goods resellers. For software localization subcontractors, banks permit ACH domestic wires in USD; for international contractor payments, MT103 via the correspondent bank network is the only universally accepted rail. To maximize speed and minimise fees, the Panama S.A. may maintain a settlement account with a U.S correspondent bank (e.g., Bank of New York Mellon or Citibank N.A.) linked to its Panamanian operating account, allowing intraday USD clearing.USD/Balboa is a legal-tender duality; no hedging is required for local-currency exposure.For EUR revenue received via merchant aggregators, Panamanian banks may execute EUR/USD spot contracts and rolling EUR/USD forward contracts (up to 12 months) with the domestic counterparty.For GBP/JPY/CHF exposure, NDFs (Non-Deliverable Forwards) referencing USD are permissible through licensed banks, provided the underlying transaction is evidenced by a signed commercial contract.Currency swaps (USD vs. EUR or USD vs GBP) are available to corporate clients meeting minimum notional thresholds (typically USD 5 million) and having a verified treasury risk management policy.Broad-based cross-currency options (barrier or vanillas) are available for non-speculative hedging purposes after completion of a derivatives capability assessment by the bank's risk committee.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% flat corporate income tax (ISR) under Ley 8 de 2010, Article 699 of the Código Fiscal, applied to Panama-source net taxable income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% definitive withholding on dividends paid out of Panama-source profits already subject to corporate ISR; 10% withholding on dividends paid from profits that were exempt or foreign-source at the entity level. Resident and non-resident shareholders face identical Panama statutory rates.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within the first three months following the fiscal year-end. For a calendar-year S.A. the deadline is 31 March; for an off-calendar fiscal year closed on 30 June the deadline is 30 September.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: In Panamanian balboas (PAB, pegged 1:1 to USD), comprehensive transfer pricing documentation (Local File and related-party transaction declaration) is required when annual related-party transactions exceed PAB 250,000; a Master File and benchmarking study are expected when cross-border related-party revenue exceeds PAB 5,000,000. The Form 1111 related-party annex must be filed in all cases where controlled transactions exist.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations code like UAE or BVI, but territorial-source doctrine and DGI anti-abuse practice impose equivalent substance expectations: a Panama S.A. claiming foreign-source SaaS income or treaty benefits must demonstrate local management, payroll or contracted engineering, office or registered operation, bank accounts, board decisions in Panama, and an active business purpose. Law 254 de 2021 mandates beneficial ownership disclosure, and letterbox or conduit structures distributing untaxed digital revenue are challenged under general anti-abuse principles and judicial veil-piercing doctrine.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Omission of Panama-Source Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud under Articles 765-769 of the Código Fiscal: fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the evaded tax, plus imprisonment of 2 to 7 years; civil reassessment of the full Panama-source revenue at 25% ISR together with the 1% monthly surcharge (up to 24%) and interest; permanent denial of the 'Visto Bueno' fiscal solvency certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent who signed false returns face joint and several civil restitution of evaded tax and penalties; DGI can impose an exit ban ('impedimento de salida') preventing travel; a criminal conviction carries imprisonment and disqualification from serving as a director in any Panama corporation.
Shell Company Abusive Structure / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DGI recharacterization of revenue as Panama-source under substance-over-form, imposing 25% ISR, late-filing surcharges, and a 10% dividend tax on any undistributed attribution; the Tax Authority and the Superintendencia de Sujetos No Financieros can suspend the entity's tax identification number and strike the company from the Registro Público for non-payment of the annual franchise fee.

Director personal liability: Judicial piercing of the corporate veil under Panamanian fraud doctrine holds shareholders and directors personally liable for corporate tax debts; involved directors face civil recovery actions, travel bans, and criminal prosecution for fraud where the shell was used to evade third-party creditors or tax.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: DGI re-determination of the arm's length price with upward adjustment of Panama taxable income at 25%; administrative penalties per infraction of up to B/.5,000; denial of deductions for undocumented related-party costs; daily surcharges apply for the unreported transaction.

Director personal liability: The CFO or authorized representative signing the incomplete annex is personally liable for penalties attributable to willful omission; repeated violations trigger a criminal fraud case, personal asset freezing, and a travel embargo.
Unlawful Dividend Distribution Without Withholding

Statutory sanction: The undistributed WHT is assessed against the S.A. with a 1% monthly surcharge and statutory interest; additional fines of B/.500 to B/.5,000 for each unfiled dividend statement; the distribution is treated as illegal and non-deductible.

Director personal liability: Directors who resolve the dividend payment without withholding become jointly and severally liable for the gross-up of the dividend tax; criminal tax withholding evasion is punishable by imprisonment of up to 3 years and a personal travel ban.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance (Law 23 de 2015 and Law 254 de 2021)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of B/.10,000 to B/.1,000,000 depending on the infringement tier; suspension of the corporate license; the Superintendencia may order the blocking of bank accounts and payment-rail settlements; criminal AML penalties apply in cases of intentional concealment of beneficial ownership.

Director personal liability: Officers who sign false beneficial ownership declarations face personal criminal liability, fines, disqualification from management, and imprisonment of up to 5 years; civil forfeiture of assets traceable to the undisclosed structure is available to the authorities.
