E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first incorporate the Sociedad Anónima and register in the Registro Público. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Available election forms

Panama has no check-the-box election. S.A. corporations are automatically taxed on Panama-source income only. Available elections include the small-taxpayer simplified ITBMS regime (annual gross receipts under USD 300,000) and the exporter refund regime permitting ITBMS credits on local purchases. For income tax, taxpayers may propose transfer-pricing advanced pricing agreements with the DGI.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold; any person engaged in economic activity must register for ITBMS. A simplified regime applies to small taxpayers with annual gross receipts up to USD 300,000. Exporters must register as 'exportador' and may apply for customs and DGI export authorization.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical goods are zero-rated under ITBMS. The seller must be registered as an exporter, the goods must physically leave Panama within a commercially reasonable period, and title must pass to a non-resident. Offshore services related to the sale (e.g., transport, logistics) are also zero-rated when provided to the foreign buyer. Documentation must demonstrate the goods were received overseas and payment was collected in foreign currency.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Panama has a mandatory electronic invoicing system (Facturación Electrónica) through the DGI's SGI platform. All taxpayers must issue e-invoices in XML format, transmit them in real time (or batch for small taxpayers), and include a digital stamp (QR/HASH) generated by the DGI. Export invoices must be submitted through the same electronic system and linked to customs declarations.

The DGI conducts targeted audits of export zero-rating claims. If documentation is missing or inadequate, the export is recharacterized as a local sale, and ITBMS becomes due retroactively plus late-payment interest and penalties up to 100% of the tax. Fraudulent submissions may lead to additional sanctions under the Tax Code, revocation of the exporter status, and criminal liability for tax fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates under full and unrestricted currency convertibility because the U.S. dollar is legal tender and the national balboa exists only as book-keeping currency. Capital and current accounts are freely convertible; there are no exchange-control laws, no central-bank exchange-rate approvals, no capital repatriation restrictions, and no payment sequencing rules for profit remittances. Remittances are executed through ordinary USD wire transfers. The only meaningful hurdles are tax-compliant substance and documentary proof: dividend or profit remittances by a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima must be grounded in corporate resolutions, distributable-profit computations, and bank due-diligence source-of-funds documentation; such remittances may also trigger Panama’s dividend withholding tax obligations, which is a tax matter rather than an FX restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panamanian law imposes no mandatory surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, no statutory deposit period, and no requirement to route export proceeds through a local Panamanian account. Because the USD is the circulating legal tender, export proceeds may be freely retained, deposited, reinvested, or remitted abroad. Nevertheless, an exporter should maintain complete documentary support, including commercial invoices, packing lists, transport documents, customs export declarations, and payment-processor settlement records, to satisfy SBP AML/CFT requirements and correspondent-bank due diligence.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the banking supervisor and prudential authority. Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the fiscal agent and clearing institution. There is no central bank and no centralised foreign-exchange administration because the U.S. dollar is legal tender in Panama.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: Medium for payment-acceptance fintechs and Low for offshore digital EMIs as bank substitutes. Stripe and Affirm BNPL are acceptable as payment aggregators that can settle omnichannel e-commerce receipts into a Panamanian corporate USD account. However, an offshore EMI not licensed by SBP cannot lawfully maintain customer deposits or act as the primary 'bank account of record' for a Panamanian S.A. within the Treasury structure. Fintechs may apply to operate within SBP’s 'Centro de Innovación Digital' under Law No. 7 of 2022, but final clearing and safeguarding of corporate cash must still be performed by a licensed Panamanian bank or a highly rated correspondent bank. Therefore, the blueprint should treat offshore EMIs strictly as collection-rail technology, not as treasury-clearing counterparties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be completed through compliant international USD wire transfers: SWIFT MT103 for cross-border instructions or a Fedwire/CHIPS credit through the corresponding U.S. bank to the Panamanian beneficiary account. The wire must clearly state the economic purpose, e.g., 'Share Subscription / Paid-in Capital Contribution', 'Capital Contribution Under Corporate Resolution', or 'Intra-Group Contribution'. Panamanian law does not require central-bank registration or FX-form approval, but the receiving bank’s AML/CDD team will expect the corporate resolution, updated share registry, UBO declaration, and Source of Funds evidence before clearing the funds. The final balance must be booked into the capital escrow or operational USD account and afterwards recorded in the S.A.’s statutory corporate books.Outbound trade disbursements for oversized-furniture procurement should be executed through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for supplier payments and commercial documentary Letters of Credit (typically SWIFT MT700, MT707 for amendments) for high-value purchase orders. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 250,000 (Panamanian Balboa) in aggregate related-party transactions within a fiscal year triggers mandatory Local File documentation and a sworn disclosure. A Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds B/. 20,000,000. Lower thresholds may apply for transactions with apparently tax-haven resident counterparties.

Economic substance scope: Panama applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) through Law 254 of 2019 and its implementing Decrees 794 of 2020, 479 of 2021, and modification Law 30 of 2021. Legal entities engaged in 'Relevant Activities' - including distribution and service centres, financing, leasing, headquarter, shipping, and investment holding - must demonstrate that core income-generating activities take place in Panama, with adequate premises, employees, and management presence in Panama. An online furniture retailer with local warehouse, local logistics staff, and decision-making in Panama satisfies the test. If operations are conducted purely from a foreign office with only a registered agent in Panama, the DGI may treat the company as a 'sociedad sin sustancia sustancial', impose a fine of up to B/. 100,000, withdraw the entity's tax residency status, and apply penalties to all related entities. Foreign-owned S.A. groups should annually document board meetings, employment structure, and physical business locations. In addition, bearer shares have been fully suppressed and UBO registration with the UAF is mandatory. A failure to file an annual economic substance report is an independent violation subject to periodic penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Under-Omission of Panama-Source Revenue

Statutory sanction: Administrative assessment of evaded tax, plus surcharge of 20% to 40% of the omitted tax, monthly late-payment interest, and administrative fines up to B/. 50,000. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Código Fiscal Articles 706 and 738 carries imprisonment of 2 to 10 years. Costs of audit and expert fees are assessed against the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, finance manager, and directors who signed the false declaration are jointly and severally liable for the restitution of the evaded tax. Personal assets may be embargoed, travel bans issued to prevent flight, and the responsible officers may be subject to pre-trial detention if the amount exceeds B/. 150,000.
Shell Company with No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Law 254 of 2019 and Decreto 479 de 2021, the entity is classified as lacking economic substance: annual penalty from B/. 10,000 to B/. 100,000, mandatory correction within 45 days, and, if ignored, administrative dissolution. The company may also be denied DTA benefits and prohibited from opening bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and the registered agent may face professional disciplinary liability for knowingly operating a shell. If the shell is found to hide beneficial ownership, the directors may be prosecuted for money laundering under the Penal Code, with penalties of 5 to 12 years of imprisonment, personal asset forfeiture, and travel bans.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance in Cross-Border Related-Party Transactions

Statutory sanction: The DGI may adjust the taxable income to arm's length plus apply a surcharge of 25% to 40% of the omitted tax, impose fines from B/. 10,000 to B/. 100,000, and deny deductions for the undocumented transaction. Interest and late payment surcharges continue until payment.

Director personal liability: The finance director and local legal representative are personally liable for the additional tax if they signed an incomplete transfer pricing declaration or authorised transactions without economic documentation. Civil liability extends to the recovery of the tax plus interest, and criminal liability for false tax evidence may result in imprisonment up to 6 years.
Dividend Withholding Tax Evasion and Unlawful Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: The S.A. is liable for the full unpaid dividend withholding tax, plus a 100% surcharge, statutory interest, and administrative fines. The DGI has authority to attach the company's bank accounts and enforce through summary proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the clandestine distribution are jointly and severally liable as the withholding agents. Personal liability includes repayment of the unpaid tax and penalties; travel bans and civil embargo of personal real estate may be ordered. If the distribution scheme is done with fraudulent intent, directors face up to 7 years of imprisonment for tax fraud.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: Offenses under Law 23 of 2015 and UAF Resolution 05-2014: financial penalties up to USD 1,000,000 for regulated financial and non-financial persons, suspension of operations, and compelled deregistration of the entity. The government may impose an economic substance visit and freeze bank accounts for 3 to 6 months during investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers who enable laundering of proceeds from e-commerce fraud or knowingly conceal ownership are criminally liable for money laundering under Articles 254 to 256 of the Panamanian Penal Code: 5 to 12 years of imprisonment, final forfeiture of personal properties, travel restrictions, and disqualification from any corporate office in Panama thereafter.
