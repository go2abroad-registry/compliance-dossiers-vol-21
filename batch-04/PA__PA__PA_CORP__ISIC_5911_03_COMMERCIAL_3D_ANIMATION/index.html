Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas, Panama; online portal eTax 2.0 via https://etax.dgi.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity must apply for a Panama Tax ID (RUC) through an appointed resident agent, submitting articles of incorporation, public registry certificate, identity documents of directors and beneficial owners, and evidence of registered office; DGI issues RUC after proving residence of the agent and opening a fiscal file; for non-resident electronic service suppliers, simplified registration via eTax may be allowed when a Panama business establishment is created.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope; Panamanian corporations are taxed only on Panama-source income; foreign-source active business income is outside the net income tax base; entity itself remains a separate taxpayer and not eligible for a United States-style check-the-box pass-through election.

Available election forms

No general check-the-box election exists; taxpayers may elect optional consolidated filing regime upon approval for groups with common ownership; may elect to be treated as a small taxpayer (Pequeño Contribuyente) under the simplified ITBMS regime if annual turnover does not exceed the legal threshold; holding companies may elect special tax status under applicable regimes (e.g., Multinational Headquarters) only upon separate statutory qualification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Registro obligatorio sin umbral de ventas para toda persona que realice actividades gravadas; no obstante, existe régimen simplificado para pequeños contribuyentes con ventas brutas anuales hasta USD 36,000 (umbral referencial); for non-resident digital services, registration is required from the first B2C supply with Panamanian consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Panama and services rendered entirely abroad or provided to non-resident clients for use/benefit outside Panama are zero-rated for ITBMS, provided the taxpayer remains a registered VAT/ITBMS taxpayer and the export is properly documented. Commercial CGI/3D animation services produced in Panama for a foreign broadcaster/platform are considered an export of services when the legal domicile of the client is outside Panama and the economic use occurs abroad, and may thus benefit from 0% VAT and input credit refunds.

  • Panama Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Mercancías de Exportación)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill with consignee outside Panama (for physical deliverables)
  • Commercial invoice and signed international service contract
  • Certificate of Non-Receipt / proof of delivery or acceptance by the foreign client
  • Bank transfer or SWIFT advice evidencing inward payment of export proceeds

All taxpayers are required to issue electronic invoices through the DGI authorization system (Facturación Electrónica), with pre-clearance of invoice numbering and a digital fiscal code; real-time electronic transmission to the tax authority is mandatory for taxpayers in the general regime, including B2B invoices; PDF/XML formats must include the DGI-expedited authorization number and QR code; foreign suppliers of digital services must self-declare through eTax 2.0 without local invoicing.

Panama's tax authority may retroactively reclassify zero-rated exports as taxable supply if the exporter fails to deliver documentary proof or if the foreign-consume test is not met; penalties include a 50% surcharge on the omitted ITBMS, default interest, fines up to USD 10,000 for missing e-invoice records, and clawback of VAT refunds; repeated fraud may trigger criminal tax liability and suspension of the taxpayer's operating license.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarised economy (the US dollar is the legal tender). There is no separate national currency, no exchange-control authority, and no dual-tier market. Both capital and current account transactions are freely convertible, and profit remittances require no prior exchange-reserve approval. Practical hurdles are limited to bank-level AML/KYC reporting and documentary evidence of the commercial substance of the transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Because Panama uses the US dollar as legal tender, export receipts are already in the domestic currency equivalent; there is no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to remit export proceeds to a designated account. Exporters retain free disposition of receipts.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Banking Superintendent of Panama) acting as the banking and foreign-exchange oversight body; the Republic has no central bank in the conventional sense, and Banco Nacional de Panamá serves as the fiscal agent.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Panama does not yet provide a dedicated electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on Panamanian-source taxable income with a territorial scope; income derived from foreign sources is outside the Panamanian corporate income tax base.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends distributed by a Panama S.A. to shareholders, resident or non-resident, out of taxable Panama-source profits. The withholding is borne by the paying corporation and is separate from the 25% corporate income tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB/USD 2,000,000 in annual controlled transactions with related parties triggers mandatory Local File documentation; a Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for multinational groups meeting consolidated revenue thresholds under Panama's transfer-pricing implementing rules. Below the threshold, related-party transactions must still be supported by adequate functional and pricing analysis if the DGI initiates a review.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 254 of 2021 and enabling rules) apply to entities carrying on geographically mobile relevant activities. A Panama S.A. deriving income from 3D animation/VFX production, licensing of CGI content, or related service contracts must demonstrate real management, physical premises, qualified employees, and locally incurred operating expenditures. A pure tax-optimized shell without substance will be denied territorial benefits and treated as economically present under the profit-attribution rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion under Panama-Source Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Administrative correction of tax, default interest at 1% monthly, surcharge of up to 50% of the omitted tax, and criminal sanctions under Panama Criminal Code and Fiscal Code including imprisonment between two and six years and a fine up to the amount of tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who sign a false declaration or participate in the misclassification are jointly and severally liable for the tax, interest, and penalties. Courts may pierce the S.A. veil for abuse of legal personality, order civil restitution, freeze personal assets, and impose travel bans pending recovery.
Shell Company / Artificial Territoriality Arrangements

Statutory sanction: DGI may disregard the juridical entity, re-source the revenue as Panama-source, deny the foreign-source exemption, assess the 25% corporate tax, apply a 50% fraud surcharge, and impose economic-substance penalties under Law 254 of 2021.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil-piercing applies when the S.A. is used as an instrument of fraud. Nominee directors face civil restitution, disqualification from serving as directors in Panama, asset seizures, and criminal liability for false declarations filed with the public registry.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: DGI may recharacterize the cross-border transaction at arm's length, adjust taxable income, impose late interest and a penalty of up to 100% of the transfer-pricing adjustment, and assess separate fines for non-filing of documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or execute non-compliant intercompany contracts are jointly responsible for the tax deficiency where they acted with knowledge of the underpayment. Court orders may include restitution, freezing of director accounts, and prohibition on leaving Panama until the assessed liability is secured.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Noncompliance

Statutory sanction: Panama Law 23 of 2015 imposes monetary fines on legal entities and responsible officers; the Criminal Code's money-laundering provisions carry 5 to 12 years imprisonment and confiscation of assets derived from or related to the offence.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be prosecuted personally for failing to identify the beneficial owner or for facilitating anonymous financial flows. Remedies include civil forfeiture, restitution orders, travel restrictions, and disqualification from regulated industries.
