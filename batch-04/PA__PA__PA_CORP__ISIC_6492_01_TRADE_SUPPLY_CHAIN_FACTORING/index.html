Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement under Panamanian law; the company must issue at least one share of any nominal value. Paid-up capital may be nominal or zero, and no escrow account is required for incorporation.

For a dedicated office used for financial operations, fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and basic workplace safety signage must be installed and certified before the municipality issues final operation approval.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); online services via Sede Electrónica DGI (https://dgi.gob.pa).

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide check-the-box or pass-through elections for S.A.s; the S.A. is a per se corporation taxed at entity level only on Panama-source income under the territorial system. Available statutory elections include: (i) the simplified small-taxpayer regime under Law 83/2015 (Régimen Especial Simplificado) where annual gross revenue is below the DGI-published cap; (ii) special elective regimes such as Multinational Headquarters (SEM, Law 41/2007), Colon Free Zone, Panapass, and City of Knowledge; (iii) election to allocate/prorate common expenses between taxable Panama-source income and exempt foreign-source income; and (iv) election to issue zero-rated electronic invoices with DGI hash/QR stamping for documented export transactions. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized; Panama has not enacted BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies: every person engaged in taxable transfers of goods, taxable services, or imports must register for ITBMS (RUC-linked) before commencing operations. A reduced-compliance track (Régimen Especial Simplificado, Law 83/2015) is available for qualifying small taxpayers below the statutory annual gross-receipts cap published by DGI, but exporters, financial entities, and factoring companies must always obtain full ITBMS registration in order to issue zero-rated/exempt invoices and preserve input-credit or refund claims.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for 0% ITBMS where the goods physically leave Panama under a valid Aduana export declaration within the prescribed customs time window. Services, including factoring and trade-finance services, qualify for 0% (or are statutorily exempt as pure financial services) where the recipient is a non-resident and the service is effectively consumed, used, or exploited outside Panama; the factoring/discount interest margin is exempt as a financial service, while fee-based commission components may be zero-rated where the foreign use/destination test is satisfied. The claimant must be a fully registered ITBMS taxpayer, issue 0%-rated electronic invoices, and retain documentary proof of physical export or offshore enjoyment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) with Aduana validation stamp
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exit of goods
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate – bank statement or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export/factoring proceeds

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica de Panamá) is phased in by DGI according to annual-revenue segments. Taxpayers must use DGI-authorized billing software or the DGI Sede Electrónica and transmit invoices as structured XML via REST/API web services; each invoice receives a DGI sequential authorization number, digital hash, and QR-code stamp. Series subject to pre-clearance (validación previa) are validated before issuance. The electronic Comprobante de Crédito Fiscal (fiscal credit voucher) is required to support input ITBMS credits. Financial services and factoring discount/financing income must be coded with the applicable exempt or 0% rate, and underlying export documentation must be retained for DGI inspection.

DGI cross-matches zero-rating claims against customs declarations, electronic invoice data, and bank inward-remittance records. Unjustified 0% treatment is reversed retrospectively with the ITBMS assessed at 7% on the taxable amount, plus statutory monthly surcharge, interest, and fines up to 100% of the evaded tax for fraudulent misstatements. Improperly obtained refunds are clawed back with penalties, the exporter registration may be suspended, and criminal tax/customs fraud liability may be pursued under the Código Penal. The general assessment and clawback limitation period is five years, extended in cases of fraud or omitted registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full currency convertibility prevails for both current and capital account transactions. No exchange controls, multiple exchange rates, surrender quotas, or advance approval mechanisms apply. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and capital repatriations are unrestricted, subject only to Panamanian corporate formalities and territorial-tax compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama does not impose statutory timelines, mandatory surrender percentages, or conversion obligations for export receipts. Exporters and trade financiers may freely hold, convert, or remit foreign currency through SBP-licensed banks.

Central bank supervision: No domestic central bank exists; the de facto monetary authority is the U.S. Federal Reserve under Panama's full dollarization regime. Financial, non-bank, and factoring activity is prudentially supervised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). There is no dedicated foreign exchange administration, and exchange policy is not separately regulated.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary clearing rail. Panama does not maintain a distinct electronic money institution (EMI) licence; non-bank payment service providers require SBP authorization under Acuerdo 3-2018 (payment system regulation) or must act as agents of licensed banks. Offshore digital EMIs without a Panamanian branch cannot settle domestic escrow obligations and will not be accepted as proof of banking infrastructure for factoring-license renewal or client money safeguarding.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward capital injections must be routed through SWIFT MT103 in USD to the entity's operating or escrow account maintained at a SBP-licensed Panamanian bank. The receiving bank will require a sworn declaration (Declaración Jurada) identifying the sender, ultimate originator, beneficiary, and economic purpose code. For factoring receivables, the bank also requires delivery of the invoice assignment notice and the underlying trade contract to distinguish trade remittances from equity injections. Third-party personal accounts are prohibited as collection points for client funds or capital contributions.Outbound settlements to foreign suppliers, factored debtors, or assignor clients are executed via SWIFT MT103 (single customer transfers) or MT202 (bank-to-bank aggregation). Commercial Letters of Credit are issued through a Panamanian bank via SWIFT MT700 or confirmed/negotiated by international correspondent banks. Domestic payments may use Panama's local ACH system; cross-border liquidity is cleared through the U.S. correspondent network (usually CITI, BNY, or JP Morgan) given Panama's dollarized system.Short-dated and forward FX contracts through international bank counterparties for non-USD receivables (e.g., EUR, CNY, JPY) to lock in USD-equivalent factoring proceedsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing emerging-market currencies against USD to hedge non-liquid currency exposure without physical settlementPlain-vanilla cross-currency swaps under ISDA/CSA with licensed international banks, subject to board-approved treasury, netting, and credit-risk policies

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withheld on dividends distributed from net profits subject to Panamanian corporate income tax; distributions from properly segregated foreign-source profits are not subject to Panama dividend tax. Bearer-share distributions may attract additional retention until bearer shares are exchanged and immobilised.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three calendar months after the fiscal-year end. For a calendar-year company, the deadline is 31 March of the following year. If the deadline falls on a non-business day, the due date shifts to the next business day.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall

The Panama Sociedad Anónima files as a Panamanian-incorporated resident taxpayer even when all shareholders are non-resident. No foreign ownership data is entered in Form 280. Late filing triggers a default surcharge on unpaid ISR, monthly statutory interest, administrative fines (typically B/.50 to B/.5,000 depending on delay), and denial of tax compliance certificates. Persistent non-filing may result in tax execution, judicial attachment, and criminal prosecution.
Line 1b / Box 2 Gross revenue from Panamanian-source activities: factoring discounts, commissions, underwriting fees, guarantee fees, late-payment interest, and recovered receivable profits. Include only revenue where the account-debtor, collateral, or payment risk is in Panama. Cross-border reverse factoring on foreign buyers is foreign-source revenue and must be disclosed separately. Late filing triggers a default surcharge on unpaid ISR, monthly statutory interest, administrative fines (typically B/.50 to B/.5,000 depending on delay), and denial of tax compliance certificates. Persistent non-filing may result in tax execution, judicial attachment, and criminal prosecution.
Line 2a / Box 3 Foreign-source gross revenue accurately segregated from Panamanian-source revenue. Foreign-source revenue is not subject to the 25% ISR but must be separately reported and supported by board resolutions, contracts, and invoices. Late filing triggers a default surcharge on unpaid ISR, monthly statutory interest, administrative fines (typically B/.50 to B/.5,000 depending on delay), and denial of tax compliance certificates. Persistent non-filing may result in tax execution, judicial attachment, and criminal prosecution.
Line 3a / Box 4 Deductible costs and expenses: funding cost, provisions for bad debts, operating rent, salaries, depreciation, and transfer-pricing adjustments. Claim only expenses allocable to Panamanian-source revenue. Blanket head-office cost allocations from foreign affiliates are not deductible unless substantiated. Late filing triggers a default surcharge on unpaid ISR, monthly statutory interest, administrative fines (typically B/.50 to B/.5,000 depending on delay), and denial of tax compliance certificates. Persistent non-filing may result in tax execution, judicial attachment, and criminal prosecution.
Line 4 / Box 5 Net taxable income: Panamanian-source gross revenue less deductible expenses. Compute this line before set-off of prior-year losses; loss carryforward is limited to five years. Late filing triggers a default surcharge on unpaid ISR, monthly statutory interest, administrative fines (typically B/.50 to B/.5,000 depending on delay), and denial of tax compliance certificates. Persistent non-filing may result in tax execution, judicial attachment, and criminal prosecution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB 2,000,000 (USD 2,000,000, Balboa peg) in aggregate annual related-party transactions. Above this threshold, the taxpayer must prepare Local File, Master File, benchmarking analysis, and file the annual transfer-pricing annex with the DGI.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not apply Caribbean-format Economic Substance Regulations to ordinary S.A.s. However, DGI and AML authorities apply substance-over-form and a general anti-abuse rule. SEM-licensed factoring or finance companies must maintain physical presence, local employees, and substantive revenue activities. Shell companies using treaty benefits or interposing without business purpose are exposed to recalculation, denial of treaty rates, and corporate registry sanctions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: DGI assessment of the full evaded ISR, statutory interest, administrative fines up to 100% of evaded tax, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud carrying 2 to 5 years imprisonment under the Panama Fiscal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause false declarations are jointly and severally liable for evaded taxes, interest, and fines. Serious cases trigger travel bans, asset attachments, and imprisonment of the responsible officers.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: Registry cancellation of corporate rights, administrative fines up to B/.100,000, adverse DGI anti-abuse determination, denial of treaty benefits, and filing as a reportable entity to FATF-style networks.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced when the company is a mere alter ego. Directors become personally liable to creditors and tax authorities, face disqualification from Panamanian corporate directorship, and may be named in AML sanctions.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment to arm's-length income, secondary adjustment treating the shortfall as a deemed distribution, penalties of 20% to 40% of the underpaid tax, and statutory interest. Documentation failure triggers additional fixed penalties determined by the DGI.

Director personal liability: Directors approving non-arm's-length pricing or signing false TP documentation may be held personally liable for intentional mispricing and face monetary fines and disqualification from corporate office.
Unlicensed Factoring / AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Panama's banking and AML laws, especially AML Law 23 of 2015, impose fines up to B/.1,000,000 per violation, suspension or revocation of any license, and criminal sentences of 5 to 12 years for money-laundering offences involving invoice trading.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and directors at fault face personal criminal indictment, freezing orders, travel bans, exclusion from financial-system management, and civil liability for fraud when the corporate form is abused.
Unreported Dividend Distributions / Repatriation Violation

Statutory sanction: DGI collects the unpaid dividend withholding tax from the company plus default interest and administrative penalties up to 20% of the unreported distribution tax. Intentional evasion triggers criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Officers who knowingly authorise distributions without withholding are personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax, interest, and fines, and may be subject to criminal conviction and travel restrictions.
