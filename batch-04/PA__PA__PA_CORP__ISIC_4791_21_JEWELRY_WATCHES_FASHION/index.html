Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No Panamanian analogue to the US check-the-box election exists; the Sociedad Anónima is a per-se corporation subject to entity-level taxation with no pass-through of income or losses. Available statutory elections: (i) simplified Small-Taxpayer Regime for entities with annual gross income up to USD 150,000, allowing a final 2% gross-receipts ISR and simplified monthly ITBMS compliance in lieu of the ordinary net-income rules; (ii) annual election between the regular graduated ISR (15% on the first USD 50,000 of net taxable income, 25% on the excess) and the alternative minimum tax (4.7% of net assessable income, subject to a 2%-of-gross-income floor); (iii) election for tax-neutral corporate reorganizations, subject to advance DGI authorization; (iv) application of treaty benefits under the ratified double-taxation network (Barbados, France, Mexico, Portugal, Qatar, UAE) only upon satisfaction of beneficial-ownership and substance conditions. Hybrid-mismatch and check-the-box elections are foreign-law constructs with no domestic statutory analogue.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy: the U.S. dollar is legal tender and circulates as the effective domestic currency, and the balboa is a nominal unit at a fixed 1:1 parity with no independent note issuance. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible, with no exchange-control law, no central bank FX intervention, and no administrative restrictions on capital inflows, outflows, or profit repatriation. Dividends, branch remittances, and shareholder distributions from a Panama Sociedad Anónima may be effectuated freely through licensed banks in USD or any freely convertible currency. The only practical hurdles are compliance-driven: the bank's AML/KYC due diligence under Law 23 of 2015 and SBP regulations, the filing of currency-transaction and suspicious-transaction reports when applicable, and the need to demonstrate economic substance for any entity engaged in distribution and international business activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, no minimum hold period, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Export receipts in USD or other convertible currencies may be retained in full in any licensed bank account without an obligation to convert, repatriate, sell to the government, or declare to a central bank. For the e-commerce export of jewelry, watches, and fashion accessories, the absence of an FX surrender regime means that only standard SBP transaction-reporting thresholds (CTR/STR) and the entity's own accounting records govern the treatment of export revenues.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama operates without a central bank; monetary affairs are handled indirectly through full dollarization. Banking supervision and regulation are vested in the Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá, SBP), while the state-owned Banco Nacional de Panamá functions as the government fiscal agent and as the operator of the local clearing mechanism. The Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) has no exchange-control mandate. There is no national monetary authority empowered to impose currency controls or to issue fiat currency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM, CONDITIONAL. Panama law prohibits the unlicensed taking of deposits and the operation of payment services; the SBP digital banking license introduced by Law 160 of 2020 requires substantial capital and a local compliance presence, making it impractical for a single e-commerce S.A. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, U.S. neobanks) cannot maintain a panamanian deposit account without an SBP-licensed intermediary and are therefore usable only as auxiliary settlement rails that aggregate or disburse funds into a fully licensed local bank account. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions to PA_CORP are routed as USD wire transfers via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's home bank to the panamanian beneficiary account at any licensed commercial bank. Because Panama maintains no exchange-control filings or fund-admission forms, the receiving bank relies on the remittance purpose and the underlying corporate legal file to classify the inflow as equity subscription or shareholder loan. Recommended protocols: use the registered corporate legal name exactly as recorded, include the registered agent reference, and state the memo as 'CAPITAL INJECTION - EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN - LOAN AGREEMENT DD MON YYYY'; append the notarized and apostilled share issuance resolution, the updated share register, and, for loans, the executed loan agreement and repayment schedule. No prior SBP approval is required, but amounts above the bank's internal threshold require a source-of-funds statement. For gateway-originated inflows from Stripe and PayPal, settlement is effected through card-network clearing (Visa/Mastercard) into the gateway's U.S. clearing bank and then onward via U.S. Fedwire/ACH to the Panama bank's correspondent (typically Citibank N.A., JPMorgan Chase, or Bank of America), ultimately crediting the licensed local operational account.Supplier payments for jewelry, watch inventory, and logistics are executed primarily in U.S. dollars through SWIFT MT103 credit transfers via the bank's New York correspondent network, ensuring settlement in same-day USD for beneficiaries in the United States, Switzerland, Hong Kong, and other sourcing markets. For significant or confirmed purchase orders of fine timepieces, a commercial letter of credit (governed by UCP 600) may be opened through the same bank to mitigate shipment risk; for routine e-commerce restocking, bank checks and the domestic Panama clearing house (Cámara de Compensación operated by Banco Nacional de Panamá) suffice for local service providers. Every outbound transfer should include the UETR (unique end-to-end transaction reference) embedded in the MT103 to enable straight-through processing and full audit traceability. The S.A. should retain proforma invoices, final invoices, Panama customs export declarations (DUI), courier waybills, and gateway payout statements to correlate each disbursement with the corresponding trade flow. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM, CONDITIONAL. Panama law prohibits the unlicensed taking of deposits and the operation of payment services; the SBP digital banking license introduced by Law 160 of 2020 requires substantial capital and a local compliance presence, making it impractical for a single e-commerce S.A. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, U.S. neobanks) cannot maintain a panamanian deposit account without an SBP-licensed intermediary and are therefore usable only as auxiliary settlement rails that aggregate or disburse funds into a fully licensed local bank account. For the PA_CORP jewelry/watch e-commerce model, Stripe and PayPal are permissible gateway processors, but their net settlements must discharge into a licensed Panama commercial bank account; any gateway balance cannot constitute an unlicensed stored-value or deposit-taking scheme. Legal constraint: the EMI/gateway should be screened for AML compliance and sanctions exposure, and the local licensed account must remain the treasury anchor for all concentration, counterparty risk, and audit-trail purposes.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (flat general CIT rate on Panamanian-source net taxable income for Sociedades Anónimas).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory dividend withholding tax on distributions made by Panamanian SAs; reduced to 5% when the profits distributed were subject to the general corporate income tax of 25% (no preferential regime). An additional complementary dividend tax of up to 5% applies to distributions out of exempt or preferentially taxed profits to bring the combined corporate-plus-dividend burden to a 25% effective total.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three (3) months after the fiscal year-end; for a calendar-year S.A., the filing deadline is March 31 of the immediately following year. No statutory extension; late settlement accrues the Tax-Correction Index (TCI) at annual delay interest plus a 2% annual surcharge.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 3,000,000 (Panamanian Balboa is pegged 1:1 to USD) in annual gross income of the taxpayer in the prior fiscal year, triggering mandatory transfer pricing Local File/documentation and filing of Form 391; a multinational group with consolidated annual gross revenue of USD 50,000,000+ must also prepare a Master File; sole transactions with related parties above USD 250,000 are the relevant operations threshold for documentation review.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 53 of 2017 as amended by Law 70 of 2019) require SAs engaged in 'relevant activities' – including headquarters, distribution, warehousing, service centers and IP exploitation – to demonstrate local economic substance: physical premises in Panama, adequate local payroll proportionate to the activity, local management presence and quarterly substance records submitted through the DGI e-portal. An e-commerce distribution hub established in Panama with foreign sellers is in scope unless the foreign-source sales activity is performed entirely abroad with no Panamanian fixed establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (False or Omissive Returns)

Statutory sanction: Fine equal to 200% of the omitted tax plus default interest; criminal penalties under the Fiscal Code (as amended by Law 254/2023): imprisonment of 2–5 years for evasion below USD 300,000, escalated to 5–10 years where the tax omitted exceeds USD 300,000; the evasion offence applies to both the legal entity and the responsible natural persons.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil may be pierced under Civil Code Article 2079 where fraud or abuse of legal personality is demonstrated; directors signatories of false returns are personally liable for restitution of the evaded tax, subject to asset embargoes, travel bans and imprisonment of 2–10 years.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Law 52/2017, Law 70/2019 and applicable DGI economic-presence rules: cancellation of the corporate registration in the Public Registry, fines up to USD 1,000,000, suspension of the resident agent's operating license, and denial of treaty benefits on distributions; for AML-linked shell misuse, additional sanctions of USD 10,000–1,000,000 per infraction.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent face personal administrative sanctions, disqualification from creating new Panamanian entities for up to 10 years, and joint civil liability for all tax deficiencies of the shell; exit bans and asset freezing are available when the shell engages in money-laundering or tax-evasion structuring.
AML/CFT Non-Compliance on Payment Gateways

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23/2015 and Decree 37/2016: corporate fines of USD 10,000 to 1,000,000 per violation or double the value of the asset/benefit, whichever is greater; serious infringements trigger criminal prosecution of the company and executives with imprisonment of 3–5 years plus judicial travel bans; the UAF may also order disgorgement of illicitly sourced profits.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and the registered agent bear personal responsibility for failing to maintain KYC/AML files on payment partners and beneficial owners; they are jointly liable for corporate AML fines, may be imprisoned for 3–5 years, and may be subject to Interpol red notices and local exit bans in the event of fraudulent gateways.
Transfer Pricing Mis-reporting

Statutory sanction: 40% surcharge on the transfer pricing adjustment amount plus default interest; non-filing of Form 391: USD 100 per day up to USD 10,000; submission of inaccurate or incomplete TP documentation: USD 5,000 penalty per violation; DGI may reallocate the taxable base and recapture Panamanian tax accordingly.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certify false TP documentation become jointly liable for the additional corporate tax and penalties; personal liability also arises when DGI demonstrates intentional profit-shifting through fictitious related-party structures, opening the door to civil restitution, travel bans and criminal charges of tax evasion.
