Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) - e-filing portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI at sedeelectronica.mef.gob.pa; corporate single window: Panama Emprende at panamaemprende.gob.pa; legal entity registry: Registro Público de Panamá.

E-government filing portal

On incorporation of the S.A., the resident legal counsel files the public deed with the Registro Público and opens the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) with the DGI through the Sede Electrónica, concurrently filing the Aviso de Operación (notice of operations) via Panama Emprende. The foreign shareholder appoints a resident attorney under a notarized and apostilled power of attorney; no physical presence in Panama and no minimum capital are required. The DGI then activates the ITBMS registration, the fiscal domicile record, the digital signature certificate, and the electronic invoicing account (Facturación Electrónica en Línea); the complete tax identity pack is typically issued within 3 to 5 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Panamanian law; an S.A. is taxed as a separate juridical person under the Código Fiscal. Available elective regimes include the Multinational Headquarters (SEM / Sede de Empresa Multinacional) regime of Law 41 of 2007, the Panama Pacifico and Ciudad del Saber special-zone exemptions, the small-taxpayer presumptive quota for annual sales under USD 150,000, and the option to compute annual income tax under the standard progressive table instead of the presumptive micro-enterprise quota. No hybrid-mismatch or fiscal-transparency elections are provided for S.A. entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold: ITBMS registration and monthly filing are compulsory from the first taxable activity, together with the Aviso de Operación. A simplified small-taxpayer regime (régimen de pequeño contribuyente) is available for annual gross sales up to USD 150,000, subject to prior DGI authorization and a presumptive quota.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The definitive physical export of ginned raw cotton bales from Panamanian territory is subject to ITBMS at 0% provided the sale is made to a non-resident buyer, the goods are actually shipped out of Panama, and the customs export declaration (DUA) is validated as an exit dispatch in the customs system. The 0% rate extends to freight and insurance invoiced to the foreign buyer only where contractually included in the export value; export-related brokerage services performed by the S.A. for a foreign principal are zero-rated when rendered in Panama but effectively used abroad. Input VAT on purchases supporting the export (storage, inland haulage, fumigation, sampling and brokerage) is creditable and refundable by the DGI when a permanent excess credit position arises, after written verification of the complete export file.

  • Validated Panamanian customs export declaration (DUA) issued through the customs single window (SIGA) showing the exit dispatch
  • Bill of lading or multimodal transport document evidencing the physical departure of the cotton bales from Panamanian customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued at 0% ITBMS with the exporter RUC, HS/ISIC classification of ginned cotton and the foreign buyer name
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit advice evidencing collection of the proceeds (SWIFT MT103/MT202 or documentary collection documents)
  • Export contract, phytosanitary certificate and insurance documentation required by the Ministry of Commerce (MICI) and the national agriculture authorities for cotton bales

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica en Línea) applies to all active RUC holders. Invoices must be generated in XML, digitally signed with the taxpayer's Panamanian e-certificate, and transmitted to the DGI e-Tax web service for immediate fiscal validation and the return of a national clearance stamp and QR code. The export invoice must incorporate the customs DUA dispatch number and the applicable export HS codes; invoices not validated, not transmitted, or not stamped before issuance have no fiscal effect, are not deductible, and expose the issuer to fines and closure of the establishment. The DGI reconciles the e-invoice data with the customs exit declarations transmitted through the government single window.

The DGI cross-matches each zero-rated operation against the DUA, transport documents and FX credit evidence; a failure to prove physical exit retroactively restores ITBMS at 10% on the invoice value, plus default interest at the statutory monthly rate and penalties of 50% to 100% of the tax for negligence or willful intent. Fraudulently obtained input VAT refunds are clawed back with the same surcharges, and fines under the Customs Decree-Law 1 of 2008 (contrabando rules) apply where declared merchandise did not leave the country. Criminal tax fraud is actionable before the economic and customs courts with imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, and the DGI may reopen fiscal years within the 5-year statutory prescription period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full, unrestricted convertibility in a dollarized economy: the United States Dollar is legal tender and the Panamanian Balboa circulates only as coins at 1:1 par. Panama imposes no exchange controls, no authorization requirements, and no distinction between current and capital account flows. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, loan repayments, brokerage commissions, and capital withdrawals are freely payable without central bank consent. The only operative constraints are bank-side: AML/KYC onboarding, source-of-funds substantiation, sanctions screening, and documentation of the commercial purpose for unusually large or rapid payment flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama maintains no surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation deadline, and no statutory holding period for export proceeds. The S.A. may retain cotton-bale export collections indefinitely in USD or other convertible currencies in domestic or offshore accounts. No export-license validation or central-bank deposit certification is required at receipt; the exporter merely retains normal commercial records (contracts, invoices, bills of lading) to substantiate territorial-source income for income tax purposes and to support the underlying trade for bank compliance.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) exercises banking, AML and treasury-market supervision; the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) oversees fiscal policy. Panama has no central bank and no foreign exchange administration. Banco Nacional de Panamá serves as the State's financial agent and fiscal custodian but possesses no monetary, FX-control, or lender-of-last-resort powers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Panama's SBP does not license foreign e-money institutions (EMIs) for deposit-taking or corporate treasury operations; an unlicensed offshore EMI cannot lawfully serve as the S.A.'s settlement account and would trigger SBP supervisory action and correspondent de-risking. The defensible posture is to use an SBP-licensed tier-1 bank as the sole clearing and custody layer; fintech payment gateways may be connected via read-only API or as facilitated collection tools, provided all funds clear through the licensed bank within 24 hours and no balance accumulation occurs outside the regulated perimeter.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the foreign sponsor transmits USD via SWIFT MT103 to the S.A.'s tier-1 Panamanian account; field 70 remittance information states 'Equity injection / working capital for cotton brokerage (ISIC 4620)'. Verification performed by the receiving bank: OFAC, EU and UN sanctions screening; source-of-funds questionnaire for credits above USD 50,000; board resolution authorizing the equity entry; issuance of an inflow certificate for corporate records; same-day funds availability. No central-bank filing or FX authorization is required. The S.A. must post the inflow to the shareholders' equity ledger and preserve the MT103 record to support territorial-tax treatment of subsequent brokerage income.Rail set: (1) SWIFT MT103 for open-account settlements, brokerage commission payments, and operating disbursements; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit issued under ICC UCP600 via MT700 (issuance), MT710/MT720 (amendments), MT752 (confirmation) and MT754 (settlement advice) for physical cotton-bale purchases; (3) Documentary Collections under ICC URC 522 (D/P and D/A) operated as cash-against-documents for balancing legs; (4) interbank transfer via the local clearing system where both parties hold Panamanian accounts. Every outbound message must reference the S.A. tax ID (RUC) and the underlying trade contract number. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% for dividends paid out of profits already subject to the 25% Panamanian corporate income tax; 10% for dividends paid out of foreign-source or otherwise exempt profits. A DTA may reduce the applicable rate if the recipient is the beneficial owner in a treaty jurisdiction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Within three months after fiscal year end; a calendar-year entity must file by 31 March of the following year. Extension portals open only for registered public accountants and require a tax liability payment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.250,000 (US$250,000) in aggregate annual related-party transactions. Above this threshold, the S.A. must file the annual Transfer Pricing Informative Return and maintain a full Local File, including functional analysis, benchmark study, and economic adjustment. A Master File is expected for tax-resident members of multinational groups with consolidated turnover above B/.50 million during the preceding fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Panama has no separate Economic Substance Regulations in the BVI/Cayman mould. The territorial tax system automatically excludes foreign-source income without an explicit economic-substance test. However, for DTA purposes, for transfer-pricing comparability, and under Law 70 of 2019 on beneficial-owner transparency, the Panamanian tax administration will examine whether a substantive office, personnel, decision-making functions, and bank accounts genuinely support the S.A.'s inward and outward invoice flows. A letterbox S.A. may lose treaty benefits and become subject to reclassification by the DGI or by the foreign shareholder's tax administration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fiscal Fraud

Statutory sanction: DGI reassessment covering the omitted tax, moratory interest at 10% per annum, and an economic correction of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax under Código Fiscal sanctioning articles. Criminal tax fraud is prosecuted before the Fiscal Criminal Courts with imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, fines up to three times the evaded amount, and disqualification from acting as legal representative of any Panamanian entity.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false Formulario 103 filings are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's tax debt; the veil may be pierced where the S.A. is operated as a mere instrumentality of the foreign parent. Courts may issue an impediment to leave Panama (travel ban) against the director or resident agent during the criminal and fiscal investigation, and ultimately impose custodial sentences for the officers who orchestrated the fraudulent offshore characterisation.
Shell Company / Abusive Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty withholding-rate relief; re-characterisation of the entire commission flow as taxable Panama-source service income under substance-over-form doctrine; late-payment surcharges and penalties. The Mercantile Registry may strike the S.A. after three consecutive years of non-payment of the annual franchise tax (tax única), causing loss of legal personality and the immediate transferability of assets to the residual liquidator. Foreign tax authorities may also impose CFC or anti-abuse adjustments and blacklist the structure through the Inclusive Framework.

Director personal liability: The directors and resident agent face civil liability for the corporation's post-strike debts if assets were distributed without creditor protection; international travel bans may be issued by Panama's fiscal courts for DGII debtors; and the controlling shareholder's director can be individually fined by the OECD-country tax administration for maintaining a sham vehicle where there is no genuine business purpose.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Law 23 of 2015, as amended by Law 129 of 2020, imposes administrative fines on the corporation, resident agent, and compliance officers of up to B/.5,000,000 for beneficial-ownership violations; bank accounts are frozen and financial institutions must file SOS/SAR reports with the Unidad de Análisis Financiero. Money laundering involving any prior unlawful activity is penalised with 5 to 12 years of imprisonment and forfeiture of the trade assets under the Panamanian Penal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent are personally liable for AML non-compliance up to B/.1,000,000 where the omission was grossly negligent. Prosecutors may impose precautionary travel bans and asset seizures on directors during the money-laundering inquiry. The corporate veil is inapplicable in money-laundering cases because the beneficial owner and directors are treated as principals in the illegal economic flow, and imprisonment is a realistic outcome for egregious custodians of anonymous shell accounts.
