Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) of the Republic of Panama; online taxpayer portal SITIO (Sistema Integrado de Tributación en Línea) at www.dgi.gob.pa, including the DGI electronic invoicing module for ITBMS administration.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Panama S.A.: (1) incorporate by notarized public deed and register the sociedad anónima in the Registro Público de Panamá; (2) obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación (patente) from the Alcaldía of the registered office district; (3) appoint a resident agent and board of directors, then file the SITIO registration for the Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC), attaching certified articles of incorporation, registry certificate, and notarized board resolutions; (4) receive the RUC and, where taxable economic activities are exercised in Panamanian territory, the ITBMS sales tax account; (5) enrol in the DGI electronic invoicing system and obtain an accepted advanced digital signature certificate for e-filing and invoice signing. The procedure is normally delegated to a local law or accounting firm acting as resident agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a check-the-box or pass-through election for a Sociedad Anónima, which is by default an entity-level corporate taxpayer subject to territorial source taxation. Elective statutory mechanisms include: (i) the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007 for qualifying international services, granting income tax and ITBMS benefits; (ii) the Ciudad del Saber regime for eligible technology and research businesses; (iii) the simplified small-taxpayer ITBMS fixed-payment regime for annual gross receipts up to PAB 36,000; and (iv) formal asset revaluation or capital basis elections filed with the DGI for income tax purposes. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available under Panamanian law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Panama establishes no general exemption threshold for business vendors; any person habitually carrying out taxable transfers of goods or services in Panamanian territory must register for ITBMS regardless of turnover. A simplified small-taxpayer regime applies when annual gross receipts do not exceed PAB 36,000 (approximately USD 36,000), substituting the standard monthly returns with a modest quarterly fixed payment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Definitive exports of goods are subject to the 0% ITBMS rate, and cross-border services, including cybersecurity SaaS, may be zero-rated when effectively used, consumed, or exploited outside Panama by a nonresident counterparty. The exporter must be registered in the exporters registry, hold an active ITBMS account, issue invoices clearly stating the 0% rate and export destination, and retain a signed contract identifying the nonresident buyer. The supply fails zero-rating if the services are exploited in Panama or rendered to a Panamanian resident, in which case 7% ITBMS applies.

  • Customs export declaration (DUE/SISPA export document) with verified exit date and goods classification
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing definitive export of goods
  • Commercial invoice issued at 0% ITBMS with export nomenclature and payment terms
  • Packing list, export permit, or certificate of origin where applicable
  • Inward remittance certificate issued by a Panamanian licensed bank confirming the entry of foreign currency
  • For SaaS and digital services: service agreement with the nonresident client, non-residence evidence, and technical records showing the service is consumed abroad (IP addresses, hosting location, subscriber usage logs)

Panama imposes mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) administered by the DGI for ITBMS taxpayers in staggered phases. Registered taxpayers must issue invoices through DGI-certified software or the DGI portal, sign each invoice with an accepted advanced digital certificate, and transmit the XML/PDF in near real time to the DGI clearance engine, which returns an authorization status (accepted or rejected) together with a fiscal authentication code. The invoice must display a QR code embedding the fiscal hash for buyer-side verification and customs purposes. ERP-to-DGI integration uses a web-services/API standard, and a rejected invoice has no legal validity until corrected and re-submitted.

On audit, the DGI and the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) jointly verify the documentary file and the actual entry of the export consideration. If the 0% rate is unsupported, the full 7% ITBMS is re-assessed, plus monetary surcharges, monthly default interest, and procedural fines. Under the Código Procesal Tributario (Law 76 of 2019), fraudulent zero-rating or misrepresentation of the export destination can attract penalties between 100% and 300% of the evaded tax and criminal tax-fraud liability for the legal representative, resident agent, and directors. Failure to repatriate the foreign currency within 180 days or to produce the inward remittance certificate reverses the zero-rating retrospectively and triggers unpaid tax, loss of export credits, and inclusion in the DGI targeted audit programme for subsequent periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized monetary system. The Panamanian Balboa (PAB) is a nominal unit pegged at a fixed 1:1 parity to the United States Dollar, and USD is legal tender throughout the Republic pursuant to Decree Law 160 of 1904 and subsequent codification. Both capital and current account transactions are fully convertible with zero prior authorization requirements, no surrender obligations, and no repatriation permits. Profit remittance and dividend distributions are not subject to FX approval gates; practical frictions are limited to AML/CFT documentation, corporate substance evidence, and the applicable Panamanian dividend tax on distributable earnings. For ISIC 6201 cybersecurity SaaS operations, cross-border license and penetration-testing fees constitute export of services and enjoy unrestricted convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

NONE. Panama imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation threshold on export receipts. SaaS subscription fees, cloud vulnerability scanning revenues, and penetration-testing service proceeds may be retained in USD, EUR, or any freely chosen currency either onshore or offshore without SBP intervention. The VUCE electronic export declaration is maintained for customs statistics and trade facilitation only and carries no foreign-exchange surrender effect.

Central bank supervision: Panama maintains no conventional central bank. The banking and foreign-exchange supervisory authority is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), established under Decreto Ley No. 9 de 26 de febrero de 1998. Quasi-central banking and treasury fiscal agency functions are performed by Banco Nacional de Panamá (est. 1904), which acts as the reserve and fiscal agent of the Republic. There is no separate foreign exchange administration or exchange-control tribunal in Panama.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

RATING: MEDIUM for receivable-collection rails; LOW-to-MEDIUM as a primary treasury settlement spine. Panama enacted Law 223 of 2021 establishing the FinTech Regulatory Sandbox (Innoveting), but licensed EMI participation remains a time-boxed pilot rather than a mature charter regime. Leading offshore digital EMIs and US-domiciled fintech banks typically reject or heavily condition Panamanian Sociedad Anónima entities without a demonstrable US/EU economic nexus. Recommended strategy: sustain a licensed SBP bank USD account as the legal settlement spine, and integrate EMIs such as Stripe, Airwallex, or Wise Platform exclusively for client payment acceptance and invoicing automation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB 2,000,000 (Balboa 2 million, equivalent to USD 2 million due to the 1:1 peg) of aggregate annual related-party cross-border transactions triggers the mandatory preparation of the Local File and Transfer Pricing Report under Law 365 of 14 December 2023 and DGI implementing regulations; Master File is obligatory when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds PAB 750,000,000 (USD 750 million). Documentation must be filed as an annex to the Formulario 101 at the filing deadline; administrative penalties apply for non-filing even if no adjustment arises.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not maintain a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but the doctrine is enforced through territorial-nexus rules and SEM licensing under Law 41 of 2007. The DGI, under the BEPS-aligned reforms of Law 133 of 2022 and Law 365 of 2023, may deny the foreign-source exemption of a S.A. that cannot evidence: (i) physical presence (office/lease), (ii) Panamanian employees or local subcontractors, (iii) board meetings and decision-making in Panama, (iv) local bank accounts, and (v) contracts executed with commercial substance. A 'shell' S.A. with nominee officers and no operational footprint will be re-characterized as fully Panamanian-resident, subjecting worldwide income to the 25% ISR, the 10% advanced dividend tax, and 10% dividend WHT, irrespective of the foreign client location.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Misclassification of Digital Services (Código Fiscal Articles 700, 704, 754; Código Penal Articles 338-339)

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud: imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, a fine of PAB 25,000 to PAB 100,000, and recapture of all evaded ISR plus monthly default interest (approx. 12% p.a.) and a 50% surcharge on unpaid taxes; loss of the foreign-source reserve privilege; publication of the taxpayer on the DGI blacklist and revocation of the Aviso de Operación.

Director personal liability: Directors, the resident agent, and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable under Law 133 of 2022 veil-piercing rules when the company is used as a conduit for evasion; the DGI may obtain a court-ordered impedimento de salida (travel ban) for tax debts exceeding PAB 50,000; assets of the directors can be attached for civil restitution.
Shell Company / Sham Entity - Failure of Economic Substance and Territorial Nexus

Statutory sanction: DGI re-characterization of the entire worldwide income as Panamanian-source, retroactive assessment for up to 3 fiscal years of 25% ISR plus the 10% advance dividend tax, monthly default interest of 1%, and a surcharge of up to 50% of the total omitted tax; cancellation of the SEM license, if fraudulently obtained, and disqualification from re-entry for 5 years.

Director personal liability: The court can pierce the corporate veil and hold directors and the resident agent personally liable for the full tax deficiency where the structure is a sham; personal liability extends to unpaid withholding taxes (the 12.5% royalty and interest WHT) and the 10% dividend WHT; directors face travel bans and criminal proceedings for false affidavits.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Violations (Law 23 of 2015; UAF Resolutions; Código Penal Article 254)

Statutory sanction: Law 23 of 2015 imposes fines of PAB 10,001 to PAB 1,000,000 per violation, suspension or revocation of the Aviso de Operación and bank accounts, and in serious cases, the crime of money laundering (Código Penal Article 254) carries 5 to 10 years of imprisonment plus a fine of up to PAB 1,000,000; additional risk under OFAC and international sanctions for dealing with designated ransomware actors.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for AML program failures; the Superintendencia de Bancos and UAF can impose travel bans, asset freezes, and disqualification from holding corporate officer positions in Panama; personal criminal liability attaches where directors knew or recklessly disregarded the illicit origin of subscription deposits.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (Law 365 of 2023; OECD TRG)

Statutory sanction: Administrative adjustment to the arm's-length margin with a 40% penalty on the primary TP adjustment, plus monthly default interest; failure to timely submit the TP documentation attracts an autonomous fine of PAB 2,500 to PAB 10,000 per annual return; repeated non-compliance elevates the case to the DGI Special Audit Unit and can trigger mutual agreement procedures (MAP) revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed fraudulent TP certificates or who instructed under-pricing of exported SaaS services are jointly liable for the resulting tax deficiency after veil-piercing; the DGI can obtain court orders freezing personal bank accounts of directors and refusing exit permits for penalties exceeding PAB 50,000.
