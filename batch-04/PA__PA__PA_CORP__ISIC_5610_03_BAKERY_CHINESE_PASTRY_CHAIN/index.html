Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Autoridad Nacional de Impuestos (ANI) — successor to the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; electronic administration via the ANI 'Sede Electrónica' (e-Tax) portal at etax.ani.gob.pa, with taxpayer onboarding through the Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Panamanian Sociedad Anónima: (1) file the incorporation Escritura Pública with the Registro Público de Panamá and appoint a licensed resident agent; (2) obtain the Aviso de Operación (business-operation certificate) via the national Aviso de Operación system and the relevant municipal licence; (3) register in the RUC through the ANI e-Tax portal or authorised banking/notarial kiosk by presenting the Escritura, Aviso de Operación, and identification of the ultimate beneficial owners and resident agent; (4) receive the RUC number (statutory TIN), which simultaneously activates the ISR and ITBMS accounts; (5) complete the electronic-invoicing onboarding profile in e-Tax and, if exporting, enrol with the customs single window (VUPE/SICE). A non-resident entity deriving Panamanian-source income may register directly or through its agent; a company with only foreign-source operations may remain outside the RUC but must register if it employs local workers, owns Panamanian real property, or executes taxable local supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — the S.A. is a separate entity-level taxpayer for Impuesto Sobre la Renta (ISR) on Panamanian-source income only; foreign-source profits, dividends, and out-of-territory service income are excluded from the taxable base; no pass-through or tax-consolidation election exists for an S.A.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' or hybrid-mismatch election is available under Panamanian law; territorial-source rules are mandatory. Available elections: (a) adoption of 'Sociedad de Capital Variable' flexible capital rules at incorporation; (b) simplified presumptive 'pequeno contribuyente' regime for activities with annual gross receipts up to B/.36,000; (c) optional Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime, if separately authorised, which grants special ISR and ITBMS treatment; (d) prior-approval alternative duties computation in customs valuations for related-party imports.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory ITBMS registration from the first taxable operation — no de minimis turnover exemption; as an administrative grace, independent and small businesses with annual gross income up to B/.36,000 may elect the simplified presumptive payment regime (Resolution ANI-DGA-002-2024) while remaining registered, paying a reduced quarterly ITBMS without full input-credit mechanics.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (including packaged fresh and frozen Chinese pastry products) may be invoiced at 0% ITBMS upon proof that the goods were physically removed from Panamanian customs territory by the vendor within the statutory term and title/risk passed to the foreign buyer. Services of a pure offshore character — including master-franchise royalties and technical-assistance fees billed to foreign master franchisees for activities performed entirely outside Panama — fall outside the territorial ITBMS base and are not subject to tax. Eligibility requirements: registered exporter status with the RUC, customs exit properly declared through VUPE/SICE, the seller must hold valid supporting documentation, and the seller must be in full compliance with all ISR and ITBMS filings and monthly payments. Excess input ITBMS recorded on local purchases allocated to zero-rated exports can be offset against any positive output ITBMS, with the residual balance recoverable through the formal ANI exporter refund procedure (Solicitud de Devolucion del Credito Fiscal); refund requests are subject to documentary desk review and discretionary on-site inspection by ANI.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaracion de Exportacion, DEX) submitted via SICE/VUPE (Ventanilla Unica de Comercio Exterior)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure of the goods from Panamanian customs territory
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and, where applicable, sanitary/phytosanitary export certificate for food products
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT/MT103 advice or credit slip) evidencing receipt of hard-currency export proceeds

Factura Electrónica en Línea is mandatory under the ANI e-Tax system: every invoice, credit note, and debit note must be generated by ANI-certified software or approved fiscal hardware (smart POS/fiscal signer), digitally stamped with a unique ANI authorisation code (QR-style digital stamp), and cleared in near real-time by transmission to the ANI central validation platform with the prescribed API/XML schema at the moment of issue. A non-authorised or offline invoice is not legal tax documentation, the input tax credit embedded therein is disallowed, and exporters relying on such invoices lose the corresponding export refund. Special 'control sheets' and foreign-trade invoices may require additional fiscal marks in accordance with ANI technical annexes.

ANI and the customs authority jointly audit export zero-rating and refund claims. A disallowed claim is immediately clawed back together with the underlying input-credit refund, statutory late-interest accruing monthly, and recargos (surcharges) of 10% to 25% on the omitted tax. Issuance of false e-invoices attracts fines between B/.1,000 and B/.50,000 per document. Aggravated tax fraud (Defraudacion Tributaria, Penal Code Article 280-B) is punishable by two to five years of imprisonment plus temporary closure of the establishment, and fraudulent customs export declarations additionally expose the taxpayer to smuggling-based penalties, cancellation of the exporter registry authorisation, and exclusion from the export zero-rating regime for the corresponding tax periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized monetary system under Law 84 of 1904 and Decree Law 3 of 1904. The U.S. dollar is unlimited legal tender; the Panamanian balboa (PAB) exists only as a 1:1 unit of account with no fiduciary banknote issuance. There are no exchange controls, no capital account restrictions, and no central-bank approval gates for current or capital account transactions. Profit remittance is fully convertible in USD and subject only to operational tax clearance (Paz y Salvo del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas) and, where applicable, the 5% dividend withholding tax and ITBMS compliance; no FX conversion hurdle exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Because Panama maintains no sovereign currency requiring conversion, there is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in USD in any local or offshore account without surrender to the central bank. Banking institutions must nevertheless file cash transaction reports exceeding USD 10,000 and suspicious activity reports to the UAF under Law 23 of 2015.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as the primary banking and financial regulatory authority, with the Banco Central de Panamá established under Law 23 of 2023 as the transitional monetary authority; Banco Nacional de Panamá serves as the fiscal agent of the Republic. The Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) supervises AML/CFT compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Panama has no standalone electronic-money institution (EMI) licensing regime equivalent to EU or UK passporting. Non-bank fintech providers must obtain SBP authorization to intermediate funds under Decree Law 2 of 2008, and any holding of Panamanian client balances triggers deposit-taking and AML/CFT supervision under Law 23 of 2015. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Currencycloud may operate strictly as disbursement-only rails for low-value utility or supplier payments, but they cannot serve as the primary treasury clearing layer. The bakery franchise treasury should maintain all operating liquidity in SBP-licensed local banks and use EMIs only for ancillary cross-border disbursements.

Within 3 months after the fiscal year-end; for calendar-year taxpayers the statutory date is 31 March of the following year. Advance corporate income tax payments (anticipos) are required during the current year through the DGI monthly estimate mechanism.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 / Line 1A Gross receipts from bakery sales, catering, POS cash and card revenue, and delivery income generated in Panama. A non-resident-owned S.A. reports only Panama-source gross receipts. Revenue from entirely foreign operations is excluded under territoriality; retain customer contracts and POS settlement records. Late filing triggers a fine of 10% of the tax due (minimum USD 50), a monthly surcharge of 1% per month or fraction on the unpaid tax, and monthly arrears interest at DGI-published rates. Persistent non-filing permits DGI default assessment and suspension of the commercial license (Aviso de Operación).
Box 1 / Line 1B Other Panama-source income: master-franchise royalties received from local sub-franchisees, equipment leasing, interest, and capital gains on Panama-situated assets. Royalty income received by the Panamanian franchisor is Panama-source because the underlying franchise rights are exploited in Panama; attach the franchise agreements and transfer-pricing documentation. Late filing triggers a fine of 10% of the tax due (minimum USD 50), a monthly surcharge of 1% per month or fraction on the unpaid tax, and monthly arrears interest at DGI-published rates. Persistent non-filing permits DGI default assessment and suspension of the commercial license (Aviso de Operación).

Deductible only when incurred to obtain Panama-source income and supported by DGI-compliant invoices. Purchases from the Chinese parent or affiliates must be priced at arm's length. Intangibles acquired from foreign affiliates require an independent valuation and must satisfy the arm's-length standard; statutory useful-life tables apply. Late filing triggers a fine of 10% of the tax due (minimum USD 50), a monthly surcharge of 1% per month or fraction on the unpaid tax, and monthly arrears interest at DGI-published rates. Persistent non-filing permits DGI default assessment and suspension of the commercial license (Aviso de Operación).
Box 4 / Line 20 Net operating loss deduction (NOL carryforward). Only Panama-source losses incurred by the same taxpayer can be carried forward, for a maximum of 5 years; a significant change in beneficial ownership may restrict the loss. Late filing triggers a fine of 10% of the tax due (minimum USD 50), a monthly surcharge of 1% per month or fraction on the unpaid tax, and monthly arrears interest at DGI-published rates. Persistent non-filing permits DGI default assessment and suspension of the commercial license (Aviso de Operación).
Box 5 / Line 31 Taxable income and application of graduated rates: 15% up to USD 200,000; 20% from USD 200,001 to USD 1,500,000; 25% above USD 1,500,000. Compute in US Dollar / Balboa amounts. Non-resident-owned corporations apply the same graduated tables as resident corporations. Late filing triggers a fine of 10% of the tax due (minimum USD 50), a monthly surcharge of 1% per month or fraction on the unpaid tax, and monthly arrears interest at DGI-published rates. Persistent non-filing permits DGI default assessment and suspension of the commercial license (Aviso de Operación).
Box 6 / Line 38 Advance income tax payments, withholding tax credits, and foreign tax credits where applicable. Credit local Panamanian withholding suffered on interest, royalties, and services; the balance is payable with the Form 2 submission. Late filing triggers a fine of 10% of the tax due (minimum USD 50), a monthly surcharge of 1% per month or fraction on the unpaid tax, and monthly arrears interest at DGI-published rates. Persistent non-filing permits DGI default assessment and suspension of the commercial license (Aviso de Operación).
Box 6 / Line 40 Net tax balance payable or refund; transfer-pricing annex; economic-substance declaration reference. Attach the contemporaneous transfer-pricing annex and ESR confirmation when thresholds are met; DGI may require the full Local File within 30 days of request. Late filing triggers a fine of 10% of the tax due (minimum USD 50), a monthly surcharge of 1% per month or fraction on the unpaid tax, and monthly arrears interest at DGI-published rates.
