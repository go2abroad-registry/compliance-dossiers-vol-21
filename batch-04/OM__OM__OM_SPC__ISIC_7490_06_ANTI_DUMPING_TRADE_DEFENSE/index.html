Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Registry Directorate, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP), Sultanate of Oman

Governing statute

Commercial Companies Law of the Sultanate of Oman promulgated by Royal Decree No. 18/2019 (as amended by Royal Decree No. 56/2020), Chapter on Single Person Companies and Limited Liability Companies; Foreign Capital Investment Law promulgated by Royal Decree No. 50/2020 (permits 100% foreign ownership of an SPC); Executive Regulations issued by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Commercial Registration together with the notarized Articles of Association (Memorandum of Association) of the Single Person Company, submitted through the MoCIIP One-Stop Shop (Invest in Oman e-Services Portal), culminating in issuance of the Commercial Registration Certificate and Commercial License.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an SPC for professional consulting activities under Royal Decree 18/2019; the sole member must declare the subscribed capital in the Articles of Association and ensure the funds are available in the Company's Omani bank account before Commercial Registration issuance; for a non-resident sole member the capital must be remitted inward via SWIFT wire transfer. The funds are held for corporate purposes and may be drawn following Commercial Registration issuance.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance certificate issued by the Civil Defense and Ambulance Authority (CDAA) following inspection of fire extinguishers, emergency exits, alarm systems, and signage; structural/load inspection certificate and occupancy clearance for the leased office under the Muscat Municipality Building Code; and workplace health clearance confirming compliance with the Occupational Health and Safety requirements of the Ministry of Labour.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), Sultanate of Oman, Ministry of Finance; official e-Services portal at https://otax.om. The OTA replaced the former Secretariat General of Taxation per Royal Decree 29/2019 and administers income tax, VAT, and related fiscal registration.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) in Oman: (1) Incorporate the SPC with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) and register at the Oman Chamber of Commerce and Industry (OCCI) to obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) number. (2) File the Tax Registration application with the OTA through the otax.om portal using the CR, articles of incorporation, certificate of foreign equity, registered office address, authorized signatory passport copies, and power of attorney. (3) The OTA issues a Tax Registration Number (TRN) for income tax purposes. (4) Separately register for VAT under Royal Decree 121/2020 (VAT Law) and its Executive Regulations if the aggregate taxable supplies exceed the mandatory threshold of OMR 38,500, or opt for voluntary registration when supplies exceed OMR 19,250. (5) Complete customs/environmental and municipal registrations if the SPC exports or imports goods. A non-resident foreign entity with a permanent establishment must also register its PE separately with the OTA.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-owned Single Person Company (SPC) in Oman: (1) Incorporate the SPC with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) and register at the Oman Chamber of Commerce and Industry (OCCI) to obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) number. (2) File the Tax Registration application with the OTA through the otax.om portal using the CR, articles of incorporation, certificate of foreign equity, registered office address, authorized signatory passport copies, and power of attorney. (3) The OTA issues a Tax Registration Number (TRN) for income tax purposes. (4) Separately register for VAT under Royal Decree 121/2020 (VAT Law) and its Executive Regulations if the aggregate taxable supplies exceed the mandatory threshold of OMR 38,500, or opt for voluntary registration when supplies exceed OMR 19,250. (5) Complete customs/environmental and municipal registrations if the SPC exports or imports goods. A non-resident foreign entity with a permanent establishment must also register its PE separately with the OTA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Oman does not permit check-the-box or US-style pass-through/entity classification elections; an SPC is statutorily a separate taxable person at entity level under Royal Decree 28/2009 (Income Tax Law). Available statutory elections include: (a) VAT group registration for related parties under Article 11 of the VAT Law; (b) voluntary VAT registration at OMR 19,250; (c) election to use a different VAT accounting basis in limited cases where approved by OTA; and (d) certain tax depreciation/amortization methods for depreciable assets. No domestic hybrid mismatch election is provided; OECD hybrid mismatch rules do not override the territorial corporate tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration: OMR 38,500 cumulative taxable supplies for the preceding 12 months or anticipated next 12 months. Voluntary registration threshold: OMR 19,250. Persons required/eligible to register must apply within 30 days of exceeding the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A taxable supply of goods qualifies for the 0% VAT rate when the supplier proves the goods are physically transferred outside the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) customs territory to a non-resident purchaser, with title passed to the purchaser, and the export occurs within the period prescribed by the OTA Executive Regulations (generally up to 90 days from the date of supply). Documentary evidence must include a customs export declaration (electronic bayan), bill of lading or airway bill showing the Omani supplier as consignor, and proof of removal from Oman. Services are zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident outside Oman and the benefit/use of the service occurs entirely outside Oman, including in the case of international transport and consulting services supporting foreign anti-dumping proceedings, provided no connection to taxable activities in Oman exists.

  • Valid customs export declaration (Royal Oman Police Customs electronic Bayan) showing HS code, export value, and date of exit
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading / Air Waybill consigning the goods to the non-resident purchaser
  • Signed commercial contract and tax invoice matching the customs declaration
  • Certificate of origin or supporting evidencing GCC/non-GCC origin where required by commodity/customs risk profile
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT payment advice proving foreign consideration credited to the Omani supplier's account

Oman has not implemented a mandatory real-time electronic invoicing clearance system or digital stamping as of 2025. Under VAT Law and Executive Regulations, registered persons must issue full tax invoices or simplified tax invoices in a prescribed data format; electronic invoices are permissible and once issued must be retained and filed via the OTA portal. The OTA has published an e-invoicing modernization roadmap anticipating phased clearance-based enforcement, but no API fiscal stamping obligation is currently in force.

OTA audit and enforcement: (i) the OTA may review zero-rating claims during statutory audits and retrospectively claw back VAT plus interest where export proof is incomplete, forged, or untimely; (ii) administrative penalties range from OMR 1,000 to OMR 50,000 per violation, with additional late-payment penalties at 1.5% per month or fraction thereof on any taxable amount; (iii) deliberate understatement or fraudulent documentation can result in criminal prosecution, imprisonment, and penalties up to triple the VAT evaded under VAT Law Articles 55–59 and Royal Decree 28/2009; (iv) OTA has a 5-year assessment limitation period, and claims flagged as artificial or sham transactions are subjected to substance and anti-abuse scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current-account transactions and effectively convertible for capital-account transactions, supported by the fixed peg of USD 1 = OMR 0.3845 (mid-rate) with narrow dealing margins. Equity subscription into a 100% foreign-owned SPC and dividend or profit repatriation require no exchange-control approval; the practical gatekeepers are: (a) CBO-licensed banks' source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership verification, (b) an updated tax card and absence of unresolved tax liabilities for remittances, and (c) documentary proof of the underlying corporate act (shareholder resolution) and audited accounts. Current-account payments of professional fees for anti-dumping defense work are freely remittable through banks against invoices and the engagement contract; there are no separate capital versus current account registration formalities, although banks file balance-of-payments reports to the CBO.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman does not impose a statutory surrender requirement, conversion ratio, or fixed repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Under the CBO exchange-control practice, 100% of export receipts may be retained in foreign currency in licensed Foreign Currency Accounts at commercial banks, or converted at will. Banks apply documentary checks (Commercial Registration, tax card, customs declarations) and internal trade-finance policies stipulating reasonable receipt windows (commonly up to 180 days for goods, extendable with contractual evidence), but non-compliance triggers enhanced documentary review and CBO reporting rather than mandatory conversion.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) - the sole monetary, banking-licensing, exchange-control, and foreign-exchange regulatory authority for the Sultanate of Oman, operating under the Omani Banking Law and CBO-issued circulars.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Offshore digital EMIs and foreign e-money institutions are not licensed or recognized by the Central Bank of Oman; they cannot clear OMR, access the local RTGS/ACH rails, or serve as the statutory treasury bank for an Omani SPC. Legal constraints: (i) the CBO payment-services licensing regime requires any entity providing payment or account services in Oman to obtain a CBO license; (ii) the SPC's corporate records and tax filings must reference licensed bank accounts to preserve corporate separateness; and (iii) use of unlicensed EMI rails creates AML, sanctions-screening, and permanent-establishment risks in the remittance chain. An overseas EMI may be used only for ancillary, low-discretion funding, never as the primary liquidity or settlement channel.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 1,000,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions, or OMR 5,000,000 annual gross revenue, whichever is lower, triggers mandatory preparation and maintenance of a complete Transfer Pricing Master File and Local File. A transfer pricing disclosure form must accompany the CIT-1 return when related-party transactions exceed OMR 500,000.

Economic substance scope: Oman applies a substance-over-form doctrine administratively and through the General Anti-Abuse Rules. The SPC must maintain a real physical office in Oman, employ substantive local decision-makers and professionals, keep complete accounting records, and conduct actual managerial decision-making in-country. No separate Economic Substance Regulation return is required, unlike in certain other Gulf jurisdictions, but OTA may deny treaty benefits, refunds, or tax status where the company is found to be a shell or lacks arm's-length operations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty equal to 100% of evaded tax, up to 200% on repeat offense, together with interest at 1% per month. Criminal conviction for tax fraud is punishable by imprisonment of up to 3 years and a fine of up to OMR 50,000, with seizure of the tax evaded plus all related records and assets.

Director personal liability: Directors, the sole shareholder, and the manager of the SP C who signed or knowingly directed the false return are personally jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax and penalties. Criminal courts may issue a travel ban, freeze personal bank accounts, and impose a custodial sentence not exceeding 3 years.
Artificial Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Oman Tax Authority may disregard the SPC's separate legal existence, disallow treaty benefits, reallocate profits to the foreign beneficial owner, impose a 50% civil penalty on the re-assessed tax, and refer the company for license cancellation by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced; directors and the SPC's manager become personally liable for unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and third-party liabilities. An investigation may trigger travel bans and asset freezes while the insolvency or disqualification process is pending.
Money Laundering and AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Oman AML Law attracts imprisonment of up to 10 years, a fine of up to OMR 100,000 per offense for individuals, a corporate fine of up to OMR 500,000, compulsory confiscation of the proceeds and assets involved, and cancellation of the SPC's commercial license.

Director personal liability: Senior officers and beneficial owners who direct or execute suspected transactions are personally criminally liable. Travel bans, asset freezes, extradition procedures, and personal bankruptcy orders may be imposed; a conviction automatically disqualifies an individual from corporate management in Oman.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Non-Arm's-Length Payments

Statutory sanction: Oman Tax Authority may adjust the gross revenue, deny deductions for the inflated payments, reimpose tax at 15% on the adjusted amount, and apply a 25% deficiency penalty plus monthly interest at 1%. Failure to maintain TP documentation attracts a specific fine of between OMR 10,000 and OMR 25,000 per documentation failure.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or sign non-arm's-length agreements are personally liable for the resultant deficiency where the SPC cannot pay. In egregious cases, criminal tax evasion penalties, travel bans, and imprisonment of up to 3 years may be imposed on the responsible individuals.
