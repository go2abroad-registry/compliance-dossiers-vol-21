Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) — Panama; taxpayer portal: Sede Electrónica / DGI en Línea (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) in Panama must obtain a Aviso de Operación (business notice), a Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC), and a Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT) through the DGI or the Panama Tax Portal (Sede Electrónica). The process requires apostilled certificate of incorporation, registered agent appointment, proposed economic activity codes (including ISIC 3250 manufacturing of medical/surgical equipment), a local fiscal address, and a certified copy of the officer's ID/passport. The RUC/NIT is issued electronically, and the taxpayer must activate the digital signature (Firma Electrónica) and register in the Sede Electrónica for filing and payment. Non-resident directors/shareholders are allowed without minimum capital; however, the entity must maintain a resident agent and file annual tax returns even if territorial-source-only. No separate VAT ID is issued for a period prior to the VAT registration; the RUC serves as the VAT ID (RUC with 'N' or 'V' category indicating responsible taxpayer).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not offer U.S.-style check-the-box elections. Entity classification follows the corporate form: an S.A. is a separate legal taxpayer and not automatically a passthrough. However, under the territorial tax system, foreign-source income (including export revenue from the manufacture and sale of intraocular lenses outside Panama) is generally excluded from Panamanian income tax, provided the income is not derived from a Panamanian-source activity and is not repatriated through certain banking nexus triggers. Panama has no controlled-foreign-company passthrough election for S.A.s; hybrid mismatch rules are limited to transfer pricing adjustments and treaty-based tie-breaker rules. A foreign entity may be classified as a permanent establishment in Panama only if it has a fixed place of business or dependent agent in Panama; the taxpayer may elect to file on a consolidated basis within a group only if applicable under the Fiscal Code and DGI rulings. Additionally, for companies operating in the Colon Free Zone or Panama Pacifico special regime, separate statutory elections apply under special tax laws (e.g., annual quota tax regime for Colon Free Zone).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 300,000 gross taxable sales/operations per calendar year (Panama uses USD as legal tender). Persons making taxable supplies exceeding USD 300,000 or engaging in importation for resale, regardless of amount, must register for ITBMS. Voluntary registration is permitted for exporters of goods and services to claim refunds/credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods (including manufactured intraocular acrylic lenses) are zero-rated (0% ITBMS) under Panama's tax law when the goods are physically shipped out of Panama by the seller to a foreign destination, evidenced by customs export declaration. Services physically rendered in Panama but consumed abroad and explicitly listed as 'exportación de servicios' are zero-rated if the service is supplied to a non-resident and the benefit is fully utilized outside Panama. Contract manufacturing of lenses for a foreign principal with no delivery of finished goods into Panamanian territory qualifies as territorial-source export services under DGI guidelines. The supplier must be registered for ITBMS (voluntarily or mandatorily), issue an export invoice, and retain documentation of the actual receipt of foreign currency from the non-resident.

  • DUA — Declaración Única Aduanera (Single Customs Declaration / Export Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical shipment from Panama to a foreign consignee
  • Export invoice / commercial invoice referencing the exact contract, product description, and HS Code 9004.90 / 9018.50
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (if required by destination customs buyer)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 wire confirmation from the overseas purchaser evidencing receipt of payment
  • Foreign buyer purchase order / supply agreement proving the export transaction
  • Copy of registered S.A. export license and ITBMS registration certificate (RUC)
  • Customs broker's export manifest and insurance certificate if applicable

Panama has phased in electronic invoicing (Comprobantes de Factura Electrónica, known as FEI) under DGI Resolution 201-2022 and subsequent rules. Mandatory issuance of electronic invoices using the Autorización de Impresión Fiscal or Digital Fiscal Authorization (DI-EFIS) for specified taxpayers, with real-time validation/clearing via the DGI Sede Electrónica and authorized technology providers (PIT). Required XML schema with UBL 2.1, digital signature, unique invoice code (Código Único), and QR stamp (Factura de Pago, Nota de Crédito, Nota de Débito). Certain high-risk sectors require fiscal printer integration or 'DGI-FEI' rejection controls. For exporters of goods and zero-rated services, the electronic export invoice must be linked to the Single Customs Declaration (DUA) and the DGI receives customs data through the SIEC (Sistema de Información de Comercio Electrónico).

Panama imposes penalties under the Fiscal Code (Código Fiscal) and DGI regulations for improper zero-rating: (a) retroactive ITBMS charges plus monthly interest at the legal rate (currently 10% per annum) for export documentation failures; (b) fines ranging from 1 to 5 times the tax involved for fraud or falsified export documentation; (c) criminal liability for evasion and money laundering if export paperwork is used to disguise under-invoicing or to avoid tax, subject to up to 5 years' imprisonment and/or fines under the Penal Tax Law (Law 87 of 2019); (d) loss of credit/refund rights and mandatory recalculation of the tax credit ratio; (e) suspension or cancellation of the S.A.'s RUC and export licenses; (f) an automatic 'clawback' period extending to the last open tax year (generally up to 5 years, but 10 years in cases of fraud). The DGI may cross-check customs data (SIEC), bank remittance confirmations, and the taxpayer's e-invoice stamp approvals to verify the actual departure of goods and actual receipt of foreign currency within the 180-day deadline; failure to repatriate FX or subsequent supply back into Panama triggers the reversal of zero-rating with surcharges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama maintains full convertibility with no exchange-control regime. Both capital-account and current-account transactions are entirely free of SBP licensing, prior FX approval, or quantitative restrictions. There is no central foreign-exchange market, no distinction between capital and current account for FX clearance, and no mandatory conversion of export proceeds into Balboa. Consequently, PA_CORP can remit dividends, royalties, technical-service fees, and repayment of intercompany loans without FX authorization, subject only to the territorial income-tax framework. For dividend profit repatriation, the operational constraint is the standard dividend withholding obligation on Panamanian-source earnings; there is no exchange-control hurdle. Non-resident shareholders may convert USD distributions into any foreign currency at freely determined commercial bank rates without documentary approval from the State.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender rules exist. Panama imposes no statutory timeline for the collection of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate or convert foreign-currency receipts. USD export proceeds can be retained in the PA_CORP operating account and reinvested with complete freedom. The administrative obligations are limited to documentary retention for income-tax purposes: commercial invoices, bills of lading or airway bills, customs SIC-ADURA entry forms, and bank credit receipts must evidence the territorial-source character of the revenue before the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI). In addition, the SBP-licensed relationship bank will apply its AML monitoring framework to verify that the volume and frequency of export flows are consistent with the declared ISIC 3250 high-precision intraocular-lens manufacturing activity.

Central bank supervision: No standalone central bank exists in the Republic of Panama. Banking supervision is vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) under the Ley Bancaria (Decreto Ley 9 de 26 de febrero de 1998). Banco Nacional de Panamá, the state-owned bank, performs clearing, cash-management, and fiscal-agency functions on behalf of the State. The Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) supervises AML/CTF compliance and the financial-information reporting gateway, while the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) coordinates monetary and fiscal policy. The monetary system is fully dollarized: the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) exists only as token coinage pegged at 1:1 to the United States dollar, and the U.S. dollar is legal tender for all private and public obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an auxiliary multi-currency treasury utility; Low as the primary or sole clearing infrastructure. Legal constraints: under the Ley Bancaria, only entities licensed by the SBP may take deposits or intermediate payment services from Panamanian-domiciled clients. An offshore EMI licensed in the EEA, the United Kingdom, or Singapore cannot lawfully extend a payment account directly to PA_CORP without either obtaining a Panamanian license or operating through a contractual white-label arrangement with an SBP-regulated bank. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Panama operates a dollarized system with zero exchange controls, so the inbound equity injection is executed as an ordinary SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's offshore bank into the USD operating account of PA_CORP at an SBP-licensed institution. Recommended protocol: (1) file the capital-subscription minutes and an internally documented foreign-investment declaration with the bank's compliance unit in advance, notwithstanding the absence of central registration; (2) instruct the remitter to include in SWIFT field 70 the subscription reference number and the corporate RUC number, and to identify the remitter in field 50K as the exact contributing shareholder, with the beneficiary in field 59 as PA_CORP's legal name and account number; (3) the wire settles in USD through Fedwire or CHIPS in New York and is credited locally through the Banco Nacional de Panamá clearing switch; (4) operations at or above USD 10,000 enter the UAF reporting grid, and single injections above USD 100,000 trigger enhanced due diligence with documentary verification; (5) no Balboa conversion, no surrender declaration, and no SBP pre-approval are required. Shareholder loans must be evidenced by written loan agreements with arm's-length interest rates to prevent recharacterization as equity by the DGI.Disbursement rails are USD-native. Low-value and recurring supplier payments, covering acrylic polymer pellets, hydrophobic monomers, polishing solutions, and clean-room consumables, clear by SWIFT MT103 through the bank's New York USD correspondent to the supplier's account with standard one-day to four-hour value dating. Large capex items, in particular high-precision CNC lathes, laser marking systems, and lens-inspection equipment, are settled by irrevocable documentary letters of credit issued under UCP 600 rules, with sight or sixty-to-ninety-day usance drafts. The issuing Panamanian bank requires the underlying purchase order, supplier pro-forma invoice, and, for each subsequent drawing, the negotiable transport document (bill of lading or airway bill) together with a customs entry reference.
