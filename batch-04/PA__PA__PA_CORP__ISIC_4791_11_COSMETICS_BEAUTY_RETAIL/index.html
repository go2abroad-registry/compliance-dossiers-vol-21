Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists for a standard S.A.; the Articles fix the authorized capital (commonly US$10,000) and the initial subscribed/paid-up capital (commonly one share of US$100 nominal value). No bank escrow account or capital deposit requirement is imposed by Law 32 of 1927.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) — Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas de la República de Panamá; Sede Electrónica: https://sede.dgi.gob.pa; Portal informativo: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity intending to engage in Panamanian sitused trade must (1) appoint a resident legal representative with DGI-recognized powers, (2) obtain or rely on a Panama Sociedad Anónima incorporation certificate filed in the Registro Público, (3) execute and notarize a power of attorney in favor of the representative, (4) register the address of the local registered office, (5) submit Formulario de Inscripción en el Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC) through the DGI Sede Electrónica, and (6) obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación before any taxable activity. DGI issues a provisional RUC immediately upon registration; definitive RUC follows after validation and physical inspection where required. Invoices may only be issued once the RUC appears as 'activo' in the DGI registry.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box election, no hybrid mismatch election, and no voluntary entity-classification regime exists under Panamanian law. A Panama Sociedad Anónima is per se a juristic entity taxed at entity level exclusively on Panamanian-source income. Foreign shareholders do not alter this classification. Special regimes such as tax-privileged Private Interest Foundations (PIFs) and certain multinational licenses are statutory regimes, not election-against-default classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.0 — no monetary threshold. Registration in the RUC is mandatory for any person or entity engaged on a habitual basis in taxable transfers, imports, or services situated in Panama. Small taxpayers with annual gross income not exceeding B/.150,000 may qualify for a simplified quarterly remittance mechanism without relief from registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (0% ITBMS) applies to movable goods physically removed from Panama under a valid customs export declaration, provided the sale is conditioned upon export and title passes to a foreign buyer. Offshore services qualify if the benefit, performance, and use occur entirely outside Panama and the recipient is a non-resident. Cosmetic and personal-care retail products must satisfy the same documentary test as general merchandise, including proof of delivery to a foreign address and receipt of funds from abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration — DUCA (Declaración Única de Aduana para Exportación)
  • Commercial invoice expressly stating 'Exportación a 0% ITBMS'
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or international freight manifest evidencing departure from Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/IBDC credit evidence in the name of the exporting entity
  • Packing list and weight/cube confirmation
  • Certificate of Origin where preferential tariff treatment is claimed

Mandatory authorisation-based electronic invoicing applies to all RUC-active taxpayers. Invoices must be transcribed into DGI's electronic system as structured XML using approved digital certificates, carry an electronic authorization number and/or QR stamp, and be transmitted to the DGI platform by the next business day after issuance. Export invoices require pre-validation through the DGI export submodule. API/web-service integration is mandatory for enterprise taxpayers; smaller taxpayers may use DGI's free web portal.

DGI may retrospectively recharacterise an export as taxable local sale if any element of the documentary chain is inconsistent, unverifiable, or not received before the audit closing date. The taxpayer bears the burden of proof. Consequences include a 7% ITBMS clawback computed on the invoice value, statutory monthly surcharges compounded up to 50%, fines up to B/.5,000 for false invoicing, business-closure orders, and criminal liability for fraudulent zero-rating declarations. Retroactive assessment generally covers the applicable five-year statute of limitations from the date of invoice issuance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized economy with the US Dollar as legal tender; there is no central bank issuing domestic currency and no foreign exchange control law. Capital account transactions and current account profit remittances are unrestricted for foreign-owned entities, subject only to standard AML/KYC bank-level compliance. Dividends paid by a Panamanian corporation to non-resident shareholders are generally not subject to Panamanian income tax under the territorial tax system, provided the underlying income was derived from foreign sources and not from Panama-sourced activities. Repatriation is executed via normal US-dollar wire transfers without SBP approval. Practical hurdles are limited to bank due-diligence documentation for outward remittances above compliance thresholds and the absence of a domestic lender of last resort, which elevates the importance of correspondent banking relationships.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements in Panama. Exporters are not obliged to convert, deposit, or repatriate foreign currency earnings within any timeline. All transactions may be settled in US dollars or any freely convertible currency, and proceeds can be retained offshore or in any local bank account at the company's discretion. The only applicable obligation is the general AML/CFT requirement to maintain accurate books and records of cross-border wire movements under Law 23 of 2015 and SBP regulations.

Central bank supervision: Banco Nacional de Panamá (BNP) — Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — High with structure. Panama has no dedicated EMI licensing regime for non-bank digital payment institutions; the SBP regulates payment systems and Payment Service Providers under the Ley de Sistemas de Pagos. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-authorized, EU-licensed EMIs, or US money transmitters) may serve as auxiliary collection/settlement accounts for cross-border e-commerce receivables, but they cannot replace a Panamanian bank account for statutory corporate substance, tax residency, or transaction record-keeping. Legal constraints include: (i) no SBP sandbox for foreign EMIs without a domestic banking license; (ii) EMIs often reject Panama-domiciled S.A. clients due to perceived LATAM/offshore risk; (iii) incoming proceeds to an EMI and onward transfer to Panama may trigger enhanced due diligence under FATF-style wire transfer rules; (iv) a Panama S.A. cannot open a corporate account with most EU/UK EMIs without a local director or UBO passport, utility bill, and source-of-funds evidence. Recommended structure: use the Panamanian S.A. as the principal seller-of-record with a local Tier 1 bank account, and use an EMI (e.g., Payoneer, Airwallex, or Dlocal) only as a collection sweep rail from marketplaces like Amazon, Shopify, or TikTok Shop, subject to monthly sweep to the Panama corporate account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Panama S.A. with bearer shares or opaque ownership structure

Root cause: Panama S.A. with bearer shares or opaque ownership structure

Defense evidence: Provision of (i) updated share register (Registro de Accionistas) filed with the Registro Público; (ii) sworn declaration that no bearer shares exist; (iii) notarized and apostilled Certificate of Good Standing; (iv) personal identification and source-of-wealth documentation for all UBOs above 10%; (v) a signed AML compliance attestation accepting the bank's due-diligence terms.
Non-resident directors/managers without tax ID or local economic substance

Root cause: Non-resident directors/managers without tax ID or local economic substance

Defense evidence: Copy of the corporate license and Panama Tax ID (RUC) if registered; (ii) proof of registered agent (Resident Agent) engaged under Law 52 of 2016; (iii) Board Resolution confirming commercial purpose in online retail; (iv) a notarized declaration of the entity's foreign-source income model; (v) signed Form S-15/S-17 if applying for a tax certificate; (vi) annual corporate income tax return history to evidence good standing.
High-volume e-commerce receipts from fragmented cross-border PSPs (PayPal/Shopify) triggering unexplained wealth red flags

Root cause: High-volume e-commerce receipts from fragmented cross-border PSPs (PayPal/Shopify) triggering unexplained wealth red flags

Defense evidence: A forensic treasury file comprising (i) platform KYC evidence and settlement statements from Shopify Payments and PayPal; (ii) a reconciled cash-flow ledger mapping each inbound transfer to a customer order ID and market; (iii) the entity's website/storefront URL and supplier invoices; (iv) sales tax/ITBMS registration (código de registro) if Panama-sourced sales occur; (v) a signed auditor's letter or CPA-prepared financial statement covering the transaction period.
Transactions routed via high-risk jurisdictions or sanctioned counterparties

Root cause: Transactions routed via high-risk jurisdictions or sanctioned counterparties

Defense evidence: Trade-partner shielding file: (i) OFAC/SDN screening reports for all counterparties and beneficial owners; (ii) negative media reports from World-Check or LexisNexis; (iii) commercial invoices and contracts evidencing the underlying trade; (iv) copies of the passport and residency proof for the ultimate supplier/manufacturer; (v) a written statement that no transactions involve Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Venezuela, or sanctioned Crimea regions.
Rapid deposit-and-sweep liquidity pattern through EMIs posing potential layering risk

Root cause: Rapid deposit-and-sweep liquidity pattern through EMIs posing potential layering risk

Defense evidence: Liquidity provenance pack: (i) a board-approved treasury policy describing the sweep mechanism from EMIs to the Panama parent account; (ii) bank statements demonstrating the end-client collection nature of the sweeps; (iii) a contractual copy of the PSP/EMI terms and settlement cycle; (iv) evidence of actual goods sold (invoices, logistics tracking numbers, marketplace reports); (v) an MLRO-style explanatory memorandum signed by the compliance officer of the client.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the Panama S.A., capital injections from foreign shareholders should be executed in US Dollars via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the entity's Tier 1 Panama account. Compliant routing: (i) remitter must be the actual shareholder or a disclosed loan counterparty; (ii) purpose of payment field 70 should reference one of the following clearly worded purpose codes: 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION', 'REIMBURSEMENT OF CAPITAL', 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN', or 'APORTE DE CAPITAL'; Panama does not operate an official central bank purpose-code registry, but banks apply their internal OFAC/AML checks; if the bank requires a standardized code, use 'CAPT' (capital contribution) on the credit narrative or 'LOAN' for shareholder advances; (iii) supporting documentation must include a notarized Board Resolution/minutes approving the capital injection, a share subscription agreement or scanned share certificate, and a shareholder source-of-funds form if above USD 10,000 equivalence; (iv) if the injection exceeds the account's expected transaction profile, a pre-advice sent to the relationship manager should include the corporate registration extract, UBO passports, and a consolidated group structure chart; (v) funds can be held in the operational USD account; there are no reserve requirements or surrender ratios applicable.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct US-dollar trade settlements to suppliers (China, US, EU) and to PSP settlement accounts; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C at sight or usance) via the Panama bank's trade finance desk for purchases of significant cosmetic inventory from Asia; (iii) SWIFT MT202/MT202 COV for interbank transfers when settling PSP/EMI sweeps of stored balances; (iv) ACH/Wire via the Federal Reserve system for US-domestic supplier disbursements if the Panamanian bank is a US correspondent bank client; (v) corporate procurement cards for low-value office supplies may be issued by the same bank against the operational account. For each outbound wire, the bank requires: invoice, transport/shipping document (or order receipt), and a stated commercial purpose. Currency is always USD, which eliminates FX conversion risk in the payment flow. There are no Panamanian withholding taxes on interest or payments to foreign suppliers on foreign-source purchases; however, supplier payments for services rendered or goods sold in Panama may attract a 12.5% withholding if the supplier is a non-registered foreign entity — for cross-border DTC cosmetics retail, most supplier payments are for overseas goods and fall outside Panama-sourced income.USD is legal tender in Panama; no local FX market exists, so no onshore deliverable forwards or Panama Balboa (PAB) forwards are available or necessary.Cross-currency forwards (USD/CNY, USD/EUR) may be executed with global banks (e.g., Citi, BBVA, MUFG) through the Panama desk, provided the company maintains an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with CSA or margin collateral arrangement.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) in USD against emerging-market currencies for supplier-side hedging may be entered into offshore under any governing law chosen by the parties, subject to bank's internal credit limits; Panama imposes no statutory restriction on any person entering into NDF contracts.Cross-currency swaps (USD/CNY, USD/EUR) are permissible as qualified financial contracts under Chapter 53 of the Panamanian Commercial Code; there are no exchange-control approvals, taxes on derivative gains, or mandatory clearing requirements.OTC FX options on USDPAB or USD/third-currencies are permissible but are rarely needed because all domestic revenue and expenses are USD-only.Commodity/input hedges for packaging and raw materials (e.g., freight fuel surcharge) are not a treasury requirement under this blueprint.
