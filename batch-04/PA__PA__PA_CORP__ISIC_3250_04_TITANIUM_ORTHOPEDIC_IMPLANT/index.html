Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); online taxpayer portal 'DGI en Línea' at https://www.dgi.gob.pa (SISTEMA e-Tax registration and filing); electronic invoicing operated through the DGI Facturación Electrónica platform; customs export declarations processed via SUCA (Sistema Único de Códigos Aduaneros) of the Dirección General de Aduanas, with corporate registration at the Registro Público de Panamá.

E-government filing portal

Step 2 – Appoint a resident fiscal agent (mandatory for non-resident shareholders under Law 52 of 2016) and a resident legal representative with sufficient powers. Step 3 – File the Aviso de Operación (business commencement notice) in the Ventanilla Única/Registro Público within the statutory period. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope: the S.A. is a resident stock corporation taxed at the entity level (25% corporate income tax) exclusively on Panamanian-source income; foreign-source income is outside the Panamanian tax base; no pass-through or fiscally-transparent election exists for S.A. entities.

Available election forms

Panama has no US-style check-the-box or elective entity-classification regime; all S.A. entities are per se taxpaying corporations. Available elections for an active export manufacturer: (a) Régimen Simplificado (Law 66 of 2017) – election to pay a single quarterly tax replacing ISR and ITBMS when annual gross income ≤ B/. 230,000; (b) SEM multinational-headquarters regime (Law 41 of 2007) – 5% CIT election for qualifying regional service operations; (c) special customs/tax free-zone regimes (e.g., Colon Free Zone, Panamá Pacífico) – election to operate under 0% ITBMS and income-tax exemptions for zone sales/exports; (d) City of Knowledge incentives for science/technology tenants; (e) CAIR accelerated inflation adjustment (Art. 705-A Código Fiscal) – election to adjust inventories and fixed-asset costs faster than book depreciation; (f) dividend withholding election – quarterly provisional dividend tax method (5%) versus 10% final withholding on declared distributions. Hybrid-mismatch elections are not available; Panama's CFC rules (Law 254 of 2021) and participation-exemption regime apply mandatorily.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.07%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 230,000 (Balboas at 1:1 USD) of annual gross income – taxpayers up to this threshold may elect the Simplified Regime (single quarterly payment, no separate ITBMS filing); taxpayers above the threshold must register and file monthly ITBMS. DGI administrative practice additionally applies a B/. 36,000 annual turnover de minimis for mandatory ITBMS registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Sales of manufactured goods (e.g., porous titanium hip-joint implants) qualify for 0% ITBMS when: (i) goods leave Panamanian customs territory under a definitive export declaration (DUA) filed in SUCA; (ii) the seller holds active RUC/exporter status with the DGI; (iii) the purchaser is non-resident and title/risk passes outside Panama; and (iv) export proceeds are received through the banking system. Input ITBMS incurred on production inputs (titanium powder, electron-beam consumables, utilities, packaging) is creditable against the 0% output rate, and net excess credits are refundable (reintegro) or offsettable after DGI fiscal review. Services rendered to non-domiciled clients are zero-rated only when the services are effectively utilized abroad; goods destined for national free zones or re-delivered to the domestic market do not qualify.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA – Declaración Única Aduanera) validated in SUCA and accepted by the Dirección General de Aduanas
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical shipment from Panama to the foreign purchaser
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 bank credit confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds within the 180-day repatriation window
  • Commercial export invoice (0% ITBMS rate stated), packing list, and certificate of origin (if preferential treatment claimed)
  • DGI exporter-registry certificate and prior authorized-export documentation archived for audit

DGI electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) is mandatory for ITBMS contributors by phased implementation. Invoices and equivalent documents must be sent in XML to the DGI clearance platform via web-service API (large/medium taxpayers) or the free web portal (small taxpayers); the platform validates the digital certificate (.p12 from an authorized CA), verifies the RUC status, and returns in real time a four-digit 'código de aceptación' (acceptance code) or a rejection/reason code. Accepted documents carry a unique 'número de comprobante' and a QR verification code printed on the customer copy. Offline issuance is tolerated for maximum 48 hours; such documents must be pre-generated with a contingency QR and transmitted before the deadline. No physical stamping or paper fiscal seals are required; only DGI-accepted e-invoices generate deductible input-credit.

DGI export audits (fiscalización) reconcile SUCA customs exit data against ITBMS filings, production capacity, and banking records; physical inspection of the manufacturing plant is routine for high-value credits. Failure to bank export proceeds within 180 days or mismatched customs/payment evidence triggers administrative reassessment of 7% output VAT on the full export invoice, monthly late-payment interest (1.5% per month under Art. 730 Código Fiscal), a 25% surcharge on the assessed tax, and fines of 50%–200% of the evaded amount. Fraudulent zero-rating (forged bills of lading, fictitious exports, back-to-back sham transactions) is a criminal tax offense under the Panamanian criminal-tax regime carrying 2–5 years' imprisonment, increased to 6–10 years when false documents or organized schemes are used; the exporter registry may be suspended up to two years and all previously refunded input credits are clawed back with interest. The statute of limitations for assessment is six fiscal years, extended to ten years in cases of fraudulent conduct.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Because Panama formally dollarized in 1904, there is no national fiat currency and therefore no capital or current account convertibility restrictions. Both capital and operating transactions are freely convertible. Profit remittance by foreign-owned S.A. entities is subject to Panama's territorial dividend tax (typically 5% once Panamanian-source profits have been taxed at entity level), but there are no separate exchange-control approvals, no remarkable remittance hurdles, and no limitation on repatriation of liquidation proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama does not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export proceeds. An exporter such as the titanium orthopedic implant manufacturer may retain all foreign currency earnings in any authorized SBP-licensed bank, whether offshore or domestic, without reporting to any exchange-control body. Customs declarations must accurately value goods, but no FX surrender is required.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank. Banking supervision is exercised solely by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). Monetary and financial policy coordination is handled by the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF). The US Dollar is the only legal tender with unlimited convertibility; the Balboa is a nominal coinage unit only. There is no foreign exchange administration separate from the SBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Panama has not enacted a distinct Electronic Money Institution (EMI) license analog for cross-border deposit-taking. Offshore digital EMIs generally cannot offer a Panamanian-local S.A. a compliant corporate account unless they have a local SBP-licensed banking partner. An S.A. may use a fintech for payment initiation or settlement only when the underlying corporate account is maintained at an SBP-supervised commercial bank. Standalone EMIs without a Panama banking license are legally prohibited from receiving deposits from domestic entities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from a parent company or non-resident shareholder are settled only through high-value SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 if interbank) to the S.A. operational account. The payment narrative must clearly state 'Capital contribution under S.A. Shareholder Resolution' or 'Shareholder loan under private pact'. A local bank will require the certified board/shareholders resolution, updated public registry (Ficha), passport copies of signatories, and a signed affidavit. No exchange-control approval is needed; however, the bank may request a letter of explanation for any capital injection exceeding USD 1.5 million, plus proof of economic substance in Panama (payroll, physical office, or storage evidence) to satisfy SBP regulations.Outbound trade settlement to suppliers of titanium alloy powder, electron beam 3D printers, and surface finishing consumables is executed via (i) SWIFT MT103 payment orders for open-account purchases, and (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit issued per ICC UCP 600 (documentary credit transmitted by MT700) for high-value or recurring purchase orders. Typical sanitization requires full commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, quality certificates conforming to ASTM F1472 or ISO 5832-3, and transportation documents. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Panama has not enacted a distinct Electronic Money Institution (EMI) license analog for cross-border deposit-taking. Offshore digital EMIs generally cannot offer a Panamanian-local S.A. a compliant corporate account unless they have a local SBP-licensed banking partner. An S.A. may use a fintech for payment initiation or settlement only when the underlying corporate account is maintained at an SBP-supervised commercial bank. Standalone EMIs without a Panama banking license are legally prohibited from receiving deposits from domestic entities.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 1,500,000 (Balboa/USD at par) in annual aggregate related-party transactions; taxpayers below threshold must still perform a functional analysis if requested, and documented companies must file the DGI TP informative declaration.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 70 of 2019) require tax-resident entities carrying on relevant activities to prove premises, employees, board management and core income-generating activity in Panama. For an orthopedic-implant manufacturer, substance is presumed if DGI files show real production, payroll and local factory costs; artificial 'mailbox' S.A.s or just-in-time booking through shell suppliers fail ESR and can be deregistered.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud (Ley 76 de 2019, Código Fiscal): fine up to twice the tax evaded, disgorgement of deductions, asset seizure and imprisonment from two to eight years.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives and tax advisers who sign false returns are jointly liable for civil restitution; travel bans, passport withholding, disqualification from public contracts, and criminal conviction.
Shell Company / ESR Violation

Statutory sanction: ESR fines under Law 70/2019 up to USD 1,000,000 or 2% of turnover, deregistration/disbarment of the company, nullity of tax benefits, and recalculation of 12.5% withholding on payments.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for taxes reassessed if the corporate veil is pierced; civil claims can attach personal assets; commercial interdiction and prohibitions from serving on any Panama company.
AML / Money Laundering (Law 23 of 2015)

Statutory sanction: Money-laundering penalties: fines up to USD 5 million or 5% of assets; forfeiture of instrumentalities; imprisonment from 5 to 15 years under the Penal Code and AML Law, plus revocation of corporate license.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability applies to officers and beneficial owners who control accounts; civil restitution orders, travel bans, and extradition exposure for predicate foreign corruption.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: TP non-compliance: 2% penalty on the value of unreported controlled transactions, automatic upward profit adjustment at 25%, monthly interest from the assessment date, and disallowance of related-party expense deductions.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives who fail to file the TP informative return are personally liable for fines; operating directors may face civil liability for unauthorized dividend distributions based on wrong TP results.
