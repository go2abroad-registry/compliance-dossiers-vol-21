Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá – Sección Mercantil (Mercantile Section of the Public Registry of Panama), operating through the SPL electronic filing portal.

Governing statute

Law 32 of 4 February 1927 on Sociedades Anónimas (as amended and supplemented by Law 5 of 2020 on electronic incorporation and registry modernization), Articles 4 (minimum capital), 7-11 (contents of the incorporation instrument), 28-31 (registered agent), and 39-56 (directors and officers).

Formation / constitutional filings

Acta Constitutiva y Estatutos (Certificate of Incorporation and Bylaws), executed as a private instrument before a licensed Panamanian attorney-lawyer, submitted electronically to the Public Registry and recorded on the Mercantile Folio (Ficha).

Incorporation processing time

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas de la República de Panamá; electronic portal (Sede Electrónica / Sistema E-Tax): https://dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

Step 2: Obtain the Aviso de Operación from the municipal treasury. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation, Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Panama. Available elections: (1) Régimen Especial de Pequeño Contribuyente (Articles 798-B/798-C Código Fiscal): optional fixed-sum ITBMS regime for annual gross receipts of B/.36,000 or less; (2) election to register as ITBMS withholding agent; (3) exporter registration election (Registro de Exportador) with DGI/Servicio Nacional de Aduanas to formalize zero-rated export status; (4) option for the Drawback customs-duty restitution regime on exported inputs; no hybrid-mismatch election is recognized because Panama applies territorial sourcing and general anti-avoidance rules under Law 254 of 2022.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general statutory registration threshold: any person habitually selling movable goods or providing services in Panama must register for ITBMS. Simplified 'Pequeño Contribuyente' threshold: B/.36,000 (USD-equivalent Balboas) in annual gross receipts, which permits a fixed monthly tax in lieu of standard 7% collection.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for the 0% ITBMS rate when the goods physically leave Panama and the sale is made by a Panamanian exporter to a foreign buyer; the export must be declared to the Servicio Nacional de Aduanas (SNA) in the Declaración Única de Aduanas (DUA). Services qualify for 0% where the recipient is domiciled outside Panama and the service is effectively rendered and consumed abroad. The exporter must hold an active RUC, be registered in the exporter register, and retain the complete proof-of-export file for DGI inspection.

  • Declaración Única de Aduanas (DUA) – Panama customs export declaration approved by the Servicio Nacional de Aduanas (SNA)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (Conocimiento de Embarque / Guía Aérea) evidencing physical shipment from Panama
  • Commercial Invoice (Factura Comercial) reconciling the sale price, buyer and goods description with the e-invoice and DUA
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) or Panamanian bank statement demonstrating receipt of export proceeds in Panama
  • For services exports: signed contract with the non-resident client, service delivery evidence, and foreign-source payment proof

Nationwide e-invoicing mandate (Factura Electrónica, XML format). The DGI operates a real-time clearance model: the issuer transmits each invoice to the DGI Web-Service API (OAuth 2.0-authenticated) before issuance; DGI validates the document against tax registration and returns an authorization number and QR code (código QR) that must be embedded in the final invoice. Small issuers may use the free DGI portal; mid- and large-size taxpayers must use certified ERP or software with API integration and electronic delivery to recipients. The mandate was phased from 2022 for large taxpayers through mandatory full coverage in 2025. Invoices must follow a chronological sequence, cancellations occur through the same digital channel, and a valid digital certificate (firma digital) is required.

DGI and SNA jointly audit export zero-rating files. Missing, inconsistent, or fraudulent documentation causes rejection of the 0% rate and retroactive charging of 7% ITBMS on the gross sale, increased by a 20% add-on surcharge (recargo) and monthly legal interest calculated from the original filing date; fines range from 25% to 100% of the omitted tax under the Law 254 of 2022 tax reform. Fabricated exports, false invoices, and simulated foreign payments constitute tax and customs fraud: the taxpayer loses refunds or credits for up to 5 prior fiscal years, the signing CPA may be disqualified, and criminal tax-evasion charges apply under Law 14 of 2010 with imprisonment of 1 to 5 years, plus seizure and forfeiture of the goods involved.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized jurisdiction: the US dollar is unrestricted legal tender under Law 84 of 1904, and the balboa is a nominal unit issued only in fractionary coinage. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible with no exchange-control approvals, no surrender requirements, no capital repatriation permits, and no restrictions on dividend or profit remittance by foreign-owned Sociedades Anónimas. Panama has maintained IMF Article VIII status, and convertibility risk is therefore nil; the operative constraint is correspondent-bank de-risking, not regulatory FX control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation window for export proceeds. Receipts from the online sale of musical instruments and audio gear (ISIC 4791) may be retained indefinitely in USD in any onshore account without FX conversion obligations or reporting to the SBP beyond normal tax filings.

Central bank supervision: No central bank exists in Panama. The Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá, SBP) is the sole prudential, banking-conduct, and AML/CFT regulator under Decree Law 9 of 1998, Law 23 of 2015, SBP Agreement 3-2012, and the Payment System Law (Law 15 of 2010). Banco Nacional de Panamá serves as the Government's fiscal agent and minting agent for the balboa coin, but exercises no monetary policy or exchange-control powers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility with strict legal constraints. Unlicensed offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, dLocal, Stripe Treasury) are not recognized by the SBP as settlement banks and cannot provide Panama-local multi-currency account infrastructure. As an ancillary rail, they are viable for collection aggregation and supplier payouts at modest volumes, but core treasury liquidity and escrow must reside with SBP-licensed banks to avoid enhanced due diligence friction and counterparty concentration risk. The legal perimeter is governed by the Payment System Law, SBP payment-facilitator rules, Law 23 of 2015 AML obligations, and correspondent residual-risk policies; EMIs holding a US MSB or an authorized EEA e-money license are preferred to preserve clean SWIFT corridors.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity injections into the Panama S.A., use SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the shareholder's own bank account in USD to the beneficiary account titled exactly as the corporate entity. Payment purpose fields must reference 'Capital Contribution' or 'Additional Paid-in Capital' with the declaring party providing the board resolution, updated share ledger and, if the subscription is evidenced abroad, apostilled documents. Panama imposes no exchange-control license for inbound capital and no withholding tax on equity injections; however, transactions above USD 10,000 should be preserved with the corresponding SWIFT keys, and the paid-in capital must be recorded in the Public Registry (Registro Público) when shares are issued, plus notified to the RUC file for corporate tax declarations.For supplier payments on music instrument and audio gear purchases, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 from the Panamanian bank's USD nostro routed through Fedwire/CHIPS to the beneficiary's US major bank, or through correspondent banks to Asian and European suppliers. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with strict legal constraints. Unlicensed offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, dLocal, Stripe Treasury) are not recognized by the SBP as settlement banks and cannot provide Panama-local multi-currency account infrastructure. As an ancillary rail, they are viable for collection aggregation and supplier payouts at modest volumes, but core treasury liquidity and escrow must reside with SBP-licensed banks to avoid enhanced due diligence friction and counterparty concentration risk. The legal perimeter is governed by the Payment System Law, SBP payment-facilitator rules, Law 23 of 2015 AML obligations, and correspondent residual-risk policies; EMIs holding a US MSB or an authorized EEA e-money license are preferred to preserve clean SWIFT corridors.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 250,000 of intercompany transactions triggers the mandatory Local File; the Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated gross revenue exceeds USD 50,000,000. The related-party annex to Form 26 must be filed whenever intercompany transactions exceed USD 100,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to entities carrying on relevant activities. Online sale of musical instruments may be treated as a commercial activity if the company has local management, employees or warehouses. If the company claims exemption as non-Panama-source income, it must maintain no substantive presence or management activity in Panama; otherwise DGI may recharacterize the operations as Panama-source and assess tax with penalties. For entities that opt into DTA benefits or tax-resident status, ESR requires boards meetings in Panama, adequate local employees, premises and operational records.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Under-Reporting of Panama-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine from 20% to 300% of the omitted tax; default interest at 1% per month; criminal conviction carries 2 to 5 years imprisonment for fraudulent tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who signed false returns may be held personally liable for civil restitution and unpaid taxes; DGI may seek travel bans, asset seizures, and imprisonment, and Panamanian courts may lift the corporate veil under Law 30 of 1996.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of all income as Panama-source; denial of foreign-source exclusion; ESR fines up to USD 1,000,000; cancellation of the corporate license or refusal of tax residency certificates.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly maintain a shell company without substance can be jointly and severally liable for the reassessed tax, debarred from serving as directors, and subject to immigration control and asset restraining orders.
AML and Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties under Law 23 of 2015 of up to USD 1,000,000 per violation; freezing of corporate bank accounts; criminal liability for money laundering with imprisonment of 5 to 12 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may personally face criminal prosecution, extradition, confiscation of assets, and a 30-year prohibition from holding financial or corporate governance positions.
Repatriation Without Withholding and Tax Clearance

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent's liability equal to 100% of the unpaid withholding; late interest of 1% per month; additional surcharge of 10%–50% of the dividend amount; criminal liability for willful failure to remit collected taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally and jointly liable for unreported withholding; the resident agent may also face license suspension; directors can be blocked from exiting Panama until the tax debt is settled.
