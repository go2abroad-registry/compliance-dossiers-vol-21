Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá (Public Registry of Panama) – Dirección General de Registro, Sección de Registro Mercantil / Sociedades.

Governing statute

Ley 32 de 1927 (Sociedades Anónimas), as amended by Ley 5 de 1998, Articles 2–15 (incorporation, share capital, directors); supplementary application of the Código de Comercio de Panamá; Ley 70 de 2018 (mandatory Beneficial Ownership Registry filing for all legal entities).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement under Panamanian law. The Articles of Incorporation must state the authorized capital (customarily USD 10,000 divided into 100 registered shares of USD 100 each); subscribers must take at least one share, and capital may be paid in whole or in part as determined by the board.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), part of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas), official portal www.dgi.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must appoint a licensed Panamanian resident agent and registered legal counsel, then file an application with the DGI using the company's public deed, certificate of incorporation, and proof of appointment of directors/officers. The DGI issues a provisional RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) followed by a definitive RUC after verification of the registered office, beneficial ownership and compliance with Law 41 / AML due diligence. All filings may be completed electronically through the DGI online portal, with authentication by the resident agent's digital signature.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign entity must appoint a licensed Panamanian resident agent and registered legal counsel, then file an application with the DGI using the company's public deed, certificate of incorporation, and proof of appointment of directors/officers. The DGI issues a provisional RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) followed by a definitive RUC after verification of the registered office, beneficial ownership and compliance with Law 41 / AML due diligence. All filings may be completed electronically through the DGI online portal, with authentication by the resident agent's digital signature.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No OECD-style check-the-box regime exists. A Panamanian S.A. may elect into the small taxpayer simplified net-estimated-income regime; qualified entities may apply for the SEM (Multinational Company Headquarters) preferential regime, Panama Pacifico Special Economic Area benefits, or tourism development incentives. Hybrid mismatch elections are unavailable because Panama adheres to BEPS minimum standards; a standard S.A. is taxed at entity level on Panama-source income and cannot elect pass-through taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic threshold; any person supplying taxable goods or services in Panama must obtain a RUC and register for ITBMS. A simplified small-taxpayer regime is available for annual taxable turnover of up to PAB 300,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated ITBMS (0%) applies on the supply of tangible goods sold to a non-resident customer where the goods are physically exported from Panama within the ordinary statutory period (generally 60 days from invoicing). Services qualify when provided to a non-resident and the benefit is wholly consumed outside Panama, excluding services related to Panamanian real estate. The exporter must hold documentary evidence proving actual exit of goods or offshore use of services, and comply with any DGI ruling or prior-authorisation requirement. Services provided to licensed SEM (Multinational Company Headquarters) companies may also qualify as exported services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (eDUA/SIUCA) showing goods actually departed the Panamanian customs territory
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill or combined transport document issued to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank confirmation of the export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice marked 'Exportación Cero por Ciento' / 'Zero-Rated Export'
  • Packing list and certificate of origin, if applicable

Registered taxpayers must issue all invoices through DGI-authorised electronic billing (facturación electrónica) software, using DGI-validated digital certificates and a fiscal QR stamp. Invoices and credit/debit notes for goods and services must be transmitted to the DGI portal in real-time or near-real-time batches; customs export invoices must be cross-linked with the electronic customs declaration (eDUA/SIUCA) and are subject to digital clearance validation at the port of exit.

DGI may require exporters to substantiate each zero-rated invoice with customs and banking records. Failure to prove actual export or any misrepresentation triggers immediate disallowance of the zero rate, reassessment of ITBMS at 7%, plus compensatory interest and a fine generally ranging from 50% to 100% of the omitted tax. Fraudulent zero-rating claims may constitute criminal tax fraud punishable by imprisonment and additional penalties. The standard five-year assessment period is extended in cases of fraud or non-filing. Panama does not operate a statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day parameter is applied as an administrative reasonableness benchmark for reconciling export proceeds, with bank records accepted as remittance evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility with no exchange-control regime. The US Dollar (USD) is legal tender; the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) exists only as coinage pegged 1:1 to USD. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted, and no prior approval, license, or declaration is required for inward or outward transfers. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to Panamanian tax compliance rather than FX authorization: dividends sourced in Panama are subject to a dividend withholding tax (generally 10%, with the underlying corporate income tax creditable in the hands of the shareholder in certain integrated structures, and reduced rates under specific legal provisions), whereas dividends derived from strictly non-Panama-source profits fall outside the territorial income-tax net if properly documented.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export receipts. A medical-device manufacturer may retain 100% of USD export proceeds in any SBP-licensed commercial bank. The only mandatory controls are documentary and AML-oriented: export customs declarations must be filed with the Dirección General de Aduanas, invoices and bills of lading must be retained in accordance with Ley 23 de 2015 record-keeping obligations, and all wire transfers must carry full originator/beneficiary data under FATF Recommendation 16.

Central bank supervision: None; the Republic of Panama is fully dollarized and does not maintain a central bank. Banking and financial regulation are vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), while AML/CFT supervision is exercised by the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) under Ley 23 de 2015.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rated Medium for auxiliary settlement and Low for primary treasury holding. Panamanian banking law (Decreto Ley 9 de 1998, as amended) reserves deposit-taking business for banks licensed by the SBP; unlicensed offshore fintech EMIs and digital banks (e.g., US or UK e-money platforms) have no statutory standing to operate a regulated Panamanian corporate account. They may be used only as ancillary collection or vendor-payment conduits, provided funds are swept daily to an SBP-licensed master account. Overreliance on an EMI creates CRS/FATCA reporting misalignment, economic-substance audit risk, and exclusion from LC-based trade finance.

Compliant routing: originating bank of the UBO → New York correspondent (e.g., JPMorgan Chase, Citi, or Standard Chartered) → SBP-licensed Panama beneficiary bank → the S.A.'s USD operational account. SWIFT MT103/MT202 must include full originator and beneficiary name, address, and account details (FATF Recommendation 16) together with the Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference (UETR). The wire message should cite purpose code GFSR or CAPT and reference the notarized corporate resolution authorizing the capital subscription. No central-bank authorization or exchange-control license is required; the receiving bank will perform an AML name-screen, request a source-of-funds declaration, and apply ordinary client due diligence. Funds are available immediately in USD subject to the bank's internal hold period.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 in USD for settlement of supplier invoices, intercompany services, and distributor commission payments, each with correct purpose code (e.g., GDS for trade in goods) and UETR; (2) Commercial documentary Letters of Credit (UCP 600) for high-value shipments of 4K laparoscopy towers to Latin American and Caribbean distributors, opened by the buyer's bank and confirmed by a Panama or New York bank, payable against full original documentation (commercial invoice, packed list, bill of lading, MINSA certificate, and inspection certificates); (3) domestic dollar clearing through Panama's local ACH infrastructure for payroll, rent, and domestic logistics providers; and (4) direct correspondent-bank transfers to suppliers in Asia and Europe via the issuing bank's nostro accounts. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% statutory dividend withholding tax on gross distributions paid, credited, or made available by a Panamanian S.A. to its shareholders, applied by the corporation as withholding agent. Where the distributed profits have not borne the 25% corporate ISR, a 10% rate applies. Distributions among Panamanian corporations from already-taxed profits may be exempt under the territorial system.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months following the fiscal year-end. For calendar-year taxpayers the return must be filed by 31 March; non-calendar fiscal years require DGI authorization.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD (Panama Balboa/USD legal tender at par). Mandatory Local File: aggregate controlled transactions exceeding USD 1,000,000 per fiscal year. Mandatory Master File: group consolidated revenue exceeding USD 20,000,000. A full transfer-pricing report (Local + Master) plus benchmarking study is required when controlled transactions exceed USD 2,000,000. The Form 100 Anexo TP annex is mandatory for any controlled transaction value exceeding USD 250,000, irrespective of the documentation threshold.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Ley 116 de 2019 and Executive Decree 291 of 2019) apply to entities licensed in Panama carrying out 'relevant activities' (banking, financing, leasing, headquartering, distribution and logistics, shipping, IP holding, insurance, etc.). Manufacturing of surgical laparoscopy towers (ISIC 3250) is not enumerated as a relevant activity and therefore falls outside the ESR reporting matrix. Nevertheless, the S.A. must maintain substantive local operations, including a physical office or factory, qualified employees, production records, and the mandatory Aviso de Operación / industrial license, to preserve territorial tax exemptions and treaty access and to avoid being recharacterized as a 'shell entity' under Panama's beneficial-ownership framework.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Omitted Panama-Source Revenue

Statutory sanction: Restitution of the omitted ISR with a 25% to 50% surcharge plus 1% monthly interest; closure of business license; criminal tax fraud conviction under the Código Penal (defraudación fiscal) carrying imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and a fine of up to 50 times the monthly national minimum wage; administrative embargo of bank accounts and goods.

Director personal liability: Directors and general managers who signed false returns or arranged sham export documentation are held jointly and severally liable (responsabilidad solidaria) for the resulting tax debt; courts may issue travel bans (prohibición de salida), freeze personal assets, and impose custodial sentences; the corporate veil is pierced for fraud under the Código Judicial.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the legal entity with retroactive re-allocation of all manufacturing profits to the foreign parent; denial of treaty relief; revocation of the S.A.'s taxpayer registration; economic substance levies and penalties under Panama's ESR framework; inclusion in the DGI 'shell entity' risk register leading to automatic CRS exchange.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally taxed on any distributions received, fined up to USD 100,000 for false corporate declarations, and banned from serving on any Panamanian board for up to 5 years; asset freezes and travel bans apply in flagrant abuse cases.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and False Documentation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority re-determination of the taxpayer's profit using the most appropriate OECD or statutory method; deficiency assessment of 25% plus a 30% valuation penalty; interest at 1% per month; public disclosure of the offender's name in the DGI audit list; criminal penalties for filing fraudulent TP documentation.

Director personal liability: Finnancial directors who approved the TP policy are jointly liable for the assessed deficiency up to the amount of profit shifted; in cases of document forgery, directors face imprisonment of 3 to 6 years and disqualification from corporate management; international exchange of information exposes them to parallel prosecution in treaty-partner states.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Monetary fines from USD 500 up to USD 2,000,000 per violation imposed by the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF) or the applicable supervisory body; 30% surcharges on unpaid taxes; possible suspension of banking licenses and the industrial operating license; criminal money-laundering charges under the Código Penal with imprisonment of 5 to 12 years for habitual non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for AML breaches, unrelated to any tax obligation: travel bans are routinely issued during UAF investigations; personal bank accounts and property are attachable; imprisonment for up to 5 years applies to directors who knowingly collected or laundered proceeds from export transactions involving sanctioned end-customers.
