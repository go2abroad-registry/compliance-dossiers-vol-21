Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); electronic filing conducted through the DGI Sede Electrónica portal (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa / https://etax.gob.pa).

E-government filing portal

A corporate S.A. obtains its RUC by: (i) completing incorporation with the Registro Público de Panamá; (ii) filing the DGI inscription application (Formulario de Registro Único de Contribuyente) together with the notarized incorporation deed, registered-agent appointment, Panama fiscal-address certificate, and legal representative identification; (iii) securing the municipal Aviso de Operación; and (iv) activating the Sede Electrónica digital certificate. The DGI issues the RUC certificate; the taxpayer additionally flags ITBMS exporter status and, upon commencement of operations, requests enrollment in the DGI electronic-invoicing registry if not automatically phased in.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (territorial basis: the resident S.A. is taxed at the entity level at the 25% corporate income tax rate solely on Panamanian-source income; foreign-source income is excluded from the Panamanian tax base. Pass-through or check-the-box treatment is not available under Panamanian law.) — A corporate S.A. obtains its RUC by: (i) completing incorporation with the Registro Público de Panamá; (ii) filing the DGI inscription application (Formulario de Registro Único de Contribuyente) together with the notarized incorporation deed, registered-agent appointment, Panama fiscal-address certificate, and legal representative identification; (iii) securing the municipal Aviso de Operación; and (iv) activating the Sede Electrónica digital certificate. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (territorial basis: the resident S.A. is taxed at the entity level at the 25% corporate income tax rate solely on Panamanian-source income; foreign-source income is excluded from the Panamanian tax base. Pass-through or check-the-box treatment is not available under Panamanian law.)

Available election forms

No Panamanian analogue of the U.S. check-the-box election exists. Election options include: (i) the Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente for annual gross income up to B/. 36,000, providing simplified ITBMS and income-tax administration; (ii) the Ley 41 de 2007 Special Purpose Entity / Sede de Empresa Multinacional (SEM) regime, which may be elected for tax-neutral regional headquarters and support services; and (iii) the ITBMS 'external-sale' election for export transactions. Hybrid-mismatch elections are not recognized; Panama applies the OECD-aligned recharacterization provisions of Ley 57 de 2016 (Tax Procedure Code).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory ITBMS registration for any business whose gross annual taxable turnover exceeds B/. 36,000, or that performs taxable imports or domestic first sales regardless of volume. A foreign e-commerce operator operating through a Panamanian S.A., holding a local bank account, or using a Panamanian fulfillment/delivery point is deemed to have ITBMS nexus in Panama.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for ITBMS at the 0% rate provided the merchandise is declared to Panamanian customs, physically departs the customs territory, and ownership passes to a non-resident buyer; cross-border e-commerce sales of hydroponics and gardening equipment to foreign end-customers therefore qualify. Services rendered to non-residents for use exclusively outside Panama are outside the territorial tax base. Domestic online sales to Panamanian consumers remain subject to 7% ITBMS. Input ITBMS attributable to exported goods is recoverable via the DGI export-refund procedure.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera – DUA, cleared through the SICOA/VUPEA system)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (conocimiento de embarque / guía aérea) evidencing physical departure of the goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (documented substitute: bank credit advices or SWIFT MT103/MT202 evidencing receipt of payment from the foreign buyer; Panama issues no statutory certificate, so the payment trail is the operative evidence)
  • Commercial invoice (factura comercial) in Spanish or English expressly referencing the ITBMS 0% or 'venta exterior' basis
  • Packing list and courier or postal despatch manifest for consolidated B2C parcels
  • Customs exit confirmation (levante) or the SICOA-generated electronic export record

Mandatory electronic invoicing through the DGI's Facturación Electrónica system, phased by DGI resolution and taxpayer category (large and medium taxpayers fully bound; small taxpayers progressively onboarded). Invoices must be transmitted in XML/JSON via the DGI web-service API for near-real-time fiscal validation; a valid control code and digital QR must appear on the PDF customer document. Legacy fiscal printers are admissible only during the transition window. Non-compliant issuance blocks valid invoice clearance and triggers administrative fines under Ley 57 de 2016.

Panama is fully dollarized (Balboa/USD parity) and imposes no central-bank exchange controls; the 180-day period therefore functions as the DGI's documentary-contemporaneity benchmark for export proceeds rather than a statutory repatriation deadline. DGI audits center on physical export validity, payment traceability, and ITBMS return consistency. False zero-rating or fraudulent refund claims trigger retrospective clawback of the 7% ITBMS differential plus official-rate interest, fines of 50%–100% of the evaded tax, administrative sanctions of up to B/. 50,000, and criminal liability for aggravated tax fraud under Ley 70 de 2019; the assessment statute of limitations is generally 5 years, extended in fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama's economy is fully dollarized with no central monetary authority issuing local currency. Both capital and current account transactions are freely convertible, with no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no documented barriers to profit remittance. Dividends, royalties, and capital repatriation can be executed via normal SWIFT transfers, subject only to standard AML/KYC documentation and the absence of a Panamanian source-of-income tax for territorial transactions. No prior approval is needed for FX conversions between the U.S. dollar and any other currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender rules for export proceeds in Panama. Exporters are not required to convert foreign currency into local currency because the U.S. dollar is already legal tender. There is no mandatory timeline for repatriation or conversion, and all export receipts may be retained in USD or any acceptable foreign currency bank account.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) - Panama has no central bank; the SBP is the sole banking and currency supervision authority, while the U.S. dollar serves as the national legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for payment collection (Stripe, PayPal, and similar offshore digital EMI payment processors) but Low as a primary bank substitute. Offshore EMIs may issue virtual USD accounts and facilitate settlement from global card networks, but they are not licensed Panamanian banks and cannot provide full treasury services with local clearing. Legal constraints include compliance with Panama Law 23 of 2015 and SBP AML/CFT directives. The accounts must be maintained with an underlying local bank for fund settlement; the company may use EMIs only as an auxiliary payment rail, not as a regulated local deposit-taking institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax on net Panama-source taxable income (Código Fiscal Article 712); a preferential 15% bracket applies to the first PAB 50,000 of taxable income for qualifying small taxpayers; no municipal or provincial corporate surtax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% definitive withholding on dividends paid out of profits already subject to the 25% corporate income tax; 20% withholding on distributions sourced from untaxed territorial-exempt income. The 10% rate is the standard treatment for a Panama S.A. distributing after-tax e-commerce profits.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within three months after the fiscal year-end; for a 31 December year-end, the filing deadline is March 31 at the latest, with staggered due dates between 1 March and 31 March according to the last digit of the RUC. Prepayments: monthly Pagos Adelantados of 2% of gross revenue are due 15 October, 15 November, and 15 December of the current tax year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same statutory deadline as Form 102 (March of the following year, staggered by RUC digit); the Local File and Master File must exist at the filing date and be produced within 45 working days of the DGI summons.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB 2,000,000 in related-party transactions during the fiscal year triggers the mandatory informative return and Local File under Executive Decree 62 of 2019. The Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required when multinational consolidated revenue exceeds PAB 750,000,000. Even below the threshold, every related-party transaction must be priced at arm's length and contemporaneous documentation kept for 5 years.

Economic substance scope: The Panamanian Economic Substance Regulations (Law 254 of 2021, implemented by Executive Decree 649 of 2022) require a Sociedad Anónima carrying on relevant activities, including distribution and service centres, financing and leasing, intellectual-property holding, and headquarters operations, to maintain a physical office in Panama, employ economically significant staff, and incur operating expenditure proportionate to the booked income. An online hydroponics retailer that contracts, invoices, and manages logistics risk from Panama will satisfy the substance test. A mail-drop or nominee-only entity that merely routes Stripe and PayPal settlements while the real management is abroad will be treated as a shell company, triggering ESR penalties, dissolution procedures, and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Underreporting Panama-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Civil fiscal penalty of 100% to 200% of the evaded tax (Código Fiscal Articles 748-751), plus the 4% monthly late-surcharge, 1% monthly default interest, and criminal fines of 2 to 5 times the amount defrauded when prosecuted for criminal tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and the legal representative who sign false returns are personally liable and face 2 to 5 years' imprisonment, a 5-year ban from managing Panamanian companies, seizure of personal assets, and piercing of the corporate veil where fraud is established.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties of PAB 10,000 to PAB 500,000 per fiscal year, cancellation of the registered agent, administrative dissolution, retroactive disallowance of the territorial-source position, and a 10% dividend tax on presumed distributions.

Director personal liability: De facto directors face personal fines of PAB 5,000 to PAB 100,000, a 2-year prohibition from serving as directors of Panamanian companies, and extraterritorial enforcement through the Inter-American Convention on Foreign Judgments.
AML / Customer Due Diligence Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015, corporate fines of PAB 100,000 to PAB 1,000,000, freezing of merchant and bank accounts, confiscation of proceeds, and cancellation of the regional headquarters or general licence.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers face up to 5 years' imprisonment for money laundering, travel bans, asset confiscation, and extradition exposure under FATF-recommended frameworks.
Foreign Permanent Establishment / Unlicensed Overseas Nexus

Statutory sanction: The foreign jurisdiction may impose corporate income tax on the permanent-establishment profit at rates of 15% to 30%, penalties of up to 25% of the underpayment, and disallowance of intercompany warehouse fees under the BEPS Action 7 agency-PE doctrine.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally served with foreign tax assessments if the corporate veil is abused, face civil restitution orders, travel bans upon conviction for wilful foreign tax evasion, and potential imprisonment in the host jurisdiction.
