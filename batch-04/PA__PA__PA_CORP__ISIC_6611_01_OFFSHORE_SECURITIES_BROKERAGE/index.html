Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), dependency of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); official portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa; taxpayer self-service platform: e-Tax 2.0 / SICE (Sistema de Información de Clientes Externos) accessible via DGI web services; electronic invoicing subsystem: Facturación Electrónica DGI, with SMV (Superintendencia del Mercado de Valores de Panamá) as the concurrent securities regulator for the licensed Casa de Valores.

E-government filing portal

Foreign shareholders act through a licensed Panamanian Resident Agent and counsel. Steps: (1) incorporate the Sociedad Anónima in the Registro Público de Panamá and file the Aviso de Operación (commencement notice) with DGI via Form 600/o or e-Tax 2.0; (2) apply for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), the sole TIN, submitting the certified articles of incorporation, Registro Público 'Ficha' excerpt, Resident Agent acceptance, officer/shareholder ID or passport copies, and the signed RUC application through e-Tax 2.0 or a notarized DGI form; (3) the RUC application simultaneously opens the ITBMS account, with principal activity coded ISIC 6611 / NAICS 523120; (4) obtain the Firma Digital (digital signature certificate from an AIG-accredited CA) to authenticate all e-Tax 2.0 and e-invoicing transactions; (5) obtain the SMV 'Casa de Valores' license (60-120 days) and the DGI Paz y Salvo before launching operations. No minimum capital and no local ownership are required; a Panama-domiciled Resident Agent is mandatory. Estimated DGI turnaround for the RUC is 5-10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — the Panamanian S.A. is an entity-level taxpayer (no pass-through, no check-the-box) subject to corporate income tax (ISR) at the 25% statutory rate only on Panamanian-source net income under the territorial principle of the Código Fiscal. For a retail offshore securities broker, income from execution, custody, CFDs, and margin services rendered to non-resident clients on foreign markets, contracted and settled abroad, is foreign-source and excluded from ISR and ITBMS. No CFC regime applies; dividends paid from non-taxed foreign-source profits are not subject to the 5% Panamanian dividend withholding. Tax-exempt-zone regimes (e.g., Semilla, PEAR) are not applicable to an SMV-licensed broker operating in the national territory.

Available election forms

Panamanian law provides no US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch election. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) 'Exportador de Servicios' election — a sworn declaration to DGI enabling 0% ITBMS on qualifying offshore services and access to input ITBMS 'reintegro' (refund/credit); (ii) ISR group consolidation election for affiliated Panamanian entities with 80%+ common ownership, subject to DGI notification; (iii) optional partial-payment regime under Article 699-A of the Código Fiscal (quarterly installments on gross revenue) as a proxy for final ISR; (iv) the small-taxpayer ITBMS exemption election, expressly unavailable to licensed securities intermediaries; (v) no treaty election is available, as Panama has no OECD-model DTTs relevant to brokerage income, only TIEAs, making territorial sourcing the sole structural reliance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PAB 36,000 (B/.36,000, equivalent to US$36,000 under the 1:1 Balboa/USD constitutional peg), being the annual gross taxable turnover above which ITBMS registration, monthly returns, and e-invoicing become mandatory (Código Fiscal Art. 1918-C). An SMV-licensed securities brokerage is a de facto 'contribuyente' because its licensed activity carries material turnover; it must register for ITBMS from the first day of operations and will typically be administered as a Grande Contribuyente (large taxpayer) by DGI.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Two layers apply. (a) Territorial exclusion: brokerage, CFD, and advisory services to non-resident clients where the order is routed to and executed on foreign venues, settlement occurs in the client's overseas account, and the services are not used or exploited in Panama are foreign-source and outside the ITBMS base entirely; no zero-rating is needed. (b) 'Exportador de Servicios' convention: where DGI re-characterizes any element as a Panamanian service (e.g., local advisory or Panama-listed asset custody), the exporter election zero-rates the invoice at 0% and activates the input ITBMS credit/refund path. Eligibility conditions: non-resident certification of the client (tax-domicile affidavit), a written service agreement stating foreign situs and offshore benefit, SMV order-routing evidence, and proof that consideration was collected from abroad.

  • Declaración Única Aduanera (DUA) filed via SICE/VUPEA — required only when physical goods (e.g., computer hardware, banknotes) cross the border; not applicable to pure offshore service exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (goods only); for services: executed brokerage agreement, ISIC 6611 scope statement, and SMV order-routing records evidencing execution on foreign exchanges
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank credit advice or SWIFT MT103/MT202 showing settlement of foreign-source fees received from the non-resident client within the repatriation window and credited to the S.A.'s Panamanian bank account
  • DGI Exporter of Services annual declaration and the client's non-residency tax-domicile affidavit, together with segregated client account statements from the foreign clearing venue

Mandatory 'Factura Electrónica' regime under DGI Resolutions 201-5455 and 202-4580, as consolidated by the Código de Procedimiento Tributario (Ley 254 de 2024). All taxpayers, including Casas de Valores, must issue invoices through DGI-authorized e-invoicing operators or the DGI free web tool; each invoice is transmitted in near-real time over REST/SOAP API to the DGI clearance platform, automatically validated, and returned with a unique Código de Trazabilidad and QR code that must appear on the invoice before delivery to the client; digital stamping via Firma Digital is required, and legacy pre-printed paper invoices are no longer admissible as tax documents.

Panama is fully dollarized with no exchange-control statute, so the 180-day window represents the DGI's practical evidentiary expectation that foreign-source revenue be 'realizada' (materialized) in the S.A.'s books; collections deferred beyond 12 months without documented agency or custody justification invite recharacterization as Panamanian-source income. Fraudulent zero-rating or refund claims trigger Código Fiscal Articles 748-760: a 100% surcharge on evaded ITBMS plus interest at 1.5% per month (18% annual) from the default date; the tax reassessment window is 3 years, extended to 6 years for unfiled returns and 10 years for fraud or missing records. Law 8 of 2010 criminalizes tax evasion with 2-5 years imprisonment, and DGI will claw back every 'reintegro' credit with doubling where exporter status was obtained by misrepresentation. Concurrently, the SMV may impose administrative fines on licensed Casas de Valores for false or incomplete books and suspend the securities license.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized. The Balboa (PAB) exists only as a notional unit at fixed 1:1 parity with the USD, and the USD is the sole circulating legal tender. Both capital and current account transactions are freely convertible, with no central-bank approvals, currency budgets, exchange-control registration, or remittance surcharges. For the territorial-tax Sociedad Anónima licensed as an offshore securities broker under ISIC 6611, income earned outside Panama is excluded from the Panamanian income-tax base; Panama-sourced income is taxed at 25%, and dividends remitted to non-resident shareholders are exempt from Panamanian withholding tax. The only practical hurdles are AML/EDD screening by SBP-regulated banks, documentary evidence of lawful source of funds, and SMV compliance on client-asset segregation and margin financing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no export-proceeds surrender, conversion, or retention rules. There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export trade receipts or any other foreign-currency earnings, because the USD is legal tender and banks may freely maintain foreign-currency balances. Accordingly, the brokerage's operating receipts, including client money in segregated accounts and commissions in USD or other convertible currencies, are not subject to any surrender obligation, central-bank preclearance, or conversion tax, and may be applied directly to settlement, disbursement, and treasury management.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). Panama has no de jure central bank; the SBP is the statutory banking regulator and licensing authority for banks, foreign-exchange houses and payment-service providers. Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent and clearing banker for the State, while the Superintendencia del Mercado de Valores (SMV) supervises licensed securities brokers under Decree-Law 1 of 1999, as amended notably by Law 60 of 2012. These authorities jointly define the de facto monetary-banking supervisory perimeter for USD clearing, treasury operations and correspondent banking in Panama.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium as an auxiliary disbursement rail; Low as the sole primary treasury banker. Any entity engaged in money or value transfer, payment initiation, or e-money issuance must be licensed by the SBP under the Panamanian Banking Law (Decree-Law 9 of 1998) or hold a valid SBP payment-service provider authorization. An offshore digital EMI not licensed or recognized by the SBP cannot legally hold settlement accounts for the broker's Panamanian client funds and will not be accepted by SBP-regulated banks as a pass-through account absent demonstrated regulatory equivalence. A non-client treasury-side balance may be held with an approved EMI maintaining a qualifying arrangement with an SBP-licensed partner bank, executing SWIFT-compliant MT103 messages, and receiving a favorable AML risk opinion from the broker's compliance officer; crypto-asset-focused EMIs are prohibited for any client-money function.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity or quasi-equity capital is injected in USD via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202/202COV where a correspondent bank participates) from the shareholder's bank to the broker's operational USD account at an SBP-licensed bank in Panama. Panama does not require a national purpose-code filing; the beneficiary bank applies an internal transaction classification for capital or related-party financing and references the corporate RUC for lodgement confirmation. The supporting bundle comprising shareholder minutes, the amended Public Registry excerpt, share certificates or loan agreements, and evidence of the shareholder's financial capacity must be delivered within 10 to 15 business days of the lodgement. Central-bank preclearance is not required; the receiving bank performs standard CDD and beneficial-ownership verification before any onward release.Outbound disbursements are settled from the segregated client-money account or the proprietary operational account depending on purpose. Client trade settlements, margin calls, and withdrawal requests are cleared via SWIFT MT103 across the correspondent network, settling in USD through Fedwire and CHIPS. Institutional bond and equity purchases settle on a delivery-versus-payment basis through international central securities depositories (Clearstream and Euroclear), while commercial letters of credit governed by UCP600 support structured trade-finance disbursements. Box 1 - RUC/NIF 15-digit Registro Único de Contribuyente issued to the Sociedad Anónima. File under the S.A.'s Panamanian RUC only; do not enter a foreign tax reference of a non-resident shareholder. Line 401 - Ingresos de fuente panameña Gross Panama-source commission, spread, custody fee and other brokerage income. Include only income from Panamanian counterparties, Panama-listed securities or execution occurring within Panama; all other income is excluded. Line 402 - Ingresos de fuente extranjera y exentos Gross income excluded from Panamanian tax by the territorial-source rule. Claim the territorial exclusion for foreign-market orders, non-resident client commissions and offshore CFD gains; retain client KYC, order routing and execution records proving the economic activity occurred abroad. Late filing attracts a 10% surcharge on the assessed ISR where tax is due, and a fine of B/.50-B/.500 where no tax is due; late payment accrues a 2% monthly arrears surcharge plus legal interest; prolonged non-filing leads to inclusion in the DGI public defaulters registry and possible attachment of bank accounts.
Deductions - Costos y gastos deducibles Costs and deductions attributable to Panama-source gross income. Claim only documented expenses (facturas, bank statements) allocable to Panama-source revenue; costs of a purely foreign book are not deductible against Panamanian income. Late filing attracts a 10% surcharge on the assessed ISR where tax is due, and a fine of B/.50-B/.500 where no tax is due; late payment accrues a 2% monthly arrears surcharge plus legal interest; prolonged non-filing leads to inclusion in the DGI public defaulters registry and possible attachment of bank accounts.
Renta neta gravable Panama-source gross income less allowable deductions. An offshore-only S.A. should present a null or fully exempt computation, never a negative tax base. Late filing attracts a 10% surcharge on the assessed ISR where tax is due, and a fine of B/.50-B/.500 where no tax is due; late payment accrues a 2% monthly arrears surcharge plus legal interest; prolonged non-filing leads to inclusion in the DGI public defaulters registry and possible attachment of bank accounts.
ISR 25% Corporate income tax at the statutory 25% rate on net taxable income. Compute 25% only if taxable Panama-source income exists; credit any monthly provisional payments (Form 103) in the corresponding field. Late filing attracts a 10% surcharge on the assessed ISR where tax is due, and a fine of B/.50-B/.500 where no tax is due; late payment accrues a 2% monthly arrears surcharge plus legal interest; prolonged non-filing leads to inclusion in the DGI public defaulters registry and possible attachment of bank accounts.
Saldo a pagar / saldo a favor Final net tax remittance or refund balance after credits. Remit the balance by 31 March; a zero-tax return must still be filed to preserve the territorial exemption and to avoid the B/.50-B/.500 penalty. Late filing attracts a 10% surcharge on the assessed ISR where tax is due, and a fine of B/.50-B/.500 where no tax is due; late payment accrues a 2% monthly arrears surcharge plus legal interest; prolonged non-filing leads to inclusion in the DGI public defaulters registry and possible attachment of bank accounts.
RUC / NIF Tax identification of the S.A. remitting the provisional payment. Use the Panamanian RUC; no separate foreign identification is admissible. Failure to file the monthly declaration carries a fine of B/.50-B/.500 and 2% monthly surcharge on unpaid provisional ISR, plus escalation to a larger administrative fine in cases of repeated non-compliance.
Base estimada gravable Estimated current-year Panama-source taxable base for the month. Estimate zero for a purely foreign-source brokerage book; the DGI may demand a written technical explanation if repeated zero declarations are made. Failure to file the monthly declaration carries a fine of B/.50-B/.500 and 2% monthly surcharge on unpaid provisional ISR, plus escalation to a larger administrative fine in cases of repeated non-compliance.
Pago provisional Amount remitted, calculated as 25% of the estimated monthly taxable base. Credit all provisional payments against the final Form 101 liability; overpayment is refundable or offsettable. Failure to file the monthly declaration carries a fine of B/.50-B/.500 and 2% monthly surcharge on unpaid provisional ISR, plus escalation to a larger administrative fine in cases of repeated non-compliance.
