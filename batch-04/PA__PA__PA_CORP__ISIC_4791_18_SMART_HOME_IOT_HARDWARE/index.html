Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama has no US-style check-the-box election; the S.A. is irrevocably a separate entity-level taxpayer. Available elective regimes relevant to an IoT exporter include: (i) the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007, providing a reduced income tax rate on qualifying export services; (ii) the optional simplified small-taxpayer regime for natural persons or micro-enterprises with turnover below PAB 30,000, generally unavailable to a standard capital S.A.; and (iii) elective accelerated depreciation of fixed assets under the Código Fiscal. On hybrid mismatches, Panama applies OECD BEPS-aligned rules enacted via Law 134 of 2020: a deduction is denied where a payment suffers inconsistent characterization as debt or equity between Panama and a no-tax or preferential-regime jurisdiction, with no unilateral treaty-override election available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary turnover threshold applies to companies: every entity habitually transferring movable goods or services in Panama must register for ITBMS from the first taxable transaction under the territorial supply test. A Panama S.A. retailing IoT hardware locally must register regardless of revenue; only unincorporated micro-taxpayers may elect the simplified 'pequeño contribuyente' regime up to PAB 30,000 annual receipts.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% ITBMS applies to: (i) definitive export sales of movable goods physically leaving Panamanian customs territory under an ANA export DUA; (ii) export services supplied to non-residents for use entirely abroad; and (iii) supplies to special fiscal zones such as the Colón Free Zone and Panamá Pacífico. For smart-home IoT hardware retail, an online sale to a foreign end-customer qualifies where the S.A. ships the devices from Panama to a foreign address via a licensed courier or customs carrier, files the DUA export declaration, retains the courier waybill or airway bill, and receives the purchase price from abroad. Re-exports of imported goods in transit are equally zero-rated provided the goods do not enter local consumption.

  • ANA customs export declaration — Declaración Única de Aduanas (DUA) with 'salida del país' customs exit status
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing physical departure from Panamanian customs territory
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • ITBMS zero-rated export invoice bearing the DGI fiscal clearance code
  • Purchase and provenance documentation for cost traceability and audit substantiation

Mandatory electronic invoicing under DGI Resolution 201-1401 (2019) and implementing rules: all registered taxpayers must issue invoices through the Sistema de Facturación Electrónica de Panamá (SFEP), either through the DGI's free web portal or an authorized Operador Tecnológico using system-to-system API integration. Each invoice is transmitted as XML and validated in real time by the DGI, which assigns a unique fiscal clearance code and QR digital stamp before the invoice may be delivered to the customer; B2C receipts through Stripe/Shopify Payments rails must be integrated into the SFEP billing flow or are treated as invalid for ITBMS credit purposes, with fines per uninvoiced operation.

Export zero-rating is a recurrent DGI audit focus cross-checked against ANA export manifests and bank records. If the exporter cannot substantiate the physical exit of goods, collection from the foreign buyer, or repatriation of proceeds within the documented 180-day commercial term, the DGI recharacterizes the sale as domestic, assesses 7% ITBMS on the gross invoice, applies recargos and monthly statutory interest, and imposes administrative fines of up to 150% of the omitted tax under the Código Fiscal. Fraudulent or sham export documentation constitutes the criminal offense of tax defraudación in Panama, punishable by 2 to 5 years' imprisonment, with the audit statute of limitations extended from 5 to 10 fiscal years and retrospective clawback of any ITBMS refunds or tax credits previously granted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full bilateral convertibility in both current and capital accounts. There are no restricting surrender requirements, no prior import payment authorization, no foreign-owned currency borrowing limits, and no profit-remittance licensing for a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima under the Territorial tax regime established by the 2004 Fiscal Code reform. Repatriation friction is fiscal, not regulatory: dividends declared by an S.A. to non-resident shareholders are subject to a 10% dividend withholding tax (5% where the lower 4% prefixed CINII-type regime applies), while branch remittances are exempt. The IoT hardware retailer (ISIC 4791) may therefore receive working capital injections, advance shareholder subordinated loans, and deferred trade payables denominated in USD without any quantitative ceiling.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion timeline exists. Panamanian law imposes zero mandatory conversion ratios for export proceeds because no non-dollar domestic monetary base exists; the US Dollar is the clearing and store-of-value currency. The only enforceable duties regarding export receipts are documentary and AML/CFT-based: cross-border e-commerce sales proceeds must be traceable via commercial invoices, shipping manifests, logistics waybills, and payment settlement reports (Stripe/Shopify Payments statements) that substantiate each remittance to the Caribbean/regional banking compliance function, with a 5-year record retention standard under the SBP AML rule framework.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama has no traditional central bank. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), created under Decree-Law No. 9 of 1998, acts as the sole bank supervisor, licensing and regulating banks, fintechs, and payment processors under the General Banking Law. Banco Nacional de Panamá (BNP), a state-owned institution, performs fiscal depository and some clearing functions but has no monetary policy, reserve, or exchange-control mandate. Because the US Dollar has been legal tender since 1904 and the Balboa circulates only as a 1:1 pegged coin unit, monetary sovereignty is effectively delegated to the US Federal Reserve system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate-to-Low. Foreign open-banking EMIs (UK/EEA/Asian e-money houses) may accept settlement and provide onboarding agility, but they cannot lawfully open a Panamanian pass-through treasury account or clear FX locally unless licensed as an electronic-money issuer or authorized sandbox participant under Law No. 16 of 2020 and SBP licensing rules. Viable combined model: a SBP-licensed local banking partner holds the GDP-level treasury account while Stripe/Shopify Payments operating flows (where the merchant entity is a US or other qualifying subsidiary) settle onward through SWIFT into the Panamanian corporate account; unlicensed offshore EMIs should be used only as supplementary receivables funnels, never as the primary liquidity reservoir.
