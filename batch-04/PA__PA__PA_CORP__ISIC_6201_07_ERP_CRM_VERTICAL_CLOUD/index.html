Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Panama; official taxpayer portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI / e-Tax 2.0 (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa and https://etax.dgi.gob.pa).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident-owned Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) must: (1) complete incorporation before the Registro Público de Panamá, including appointment of a resident agent (agente residente) and filing of the public deed; (2) legalize the corporate ledger before the DGI or other competent authority as applicable; (3) file the RUC registration application (Solicitud de Inscripción / Aviso de Inscripción) with the DGI, accompanied by the notarized power of attorney, certificate of incorporation (Ficha Registral), resident agent certification, and proof of fiscal address; (4) receive the permanent RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) tax identification number; (5) register the ISIC 6201 economic activity (computer programming / vertical ERP-CRM cloud software) and any relevant sub-activities; (6) activate the DGI e-Tax account and obtain an advanced digital signature (firma avanzada) issued under Panama's authorized digital-certification framework; and (7) apply for DGI authorization to issue invoices (authorized invoice booklets or DGI-certified electronic invoicing) and for any applicable withholding registrations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope (entity-level taxation): the S.A. is taxed as a separate corporation exclusively on Panama-source income under the territorial/source system; foreign-source income is statutorily exempt from Panamanian income tax; no pass-through or tax-transparent treatment exists.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid entity election is available under Panamanian law; the S.A. is mandatorily treated as a corporation. Available optional elections: (i) voluntary registration for ITBMS before breaching the B/.36,000 annual threshold, necessary to recover export-related input ITBMS credits; (ii) election of the simplified small-taxpayer regime (RISPEC) if the entity meets statutory low-revenue and low-asset thresholds; (iii) formal export zero-rating status as an exporter of services with DGI-approved documentation; (iv) no CFC or hybrid mismatch elections; economic substance and territorial-nexus documentation must be maintained to preserve the foreign-source income exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 (Panamanian balboas, at par with US$) of annual gross receipts from taxable sales of goods and services; mandatory RUC and ITBMS registration once the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold, especially for exporting SaaS/cloud entities seeking input credit recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are eligible for 0% ITBMS when they leave Panama to a non-resident purchaser and are covered by a valid customs export declaration. Services, including vertical cloud ERP/CRM subscriptions, qualify for 0% export treatment when rendered to a non-resident subscriber whose use/benefit occurs wholly outside Panama; the DGI examines the foreign subscriber status (foreign RUC/tax ID), absence of Panama-source exploitation, offshore platform delivery, service contract terms, and payment remitted from abroad. Pure SaaS/cloud download and access qualify if the company demonstrates the non-Panamanian nexus; related input ITBMS may be credited or refunded as an export over-credit. Panama's dollarized system imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline, but the 180-day collection window below is the customary DGI/banking evidentiary benchmark for export proceeds.

  • Customs export declaration (Declaración de Exportación / DUA) electronically filed where physical media, servers, or equipment leave Panama; not required for pure cloud delivery
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier receipt evidencing physical export (if goods are shipped)
  • Original DGI-authorized 0% ITBMS export invoice addressed to the non-resident client
  • Executed software subscription/SOW showing the foreign subscriber, delivery method, and offshore use/benefit
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit advice (abono bancario / certificación de divisas) proving receipt of funds from abroad
  • ERP/CRM platform usage, login, and download logs for the subscription period evidencing remote offshore fulfillment

Panama mandates DGI-authorized electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica de Panamá). The S.A. must generate invoices in DGI-compatible XML format through a certified billing system integrated with the vertical ERP/CRM cloud platform, transmit to the DGI via the e-Tax portal/web-services (API), obtain automated DGI fiscal authorization (validación/autorización fiscal) with digital signature and QR-code labeling, and preserve electronic books of sales and purchases; real-time clearance is phased by taxpayer category, and only DGI-validated invoices support input credits, exporter zero-rating, and refund claims.

The DGI audits export zero-rating by cross-matching invoices, customs declarations, contracts, bank proceeds, and platform delivery logs. Penalties for fraudulent or improperly documented export zero-rating include retrospective reassessment at 7% ITBMS plus statutory monthly surcharges/interest, substantial fines scaled to the tax omitted (including aggravated amounts for fraud), criminal sanctions for tax crimes under Ley 76 de 2019 (defraudación tributaria) and the Código Penal, cancellation of RUC/electronic-invoicing authorization and of exporter zero-rating status, denial or clawback of ITBMS refunds, and non-deductibility of penalties; documentation must be retained for the Código Fiscal prescription period, which is extended in cases of fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized, freely convertible jurisdiction: the U.S. dollar is legal tender for all transactions and the balboa exists only as an accounting unit, so there are no capital account restrictions, no exchange licensing, and no prior-approval regime for dividends, intercompany loans, or equity injections. Current-account convertibility is unrestricted and profit remittance is unimpeded; the only statutory friction for foreign shareholders is Panama's territorial income tax, under which Panamanian-source profits distributed to shareholders carry a 5% withholding dividend tax (or 10% if the corporate tax made-effective threshold is bypassed), while foreign-source profits of the S.A. are distributable free of Panamanian tax. No shareholder registry filing or currency conversion certificate is required before remittance; the practical gate is instead banking compliance under Ley 23 de 2015 plus a valid Paz y Salvo Fiscal (tax clearance) for any disbursement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export proceeds, because Panama does not issue a national fiat currency. Software and cloud service exports (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 511210) earned through ERP/CRM subscriptions may be held offshore indefinitely or credited directly to any SBP-licensed bank in U.S. dollars; the only obligations are the bank's suspicious-transaction reporting and the exporter's proper service-export invoicing, which in Panama requires an issued factura for exported services and inclusion of the corresponding Swift MT103 record in the annual KYC file of the exporter.

Central bank supervision: Panama maintains no central bank, no monetary authority and no exchange control administration. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) supervises licensed banks as the de facto financial regulator, while Banco Nacional de Panamá operates merely as the State's fiscal agent, clearing agent for checks and ACH, and lender of last resort without currency issuance powers; the balboa coin is issued by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) solely as a fractional token fully backed 1:1 by the U.S. dollar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium as a secondary settlement rail but Low as the primary treasury account. Panama's fintech framework (Ley 142 de 2021 and SBP implementing agreements) grants SBP authorizations to locally incorporated non-bank payment providers, but there is no direct EMI licence class; offshore EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Mercury, etc.) hold only custodial client funds through correspondent partners and cannot obtain a Panamanian-style bank account number, cannot clear through ACH Panamá, and would expose the S.A. to vendor-risk AML flags if used as the principal operating account. For card collection, Stripe Billing is not directly usable by a Panama-incorporated S.A. because Panama is outside Stripe's currently published merchant-support list; the practical blueprint is a Delaware or U.S. contracting entity collecting Stripe settlements and re-invoicing PA_CORP, with monthly intercompany SWIFT sweeps; local Direct Debit collection is instead performed through ACH Panamá and card clearing through DataClave and SBP-bank acquirers.

For card collection, Stripe Billing is not directly usable by a Panama-incorporated S.A. because Panama is outside Stripe's currently published merchant-support list; the practical blueprint is a Delaware or U.S. contracting entity collecting Stripe settlements and re-invoicing PA_CORP, with monthly intercompany SWIFT sweeps; local Direct Debit collection is instead performed through ACH Panamá and card clearing through DataClave and SBP-bank acquirers.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on Panamanian-source income for a standard Sociedad Anónima (S.A.); additional complementary dividend withholding applies at distribution.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% was the general statutory rate for dividends paid out of profits subject to corporate income tax; however, for distributions from profits that have already borne the 25% corporate tax (i.e., “fully taxed profits”), the dividend withholding acts as a final tax at the shareholder level, effectively the DGI receives a split: 10% directly on the dividend paid. Dividends paid out of tax-exempt foreign-source income of a Panama holding company are subject to a 0% rate if the company is not otherwise in a profit-distribution limitation; but distributions from a company benefiting from Panama Pacifico full exemption are subject to a 5% distribution tax at the time of remittance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is due on April 30 of the fiscal year following the calendar tax year. For non-calendar fiscal years, the due date is the last day of the fourth month after the fiscal year-end, unless a different date has been authorised by the DGI. Monthly advance withholding returns (for employees, vendors, rental payers, and dividend distributions) are required if applicable under Article 678 or under the specific regime of the taxpayer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 1,000,000 (one million Panamanian Balboas, equal to USD) aggregate related-party transactions during the taxable year triggers mandatory transfer-pricing documentation. Transactions between a local S.A. and a foreign parent or sister company (e.g., intra-group software licensing, cloud hosting fees, management charges, and the said CRM vertical’s intercompany service contracts) are covered. Additionally, the taxpayer must file an electronic informative return “Declaración Informativa de Transacciones con Partes Relacionadas” when annual related-party transactions exceed B/. 300,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama tax authorities and business courts apply a “economic substance” doctrine to prevent perceived artificial structures. While no general statutory economic substance requirement yet applies to every S.A., the Tax Code (as reformed by Law 57 of 2018) requires that: (i) any deduction for intra-group payments must have a genuine business need and actual economic activity; (ii) “mannequin” or so-called shell corporations are subject to recharacterisation and the DGI may ignore legal personality where the company exists solely to avoid Panama-source taxes; (iii) the Tax Administration can apply the “place of effective management” test for classifying a Panama company as ultra-territorial when Board meetings and operational decisions are taken abroad. Financial criminal courts in Panama additionally consider the substance criteria under FATF-inspired AML/CFT measures, requiring a physical office, real employees, accounting records, and compliance with AML beneficial ownership reporting. Regular use under an offshore cloud structure without any local employees, local revenue operations, or proper governance will normally trigger compulsory liquidation under the “ley de licencia anual” or serve as evidence of fraud.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under the Panama Tax Code (Articles 1147, 1148, and 1181), the tax authority may impose up to 100% of the evaded tax differential, plus compound interest from the original due date. Additional fine for not filing the annual declaration: between 2% and 25% of the tax due. The evasion amount may be escalated for using fictitious invoices or shell entities.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing is permitted under Panama civil procedure when a director signs an incomplete or false tax return in bad faith or knowingly participates in hiding Panamanian-source income; such directors may be personally liable for restitution of the unpaid corporate tax, plus interest, and may face criminal imprisonment from 2 to 5 years and a lifetime prohibition from serving as a director in Panama if convicted of tax fraud involving amounts exceeding B/. 300,000.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DGI can disallow the entire claimed exemption, recharacterise 100% of the profits as Panamanian-source, and levy the 25% corporate rate plus 10% dividend withholding on unpaid distributions. The company may be struck off the Registro Público after two consecutive annual licence fees are unpaid; the Financial Intelligence Unit may issue a “destructive measure” freezing assets, and the tax authority may seek a judicial order for judicial dissolution of the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors of an alleged shell may be held in civil restitution for intra-group assets approaching the company’s lost income, be barred from leaving Panama pending the criminal process, and face charges under Law 41 of 2000 and the Panama Criminal Code, Article 290, for false corporate declarations, with penalties of up to 6 years’ imprisonment.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Disregard

Statutory sanction: Panama’s Decree Law 12 of 2015, further reformed in 2018 and 2020, provides fines of up to B/. 5 million for a legal entity, suspension of the licence, and indefinite disqualification for officers. Failure to report beneficial ownership to the UAF (Unidad de Análisis Financiero) is an infraction punishable by B/. 100,000 per non-filed report.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false beneficial ownership declarations or knowingly allow undisclosed ownership expose themselves to civil penalties up to three times the financial loss generated, travel bans of up to 90 days at the request of the Public Ministry, and imprisonment from 3 to 7 years under Article 254-C of the Panamanian Penal Code for money laundering.
Transfer Pricing / Arm’s Length Violation

Statutory sanction: The DGI can adjust taxable income, deny deductions for excess royalty transfers, and impose a 10% penalty on the TP adjustment plus interest at the statutory bank rate. Should the adjustment exceed B/. 1 million, the DGI may treat the case as a serious infraction and apply a 25% surcharge. Failure to provide documentation within 45 business days of the DGI’s request triggers an automatic adverse inference and a separate fixed fine of B/. 10,000 to B/. 300,000, depending on the company’s size.

Director personal liability: Where the TP policy was orchestrated by the parent company’s directors and supported by artificially low documentation, Panamanian courts may hold local directors jointly and severally liable for the tax adjustment, may issue an embargo of their personal property abroad, and, where intentional evasion is proven, can order their extradition to face criminal tax evasion charges carrying a 3 to 5-year prison sentence.
