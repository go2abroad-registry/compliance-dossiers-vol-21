Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Panama law imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a Sociedad Anónima. Shares may be subscribed without full payment. Escrow is not statutorily required; however, for maritime installment LC and project financing structures, subscription funds may be placed in a Panamanian banking escrow account by contractual arrangement.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; online fiscal portal: SIT/e-Tax (Sistema de Información Tributaria) at https://etax.mef.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) obtains its Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC/TIN) by filing Form 911 through the SIT/e-Tax portal. The filing package comprises: (i) the notarized and publicly registered certificate of incorporation (escritura pública and inscripción en el Registro Público); (ii) bylaws and certified identification of directors and ultimate beneficial owner; (iii) an irrevocable power of attorney naming a Panamanian resident agent/legal representative; (iv) a local address, which may be the registered agent's office; and (v) the ITBMS and e-invoicing questionnaire. The DGI reviews the application and issues the RUC certificate and cédula RUC, automatically integrating income tax, ITBMS, withholding, and electronic-invoicing obligations. No minimum paid-in capital is required. Non-resident ownership is unrestricted. Processing is fully electronic and is generally completed within 5-10 business days; the shipyard S.A. must register before commencing operations or within 30 days after incorporation in Panama.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope (entity-level taxation): The S.A. is a taxable corporation subject to Panamanian corporate income tax (ISR) at the statutory 25% headline rate on Panama-source income only. Foreign-source income of the shipyard from export contracts is excluded from the Panamanian tax base; the entity is not subject to worldwide income or CFC inclusion rules and is not eligible for pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election system. A shipyard S.A. may nonetheless elect: (i) the dividend anticipado (quick/estimated dividend) mechanism to close the fiscal year with a deemed 30% profit distribution; (ii) fiscal consolidation for affiliated taxpayers under DGI authorization; (iii) special export-zone, free-zone, or SEM (multinational headquarters) regimes conferring ITBMS/ISR exemptions if the yard or headquarters service qualifies; (iv) the special international vessel registration regime for vessels engaged in international trade, with associated tax benefits; and (v) territorial-tax treatment for non-Panamanian yard work (default). All elections must be prospectively approved by the DGI.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$250,000 (USD/PAB equivalent) of annual gross taxable turnover; mandatory registration as of the first calendar month in which the rolling 12-month turnover exceeds the threshold, with voluntary registration permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A shipyard S.A. may zero-rate (0% ITBMS) the supply of newly constructed ocean container vessels, as well as ship repair/conversion services, when the vessel is delivered to a non-resident owner and physically exported from Panama, in accordance with Article 24 of the ITBMS law (Law 63 of 2009). Services such as design, engineering, and vessel supervision performed for foreign principals and consumed entirely outside Panama are also eligible. To claim the zero rate, the yard must evidence: (a) non-resident status of the buyer; (b) physical exit of the vessel from Panamanian territorial waters; (c) transfer of title and delivery/acceptance abroad; (d) absence of domestic use; and (e) proper reporting of the export through the SIGA single window with DGI cross-validation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Mercancías - DUM) accepted by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) in the SIGA electronic single window
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent Sea Waybill issued to the foreign owner
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) from a Panamanian licensed bank evidencing proceeds received
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • Certificate of vessel registry/flag-out (e.g., Patente de Navegación or deletion certificate)
  • Delivery and Acceptance Protocol (Protocolo de Entrega) with sea-trial report signed by the foreign buyer

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica) via the SIT platform. The shipyard must issue XML invoices through a DGI-authorized electronic invoicing provider (facturador electrónico) or the SIT/e-Tax channel, obtain a digital finalization code (constancia de autorización) from the DGI before the invoice is legally effective, and transmit transactions synchronously to the SIT via REST APIs. Paper invoices no longer support input VAT credits after the phased mandate (fully applicable to large and mid-size taxpayers as of 2024). The monthly ITBMS return (Form 701), annual income tax return, and the mandatory Digital Tax Ledger (Libro de Compras y Ventas Electrónico) must be filed electronically; the DGI's system automatically validates and cross-references all invoices with export declarations.

DGI jointly with ANA conducts risk-based audits of zero-rating claims, verifying the DUM, the B/L, bank records, and FX repatriation. Non-repatriation of export proceeds within the 180-day deadline (unless a justified extension is granted by the MEF for open-account maritime financing) or failure to maintain the required documentation triggers: (i) retroactive ITBMS assessment at 7% on the entire consideration; (ii) late-payment interest at 1.5% per month; (iii) administrative fines ranging from $500 to $25,000 per false or missing invoice; (iv) suspension or cancellation of electronic-invoicing authorization and RUC; and (v) criminal tax-fraud liability under Articles 451-452 of the Código Penal (two to five years of imprisonment) for deliberate falsification, with the registered agent's license also exposed to sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama imposes no exchange controls and maintains complete convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. There are no central bank authorizations, no currency approval regimes, and no restrictions applicable to capital injections, dividend remittances, debt-service payments, or repatriation of proceeds. Profit remittance by a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima is constrained only by corporate and tax formalities, not by FX restrictions. A dividend remittance must be supported by current registered financial statements or audited accounts, a director resolution establishing the dividend, and proof of compliance with the applicable dividend withholding tax. Once the withholding tax is discharged, the remaining dividend amount may be freely wired abroad through a licensed Panamanian bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion rules applicable to export proceeds earned by a Panamanian shipyard. Export receipts may be retained in U.S. dollars, converted to any other currency, or transferred offshore without any designated timeline or mandatory conversion ratio. The only operational discipline required is banking and AML compliance: the exporting company must retain underlying commercial documentation, including the bill of sale, customs exit documents, certificate of vessel delivery, and bank reference records, so that the receiving bank can complete its source-of-funds and sanctions reviews.

Central bank supervision: No domestic central bank exists in the Republic of Panama. The financial system is fully dollarized and the U.S. dollar is legal tender. The Balboa is a currency of account at par with the U.S. dollar and circulates only as subsidiary coinage. The principal supervisory and regulatory authority for banking, foreign currency intermediation, and AML/CFT is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), with the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) acting as the financial intelligence unit. Executive Decree No. 246 of 2004, as subsequently amended, governs the AML/CFT framework for supervised entities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for a pure offshore digital EMI without local substance. Panama does not have a statutory category that permits an offshore electronic-money institution or payment institution to act as a deposit-taking bank. Under current fintech regulations, notably Law No. 81 of 2019 and related SBP authorizations, a non-bank digital payment provider would be required to obtain a locally supervised fintech license or establish a partnership with a licensed Panamanian bank. For a commercial shipyard with medium-risk AML classification, the legally realistic path is to open a segregated escrow trust account at a Tier 1 Panamanian bank and to use that bank's correspondent network for cross-border settlement. Offshore EMIs may be acceptable only as pass-through invoicing and reconciliation tools, but they cannot lawfully hold client construction funds unless integrated with a licensed local clearing bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are to be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the parent company or investors to the Panamanian S.A.'s operational or escrow account maintained at a licensed Panamanian bank. No central bank exchange authorization or prior quota is required. The wire advice must include the following identifiers: the exact corporate name of the Panamanian S.A., the account number, the corporate address, the tax ID, and a payment narrative such as 'capital contribution for ocean container vessel construction' or 'escrow-funded buyer installment'. The receiving bank will normally request a source-of-funds declaration, a board resolution approving the contribution, and a copy of the underlying financing or shipbuilding contract. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding on dividends distributed from Panamanian-source taxable profits; 5% on dividends paid out of profits that have borne foreign income tax; 20% on distributions from Panamanian-source income that has not been subject to the 25% corporate tax (exempt or unallocated profit pools).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed within three months after the fiscal year-end; for calendar-year taxpayers the statutory deadline is 31 March. No filing extension exists; deferred tax payment accrues moratory interest at 1% per month.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File documentation and the annual Form 230 sworn related-party return are required when aggregate related-party transactions exceed PAB/B/.2,500,000 (USD 2.5 million); transactions below the threshold must still be supported by contemporaneous internal arm's-length analysis.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), enacted under Ley 70 de 2019 and implemented by Decreto Ejecutivo 259 de 2020, require entities conducting relevant activities, including shipping, intra-group financing and distribution, to maintain physical presence, qualified local employees, sufficient local operating expenditure and core income-generating activity management in Panama. Industrial shipbuilding in a physical yard inherently demonstrates substantive presence, but any intra-group procurement, financing or IP-holding subsidiary associated with the shipyard must file the annual ESR declaration and evidence substance; non-compliance exposes the entity to fines and loss of territorial tax treatment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Evasion Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the omitted 25% corporate tax plus a 10% surcharge and monthly moratory interest of 1%; administrative fines of up to 100% of the omitted tax; criminal prosecution for tax fraud carrying imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and pecuniary penalties of 2 to 3 times the evaded amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false Form 23 are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's tax debt; the DGI may pierce the corporate veil, attach personal assets, impose travel bans (prohibicion de salida) and pursue extradition for flight-risk principals.
Shell Company / Artificial Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties per year of assessment plus reclassification of purportedly foreign-source income as Panama-source and taxation at 25%; deregistration of the company and revocation of tax exemptions; directors may be barred from managing Panamanian entities for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing is available when the S.A. is used as a facade; directors are personally ordered to restitute diverted funds, are subject to travel bans and may be criminally prosecuted for fraudulent insolvency or tax fraud.
Anti-Money Laundering (Law 23 de 2015, as amended)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to USD 1,000,000; suspension of the corporate license and cancellation of vessel registration privileges; criminal AML conviction may result in imprisonment of up to 10 years and confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and legal representatives face personal criminal liability, travel bans and disqualification orders; personal assets may be attached where the corporate form is used to launder ship finance proceeds.
