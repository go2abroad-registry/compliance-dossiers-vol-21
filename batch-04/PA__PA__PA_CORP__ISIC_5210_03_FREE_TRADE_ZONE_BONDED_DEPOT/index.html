Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized jurisdiction: the U.S. dollar is legal tender for all payments and obligations, while the balboa is a nominal accounting unit pegged 1:1 to the USD and exists only as coins. There are no exchange controls, no capital account restrictions, no foreign investment prior-approval requirements, and no limitation on inbound or outbound transfers. Current and capital account transactions are fully convertible. Profit and dividend remittance is unrestricted from an FX perspective; the only practical treasury hurdles are bank-level source-of-funds documentation and the statutory dividend tax payable by the distributing Panamanian entity (10% for onshore companies, reduced to 5% for Free Trade Zone licensed companies), plus the obtaining of fiscal clearance certificates where distributions are routed through the banking system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable: Panama imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, domiciliation, repatriation, or holding-period requirement for export proceeds or for receipts generated by bonded depot trade activity. Export collections may be retained indefinitely in USD or any freely convertible currency, in Panama or abroad. The only reporting duties are statistical balance-of-payments filings and suspicious-transaction reports submitted by banks to the SBP; these do not constitute exchange control measures.

Central bank supervision: Effective financial regulator for banking, payments, and AML/CFT: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), operating under Decreto Ley No. 9 de 1998. No traditional central bank exists and no national fiat currency is issued; the U.S. Federal Reserve acts as the de facto monetary authority under full dollarization established by Decree Law No. 196 of 1904. Free Trade Zone and bonded depot concessions are administered by the Administración de la Zona Libre de Colón and the Servicio Nacional de Aduanas (Panama Customs Authority), under the general policy umbrella of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM for supplementary payment rails only; LOW as the primary operating treasury. Panama's banking law (Decreto Ley No. 9 de 1998) restricts deposit-taking and payment account operation to licensed banks; offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Payoneer do not hold Panamanian banking licenses and cannot independently maintain the company's core transactional balances. Such processors may be used strictly as a disbursement or collection conduit through a licensed partner bank, with every underlying trade transaction separately recorded in the Panamanian accounting books and visible to the SBP upon request. The SBP operates a Fintech Innovation Hub / sandbox permitting supervised electronic-money pilots with bank sponsorship, but a bonded depot's statutory license, customs bond, and trade clearing profile require settlement through the regulated banking system. Treasury policy must therefore treat EMIs as ancillary channels, not as the clearing rail of record.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inward USD routing: the shareholder or parent company initiates a Fedwire or CHIPS transfer from its account into the Panamanian bank's U.S. correspondent network (e.g., Banco General via JPMorgan Chase, Banistmo via Wells Fargo or Citibank, BAC via its intra-group U.S. corridor). The correspondent forwards an SWIFT MT103 to the Panamanian receiving bank with field 70 'Details of Payment' citing 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / APORTE DE CAPITAL', the beneficiary client ID, and the Ficha Mercantil corporate registration number. The receiving bank applies sanction screening, PEP screening, and source-of-funds verification; for injections above USD 50,000, the bank will typically require a signed source-of-funds declaration and, for shareholder loans, the executed facility agreement. No FX conversion, central bank approval, or repatriation event is required as the funds remain USD. All inbound capital wires must be reflected in the corporate share registry and statutory accounting books to match the paid-in capital position, and retained as documentary evidence for future bank compliance reviews.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer for direct supplier payments and international settlements, executed through the bank's electronic channel with dual-approval control and TBML pattern monitoring; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) issued by the Panamanian bank, optionally confirmed by the beneficiary's bank, for high-value or first-time trade counterparties; (3) Documentary Collections (DP/DA, SWIFT MT400/MT422) where letters of credit are not commercially required; (4) Standby Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT760) for performance security under bonded depot and logistics contracts; (5) Local USD clearing through the SBP-supervised national ACH/cámara de compensación for domestic obligations such as payroll, utilities, customs duties, and withholdings. The Colón Free Trade Zone's internal concessionaire clearing arrangements may support intra-zone book transfers, but all arm's-length external trade must settle via the licensed banking system. Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Line 1a Gross income from Panamanian-source activities, including bonded-warehouse rental, handling, storage, and customs-zone services physically rendered in Panama. Non-resident entities must report only income sourced from Panamanian territory; foreign-source income is excluded under territorial taxation. Late filing of Form 200 triggers a fine of up to PAB 10,000, a late-filing surcharge of 10% of the tax due, and monthly moratory interest of 1.5% on outstanding tax. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PAB 1,500,000

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations require all companies, including free-trade-zone bonded depots, carrying on relevant activities to demonstrate adequate physical presence, qualified personnel, and locally guided core income-generating activities within Panama. A mandatory annual economic substance sworn return must be filed; free-zone operators must evidence real warehouse facilities, active customs operations, inventory management, and local executive decision-making in their tax records.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporate Filing

Statutory sanction: Administrative reassessment, 100% fraud penalty, moratory interest at 1.5% per month, and criminal prosecution under the Fiscal Code carrying imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and fines up to PAB 50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause a fraudulent return may be held joint and severally liable for the unpaid tax, face civil restitution orders, travel bans, asset attachment, and disqualification from managing Panamanian companies.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The DGI may adjust taxable income to the arm's-length result and impose deficiency interest plus administrative penalties from 10% to 50% of the tax understated; failure to prepare a mandated Local File or Master File carries additional fixed penalties.

Director personal liability: Senior finance officers and directors who approve non-compliant intercompany pricing are civilly liable for the deficiency, surcharge, and interest, and may be barred from leaving Panama pending collection.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Non-filing or inaccurate economic substance reporting may incur initial penalties of up to PAB 50,000, repeated non-compliance up to PAB 100,000, cancellation of the free-trade-zone licence, and strike-off of the company from the Public Registry.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined for late economic substance declarations, ordered to refund state licensing benefits, subjected to civil compensation actions, and prohibited from holding directorships for up to five years.
AML / Money Laundering and Cash-Based Bonded Depot Operations

Statutory sanction: Under Panama's AML legislation (Law 23 of 2015, as amended by Law 122 of 2020), customs fraud and money laundering carry prison terms of 5 to 10 years, fines of up to PAB 1,000,000, forfeiture of goods and accounts, and mandatory revocation of the commercial or free-zone licence.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to prevent or knowingly ignore suspicious warehouse cash flows may be prosecuted as co-authors, face travel bans, extradition to treaty partners, and personal forfeiture of profits derived from illicit customs operations.
