Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); taxpayer portal: Sede Electrónica DGI (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa). Customs and export control: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas de Panamá (ANA) via the Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior (VUCE) and SIGA system.

E-government filing portal

1. The S.A. is incorporated by public deed and registered in the Registro Público and, before commencing activities, obtains the Aviso de Operación from the Ministerio de Comercio e Industrias (MICI). 2. The Panamanian legal representative obtains a digital signature (firma electrónica) from a DGI-authorized certification authority and creates the entity's account in the DGI Sede Electrónica portal. 3. The entity files the unified RUC application online, attaching the Ficha registration, public deed, notarized fiscal domicile, and identity of the legal representative; non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the capital, no minimum capital is required, and the RUC and digital billing certificates are issued after automated validation. 4. For export activity, the entity must additionally register as an exporter and obtain the customs user profile in ANA's SIGA system via VUCE. Registration is a precondition to starting taxable operations; failure to register within 30 days of commencing activities triggers fines. Non-resident digital-service providers may obtain a simplified RUC for ITBMS under DGI regulations, but the onshore S.A. uses the standard RUC process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope. The S.A. is a per se taxable corporation (no pass-through or check-the-box available) subject to 25% corporate income tax on Panama-source income only; foreign-source income is exempt from ISR. ITBMS applies to taxable Panamanian transfers and imports; dividends paid from taxed Panama-source profits bear a 5% dividend tax (10% on sums not subject to ISR). Municipal business tax (industria y comercio) also applies locally.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Panamanian law. Optional statutory regimes: (i) Sede de Empresa Multinacional (SEM) license under Law 41 of 2007 for qualifying regional headquarters (5% ISR on Panama-source income, ITBMS and dividend-tax relief); (ii) Régimen Simplificado de Pequeño Contribuyente for gross annual revenue up to USD 36,000, with simplified ITBMS instalments; (iii) customs bonded warehousing (depósito aduanero) election to suspend import ITBMS on goods destined for re-export; (iv) no pass-through election for S.A. entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold: every person engaged in taxable transfers or imports must obtain the RUC and register for ITBMS before starting operations. An optional simplified small-taxpayer regime applies below USD 36,000 gross annual revenue (Ley 66 de 2017); the off-price wholesale liquidation trade is treated as a standard-rate general-regime business above that level.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of movable goods are zero-rated at 0% ITBMS when title passes to a non-resident buyer and the goods physically leave Panamanian customs territory; the validated customs export declaration is the operative legal evidence. For the off-price apparel wholesale trade, finished branded garments sold FOB/CIF to foreign wholesalers qualify for 0% provided the export e-invoice, commercial contract, and transport documents are consistent. Offshore services (logistics, sourcing commissions) to non-residents for use outside Panama fall outside ITBMS. Imported overstock may be held in customs bond (depósito aduanero) with suspended import ITBMS and cleared at 0% on definitive export. The exporter must hold valid FEP supplier invoices and proof of payment to substantiate any input ITBMS credit or refund claim.

  • Declaración de Exportación filed electronically via VUCE/SIGA and validated by Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (with customs exit control)
  • Export commercial invoice (FEP export invoice at 0%)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR/carta de porte evidencing physical departure from Panama
  • Purchase order or sales contract with the non-resident buyer evidencing offshore title transfer
  • Inward remittance certificate (bank credit advice / certificación bancaria) evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Supplier FEP invoices and bank payment proofs supporting the input ITBMS credit and refund computation
  • Packing list and, when applicable, certificate of origin or customs bond/depósito aduanero documentation

Facturación Electrónica de Panamá (FEP) is mandatory under DGI Resolution 201-2755 of 2021, as amended, phased since April 2022 and fully obligatory for all RUC-registered taxpayers by 2025. Invoices must be generated via DGI-approved billing software or the free DGI portal and submitted to the DGI Sede Electrónica web services for near-real-time validation; the DGI returns an authorization code and QR/digital seal which must appear on every invoice. Batch transmission is mandatory, and paper or fiscal-printer invoices no longer support input tax credits or expense deductions.

DGI fiscalizaciones will verify physical exit, commercial reality of the foreign buyer, FEP invoice chain, and banking remittance. Panama is dollarized and imposes no exchange-control or statutory repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects DGI practice for evidencing proceeds, and a materially longer gap may trigger recharacterization as domestic sales. On a false export or refund, the DGI will reverse the 0% rate to 7%, claw back refunds with monthly interest of 1% per Article 1020 Código Fiscal, impose surcharges and fines of 50%–100% of the tax omitted plus documentary fines of USD 5,000–50,000, and refer the case for criminal prosecution. Intentional evasion under Ley 70 de 2019 (Código Penal Article 377-B) carries imprisonment of 3–8 years when the amount evaded exceeds USD 50,000, with joint and several liability of the legal representative and resident agent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized: the US dollar is legal tender and circulates at 1:1 with the Panamanian balboa (issued only as coinage). Both capital- and current-account transactions are fully convertible, with no exchange-control statute, no central-bank transfer authorization, and no restriction on dividend, interest, or profit repatriation. The only remittance hurdle is fiscal rather than FX-related: dividends paid to non-resident shareholders out of Panama-source net earnings are subject to a 10% dividend withholding tax under the Isisdación? (Panama Tax Code) regime, but no conversion quota or administrative approval is required to remit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panama imposes zero surrender obligations: exporters of finished-garment liquidation inventories may retain 100% of their USD export proceeds in any onshore or offshore account, with no mandatory conversion, no statutory repatriation window, and no deposit requirement with a designated state bank. Exporters remain subject only to documentary retention duties under the Tax Code and to customer due-diligence/record-keeping obligations imposed by AML Law 23 of 2015 and the SBP.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) acting as the exclusive banking and financial-system supervisor, with Banco Nacional de Panamá (BNP) functioning as the Republic's fiscal agent; Panama maintains no conventional central bank and emits no national paper currency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Qualified Limited Viability. Under Law 30 of 2023 (financial inclusion and innovation regime), Panama permits licensed fintech activity, but foreign digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, or Airwallex) are not authorized as SBP-supervised credit institutions and cannot hold their own settlement accounts at the Panama clearing house. Such EMIs are feasible only as ancillary collection/disbursement channels, while the S.A. must maintain its principal liquidity, treasury, and clearing relationships with a fully licensed Panamanian bank to satisfy substance, UAF (Unidad de Análisis Financiero) reporting, and general AML credibility standards.
