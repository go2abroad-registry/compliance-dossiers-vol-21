Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; electronic portal: e-Tax (https://etax.mef.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned captive reinsurer must: (1) appoint a Panamanian resident agent; (2) incorporate a Sociedad Anónima by notarized public deed and register it in the Registro Público; (3) obtain the Aviso de Operación and register in the DGI to receive the Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC); (4) activate the ITBMS and withholding-tax accounts in the e-Tax portal using an authorized digital-signature credential; (5) obtain the captive reinsurance license from the Superintendencia de Seguros y Reaseguros de Panamá before commencing underwriting. DGI issues the RUC upon submission of the public deed, resident-agent certificate, Articles of Incorporation, and proof of license application; the RUC is mandatory for issuing any invoice or receiving taxable premiums.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama has no check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch entity election; the Sociedad Anónima is a separate taxable entity, not a pass-through. Territorial taxation is mandatory: only Panama-source income is subject to corporate income tax (ISR), while overseas-risk reinsurance income is outside the Panamanian net. Options through advance tax rulings include the accounting basis for technical reserves and deductibility of captive reinsurance commission; there is no free election to change entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general ITBMS registration threshold; all legal entities engaged in taxable supplies of goods or services in Panama must register. Only natural persons with annual gross income up to B/.36,000 (USD/PAB) may use the simplified small-taxpayer regime; corporate captive reinsurers cannot use it.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated if sold by a Panamanian exporter to a non-resident, physically leave Panama under customs control, transfer title to the foreign buyer, and the price is paid by the foreign buyer. Services are zero-rated (or outside the ITBMS base) if supplied to a non-resident for use wholly outside Panama; for captive reinsurance, the relevant test is that no insured risk is located in Panama, the reinsured is a foreign licensed insurer/group captive, and the underwriting and risk management are carried out abroad. Input ITBMS attributable to zero-rated exports can be credited or refunded through DGI returns.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUCA / Boleta de Exportación) validated by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by an independent carrier evidencing physical exit from Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT credit confirmation from the Panamanian bank evidencing receipt of the export consideration

Mandatory electronic invoicing under the DGI's Facturación Electrónica de Panamá: invoices must be transmitted through DGI-authorized software to the e-Tax API, validated in real time, and issued only after receiving a unique Código de Autorización (UAC) and QR code. The API supports SOAP/REST clearance with mutual TLS and digital certificates; paper/fiscal-printer fallback is allowed only during transition or for non-digitized taxpayers, not for a licensed reinsurer.

DGI verification programs cross-match customs declarations, bank receipts, and ITBMS returns. Unsupported or simulated zero-rated exports are reclassified as taxable at 7% with legal interest (approximately 1.5% per month); fines range from 100% to 500% of the omitted tax, and false documentation can trigger criminal tax-fraud charges, suspension or cancellation of the RUC, and retroactive reassessments for up to five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy; the US dollar is legal tender under Cabinet Decree No. 238 of 1904. There is no exchange-control regime and no legal distinction between current and capital account transactions for convertibility purposes. Profit remittances and capital repatriation are freely permitted without central-bank approval, subject only to banking-sector source-of-funds validation, the captive's statutory licensing obligations under the Superintendencia de Seguros y Reaseguros de Panamá (SSRP), and compliance with the Money Laundering Prevention Act (Law 42/2014).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rule or mandatory conversion ratio applies. Panama imposes no export-proceeds surrender or repatriation timeline; a captive reinsurance SPV may retain US-dollar receipts in any SBP-licensed bank without conversion requirements.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as the primary banking and AML/CFT supervisory authority; Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent of the Republic of Panama, but Panama has no formal central bank or foreign-exchange administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI passports are not recognized in Panama. Under Law 124 of 2020, a locally licensed 'empresa de dinero electrónico' (EDE) is permitted to provide payment and e-money services, but such entities lack full banking powers and cannot hold or safeguard reinsurance trust assets, statutory capital escrow balances, or captive reserves. The SBP does not authorize offshore EMIs to operate a deposit-taking or clearing account for a Panamanian S.A. without a branch or subsidiary license; for this captive reinsurance SPV, a licensed commercial bank must serve as the clearing and custody counterparty.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Newly formed Panama S.A. with historical bearer-share flexibility, nominee directors, and insufficient local substance.

Root cause: Newly formed Panama S.A. with historical bearer-share flexibility, nominee directors, and insufficient local substance.

Defense evidence: Cancelled or certified registered share ledger evidencing no outstanding bearer shares, SBP beneficial ownership declarations, sworn passports, director CVs and tax ID, a Panamanian registered-agent attestation, board resolutions demonstrating the captive's held-in-trust purpose, audited financial statements or opening balance sheet, and an AML/KYC policy manual aligned with Law 42/2014.
Reinsurance contributions or capital injections sourced from FATF grey-listed jurisdictions or complex multi-tier group structures.

Root cause: Reinsurance contributions or capital injections sourced from FATF grey-listed jurisdictions or complex multi-tier group structures.

Defense evidence: Group consolidated financial statements, audit trail of the remitting entities, certificates of tax residency, transfer pricing documentation, intercompany reinsurance agreements, source-of-wealth statements for all ultimate beneficial owners, sanctions screening results, and a compliance-committee sign-off evidencing the direct link between the premium flow and the captive's active underwriting portfolio.
Unexpected high-velocity US-dollar transactions or cross-border movement outside the approved treasury plan.

Root cause: Unexpected high-velocity US-dollar transactions or cross-border movement outside the approved treasury plan.

Defense evidence: Actuarial reserve certificate issued by a qualified actuary, reinsurance treaty schedule, segregated portfolio statement, board-approved treasury and investment policy, SWIFT MT103 transaction samples, SSRP statutory declaration, and a detailed variance report reconciling all inflows with ceded premiums and reserve obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the ultimate controlling company to the captive's operational or capital escrow account at an SBP-licensed Panamanian bank. Because Panama has no exchange control, no central-bank authorization is required; however, to support KYC and AML reviews, the SWIFT field must state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / PAID-IN CAPITAL – LICENSED CAPTIVE REINSURANCE SPV' and should be accompanied by a board resolution, notarized capital subscription agreement, and source-of-funds evidence. The receiving bank will verify the intended purpose code, the beneficial owner against sanctions lists, and the consistency of the remittance name with the corporate KYC file; disbursement to the operational account is permitted only after the funds are cleared and the bank's enhanced due diligence is satisfied.Outbound payments for reinsurance settlements, retrocession premiums, fronting commissions, and brokerage fees should be executed via SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) or SWIFT MT202 COV (interbank reimbursement) with final settlement in US dollars through the bank's US correspondent (typically located in New York or Florida). For treaty documentation requiring a documentary mechanism, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit or a standby LC may be used as a collateral vehicle under an ISDA/ICMA framework. Freedom-of-payment is unrestricted, but each transfer must carry a consistent narrative referencing the captive's treaty number, claim reference, and policy schedule to satisfy the SSRP and SBP transaction-monitoring obligations.Plain-vanilla over-the-counter (OTC) USD-forward contracts with an SBP-licensed bank or an international bank under an ISDA Master Agreement; no prior government authorization is required.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in Latin American currencies (e.g., USD/MXN, USD/BRL, USD/COP) used to hedge fronting or retrocession exposures denominated in foreign currency; permitted to the extent they mirror the captive's actual treaty obligations.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) between USD and a foreign currency for net investment positions or foreign-ceded portfolio liabilities, permitted under Panamanian law when documented through an SBP-regulated counterparty and supported by a board-approved derivative authorization.Currency options and plain collars on a non-speculative basis; subject to the captive's statutory investment rules and the SBP requirement that the hedge must be economically related to a specific reinsurance contract or reserve currency exposure.
