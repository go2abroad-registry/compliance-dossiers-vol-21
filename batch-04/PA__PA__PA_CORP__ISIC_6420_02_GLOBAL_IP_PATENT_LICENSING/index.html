Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; electronic hub: SITIO portal (Sistema Integrado Tributario de Información y Optimización) at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa; customs export platform: SIGA administered by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA).

E-government filing portal

(1) After incorporation at the Registro Público (public deed deposit and issuance of certified testimonio), the appointed fiscal resident agent (abogado idóneo) files the RUC registration form in SITIO or at DGI service windows, attaching: certified testimonio of the Escritura Pública, certificate of corporate existence, appointment of directors/officers, power of attorney of the resident agent, notarized fiscal address, and identity documents of directors and beneficial owners. (2) DGI issues the permanent RUC, which simultaneously registers the S.A. as the ITBMS taxpayer; the S.A. next obtains the municipal Aviso de Operación (operating license) from the district mayor (e.g., Municipio de Panamá). (3) The S.A. acquires a Firma Electrónica (digital certificate) from a DGI-authorized certifier to operate SITIO, file returns and issue e-invoices. (4) No local TIN is required for non-resident shareholders; the S.A. RUC covers all tax obligations, and the resident agent receives official service of process. Processing time: 3–7 business days upon complete submission.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — the S.A. is an opaque entity-level taxpayer under the corporate income tax (ISR, flat 25%) but is taxable exclusively on Panama-source income: foreign-source royalties from patents/trademarks are outside the ISR base unless the IP is 'used or exploited' in Panama (Law 254 of 2021 economic-destination test). Dividends received from non-resident subsidiaries are exempt, and no participation-exemption or CFC regime applies to active licensing income.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists: a Panamanian S.A. is per se a territorial, entity-level taxpayer. Statutory elections available: (a) election to exclude foreign-source licensing income from ISR and ITBMS by evidencing foreign economic destination under Law 254 of 2021 (sworn affidavit + supporting documentation filed in SITIO); (b) election of the Régimen Especial Simplificado (RES) for ITBMS, available where annual turnover ≤ B/.36,000, forfeited upon issuance of export invoices; (c) election of tax-neutral corporate reorganization treatment (Fiscal Code reorganization rules) subject to prior DGI approval; (d) no hybrid mismatch, participation exemption, or preferential royalty-regime elections exist.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No exemption threshold: any person engaged in taxable transfers of movable goods or services in Panama must register for ITBMS before commencement (RUC). A simplification threshold applies: taxpayers with annual gross income ≤ B/.36,000 may opt for the RES fixed-quota regime; exporters and withholding agents are mandatorily excluded from the RES.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (i) physical exports of goods evidenced by a DUA in the SIGA customs system; (ii) services rendered to non-residents for use or exploitation outside Panama under Law 8 of 2010; and (iii) IP licensing to a non-resident licensee where the patent/trademark is not used, protected or commercialized within Panama and the licensee has no Panamanian PE (Law 254 of 2021 territoriality test). Royalties must be paid from abroad (foreign bank/SWIFT), invoiced through e-invoicing, and the taxpayer must assemble the export evidence file; the corresponding ITBMS input credits are claimable by the exporter in the quarterly return, with a right to cash refund (reintegro) within the DGI's verification framework.

  • Customs export declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera – DUA) filed in the SIGA platform (required for goods; for pure IP licensing, substituted by the license agreement and DGI territoriality affidavit)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Panamanian customs territory (goods only)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Comprobante de Ingreso de Divisas) issued by a licensed Panamanian bank, or SWIFT MT103/credit advice, evidencing inbound foreign-currency payment of the royalty
  • Signed and authenticated licensing agreement (with amendments, sub-licensing grants and foreign patent/registration certificates)
  • Factura Electrónica bearing the SITIO acceptance code
  • Sworn affidavit of the legal representative attesting to the licensee's non-resident status and the exclusive foreign use/exploitation of the licensed IP

Mandatory e-invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Panamá) established by DGI Resolution No. 201-4324 of 21 October 2021 and phased by annual revenue: ≥ B/.36 million from January 2022; ≥ B/.3 million from January 2023; ≥ B/.600,000 from January 2024; and all remaining taxpayers from January 2025. Invoices are transmitted in real time as XML 'Set de Factura Electrónica' to the DGI SITIO platform, receive online validation (Aceptada/Rechazada), and must carry a QR code and acceptance code; issuance requires a valid Firma Electrónica and authorized e-invoicing software, with automatic fines and RUC suspension for recurring non-compliance.

DGI audit units (Large Taxpayers and E-Exports) apply a strict burden-of-proof standard in IP-export cases: failure to produce the documentation bundle recharacterizes the royalties as Panama-source income, voids the zero-rating, and triggers assessment of 7% ITBMS on gross royalties plus the omitted ISR (25%) with a monthly recargo of 1% per month or fraction and an administrative penalty of 10%–100% of the defrauded tax; any ITBMS refund previously paid is clawed back with interest, and export invoices unsettled beyond the 180-day repatriation window are recharacterized as Panama-source financing income. Where false invoices, simulated export documents or sham licenses are used, the conduct is prosecuted as fiscal fraud under Law 70 of 2019, carrying imprisonment of up to 6 years and fines of up to 100% of the tax evaded, with criminal liability extending to directors and legal representatives; parallel AML sanctions under Law 23 of 2015 and registry de-registration of the S.A. may also be imposed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama uses the US dollar as legal tender. There are no exchange controls on current account or capital account transactions; the balboa is a nominal currency unit tied at par to the USD and does not create separate convertibility restrictions. Profits, dividends, royalties, loan repayments, and proceeds of capital liquidation may be freely remitted abroad in USD without central bank approval. Banking-level practical barriers include AML/CFT source-of-funds review, economic substance documentation, and sanctions screening; these are not currency controls, but they require the IP HoldCo to maintain transactional transparency and a full compliance dossier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender or conversion requirements. There is no mandatory timeline for repatriation and no prescribed conversion ratio into local currency. Export or service proceeds, including royalty receipts from foreign licensees, may be retained in USD or any freely convertible currency. The SBP and commercial banks nevertheless require supporting commercial documentation and beneficiary identification for all inward credit operations under AML/CFT obligations.

Central bank supervision: Panama does not maintain a central bank; monetary affairs are anchored by full dollarization. The banking and financial regulatory authority is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), and Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the fiscal agent for the Republic of Panama.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility. Panama's banking law restricts deposit-taking and account-based treasury services to entities licensed by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá; offshore digital EMIs cannot independently hold client funds or operate as payment settlement banks in Panama without a local fintech or banking authorization. Foreign EMIs may be used as onboarding or payment initiation gateways only if they partner with a licensed local bank or operate under a recognized Panama Fintech Law (as amended) sandbox approval. For a patent licensing treasury structure, an offshore EMI is not a compliant primary clearing rail due to significant regulatory uncertainty, shadow banking exposure, and enhanced SCR/OFC risk flags. A licensed Panamanian bank account with direct USD SWIFT access remains the legally reliable rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are executed as USD-denominated SWIFT MT103 payments from the parent entity or investor to the Panama S.A.'s operational or capital escrow account at a licensed Panamanian bank. The remitter must designate a clear payment purpose such as 'Capital Injection', 'Shareholder Loan', or 'Royalty Prepayment' in the SWIFT transaction reference; the receiving bank applies SBP-compliant beneficial ownership verification and OFAC/sanctions screening. Panama does not require Exchange Control approval or a formal purpose code, but the transaction must be supported by a capital contribution agreement, corporate issuance resolution, or loan agreement, and the funds must ticket to the issuer's equity/liability accounts in the company's ledger.Outbound trade and royalty disbursements are cleared primarily via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 correspondent bank transfers in USD. For high-value or documentary trade transactions, a commercial letter of credit under SWIFT MT700 with a Panamanian advising/nominated bank may be used, especially when the counterparty requires bank payment undertaking. Panama's high-value USD clearing is channeled through New York correspondent relationships using CHIPS or Fedwire; local low-value clearing may settle via ACH through the Panamanian clearing house. Turnover: No general statutory audit is imposed on a non-listed Sociedad Anónima solely by corporation law.
