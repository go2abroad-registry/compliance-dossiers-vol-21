Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; portal: Sede Electrónica (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) or register a foreign company branch with the Public Registry; a resident agent (agente residente) is mandatory for non-resident entities. 2) Obtain a municipal business license (Aviso de Operación) from the Alcaldía of the place of operation. 3) Register with DGI using Formulario de Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC), submitting incorporation documents, municipal license, identity documents, and proof of local commercial address. 4) Upon approval, DGI issues the NIT/RUC and simultaneously activates the ITBMS (VAT) registration for taxable activities. A taxable representative may be required for foreign entities with no physical residence in Panama.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — 1) Incorporate a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) or register a foreign company branch with the Public Registry; a resident agent (agente residente) is mandatory for non-resident entities. 2) Obtain a municipal business license (Aviso de Operación) from the Alcaldía of the place of operation. 3) Register with DGI using Formulario de Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC), submitting incorporation documents, municipal license, identity documents, and proof of local commercial address. 4) Upon approval, DGI issues the NIT/RUC and simultaneously activates the ITBMS (VAT) registration for taxable activities. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election regimes. S.A. entities are automatically treated as taxable entities subject to territorial income tax on Panama-source income. Eligible small taxpayers may elect the simplified taxation regime (Régimen Simplificado) based on annual gross income, and taxpayers may elect consolidated filing for VAT and income tax under DGI procedures. No CFC or hybrid entity classifications are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 0 – no statutory economic threshold; any person or entity engaging in taxable imports, transfer of movable goods, or services during the ordinary course of business must register for ITBMS with DGI prior to commencing operations. Small taxpayers with annual gross income below B/. 36,000 may be eligible for simplified accounting and filing obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Panama through customs exit (as evidenced by the customs export declaration) qualify for the 0% ITBMS rate. Services rendered to non-residents for use outside Panama and export-related services provided to international shipping/aircraft (e.g., freight, bunkering, repair, dockside services) may also qualify for zero-rating. The taxpayer must be an ITBMS registrant, maintain transactional records, and substantiate foreign exchange repatriation when applicable. Excess input VAT on export operations may be claimed as a refund (saldo a favor) through quarterly or annual refund filing.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA / Declaración Única Aduanera)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (B/L or AWB)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • FOB Value Evidence and Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas)
  • Certificate of Origin or National Production Certificate (when claiming preferential treatment)

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) for all ITBMS-registered taxpayers through DGI-certified software or the Sede Electrónica platform. Invoices must be transmitted in XML format in real time or within 24 hours (for large taxpayers via API integration) to DGI for fiscal clearance and digital stamping (Firma Digital). The system authenticates invoices and credit documents (Comprobantes de Crédito Fiscal); failure to obtain clearance blocks issuance of valid invoices.

DGI conducts targeted fiscal audits on zero-rated export claims, requiring full documentary proof of physical export and foreign payment collection. For fraudulent zero-rating claims, DGI retroactively claws back the full ITBMS at 7% plus 10% late surcharge and monthly interest, and imposes fines of 25% to 100% of the omitted tax depending on degree of intent. Criminal tax fraud penalties under the Código Fiscal and Decree Law 2/2020 may involve imprisonment of 2 to 5 years; the prescription period extends to 10 years in cases of fraud, preventing statute-of-limitations defenses.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Complete convertibility under a dual-currency regime: the U.S. dollar is unlimited legal tender and the Panamanian balboa (PAB) is a nominal unit pegged 1:1 to the USD (only fractional coins circulate). Capital and current account transactions are freely convertible with no exchange-control license, repatriation approval, or quantitative limits. Profit remittance friction is exclusively fiscal: DGI (Dirección General de Ingresos) requires the filing of the dividend declaration and payment of the 10% dividend withholding tax (5% for taxable income under USD 1.5 million) before external transfer; no SBP clearance is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable — Panama imposes no surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds. The manufacturing S.A. may lawfully retain 100% of USD receipts (or any other currency) in domestic or foreign bank accounts; no mandatory conversion ratio exists because the USD is the domestic currency.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) — the Republic of Panama is fully dollarized and maintains no central bank; prudential bank supervision rests with the SBP, while monetary conditions are de facto determined by the U.S. Federal Reserve. The Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) manages fiscal policy but exercises no foreign-exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary banking substitute and Medium as a supplementary disbursement rail. Panama has no domestic EMI (Electronic Money Institution) licensing category; the SBP prohibits unlicensed deposit acceptance and all final USD settlement must transit a Panama-licensed bank's U.S. correspondent. Offshore EMIs and regional fintechs may be used only as pre-funded cards or virtual accounts for small operational expenses; LC issuance, trade settlement, and regulatory compliance must remain with a licensed Tier-1 bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (graduated three-tier system: 15% on net taxable income up to B/.50,000; 20% on income between B/.50,001 and B/.1,500,000; 25% on income exceeding B/.1,500,000)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding on dividends paid out of Panama-source profits (Article 733 of the Fiscal Code, as amended by Law 28 of 2010). The rate increases to 20% when the beneficial owner is deemed resident in a preferentially low-tax jurisdiction (tax haven) under Panama's blacklist rules. Dividends paid from profits already subject to the complementary corporate-level tax or from foreign-source income are not subject to this levy.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months after the close of the calendar tax year; for a 31 December year-end the return must be filed by 31 March of the following year.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
RUC/NIT Unique taxpayer identification number (Registro Único de Contribuyente). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.300,000 (USD 300,000) of aggregate related-party cross-border transactions per fiscal year triggers mandatory Transfer Pricing documentation (Local File) and the annual informational return Form 230. A Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated annual turnover exceeds B/.5 million.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing for ordinary S.A. entities, but applies substance-based anti-abuse rules under Law 52 of 2016 and Decree 740 of 2016 (Regime of International Tax Transparency). A manufacturing S.A. must demonstrate in Panama: physical plant premises, qualified operational employees, inventory, board meetings and executive decisions, and bank accounts. The DGI may recharacterize offshore-booked income as Panama-source if the assembly, warehousing, management, or revenue-generating activity occurs within Panamanian territory. Licensed entities under SEM (multinational HQ), Colon Free Zone, or Panama Pacifico are subject to formal economic substance reporting and payroll/physical-asset thresholds.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Administrative: 50% fraud surcharge on the omitted tax plus monthly default interest and the reversal of all related deductions. Criminal: under Law 8 of 2010 and the Penal Code, imprisonment of two to five years and a fine equivalent to the amount of tax evaded; aggravating circumstances apply when false documents are used.

Director personal liability: Directors signing or approving a false return are jointly and severally liable for the omitted tax and penalties. The DGI may impose precautionary asset freezes, publish the taxpayer on the official delinquent-debtor list, impose travel bans, and pursue criminal indictment resulting in imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DGI recharacterization of foreign income as Panama-source, retroactive assessment for up to five years, application of a 50% surcharge and default interest, and cancellation of the RUC. Repeated non-compliance may lead to strike-off of the corporation under Law 32 of 1927 and loss of the benefit of limited liability.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and registered agents may face administrative fines and suspension of their licenses. Actual directors risk joint liability for re-assessed taxes and, in egregious laundering scenarios, criminal prosecution and travel restrictions.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Corporate fines up to B/.1,000,000 per infraction, asset forfeiture, suspension of operations, and cancellation of commercial registrations. Directors and compliance officers face personal fines of B/.50,000 to B/.500,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly accept illicit funds or conceal the true beneficial owner are exposed to criminal money-laundering charges under Articles 254–260 of the Penal Code, carrying imprisonment of five to ten years, bank-account freezes, and travel bans.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: A fine of 1% of the aggregate value of the relevant related-party transactions (minimum B/.10,000), plus an arm's-length adjustment adding the difference to the taxable base, with applicable default interest and surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false transfer pricing documentation are jointly liable for the resulting tax adjustment and fines; the DGI may also report the matter to the Public Prosecutor's Office where fraudulent documentation is identified.
Dividend Withholding Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent (the S.A.) is personally liable for the un-withheld tax under Article 733-A of the Fiscal Code, plus a 50% surcharge and monthly default interest; the payment may also be disallowed as an expense.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized distributions knowing that the withholding was not remitted are jointly and severally liable for the tax and penalties, and may be subject to travel bans and criminal prosecution for misappropriation of tax funds.
