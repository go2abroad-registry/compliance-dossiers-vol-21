Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized monetary system: the US dollar (USD) circulates as legal tender and is the settlement currency for all commercial and banking transactions; the Panamanian balboa (PAB) exists solely as subunit coinage at a statutory 1:1 fixed parity with USD and carries no independent exchange rate. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible; there are no exchange controls, no prior currency-purchase authorizations, no repatriation restrictions, and no profit-remittance licensing hurdles. Cross-border dividend payments are subject only to the territorial corporate income tax framework (10% withholding on dividends and a complementary tax on retained earnings under Ley 76 de 2019), not to FX controls. For the ISIC 3240 plush OEM company, all invoicing, treasury, payroll and vendor payments can be executed in USD without conversion friction, and any receivable or payable in a third currency (CNY, EUR, TWD) is freely negotiable through licensed banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation exists. Panama imposes zero mandatory conversion ratios, zero repatriation timelines, and zero reporting thresholds for export proceeds surrendered to the State. The company may retain 100% of USD export receipts in any SBP-licensed bank or foreign account. The only compliance requirements are: (i) accurate entry of export transactions in the corporate accounting records to support territorial tax filings; (ii) retention of customs/proof-of-export documentation in case of a tax audit; and (iii) reporting of cash or wire flows to the UAF only where the single transaction or linked-series exceeds the established suspicious-transaction threshold.

Central bank supervision: Republic of Panama has no central-issuing bank or foreign-exchange administration. The Banco Nacional de Panamá (state-owned commercial bank) acts as fiscal agent of the State but is constitutionally barred from issuing paper currency or performing lender-of-last-resort functions. Banking and FX-intermediary supervision is exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) under Decreto Ley 9 de 26 de febrero de 1998, as amended; the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) supervises AML/CFT compliance pursuant to Ley 23 de 2015.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with strict legal constraints. Panama's Ley 71 de 2019 regulates fintech and crowdfunding activities, but it does not create an EU-style electronic-money-institution passport and does not authorize offshore digital EMIs to take deposits or hold a Panamanian account numbering facility. An unlicensed offshore EMI (e.g., a non-Panama payment intermediary) may lawfully serve only as a settlement/payout gateway for platform revenues, provided the Panama S.A. remains the recorded beneficiary of the underlying funds and the EMI submits itself to CDD at the receiving Panamanian bank. The EMI must provide: (i) a UBO attestation; (ii) its payment-service license from the home regulator; and (iii) a reference letter from its corresponding bank. Operating the EMI as the primary liquidity rail is not viable because Panamanian banks will reject a corporate account whose main inflow originates from an unregulated pass-through design; the recommended architecture is one tier-1 Panamanian operational account as the treasury core, with the EMI used ancillary for marketplace payouts under USD 50,000 per event.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire protocol for capital and customer receipts: (1) Inward USD remittances arrive via SWIFT MT103 through the bank's New York correspondent into the company's SBP-licensed Panama account; the beneficiary name must exactly match the registered corporate account profile to avoid name-control rejection. (2) Purpose codes: direct paid-in capital (approval by board resolution and reflected in the Registro Público share-capital register; no Ministry authorization required), shareholder loan (documented in writing with arm's length interest rate and commercial substance; subject to thin-capitalization and transfer-pricing rules if the company elects taxable local activity), or advanced customer payment for future OEM export invoices (record as customer deposit against the purchase order). (3) Verification bundle: certified Escritura Pública; formation minutes approving the capital injection; UBO declaration; for amounts above USD 50,000, the underlying shareholder resolution, source-of-funds confirmation, and, for trade prepayments exceeding USD 100,000, a copy of the signed PO and proforma invoice. (4) Timing: USD clears in same-day value; the bank reconciles against the pre-notified Treasury statement; no foreign-exchange ticket is created because USD is the local unit of account.Clearing rails for settlement: (1) SWIFT MT103 is the standard rail for raw-material, design-agency and logistics disbursements of any size; for payments above USD 250,000 the treasury must evaluate MT202 COV cover instructions so that beneficiary-country reporting captures the remitter in full step-in format. (2) Commercial letters of credit under UCP600 are the preferred rail for bulk polyfill and fabric imports from China and Turkey; a confirmed irrevocable L/C issued by Banistmo, Multibank or Banco General, with US correspondent confirmation, transfers payment risk to the issuing bank and protects the Panama S.A. against documentary non-compliance. (3) Domestic ACH Panama and the high-value SIPAV network are used for payroll, rent and local vendor settlement in balboa or USD at 1:1. (4) Platform payout rails (Stripe, PayPal, Payoneer) are authorized for marketplace sales refunds and small marketing disbursements subject to a USD 10,000 single-event cap.
