Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); e-administration via the SIT (Sistema Integrado Tributario) portal and the DGI eFactura electronic-invoicing platform; customs control by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the Sociedad Anónima at the Registro Público de Panamá through a licensed attorney (3–5 business days) and appoint a resident agent (Agente Residente). 2) Obtain an advanced electronic signature (Firma Electrónica) certificate from a Registro Público-authorized certification authority. 3) File the incorporation deed, corporate data form and notarized Aviso de Operación (municipal business-activity notice) with the DGI through the SIT portal to obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente). 4) Activate the ITBMS taxpayer account in the eFactura platform, or apply for the small-taxpayer simplified regime only when annual gross receipts are below B/.36,000. 5) Register with the Caja del Seguro Social (CSS) only if local staff are hired. No minimum paid-in capital applies and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope (the S.A. is an entity-level taxpayer taxed exclusively on Panama-source income; foreign-source profits are exempt under Art. 693 of the Código Fiscal; no pass-through treatment exists for an S.A. under Panamanian law).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists; classification follows the Panamanian legal form, and territorial taxation is non-electable. Applicable optional regimes obtainable by DGI/municipal application: SEM Multinational Headquarters (Law 41 of 2007), Ciudad del Saber (Law 6 of 1998), Pan-Pacific special economic zone and Colon Free Zone regimes. Affiliated groups may elect consolidated corporate income-tax filing subject to prior DGI authorization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Registration is mandatory from the first taxable operation; there is no general de minimis ITBMS threshold (local currency B/. — Panamanian Balboa, pegged 1:1 to the US dollar). A simplified small-taxpayer regime (Régimen Especial de Pequeños Contribuyentes) is available only where annual gross receipts do not exceed B/.36,000 (US$36,000), with a flat monthly ITBMS payment; no sub-national ITBMS threshold exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible goods are zero-rated when title transfers to a non-resident buyer and the goods physically depart Panamanian customs territory, evidenced by the DUCA export declaration and the e-Export invoice. Services are zero-rated when rendered to a non-resident for exclusive use and benefit outside Panama (offshore-consumption criterion), invoiced in the eFactura export format; the exporter must retain proof of the non-resident recipient and of collection in the exporter's bank account. Supplies to Colon Free Zone licensees, SEM companies and vessels in international transit also qualify under their specific regimes.

  • Declaración Única Aduanera (DUCA) / SICE export declaration evidencing physical exit
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or road-cargo manifest issued to the non-resident buyer
  • e-Export commercial invoice carrying the DGI digital acceptance stamp
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice evidencing collection of export proceeds
  • Signed packing list, plus certificate of origin when required by the destination customs territory

Electronic invoicing is mandatory under Law 130 of 2019, Decree 57 of 2020 and the DGI eFactura technical regulation. All ITBMS taxpayers issue e-Factura, e-Ticket and e-Export documents through DGI-certified software or the free DGI portal, authenticated with the Firma Electrónica advanced signature. Fiscal clearance is real-time: the eFactura engine validates each document, allocates a sequential CAF authorization range and returns a digital acceptance stamp before the document can be delivered to the buyer. Machine-to-machine integration runs on DGI-specified XML/JSON web-service APIs. Phased implementation concluded by 1 January 2024: all taxpayers must operate exclusively on the electronic-invoicing platform, and non-validated paper invoices confer no input-tax credit.

The DGI and the ANA cross-match DUCA records, e-Export invoices and bank remittances in export audits. If zero-rating is disallowed or documentation proves false: the 7% ITBMS is retroactively assessed with monthly moratory interest of 1.5% plus a 10% late-payment surcharge; fines under the Código Fiscal range from B/.250 to B/.10,000 per invoice irregularity and up to 100% of the omitted tax for fraud; criminal tax-fraud and customs-smuggling prosecution may impose imprisonment of up to 5 years, monetary penalties and director disqualification. The DGI clawback/assessment period is 5 years (Art. 732 Código Fiscal), tolled upon proof of fraud. Panamanian law imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline; 180 days constitutes the local banking and audit expectation for export collections.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized with the US dollar as legal tender and the balboa limited to local coinage. There is no central bank exchange-control regime. Current account and capital account transfers are freely convertible and may be executed with no prior central bank authorization. Profit remittance and dividend distribution to non-residents are unimpeded, subject only to standard AML/KYC documentary requirements and, where Panama-source income is involved, confirmation of tax standing with the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Panama imposes no foreign-exchange surrender obligation and no statutory conversion ratio on export proceeds. An exporter may retain 100% of export receipts in USD or in any freely convertible currency without repatriation deadline or compulsory conversion. Banking compliance requires standard proof of export trade, such as invoices, bills of lading, and customs documentation.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). Panama has no issuing central bank; Banco Nacional de Panamá operates as the fiscal agent and clearing agent of the Republic of Panama.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-to-Medium. A purely offshore digital EMI without a Panamanian licence cannot lawfully hold client funds or provide payment/clearing services in Panama. The SBP is the sole licensing authority for banking and financial intermediation; there is no EU/UK EMI passporting regime. A cross-border EMI may only engage through a licensed Panamanian bank, a licensed international banking centre, or an approved SBP sandbox participant with a local office, local director/representative, and an AML/CFT program registered with the SBP. For the stated manufacturing entity, direct fintech-EMI clearing is not recommended; commercial banking with SWIFT and documentary LC capability is the viable rail.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Produce a chronological share-transfer register stamped by the registered agent, legal opinion from a Panamanian counsel confirming the absence of unregistered bearer instruments, updated Regulación? not required; rather, certificates of good standing for the corporate vehicle, a certificate of non-issuance or full custody immobilization of bearer shares, and a board resolution fixing current beneficial ownership.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital contribution must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank account to the Panamanian operating bank. There is no mandatory Panama-specific SWIFT purpose code, but the wire must carry a clear beneficiary reference 'PA_CORP capital contribution - digital X-ray detector manufacturing' together with a board resolution, subscription agreement, and source-of-funds documentation. The receiving Panamanian bank may independently classify the inflow as Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) and request the DGI tax identification number (RUC) and Panamanian company registration data. No central bank pre-approval, exchange-control certification, or inward FDI registration exists.Settlement of supplier invoices for components, amorphous-silicon panels, cesium iodide scintillators, and electronics is effected through (i) SWIFT MT103 international wire transfers for non-documentary high-confidence suppliers, and (ii) irrevocable confirmed commercial letters of credit (L/C) at sight, subject to UCP 600, for large capital-equipment purchases. Payment instructions must be supported by a purchase order, commercial invoice, transport document (e.g., bill of lading or air waybill), packed/commercial invoice set, and where required the MINSA import certificate. For L/C transactions, the issuing bank should route through a correspondent with the beneficiary's bank using SWIFT MT700 issuance and MT202/MT103 cover payment. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends distributed from profits that have borne ISR or CAIR in Panama; 20% on dividends distributed from untaxed profits (e.g., where the company utilized loss carryforwards or obtained a CAIR/ISR exemption).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months after the fiscal year-end; for calendar-year entities, by March 31 of the following tax year. Two mandatory advance payments of 50% each of the prior year's ISR/CAIR are due by June 30 and October 31 of the current fiscal year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same as Form 49: within three months of fiscal year-end (March 31 for calendar-year taxpayers).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 7,500,000 (Panama uses the US dollar as legal tender; no separate local-currency threshold). Mandatory submission of Form 392 and a Local File when annual gross revenue exceeds USD 7.5 million; a Master File is additionally required when the worldwide group's consolidated gross revenue exceeds USD 30 million. Taxpayers below the USD 7.5 million threshold must still maintain contemporaneous TP documentation when their related-party transactions exceed USD 250,000 in a fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) apply under Law 70 of 2019 and implementing Decree 129 of 2020 to all Panamanian-resident entities conducting 'relevant activities,' including manufacturing, distribution, and shipping. The S.A. must demonstrate physical premises in Panama, full-time qualified employees, and core income-generating activities performed locally. Annual ESR compliance filings must be submitted to the DGI. Non-compliance results in fines from USD 5,000 to USD 500,000, suspension of the RUC and business license, and potential administrative dissolution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Fine of 2 to 10 times the tax evaded, plus a 30% surcharge, monthly default interest of 1%, and potential criminal imprisonment of 2 to 5 years under Article 729-A of the Fiscal Code; the taxpayer's name is published on the DGI infractors list.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign or supervise the false return are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and surcharges; criminal conviction carries imprisonment, a travel ban, and seizure of personal assets through the 'levantamiento del velo corporativo' doctrine.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR fines of USD 5,000 to USD 500,000, suspension of the RUC and Aviso de Operación, administrative dissolution, and re-characterization of transactions by the DGI with 25% ISR, surcharges, and interest on deemed Panama-source income.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute sham transactions are personally liable for ESR penalties; foreign parents face permanent-establishment exposure in Panama; travel bans and freezing of Panamanian bank accounts can be imposed as precautionary measures.
