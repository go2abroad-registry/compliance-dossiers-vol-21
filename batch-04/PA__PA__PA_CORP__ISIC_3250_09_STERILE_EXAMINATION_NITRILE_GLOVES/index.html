Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas de Panamá; official e-services portal: SIRI (Sistema de Información de Registro de Ingresos), accessible at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

To procure the statutory Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC) for a Panama Sociedad Anónima: (1) incorporate and register the S.A. in the Registro Público de Panamá through a licensed resident agent; (2) submit the DGI electronic pre-registration via SIRI attaching the public-registry certificate, corporate bylaws, resident-agent appointment, and registered-office proof; (3) obtain the permanent RUC and activate the ITBMS (VAT) and ISR (income tax) filing profiles; (4) complete the municipal Aviso de Operación and industrial-license formalities in the plant municipality; (5) procure a DGI-accredited electronic signature for mandatory digital filing. Foreign-incorporated entities must register a local branch (sucursal) in the Registro Público and appoint a resident legal representative before the DGI will activate the RUC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not recognize check-the-box, conduit, or hybrid-mismatch entity elections; the S.A. is inherently an entity-level taxpayer on a territorial (source-based) basis. Available statutory elections include: (i) the Alternative Method (Método Alterno) under Article 699-A of the Código Fiscal, computing ISR at 25% of deemed income equal to 11.7% of net taxable gross receipts when this exceeds the ordinary net-income result; (ii) application for free-zone/maquila and drawback export regimes that exempt inputs and capital goods from ITBMS; (iii) application for the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007 for group back-office and logistics functions; and (iv) the election to evidence that dividends are paid from profits already subject to ISR, applying the 5% dividend withholding tax instead of the 10% rate on non-taxed territorial or exempt income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No corporate turnover threshold applies: every entity performing taxable supplies in Panama must obtain the RUC and register for ITBMS before commencing activities. A simplified small-taxpayer regime exists only for natural persons with annual gross receipts not exceeding PAB 36,000 (USD 36,000) and is not available to a Sociedad Anónima.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS zero-rating applies to export sales of movable goods (including sterile examination nitrile gloves) only when: the seller is an active ITBMS registrant with a compliant e-invoicing system; the goods physically leave Panamanian customs territory under a validated export declaration; title passes to a non-resident buyer; the invoice is issued at the 0% rate with the export legend; and the buyer's payment in freely convertible foreign currency is credited to a Panamanian bank account. Additionally, the glove manufacturer must hold the medical-device sanitary registration issued by the Ministerio de Salud (MINSA) and any required export certificate to evidence lawful production for the overseas market.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / Documento Único de Aduana, DUA) with port-exit validation
  • Bill of Lading (Conocimiento de Embarque) or Air Waybill issued to the non-resident buyer
  • DGI-authorized zero-rated electronic commercial invoice
  • Purchase order and international sale contract evidencing Incoterms (CIF/FOB)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificación de Ingreso de Divisas) and bank statement showing the USD proceeds credited to a Panamanian account
  • MINSA medical-device sanitary registration and export certificate
  • Packing list and sanitary/fumigation certificate as applicable

Panama enforces a mandatory electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica) system. Taxpayers must issue invoices exclusively through the DGI web portal or DGI-certified private software integrated by API; each invoice is transmitted to the DGI in real time for fiscal clearance and receives a unique acceptance code and QR verification stamp. The software must validate the seller's RUC and ITBMS status prior to issuance. Paper invoices no longer support ITBMS credits on the buyer side, and non-compliant electronic emission triggers automatic digital audit flags and per-invoice monetary fines.

Export transactions are a high-priority DGI audit segment. Failure to substantiate physical export or to repatriate foreign-exchange proceeds within the 180-day term causes the DGI to reclassify the sale as domestic, assess ITBMS at 7% on the gross invoice, and add statutory surcharges, monthly default interest, and fines of up to 200% of the tax assessed. The exporter may additionally be suspended from ITBMS zero-rating, disqualified from drawback and free-zone regimes, and exposed to customs-bond penalties. Fraudulent zero-rating declarations (false DUA, forged bills of lading, unrepatriated proceeds) constitute criminal tax evasion punishable by imprisonment of up to 8 years, joint and several liability of directors and the resident agent, and retrospective clawback of all claimed refunds and in-bond relief for the statutory limitation period (generally six years, extended in fraud cases).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility prevail. Panama has no foreign exchange transfer restrictions, no currency exchange controls, no central-bank approval for inward or outward remittances, and no limitations on profit repatriation. A non-resident-controlled S.A. can freely receive equity injections, loan proceeds, or trade payments in USD, and can freely disburse funds abroad for raw materials, equipment, royalties, and profits. The only relevant national hurdle is fiscal, not monetary: profits distributed from Panama-source income are subject to a 10% dividend tax, while foreign-source income is not taxable if clearly isolated in the ledger and record books. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are therefore restrained solely by standard corporate formality and tax compliance, not by convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export surrender regime exists. There is no statutory timeline for converting export proceeds into domestic currency, no mandatory percentage of export receipts to be sold to the central bank (Panama has no central bank), no obligation to route export billing through a local account, and no penalty for holding, investing, or disbursing export proceeds in any convertible currency. Exporters of synthetic nitrile examination gloves may invoice in USD, EUR, CNY, or any other freely accepted currency and may keep proceeds in demand deposit accounts at SBP-licensed banks without any liquidity or conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama does not operate a central bank. The U.S. dollar is the official legal tender under the 1904 dollarization arrangement, and the Balboa exists only as token coinage at a fixed 1:1 parity. Banking and financial supervision is exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) under Banking Law 2 of 2008; the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) manages fiscal policy and public debt; the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) is the national AML/CFT financial intelligence unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Panama does not currently grant a standalone e-money institution (EMI) licence comparable to EU EMI directives. Banking Law 2 of 2008, later amendments, and the Panama Fintech Innovation Hub permit sandbox or limited-purpose licensing for innovative payment services, but such authorisations are subject to SBP supervision and generally require a local legal entity, a local compliance officer, and a minimum capital threshold. An offshore digital EMI or foreign electronic money institution cannot independently settle in Panama's ACH/CAM clearing and settlement system; it must either establish a supervised Panama branch or contract with an SBP-licensed bank as a sponsored settlement sponsor. For a manufacturing S.A. with material trade flows, reliance on an offshore EMI for primary cash management is not a viable fallback; a traditional commercial bank account should be established.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route for a non-resident parent or SPV injecting equity into the Panama S.A.: execute an SWIFT MT103 from the parent bank to the Panama USD current account at an SBP-licensed bank. The wire instruction must state 'Capital Contribution — Sociedad Anónima No. [PAN]', reference the specific subscription agreement and board resolution, and provide a clear opaque reference number for the receiving bank's AML screening. The receiving bank will file a currency transaction/AML report with UAF where applicable. Supporting documents kept on file must include the capital increase corporate resolution, subscribership certificate, investor KYC packs, and good-standing certificate. Because no central-bank report of foreign debt or equity exists, no prior approval is required; the bank compliance unit merely validates the source of funds. Inward remittances can also be routed as a shareholder loan under an SPV intercompany loan agreement, with a description 'Shareholder loan — intercompany funding' and an interest rate falling within the fiscal transfer-pricing safe harbour if any. For goods-import working capital, the inbound remittance code may reference 'advance payment for machinery / medium-term loan'; no national purpose code system exists, so the wire field and corporate back-up documentation are the controlling legal evidence.For an examination-glove manufacturer importing nitrile latex, synthetic rubber, powder-free coating chemicals, and packaging cartons, the primary clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 plain remittance for open-account supplier invoices up to USD 250,000 per transaction, using our correspondent bank at the seller's bank; (ii) irrevocable Commercial Documentary Letter of Credit (LC) for capital equipment purchases, especially glove dipping lines, chlorinators, mega-washers, and ethylene-oxide sterilizers; (iii) MT700 issuance by Banco General or Banistmo, followed by MT202 cover transfers; and (iv) for local costs such as rent, payroll, utilities, harbour dusting, and warehouse charges, ACH/Electronic Fund Transfer at Panama Clearinghouse (Cámara de Compensación) in USD. For exports, the manufacturer receives MT103 proceeds into the same USD current account; no documentation other than invoice, bill of lading, and certificate of analysis are needed to convert proceeds because proceeds are already USD. To mitigate processing bank delays, maintain correspondent relationships with the supplier's bank in the US, UAE, China, or Malaysia; use exact ISO currency codes (USD, EUR, CNY), invoiced amounts, and avoid 'no further description' free-form memos that trigger UAF query.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panamanian balboa (PAB/USD): mandatory Local File and Master File documentation is required when the taxpayer's gross revenue exceeds PAB 10,000,000.00 within the immediate prior tax period or when related-party transactions individually or in aggregate exceed PAB 200,000.00. A Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) threshold applies when consolidated group revenue reaches PAB 100,000,000.00.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Law 52 of 2016 and its implementing Decree 522-08-2019 impose economic substance requirements on Panama-seated entities engaged in 'relevant activities.' High-speed dipping production of synthetic nitrile gloves is a manufacturing relevant activity. The entity must prove physical premises, a meaningful number of full-time employees, operational expenditures proportional to income, and the conduct of core income-generating decisions within Panama. A compliant sterilization and glove production facility automatically satisfies these tests; failure to maintain payroll, utilities, or minimum operational data will trigger recharacterization, a 100% penalty on the tax privilege, and reinstatement of the 25% corporate income tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Evasión Fiscal

Statutory sanction: Fine equal to one hundred percent (100%) of the evaded tax amount; criminal sanction of two to five years imprisonment under Law 70 of 2018; loss of all tax benefits and automatic closure of Export Processing Zone operating license.

Director personal liability: Directors, president, treasurer, and registered agents have joint and several personal liability for the tax debt; personal bank accounts may be attached; court-ordered travel aban, restriction from obtaining passports, and criminal imprisonment of responsible management.
Dividend Withholding Failure – Repartos de Dividendos No Gravados

Statutory sanction: Withheld amount plus monthly surcharge of 1% from date of distribution; administrative fine of 1% per month up to a total not exceeding 100% of the unpaid tax; if the distribution was made in bad faith, the law treats it as a tax evasion, triggering criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the distribution resolution are deemed personally liable for the withheld tax and any late-pay surcharge; the DGI may pierce the corporate veil and attach personal real estate or vehicles; a protective order may bar unauthorized travel outside Panama.
Shell Company – Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Law 52 of 2016, the DGI and the Superintendency of Non-Financial Entities may revoke the registration agent's license, impose a civil penalty of PAB 500,000.00 or 2% of gross revenue (whichever is less), and apply a retroactive tax at 25% on all exempt income.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil is lifted; directors and the resident agent face criminal fines of up to PAB 200,000.00; they are prohibited from acting as director of any entity in Panama for five years; travel bans are issued during the criminal investigation.
AML – ML/TF Red Flag using Medical PPE Invoices

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Panamanian Money Laundering Law (Law 23 of 2015): fine from PAB 1,000,000.00 up to twice the value of the laundered assets; criminal imprisonment from five to twelve years; freezing of commercial property, trade license suspension, and debarment from public medical procurement across Latin America.

Director personal liability: Directors, trust managers, and ultimate beneficial owners are subject to personal criminal liability; the court may impose a restraint order on personal assets and an international travel ban; if convicted, imprisonment carries a minimum of five years without conditional parole.
