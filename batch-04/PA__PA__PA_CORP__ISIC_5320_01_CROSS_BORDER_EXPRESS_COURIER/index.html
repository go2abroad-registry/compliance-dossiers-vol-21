Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); e-portal: Sede Electrónica DGI — https://sede.dgi.gob.pa. Customs export clearance is overseen by Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent first incorporates a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) by notarized public deed registered with the Registro Público de Panamá; there is no minimum capital requirement for incorporating the S.A. After the corporate entity is registered, the S.A. must register with DGI to obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), file the start-of-activities declaration, appoint a legal residence/domicilio fiscal in Panama, and obtain DGI digital credentials or an electronic signature for use of the Sede Electrónica portal. Where the S.A. will conduct taxable supplies, import/export activities, and employer withholdings, the RUC must be activated for ISR (Impuesto Sobre la Renta), ITBMS (Impuesto de Transferencia de Bienes Muebles y Servicios), and tax withholding obligations. A non-resident entity without a Panamanian permanent establishment is not normally eligible for a Panama RUC; operating a cross-border courier business through a Panamanian S.A. therefore mandates full local tax registration.

A non-resident entity without a Panamanian permanent establishment is not normally eligible for a Panama RUC; operating a cross-border courier business through a Panamanian S.A. therefore mandates full local tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope / Tax_Exempt_Zone_not_applicable

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity classification election. The S.A. is by default a juridical person taxed at the entity level on Panama-source income only, under Panama's territorial taxation model. The principal elective mechanism is the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime under Ley 41 de 2007, which qualifying service/headquarter companies may elect to obtain income tax and certain indirect tax benefits; however, a general express courier operator engaged in logistics does not automatically qualify and must meet SEM substance and export-of-services conditions. Other elective regimes are limited to sector-specific exemptions or transitional small-taxpayer/simplified-regime filings; none overrides the territorial entity-level taxation default.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis monetary threshold for registration: any entity regularly transferring movable goods or rendering services in Panama, or executing imports, must register for ITBMS before DGI. A Panamanian S.A. operating an international courier service must hold an active RUC/ITBMS registration to issue zero-rated export invoices, self-invoice imports, and comply with monthly ITBMS declaration obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim a 0% ITBMS export rate, the Panamanian S.A. must be the registered exporter or the principal of an export courier supply; the goods must leave Panama in accordance with customs regulations; customs export declaration must be accepted and physically verified by ANA; the underlying commercial transaction must be documented; the export sale must be to a non-Panama buyer; and all invoices, airway bills, courier waybills, and payment evidence must demonstrate that economic benefit from the supply is realized outside Panama. For offshore services, the exporter must show that the service is consumed abroad, the contract is with a non-resident person, and no Panamanian-resident person derives a direct benefit from the service. Zero-rated treatment does not grant an input VAT refund entitlement beyond the mechanism of compensating excess ITBMS credits against future monthly liabilities, with refund claims subject to DGI verification and, where appropriate, special refund procedures.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA — Declaración Única de Aduana / Documento Único de Aduana) with ANA exit validation
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Panamanian customs territory
  • Commercial invoice / consignor contract for the international courier shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/credit advice evidencing receipt of export consideration from abroad
  • Packing list and courier master airway/bill manifest (when applicable)

Panama mandates phased electronic invoicing through DGI's e-invoicing platform. Taxpayers must issue electronic invoices in the DGI-approved XML format, incorporating a digital signature and a unique validation code generated by the DGI system; invoices must be transmitted electronically through the Sede Electrónica portal or DGI-approved API/PSI channels in near-real-time at the time of issuance, subject to DGI Resolution rules on electronic fiscal receipts, digital stamping, and rejection codes. Exports must be supported by zero-rated electronic invoices and linked to the customs export declaration; failure to obtain DGI clearance/validation may trigger rejection of the zero-rating and conversion to a 7% taxable sale.

The DGI may scrutinize zero-rating claims through documentary cross-checks with ANA customs exit records, sworn export declarations, ITBMS filings, and bank/inward remittance documentation. A false or unsupported zero-rating triggers a retrospective clawback: the exporter is assessed 7% ITBMS on the undeclared supply as if it were a local taxable sale, plus interest for late payment and penalties ranging from 50% to 200% of the tax omitted, depending on the degree of concealment. Knowingly falsifying export declarations, using fictitious consignees, or staging commodity flows to obtain fraudulent zero-rating may constitute tax fraud under Panamanian tax and customs law, attracting criminal fines, disqualification from customs AEO / licensed courier status, suspension or cancellation of the RUC, and potential civil liability for officers of the Sociedad Anónima.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized and the US dollar is legal tender. No exchange-control approval is required for current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance abroad is unrestricted, subject only to a 10% dividend tax where the profits have a Panamanian source. No prior licenses or conversion requirements apply to inbound equity or shareholder loans.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Panama imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds, including revenues from cross-border express courier services. Service exporters may retain funds in any licensed banking institution without FX reporting obligations to a central bank.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) in conjunction with the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; Panama does not maintain a traditional central bank of issue.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium with legal structuring. Panama has no standalone electronic-money institution (EMI) licensing regime. Foreign fintech or digital EMI platforms must either partner with a Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) licensed bank or request a fintech innovation authorization under applicable SBP frameworks. Direct offshore EMI settlement in Panama is not permitted; a local sponsor bank relationship is mandatory for treasury clearing and segregated account opening.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is through a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's bank to a SBP-licensed Panamanian bank. The transfer must be categorized by the sending and receiving institutions as a capital contribution, share-subscription payment, or paid-in capital. The receiving bank will require an updated corporate resolution authorizing the capital increase, identity documents of the shareholder and directors, a beneficiary ownership declaration, and a funds-sufficiency statement. Because Panama imposes no exchange controls, the dominant regulatory test is the bank's own KYC/AML due diligence rather than a central bank approval.The preferred clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for international supplier payments and fee settlements, SWIFT MT700/MT707 for commercial letters of credit covering freight and brokerage costs, ACH Direct for high-volume validated small-parcel reimbursements inside Panama, and correspondent bank transfers through CHIPS or Fedwire for US-dollar intraday liquidity. Credit-card POS and international acquirer networks are used for the collection of metered parcel payments from overseas customers.Outright FX forwards for major non-USD currencies, entered into with SBP-licensed international banksNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing restricted Latin-American currencies under ISDA master agreementsCross-currency swaps between USD and major trade currencies with the express-courier trade counterpartiesOTC currency options in majors (EUR, GBP, CNY, MXN, BRL) for hedging operational cost exposure

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Deadline: · Frequency:

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) and fiscal identification of the S.A. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 1,000,000 (Balboa equivalent to US$1,000,000) aggregate related-party transactions per fiscal year triggers mandatory transfer pricing documentation (Informe de Precios de Transferencia).

Economic substance scope: Panama has adopted Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) applicable to entities undertaking relevant activities such as distribution and service centres, financing, leasing, headquarter, and logistics operations. A cross-border express courier S.A. must demonstrate real physical presence, local management, qualified employees, and adequate operating expenditure in Panama for its Panamanian activities. Failure to satisfy economic substance can cause the DGI to disregard the territorial exemption and re-attribute all related income as Panama-source, thereby triggering a 25% corporate tax charge plus penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, restitution of the unpaid tax plus a 30% surcharge, annual interest, and fines up to B/. 100,000. The tax authority may also seize patrimonial assets and void the shell arrangement as a sham.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign fraudulent returns or knowingly approve tax evasion are personally liable. Courts may pierce the corporate veil, impose civil restitution equal to the evaded tax plus damages, issue travel bans, and sentence directors to imprisonment. Directors may also be disqualified from holding corporate office in Panama up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Law 70 of 2019 and the Economic Substance Regulations, administrative penalties from B/. 5,000 to B/. 50,000, mandatory deregistration of the corporation, nullification of the territorial exemption, and re-assessment of all income at 25% corporate tax. Repeat violations may lead to forced liquidation.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines up to B/. 25,000, disqualification from managing corporations in Panama, and are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes arising from the entity's sham structure. They may also be barred from leaving Panama pending resolution of administrative proceedings.
AML / Money Laundering (LA/FT offences)

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is sanctioned with criminal imprisonment of 5 to 12 years under the Panama Penal Code, confiscation of the laundered assets, and monetary fines equal to 5% of the transaction value or up to B/. 10 million. The company's operating licence is revoked and its commercial bank accounts may be frozen.

Director personal liability: Directors with knowledge, consent, or negligent supervision are criminally liable with imprisonment up to 12 years, personal asset forfeiture, travel bans, and loss of employment in any regulated financial or logistics sector. Civil liability extends to full restitution of the laundered amounts plus punitive damages.
