Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Panama City; under Law 365 of 2023 the DGI and the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) are being integrated into the Autoridad Nacional de Ingresos Públicos (ANIP), with the DGI e-platform remaining the transitional filing gateway. Primary portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa ('Tu Agencia en Números' / TAN, SISCO e-services).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the S.A. before a Panamanian notary and register the public deed, resident agent (agente residente), and registered office with the Registro Público de Panamá. Step 2: File the Aviso de Operación with the respective Municipio and, for IVD manufacturing, obtain the sanitary license/operational permit from the Dirección Nacional de Farmacia y Drogas (DNFD-MINSA). Step 5: If employing staff, register as employer with the Caja del Seguro Social (CSS). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation | Territorial_Scope: the S.A. is an independent legal and tax entity under Ley 32 de 1927 and the Código Fiscal. Corporate income tax (ISR) applies exclusively to net income derived from Panamanian-source activities; foreign-source dividends, interest, capital gains, and offshore service profits fall outside the ISR base. Pass-through treatment is not available to an S.A.; the entity itself files ISR, administers dividend withholding where applicable, and is the registered ITBMS taxpayer. Tax-exempt zone alternatives exist (Colón Free Zone, SEM multinational headquarters regime) but do not alter the default S.A. classification.

Available election forms

Panama has no U.S.-style check-the-box election and no general fiscal consolidation. Available statutory elections include: (i) the presumed-income election, whereby an authorized taxpayer may compute ISR on 4.67% of gross taxable revenue instead of fully documented net profits; (ii) dividend distribution elections affecting the applicable withholding treatment on distributions from taxed profits, managed in the corporate minutes; (iii) the SEM (Sede de Empresa Multinacional) election under Law 41 of 2007 for qualifying multinational headquarters, granting ITBMS incentives and tax-stability comfort for foreign-source service income; and (iv) the election to attribute controlled foreign-company income pursuant to the transparency rules of Law 254 of 2021, relevant for hybrid mismatches and CFC substance. Each S.A. files separately; no group-level election is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD/PAB 0.00 - no de-minimis threshold for corporations: every S.A. conducting commercial, industrial, or professional activities in Panama must obtain the RUC and register for ITBMS before issuing invoices or operating under the Aviso de Operación. The simplified fixed-quota ITBMS regime is reserved for individuals and micro-sole proprietorships with annual gross income up to approximately USD 36,000; a manufacturing S.A. is not eligible for that exemption and must register under the general ITBMS rules irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Article 1165 of the Código Fiscal applies the 0% rate to goods physically exported from Panamanian customs territory, including IVD microfluidic cartridges and immunoassay analyzers once the export declaration is verified. Article 1165-B extends 0% treatment to services rendered to non-residents and wholly used or exploited abroad, provided the benefit is realized outside Panama; this covers offshore technical support, remote diagnostics, and embedded software licensing for IVD equipment. Conditions: exporter status in the RUC, registration in the VUCE/DUCA customs system, non-resident recipient evidence, no use of the service within Panama, and issuance of a 0%-rated e-invoice referencing the DUCA or export contract.

  • DUA/DUCA export declaration (Documento Único de Aduana or Declaración Única Centroamericana) endorsed with exit verification by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing physical removal of the goods from Panama to the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Purchase order or sales contract, together with SWIFT LC or TT wire instructions where payment is not prepaid
  • Inward remittance certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) issued by the receiving Panamanian bank demonstrating crediting of the export proceeds
  • Certificate of origin and MINSA/DNFD sanitary registration certificate for medical devices when required by customs or the DGI

Electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica) is mandatory for all ITBMS taxpayers under DGI Resolution 201-3274 (June 2022), with phased migration across taxpayer categories concluded by 2024. Invoices must be issued in structured XML through a DGI-authorized e-invoicing solution (in-house API or certified commercial provider), digitally signed with a Panama PKI certificate (firma digital), and transmitted to the DGI's centralized e-invoicing repository in near real time; batch transmission is permitted only for smaller taxpayers. Every accepted invoice receives a unique DGI verification code rendered as a QR code on the printed document; an invoice failing DGI schema validation is legally ineffective and cannot support ITBMS input credits. The exporter must reconcile the zero-rated e-invoice to the customs DUCA exit record within the DGI portal.

The DGI cross-references the DUCA exit record against banking evidence of inward remittances; if the foreign-currency proceeds are not credited to a Panama bank account within 180 days, the transaction is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply and the ITBMS is clawed back at 7% plus monthly compound interest (1.5% per month) and statutory surcharges. Administrative sanctions under articles 700-722 of the Código Fiscal range from 25% to 200% of the tax omitted; false declarations, fictitious bills of lading, or circular remittance arrangements expose the entity to criminal defraudación fiscal prosecution with imprisonment, suspension of the exporter RUC registration, and joint liability against the resident agent in abuse-of-form cases. The assessment statute of limitations is three years for filed taxpayers and six years for non-filers, during which retrospective clawback and penalties may be imposed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

There are no exchange controls. Under the Decree-Law No. 9 of 1970 monetary regime, both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible into U.S. dollars at a legal 1:1 Balboa/USD par; the Balboa exists only as a nominal unit with fractional coins. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, repayment of intra-group loans, and liquidation proceeds may be transferred abroad freely without SBP or MEF licenses. The practical hurdle is documentary rather than regulatory: the paying bank must classify the transaction under its SWIFT MT103 repatriation codes, provide a valid underlying agreement and beneficiary tax certificate, and comply with UAF suspicious-transaction reporting thresholds. Panamanian-source profit is subject separately to territorial income tax and a 10% dividend tax at distribution, but this is a fiscal liability, not a currency-convertibility barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panamanian law imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation of export proceeds. An IVD manufacturer may retain 100% of documentary-export receipts in USD or foreign-currency accounts, including offshore nostro accounts, and may freely allocate such funds to capital expenditure, operational disbursements, or dividends. The only obligations arise from AML/CFT due diligence: for each settlement exceeding USD 10,000, or where an aggregate suspicion pattern exists, the receiving bank must hold on file the commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, packing list, purchase order, and end-user certification for medical devices, all consistent with the UAF regulatory framework.

Central bank supervision: Panama is a fully dollarized economy with no national central bank or lender of last resort. The monetary policy anchor is the U.S. dollar, and monetary decisions are functionally delegated to the U.S. Federal Reserve System. The banking regulator and licensing authority is the Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá, SBP) under Decree-Law No. 9 of 1998. Financial intelligence and anti-money-laundering supervision rest with the Financial Analysis Unit (Unidad de Análisis Financiero, UAF). Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent and settlement bank for the State payment system, while the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) sets the framework for public-sector financial and risk management.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury route. Panama does not license offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, or Payoneer as banks; such providers cannot join the local ACH-Panama clearing system, cannot open SBP-regulated nostro accounts, and lack the SBP licensing status required to accept client deposits in Panama. Foreign EMIs may be used solely for micro-disbursements under USD 20,000 per ticket and for supplementary payment cards. A Panama-authorized payment-service provider regulated under Law 23 of 2015, offering virtual-account capability, is viable as a secondary collection rail only after full mirror-KYC, while the primary cash-management relationship must sit with a licensed commercial bank to preserve UAF reporting and documentary-export evidence.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury route. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panama's transfer pricing rules (Ley 52 de 2015, amended by Ley 120 de 2019) apply to transactions between a Panama taxpayer and its non-resident related parties; documentation (Local File and Master File) is mandatory only when the taxpayer's aggregate related-party transaction volume exceeds USD 1,000,000 in the fiscal year; no separate Panamanian country-by-country reporting threshold; CbCR obligations apply to ultimate parent groups with consolidated revenue above USD 750,000,000 under OECD-aligned rules

Economic substance scope: Panama does not have a UK-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; however, the Corporate Income Tax law adopts territorial-source taxation and the 'substance-over-form' doctrine to deny the situs of income if only formalities are performed in Panama; the Dirección General de Ingresos can re-characterize transactions where central management, decision-making, or core revenue-generating activities for export sales are located outside Panama; additionally, Panama's updated 'Sistema de Intercambio de Información' and OECD Inclusive Framework membership require beneficial ownership information, economic substance information, and transfer pricing documentation for related-party dealings; a Panama S.A. holding only IP without personnel, premises, or operational capacity remains exposed to BEPS Action Plan 5 and 13 reviews

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under Articles 114 and 115 of Código Penal (Delitos contra el Fisco); imprisonment from 2 to 4 years; fines of USD 50,000 to USD 100,000; disgorgement of unpaid tax plus interest at 10% annual and late payment surcharges up to 25%

Director personal liability: Directors, resident agents, and beneficial owners who sign or knowingly direct a fraudulent return face criminal prosecution, travel bans, extradition exposure under the Inter-American Convention on Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters, and personal civil liability for the corporation's unpaid tax if corporate assets are insufficient
Shell Company / Corporate Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of foreign-source income as Panama-source taxable income under substance-over-form; assessment of 25% corporate income tax on the full amount re-characterized; obligation to register transfer pricing documentation; fines from USD 5,000 to USD 40,000 for failure to maintain accounting records; cancellation of the 'agente residente' license

Director personal liability: Directors can be held jointly liable for unpaid tax under Law 52 of 2016 if they vote to distribute dividends without making the WHT or knowingly approve transactions lacking economic substance; travel restrictions may issue upon a tax lien exceeding USD 10,000
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Fines from USD 500 to USD 1,000,000 per violation of Law 23 de 2015 (AML/CFT regime); criminal money laundering penalties up to 5 to 10 years imprisonment under Código Penal Article 254-B; freezing of corporate bank accounts; administrative dissolution for failure to appoint resident agent

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face joint personal liability, seizure of personal assets, imprisonment, and inclusion in international AML blacklists; panama gaming and financing licensing authorities may revoke operating permits for the IVD manufacturing entity
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance / Dividend Bail-out

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax deficiency assessed on the gross dividend or royalty payment at the applicable rate (5%, 10%, or 12.5%) plus interest from the time payment was due; penalty for failure to withhold equals 100% of the unremitted WHT; non-deductibility of the royalty expense; transfer pricing adjustment to taxable income plus penalty of 10% of the adjustment

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve a dividend distribution before the withholding obligation is satisfied are jointly and severally personally liable for the withholding tax; travel bans and restriction of professional licenses apply to 'repeat offenders'; imprisonment of 1 to 3 years for aggravated withholding tax fraud
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Penalty of between 1% and 5% of the assessed transfer pricing adjustment if documentation is missing or defective; mandatory filing of a sworn statement and full TP annex to Form 2000; interest and surcharges on the primary tax adjustment; refusal of export price deductions; publication in the DGI's defaulting taxpayers list

Director personal liability: Directors and tax officers bear personal liability for negligence in preparing or approving TP documentation; if the TP default accompanies a deliberate evasion scheme, principal actors face criminal sanctions as tax offenders
FATCA / CRS Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Fines under Law 23 of 2015 for non-compliance with beneficial ownership register; coordinated penalties from the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) for bank account-linked AML deficiencies up to USD 100,000; possible closure of the corporate bank account and mandatory audits

Director personal liability: Personal liability attaches for AML compliance officers and directors who knowingly sign false beneficial ownership declarations; U.S. DOJ and IRS extraterritorial charges could target the parent as a 'qualified intermediary' failure, with civil penalties up to 50% of the USD value transacted
