UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Panama's AML/CFT and UBO regime rests on: (i) Ley 32 de 1927 (General Corporate Law), as reformed by Ley 5 de 2019, imposing resident-agent custody of UBO records and regulating bearer-share custody; (ii) Ley 23 de 27 de abril de 2015 on money laundering and terrorist financing, as amended by Ley 70 de 2019 and Decreto Ley 2 de 4 de febrero de 2019; (iii) Ley 52 de 2016 creating the Superintendencia de Sujetos No Financieros (SSNF) as the AML supervisor of resident agents, corporate service providers, and trust companies; (iv) Decreto Ley 2 de 2019 creating the Registro de Personas Jurídicas, which requires every resident agent to file a Declaración de Beneficiario Final (UBO declaration) with beneficial-ownership information; and (v) applicable UAF/SSNF regulations on customer due diligence, risk classification, and suspicious-transaction reporting.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Permissible with limitations: every Panama S.A. must maintain a licensed resident agent and a registered office, which statutorily may be the resident agent's own office address; a purely virtual address arrangement is therefore legally acceptable for the registered office. However, for ISIC 1030 manufacturing activity (canned boba/tapioca pearls), the entity must document actual operational substance if it derives Panamanian territorial-source income, files an aviso de operación, or applies for public-health (Ministerio de Salud) and food-import/export permits. Pure foreign-source income falls outside Panama's territorial tax net, but a 'brass-plate' profile with no plausible source-of-funds narrative will trigger enhanced due diligence by financial institutions and the SSNF rather than outright prohibition.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; electronic portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa (Sede Electrónica)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.), the foreign shareholders must: (1) execute a public deed of incorporation before a Panamanian notary and file it with the Public Registry through a licensed registered agent; (2) appoint a resident legal officer and maintain a registered office in Panama; (3) obtain a municipal business license (aviso de operación) from the local municipality; (4) register with the DGI within 30 days of commencing operations using the Sede Electrónica portal, submitting the deeds, business license, appointment documents, and proof of legal representation; (5) receive the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) certificate, which serves as the corporate TIN and VAT (ITBMS) identification; and (6) apply for DGI digital signature and electronic-invoice authorization credentials.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-owned Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.), the foreign shareholders must: (1) execute a public deed of incorporation before a Panamanian notary and file it with the Public Registry through a licensed registered agent; (2) appoint a resident legal officer and maintain a registered office in Panama; (3) obtain a municipal business license (aviso de operación) from the local municipality; (4) register with the DGI within 30 days of commencing operations using the Sede Electrónica portal, submitting the deeds, business license, appointment documents, and proof of legal representation; (5) receive the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) certificate, which serves as the corporate TIN and VAT (ITBMS) identification; and (6) apply for DGI digital signature and electronic-invoice authorization credentials.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box entity classification election or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Panamanian law; an S.A. is per se a corporation taxed at entity level on Panama-source income. Available elections include notional tax assimilation to a small taxpayer / simplified ITBMS fixed-fee regime where annual gross turnover does not exceed B/.100,000, election to be governed by the Multinational Company Headquarters (SEM) tax stability regime, and election to operate under the Panama Pacifico Special Economic Area regime if qualifying; no independent fiscal-transparency election is recognized.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No annual threshold exemption for registration: any person habitually transferring taxable goods or rendering taxable services in Panama must register for ITBMS with the DGI. A simplified small-taxpayer regime is available to those with annual gross turnover not exceeding B/.100,000, reducing compliance obligations but not eliminating the RUC/ITBMS registration requirement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero rate (0% ITBMS) applies to the physical export of goods from Panama where the exporter is registered for ITBMS, the goods are transported across the border under a valid customs declaration, and title or physical control passes to a foreign purchaser. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated only where the service is effectively used or consumed outside Panama and the recipient is not a Panamanian resident; documentary proof of external consumption must be retained. The exporter must have fully complied with RUC and electronic-invoicing obligations at the time of shipment and must apply the zero rate only upon exercising reasonable due diligence as to the export nature of the transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera, DUA) validated by the National Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading (original ocean or air waybill evidencing cross-border shipment)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT payment receipt showing export proceeds received from abroad
  • Commercial invoice issued under the electronic-invoicing regime with DGI authorization code
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (if required or claimed)
  • Export contract or purchase order of the foreign buyer

Electronic invoicing is mandatory for taxpayers selected by DGI as electronic invoicing agents, operating through the Sede Electrónica portal or certified third-party software connected via DGI-approved API. Invoices must carry a DGI-granted authorization code and be transmitted in near-real-time at issue; acceptance or rejection is communicated digitally for fiscal clearance. Digital stamping/QR validation is required on invoices supporting input tax credits and export zero-rating claims. Phased integration covers large taxpayers first, with mandatory digital filing of all B2B invoices completed under DGI's schedule.

DGI and the National Customs Authority jointly audit export zero-rating claims with heightened scrutiny of canned-tapioca and other processed-food exporters routed through free zones. If an exporter is found to have simulated exports, submitted false customs declarations, or failed to telegraphically repatriate export proceeds within the statutory window, the zero rate is retroactively denied and the standard 7% ITBMS is clawed back with moratory interest and surcharge. Administrative penalties scale to 50%-100% of the evaded tax; severe or repeated fraud exposes the S.A. to criminal liability for tax evasion under Panama's Tax Criminal Law, including potential imprisonment of the legal representative, suspension of the RUC, cancellation of the export registration, and forfeiture of goods. Exporters are required to retain the full documentation bundle for at least six years after export.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic is fully dollarized, meaning there is no independent exchange-rate policy and therefore no administrative block on currency conversion. Capital and current account convertibility are unrestricted: foreign investors may open USD and multi-currency accounts, purchase and sell any foreign currency at SBP-licensed bank market rates, and remit dividends, capital, interest, and management fees without exchange-control approval. Practical frictions are documentary and fiscal rather than regulatory: remittances abroad to non-residents for deductible payments are subject to a 10% remittance tax under Articles 762-A and 763 of the Tax Code, dividends attributable to Panamanian-source profits are subject to a 5% withholding tax, and banks apply standard source-of-funds due diligence on outbound wires. For a tapioca-pearl exporter collecting proceeds in USD, conversion and repatriation are functionally frictionless.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama does not impose mandatory export-proceeds surrender, no minimum percentage conversion obligation, and no statutory term for repatriation of export receipts. An exporting S.A. may retain USD and any freely convertible currency in domestic or offshore accounts indefinitely, may apply those proceeds directly to settle foreign suppliers for cassava starch, sugar, packaging, and aseptic-tubing equipment, and may convert surplus at negotiated bank rates. Only standard transaction-level AML verification and customs-document reconciliation apply.

Central bank supervision: Panama maintains no central bank and does not issue domestic paper currency; monetary and banking supervision is exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), while the Banco Nacional de Panamá performs the role of fiscal agent and clearing agent for the Republic. Official tender comprises the Panamanian Balboa (PAB), which circulates solely as a fixed 1:1 subdivision of the United States dollar (USD), with USD itself legal tender under Article 158 of the Political Constitution.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium as a primary banking rail; Medium as an ancillary settlement channel. Under Decreto Ley 9 de 1998 and SBP regulation, only licensed banks may intermediately receive deposits or provide full payment services in Panama; unlicensed offshore EMIs (UK, EU, or Singapore e-money firms) cannot lawfully operate as the treasury core for a Panamanian operating S.A., although they may clear low-value payments if funds transit a licensed correspondent bank. Panama-based digital-fintech products remain captive to sponsoring SBP-regulated banks. Recommendation: use bank-fintech hybrids for disbursement convenience only, never as the sole receiving account for export proceeds or as the LC issuing vehicle; always retain a Tier-1 local bank as principal clearing and credit anchor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity is wired by the shareholder's overseas bank via SWIFT MT103 to the Panamanian S.A.'s designated account (capital escrow account at Banco Nacional de Panamá or operational account at Banco General). Beneficiary line must match the S.A.'s registered legal name and CIF/RUC identifier; purpose description should read CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / APORTES DE CAPITAL to distinguish equity from intercompany debt. No central-bank authorization or FX declaration is required because Panama is a fully dollarized jurisdiction; the receiving bank will hold funds pending release on corporate-resoluce verification, source-of-funds declaration, and UBO confirmation. Capital inflows exceeding USD 10,000 per transaction are automatically reportable under SBP cash-reporting thresholds; for injections above USD 1,000,000, a board resolution and notarized shareholder decisions should be pre-delivered to the relationship manager to avoid funds-on-hold delays.Outbound settlements run over SWIFT MT103 for open-account supplier prepayments and over irrevocable sight Commercial Letters of Credit (issued by the Panamanian bank via Swift MT700 to beneficiary banks in Vietnam, Thailand, China, or Brazil) for large cassava-starch, sugar, packaging, and aseptic-tubing equipment purchases. Panama's domestic clearing infrastructure (Cámara de Compensación and Banco Nacional as clearing agent) settles USD book-entry transfers same day, but cross-border trade requires the SWIFT correspondent network. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (Impuesto sobre la Renta, ISR) on Panama-source taxable income; territorial system excludes foreign-source income from assessment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10.0% final withholding tax on dividends distributed out of Panama-taxable retained earnings; 20.0% final withholding tax on dividends paid from exempt or foreign-source income where the segregated accounting election was not perfected. A minimum 4% complementary dividend tax applies annually on undistributed taxable net income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 500,000 per fiscal year in aggregate related-party cross-border transactions (Panama is fully dollarized; the threshold is stated in USD). Multinational groups with consolidated revenue equal to or above USD 800 million (approx. EUR 750 million) must also prepare a Country-by-Country Report and the local Master File, to be filed via the SITTI portal within 12 months from fiscal year-end.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not enact Economic Substance Regulations modeled on the UAE Crown Dependency rules. Nevertheless, the Fiscal Code territorial test (Article 693) plus the DGI anti-avoidance doctrine and the 2015 'Materialidad Económica' rulings require: (1) management staff and commercial decisions physically sit in Panama; (2) the production plant has own industrial equipment, food-safety licenses (DIGESA/ARSENA Panama), and independent personnel; and (3) the S.A. can evidence operating substance if invoicing the canned boba product on a principal basis rather than as a commisionaire. Pure pass-through invoicing will be recharacterized under the 'substance over form' jurisprudence and the taxpayer may lose both treaty relief and the territorial advantage on the export margin.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Aggressive Misreporting of Panama-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Fiscal Code Article 748-A: administrative fine of 50% to 300% of the evaded tax; monthly recargos; criminal penalty under Código Penal Article 287: 3 to 10 years' imprisonment where the concealed amount exceeds USD 100,000, plus conviction for 'fraud against public administration' under Article 288 with disqualification from contracting with the State for five years.

Director personal liability: Panama's civil courts have regularly pierced the corporate veil for fraud (Sociedades Anónimas Law 32 of 1927, Article 58); directors who signed false returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency; the DGI can obtain an embargo (attachment) over personal assets and a salida del país (exit ban) via the 'medidas de apremio' under the Fiscal Code.
Shell Company / Empty-Passenger Corporate Structure

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the entire canned-boba supply chain; denial of the territorial exemption; 100% surcharge on deductions paid to tax havens (Fiscal Code Article 725); revocation of the 'Aviso de Operación' (business notice) and the DIGESA food-plant license; the company may additionally be blacklisted as 'sociedad sin actividad reportada' under the Registro Público's final beneficiary system.

Director personal liability: The nominal directors of the Panamanian S.A. cannot hide behind the corporate form once the DGI proves the company acted as a fictitious conduit (Jurisprudencia: Corte Suprema adopts the 'lifting of the veil' doctrine); directors may be summoned by the criminal AML court, their residence visas revoked, and they incur personal civil restitution to trade-creditor victims up to the full value of the containers of tapioca pearls wrongfully invoiced.
Missing Transfer Pricing Documentation / Sham Intercompany Margin

Statutory sanction: DGI transfer pricing audit adjustment with automatic 50% penalty on the adjustment (Article 762-J); interest and recargos; exclusion from the e-invoicing system; and for habitual offenders, publication on the 'DGI lista negra' that blocks governmental export certification (SILECE).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the intercompany price are attributable under the 'managing partner' doctrine; the DGI recourse extends to personal bank accounts where the profits were routed through shareholder loans; a criminally culpable director faces 4 to 10 years under the AML statute if the artificial margin is used to launder proceeds.
AML / UBO Transparency Breach (Trade-Based Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Law 23 of 2015 (as amended by Law 70 of 2019): fines per infraction from USD 10,000 up to USD 1 million for the S.A.; criminal money-laundering for amounts exceeding USD 10,000 with commercial cover-up carries 5 to 12 years' imprisonment (Código Penal Article 254); the UAF may freeze the company's cargo and bank balances without prior court order.

Director personal liability: Directors designated as 'representante legal' are personally criminally liable if they knowingly sign invoices for fictitious food-grade boba cargo; they may be barred from leaving Panama (travel ban issued by the Fiscal Court), placed on the INTERPOL red list, and disgorged of any bonuses received; the ultimate foreign beneficial owner loses the shield of the PIF and becomes subject to extradition risk.
