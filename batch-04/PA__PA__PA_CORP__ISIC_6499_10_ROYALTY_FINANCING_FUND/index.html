Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas, Panama; taxpayer portal: https://dgi.gob.pa (electronic services include e-Services, RUC registration, and tax return filing)

E-government filing portal

A foreign legal entity must obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) before engaging in taxable operations in Panama. The procedure is: (1) appoint a local resident agent or law firm; (2) file the RUC application with DGI using the company registry certificate, apostilled Articles of Incorporation, power of attorney, and identification of the legal representative; (3) register for the required fiscal obligations (income tax, ITBMS, and withholding) through the DGI portal or in-person at a DGI local office; (4) open a Panama bank account if payroll or local operations are anticipated; (5) if the entity imports or exports goods, obtain an importer/exporter code from the Ministry of Commerce and Industry (MICI) and customs registration with the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas de Panamá (ANA). Issuance of the RUC typically takes 5–15 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — Panama applies territorial source taxation. Non-resident-owned Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) is taxed at the entity level only on Panama-source income, while foreign-source income is generally exempt from Panamanian income tax. No pass-through eligibility exists under normal S.A. formation; the entity is the taxpayer. A Panama S.A. acting solely as an offshore royalty financing fund that receives foreign-source royalties or interest income (without Panamanian customers or operations) is taxed at 0% on that foreign income, provided the funds are not remitted into Panama's banking system in a manner creating Panama-source activity. The entity is, however, subject to the Annual Franchise Tax (B/.300) and possible DGI information declaration obligations. — A foreign legal entity must obtain a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) before engaging in taxable operations in Panama. The procedure is: (1) appoint a local resident agent or law firm; (2) file the RUC application with DGI using the company registry certificate, apostilled Articles of Incorporation, power of attorney, and identification of the legal representative; (3) register for the required fiscal obligations (income tax, ITBMS, and withholding) through the DGI portal or in-person at a DGI local office; (4) open a Panama bank account if payroll or local operations are anticipated; (5) if the entity imports or exports goods, obtain an importer/exporter code from the Ministry of Commerce and Industry (MICI) and customs registration with the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas de Panamá (ANA). Issuance of the RUC typically takes 5–15 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — Panama applies territorial source taxation. Non-resident-owned Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) is taxed at the entity level only on Panama-source income, while foreign-source income is generally exempt from Panamanian income tax. No pass-through eligibility exists under normal S.A. formation; the entity is the taxpayer. A Panama S.A. acting solely as an offshore royalty financing fund that receives foreign-source royalties or interest income (without Panamanian customers or operations) is taxed at 0% on that foreign income, provided the funds are not remitted into Panama's banking system in a manner creating Panama-source activity. The entity is, however, subject to the Annual Franchise Tax (B/.300) and possible DGI information declaration obligations.

Available election forms

Panama does not offer a 'check-the-box' entity classification election comparable to the U.S. regime. The S.A. is per se a corporation taxable as a separate legal entity. Available elections include: (a) under the territorial regime, the taxpayer may elect to claim the 'Núcleo o Grupo Empresarial' (business group) regime for consolidated transfer pricing documentation; (b) a foreign company may elect to register as a 'Centro de Llamadas' or 'Sede Regional' under specific incentive laws if qualifying, but this is not relevant to a passive holding/financing fund; (c) for ITBMS, a taxpayer with annual gross income below the B/.36,000 threshold may elect the 'Pequeño Contribuyente' (small taxpayer) regime, which applies a simplified fixed monthly tax instead of full VAT accounting. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized under Panamanian domestic law; Panama is not an EU member and has no domestic hybrid-mismatch rule analogous to ATAD, though transfer pricing and general anti-abuse principles may apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 (PAB/USD) of annual gross taxable turnover from taxable activities as established in the Código Fiscal. Taxpayers whose annual gross revenue exceeds this threshold must register as regular ITBMS taxpayers. Below the threshold, a small taxpayer may register under the simplified 'Pequeño Contribuyente' regime paying a fixed monthly quota, unless the entity voluntarily registers for the regular regime. In all cases, any entity importing goods into Panama must register for ITBMS regardless of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% ITBMS) is available for (a) exports of tangible goods that physically leave Panama under a customs export declaration; (b) services exported to non-Panamanian recipients, including offshore services, when the benefit of the service is realized wholly outside Panama and the recipient is not a Panama resident; (c) goods and services supplied to qualifying 'Centro de Llamadas' or export processing zones (Zonas Francas) under relevant regimes; and (d) international maritime and air transport services. For the financing fund's royalty income, zero-rating applies only if the royalty or financing service is treated as an 'exported service' — the service recipient must be located abroad, the royalty payments must originate from outside Panama, and the fund must not have a Panamanian customer or fixed place of business where the services are deemed rendered. Panamanian source rules deem services rendered in Panama as taxed at the standard 7% rate, so the fund must document the foreign location of the payor and the foreign place of use or economic benefit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) accepted by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA), evidencing physical departure of goods (for goods exports) or a service contract with a certificate of foreign client status
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (conocimiento de embarque) with exporter as shipper and non-Panamanian consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (comprobante de remesa de divisas) issued by a Panamanian authorized bank or evidence of receipt of the export proceeds in a foreign bank account, plus the underlying invoices and contracts

Panama mandates electronic invoicing for all ITBMS registered taxpayers through the DGI's 'Facturación Electrónica' system, which is based on the Panamanian Facturación Electrónica (dgi.facturaelectronica.gob.pa) platform. Taxpayers must issue invoices using an authorized invoicing software or the DGI free web portal, with each invoice receiving a unique 'COD' (Código de Operación) generated by the DGI system after validation of the invoice data. Transmission and validation are done in near real-time using web services/API (REST/SOAP) or via the DGI online portal. Invoices must include a QR code containing the electronic invoice reference and the DGI validation code, and must be delivered electronically to the buyer. The mandate is phased; large taxpayers are fully subject to real-time interoperability, and all ITBMS taxpayers are required to use the system as of the DGI implementation calendar. There is no physical tax stamp for B2B invoices; digital validation is absolute for fiscal credit purposes.

Panamanian tax authorities, through DGI audits and anti-fraud programs, scrutinize export zero-rating claims by verifying the physical exit of goods, the genuineness of the foreign service recipient, and the actual receipt of convertible currency from abroad. A false or unjustified zero-rating claim triggers: (1) a clawback of the omitted ITBMS (the full 7% rate) plus price-level adjustments; (2) default interest computed from the original due date; (3) administrative fines of up to 200% of the evaded tax under Article 756 and related provisions of the Código Fiscal; (4) loss of supplier VAT input credits for the period; (5) criminal liability for tax fraud under Panama's criminal legislation (including Ley 70 de 2019 on tax crimes) for wilful falsification of export documentation, carrying custodial penalties and disqualification from obtaining export certifications. Additionally, customs penalties apply for false export declarations under the ANA regulations, and the taxpayer's RUC may be suspended, which blocks future exports and taxpayer status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized, open-currency jurisdiction with no discernible distinction between current-account and capital-account convertibility. The balboa exists only as divisional coins physically interchangeable with U.S. coinage. There are no legal restrictions, licensing requirements, or periodic approval gates for profit remittance, dividend distribution, interest payments, royalty transfers, capital repatriation, or loan repayment to non-residents. The territorial tax system does not condition convertibility on tax clearance beyond standard withholding obligations on Panamanian-source income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. Exporters are free to maintain, disburse, or repatriate U.S. dollar proceeds in any form without obligation to sell foreign currency to any authority. Documentary export records and bank-related declarations remain the only compliance touchpoints, and these arise from trade finance and AML rules, not exchange-control regulations.

Central bank supervision: No Central Bank or Foreign Exchange Administration exists. Banking supervision is exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) oversees fiscal policy and external-debt management. The U.S. dollar is legal tender, and the Panamanian balboa is a convertible nominal unit at par with no independent issuance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low. Panama does not maintain a dedicated electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing framework under its Banking Law. Offshore digital EMIs can only reach the local market through a licensed Panamanian sponsor bank or through the SBP regulatory sandbox. A pure offshore EMI cannot independently hold a Panama S.A. account without local custody arrangements. Legal constraints include restrictions on accepting deposits from the public absent a banking license, the SBP's digital banking and sandbox oversight, and OFAC/AML screening obligations for all USD rails. Use of a licensed regional fintech as a settlement vehicle is permissible but not a replacement for a corporate banking relationship.
