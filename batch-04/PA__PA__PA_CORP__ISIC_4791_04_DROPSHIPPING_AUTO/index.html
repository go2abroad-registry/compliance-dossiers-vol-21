Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the Sociedad Anónima in the Registro Público de Panamá (Mercantile Section) and record the resident agent and directors; 2) file the Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC) application with Formulario de Inscripción before the DGI, submitted electronically through T@S or through a Panamanian licensed public accountant (Contador Público Autorizado); 3) attach certified copies of the Escritura Pública, the Registro Público certificate, the resident agent acceptance, and proof of local address; 4) on approval the DGI issues the RUC certificate and activates the taxpayer ledger for ITBMS and ISR filings. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope. The Panamanian S.A. is an entity-level taxpayer on Panama-source income at the standard 25% corporate ISR rate; foreign-source income, including automated dropshipping sales fulfilled abroad by third-party suppliers without physical or effective nexus in Panama, is excluded from the national tax base. The entity must still register with the DGI, file annual ISR returns (Formulario 1107), file ITBMS returns where applicable, and satisfy the economic-substance disclosure requirements under Decree 734 of 2019 and Law 254 of 2021 even when all income is foreign-source. A pass-through regime is not available; tax-exempt free-zone classification is only obtained by elective licensing.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Panamanian law; classification is governed by Corporate Law 32 of 1927 and territorial sourcing rules. Available elections: (a) opt-in to the simplified small-taxpayer regime of Law 253 of 2021 where gross annual income does not exceed B/. 300,000; (b) application for a Multinational Company Headquarters (SEM) license under Law 41 of 2007; (c) licensing within the Colon Free Zone or Panama Pacifico to activate free-zone tax exemptions; (d) election of ITBMS zero-rating on qualifying export sales under Fiscal Code Article 1040-g.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 300,000 in annual gross taxable sales is the ceiling of the Law 253 of 2021 small-taxpayer simplified regime, below which a reduced aggregate ITBMS/ISR compliance mode may be used; above B/. 300,000 full ITBMS registration, 7% charging, monthly filing, and complete invoicing obligations are compulsory. RUC registration itself is mandatory from commencement of activities without a de minimis exemption, and non-resident digital suppliers must register under Decree Law 148 of 2020 regardless of turnover (B/. 0).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS 0% applies to the export of tangible goods and to designated international services effectively consumed abroad, provided (i) the exporter holds an active RUC, (ii) the goods physically leave Panamanian customs territory within the legally established transport window, and (iii) the sale is evidenced in the export ledger and customs warehouse records. Exporters may credit offsetable ITBMS inputs against zero-rated exports. For pure automated dropshipping where third-party suppliers ship directly to foreign end-customers and goods never enter Panama, transactions are treated as foreign-source business outside the Panamanian taxable base rather than as zero-rated exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) processed through VUPA/SIGA, cross-referenced with the RUC and endorsed with the customs exit record
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (electronic equivalent permitted) evidencing physical departure of the goods from Panamanian customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list aligned to the customs export declaration and transport docs
  • Bank wire / SWIFT credit to the S.A.'s Panamanian or foreign account (functional equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate) evidencing receipt of the export proceeds

Mandatory electronic invoicing per DGI Resolution 201-008 of 2021, phased by taxpayer category: invoices must be issued through DGI-accredited billing software or fiscal equipment and transmitted to the DGI in real time for fiscal clearance via the eFactura service inside T@S; legacy electronic documents are handled through SEDI. The DGI requires prior technical validation of the issuing system; no post-issuance digital stamp is used. Foreign digital suppliers registered under Decree Law 148 of 2020 file simplified ITBMS returns and issue receipts through the SIMITBMS web portal.

Panama is a fully dollarized economy with no central-bank FX controls, so the 180-day benchmark is applied as the internal documentary benchmark for export audits rather than a statutory repatriation deadline. The DGI matches the export declaration against physical-exit records in SIGA/VUPA, transport documents, and bank flows on every zero-rated claim. Missing or mismatched bills of lading, overstated values, or simulated exports permit the DGI to retroactively claw back the uncollected 7% ITBMS with late interest from the date of supply, impose fines of 100%-200% of the omitted tax under the Código Fiscal, suspend the RUC and invoicing authorization, and refer fraudulent filings to the criminal tax-offense track (2 to 6 years of imprisonment depending on amount and aggravation).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies. Panama imposes no exchange-control, central-bank pre-approval, surrender, or repatriation requirements on either current-account or capital-account transactions. The domestic operating currency is the US Dollar, so non-resident shareholders, e-commerce merchants, and trade creditors may freely inject equity, receive dividends, service intercompany loans, and transfer sales proceeds in USD or any other foreign currency without an FX license. The genuine friction points are (i) AML/KYC and beneficial-owner documentation at the onboarding bank, (ii) US correspondent-bank screening of Panamanian beneficiary accounts, (iii) Panama's territorial tax characterization of foreign-source automated-dropshipping income, and (iv) CRS/FATCA reclassification risk if the Sociedad Anónima is deemed managed from abroad. Profit remittance is unrestricted; Panamanian-source earnings distributed as dividends are subject to the standard 5% dividend tax, while properly characterized foreign-source profits retained or distributed through supervised banking channels normally fall outside the Panamanian gross-up base if the territorial-source evidence file is maintained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion mandate exists. Panama does not prescribe a repatriation timeline, a mandatory conversion ratio, or a designated bank for export or e-commerce receipts. A Sociedad Anónima conducting automated dropshipping under ISIC 4791 may retain collections with any SBP-regulated bank or abroad, but the taxpayer must be able to evidence foreign-source characterization of the income, keep the receipts inside verified banking channels to satisfy UAF/SBP anti-money-laundering expectations, and demonstrate minimal physical substance. Operational prudence requires wiring platform settlements to a Panama operating account in USD so that source-of-funds and territorial-source analyses remain auditable by the bank and the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI).

Central bank supervision: Panama has no issuing central bank. Banking, monetary and foreign-exchange supervision is exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) for AML/CFT, the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) for financial-policy coordination, and the Comisión Nacional de Valores for securities markets. The US Dollar is legal tender under the 1904 Monetary Agreement, and the Panamanian balboa exists only as nominal coinage at a fixed 1:1 parity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-medium direct viability. Panama does not recognize the EU-style Electronic Money Institution license, and a foreign EMI such as Wise, Payoneer, or Revolut Business cannot act as the primary treasury account for a Panamanian S.A. because it lacks SBP authorization and a local UAF presence. The legally robust architecture is three-layered: (i) an SBP-regulated bank current account as the primary treasury and settlement anchor; (ii) a US or EU licensed payment facilitator to capture Stripe-type or PayPal-type card flows for the dropshipping platform; and (iii) the EMI used only as a limited payout corridor for supplier disbursements. A Panama S.A. that mirrors funds through an unlicensed EMI in a pass-through pattern may trigger UAF suspicion, correspondent-bank termination, or refusal of the local account; all money trails must therefore reconcile to the supervised SBP bank statement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use a disciplined SWIFT routing protocol. (i) The shareholder originates an MT103 in USD to the PA_CORP S.A. account at the tier-1 Panamanian bank, using the bank's US correspondent (Fedwire or CHIPS) because Panama lacks a central-bank RTGS. (ii) In field 70, state the concept as 'Capital contribution - non-reimbursable - inscribed share issue' and reference the Registro Público inscription number; avoid vague personal remarks. (iii) Segregate the injection from operating flows by directing it to a dedicated capital-contribution sub-account or escrow sub-ledger, then transfer to the operating account after the bank has verified the subscription minutes. (iv) Deliver to the bank a notarized board resolution approving the capital increase, the shareholder subscription list, and the updated share registry. (v) For related-entity inflows, provide either a formal capital-subscription agreement or an intercompany loan agreement with maturity, interest, and repayment terms so that the flow is not recharacterized as disguised equity or a hidden loan. (vi) Preserve the founder's 12-month bank statements and source-of-wealth evidence; electronic wire transfers have no fixed declaration threshold in Panama, but transactions that appear suspicious at the SBP-defined risk triggers must be supported by a complete evidentiary file. (vii) Do not use crypto-asset transfers for capital injection; Panamanian banks will reject or suspend the account.The clearing-rail stack is layered. (1) SWIFT MT103 is the primary rail for supplier payments, freight, customs, and intercompany settlements, cleared through the Panamanian bank's US correspondent over Fedwire or CHIPS with invoice numbers and purchase-order references in field 70. (2) Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600 are used for high-value stock purchases, issued in USD and advised through a confirming bank when the supplier requires bank undertaking. (3) Card-acquiring rails are handled by an SBP-licensed acquirer or US/EU payment facilitator for Visa/Mastercard settlement, with payout sweeps to the Panama operating account; Stripe does not officially onboard Panama-domiciled merchants, and PayPal Business onboarding is available but subject to transaction limits and reserve rules, so both should be fronting rails only. (4) Panama ACH operated by the Cámara de Compensación is used for domestic payroll, local supplier payments, and utility debits. (5) For platform payouts to foreign suppliers, the treasury procedure is to aggregate settlement in the Panama USD account and then disburse by MT103, never by fragmented EMI pass-through, so the audit trail is fully supervised. (6) Fiscal discipline: DGI electronic-invoicing obligations apply to Panamanian-counterparty sales, while outbound wire transfers require no export-surrender filing; retain the full purchase-order-invoice-payment reconciliation for territorial-source and transfer-pricing defense.USD/CNH deliverable outright forwards (tenor up to 12 months) booked by an SBP-licensed bank with documented underlying trade exposureUSD/CNY non-deliverable forwards (NDF) for yuan exposures when physical CNY settlement is unavailable in PanamaUSD/EUR outright forwards and FX swaps for European supplier and customer flowsCross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) for long-dated inventory financing in USD against EUR or CNHZero-cost FX collars and plain-vanilla currency options under an ISDA Master Agreement with collateral arrangementsCurrency futures executed through a US futures commission merchant, used only as short-term macro hedges and not as a Panama banking product

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-medium direct viability. Panama does not recognize the EU-style Electronic Money Institution license, and a foreign EMI such as Wise, Payoneer, or Revolut Business cannot act as the primary treasury account for a Panamanian S.A. because it lacks SBP authorization and a local UAF presence. The legally robust architecture is three-layered: (i) an SBP-regulated bank current account as the primary treasury and settlement anchor; (ii) a US or EU licensed payment facilitator to capture Stripe-type or PayPal-type card flows for the dropshipping platform; and (iii) the EMI used only as a limited payout corridor for supplier disbursements. A Panama S.A. that mirrors funds through an unlicensed EMI in a pass-through pattern may trigger UAF suspicion, correspondent-bank termination, or refusal of the local account; all money trails must therefore reconcile to the supervised SBP bank statement.

10.0% on dividends paid from Panamanian-source profits that have borne corporate income tax; distributions from non-taxed or foreign-source profits attract a 10% complementary withholding, producing an aggregate effective burden up to 20% in DGI interpretations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.1,500,000 (approx. US$1,500,000) in gross annual revenue from international related-party transactions triggers the mandatory Local File and Master File requirement.

Economic substance scope: Panama's ESR (Law 254 of 2021 and subsequent Executive Decrees) requires entities carrying on relevant activities (including distribution and service center operations, headquarters, and IP holding) to maintain physical presence, local management, and adequate staff and operating expenditure in Panama. Dropshipping entities relying on foreign activity must document the foreign profit-generating functions to avoid ESR classification as a shell arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and fines of up to the amount of evaded tax, plus loss of tax benefits and mandatory re-audit.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or direct evasion face criminal conviction, civil restitution of all unpaid taxes and surcharges, travel bans, and lasting disqualification from directorship in Panama.
Shell Company / ESR Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties up to B/.1,000,000, administrative strike-off of the company, nullification of tax-residence certificates, and cross-border information exchange exposure.

Director personal liability: Board members and nominee directors may be personally assessed for penalties where they authorized the absence of substance; travel bans and director disqualification apply in aggravated cases.
AML Violation (Ley 23 de 2015)

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to B/.5,000,000 per violation, freezing of business accounts, and criminal money-laundering charges carrying imprisonment of 5 to 12 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal fines, asset seizure, civil restitution, and criminal prosecution for failure to implement AML/KYC controls or for permitting anonymous beneficiary operations.
Territoriality Mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the entire revenue base at 25% corporate tax, 10% dividend WHT, 3% municipal tax surcharges, plus interest and penalty surcharges of up to 50% of the tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false territoriality classifications are jointly and severally liable for the reassessed taxes and penalties; deliberate fraud triggers criminal sanctions and travel restrictions.
