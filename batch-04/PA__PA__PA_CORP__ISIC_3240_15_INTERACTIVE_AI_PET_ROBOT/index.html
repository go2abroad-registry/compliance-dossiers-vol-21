Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; online taxpayer portal: Sede Electrónica DGI – https://dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

For a Panama Sociedad Anónima, the resident agent files incorporation articles with the Registro Público and obtains the corporate 'ficha', then applies to the DGI for the Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC). Required submissions include notarized articles of incorporation, identification of directors and the resident agent, proof of registered address, and a sworn registration affidavit. The DGI issues the RUC and, when applicable, the ITBMS tax registration certificate. A Panamanian digital signature (Firma Digital) is required for Sede Electrónica access. The municipal Aviso de Operación is also required before commencing commercial activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide US-style check-the-box elections; classification follows civil-law form and the S.A. is a distinct legal entity taxed only on Panamanian-source income under the territorial system. Taxpayers may apply for the simplified small-taxpayer ITBMS regime (gross receipts not exceeding B/.36,000 annually), voluntary advanced pricing arrangements with the DGI, and certain import-processing or free-zone customs regimes; no general tax-consolidation or hybrid-mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold; every person or entity undertaking taxable transfers of movable goods or rendering taxable services in Panama must register. A distinct simplified small-taxpayer regime applies to annual gross receipts not exceeding B/.36,000 (Panamanian balboas, at par with USD).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods qualify for 0% ITBMS when the goods are physically removed from Panamanian territory under a valid customs export declaration. Exported services, i.e. services performed for a non-resident and utilized or commercially exploited entirely outside Panama, are also zero-rated. The exporter must retain complete proof of export and source documentation demonstrating actual foreign destination or offshore benefit.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Panama mandates phased electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) through DGI-authorized systems. Invoices must be digitally signed using the taxpayer's Firma Digital and transmitted near-real-time to the DGI's Sede Electrónica or a certified service provider; each invoice receives a unique fiscal code and must display a valid QR/electronic seal. Non-compliant invoices are not admissible for ITBMS input credits.

The DGI and the National Customs Authority jointly audit export zero-rating claims using risk matrices. Where the zero rate is improperly applied, the DGI retroactively assesses unpaid ITBMS with monthly interest and a 25% surcharge, plus fines up to 100% of the omitted tax under the Tax Code. False or fabricated export documentation may trigger customs smuggling penalties, criminal prosecution, forfeiture of refunds, and exclusion from public procurement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a legally dollarized jurisdiction: the United States dollar is constitutional legal tender, and the Panamanian balboa (PAB) circulates only as subsidiary coinage at a fixed 1:1 parity with USD. Current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible with no exchange controls, no registration gates, and no prior approval for capital entries or exits. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation are unrestricted at the market-operational level; the only practical hurdles are Panamanian territorial-tax withholdings on domestically sourced distributed profits and the corporate governance documentation required by banks (UBO declaration under Law 70 of 2019, resident agent certificate, and good-standing certificate).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panamanian law imposes no mandatory surrender, compulsory conversion, or statutory repatriation timeline for export receipts. An exporting S.A. may retain proceeds in USD or any foreign currency in onshore or offshore accounts without SBP notification; banks only document wire transfers under the AML/CFT framework (FATF Recommendation 16 remittance rules) and do not apply exchange-control limits.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the banking, trust, and payments regulator; Banco Nacional de Panamá (BNP) acts as the State's fiscal agent and clearing-house operator, but Panama has no issuing central bank, no monetary authority, and no statutory foreign-exchange control body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Mercury Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-to-Moderate. Panama's Law 75 of 2021 (fintech sandbox) does not establish a full electronic-money-institution license, and offshore digital EMIs (electronic money institutions) are not permitted to run balance-holding payment accounts inside Panama without SBP authorization. Foreign EMIs (e.g., Mercury, Payoneer, relay-type platforms) frequently screen out PAN-domiciled non-resident manufacturing S.As. When available, an EMI may serve only as a thin collection sub-account, with automatic daily sweeping via SWIFT MT103 to the primary licensed Panamanian bank account; legal constraint is the absence of EMI passporting and the SBP's requirement that Panama-situs payment activities be performed by licensed banks or licensed payment service providers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire routing uses SWIFT MT103 with Field 70 remittance information strictly identified as APORTE DE CAPITAL - PA_CORP or SHAREHOLDER LOAN - PA_CORP; banks classify the credit by concept and require the supporting board resolution, apostilled formation documents, UBO register receipt, and source-of-funds declaration. Panama requires no SBP prior approval, no balance-of-payments registration, and no special-purpose escrow for capital inflows; once received, the treasury department tags the funds in the ERP as equity or intra-group debt and mirrors the entry in the S.A.'s books. For card-generated treasury inflows, Stripe does not natively support Panama-domiciled sellers; the PA_CORP must route e-commerce collections through a supported foreign merchant-of-record or local payment facilitator, with USD sweeps from the acquiring settlement account to the Panamanian treasury account via SWIFT/Fedwire ACH on a daily T+1 schedule.Routine supplier settlements are executed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 through SBP-regulated banks to Asian and European component suppliers, with any required FX spot conversion. High-value purchase orders for robotic-animal components and speech-synthesizer modules are settled using commercial documentary letters of credit issued under UCP 600 by Banistmo or Banco General, advised through correspondents in the supplier country, with reimbursement governed by URR 725. Domestic USD payments within Panama clear through the local clearing-house ACH and check-clearing rails operated under the Banco Nacional de Panamá. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% top marginal CIT rate on taxable income exceeding USD 1.5 million, applied through a graduated bracket structure (15% on the first USD 11,000; 25% on the band from USD 11,001 to USD 50,000; 27.5% on the band from USD 50,001 to USD 1,500,000; 25% above USD 1,500,000).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% complementary dividend withholding tax on distributions made out of the register of taxed earnings; 20% on distributions made from untaxed earnings or from the untaxed portion of the taxed register.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months after the close of the fiscal year; for a calendar-year taxpayer the annual return and settlement of the balance due are due no later than March 31 of the following year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on the 15th day of each month following the month to which the advance corresponds; if the 15th falls on a non-business day, payment shifts to the next business day. Under Modality B, two semiannual installments of 50% each are due on 15 August and 15 November.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within ten calendar days following the date of the general shareholders' meeting resolution declaring the dividend or the actual payment date, whichever occurs first.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 1,000,000 (B/.1,000,000) in aggregate related-party transactions during a fiscal year, which activates mandatory Local File and Master File preservation in accordance with Executive Decree 165 of 2011 and OECD-aligned DGI Resolution criteria; multinational groups with consolidated revenue equal to or greater than USD 850,000,000 must additionally file Country-by-Country reporting with the DGI.

Economic substance scope: Panama Law 254 of 2021 (Economic Substance Regulations) classifies manufacturing of goods — expressly including the assembly of bionic robotic pet units under ISIC 3240 — as a designated relevant activity, so the S.A. must demonstrate compliance through a physical office in Panama, inspection of qualified engineering and production staff in Panama, execution of strategic management meetings in Panama, and operating expenditure proportional to revenue derived from the activity. The economic substance test must be evidenced through payroll records, lease agreements, board minutes, and cost accounting in Panama. Failure of an ESR review exposes the entity to administrative fines from USD 5,000 to USD 50,000, revocation of the fiscal certificate of economic substance, and ultimately strike-off of the registry of companies; treaty benefits and attributions of profits to the Panamanian entity may also be denied in international audits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fiscal Fraud

Statutory sanction: The Fiscal Code imposes a 10% late surcharge, monthly interest of 1%, administrative fines up to USD 5,000 per return, and recalculations demanding up to 200% of the evaded tax. The Criminal Code (as integrated into fiscal matters) punishes fiscal fraud with imprisonment of two to ten years depending on the amount, confiscation of assets, and permanent exclusion from state contracting.

Director personal liability: Directors of the S.A. who sign a fraudulent return or direct the evasion lose the protective veil under Panamanian piercing doctrine: civil restitution for the full evaded amount plus interest, court-issued travel bans (veda de salida) during the fiscal investigation, and imprisonment for aggravated cases; the nominee resident agent who assists the scheme is also exposed to disqualification.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Law 254 of 2021 the penalty range is USD 5,000 to USD 50,000 per default, with escalation to suspension of the corporate rights and cancellation of the registry entry if the deficiency is not cured within the statutory notice period; Law 47 of 2013 imposes a fine of up to USD 10,000 for each violation of the bearer-share custody rules.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot invoke the corporate shield where the S.A. functions as a pure instrument of the foreign parent; personal liability attaches for the unpaid corporate income tax of the manufacturing activity, and directors may be barred from acting as officers or managers in Panama for up to ten years.
AML / Payment-Rail Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Law 23 of 2015 empowers the Unidad de Análisis Financiero to impose fines of up to USD 1,000,000 per violation, to suspend the account, and to coordinate asset seizures; a criminal money-laundering prosecution can run concurrently with tax evasion and carries imprisonment of five to twelve years.

Director personal liability: Directors who designate a nominee without record-keeping, who fail to adopt customer due-diligence protocols for merchant sales, or who sign shipments supporting fraudulent invoices face freezing orders, travel bans, disqualification from Panamanian financial industry positions, and criminal liability for money laundering where the funds in the corporate account trace to tax fraud.
Abusive DTA Utilization / Dividend Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: The DGI recharacterizes the payment as a distribution, applies the 20% untaxed-earnings rate, adds the 10% late surcharge and 1% monthly interest, and levies an additional abuse-of-treaty penalty of 50% of the avoided withholding tax; the treaty relief certificate is revoked and a second infraction triggers publication in the official registry.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify the TRC or approve the conduit structure are personally assessed for the uncollected withholding amount and must compensate the corporate treasury; prolonged abuse constitutes fraudulent breach of fiduciary duty under Law 32 of 1927, enabling civil suits by minority shareholders and foreign partners, plus potential criminal prosecution for forgery where the TRC documentation is materially falsified.
