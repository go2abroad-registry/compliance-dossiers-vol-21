Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

None for entity classification: the S.A. is mandatorily a taxable corporation (25% corporate ISR on Panama-source income) and cannot be disregarded or treated as a pass-through; Panama has no check-the-box rule and does not recognize foreign entity-election outcomes for treaty purposes. Available elections relevant to the exporter: (i) simplified small-taxpayer ISR/ITBMS gross-receipts regime where annual gross revenue is below the DGI ceiling; (ii) monthly versus quarterly ITBMS filing cycles according to DGI taxpayer categorization; (iii) election to offset excess ITBMS export input credits against other tax liabilities or to claim an annual cash refund (reintegro) from the DGI.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary registration threshold: any person habitually engaged in the sale of movable goods or taxable services in Panama must register for ITBMS (RUC) from the first taxable transaction. The B/.36,000 annual gross-revenue ceiling is relevant only for qualifying to the optional simplified small-taxpayer reporting regime, not for triggering or exempting registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% ITBMS applies to: (i) exports of tangible goods (e.g., color Doppler ultrasound consoles under HS 9018.12) sold to a non-resident purchaser and physically removed from Panamanian customs territory, evidenced by an ANA SICE export declaration; and (ii) services rendered to non-residents for use and economic enjoyment entirely outside Panama, paid from abroad. The transaction remains within the ITBMS system at 0% (not exempt), preserving the exporter's right to recover the 7% ITBMS borne on imported raw materials, components, and local inputs via monthly offset or an annual refund (reintegro) from the DGI. The exporter must maintain audit-compliant inventory and sales records proving the goods' origin and export custody.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) filed electronically through the ANA SICE system
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical shipment from Panamanian territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (certificado de remesa) issued by the Panamanian bank evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in USD/Balboa
  • Commercial invoice referencing the applicable HS code (9018.12) and the SICE declaration number
  • Packing list
  • Non-resident purchase order or export contract evidencing the foreign buyer and payment terms
  • Certificate of origin where preferential tariff treatment is claimed

Yes — mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica en Línea) under Law 60 of 2019, Executive Decree 103 of 2021, and DGI resolutions, phased by taxpayer class: large taxpayers from July 2022, medium taxpayers from 2023, remaining small taxpayers from 2024. Every invoice (taxable, exempt, or zero-rated) must be generated in a DGI-authorized system and transmitted in real time in XML/JSON to the DGI; the DGI validates and returns a unique Número de Autorización, and the final invoice must carry the authorized alphanumeric series, a DGI QR (2D) code, and, for zero-rated exports, the ANA SICE export-declaration number. Corporate issuers must integrate through a certified API gateway or the DGI online portal; offline contingency issuance is restricted to DGI-approved system-outage procedures.

DGI electronically cross-checks every zero-rated supply against ANA exit records, banking remittance certificates, and the purchaser's non-resident status. Failure to evidence the receipt of export funds within the standard 180-day repatriation/corroboration window or any documentary deficiency triggers recharacterization as a domestic supply, assessment of 7% ITBMS on the FOB value plus monthly statutory interest, and retrospective clawback within the general three-year statute of review, extended to six years for incomplete, inaccurate, or fraudulent declarations. Administrative fines of up to 100% of the tax and up to B/.10,000 per irregularly issued invoice may be imposed; criminal liability for fiscal defraudation carries 2-5 years' imprisonment, and customs smuggling fines reach up to 300% of the merchandise value. Directors, the legal representative, and the registered agent may be held jointly and severally liable when false export documentation is presented.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% Impuesto sobre la Renta (ISR) on net taxable income; a 2% presumptive minimum income tax (Rentas Presuntas) applies when the standard computation yields less than the imputed floor.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% for dividends paid out of profits already taxed at the corporate level (both resident and non-resident shareholders); 10% when the distribution originates from profits not subject to Panamanian ISR (e.g., exempt foreign-source income or free-zone preferential income). The tax is definitive and levied on the gross dividend, with no additional non-resident surcharge.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months after the fiscal year-end (standard calendar year: January 1 - December 31). The DGI annual calendar staggers the due date in February-March of the following year by the last digit of the RUC, with March 31 as the maximum statutory end-date.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Gross Income All income accrued from Panama-source activities, including intra-group sales, services, and royalties economically attributable to Panama. Non-resident S.A. without local trade: enter zero and attach a sworn statement describing the foreign-source nature of export sales; keep bills of lading, letters of credit, and purchase orders evidencing offshore title transfer. Late filing: surcharge of 5% per month or fraction up to 25% of the tax due, plus late-payment interest of 1% per month; formal-infraction fines for non-filing range B/.50 - B/.5,000; persistent default triggers DGI collection procedures, bank garnishment, and suspension of the corporate registration.
Box 2 - Costs and Deductions Deductible expenses directly attributable to taxable Panama-source income: COGS, freight, customs duties, local overhead, depreciation, and transfer-pricing-compliant related-party charges. Claim only deductions supported by documentary flow and economically linked to Panama-source revenue; cap related-party recharges at arm's length. Late filing: surcharge of 5% per month or fraction up to 25% of the tax due, plus late-payment interest of 1% per month; formal-infraction fines for non-filing range B/.50 - B/.5,000; persistent default triggers DGI collection procedures, bank garnishment, and suspension of the corporate registration.
Box 3 - Net Taxable Income Gross income minus allowable deductions; subject to the 25% headline rate and the presumptive minimum tax floor. If zero or negative, reconcile the loss carryforward (maximum 5 years) on the attached schedule; substantiate any transfer-pricing adjustment. Late filing: surcharge of 5% per month or fraction up to 25% of the tax due, plus late-payment interest of 1% per month; formal-infraction fines for non-filing range B/.50 - B/.5,000; persistent default triggers DGI collection procedures, bank garnishment, and suspension of the corporate registration.
Box 4 - Tax Determined ISR at 25% of net taxable income or 2% presumptive minimum tax on the imputed base, whichever is higher. Compute ISR only on Panama-source net income; export proceeds routed through offshore accounts remain outside the tax base only when the economic substance of the sale is foreign. Late filing: surcharge of 5% per month or fraction up to 25% of the tax due, plus late-payment interest of 1% per month; formal-infraction fines for non-filing range B/.50 - B/.5,000; persistent default triggers DGI collection procedures, bank garnishment, and suspension of the corporate registration.
Box 5 - Credits and Withholdings Advance payments (anticipos), third-party withholdings, and tax credits applied against the tax determined. Offset only Panamanian withholdings (e.g., 2% on local service fees) and properly documented SEM/free-zone credits; refund requests trigger a DGI audit. Late filing: surcharge of 5% per month or fraction up to 25% of the tax due, plus late-payment interest of 1% per month; formal-infraction fines for non-filing range B/.50 - B/.5,000; persistent default triggers DGI collection procedures, bank garnishment, and suspension of the corporate registration.
Box 6 - Dividend Tax Reconciliation Computation of the 5% dividend withholding tax on dividends declared out of taxed earnings or the 10% rate on distributions from non-taxed earnings. The S.A. acts as withholding agent for the non-resident shareholder; declare distributions on Form 745 even when the dividend is wired offshore. Late filing: surcharge of 5% per month or fraction up to 25% of the tax due, plus late-payment interest of 1% per month; formal-infraction fines for non-filing range B/.50 - B/.5,000; persistent default triggers DGI collection procedures, bank garnishment, and suspension of the corporate registration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.1,000,000 (USD 1,000,000 equivalent): annual aggregate related-party transactions exceeding this threshold trigger the mandatory TP Informative Return (Formulario 735) and preparation of a Local File study; Master File obligations attach for groups exceeding the Decree 475/2013 group-revenue thresholds (B/.250 million group consolidated revenue).

Economic substance scope: Panama's territorial system does not impose a universal ESR regime like UAE or Bermuda, but substance is enforced measurably: SEM licensees and Free Zone/ Panama Pacifico operators must satisfy minimum local payroll and operating-expenditure ratios; DGI's GAAR (Law 254/2021) permits recharacterization or denial of tax-residence certificates where the S.A. lacks premises, personnel, and decision-making functions; the Beneficial Ownership Registry (Law 23/2015, UAF) requires accurate ultimate-owner disclosure. A medical-device export S.A. functioning as a pure invoicing conduit - without books, office, or management in Panama - is treated as a tax-haven shell and loses treaty and territorial protections.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to 300% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of 2-5 years, increased to 4-10 years where the evasion is aggravated by use of offshore accounts, false documents, or a shell nominee structure; revocation of regime benefits and 25% tax re-determination plus surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who certify false returns face personal criminal exposure, civil restitution for the unpaid tax, precautionary seizure of personal and corporate assets, and travel bans pending criminal proceedings.
Shell Company Abuse / Dormant S.A. Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Monetary fines per the Fiscal Code for each unfiled obligation (B/.50 - B/.5,000 per infraction), plus the 5%-monthly late-filing surcharge; the Corporate Registry may suspend or cancel the S.A.'s legal status, triggering asset illiquidity and contract nullity; economic recharacterization under GAAR at 25% plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the entity's tax debts when they directed the inactivity; continued operation under a suspended entity exposes directors personally in civil proceedings, and the tax court may impose a travel restriction.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to B/.1,000,000 and suspension of licenses under Law 23/2015; money-laundering offenses under the Penal Code carry imprisonment of 5-10 years and confiscation of assets; DGI may treat unexplained commissions as non-deductible distributions subject to 10% dividend tax.

Director personal liability: Individual officers who authorized the transaction are criminally prosecutable; risk of UAF asset freezes, personal travel bans, and disqualification from managing Panamanian companies.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: DGI transfer-pricing adjustment and re-computation of taxable income at 25%; penalty surcharge of up to 25% on the adjusted tax; mandatory compliance fines of B/.5,000 - B/.50,000 for omitted documentation; interest at 1% monthly.

Director personal liability: The legal representative who signs the informative return or approved intercompany contracts may be held personally liable for the penalty portion under the tax-procedure liability rule and may face a director ban in egregious cases of document falsification.
