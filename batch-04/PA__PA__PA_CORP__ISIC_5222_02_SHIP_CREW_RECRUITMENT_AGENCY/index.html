Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections; classification follows the statutory territorial source rule. Available structuring elections include: (a) SEM / Multinational Headquarters election under Law 41/2007 to treat regional crew-management back-office income as exempt from ISR and ITBMS; (b) an election, recognized by the Panama Maritime Authority under Law 56/1996, to be classified as a maritime auxiliary services provider, confirming that international crew-supply fees are not Panamanian-source income; and (c) a pre-filing source-of-income ruling (Consulta) with the DGI to segregate Panamanian domestic manning services from offshore crew-placement fees. There is no pass-through election; the S.A. is irrevocably subject to entity-level territorial taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 per annum under Law 15/2017 and its implementing regulations; Balboa is at 1:1 par with the US Dollar. Taxpayers with annual gross taxable sales/income above B/.36,000 must register for ITBMS; registration is compulsory from business commencement for importers and digital-service providers irrespective of the threshold. Businesses below the threshold may opt into the simplified small-taxpayer regime without input VAT credit.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS zero-rating (Art. 64, Law 15/2017) is claimable for exports of goods and for services rendered in Panama but effectively exported, i.e., economically used or consumed outside Panama by a non-resident party. A crew recruitment agency may zero-rate manning and crew-management commissions if: (i) the contractual counterparty is a non-resident shipowner or operator without a Panamanian taxable establishment; (ii) seafarers are deployed on foreign-flag or internationally trading vessels; (iii) the DGI e-invoice designates the supply as 'exportación de servicios'; and (iv) the agency retains the signed service contract, vessel registration certificate, crew employment agreements, and bank SWIFT/remittance evidence proving foreign payment. Domestic manning of Panamanian coastal vessels remains taxable at 7% ITBMS and must be segregated in accounting; combined export/domestic billing without separate documentation risks a full-turnover reassessment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación filed through Panama's ASYCUDA/SIEC customs clearance system) for any physical goods or vessel stores
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or International Carrier's Certificate evidencing foreign destination or international voyage
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing collection of the export proceeds
  • Non-resident shipowner/operator identification (corporate extract, IMO company code, foreign tax registration)
  • Signed Crew Manning Service Contract specifying commission/fee calculation and place of performance
  • Foreign or Panama Ship Registration Certificate and crew articles confirming international-trade deployment

Mandatory DGI electronic invoicing: all ITBMS-registered businesses must issue invoices as XML through the DGI e-Tax 7.0/SII portal or an authorized PSFE (Proveedor de Servicios de Facturación Electrónica). Each invoice must be transmitted to the DGI in real time and obtain a 'Código de Rastreo' plus a validatable fiscal QR before delivery; paper invoice formats were phased out during the 2020–2023 compulsory rollout. The DGI automatically validates RUC status, activity class (ISIC 5222), and tax-treatment code; zero-rated export invoices must reference the underlying export evidence. Absence of integrated e-invoicing triggers automatic disallowance of input VAT credits and automatic monthly ITBMS assessments.

DGI audit programs specifically target maritime service exporters. Unsupported zero-rating is reclassified as taxable at 7% on the gross invoice amount, with monthly mora interest at 1% and a 10% recargo on the unpaid tax (Arts. 756–761 Código Fiscal), plus fines that can reach 200% of the evaded tax under Arts. 748–756 Código Fiscal. Fabricated bills of lading, false non-resident certificates, or ante-dated remittance evidence trigger criminal liability for 'ilícito tributario' under Art. 55 of Law 15/2017 (2–6 years imprisonment), cancellation of the RUC, and joint and several liability of the resident agent and directors for proven fraud. DGI clawback limitation is 5 years, extended to 10 years where no return was filed or fraud is demonstrated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy; the US Dollar is legal tender, and the Panamanian balboa exists only as coinage at par with the USD. Full convertibility is available for both capital and current account operations, with no exchange controls, no authorization requirements for dividend remittances, and no restrictions on profit repatriation. Corporate profits are freely transferable abroad after compliance with Panamanian source-income taxation and treaty reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion rules apply. Panama does not require exporters to repatriate, convert, or hold export proceeds in any designated currency or account.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as exclusive banking regulator; the Republic of Panama has no traditional central bank. Banco Nacional de Panamá performs fiscal agency functions for the State and facilitates payment clearing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium feasibility. Panama's Digital Hub Law (Law 166 of 2020) provides a regulated licensing framework for FinTech and electronic payment companies, but offshore digital EMIs are not permitted to service Panamanian entities without a local license, physical presence, and SBP compliance. Unlicensed offshore EMIs pose concentration and AML risks and cannot serve as the principal treasury rail for crew payroll settlement.

10% (final) on dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders; 5% for resident shareholders. Where paid profits had not been subjected to 25% corporate tax, the distributing company must first pay a 10% complementary tax (impuesto complementario) at the corporate level.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months after the fiscal year-end. For a calendar-year corporation, the filing and payment deadline is March 31 of the following year; no automatic filing extension exists.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 3,000,000 (three million Panamanian balboas, equivalent to USD 3,000,000) in gross annual revenue or in cross-border related-party transactions triggers the obligation to prepare and submit the Local File and Master File. An annual transfer pricing annex (Anexo de Partes Relacionadas) is required for controlled transactions exceeding B/. 250,000 in the fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not administer a UAE-style ESR code. However, Article 766 of the Fiscal Code and AML Law 23 of 2011 require real office premises, local employees, payroll records and board-management substance in Panama to support Panama-source treatment, DTA claims and the validity of the corporate seat. For a crew recruitment agency, the statutory maritime crewing license, local crew consultants and payroll ledgers constitute the core evidence of substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Tributaria)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and fines equal to 100% to 300% of the tax evaded; mandatory inclusion on the DGI public defaulters' register; suspension of the taxpayer's RUC (tax identification number) and cancellation of maritime crewing licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers who sign false returns face criminal prosecution as co-authors; personal assets may be seized under the DGI tax lien; travel bans may be imposed by the migration authorities; director disqualification of up to five years can be ordered.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DGI recharacterization of all profits as Panama-source if management is factually exercised from Panama; denial of treaty relief; fines under Law 23 of 2011 of up to USD 1,000,000 for related financial institutions; administrative deregistration by the Public Registry after prolonged annual fee non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under Panama's fraudulent-veil doctrine; directors become personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and crew wages; immigration and customs watchlists may block exit from Panama; criminal asset forfeiture extends to personal properties used in the scheme.
Transfer Pricing and Non-Arm's-Length Arrangements

Statutory sanction: DGI adjustment to arm's-length income; fine equal to 10% of the aggregate value of undocumented related-party transactions; monthly interest and surcharges on the underpaid CIT; possible reclassification of the excess as a deemed dividend subject to 10% withholding.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's-length contracts may be jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency where the company is undercapitalized or insolvent; signing officers face criminal liability for submitting false TP certifications.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership (Law 23 of 2011)

Statutory sanction: Fines from USD 10,000 to USD 1,000,000 per violation; suspension or revocation of the crewing license issued by the Dirección General de Marina Mercante; criminal prosecution for money laundering under Panama Criminal Code Article 254 with imprisonment of 5 to 12 years.

Director personal liability: Board members and the designated compliance officer can be indicted for money laundering; personal and corporate accounts are subject to freezing and forfeiture; exit bans are routinely imposed during the criminal investigation.
Failure to File, Registry Delinquency and Record-Keeping Breach

Statutory sanction: Late-filing surcharge of 10% plus 1% monthly interest; Public Registry delinquency penalties of USD 300 per year plus surcharges; RUC suspension blocks bank account operation, maritime license renewal and vessel-crew contracting; failure to keep records triggers fines of up to B/. 5,000 and DGI's right to issue a deemed assessment.

Director personal liability: After deregistration, directors become personally liable for obligations contracted while the company lacked legal standing; a DGI travel ban may be issued for unfiled tax debts; resident agents may file criminal complaints if directors mislead compliance.
