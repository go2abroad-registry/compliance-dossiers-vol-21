Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá – Sección Delegada de Personas Jurídicas (Mercantile/Corporate Section), Dirección General del Registro Público.

Governing statute

Ley 32 de 26 de febrero de 1927 (General Corporation Law) as consolidated into the Código Administrativo (Legislative Decree 1 of 2020, Book II – Sociedades), principally Articles 1-60; Registration authority per Ley 18 de 2006 (Organic Law of the Public Registry).

Formation / constitutional filings

Escritura Pública de Constitución – Pacto Social (Articles of Incorporation), executed before a Panamanian Notary and inscribed in the Public Registry.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Sociedad Anónima; the charter must state an authorized nominal capital (commonly US$10,000 divided into shares of par value), issued shares may remain uncapitalized at formation, and no escrow bank account or capital-deposit certificate is required by Panamanian law.

Funding & unlock path: Certificado de Seguridad del Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá covering fire extinguishers, hydrant access, exit routes, and suppression systems; structural-load review and occupancy certificate from the Municipal Engineering Department with MOP structural inspection; workplace health and ventilation clearance from the Ministerio de Salud (MINSA); and occupational-safety inspection by MITRADEL covering the parcel sorting line, manual-handling ergonomics, and conveyor safety.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) - General Directorate of Revenue, Ministry of Economy and Finance of the Republic of Panama; online taxpayer portal: Sistema de Información Tributaria (SIT) at https://sit.tributarios.gob.pa; export customs control delegated to Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) acting through the Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior (VUCE) single window.

E-government filing portal

Foreign shareholders may hold 100% of a Panama S.A. with no minimum capital or residency requirements. The DGI-assigned RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) is the sole statutory tax identification number. Procedure: (1) incorporate the S.A. at the Registro Público de Panamá through a licensed resident agent and legalize the corporate ledger; (2) file the annual Aviso de Operación municipal business license, which is linked to the RUC; (3) register on the SIT portal using a qualified e-signature certificate issued under the DNIA public-key infrastructure and submit the public registration certificate, bylaws, officer and resident-agent identification, and beneficial-ownership declaration; (4) obtain the definitive RUC and use it for ITBMS, corporate income tax (ISR), customs declarations, and bank account opening.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under Panamanian law. Panama applies mandatory territorial sourcing: only income derived from Panamanian sources is subject to the 25% corporate income tax, while income economically attributable to operations outside Panama falls outside the tax base. Elective options include the SEM multinational-headquarters regime under Ley 41 de 2007, which grants a preferential reduced rate and VAT exemptions for qualified international services physically coordinated from Panama, and targeted regimes for specific service exports. Household-level and treaty-based transparent elections are not available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de minimis turnover threshold: any person habitually selling taxable goods or rendering taxable services in Panama must register for ITBMS. Businesses with annual gross income up to USD 200,000 may file under the simplified small-taxpayer regime on a quarterly basis; above that threshold, monthly ITBMS filing is mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The ITBMS 0% rate applies to cross-border parcel consolidation, sortation, and direct-injection linehaul services where the service is rendered to a non-resident customer and is deemed used, effectively employed, or economically consumed outside Panama. Eligibility requires documentary proof of the customer's foreign tax residence, a contract with a non-resident counterparty, and evidence that the international carriage continues beyond Panama, such as foreign warehouse receipts, airway bills, or final-destination records of the e-commerce platform. Services failing the foreign-consumption test are taxed at the 7% standard rate.

  • Declaración Única Aduanera (DUA) export entry filed electronically through VUCE and the ANA customs system
  • Master and house bills of lading or air waybills evidencing physical exit from Panama and final destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list cross-referenced consistently with the DUA
  • Inward remittance certificate or Panamanian bank account statement showing receipt of the export consideration (USD is legal tender, so no FX conversion is required)
  • Supporting contract or purchase order with the non-resident counterparty and end-destination evidence, including foreign customs stamps, warehouse records, or e-commerce platform sales data

Panama mandates full-cycle electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) for all RUC holders. Invoices must be issued through the DGI SIT platform or a certified equivalent and receive a pre-clearance authorization number (correlativo) from DGI servers before delivery to the client. Each invoice must carry the issuer's digital signature certificate, and invoice data are centrally cross-referenced against ITBMS, ISR, and customs filings. Export and zero-rated operations require a dedicated export-type e-invoice series explicitly linked to the customs export declaration.

DGI and ANA jointly audit 0% export claims against DUA filings and physical-exit evidence. Unsupported or paper-only export sales trigger retrospective clawback of the zero-rating benefit, reassessment at 7% ITBMS, statutory monthly interest on the deficiency, fines of 5%-15% of omitted tax, and administrative penalties up to 100% of the tax due, with possible suspension of the RUC and customs operating license. Failure to receive export proceeds in the Panamanian bank account within the 180-day deadline rebuts the offshore-export presumption. Counterfeit export documentation or circular transactions are prosecuted as tax fraud under the Código Fiscal and Código Penal, are treated as predicate offenses for money-laundering charges, and may result in imprisonment and disqualification of responsible officers. A contemporaneous foreign-consumption documentation file and reconciled customs and remittance evidence are essential audit mitigations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Panamanian regime is a fully dollarized, unrestricted convertibility system: the Balboa circulates only as fractional coinage and the USD circulates freely for all transactions. Capital account and current account convertibility are unlimited; there are no exchange controls, no Central Bank prior-approval requirements, no foreign exchange budget or quota, and no restriction on the repatriation of dividends, profits, or proceeds from the sale of the parcel consolidation hub. The only fiscal constraints are territorial-source income tax, a 5% dividend withholding tax (10% if remitting to a jurisdiction without a tax information exchange agreement), and standard transfer-pricing documentation. No investment registration visa or exchange allocation certificate is required for foreign direct investment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline applies to export receipts. Panama does not impose mandatory conversion ratios, forced deposits, or an export proceeds surrender period for the e-commerce parcel consolidation hub's service revenues received in USD or any foreign currency. The only administrative obligations are (i) declaration of territorial-source income on Form 202 (Informative Income Tax Return) and (ii) AML transaction reporting thresholds under SBP Agreement 2 of 2015 and Cabinet Decree 168 of 2012, including the reporting of cash transactions exceeding USD 10,000 and denial-of-service due diligence for unusual clearing volumes.

Central bank supervision: Banco Nacional de Panamá (National Bank of Panama) in coordination with Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) and Superintendencia del Mercado de Valores de Panamá (SMV). Panama does not maintain a conventional monetary-policy central bank, does not issue fiat paper currency, and has adopted the US Dollar as full legal tender under Law Decree 3 of 1904, with the Balboa (PAB) as a fiduciary accounting unit pegged at 1:1 to the USD.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility, with strict legal constraints. Panama does not currently issue a dedicated fintech EMI license; a foreign digital bank or EMI (e.g., Airwallex, which holds an Australian AFSL, an Irish EMI license, or other offshore authorizations) cannot open a proprietary account in its own name with the SBP-regulated banking system without a local banking license. The viable structure is a sponsored client-access arrangement: the Panamanian S.A. opens a local operational account at an SBP-licensed bank and integrates Airwallex or Local_Clearing rails as a downstream payment processor for outbound trade disbursements. The SBP requires the fintech intermediary to be contractually vetted as a third-party payment processor, subject to cybersecurity review under SBP Agreement 1 of 2014 (as amended) and data localization rules for customer information. The Panama Crypto Law (Law 697 of 2022) does not license EMIs and is immaterial to fiat-based parcel clearing except where stablecoin settlement is contemplated.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Standard SWIFT MT103 wire transfer via the Panama-based corporate account (USD is the local legal tender, so no FX conversion occurs). The beneficiary must use the 'capital contribution' payment purpose code 'A' (as per the SBP and the Panama banking system code list for inward direct investment) and the receiver must quote an internal 'Inward Capital Contribution' reference. The inbound transfer must be accompanied by: (i) notarized board resolution authorizing the equity increase; (ii) a contemporaneous capital contribution protocol detailing the per-share premium, if any, and the account of deposit; (iii) the UBO declaration proving the source of funds; and (iv) a funds-origin affidavit. For shareholder loans, the route is a standard MT103 with code 'L' (loan disbursement) together with a notarized loan agreement, a repayment schedule, and the Panama withholding tax rate declaration (0% under Panama's territorial source regime for intercompany loans). For remittances in foreign currency other than USD, clearing uses the US correspondent banking network (e.g., Citibank NY, JPMorgan Chase NY) with a final USD leg into the Panama account; no central bank allocation or exchange approval is required, but the originating bank may require a purpose-code declaration and the receiving bank may require a written release of funds from the compliancy officer within at most 5 business days.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 for any international payment to overseas merchants, forwarders, and last-mile carriers. For Chinese (CNH) and Asian suppliers, use SWIFT MT700/MT710 commercial letters of credit or documentary collection (DP/DA) for orders above USD 25,000 per shipment, issued by the Panamanian bank and advised through a Chinese or Hong Kong advising bank, with a 30-to-60-day tenor to manage float. For low-value disbursees below USD 5,000, the airwallets / API virtual account network (e.g., Airwallex global account) can issue local rail transfers (Local Clearing ACH, Faster Payments, SEPA) after receiving a USD sweep from the Panamanian account. The Panamanian local clearing of USD checks and same-day ACH is executed through the Sistema de Pagos de Alto Valor (SPAV) / Cámara de Compensación de Panamá for interbank settlement between Banco General, Banistmo, Global Bank, and other SBP banks; however, the e-commerce hub will predominantly settle via SWIFT since its counterparties are cross-border. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Standard statutory dividend withholding tax rate is 10% on distributions paid out of Panama-source taxable profits. Dividends paid from foreign-source income are outside the territorial tax base and are not subject to Panama withholding. A reduced 5% dividend withholding rate applies when the distribution is made from profits taxed under the simplified small-taxpayer or qualifying special-regime basis.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

No later than 15 March following the close of the calendar tax year; for taxpayers with an authorized different fiscal year, within three months after the approved year-end date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 2,000,000 in gross revenues, or USD 1,000,000 in cross-border related-party transactions during the taxable year, triggers the mandatory preparation and filing of the Panamanian transfer pricing report (Local File/Estudio de Precios de Transferencia). A consolidated Master File obligation arises for multinational groups whose worldwide Switzerland-level consolidated revenue exceeds USD 50,000,000 when the Panamanian entity is a material related-party participant. Transactions with affiliates include parcel linehaul service fees, software licensing, group brand royalties, intercompany financing, and shared logistics infrastructure charges.

Economic substance scope: Panama’s Economic Substance Regulations (Ley 70 de 2019, as amended) apply to Panamanian resident entities and licensed non-resident entities carrying out relevant activities. A cross-border parcel consolidation and direct-injection linehaul business is classified as a distribution and service center activity for ESR purposes. The Panama entity must demonstrate (i) a physical place of business in Panama, (ii) full-time personnel in Panama proportionate to the relevant activity, (iii) adequate operating expenditure incurred locally, and (iv) core decision-making and management control conducted in Panama. Outsourced third-party service providers do not automatically satisfy the controlled-personnel or expenditure test. The entity must also file the annual economic substance return with DIGER; failure to do so exposes the company to fines, strike-off, or dissolution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false territorial source allocation

Statutory sanction: DIGER recharacterizes the revenue as Panama-source, assesses the full 25% tax, imposes a monthly late-payment surcharge of 1% up to 25%, adds statutory interest, and applies an administrative penalty of up to 20% of the corrected tax. Where fraud is proven, criminal tax evasion sanctions include imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and a fine of one to three times the evaded tax, with confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve a knowingly false return may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax. A criminal finding of fraud pierces the corporate veil, allowing personal civil restitution, seizure of personal assets, judicial travel bans, solicitation of extradition under mutual legal assistance, and imprisonment.
Transfer pricing documentation failure and base erosion

Statutory sanction: DIGER may impute an arm's-length price, disallow deductions, and increase taxable income by the full adjusted margin. Noncompliance carries transfer pricing penalties of approximately 5% to 20% of the amount of related-party transactions, plus late payment surcharges and statutory interest. Material false TP documentation can be prosecuted as tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who authorized intercompany pricing known to strip Panama-source profit are liable as responsible persons for the resulting assessment; deliberate concealment of related-party counterparties triggers personal civil restitution, travel bans, and criminal exposure for false public documents.
Economic substance regulations noncompliance

Statutory sanction: The Panamanian economic substance authority may impose administrative fines beginning at approximately USD 50,000 and increasing to USD 1,000,000 for persistent violations, require submission of a corrective substance plan, suspend the entity’s operations, and ultimately strike off or dissolve the company. A compulsory re-registration process will also attribute the income to the place of effective management.

Director personal liability: Directors have a statutory duty to ensure that economic substance information is truthful and updated. If the corporate entity is dissolved without assets, directors and nominee shareholders remain personally exposed to the unpaid fines, administrative travel bans, and prosecution for false declarations before notary, residency, and tax authorities.
Fraudulent dividend withholding and profit repatriation concealment

Statutory sanction: DIGER deems the full distribution taxable, applies the 10% dividend withholding tax on the gross amount plus a 1% monthly surcharge up to 25%, adds interest, and sets an administrative fine of up to 20% of the withheld amount. Intentional concealment is prosecuted as tax evasion with penalties of 2 to 5 years' imprisonment and monetary fines.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the distribution scheme is found to be a sham. Directors who instructed or signed the wire instructions face personal liability for the unremitted withholding tax and penalties, civil restitution of the distributed funds, travel bans during criminal investigation, and direct imprisonment of the responsible officers.
AML and beneficial ownership concealment

Statutory sanction: Violations of Panama AML Law 23 of 2015 and beneficial ownership Law 52 of 2016 carry administrative fines of up to USD 1,000,000 for institutional noncompliance, cancellation of the economic activity registration, and mandatory disclosure to the Financial Analysis Unit. If evidence connects fund flows to laundering or sanctions violations, criminal penalties range from 5 to 12 years' imprisonment, asset forfeiture, and blacklisting from banking and payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors and resident agents who knowingly maintain false beneficial ownership records are individually criminally liable; personal assets can be seized, directors may be prohibited from leaving Panama by judicial order, extradition can be sought, and imprisonment of the natural persons involved is the standard outcome in significant AML prosecutions.
