Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates an unrestricted dual-currency system in which both the U.S. dollar and the balboa are freely convertible in the current account and the capital account without central bank prior approval, exchange controls, surrender requirements, or settlement delays. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, capital repatriation, and offshore debt-service payments may be transferred freely through the banking system. The only material constraint is territorial taxation: dividends paid out of Panamanian-source taxable income attract a 10% dividend withholding tax (5% on bearer shares where still permitted, now immobilized); dividends consisting purely of extraterritorial charter hire are outside the Panamanian income-tax net provided the vessel-leasing activity is executed, delivered, and performed outside Panamanian territory and no Panamanian-source activity is interposed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion obligation exists. Panama does not impose a mandatory timeline, minimum conversion ratio, or export-proceeds repatriation requirement. Charter hire and operating revenues received in USD are immediately lawful domestic currency; receipts denominated in EUR, GBP, or other foreign currencies may be retained in foreign-currency accounts and converted at freely negotiated interbank rates without central bank filing or declaration. For territorial tax substantiation, the company should retain charter-parties, invoices, inward remittance advice, and bank statements demonstrating that the proceeds are booked outside Panama but repatriated to a Panamanian operating account.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank in a monetary-authority sense. The U.S. dollar is legal tender and the Panamanian balboa circulates only as divisional coins at a 1:1 fixed rate. The fiscal agent for the Republic is Banco Nacional de Panamá (BNP), while the prudential regulation of banks, foreign exchange clearing, and AML/CFT enforcement is vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). The Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) retains sovereign reserve and external debt policy functions. For practical FX control purposes, the SBP and BNP constitute the reference authorities for dollarized clearing and cross-border banking compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with serious legal restrictions. Panama does not maintain a dedicated electronic money institution (EMI) licensing class. Foreign digital EMIs may not take deposits or issue payment instruments in Panama without an SBP banking license, and unlicensed cross-border EMIs are generally refused onboarding by local compliance departments. In practice, an offshore vessel chartering treasury can use an EMI only as a payments distribution layer through a sponsored settlement account held at an SBP-licensed bank, with all funds passing through the local bank's nostro chain. Legal constraints arise under Law 42 of 2001 on Anti-Money Laundering, which requires all fund-transfer service providers to be supervised by the SBP or to clear exclusively through an SBP-licensed bank. Consequently, the resilient blueprint is a bank-provided virtual account / API cash-management gateway rather than a standalone foreign EMI relationship.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1a (Ingresos Gravables) Gross revenue from Panamanian-source charter operations, including vessel leasing fees, freight, and related services performed in Panama. Non-resident companies must report only revenue attributable to activities physically carried out in Panamanian territory; all foreign-source charter revenue must be excluded and disclosed separately in Box 1b. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 5% of the tax due, capped at 25%, plus default interest at 4% per month and a treasury charge. Non-filing incurs an administrative fine of B/. 500 to B/. 5,000; willful fraud is criminally punishable with imprisonment.
Box 1b (Ingresos no Gravables / Fuentes Exteriores) Gross revenue from foreign-source activities and exempt Panamanian-source categories. Attach a schedule allocating charter revenue by country of performance. This amount is not taxed but is required to calculate the undistributed dividend WHT characterization. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 5% of the tax due, capped at 25%, plus default interest at 4% per month and a treasury charge. Non-filing incurs an administrative fine of B/. 500 to B/. 5,000; willful fraud is criminally punishable with imprisonment.
Box 2a (Deducciones Pareadas) Expenses, documented costs, depreciation, and NOL carryforwards directly attributable to producing Panamanian-source income. Allocate expenses between Panamanian and foreign-source revenue using a consistent cost-accounting ratio. Exclude disallowed expenses and any charges attributable to foreign-source activities. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 5% of the tax due, capped at 25%, plus default interest at 4% per month and a treasury charge. Non-filing incurs an administrative fine of B/. 500 to B/. 5,000; willful fraud is criminally punishable with imprisonment.
Box 3 (Renta Neta Imponible) Net taxable income computed as Box 1a minus Box 2a, after applying the five-year NOL carryforward. Only carry forward losses previously reported on Form 2300 and substantiated in Panama; losses from foreign-source operations are not deductible. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 5% of the tax due, capped at 25%, plus default interest at 4% per month and a treasury charge. Non-filing incurs an administrative fine of B/. 500 to B/. 5,000; willful fraud is criminally punishable with imprisonment.
Box 4 (Impuesto a Pagar) Tax payable at the 25% headline rate on net taxable income, reduced by advance payments made during the year. Reduce the gross tax by the quarterly Pagos Directos prepaid; do not claim foreign tax credits inside the Panamanian return. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 5% of the tax due, capped at 25%, plus default interest at 4% per month and a treasury charge. Non-filing incurs an administrative fine of B/. 500 to B/. 5,000; willful fraud is criminally punishable with imprisonment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Economic substance scope: Panama applies Economic Substance Regulations under Law 254 of 2021, effective 1 January 2022. An offshore supply vessel lessor falls within the 'shipping' relevant activity and must maintain physical premises, employees, operational expenditure, and Board control in Panama proportional to the chartering business. The entity is required to file the annual economic substance return and keep documentary evidence; penalties for non-compliance range from B/. 5,000 to B/. 500,000 and may lead to cancellation of registration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to five times the evaded tax and imprisonment from 2 to 6 years under Panama's Fiscal Code and Criminal Code; the DGI may also close the commercial registry if the entity operates without true activity.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and interest; criminal conviction triggers disqualification from office, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties for non-filing or false declaration range from B/. 10,000 to B/. 500,000; repeated failure to demonstrate substance results in disolución and removal from the companies registry.

AML / Sanctions Violation

Director personal liability: Directors are treated as gatekeepers; they face criminal prosecution, personal asset freezing, travel bans, and possible extradition to other sanction-enforcement jurisdictions.
