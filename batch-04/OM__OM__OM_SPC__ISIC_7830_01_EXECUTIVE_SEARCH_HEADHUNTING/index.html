Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Commercial Companies Law (RD 18/2019) imposes no fixed statutory minimum share capital for an SPC; however, MoCIIP activity-classification practice for ISIC 7830 (executive search and employment services) requires a minimum declared capital of OMR 5,000 (approx. USD 13,000) subscribed in full by the sole shareholder. The amount must be deposited into a blocked/escrow corporate bank account at a licensed Omani bank, and the bank's blockage certificate is submitted with the incorporation file; the block is released upon issuance of the Commercial Registration certificate.

Funding & unlock path: CDCAA fire-safety certification covering fire extinguishers, emergency lighting, evacuation plan and annual civil-defence inspection; municipal structural and occupancy load inspection for offices (1 person per 6 sqm occupancy standard); occupational safety clearance with a designated safety officer and first-aid provisions where the employee headcount exceeds the Labour Law threshold.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA; successor to the Secretariat General for Taxation), Muscat, Sultanate of Oman; unified e-services and filing portal 'Taxomania' at https://taxomania.gov.om; company incorporation is effected through the MOCIIP 'Invest Easy' platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Single Person Company (SPC) under the Commercial Companies Law (Royal Decree 18/2019) with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) via Invest Easy; the Commercial Registration (CR) is the constitutive document and precondition for tax onboarding. Step 2: Open the corporate tax file with the OTA on the Taxomania portal using the CR, Memorandum and Articles of Association, registered-office/lease evidence, and corporate bank certificate, to obtain the Tax Identification Number (TIN). Step 3: On the same portal, register for VAT when taxable supplies exceed OMR 38,500 in any 12-month period (mandatory) or OMR 19,250 (voluntary); a non-resident supplier making taxable supplies in Oman has a zero threshold and must register before commencing supply. Step 4: File the annual corporate income tax (CIT) return and prescribed VAT returns electronically; the OTA issues the tax registration card and VAT registration certificate after compliance checks, generally within 5-15 working days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Oman does not recognize check-the-box or LLC pass-through elections; the SPC is an independent juridical person taxed at entity level. The principal statutory election is the tax-group (fiscal unity) regime under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree 28/2009), under which a resident parent holding at least 95% of the capital and voting rights of a resident subsidiary may elect consolidated filing with OTA prior approval. Other administrative elections include taxable-year alignment, depreciation policy election (straight-line over prescribed rates), loss carry-forward utilization order, and transfer-pricing method selection, all declared in the annual CIT return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 38,500 of cumulative taxable supplies and taxable imports in the preceding 12 months; voluntary registration: OMR 19,250; non-resident persons supplying taxable goods or services in Oman are subject to zero registration threshold (mandatory pre-supply registration). The reverse-charge mechanism reallocates VAT liability to the Omani recipient where the supplier is non-resident.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Executive search and headhunting services qualify for the 0% export rate when supplied to a recipient located outside Oman and outside the GCC, provided the place of benefit and actual economic enjoyment of the search work are outside the Sultanate, the invoice and written contract clearly evidence an export supply, and the supplier retains documentary proof of the client's non-residence (foreign trade register extract, foreign VAT/tax ID, or residence certificate). Where the client is a GCC taxable person, the supply generally falls within the reverse-charge mechanism of the client's state and is not subject to Omani VAT; services physically rendered inside Oman for an Omani or GCC-established customer are standard-rated at 5%.

  • Zero-rated VAT export invoice and signed executive-search agreement naming the non-GCC client and the offshore hiring destination
  • Non-residency evidence of the client (foreign tax ID, trade register extract, or contractual declaration of place of establishment)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign-currency bank credit advice showing receipt of the fee from abroad
  • Delivery and benefit evidence (search work files, interview/candidate reports, placement records, and itemized expense documentation) demonstrating that the service and its economic benefit were enjoyed outside Oman
  • Customs export declaration or bill of lading is required only if the engagement incidentally transfers physical goods or paraphernalia, which is not typical for headhunting services

Tax invoices and simplified invoices are statutorily required in Arabic/English and must contain all mandatory particulars (supplier and customer names and tax registration numbers, description, tax rate and amount, date, and sequential numbering). VAT returns and data are filed through Taxomania; large taxpayers are progressively required to integrate via API/ERP channels. As of the current gazetted regime, there is no fully operational real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate; however, the OTA has announced phased e-invoicing interoperability, and taxpayers should be prepared to issue machine-readable XML/EDIFACT invoices and archive them for at least the five-year statutory record period.

The OTA may audit export zero-rating claims for up to five tax years; if the documentation bundle is deficient, the 180-day inward-remittance benchmark is not met, or the place-of-benefit test fails, the supply is reassessed at 5% VAT with retrospective effect, and the exporter is liable for the tax, administrative violation fines under the Executive Regulations schedule (per-violation fines up to OMR 20,000, with escalation for repeat offenses), and statutory late-payment accruals on the assessed amount. Deliberate falsification of export evidence or knowledge-based under-declaration constitutes tax evasion under the VAT Law, exposing responsible persons to a criminal fine of up to OMR 20,000, imprisonment of up to three years, or both, in addition to recovery of any refund paid and automatic forfeiture of the zero-rating relief.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts and is pegged to the USD at 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD. Oman imposes no exchange-control licensing requirement for foreign direct investment, dividend repatriation, or settlement of service trade; all transfers are processed through CBO-licensed banks on a documentary basis. Profit remittance by the 100% foreign-owned SPC is permitted once annual corporate tax filings are complete and the Oman Tax Authority clearance certificate is presented to the remitting bank. Omani withholding tax at 10% may apply to Oman-source payments for services, royalties, interest, and management fees made to non-resident payees where applicable; however, dividend distributions paid from after-tax profits by an Omani tax-resident entity to a non-resident shareholder are not subject to additional dividend withholding in standard practice.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or compulsory local-currency settlement regime for export proceeds. Under CBO foreign-exchange and balance-of-payments monitoring guidelines, export service receipts (including executive-search retainers and placement fees earned from overseas clients) must be repatriated into the Omani banking system through an approved correspondent channel without undue delay, credited to the SPC's operational account, and properly coded for statistical reporting. No minimum holding period or conversion ratio applies, and the SPC may retain such receipts in USD or convert to OMR at the freely available pegged rate.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) is the sole licensing and supervisory authority for banking, foreign exchange, payment systems, and AML/CFT compliance. Supporting authorities include the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) for foreign-investment licensing and company incorporation, the Oman Tax Authority for tax-clearance prerequisites on outward remittances, and the National Committee for Combating Money Laundering and Terrorism Financing (NCCMLTF) for national AML policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility subject to strict constraints: the CBO does not license foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) or payment aggregators as deposit-taking banks, and Wise B2B does not possess an Omani payment-service or EMI license enabling direct OMR settlement. Wise B2B may be used only as a cross-border transmission channel for inbound client retainers in foreign currency, with final credit routed through a licensed Omani correspondent bank to the SPC's account. The SPC must disclose the aggregated-transfer structure in its KYC file, provide Wise settlement statements and underlying contracts for every received batch, and ensure that no third-party funds are commingled, otherwise the bank will treat the arrangement as unlicensed payment-service activity or a money-laundering conduit.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder must transfer the subscribed share capital via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own bank account in the shareholder's name to the SPC's provisional 'under incorporation' account at the selected Omani bank, using a remittance purpose code reflecting direct investment equity contribution (e.g., capital subscription/share subscription); the remitting bank account name must exactly match the registered shareholder name, and no third-party payer is acceptable. The Omani bank issues a capital-deposit certificate upon receipt, which is presented to MOCIIP for final incorporation; following issuance of the Commercial Registration (CR), the provisional account is converted to the operational current account and the funds become available for working capital. Documentation required includes the MOCIIP name-reservation certificate, draft memorandum of association, board resolution appointing signatories, and passport copies of the directors; the Funds are then subject to normal AML ongoing monitoring.For executive-search retainer payments, refunds to clients, and disbursements to overseas subcontractor headhunters, the standard clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 through the Omani bank's nostro network, with USD settled via CHIPS and EUR via EURO1/TARGET2, typically achieving one-to-three business day value dating. Commercial letters of credit are largely irrelevant for headhunting services and are used only when a foreign institutional client demands documentary credit; the more practical alternatives are direct MT103 transfers, trade-settlement portals offered by the Omani bank, and real-time gross settlement (RTGS) via the CBO system for same-day OMR payments to domestic recipients. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 3,000,000 gross revenue or OMR 1,000,000 in aggregate related-party transactions; Country-by-Country Report required if group consolidated revenue exceeds OMR 30,000,000

Economic substance scope: Oman's Economic Substance Regulations (RD 33/2021) apply to entities undertaking 'relevant activities', which do not include executive search or headhunting. However, the SPC must still demonstrate adequate premises, employees, and business direction in Oman to avoid characterization as a shell or conduit; general anti-avoidance provisions and the OECD substance requirements will be considered by the Tax Authority.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years and fine of OMR 5,000 to OMR 50,000; full payment of evaded tax plus 25% understatement penalty and monthly interest charges

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or authorize false tax returns are jointly and severally liable for evaded amounts; travel bans, personal asset attachment, and imprisonment are legally available sanctions
Shell Company / Conduit

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of treaty withholding tax relief, recharacterization of fees as taxable income, imposition of 10% withholding tax, and administrative fines up to OMR 5,000

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for deficits if corporate assets are insufficient; disqualification from board service and personal liability for unconscionable arrangements under Omani commercial law
AML / CFT

Statutory sanction: Fine up to OMR 1,000,000 under Oman AML Law (RD 30/2016); licence revocation; criminal prosecution for money laundering; freezing of bank accounts

Director personal liability: Directors may be arrested and personally prosecuted for money-laundering predicate offences; personal assets are subject to forfeiture, and travel bans apply during investigation
Unlicensed Recruitment Activity

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of OMR 2,000 to OMR 10,000; suspension or cancellation of the SPC trade licence; blacklisting of the company and its foreign candidates

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid fines and damages to clients; egregious unlicensed placement leading to deportation matters may attract imprisonment for fraud under Omani Penal Law
