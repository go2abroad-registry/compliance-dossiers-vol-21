Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); official tax administration portal: https://dgi.mef.gob.pa and the 'Sede Electrónica' taxpayer portal for online registration and filings.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Panamanian Sociedad Anónima must: (1) incorporate and register with the Public Registry through a licensed resident agent; (2) file the Aviso de Operación (Business Notice) with the DGI; (3) register in the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) database and obtain the RUC tax identification number; (4) obtain the Ficha de Registro de Contribuyente and activate an electronic mailbox (buzón fiscal) in the DGI portal; (5) obtain a qualified electronic signature (Firma Digital) recognized by Panama for electronic filings and e-invoicing. The RUC must be obtained before starting business operations, and the S.A. must maintain its resident agent, corporate books, and updated share register.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope / Entity_Level_Taxation. A Panama S.A. is a separate legal taxable entity; it is not a pass-through vehicle. Corporate income tax applies at the entity level only to Panama-source income; non-Panama-source income of an S.A. with effective management outside Panama is outside the territorial tax net and is not subject to Panamanian corporate income tax unless it becomes Panamanian-source under specific statutory rules.

Available election forms

Panama does not have a US-style 'check-the-box' election for a Sociedad Anónima. Available statutory elections and regimes include: (i) cash vs. accrual accounting election for small taxpayers under the Código Fiscal; (ii) election to be taxed as a Panama Pacifico or SEM (Sede de Empresas Multinacionales) qualifying entity with special incentives, subject to meeting employment and investment thresholds; (iii) election to apply simplified transfer-pricing documentation for low-risk intercompany services if under the de minimis threshold; (iv) the option to voluntarily register for ITBMS despite being below the registration threshold in order to claim input tax credits; and (v) for holding/trading vehicles, election to treat certain treasury and market-making transactions as offshore non-Panama-source income when the operative activity takes place abroad.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 per calendar year of gross taxable sales of goods and services (Balboa/Panama Calificado; B/.1.00 = USD 1.00); registration is mandatory for taxpayers that exceed this economic threshold, and is also required for all importers and exporters regardless of annual sales volume.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS zero-rating applies to: (i) physical export of tangible goods from Panama, provided the goods leave Panama within the legally established period and the exporter is the registered seller; (ii) services performed and effectively used outside Panama by non-resident recipients, including proprietary offshore algorithmic market-making and sub-trading services rendered through foreign clearing and settlement systems; and (iii) sales of goods and related services to qualifying free zones (Zona Libre de Colón, Panamá Pacífico, and other special economic zones), subject to their administrative regimes. To claim zero-rating, the taxpayer must sustain that the benefit of the service is realized outside Panamanian territory, evidenced by contracts, trading platform execution logs, Fedwire/Direct Exchange Clearing transaction records, clearing statements, and invoices to non-resident counterparties. If the HFT trading entity has routine decision-making and operational control in Panama, the DGI may recharacterize the service as Panamanian-source and apply 7% ITBMS, so the offshore substance evidence must be contemporaneous.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) accepted by the National Customs Authority (ANA), including the bill of lading/airway bill and exporting invoice
  • Original Bill of Lading or transport document evidencing physical departure of the goods from Panama (not applicable to pure offshore services)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) from a licensed Panamanian bank evidencing repatriation of export sale proceeds into Panama

Mandatory electronic invoicing applies to taxpayers required to register for ITBMS. The DGI operates the Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (e-invoicing platform) requiring XML e-invoices with a unique sequential folio, digital signature of the taxpayer or authorized software provider, and transmission to the DGI in near real time for validation, digital stamping, and issuance of an acceptance message with a QR code. Invoices must be issued through DGI-authorized billing software or the DGI online portal; API integration and secure authentication are mandatory for high-volume taxpayers. Transitional paper invoices require express DGI authorization and are subject to inspection visit controls. E-invoice records must be retained for six years, and debit/credit notes must similarly obtain fiscal validation.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to DGI and ANA joint audit scrutiny. If the DGI determines that the taxpayer failed to substantiate the export, the services were actually consumed in Panama, or the goods never left Panamanian territory, it will issue a retrospective clawback of the zero rate, assessing the 7% ITBMS plus compensatory interest from the original invoice date. Administrative fines under the Código Fiscal and fiscal legislation apply, typically ranging from B/.500 to B/.20,000 per infraction, with additional penalties equal to 100% of the omitted tax in cases of fraudulent mis-declaration. Knowingly issuing false export invoices or using forged customs documents can trigger criminal fraud charges under Panama's Criminal Code, the suspension or cancellation of the RUC and export-trade licenses, and civil liability for directors, the resident agent, and authorized signatories. The standard ITBMS statute of limitations generally permits DGI assessment within five years, which may be extended in cases of omitted filings or concealed offshore arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility and complete dollarization. Panama maintains no legal distinction between current and capital account convertibility, and there are no exchange controls, surrender rules, or repatriation limits. Dividends, interest, royalties, trading profits, liquidation proceeds, and capital returns are freely remittable in U.S. dollars. The only practical constraints are SBP-mandated AML/KYC documentary requirements and the need to evidence lawful source of funds for tax and regulatory transparency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained abroad or in any local USD account without any Central Bank approval, timeline, or exchange-rate conversion. Books and records must still be retained for DGI (Dirección General de Ingresos) and SBP AML inspection purposes.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as the banking and AML/CFT supervisory authority; monetary authority is effectively the U.S. Federal Reserve because Panama is fully dollarized; Banco Nacional de Panamá performs certain fiscal agency functions but is not a central bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Panama's banking law reserves deposit-taking and settlement functions to SBP-licensed banks. Offshore digital EMIs are not directly chartered to open local clearing accounts or hold client funds in Panama. They may serve as payment-instrument gateways only if contractually backed by a licensed correspondent bank and approved by the receiving Panama institution. For a high-frequency treasury operation, an offshore EMI is not a substitute for a Tier-1 bank relationship; at most it is a supplementary API-accessible payment channel with heightened operational risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 from the ultimate shareholder's bank to the Panama account-beneficiary bank, with field 70 containing 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / PANAMA S.A. OPERATIONAL ACCOUNT'. Because Panama has no FX-control approval regime, no Central Bank filing is required; the only gate is SBP-compliant KYC/AML verification and the bank's internal source-of-wealth check. If the receiving bank has a Federal Reserve master account, Fedwire can be used for same-day USD settlement (e.g., Banco Nacional de Panamá). For proprietary HFT operating payments, preferred rails are SWIFT MT103 with structured remittance data and Fedwire for USD-domestic instant settlement. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% on cash dividends distributed from Panama-source net profits; 10% applies to dividends paid in excess of distributable taxable profits or when the payor failed to comply with CIT disclosure rules. Dividends paid from foreign-source (non-Panama) profits are not subject to Panamanian dividend WHT.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months after fiscal year-end; for a standard December 31 fiscal year-end, the filing deadline is March 31 of the following year. No automatic extension; a one-time 2-month extension may be granted upon prior request.

Failure to file electronically triggers suspension of tax clearance certificate (Paz y Salvo).
Box 5 Deductible costs and expenses (Costos y Gastos Deducibles) attributable to taxable Panamanian-source revenue. Expenses attributable to foreign-source income may not be netted against Panama-source revenue; maintain contemporaneous expense allocation. Late filing: fine from USD 50 to USD 5,000 for formal non-compliance; late payment: monthly surcharge (recargo) of 1.5% on the unpaid tax, capped at 50%, plus moratory interest of 1% per month. Failure to file electronically triggers suspension of tax clearance certificate (Paz y Salvo).
Box 8 Net taxable income (Renta Neta Imponible) after cost of sales, expenses, and loss carryforwards (maximum 5 years). Carryforward losses may only offset Panama-source taxable income; foreign trading losses are non-deductible. Late filing: fine from USD 50 to USD 5,000 for formal non-compliance; late payment: monthly surcharge (recargo) of 1.5% on the unpaid tax, capped at 50%, plus moratory interest of 1% per month. Failure to file electronically triggers suspension of tax clearance certificate (Paz y Salvo).
Box 11 Corporate income tax determined (ISR a cargo) at 15%/25% progressive schedule. Computed exclusively on Panama-source net income; foreign tax credits are not applicable under territorial law. Late filing: fine from USD 50 to USD 5,000 for formal non-compliance; late payment: monthly surcharge (recargo) of 1.5% on the unpaid tax, capped at 50%, plus moratory interest of 1% per month. Failure to file electronically triggers suspension of tax clearance certificate (Paz y Salvo).
Box 14 Withholdings applied at source (Retenciones Sufridas) and advance tax payments (Anticipos) credited against final liability. Verify broker, exchange, and clearinghouse Panama-source WHT certificates before claiming credit. Late filing: fine from USD 50 to USD 5,000 for formal non-compliance; late payment: monthly surcharge (recargo) of 1.5% on the unpaid tax, capped at 50%, plus moratory interest of 1% per month. Failure to file electronically triggers suspension of tax clearance certificate (Paz y Salvo).
Box 17 Net tax payable or balance in favor (Saldo a Pagar / Saldo a Favor). Payment must be wired to DGI collection accounts in USD; zero-balance filings for fully foreign-source HFT operations are acceptable only if documentation is complete. Late filing: fine from USD 50 to USD 5,000 for formal non-compliance; late payment: monthly surcharge (recargo) of 1.5% on the unpaid tax, capped at 50%, plus moratory interest of 1% per month. Failure to file electronically triggers suspension of tax clearance certificate (Paz y Salvo).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 250,000: annual aggregate of cross-border related-party transactions exceeding this threshold triggers the mandatory sworn transfer pricing annex (Formulario 1230) to the annual CIT return. Comprehensive Local File style study and documentation are required when the annual related-party transaction value exceeds USD 1,000,000. Panamanian TP rules apply regardless of the entity's net profit; failure to file triggers presumptive adjustments.

Economic substance scope: Panama adopted Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) under the EU-driven transparency reforms (Executive Decree 270 of 2021 and DGI Resolution 201-2127). Relevant activities include financing/leasing, IP holding, distribution and service centers, headquarters, shipping, and pure equity holding. A proprietary algorithmic HFT entity that books trades, holds trading platforms, or extends margin/clearing credit from Panama may be classified under the financing/leasing category and must demonstrate: (i) physical premises in Panama; (ii) locally employed qualified staff; (iii) strategic decision-making by directors resident in Panama; and (iv) adequate local expenditure. Annual ESR return is mandatory for qualifying entities; penalties range from USD 10,000 to USD 1,000,000 for false or missing declarations, plus strike-off and exchange-of-information consequences.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Tributaria)

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% surcharge on the tax evaded plus full back assessment and moratory interest; criminal conviction under Law 70 of 2019 where the evaded amount exceeds USD 300,000: imprisonment of 3 to 8 years, fines up to double the evaded tax, and publication of the taxpayer's name.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable for taxes subject to withhold-and-remit obligations (Article 42 Tax Code); criminal liability may attach to the legal representative who signs the false return; travel bans and exit prohibitions can be imposed during criminal tax proceedings; corporate veil piercing for fraud or sham arrangements exposes personal wealth.
Shell Company / Substance Deficiency (ESR and Beneficial Ownership)

Statutory sanction: ESR penalty from USD 10,000 to USD 1,000,000; immediate cancellation of tax clearance certificate; administrative strike-off of the company; inclusion in the EU/global tax transparency blacklist triggers defensive measures and automatic exchange of information with foreign tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors of a non-compliant shell entity face personal fines equal to 25% of the company's penalty and are barred from registering new companies in Panama; travel bans may be issued by the DGI; persistent abuse supports piercing of the limited liability veil by creditors and tax authorities.
AML / Terrorist Financing (Law 23 of 2015)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to USD 1,000,000 per violation; suspension of operations; criminal money laundering penalties of 5 to 10 years imprisonment for predicate offenses and confiscation of trading assets and bank balances.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners are personally liable for willful AML non-compliance; international travel bans and asset freezes can be imposed by the Superintendencia de Bancos and UAF; extradition risk exists for predicate tax offenses and money laundering rings.
Withholding Fraud / Improper Treaty Relief

Statutory sanction: Retroactive denial of treaty benefits, collection of the full 12.5%/25% WHT differential, 50% surcharge, and moratory interest; criminal tax evasion proceedings under Law 70 of 2019 where deliberate; blacklisting from withholding clearance procedures.

Director personal liability: Signing officers face joint and several civil liability for the unpaid WHT differential; criminal prosecution for falsification of public documents in the TRC or WHT reduction applications; imprisonment up to 5 years; travel bans pending final assessment.
