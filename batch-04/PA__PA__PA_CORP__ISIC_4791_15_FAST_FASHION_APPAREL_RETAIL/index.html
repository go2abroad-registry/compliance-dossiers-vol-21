Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá — Departamento de Registro Mercantil y de Personas Jurídicas (Public Registry of Panama, Mercantile and Legal Persons Registration Department), Panama City.

Governing statute

Ley No. 32 de 1927 (General Corporation Law of the Republic of Panama), as amended and consolidated; relevant provisions: Articles 1–13 (formation and incorporation), Articles 14–26 (capital, shares and subscriptions), Articles 27–49 (directors, officers and corporate governance), and Article 28 (reserved powers of the board).

Formation / constitutional filings

Public Deed of Incorporation of a Corporation — 'Escritura Pública de Constitución de Sociedad Anónima' (containing the Pacto Social y Estatutos), presented for inscription in the Mercantile Registry.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 12 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement under Law 32 of 1927. The S.A. must subscribe and issue at least one (1) share; the incorporators declare the subscribed capital in the deed for statutory and stamp-tax purposes, and full payment may be deferred as provided in the Articles.

Funding & unlock path: Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá fire-safety certificate for offices and storage areas (NFPA-compliant detectors and extinguishers); municipal engineering structural/inspection clearance for any physical premises used by the e-commerce retailer; and, where staff exceed 10 employees, workplace hygiene registration with the Ministerio de Trabajo y Desarrollo Laboral (MITRADEL).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), dependent organ of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); official e-filing portal: 'Sede Electrónica' at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa (and https://www.dgi.gob.pa). The DGI administers the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC), the ITBMS, income tax (ISR), the simplified SME regime, and electronic invoicing authorization.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) at the Registro Público de Panamá through a licensed agent with a resident agent and registered officers (Canon corporate annuity paid). Step 2 — Obtain municipal 'Aviso de Operación' (Comercio/licencia) at the appropriate Municipio for the commercial address. Step 3 — Register with the DGI's Sede Electrónica using the incorporated company's notarial documents, municipal license, and a Firma Electrónica Calificada (digital certificate issued by a certified authority); DGI issues the RUC for the Panamanian branch/S.A. including the indicative tax classification (Territorial ITBMS, employer at PAYEE). Step 4 — If gross taxable income exceeds B/.36,000 per year (or voluntarily), file the ITBMS registration form (Formulario 111/Inscripción ITBMS) at the DGI with proof of bank account and local legal representation. Step 5 — Register for the electronic invoicing system (DGI-FEL) selecting the free 'Facturador Web' or a certified billing software, and obtain the activation codes and digital stamp. Foreign non-resident principals must act through the resident agent and provide certified incorporation certificates, passport/legal entity documents, and AML/KYC file (Law 23 of 2015).

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A Panamanian S.A. is taxed at the entity level solely on Panama-source income; foreign-source income (including foreign e-commerce retail sales) is outside the taxable base and no remittance tax applies. Panamanian inside-Panama B2C sales of fast-fashion apparel are attributed to Panama and subject to income tax (25% standard ISR; effective progressive rate for the regulated small/medium business) plus ITBMS on the gross sale. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — A Panamanian S.A. is taxed at the entity level solely on Panama-source income; foreign-source income (including foreign e-commerce retail sales) is outside the taxable base and no remittance tax applies. Panamanian inside-Panama B2C sales of fast-fashion apparel are attributed to Panama and subject to income tax (25% standard ISR; effective progressive rate for the regulated small/medium business) plus ITBMS on the gross sale. The entity is not a pass-through; dividends distributed from already-taxed Panama-source profits attract a 5% dividend tax (when paid to residents) or 10% (to non-residents), while undistributed foreign-source profit remains exempt. No economic-substance minimum is required by law, but DGI may attribute offshore activity where administrative, decision-making, and invoicing functions are actually performed from Panama; the 'Tax Exempt Zone' classification is unavailable except where the company elects the Colón Free Zone or SEM regime for qualifying service hubs, which does not apply to this fast-fashion e-commerce scope.

Available election forms

Panama does not adopt US 'check-the-box' or hybrid-mismatch elections; the S.A. is a taxable corporation by default. Available elections/regimes: (i) voluntary ITBMS registration for taxpayers below the B/.36,000 gross-turnover threshold; (ii) election to be taxed under the 'Régimen Simplificado de Tributación' (RST, simplified method) for small retailers with annual gross income under B/.200,000 and payroll parameters, where income tax is replaced by a 2% (gross) ITBMS-equivalent advance, reducing bookkeeping burden; (iii) election between territorial versus non-territorial treatment is not available, but a taxpayer can choose branch (permanent establishment) versus subsidiary structure for foreign expansion; (iv) election to apply the 'Sede de Empresa Multinacional' (SEM) benefits (5% royalty/Dividend treatment, ITBMS exemption on imported services) requires a separate license from the SEM Commission and is not applicable to mere apparel merchandising; (v) option to request the 'Régimen de Tributación Internacional (RIT)'? — no; fiscal consolidation is not elective. DGI prior filing is required for any election to change the taxable year or to obtain an advanced-pricing agreement.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 (equivalent to USD 36,000) of gross taxable income per calendar year in Panama; mandatory ITBMS registration and monthly/ bimonthly returns above this threshold; registration below the threshold is voluntary but advisable to recover input credits. E-commerce and cross-border digital retail of apparel is within the taxable subject matter: each delivery of goods physically in Panama triggers ITBMS at 7% (plus 10% for alcohol/tabacco/hotels, which does not impact fashion apparel).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: Exports of tangible merchandise from Panama are zero-rated (0% ITBMS) where (i) the seller holds legal title, (ii) the merchandise physically departs Panama under a valid customs export process, (iii) the final delivery address is outside Panama and the sale is to a non-resident purchaser (export evidence: DUEX + B/L + payment from abroad), and (iv) the sale is made and invoiced by the Panamanian company. Services: services qualify as 'exportados' when the beneficiary is a non-resident (or a SEM/ multinational head office abroad), the benefit/use occurs outside Panama, and the consideration enters Panama's banking system; offshore-targeted logistics, fulfillment, marketing and marketplace-support services bundled with fast-fashion e-commerce can be zero-rated if supported by a service agreement showing foreign use. Critical caveats: sales delivered to a Panama address (local warehouse/ P.O. box) are always taxed at 7%, even if the payment gateway is foreign; returns are subject to ITBMS reversal rules where the original invoice was zero-rated — the refund credit requires a negative export invoice. Input tax credits (ITBMS paid on local purchases of inventory? — inventory imports, logistics, packaging, and marketplace fees) are recoverable under the exporter's 'crédito fiscal' regime; a refund ('devolución de ITBMS') is claimable quarterly, however DGI practice requires offset against other taxes first and physical audited verification.

  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill — transport document showing the port/airport of exit and foreign delivery point
  • Commercial invoice marked 'EXPORTACIÓN / 0% ITBMS' with item-level description of fast-fashion SKUs, Incoterms, and foreign buyer VAT number
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank statement — credit advice from Banco Nacional de Panamá or a licensed bank evidencing the proceeds received from the foreign customer within the Panamanian banking system (proceeds may be received in USD with no exchange requirement due to the Balboa/USD peg)
  • Proof of foreign destination — online order details, shipping manifest from the e-commerce platform and logistics contract (for Shopify / Klarna / marketplace receipts)
  • Certificate of Origin (optional yet recommended) and the customs 'Formulario de Movimiento' where goods move through a zona franca

Mandatory since September 2023 for all ITBMS-registered taxpayers. Issuance rules: (i) invoices must be generated through DGI-FEL (Facturación Electrónica en Línea) using either DGI's free 'Facturador' web tool or certified ERP/POS software integrated by APIs in XML/JSON; (ii) each invoice must contain a unique consecutive number, QR code, tax classification code, the RUC-N point of sale, and an authorization number/ 'Sello Digital' issued by DGI in near-real time; (iii) Panama's system is 'pre-issued authorization' — the invoice must be transmitted to DGI's platform before issuance to obtain the cufe-like authorization code (not a continuous real-time clearance as in Mexico, but an online pre-issuance control with 24-hour transmission windows for retailers); (iv) the free DGI portal is available to small filers without API costs; no additional plataforma of 'retención' applies at the B2C level for payment gateways (Klarna BNPL / Shopify Payments) unless the gateway is a Panamanian financial institution subject to withholding; (v) fiscal point-of-sale (digital fiscal equipment) is no longer mandatory for purely online sellers; the electronic receipt is sufficient; (vi) penalties for non-issuance: surcharge of 10% of the invoice, fine, and possible suspension of the RUC within the DGI registry.

DGI audits zero-rating claims with a documentary chain-of-evidence requirement; missing B/L or payment credit triggers automatic recharacterization of the invoice as a domestic taxable sale, with retrospective assessment of 7% ITBMS plus interest. Clawback window: the DGI may review and claw back input credits and zero-rating benefits within a 5-year limitation period (Art. 790 Código Fiscal), extended to 8 years where no return was filed or where the taxpayer intentionally concealed documents (Art. 790 As amended by Ley 8 de 2010). Penalties: (i) surcharge of 10% per annum on the unpaid ITBMS (Art. 741); (ii) fines of B/.50 to B/.10,000 plus a 20% increase of the omitted tax when forms were incorrectly filed; (iii) for simulated export operations / fraud, the 'delito fiscal' offense under Art. 792-H carries imprisonment of 3 to 5 years, increased to 5 to 10 years where the evaded amount exceeds B/.100,000; (iv) civil liability of the S.A. directors and the resident agent for knowingly issuing false export documents, under the Código Fiscal and Law 23 AML-restrictions; (v) denial of the export refund and mandatory registration in the DGI defaulters list, blocking the RUC and customs clearance. In practice, the DGI also cross-checks freight-forwarder manifests, Amazon/eBay/Shopify payment reports, and credit-card acquirer records from Klarna/Shopify Payments to flag mismatches between declared export destinations and physical delivery addresses.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. The Panamanian balboa is a token currency pegged 1:1 to the U.S. dollar, and the U.S. dollar is legal tender. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible without prior Central Bank authorization or reporting. Profit remittance is unrestricted; dividends derived from Panama-source income are subject to a 10% withholding tax after effect? place. There are no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on holding foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory obligation to repatriate, deposit locally, convert, or surrender export proceeds. Exporters may retain and use proceeds in USD or any convertible currency, whether onshore or offshore.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank. Banking supervision rests with the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá; Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent and clearing agent; due to full dollarization, monetary policy is effectively set by the U.S. Federal Reserve.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Moderate. Panama allows payment-service providers and e-money institutions to operate only under a license from the Superintendencia de Bancos or as regulated fintech companies under Law 147 of 2021 and related rules. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut) are not locally chartered and cannot issue Panamanian local clearing accounts or local IBANs (Panama uses no IBAN standard). They are viable only as auxiliary payment aggregators or inbound/outbound remittance rails, not as primary treasury clearing institutions for a Panamanian S.A.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 250,000 in aggregate related-party transactions per fiscal year triggers the mandatory Form 1523 filing; Local File and Master File documentation must be prepared when annual consolidated gross revenue exceeds B/. 3,000,000 or affected transactions exceed B/. 1,000,000, and must be submitted within 45 days of a DGI request.

Economic substance scope: Panama's ESR (Law 254 of 2019), in force since FY 2019, subject S.A.s to substance requirements where they perform relevant activities, inclusive of distribution and service centers and IP-bearing operations. The fast-fashion e-commerce S.A. must demonstrate local management and execution of core income-generating activities—inventory procurement, logistics chain governance, pricing, and treasury decisions—through a physical office, resident officers and proportionate local spend; failure exposes the entity to ESR fines, loss of tax-residence certification and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud under Law 70 of 2019: 3 to 5 years' imprisonment for simple evasion and 5 to 10 years for aggravated evasion using sham structures and false records, together with a fine equal to 100% of the evaded tax, closure of the commercial establishment and revocation of the business license.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who directed the concealment are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, interest and fine; criminal courts may impose travel bans, asset freezing, and disqualification from corporate management during the proceedings.
Shell company and inadequate economic substance

Statutory sanction: ESR administrative fines, forced deregistration of the corporate license, DGI denial of tax-residence certificates, and recharacterization of the income as taxable at 25% with the further 10% dividend complement on deemed distributions.

Director personal liability: Resident agents and directors who certify false substance filings are personally fined and may be prosecuted for falsifying corporate books; intentional evasion through a shell entity aggravates criminal exposure up to the maximum fraud sanction.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial ownership breach

Statutory sanction: AML administrative fines under Law 23 of 2015 scaled by subject category (up to B/. 1,000,000 for regulated entities); money-laundering prosecution carries imprisonment of 5 to 12 years, extendable in aggravated cases, plus confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false beneficial-ownership declarations face personal criminal prosecution, travel bans, seizure of personal and corporate assets, and disqualification from serving on Panamanian boards.
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Daily penalty of B/. 100 for non-filing plus upward adjustment of the taxable base, 1% monthly interest and a 10% surcharge on the understated CIT.

Director personal liability: Management approving non-arm's-length intercompany prices is jointly assessed for the underpaid tax and may be barred from filing tax declarations for any Panamanian entity for a period fixed by the DGI.
