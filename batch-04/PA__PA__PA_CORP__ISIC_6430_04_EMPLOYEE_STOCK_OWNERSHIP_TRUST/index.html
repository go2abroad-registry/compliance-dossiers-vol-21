Governing statute

Ley No. 32 de 26 de febrero de 1927 (Panama General Corporation Law for Sociedades Anónimas), as amended, including provisions on the Pacto Social and Estatutos, capital subscription, registered agent, board of directors and statutory officers; read together with the Public Registry Regulations (Reglamento del Registro Público) and, where ESOP fiduciary structures are used, Ley No. 1 de 1984 (Panama Trust Law).

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; a Panamanian S.A. may be formed with nominal authorized capital (commonly USD 10,000) and a token paid-in amount. Subscribed and paid capital fractions are declared in the Pacto Social; capital may be contributed in cash or in kind, and no escrow or pre-incorporation trust account is mandated by the Public Registry.

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must appoint a local resident legal representative and file notarized/apostilled constitutional documents with the Public Registry; the representative then registers the entity with the DGI through the e-Tax 2.0 portal to obtain the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC), which integrates income tax, ITBMS, and other tax obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign entity must appoint a local resident legal representative and file notarized/apostilled constitutional documents with the Public Registry; the representative then registers the entity with the DGI through the e-Tax 2.0 portal to obtain the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC), which integrates income tax, ITBMS, and other tax obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box regime exists; Panama S.A. is taxed at entity level on Panama-source income only. Elections available include the simplified small-taxpayer regime for gross annual income below the statutory threshold and the optional advance ITBMS payment methodology; no hybrid mismatch or consolidated filing elections are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold; all persons performing taxable transfers of goods or services in Panama must register. A simplified small-taxpayer regime applies to annual gross income up to USD 36,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to tangible goods physically exported from Panama and to services performed in Panama for non-resident use abroad, provided the recipient is located outside Panama and title/benefit of the service lies outside Panama. The supplier must retain proof of export/foreign consumption and the transaction must be documented through the ITBMS export mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación)
  • Bill of Lading (Conocimiento de Embarque)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Remesa de Fondos)

Mandatory electronic invoicing via DGI's e-Tax 2.0 platform; invoices must be digitally authorized with a DGI-issued sequential number and QR/secure hash stamp. Registered taxpayers are required to transmit invoice data electronically before or at point of sale for large-taxpayer and authorised software users, with periodic batch clearance available for certain small taxpayers.

Fraudulent export zero-rating triggers immediate ITBMS reassessment with surcharges and interest, monetary fines of up to 100% of the evaded tax under the Fiscal Code, and potential criminal prosecution for tax fraud. The DGI may also claw back export refunds and revoke the exporter's registration for a period up to two years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Panamanian banking system is fully dollarized and operationally free from exchange controls. Current and capital account transactions are freely convertible, and no central-bank permit or exchange-control approval is required for profit repatriation, capital injections, or dividend remittances. Remittance of Panamanian-source net profits may still attract the applicable dividend tax, but this is a tax obligation, not a convertibility or transfer restriction. Non-resident owned S.A. vehicles may therefore receive and disburse USD without material FX hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for export proceeds or any foreign-currency receipts. Exporters may retain, disburse, or convert export earnings freely through SBP-licensed banks. Only standard anti-money-laundering thresholds, currency transaction reports, and record-keeping obligations apply.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) – Panama has no formal central bank; the U.S. dollar is legal tender and the Panamanian balboa circulates only as fractional currency at a 1:1 peg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to Medium. Panama has no digital EMI licensing regime, and offshore EMI accounts (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, etc.) are generally not recognized as Panamanian bank accounts. They can provide USD settlement via U.S. correspondent rails and SWIFT MT103, but they cannot execute Panamanian local payroll, Société Anonyme corporate banking, or capital-escrow documentation. Such services are viable only as auxiliary flow-through accounts; primary treasury must sit with an SBP-licensed bank to preserve statutory and AML integrity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound USD capital contributions for share subscriptions or employee share purchases should be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the SBP-licensed beneficiary bank's correspondent account in the United States, using the bank's Fedwire/CHIPS ABA and final account number. Field 70/72 must contain a precise remittance narrative, e.g., 'ESOP capital contribution for employee share purchase', and where required by the receiving bank, a sworn declaration of origin of funds (Declaración de Origen de Fondos) is obtained for amounts above standard thresholds. Panama does not require a central-bank purpose code, but the receiving bank will complete inward-processing verification by screening counterparties, matching the legal entity name, confirming the RUC/unique tax ID, and filing a currency transaction report if applicable.For outbound trade settlements, use direct USD-denominated SWIFT MT103 wires for clean payments and commercial letters of credit governed by UCP 600 for documentary, escrowed, or high-value ESOP share purchases. For local payroll distributions to employees, the domestic clearing house (Cámara de Compensación) and the SBP-licensed bank's electronic ACH/EDI modules are the most efficient rails. Outbound disbursements should be supported by invoices, share-sale agreements, or trust instructions, and the bank will run OFAC/sanctions screening before releasing funds; any unusual pattern will result in an enhanced due-diligence request.FX spot contracts (USD against EUR, GBP, CHF, and other major currencies) executed through SBP-licensed banks or their international bank counterparts.Deliverable FX forwards with collateralization or margin support, permitted between the Panamanian S.A. and a licensed Panamanian bank or an international dealer.Cross-currency swaps (USD against foreign currencies) executed under ISDA/CSA documentation, provided the Panamanian entity has proper authorization and tax identification.European-style FX options negotiated with SBP-licensed banks or international investment-grade counterparties, used solely to hedge ESOP-denominated liabilities.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible as an offshore fallback through qualified international counterparties because Panama maintains no FX controls; local NDF fixing is unavailable due to full dollarization.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 1,500,000 in related-party transactions annually, or USD 3,000,000 in total Panama-source gross income, triggers mandatory local transfer pricing documentation. Local File and Master File requirements follow Executive Decree 9 of 2010; Country-by-Country reporting applies when the group consolidated revenue exceeds USD 850,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama's ESR under Law 254 of 2019 applies to all legal entities and requires an annual notification to the DGI. The statute imposes substantive presence, income generation, and management tests for entities engaged in listed relevant activities, including bank, lease, financing, service center, and intellectual property dealings. ESOP trust administration as a pure fiduciary arrangement is generally outside the statutory listing, but the S.A. must still demonstrate a physical registered office, adequate local personnel, and board control; where the trust undertakes treasury, lending, or IP holding, full ESR compliance is mandatory.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of 100% to 300% of the evaded tax plus default interest at 1% monthly; criminal sanctions under Panama's Fiscal Code and Criminal Code may lead to imprisonment for 4 to 8 years and permanent disqualification from acting as a director or officer.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the S.A. is used as a sham; directors and signatories face joint and several civil restitution of the evaded taxes, travel bans, bond orders, and imprisonment without bail where the tax fraud is aggravated by money laundering.
Shell Company / Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to USD 500,000 under the ESR and Law 23 of 2015 enforcement; deregistration of the entity and publication of the ultimate beneficial owners in the Panamanian UBO registry; DGI may reverse the corporate disclaimer and assess tax on undeclared benefits on a deemed basis.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be displaced; nominee directors who act on instructions without maintaining records are jointly liable for penalties and face criminal exposure including travel restrictions, civil restitution, and imprisonment of up to 5 years for filing false declarations.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015, fines of up to USD 5,000,000 per violation and imprisonment of 5 to 10 years; the Superintendencia de Bancos may freeze bank accounts, revoke the financial license, and order the forfeiture of assets linked to unlawful conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for willful or grossly negligent non-compliance with AML checks; penalties extend to civil seizure of personal assets, travel bans, prohibition of holding fiduciary roles, and criminal sentences of up to 10 years.
Transfer Pricing / Related-Party Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The DGI is empowered to adjust the Panama-source profit, apply the 25% corporate tax on the adjusted amount, impose monthly interest of 1%, and levy fines of 5% to 50% of the tax deficiency; cross-border information exchange under the OECD framework may trigger audit in the treaty partner state.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structure the controlled pricing to evade Panama tax are jointly and severally liable for the deficiency and may be subjected to civil restitution, international travel bans, and imprisonment for tax fraud upon a showing of criminal intent.
