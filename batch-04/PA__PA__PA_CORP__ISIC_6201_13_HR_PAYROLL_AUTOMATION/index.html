Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) - General Directorate of Revenues, Ministry of Economy and Finance (Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas, MEF). Online portal: SIT - Sistema de Información Tributaria (https://sit.dgi.gob.pa), with e-filing, registry and electronic-invoice validation services; ancillary site: https://www.dgi.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Panama S.A. must obtain its RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) through the DGI within 30 days after inscription of the incorporation deed in the Registro Público of Panama. Procedure: (i) file the Aviso de Operación (Notice of Operations) with DGI Form 1410; (ii) upload the public deed, the Registro Público certificate, board resolution appointing a resident legal representative, and the IMF (Información de Accionistas/beneficiario final under Law 23/2015); (iii) obtain the 11-digit RUC for 'Persona Jurídica con fines de lucro'; (iv) activate the taxpayer profile as general-regime (no simplified small-taxpayer cap at B/. 123,000 annual revenue); (v) request habilitación under DGI Resolution 201-1521/2022 for mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica en Línea - FEL) and register the fiscal accounting books (SIC - Sistema de Información Contable); (vi) if SaaS exports to non-residents are intended, file the DGI 'Registro de Exportador de Servicios' application under Decreto Ejecutivo 84 de 2005 to support 0% ITBMS treatment and territorial-source deferral until repatriation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope : The Panama S.A. (Sociedad Anónima) is a resident legal entity subject to entity-level income tax under the territorial system; only Panama-source income is taxed, including services exported from Panama once the consideration is deemed received/repatriated (overall no CFC or worldwide taxation rules). The S.A. cannot be classified as a pass-through; dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are subject to a 5%/10% dividend tax (with a 4%/7.5% advance credit system depending on the source income taxed under the normal rate).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Available statutory elections: (i) alternatively, the taxpayer may elect to compute income tax under the 'normal profit-based' method versus the presumptive minimum gross-income method (Art. 699-I Código Fiscal), with the larger amount payable; (ii) election to use the simplified fixed-rate cash method for small taxpayers is unavailable to PA_CORP given the B/. 123,000 revenue cap; (iii) election to be treated as an 'empresa exportadora de servicios' under Decree 84/2005 - automatically renewing for each fiscal year - which preserves 0% ITBMS and taxation of export income upon repatriation (explicit board resolution and DGI registry); (iv) hybrid mismatch elections: not available; Panama has not adopted OECD hybrid-mismatch or check-the-box rules, and treaty benefits under Panama's limited income-tax treaty network require actual residency and beneficial-ownership substantiation rather than an entity classification election.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and 'exportación de servicios' are taxed at 0% ITBMS under Art. 1054 Código Fiscal and Art. 53 of Law 25/1995. For ISIC 6201 (Automated Global Payroll and Staff Compliance Software as a Service), the S.A. must prove: (i) the SaaS subscription contract is executed with a non-resident recipient; (ii) the service is technically rendered from Panama but effectively used/benefited abroad; (iii) consideration is invoiced in a freely convertible foreign currency accepted by the DGI (USD/Balboa is acceptable); (iv) payment is remitted to a Panamanian bank account from abroad; and (v) the S.A. is registered as an export service company with the DGI. Upon compliance, input ITBMS on payroll software development, cloud costs and local overhead becomes creditable against 0% output, generating an ITBMS credit/refund position and an income-tax deferral of territorial-source service revenue until funds actually enter Panama.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized, freely convertible monetary system. There are no exchange-control laws, no prior import or export license requirements for foreign exchange transactions, and no restrictions on the movement of capital for current-account or capital-account purposes. Profit remittance through dividends, capital reductions, or intercompany loan repayments is lawful and does not require central bank approval; the only statutory condition is the prior settlement of any applicable Panamanian taxes, including dividend taxes or branch remittance taxes, and compliance with the company's corporate records and beneficial ownership disclosure obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama does not impose mandatory export proceeds surrender, repatriation deadlines, or minimum conversion ratios. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign currency or U.S. dollars in any Panamanian or foreign bank account. The only obligation is to record the transactions accurately for tax and anti-money-laundering purposes in compliance with SBP regulations.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no conventional central bank. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the primary banking regulator and Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the government's fiscal agent and clearing channel for public-sector transactions. Monetary policy is effectively determined by the U.S. Federal Reserve because Panama is fully dollarized with the U.S. dollar as legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low feasibility as the primary settlement rail. Offshore fintech electronic money institutions are not licensed or supervised by the SBP and therefore cannot provide the same legal certainty, statutory protection, or local clearing access as a Panamanian licensed bank. Such EMIs may be used as ancillary receiving channels for inbound client payments or for disbursements to sub-contractors in certain jurisdictions, but the core payroll escrow and treasury function should sit with a locally licensed commercial bank to permit ACH clearing, direct debit, SWIFT MT103 outbound wires, and SBP-regulated dispute resolution. Any foreign EMI must demonstrate a correspondent banking relationship with a Panamanian bank and comply with SBP KYC expectations for virtual account providers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be made by SWIFT international wire or correspondent-bank transfer from the investing company's or beneficial owner's regulated bank account into the S.A.'s stated USD account in Panama. The wire instruction must identify the S.A. as beneficiary by exact legal name and bank account number, include the bank SWIFT/BIC, and in the payment narrative clearly state 'Capital Contribution to Sociedad Anónima' or 'Share Capital Subscription'. No Central Bank approval or exchange-control permit is required. For enhanced compliance, the company should provide the receiving bank with a certified shareholders' meeting resolution approving the capital increase, an updated share registry, and an undertaking to file the relevant corporate amendment with the Registro Público if the contribution is formalized through a share issue. The remitter should avoid combining payroll operating funds with one-off capital subscriptions in the same wire; separate references should be used to allow clean audit trail segregation.Payroll settlements and trade disbursements should use SWIFT MT103 for cross-border bank wires where the beneficiary holds an account directly with a SWIFT member bank. For local dollar-denominated payroll, the most efficient rail is Panama's Automated Clearing House (ACH), accessed through the local commercial bank, together with direct debit and direct-credit files generated by the payroll SaaS. For larger, documented trade disbursements, a commercial letter of credit (documentary L/C) is available through Panamanian banks, particularly for software licensing, hardware procurement, or regional subcontracting arrangements. Where the client vendor is not SWIFT-connected, disbursement can be routed to a regulated EMI or local partner bank through an aggregated MT103 or other permitted network, but each destination must undergo sanctions and AML screening.Deliverable USD/EUR and USD/GBP forwards for payroll obligations in European currenciesUSD/MXN and USD/COP deliverable swap or forward contracts through international bank counterpartiesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for Latin American restricted currencies, including Argentine peso, Venezuelan bolívar, and similar controlled currenciesCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for long-term intercompany payroll funding arrangementsPlain-vanilla foreign exchange options for hedging projected multi-year payroll disbursement exposure in volatile emerging-market currencies

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-Low feasibility as the primary settlement rail. Offshore fintech electronic money institutions are not licensed or supervised by the SBP and therefore cannot provide the same legal certainty, statutory protection, or local clearing access as a Panamanian licensed bank. Such EMIs may be used as ancillary receiving channels for inbound client payments or for disbursements to sub-contractors in certain jurisdictions, but the core payroll escrow and treasury function should sit with a locally licensed commercial bank to permit ACH clearing, direct debit, SWIFT MT103 outbound wires, and SBP-regulated dispute resolution. Any foreign EMI must demonstrate a correspondent banking relationship with a Panamanian bank and comply with SBP KYC expectations for virtual account providers.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 250,000 for aggregate related-party transactions or USD 2,000,000 for annual gross revenue; the taxpayer must prepare contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation and file the informative transfer pricing return with DGI when either threshold is exceeded.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Decree 123 of 2019) apply to entities conducting relevant activities. A payroll SaaS company holding Panamanian IP, financing, or a holding structure must evidence physical office, Panamanian employees, decision-making meetings, and regular management presence. DGI approves a simplified annual substance notification, and failure to show economic substance in Panama for relevant income renders the entity liable to fines, corrective reclassification of foreign income, and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fiscal Code Articles 731-735 impose fines from 25% to 10 times the evaded tax for serious misdeclaration, plus statutory interest; criminal tax evasion carries imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and a monetary penalty based on the tax amount at risk.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and beneficial owners who sign false declarations or instruct the under-declaration are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, surcharges, and criminal fines; the DGI may obtain travel bans and preventive asset freezes before final criminal conviction.
Shell Company / Piercing Corporate Veil

Statutory sanction: The Panamanian Economic Substance Rules and the Fiscal Code allow DGI to demand the company's substance, impose fines of up to USD 100,000 for failure to file the economic substance declaration, and disregard juridical personality where the entity is a sham. Courts may levy civil restitution equal to the avoided tax plus 30% of unpaid liabilities.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and statutory directors risk veil-piercing remedies in Panamanian civil courts where inadequate capitalization, commingling of funds, and the absence of corporate formalities have caused damages or tax losses; liability extends to fraudulent transfer of assets and director disqualification in the Public Registry.
AML / International Wire and Payroll Funds Abuse

Statutory sanction: Legislative Decree 2 of 2019 and Law 21 of 2013 set administrative fines of up to USD 1 million for material AML deficiencies; criminal money laundering, including using digital payment rails and shell invoicing, triggers imprisonment of 5 to 12 years and confiscation of laundered assets under the Panamanian Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers with direct involvement in onboarding payroll clients used for layering are individually liable; the UAF (Financial Analysis Unit) and the Public Ministry may order account seizure, travel restrictions, and a prohibition from serving as directors of regulated Panamanian companies.
