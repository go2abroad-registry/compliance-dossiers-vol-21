Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima. The articles of incorporation must state a nominal capital sum, and shares may be subscribed at formation without a mandatory escrow deposit. If the NVOCC licence demands evidence of financial capacity, paid-in capital may be demonstrated through bank statements, capital contributions in kind, or a surety bond; no government escrow account is required by company law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety inspection and certificate from the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá; municipal use-of-land and occupancy permit issued by the local municipality; occupational workplace registration and safety clearance under the Ministry of Labor and Labor Development (MITRADEL) where employees are physically present.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); online portal: 'Sede Electrónica DGI' (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa / https://etax.dgi.gob.pa); ancillary registrations with Registro Público (Public Registry) for corporate existence and MICI (Ministerio de Comercio e Industrias) for the Aviso de Operación commercial license

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must (1) appoint and maintain a Panamanian resident agent; (2) incorporate the Sociedad Anónima by notarized public deed and inscribe it in the Registro Público; (3) register with DGI to obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), which serves as the unified tax ID for income tax, ITBMS, and customs operations; (4) obtain the Aviso de Operación from MICI; and (5) for non-domiciled foreign entities without a branch that nonetheless earn Panama-source income, apply directly to DGI for a standalone NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) / RUC-NIT extranjero. Registration must be completed no later than 30 days after commencement of taxable activity; the RUC is a prerequisite for lodging electronic export declarations, issuing electronic invoices, and claiming ITBMS export refunds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Panama; the available statutory mechanisms are: (a) election to enter the SIMPLE simplified small-taxpayer regime where annual gross receipts do not exceed B/.300,000; (b) election to file consolidated income tax returns for affiliated corporations subject to DGI authorization; (c) advance pricing agreement (APA) election to fix transfer-pricing methodology with DGI; (d) election to classify cross-border NVOCC freight services as zero-rated 'export services' where the benefit is consumed outside Panama; and (e) election to apply for monthly/periodic ITBMS export credit refunds (devoluciones) through the exporter's special clearance procedure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis economic turnover threshold for regular ITBMS registration: any person engaged in taxable transfers of movable goods or rendering of services in Panama must register with DGI from the first taxable act (B/.0 of taxable sales). However, the SIMPLE small-taxpayer regime (Law 18 of 2021) applies where annual gross income does not exceed B/.300,000, permitting enrolment with reduced quarterly compliance instead of full monthly ITBMS filing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% ITBMS) applies to: (1) tangible goods physically exported from Panama, evidenced by a customs export declaration; (2) services rendered in Panama to a non-resident where the benefit, use, or exploitation occurs entirely outside Panama; (3) international maritime and air cargo transportation services, including NVOCC-consolidated ocean freight brokerage, where international waters are traversed and the service is supplied for the transport of goods to a foreign destination; and (4) supplies delivered into drawback regimes, Colon Free Zone operations, or Pacific Special Economic Zone operations (distinct regimes requiring specific licensing documentation). Export-neutrality allows the NVOCC to credit ITBMS paid on taxable inputs against output tax and to claim a refund (devolución) where creditable input tax exceeds output ITBMS.

  • Declaración Única de Exportación (DUA) / Panama Customs Export Declaration bearing DGI and customs validation codes
  • Original Bill of Lading (house or master) evidencing international carriage of the consolidated containerized cargo
  • Final commercial invoice naming the foreign consignee with FOB or CIF terms and ocean freight charges
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) from a Panamanian licensed bank evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in Panama
  • Bank credit advice or SWIFT MT103 referencing the shipment and corresponding to the invoiced export value

Panama mandates electronic invoicing ('Facturación Electrónica') through DGI-authorised systems under Resolución 202-11772 and subsequent implementing regulations. From 1 January 2024, all taxpayers, including NVOCCs, must issue facturas electrónicas via either (i) the DGI Sede Electrónica web portal or (ii) an ERP/software integrated with the DGI 'Servicios Web API' for real-time fiscal validation and clearance ('validación previa'). Each validated invoice receives a DGI control number, digital signature, and QR fiscal stamp; legacy fiscal printers remain acceptable only for enumerated residual retail categories and offline contingency cases (maximum 48 hours before forced online transmission).

DGI maintains a dedicated export-control audit unit that retrospectively verifies the complete export file for every refund claim. If zero-rating is improperly applied, the transaction is re-characterized as taxable at 7%, resulting in clawback of the unpaid ITBMS plus a mandatory 50% surcharge (recargo), monthly delay interest indexed by the Banco Nacional de Panamá rate, and fines of B/.250 to B/.5,000 under the Código Fiscal. Wilful falsification of bills of lading, remittance certificates, customs declarations, or forged consignee data invokes Panama's criminal tax regime (Law 9 of 2021): defraudación tributaria carries imprisonment of 4 to 8 years plus a monetary penalty of 100% to 300% of the evaded amount, with joint and several civil liability of the entity, its legal representatives, and the resident agent. DGI may additionally freeze the RUC, nullify the Aviso de Operación, and suspend the exporter's electronic-invoicing clearance privileges pending final judicial resolution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility on both current and capital account. Because the USD is the circulating currency, there are no exchange-control laws, no central-bank licensing of FX transactions, no restrictions on profit or dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders, and no limits on inbound or outbound capital flows. The practical constraints arise exclusively at the bank level: SBP AML Rule 002-2013 and Law 23 of 2015 require each financial institution to apply risk-based due diligence to cross-border wires, beneficial-ownership disclosure and source-of-funds documentation. Profit remittances by an NVOCC S.A. are not subject to withholding tax under Panama's territorial income-tax regime, but the distributing entity must evidence payment of all attributable taxes. There are no surrender, surrender-ratio or conversion-timeline requirements for freight receivables denominated in foreign currencies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory obligation to repatriate, surrender, convert or hold export proceeds for a minimum period. Consequently, ocean-freight and NVOCC receivables may be received in USD, EUR, GBP or CNY and settled freely. Nevertheless, SBP Rule 002-2013 (Arts. 42-46) mandates retention of underlying commercial and transport documentation (house bills of lading, master bills of lading, commercial invoices, cargo manifests) for at least five years to substantiate the lawful-origin analysis conducted by the collecting bank; the Panama Maritime Authority (AMP) additionally requires freight-forwarding licensees to maintain voyage-level records for audit.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no statutory central bank and no independent monetary authority issuing domestic fiat currency. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) exercises full prudential banking, AML/CFT and payment-system supervision; Banco Nacional de Panamá, a state-owned bank, acts as fiscal agent and custodian of public funds. The legal-tender regime derives from the 1904 Monetary Agreement and Political Constitution of Panama (Arts. 169-170), under which the United States dollar (USD) circulates freely and the Panamanian balboa (PAB) exists solely as divisional coinage at a rigid 1:1 parity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-Medium. Panama lacks a functional non-bank electronic-money institution (EMI) licensing framework; offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Mercury) cannot hold Panamanian corporate USD accounts nor clear through the local ACH without an SBP-licensed bank sponsor. Law 23 of 2015 and SBP Rule 002-2013 prohibit anonymous or intermediary-permissive account structures, and no sandbox regime has yet authorized cross-border EMI pass-through for a Panama S.A. A viable hybrid architecture is a tier-1 Panama USD operating account coupled with a US-domiciled fintech (Mercury/Relay) for receivable concentration, provided the Panama entity remains the documented contracting counterparty and all treasury records reconcile to the AMP NVOCC license. The legal risk of routing NVOCC freight collections exclusively through an unlicensed offshore EMI is that the Panama bank will reject outbound wires to a non-vetted payee class and flag the operation as a money-service-burden red flag.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Low-Medium. Panama lacks a functional non-bank electronic-money institution (EMI) licensing framework; offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Mercury) cannot hold Panamanian corporate USD accounts nor clear through the local ACH without an SBP-licensed bank sponsor. Law 23 of 2015 and SBP Rule 002-2013 prohibit anonymous or intermediary-permissive account structures, and no sandbox regime has yet authorized cross-border EMI pass-through for a Panama S.A. A viable hybrid architecture is a tier-1 Panama USD operating account coupled with a US-domiciled fintech (Mercury/Relay) for receivable concentration, provided the Panama entity remains the documented contracting counterparty and all treasury records reconcile to the AMP NVOCC license. The legal risk of routing NVOCC freight collections exclusively through an unlicensed offshore EMI is that the Panama bank will reject outbound wires to a non-vetted payee class and flag the operation as a money-service-burden red flag.
