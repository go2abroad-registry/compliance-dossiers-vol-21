Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies. The company may be formed with authorized capital divided into registered or bearer shares, with or without par value; no escrow or bank deposit is required. A standard authorized capital of USD 10,000 may be declared with fully subscribed but unpaid shares.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate (Visto Bueno de Bomberos) from the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá; structural load inspection and certificate of occupancy issued by the Dirección de Ingeniería Municipal based on a licensed engineer's certification; occupational health and safety clearance from the Ministerio de Trabajo y Desarrollo Laboral (MITRADEL) for cargo-handling equipment, forklifts, and warehouse platforms.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) – Ministry of Economy and Finance; taxpayer portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI (https://www.dgi.gob.pa) and SIE – Sistema de Facturación Electrónica para emisión electrónica.

E-government filing portal

A foreign or non-resident entity must first incorporate the Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) in the Panama Public Registry with a notarized articles of incorporation, registered agent, and resident legal representative. Thereafter, the S.A. must apply to the DGI for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) using the official registration form, attaching the public registry certificate, notarial corporate documents, identification of directors/officers, proof of registered address in Panama, and, where applicable, the operating license or prior approval from the relevant sector regulator. Upon approval, the S.A. receives a permanent RUC number and is automatically registered for ITBMS, income tax advance payments, and payroll withholding where personnel exist. The DGI also requires activation of electronic invoicing in the SIE platform and, for larger taxpayers, interoperability through the DGI e-invoicing API or web service.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide U.S.-style check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch elections. The territoriality principle is statutory and mandatory: an S.A. is taxable on income derived from Panamanian-source activities, while foreign-source income not attributable to a Panama permanent establishment is generally outside the Panamanian income tax base. Separate elective regimes include: (i) Multinational Company Headquarters Regime (SEM) under Law 41 of 2007; (ii) Panama Pacifico Special Economic Zone regime; and (iii) special licensing regimes for international logistics operators. Each election requires prior DGI/MICI approval and strict maintenance of substance and records.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de minimis turnover threshold triggers ITBMS registration; every person or entity performing taxable transfers of movable goods or services within Panama must register and obtain an RUC before commencing activities. A special simplified small-taxpayer regime exists, but it is not available to a standard S.A. engaged in intermodal freight forwarding and does not apply where the entity is a legal person formed as a Sociedad Anónima.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Law 8 of 2010 and the Panamanian Fiscal Code, ITBMS applies at 0% to the export of goods and to services deemed 'export services' where the recipient is located outside Panama and the economic benefit or use occurs outside Panama. For an intermodal rail freight forwarding S.A., zero-rating is available when: (i) the cargo is carried from an origin outside Panama to a destination outside Panama, or exits Panamanian territory under a customs export declaration; (ii) the freight forwarding service is performed for a foreign client or for a local exporter in connection with an actual export; (iii) the invoice states '0% - export service' and the S.A. is ITBMS-registered; and (iv) the entity maintains documentary evidence of the international cross-border nature of the carriage and receipt of payment from outside Panama. Transactions that merely take place in Panama but benefit a Panamanian recipient are subject to 7% ITBMS.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) filed through the Panamanian Single Window for Foreign Trade (Sede Electrónica / SIEC) for goods physically exported from Panama
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent intermodal transport document evidencing international route from foreign origin to foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice issued through the SIE electronic invoicing system with zero-rated line item
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/transfer confirmation showing payment of export proceeds from the foreign customer or foreign bank
  • Corresponding import/transit documentation for goods passing through Panamanian territory in international transit
  • Freight forwarding or logistics service contract, including Incoterm and destination evidence
  • Cargo manifest or railway/intermodal waybill, where applicable
  • Foreign currency exchange or bank credit advice demonstrating actual repatriation of the invoice amount
  • Proof that the recipient is a non-resident entity or a resident exporter in an actual international cargo movement

All DGI-registered taxpayers are required to issue electronic invoices through the SIE (Sistema de Facturación Electrónica). The SIE performs near-real-time validation, assigns a unique electronic authorization number and fiscal QR code, and requires digital stamping on every invoice. Large taxpayers and certain ranking/risk categories must use the official DGI API/web-service integration for transactional clearance. Invoices must be transmitted within the statutory period before delivery of goods or completion of services; corrections require electronic credit/debit notes pre-authorized through the same SIE platform. Failure to clear invoices through SIE invalidates the document for ITBMS credit purposes and supports tax penalties.

The DGI may deny zero-rating retroactively if the exporter fails to prove the goods left Panamanian territory or the service was actually consumed abroad within the evidentiary period of five years from the taxable event. The legal consequence is the reclassification of the omitted ITBMS at 7% plus monthly interest computed at the statutory rate, and an automatic surcharge of 10% where amounts are unreasonably overstated. False or fraudulent export declarations used to support zero-rating attract the fiscal fraud penalty under Articles 688 and 689 of the Fiscal Code, which is generally one to five times the omitted tax for fiscal evasion, with criminal prosecution available where the understatement exceeds specified thresholds. Repeat violations can lead to suspension of the electronic invoicing license, disqualification from DGI export refund procedures, and up to 10 years’ imprisonment for aggravated tax fraud. Under Panama’s territorial taxation model, the outward remittance of the foreign-exchange proceeds is not required by exchange-control law, but the absence of payment documentation within 180 days will automatically be treated as strong prima facie evidence that the transaction was not a genuine export for ITBMS zero-rating and VAT-refund purposes.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully dollarized economy; the Balboa (PAB) is pegged at 1:1 with the U.S. dollar, which circulates as legal tender. Both capital and current account transactions are fully convertible with zero exchange-control authorization hurdles; profit and dividend remittances are unrestricted and subject only to normal withholding taxes and AML reporting. No foreign-exchange license or central-bank approval is required for any cross-border payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panamanian law imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation window for export receipts. Proceeds from rail-freight forwarding services are freely retainable in USD; exporters are required only to file electronic customs and statistical declarations via the Single Window for Foreign Trade (VUMPE/SISEX) for documentation and duty assessment.

Central bank supervision: None. Panama has no central bank; banking regulation and supervision are vested in the Superintendency of Banks of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá), with monetary, fiscal, and statistical functions distributed among the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF), the General Directorate of Revenue (DGI), and the National Institute of Statistics and Census (INEC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High subject to legal limits. Offshore digital EMIs (UK, EU, and BVI-type payment institutions) are not licensed by the Superintendency of Banks and cannot access Panamanian domestic ACH clearing; they may provide USD multi-currency IBANs solely via correspondent banks. However, onboarding of Panama-domiciled S.A.s remains discretionary due to residual FATF grey-list perceptions (Panama was removed from the FATF grey list in October 2023). Recommended use: supplementary treasury rails, not a substitute for an SBP-regulated bank account.
