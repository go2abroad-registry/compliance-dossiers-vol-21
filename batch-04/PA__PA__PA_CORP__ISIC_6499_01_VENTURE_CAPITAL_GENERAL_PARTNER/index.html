Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; fiscal portal: 'T@PA' (Tributación en Panamá).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first formally incorporate a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) through the Public Registry. Upon incorporation, the resident agent files the company's Constitución and Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) application with the DGI via the T@PA portal or written form. The DGI posts an electronic credential allowing the entity to obtain its RUC, enable e-invoicing, and be recognized for ITBMS and corporate income tax filings. A certified copy of the Articles of Incorporation, proof of registered agent, and resolutions appointing the legal representative and fiscal domiciliary are mandatory.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists under Panamanian law; entity classification follows the legal form under the Código de Comercio. Tax classification for an S.A. is entity-level corporate income tax at 25% on Panamanian-source income. No hybrid mismatch elections are available. A qualified entity may elect the 'Multinational Company Headquarters' (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007 and Law 51 of 2011 for preferential territorial sourcing and employment-VAT concessions, provided it generates certain services exclusively for foreign affiliates.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold in law; every natural person or juridical entity habitually transferring taxable goods or services in Panama must register for ITBMS and obtain RUC. A simplified fixed-rate ITBMS regime applies to small taxpayers whose annual gross revenue does not exceed the level periodically fixed by DGI resolution (currently B/.48,000.00 per applicable simplified-taxpayer regulations).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: export zero-rating requires material exportation from the Panamanian customs territory, supported by a customs over-declaration (DUA) showing physical dispatch abroad. For services: ITBMS zero-rating applies only to exported services when the service is fully rendered outside Panama, is wholly for the benefit of a non-resident person, and does not relate to Panamanian-situs real estate or property. Where a service is partially performed in Panama, the taxpayer must evidence that the economic benefit and consumption are exclusively attributable to the non-resident recipient. Specific contracts, invoices, and methods of payment to demonstrate provenance and foreign use are required.

  • Declaración Única de Aduanas (DUA) / Electro-DUA export declaration with customs clearance stamp
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill) issued by the common carrier evidencing physical departure
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Comprobante de Liquidación de Divisas or Bank Swift/MT103 remittance certificate) proving the export sale was received from outside Panama

Mandatory electronic invoicing on the DGI-certified 'Facturación Electrónica' platform. Taxpayers must use approved private software or the DGI T@PA module, transmit each invoice to the DGI via web services/API in near-real time, and include the DGI-furnished electronic stamp ('Sello Digital') and unique serial number. Clearance occurs prior to issuance; no face-to-face paper invoicing is permitted except under DGI-approved contingency procedures for system outages.

If DGI determines that the zero-rating was not lawfully substantiated, it may recharacterize the transaction as taxable and assess the full 7% ITBMS on the gross invoice amount, plus monthly surcharges of 3% per month subject to a maximum collection cap under the Tax Code, statutory fines of B/.5,000–B/.50,000, and disallowance of all claimed input VAT credits. Intentional misdeclaration or document falsification constitutes customs fraud under Ley 30 de 2011 and tax evasion under Arts. 272–276 of the Código Penal, carrying imprisonment of 2–5 years and disqualification from public procurement and financial licenses. Export entitlements and goods clearance privileges may also be suspended by the DGI and the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy; the balboa is a nominal unit pegged at par with the U.S. dollar, and U.S. dollar banknotes are legal tender for all transactions. Both current and capital account transactions are freely convertible, and there are no legislative exchange controls. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and repatriation of capital to non-residents are permitted without prior administrative authorisation; practical hurdles are limited to bank-level AML/KYC documentation requirements, such as board resolutions, audited financial statements, proof of underlying contracts, and evidence of the payee's legal existence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may freely retain U.S. dollars or other convertible currencies in local or foreign banks, with no obligation to surrender proceeds to a central bank and no minimum conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no issuing central bank. The banking and financial supervisory authority is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent of the State and as a state-owned commercial bank, not as a monetary authority. No separate foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control commission exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low. Panamanian law does not recognise offshore digital e-money institutions as licensed banks. An offshore EMI holding a European or regional e-money licence cannot operate a domestic payment account, clear payments through Panama's local ACH system, or hold settlement accounts without obtaining a Panamanian FinTech or banking licence from the SBP. Tier 1 Panamanian banks will often reject or freeze wires originating from unlicensed EMIs because such flows carry elevated OFAC and FATF screening risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital calls are routed through direct SWIFT MT103 wires from the limited partners' bank accounts to the GP's designated capital-escrow or segregated collection account at a Tier 1 Panamanian bank. The MT103 narrative must identify the remitting LP, the capital call number, the subscription agreement reference, and the stated purpose 'venture fund capital contribution'. No Panamanian exchange-control form is required, but the receiving bank will apply its internal AML risk model, may request the capital call notice and underlying LP resolutions, and will require prior notification on any transfer over its reporting threshold. Transfers originating from OFAC/FATF-listed jurisdictions require executive-level bank approval and a documented investment mandate.Portfolio disbursements are executed via SWIFT MT103 for cross-border investments and via local ACH for Panamanian portfolio companies. Commercial letters of credit are used only for structured trade-related transactions, not for equity seed capital. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends paid out of profits that have borne the 25% Panamanian CIT; 5% final withholding tax on dividends or profit-sharing distributions (including carried-interest allocations treated as profit distributions) paid out of Panama-source profits that were exempt from or not subject to CIT; 0% on distributions made purely from foreign-source income outside Panama's territorial tax net.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual Form 202 must be filed within three months of the close of the fiscal year-end (Código Fiscal, Article 710); a calendar-year S.A. must e-file by 31 March of the following year. The DGI may publish administrative extensions to the e-filing calendar, but late-payment interest accrues from the original statutory due date.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Line 7 – Ingresos brutos gravables (Gross taxable Panama-source income) Aggregate revenue, management fees, advisory fees, and gains arising from economic activities performed or utilized within Panama. A VC GP must include only Panama-source receipts; carried interest allocated by an offshore fund is not Panama-source and is reported off-book or as exempt income. Non-resident shareholders should ensure the resident director signs a board resolution identifying each revenue stream with a territoriality analysis; management fees invoiced by the GP to a Cayman/LP fund for services physically rendered in Panama are taxable in Panama even if paid from abroad. Late filing attracts an administrative fine of B/. 500 (increased to B/. 5,000 upon DGI formal assessment), a monthly late surcharge of 10% of the unpaid tax (capped at 50%), and 1% monthly interest under Código Fiscal Articles 730–732 and 735–736. Failure to file after a DGI notice permits a presumption-based assessment, suspension of the RUC and Paz y Salvo certificate, and criminal perjury exposure for the resident director who signs a false sworn return.
Line 10 – Devoluciones, descuentos y bonificaciones (Returns, discounts and rebates) Contractual rebates, fee waivers, and reimbursements of fund expenses returned to investors; must be supported by executed side letters or fund legal documents. Foreign investors should maintain a reconciliation between capital-call notices and expense reimbursements to avoid a DGI reclassification of rebates as untaxed distributions. Late filing attracts an administrative fine of B/. 500 (increased to B/. 5,000 upon DGI formal assessment), a monthly late surcharge of 10% of the unpaid tax (capped at 50%), and 1% monthly interest under Código Fiscal Articles 730–732 and 735–736. Failure to file after a DGI notice permits a presumption-based assessment, suspension of the RUC and Paz y Salvo certificate, and criminal perjury exposure for the resident director who signs a false sworn return.
Line 13 – Costo de ventas / costo de inversión (Cost of sales / cost of investment) Direct costs attributable to portfolio-company investments, carried deal-breaking due-diligence costs, and the historical cost of disposed stakes. Venture-capital write-downs are deductible only when realized and economically evidenced. Non-resident principals should document the acquisition cost of each portfolio interest with signed closing statements and SWIFT records; unrealized fair-value losses are not deductible under Panamanian realization rules. Late filing attracts an administrative fine of B/. 500 (increased to B/. 5,000 upon DGI formal assessment), a monthly late surcharge of 10% of the unpaid tax (capped at 50%), and 1% monthly interest under Código Fiscal Articles 730–732 and 735–736. Failure to file after a DGI notice permits a presumption-based assessment, suspension of the RUC and Paz y Salvo certificate, and criminal perjury exposure for the resident director who signs a false sworn return.
Line 18 – Gastos operativos (Operating deductions) Salaries, rents, professional fees, audit costs, regulatory licenses, and amortization that are necessary and proportional to the production of Panama-source income; related-party expenses must satisfy the arm's-length standard and documentary rules. Prepare a bottom-up reconciliation from IFRS/GAAP books to the territorial tax basis; separate offshore carry and foreign exchange items that are exempt to keep the return defensible on audit. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 2,500,000 (equivalent to USD 2,500,000) of aggregate cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory preparation and retention of a Local File and the filing of the related-party informative return (Formulario de Transacciones con Partes Relacionadas / Form 34). A Master File is required where the consolidated group turnover exceeds B/. 85,000,000 (CbCR threshold). Below-threshold GPs must still retain contemporaneous margin evidence for management fees, expense recharges, capital calls, and carry allocations in response to a DGI audit demand.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 254 of 2020, implemented by Executive Decrees 585–588 of 2021) apply to resident S.A.s carrying on 'relevant activities.' A venture-capital general partner performing investment management, administration, coordination of investments, or holding of securities is a relevant-activity taxpayer. The GP must file an annual Declaración Jurada de Sustancia Económica proving: locally owned or leased premises; qualified personnel physically present in Panama; board-level decision-making exercised in Panama; and expenditure and output proportionate to the income booked. A passive holding or pure-equity participation may qualify for the reduced 'pure holding' substance standard only if the GP performs no active management; once management fees and capital-call functions are exercised, full ESR compliance is mandatory. Non-compliance precludes issuance of a tax-residence certificate and triggers fines.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham Distribution of Profits (Código Fiscal Art. 761; Law 8 of 2010)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment at the 25% CIT rate plus a 10%-per-month late surcharge (capped at 50%), a civil fine of up to 100% of the evaded tax, and criminal prosecution for willful evasion with imprisonment of 2–5 years and a fine equal to the evaded amount.

Director personal liability: Directors acting as withholding agents are jointly and severally liable for the un-withheld 5% dividend tax; during criminal proceedings the court may impose travel bans, asset embargoes, and personal seizure of the directors' assets, including piercing the corporate veil where the S.A. is used as a mere instrument of fraud.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure (Law 254 of 2020, Executive Decrees 585–588 of 2021)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to USD 50,000 per fiscal period, denial of the Certificado de Residencia Fiscal, cancellation of the entity's tax-clearance certificate, and, for persistent default, administrative dissolution proceedings before the Registro Público.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false substance declaration face perjury liability under the Panamanian Penal Code with imprisonment of 1–4 years and are personally barred from incorporating new Panamanian entities while the non-compliant company remains in default; the corporate veil is pierced to recover taxes and fines.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (Código Fiscal TP Chapter, DGI Form 34 obligations)

Statutory sanction: The DGI may re-determine taxable income using the most appropriate arm's-length method, assess the resulting deficiency at 25%, add a 20% penalty on the underpayment, and accrue monthly interest; failure to file Form 34 adds administrative fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign transfer-pricing declarations lacking factual support are jointly liable for the penalty component and may be subject to criminal charges of false tax documentation; resignation does not extinguish liability where the director approved the offending transactions.
Anti-Money-Laundering Violations (Law 23 of 2015; Law 126 of 2020, UAF regime)

Statutory sanction: Fines from USD 10,000 to USD 1,000,000 per violation, suspension of operations, freezing of bank accounts by UAF order, cancellation of the company's registration with the Registro Público, and confiscation of assets through the 'extinción de dominio' mechanism even absent a criminal conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors and the compliance officer incur personal criminal exposure of 5–12 years' imprisonment for aggravated money-laundering; preventive travel bans and passport retention may be ordered during investigation, and corporate-veil piercing applies where the S.A. is a conduit for third-party illicit funds.
Willful Non-Filing / Chronic Delinquency (Código Fiscal Arts. 710, 730–736)

Statutory sanction: Cumulative penalties: B/. 500 to B/. 5,000 per unfiled return, 10%-per-month surcharge capped at 50% of the unpaid tax, 1% monthly interest, automatic presumption-based assessment, suspension of the RUC and Paz y Salvo, and blocking of any dividend remittance or notarial act before the Registro Público.

Director personal liability: The resident director is personally responsible for surcharges where he or she failed to instruct filing; Panamanian courts may impose a preventive exit ban, and the director is disqualified from serving on any other Panamanian corporation until the outstanding liabilities are settled, effectively piercing the veil to the company's beneficial owners.
