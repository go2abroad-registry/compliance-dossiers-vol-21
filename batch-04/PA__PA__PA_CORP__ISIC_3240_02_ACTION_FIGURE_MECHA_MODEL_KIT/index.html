Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); online via the DGI Portal 'Sistema T@-Costo' and official website https://dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first appoint a local resident agent (agente residente) with a Panama notary public, register the notarial public deed and the 'Aviso de Operación' with the Registro Público de Panamá, then apply to the DGI for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) by submitting the corporate registration certificate, the public deed, the resident agent acceptance form, tax domicile evidence, and the DGI's Form 610 registration application; the RUC is issued within 2-3 business days and is the single statutory tax and ITBMS identification number required for invoicing and customs operations.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign entity must first appoint a local resident agent (agente residente) with a Panama notary public, register the notarial public deed and the 'Aviso de Operación' with the Registro Público de Panamá, then apply to the DGI for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) by submitting the corporate registration certificate, the public deed, the resident agent acceptance form, tax domicile evidence, and the DGI's Form 610 registration application; the RUC is issued within 2-3 business days and is the single statutory tax and ITBMS identification number required for invoicing and customs operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election is available for a Panama S.A.; the S.A. is an entity-level corporate taxpayer by default, but it may elect to operate under the special Multinational Company Headquarters (SEM) regime, the City of Knowledge regime, or the Panama Pacifico Special Economic Zone regime, each of which provides bespoke tax exemptions and registration procedures, while remaining fully subject to territorial source rules for ordinary operations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 36,000 of annual taxable gross receipts from the sale of goods or services in Panama; new businesses register when expected taxable turnover exceeds this threshold in the first year of operations, except for small contributory regimes (pequeño contribuyente) which have an optional simplified registration for gross income up to USD 36,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-percent ITBMS rate applies to (i) goods physically exported from Panama under a customs export declaration, and (ii) services rendered entirely outside Panama, to non-residents, or considered 'export services' under DGI rules provided that the supplier is a Panama-registered person and the service is received abroad; the seller must always maintain proof of the exportation, proof of transportation out of Panama, and, for services, a contract with the non-resident recipient and bank records evidencing foreign-source payment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / DEX)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill, or other international transport document proving that the goods left Panamanian territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in Panama within the statutory repatriation period

Panama mandates electronic invoicing through the DGI's 'Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (SFE)' under Resolution No. 201-5429. Each invoice must be generated by an DGI-authorized billing software or online platform, submitted in XML in real time or near real-time to the DGI through authorized API channels, and include a standard 'QR' validation code; pre-printed fiscal invoices are permitted only under prior DGI 'Autorización de Impresión Fiscal' and must be reported through the 'Control de Facturación' system. No physical tax stamp is required for digital invoices, but a unique, DGI-validated folio is mandatory.

The DGI may audit export zero-rating claims retroactively; failure to substantiate the physical export, the foreign banking confirmation, or the 180-day repatriation timeline triggers the reversal of the zero rate, assessment of the 7% ITBMS plus surcharge interest, and a tax deficiency penalty equal to 100% of the evaded tax; fraudulent or simulated exports additionally attract criminal tax fraud penalties under Panama's Criminal Code and suppression of the exporter's ITBMS registration for a period determined by the DGI.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized, open-currency regime without exchange controls. Both capital and current account transactions are freely convertible, and there are no statutory restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or repatriation of offshore-owned corporate funds. No prior SBP authorization is required for foreign exchange operations, provided transactions comply with anti-money laundering (AML) reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no mandatory export proceeds surrender requirement, no statutory conversion ratio, and no time limit for holding export receipts in foreign currency. Exporters may retain proceeds in USD or any other convertible currency and may use them freely for domestic or cross-border settlements.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no independent central bank; the banking and trust sectors are regulated by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). The territorial monetary system relies on the U.S. dollar as legal tender and the freely circulating equivalent balboa coinage.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) onboarding for a Panamanian S.A. is rated 'Medium to High' with strict structural caveats. Panama's SBP does not license standalone EMIs as banks, but supervised non-bank regulated financial institutions and licensed e-money/trust operators may provide virtual IBAN services. Cross-border EMI accounts are viable for transaction settlement, but the SBP expects full AML/KYC substance verification and an appropriate registered local corporate nexus. Unlicensed foreign EMIs are not permitted to open in their own right without passported or reciprocal banking arrangements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panamanian Balboa (PAB): mandatory Local File and Master File must be prepared when the taxpayer's gross annual turnover equals or exceeds PAB 1,000,000 or when aggregate related-party transactions equal or exceed PAB 250,000.

Economic substance scope: The Panamanian Economic Substance Requirements apply to entities holding income from relevant activities such as banking, financing, leasing, distribution, service centers, and intellectual property. Steel injection-molding manufacturing of action-figure model kits is not a covered relevant activity; however, the DGI and the Financial Entities Watchdog may re-characterize a non-resident-owned S.A. as a shell where there are no physical premises, no human resources, no manufacturing equipment, and no real economic risk in Panama.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, a monetary fine equal to twice the evaded tax, default interest at 10% per annum, and cancellation of the taxpayer's registration in the DGI active taxpayer registry.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or knowingly cause the filing of false annual returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficit, restitution of any illicitly transferred profits, and are subject to travel bans, asset freezes, and disqualification from acting as officers of another Panamanian corporation.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Where relevant activities are present, fines up to PAB 75,000 and dissolution of the entity; for non-relevant activities, the DGI re-characterizes the S.A. as a Panamanian tax resident, taxes the global manufacturing profit at 25%, and adds a 10% complementary dividend withholding on all amounts deemed repatriated.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil attaches when directors beneficially receive corporate funds or knowingly execute contracts without actual economic substance; each director becomes jointly liable for all corporate taxes, interest, and penalties, and may face personal travel restrictions pending payment.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: DGI may adjust the taxable base under the arm's-length principle, assess the resulting 25% tax shortfall, charge 10% annual interest, and apply a penalty of up to 100% of the under-declared transfer pricing adjustment under the Panamanian Fiscal Code transfer pricing articles.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or included the non-arm's-length pricing in board minutes are personally liable for the additional tax, interest and penalty; intentional structuring of such pricing is treated as tax evasion and can lead to criminal prosecution and civil restitution.
Money Laundering / AML

Statutory sanction: Violation of Panama's Act 23 of 2015 on AML/CFT exposes the entity to administrative fines of up to PAB 1,000,000, criminal penalties of up to 8 years' imprisonment for responsible officers, confiscation of the funds, and mandatory removal from the public registry.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly ignore warning indicators or direct the receipt of suspicious funds without reporting are individually, criminally, and civilly liable; courts may issue freezing orders over personal bank accounts and passeports, and conviction results in imprisonment.
