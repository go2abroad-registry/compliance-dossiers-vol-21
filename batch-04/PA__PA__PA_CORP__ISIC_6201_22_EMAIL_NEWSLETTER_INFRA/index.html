Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is required under Panama law; paid-up capital is at the subscribers' discretion and may be nominal. Capital may be either registered or bearer shares, and no escrow bank account is mandated for an S.A.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection and extinguisher certification obtained from the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá (Bomberos Unificados) with an annual inspection report; structural load clearance and interior safety certificate issued by the municipal engineering department; and occupational hygiene clearance via the CSS (Seguridad y Salud en el Trabajo) for workplace adequacy. Approved certificates are recorded in the municipal Aviso de Operación file.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama employs a territorial-taxation model for S.A.s: only Panamanian-sourced income is taxed at entity level, while foreign-source income is generally outside the net. No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists. A small-taxpayer election (Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente) is available for annual income up to USD 36,000, allowing a simplified 2% ITBMS payment. Taxpayers may also elect the special regime for qualifying digital/export services under applicable sectoral incentive laws. No hybrid mismatch or transparent entity elections are available under general Panamanian law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 0 (no general registration threshold; any person habitually selling taxable goods/services must register for ITBMS upon incorporation. A small-contributor threshold of USD 36,000 per annum applies for the simplified regime)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% ITBMS rate applies to the export of goods and the rendering of services that are provided to a non-resident entity and beneficially used or consumed outside Panama. The taxpayer must retain evidence of foreign-resident status, the contract or service order, and proof that title/benefit passes overseas. Pure offshore IT/licensing services (including email-API SaaS) are eligible when they qualify as services destined to a foreign client and effectively rendered outside Panama's territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración/solicitud de exportación)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice issued with RUC
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit proof from foreign client
  • Contract or service order establishing cross-border provision

Mandatory electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica) applies in phases: large taxpayers on SIT electronic invoice/clearance, with the DGI validating batches and issuing fiscal authorization codes. Invoices must carry a QR code and the DGI validation number. E-invoicing via SIT API integration is required for all registered taxpayers; taxpayers above the revenue threshold must use the SIT e-invoicing platform. Panama follows a near-real-time clearance model rather than fully synchronous transaction-level fiscal stamping. A digital stamp is created by the SIT authorization response.

The DGI may retroactively adjust export zero-rating claims within the statute of limitations (generally 3 years; extended to 6 years for unregistered taxpayers or fraud). If the exporter fails to prove effective exportation or the proceeds are not repatriated within the regulatory period, the ITBMS becomes payable with default interest (surcharge) and fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the omitted tax. Deliberate misuse of zero-rating is tax fraud punishable by additional fines and imprisonment under Panama's Fiscal Penal Code. The DGI may deny the refund or invoke clawback for false certificates, fictitious exports, or round-tripping schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama has full convertibility because the US dollar is legal tender and the Balboa is only a tokenized 1:1 local coin with no separate managed currency. There is no capital-account versus current-account exchange-control distinction, no central bank approval for inward or outward remittances, and no statutory restriction on profit repatriation. A foreign-owned Panamanian S.A. may freely invoice, hold, receive and disburse USD; only tax withholding at the moment of distribution and AML/KYC verification of payment chains apply. A USD payout from Stripe Billing is directly compatible with this regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no mandatory surrender, no custodial timeline, and no conversion ratio on export receipts. Export proceeds from software/SaaS services, including cross-border email-infrastructure invoices, may be retained in USD, deposited in local banks, or held offshore without exchange-control authorization.

Central bank supervision: No Central Bank exists. Panama is a fully dollarized jurisdiction; the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the licensing, regulatory and supervisory banking authority, while Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent of the Republic for public-sector treasury functions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with legal constraints. Panama is dollarized and open to digital financial services, but it has no central bank settlement access for non-bank EMIs. Cross-border EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise Business) are not SBP-licensed banks and generally require a local SBP-licensed partner bank or a shadow-account model to clear USD. For a low-risk email-infrastructure SaaS, a tier-one bank account is preferable to an offshore EMI because the local account provides direct SWIFT/CHIPS clearing and reduces counterparty settlement and onboarding risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity contributions by SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pain.001 from the shareholder's foreign account directly to the Panamanian S.A.'s USD operating account. There is no central bank exchange-control approval. The remittance information should state 'Capital Contribution / Equity Injection' and include a corporate resolution citation and registered capital increase reference when paid-in capital exceeds the authorized capital recorded in the Registro Público. Retain the originator bank's KYC file, executed shareholder resolution, updated shareholder registry and bank receipt for the transaction. Commercial letters of credit or standby letters of credit should be reserved for significant procured hardware or supplier contracts where payment security is required; they are permitted under Panamanian banking law for S.A. companies but entail documentary compliance and are inefficient for recurring SaaS invoices.
