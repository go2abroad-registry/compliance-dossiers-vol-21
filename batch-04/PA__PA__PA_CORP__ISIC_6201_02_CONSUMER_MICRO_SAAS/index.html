Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direccion General de Ingresos (DGI), subordinated to the Ministerio de Economia y Finanzas (MEF) of the Republic of Panama. The official digital platform for registration, filing, and fiscal interaction is the SIT (Sistema de Informacion Tributaria), accessible at https://sit.dgi.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

Sociedad Anonima (S.A.) registration sequence: (1) execute the incorporation deed before a Panamanian notary through a licensed resident agent and record the company with the Registro Publico; (2) file the Aviso de Operacion with the relevant municipality if any physical or local business activity is conducted; (3) apply for the RUC (Registro Unico de Contribuyente) through the SIT portal, submitting the public deed, the appointment of the resident agent and legal representative, the Panamanian fiscal address, and the board resolution appointing the local compliance officer; (4) the RUC certificate will include the ITBMS taxpayer category and the applicable billing authorization; (5) for a micro-SaaS deriving revenue exclusively through foreign Merchant-of-Record platforms (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy), the S.A. should register with the DGI declaring the territorial source position (foreign-source software services) and, if projected Panamanian turnover does not exceed the statutory small-taxpayer threshold, consider electing the simplified small-taxpayer regime; (6) if the entity will issue invoices locally, request simultaneously the electronic invoicing (facturacion electronica) authorization from DGI, either through DGI-approved electronic billing software or an authorised fiscal printer.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide the United States style check-the-box election, nor does it maintain hybrid mismatch or CFC election mechanisms. The available statutory elections are: (i) the Regimen Simplificado de Pequenos Contribuyentes (unified annual quota of PAB 450) for taxpayers with gross annual income not exceeding PAB 36,000, excluding them from standard ITBMS remittance mechanics; (ii) the formal election to compute corporate income tax (ISR) on territorial-source income while attaching a detailed reconciliation of excluded foreign-source revenue; (iii) consolidated income tax filing for affiliated groups only where the statutory requirements of the Codigo Fiscal are satisfied; and (iv) advance rulings or advance pricing agreements requested from the DGI. As a territorial jurisdiction, Panama does not tax foreign-source income arising to the S.A., so no hybrid entity election is necessary or available under domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Panama establishes no general ITBMS registration exemption threshold for corporations; all RUC holders that conduct taxable transfers of goods or services in Panama must charge, declare, and pay ITBMS. A special carve-out applies to small taxpayers under the Regimen Simplificado de Pequenos Contribuyentes: natural persons and small businesses whose gross annual revenue does not exceed PAB 36,000 (equivalent to USD 36,000 at the fixed PAB/USD peg) may elect a unified annual quota of PAB 450 and are released from the standard 7% ITBMS mechanics. Once annual revenue exceeds PAB 36,000, the taxpayer must withdraw from the simplified regime and comply fully with ITBMS registration, invoicing, and monthly declaration obligations. For the consumer micro-SaaS, if all end-consumer sales are invoiced by a foreign Merchant of Record (Paddle or Lemon Squeezy) that collects applicable destination taxes, the Panamanian S.A. has no Panamanian taxable turnover; nonetheless, the S.A. remains registered and may be subject only to the annual territorial ISR regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Panama's territorial ITBMS system, the export of services, including software subscription licences and digital cloud services, is eligible for a 0% ITBMS rate when the service is effectively used, consumed, or exploited outside Panama. The claimant must demonstrate: (i) the recipient is a foreign non-resident; (ii) the subscription, licence agreement, or service contract indicates offshore use; (iii) the payment is received from abroad, or from the foreign Merchant of Record settlement statement; and (iv) contemporaneous technical and accounting records show the foreign destination of the digital supply. Conversely, transactions with customers physically located in Panama remain subject to the standard 7% ITBMS; in the MoR flow, the payment platform is expected to collect and remit the applicable Panamanian tax on the B2C sale, with the S.A. retaining the export zero-rating only on its intercompany or intermediary fee.

  • Customs export declaration and removal permit (required only when software is shipped on physical media or cryptographic hardware; pure digital deliveries require no Panamanian customs declaration)
  • Bill of lading or airway bill (applicable to physical media shipments; for cloud deliveries, the cloud provisioning log and IP-address or geolocation evidence of foreign use)
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103 confirmation, or processor settlement statement from Paddle or Lemon Squeezy evidencing receipt of payment from abroad
  • Licence or subscription contract (EULA) with the foreign end customer, or the MoR consolidated subscriber report in machine-readable format identifying country of residence
  • Commercial invoice bearing a separate line item stating ITBMS 0% - Export of Services, together with the RUC and the DGI e-invoice acceptance code

Panama operates a mandatory electronic invoicing (facturacion electronica) clearance model administered by the DGI. Issuers must be pre-authorised by the DGI and must use DGI-approved billing software or an authorised fiscal printer connected to the DGI environment. Each electronic invoice must be transmitted in near real time to the DGI platform through the web-service API of an accredited technology provider (Tercero Proveedor Autorizado, TPA) using the prescribed XML schema and the taxpayer's digital signature certificate; the DGI validates and returns a unique acceptance code that must appear on the invoice delivered to the customer. The system requires sequential numbering, tamper-evident digital stamping, and end-of-period reconciliation of voided and issued documents. For consumer micro-SaaS operations, where the payment platform acts as Merchant of Record, the foreign platform issues tax-compliant receipts to the final consumer in the relevant jurisdiction, while the Panamanian S.A. must issue valid electronic invoices only for its own service or licence fees charged to the MoR platform and for any Panamanian-sourced B2B supplies, which also remain subject to the e-invoicing mandate unless the taxpayer benefits from the small-taxpayer invoicing exception.

The DGI routinely audits digital exporters claiming the 0% ITBMS rate, placing the burden of proof on the taxpayer to evidence foreign effective use of the service. Where the documentation is deficient or the service is recharacterised as a Panamanian taxable supply, the DGI will assess the unpaid 7% ITBMS, reverse any input tax credits, and apply statutory surcharges (recargos), monthly interest at the DGI quarterly rate, and the penalty provisions of the Codigo Fiscal, which may include fines calculated on the tax affected and, in cases of deliberate falsification, criminal sanctions under the Codigo Penal for tax fraud. Export refunds that have been obtained on the basis of simulated or supported invoices are subject to retrospective clawback with interest and penalties. Although Panama imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline because the balboa is pegged to the United States dollar, the 180-day convention is accepted for treasury substantiation and inward-remittance matching. The S.A. must therefore preserve the MoR settlement statements, DGI e-invoice logs, and bank credit records for the statutory limitation period, generally five years, to withstand DGI audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates under full dollarization and imposes no exchange-control restrictions on current or capital account transactions. Capital is freely convertible, inward investment is unrestricted, and profit remittance is not subject to central-bank approval. Practical remittance hurdles are limited to AML/KYC documentation obligations such as proof of source of funds, updated beneficial ownership records, and corporate good-standing certificates issued by the Registro Público.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio in Panama. SaaS export receipts denominated in USD may be retained in Panamanian bank accounts or abroad. For regulatory clarity, banks require that export transactions be supported by commercial invoices, platform settlement reports, and, where relevant, the underlying service agreements to evidence the source of funds.

Central bank supervision: No traditional central bank exists in Panama. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the banking and foreign-currency regulatory authority; Banco Nacional de Panamá operates as a state-owned commercial bank, not a central bank. The US dollar is legal tender and provides the monetary anchor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital injections must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the overseas shareholder's bank to the Panama S.A. USD account. There is no Panamanian statutory purpose-code system, but the bank's swift text field 70 should state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO PA_CORP, S.A. / EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION' and may include ISO purpose code 'COCP' or 'CRED' if the correspondent bank requires a codified classification. Verification includes a certified extract from the corporate share ledger, board resolution approving the share issuance, notarized placement certificate, and a sworn source-of-funds declaration. Given no minimum legal capital requirement, banks may still apply internal minimum operating deposits such as USD 1,000 to USD 10,000.For SaaS operating expenses, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for supplier and contractor payments, supported by physical or electronic commercial invoices and service agreements. For larger infrastructure or equipment purchases, a documentary commercial letter of credit governed by UCP600 is permissible. Panamanian domestic USD clearing is supported by the national Automated Clearing House and the camera de compensación; cross-border trade settlements flow through the international correspondent banking network. Each outbound payment must be accompanied by the underlying invoice, contract, or purchase order to satisfy SBP AML record-keeping duties.Outright USD/EUR and USD/GBP forward contracts with SBP-licensed commercial banks or international counterparties; physical settlement in USD is the default given the Panamanian dollarized legal tender regime.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for minor or exotic currency exposures where physical delivery is not administratively practical; legally permissible under Panama's liberal foreign-exchange regime but generally redundant for a USD-functional-consumer-SaaS entity.Cross-currency swaps for material multiyear commercial liabilities such as cloud infrastructure agreements, nearshore payroll obligations, or vendor leases; such swaps must be executed with institutionally approved counterparties and documented as treasury risk-management instruments rather than speculative positions.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% flat rate on Panama-source taxable income of legal entities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on declared dividends paid out of Panama-source taxable profits. Dividends paid out of foreign-source profits that were never subject to Panamanian corporate income tax may be subject to the same 10% dividend withholding if the DGI considers the distribution to be a taxable dividend from a Panamanian legal entity. For this reason, the prudent default position for a Panama S.A. is that all dividend distributions to shareholders, irrespective of the underlying source of profit, carry a 10% withholding tax unless the company obtains a specific DGI source ruling or applicable treaty reduction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For calendar-year taxpayers, the Form 101 is due on 31 March following the close of the fiscal year. For corporations using an off-calendar fiscal year, the due date is the last day of the third month following the fiscal year-end. Extension requests do not extend the statutory payment date; interest accrues from the original due date.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Quarterly advance payment declarations are due within the first 30 days after the end of each quarter for corporations with prior-year taxable income exceeding a de minimis threshold. Micro SaaS entities with nil Panama-source taxable income are generally still expected to submit a zero Form 102 if the DGI has issued a payment code.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 5,000,000 of annual gross income is the mandatory threshold for a Panamanian taxpayer to prepare and submit transfer pricing documentation (Local File) for related-party transactions. Master File documentation is required once the multinational group's consolidated revenues exceed USD 50,000,000, and Country-by-Country Reporting applies at the USD 750,000,000 group revenue threshold. The target consumer micro SaaS, assuming far lower revenue, falls below the mandatory Local File threshold but must still be able to demonstrate arm's length pricing if challenged by the DGI.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Requirements (ESR), introduced by Law 254 of 2020 and regulated by Executive Decree 743 of 2021, apply to Panamanian tax-resident legal entities and private interest foundations performing relevant activities. For a micro SaaS company, the relevant activity classification may be 'holding company' or 'provision of digital services', depending on actual functions. To maintain the 0% effective tax rate on foreign-source income and to avoid reclassification as a shell, the S.A. must demonstrate local substance: physical office or leased space in Panama, at least one employee or independent contractor with management authority, participation in strategic decisions in Panama, and maintenance of full corporate accounting records in Panama. An annual ESR declaration must be filed with the DGI. Failure to file or satisfy substance requirements voids the territorial-source exclusion and may trigger deemed Panama-source income determination.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Source Characterisation

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the evaded tax, a surcharge of 50% to 100% for fraudulent misrepresentation, annual interest of 10%, and administrative fines under Panama's Tax Code articles 158-171. Criminal tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment for two to five years under Law 38 of 2001.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or direct the mischaracterisation of source are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt. Panama courts may pierce the corporate veil, order civil restitution, seize personal assets, prohibit travel (travel ban / impedimento de salida) and impose imprisonment on the responsible directors.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Law 254 of 2020, ESR non-compliance attracts administrative penalties ranging from USD 50,000 to USD 200,000, with potential strike-off or dissolution of the S.A. The DGI may reject the foreign-source exclusion and impose 25% CIT on a deemed profit of at least 20% of gross revenue, plus 10% dividend withholding and TP adjustments with a 50% penalty.

Director personal liability: The statutory director, resident agent, and beneficial owner may be jointly liable for ESR penalties and tax deficiencies. Panama has increasingly issued foreign travel bans and asset freezes against directors of non-compliant shell companies; the resident agent's license can be suspended for repeated violations.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Filing Offence

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015 and Law 129 of 2020, administrative fines for AML breaches can reach USD 1,000,000 for legal entities. Criminal money laundering carries imprisonment from five to twelve years, based on the aggregate funds involved. The Financial Analysis Unit (UAF) may order a freeze of the S.A.'s bank accounts and revocation of the registered agent mandate.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and resident agents are criminally liable if they knowingly allow the S.A. to be used as a laundering vehicle. Personal liability includes imprisonment, extradition on international warrants, confiscation of personal property, and permanent prohibition from serving as directors in Panama. Travel bans are routinely applied to the individuals controlling the shell.
