Governing statute

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized economy with full legal tender for the U.S. dollar; the balboa is a money of account only. There is no exchange control law, no capital account restrictions, and no requirement for Central Bank authorization for foreign investment, profit repatriation, or dividend remittance. Current and capital transactions are freely convertible. Profit remittances are not subject to FX restrictions, but a dividend withholding tax of 10% applies on distributions (subject to certain advanced 'dividend tax' credits under Panama's territorial tax regime). Inward direct investment and capital injections are unrestricted; only AML/KYC compliance and beneficial ownership disclosure requirements imposed by the SBP under Law 23 of 2015 (Vega Act) must be satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender requirements are in place. Panama does not impose any mandatory conversion or domiciliation of export earnings in the local banking system. Exporters retain full freedom to hold export proceeds in USD or any other currency in any authorized bank. The notional surrender ratio is 0%, and the statutory timeline is N/A.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) — the Panamanian banking regulator; Panama has no central bank. U.S. dollar is legal tender under the Monetary Law (Ley 84 de 1904); foreign exchange administration is customarily supervised by SBP for banks under Decree Law 9 of 1998, as amended. There is no foreign exchange control authority or board.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional / Medium. Panama has not introduced a full 'e-money token' or digital EMI licensing framework; non-bank payment service providers may operate under the SBP's pending Fintech Law (Bill 697) but for now must either be a licensed bank, a licensed payment processor, or use a commercial agent arrangement with an authorized local bank. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., licensed by FCA, BaFin, or Banque de France) are not allowed to independently take deposits or offer payment accounts to Panamanian-domiciled companies without entering into a partnership with a Panamanian licensed bank or creating a local branch with SBP authorization. In practice, a Panamanian treasury may use an EMI as an auxiliary disbursement rail for international suppliers, but the primary corporate bank must hold legal entity funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the Panamanian entity should be routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the account in the name of the S.A. The purpose code in SWIFT field 72/77T can include 'N/A' as Panama does not mandate a central-bank purpose code; however, we recommend using the ISO 20022 'Corporate Action' code or legacy format 'CAPITAL ACCOUNT' in field 70. For loans, the transfer must reference 'Loan Agreement dated [YYYY-MM-DD]'. Verification requires: (i) a certified copy of the corporate resolution approving the capital contribution or loan; (ii) a notarized share subscription sheet; (iii) a board director certificate listing borrowing powers; (iv) a source-of-funds declaration; and (v) the bank's internal onboarding checklist signed by the compliance officer. Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Line 1 - Panamanian-source gross receipts Gross revenue from sales of used construction excavators and rollers delivered or sold in Panama, including domestic resales, commissions from Panamanian buyers, and warehousing/service income realized in the territory. This is the taxable territorial base; the non-resident-owned S.A. must include all sales where title passes or delivery occurs in Panama regardless of the residence of the buyer or seller. Late filing triggers a monthly recargo of 5% of the tax due (capped at 50%), monthly moratory interest of 1%, and formal fines ranging from USD 25 to USD 10,000; understatement of gross income or improper export exemption claims carry penalties of 25% to 100% of the omitted tax plus 1% monthly interest.

Deductions must be normal and necessary, properly documented, and economically connected with Panamanian-source income; payments to related foreign suppliers require transfer-pricing support. Line 3 - Net taxable income Taxable gross receipts minus allowable deductions, before loss carryforward and before application of the minimum alternative tax. A taxable loss is permissible, but sustained three-year losses with continuous distributions to shareholders will be examined for economic substance and interposed-company recharacterization. Late filing triggers a monthly recargo of 5% of the tax due (capped at 50%), monthly moratory interest of 1%, and formal fines ranging from USD 25 to USD 10,000; understatement of gross income or improper export exemption claims carry penalties of 25% to 100% of the omitted tax plus 1% monthly interest.
Line 4 - Income tax at graduated rates Apply the 15% / 20% / 25% graduated scale to net taxable income to compute the regular income tax (ISR). Use the same schedule for closely held S.A.s with non-resident shareholders; no reduced or preferential corporate rate is granted by reason of foreign ownership. Late filing triggers a monthly recargo of 5% of the tax due (capped at 50%), monthly moratory interest of 1%, and formal fines ranging from USD 25 to USD 10,000; understatement of gross income or improper export exemption claims carry penalties of 25% to 100% of the omitted tax plus 1% monthly interest.
Line 5 - CAIR minimum alternative tax Compute 4.67% of the higher of taxable costs of goods sold or gross taxable income; declare the CAIR where it exceeds the regular ISR, for taxpayers with gross income above USD 1.5 million. The CAIR is payable as an advance of the dividend tax credit mechanism; document the cost base carefully to avoid double counting with Line 2. Late filing triggers a monthly recargo of 5% of the tax due (capped at 50%), monthly moratory interest of 1%, and formal fines ranging from USD 25 to USD 10,000; understatement of gross income or improper export exemption claims carry penalties of 25% to 100% of the omitted tax plus 1% monthly interest.

Non-resident shareholders should ensure that all distributions in the year carried the mandatory dividend WHT; the associated tax is remitted by the S.A., not credited here. Late filing triggers a monthly recargo of 5% of the tax due (capped at 50%), monthly moratory interest of 1%, and formal fines ranging from USD 25 to USD 10,000; understatement of gross income or improper export exemption claims carry penalties of 25% to 100% of the omitted tax plus 1% monthly interest.
