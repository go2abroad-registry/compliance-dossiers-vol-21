Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Unitary National authority without sub-national income-tax agencies; official portal: dgi.mef.gob.pa; transactional e-file and e-invoicing platform: Sistema de Gestión Fiscal (SGF) at sgf.mef.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident-owned Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) conducting ship bunkering and refueling at Panamanian ports obtains its TIN as follows: (1) notarial incorporation and inscription of the Escritura Pública in the Registro Público de Panamá, with a licensed resident agent and capital declaration; (2) filing of the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) application with the DGI via the SGF online portal, attaching the certified corporate charter, resident-agent acceptance, and the beneficial-ownership report required under Ley 129 de 2020; (3) issuance of the RUC certificate with simultaneous ITBMS taxpayer registration; (4) procurement of the municipal Aviso de Operación from the relevant port municipality and of sectoral permits, namely the AMP bunkering licence (Autoridad Marítima de Panamá), the fuel-trading licence of the Secretaría Nacional de Energía, and the exporter registration at the customs single window (VUCE/ANA) for electronic export declarations; (5) activation of the DGI electronic-invoice credential. Non-resident shareholders may remain shielded in standard S.A. filings, but must be disclosed in the restricted beneficial-ownership register filed at the DGI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Panama. Available statutory elections relevant to this bunkering S.A. include: (i) the exporter ITBMS export-credit refund election permitting recovery of input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports; (ii) the optional tonnage/flag tax settlement for vessels owned by the S.A. where it qualifies as an international shipping enterprise under Panamanian registration law; (iii) the small-taxpayer (Pequeño Contribuyente / simplified SME) regime election while annual taxable turnover remains below B/242,000; (iv) special investment-zone stabilization elections (for example Panamá Pacífico or Colon Free Zone regimes) fixing ITBMS at zero on exported bunkering supplies; and (v) source-classification elections under the territorial sourcing rules of the Código Fiscal (Article 694 et seq.) to segregate Panamanian-source income from foreign-source freight and supply income. Hybrid-mismatch rules are not separately codified; Panama follows its BEPS MLI positions at treaty level, with no domestic anti-hybrid check-the-box election available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None, expressed in Balboas (B/., pegged at 1:1 to the US Dollar); any continuous commercial transfer of movable goods or provision of services occurring in Panamanian territory obliges ITBMS registration from the first taxable transaction; no general de-minimis threshold applies to incorporated enterprises, although occasional non-resident suppliers may be required to register only to document a single taxable transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% ITBMS applies under Ley 6 de 2016 where marine gas oil (MDO), heavy fuel oil (IFO 380), marine gasoline, and lubricants are transferred (a) to vessels engaged in international navigation on a foreign-bound voyage (domestic cabotage and coastal fishing excluded); (b) at a Panamanian port, anchorage, or bonded fuel terminal; (c) against a bunkering order issued by a non-resident shipowner, charterer, or managing agent; and (d) physically delivered under a Bunker Delivery Note (BDN) with customs-supervised export movement. The supplier must hold a valid AMP bunkering licence and the customs export documentation must evidence the next foreign port of destination; otherwise the supply is reclassified as a taxable domestic delivery at 7%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA) filed electronically through the ANA SIDUNEA system and the VUCE single window
  • Bunker Delivery Note (BDN) signed by the vessel's Chief Engineer and endorsed by the local shipping agent
  • Bill of Lading, ship's manifest, or port clearance (Zarpe) evidencing the international destination of the voyage
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an authorized Panamanian bank (or equivalent foreign-bank remittance evidence) proving collection from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice and bunker supply contract identifying the non-resident buyer and the international-voyage condition
  • Tank/hose-meter calibration receipts and weight-survey certificate evidencing delivered metric tons

Mandatory under the phased DGI rollout completed in 2023: every ITBMS taxpayer, including bunkering exporters, must issue electronic invoices through the SGF Facturación Electrónica platform, obtain a DGI authorization code and a digital stamp (QR 'sello digital') before delivering the invoice, and transmit dataset files to the DGI web-services API (REST/SOAP with X.509 digital credentials) within 24 hours of issuance; export invoices must cite the ANA customs DUA reference. The fiscal-printer regime is limited to simplified small taxpayers. Zero-rated export transactions are subject to the same e-invoicing clearance before the related customs export is released.

Unsubstantiated or fraudulent zero-rating is treated by the DGI as a taxable domestic supply: the DGI assesses the 7% ITBMS, reverses any export-credit refund already allowed, applies a monthly surcharge (recargo) of 1% up to 100% of the tax, and imposes fines scaled under Ley 6 de 2016 and the Código Fiscal between 10% and 200% of the evaded tax for repeated or concealment-related offenses. Forged BDN or DUA documents trigger criminal tax-fraud prosecution with imprisonment of up to 8 years under Panama's tax and anti-money-laundering fraud provisions, confiscation of the fuel or proceeds, revocation of the AMP bunkering permit, and publication of the sanction. Refunds recovered are immediately repayable with the same accruals running from the refund payment date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama permits full and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. Because the US dollar is the domestic currency, no exchange authorization, central bank return, or conversion spread is required. Profit remittances and dividend distributions may be freely executed through SBP-licensed banks; the de facto hurdles are exclusively bank-level AML/KYC due-diligence, documentary evidence of source of funds, and confirmation that the Panamanian entity is in good standing and holds a certificate of foreign tax residency if treaty relief is sought. There are no capital controls, no repatriation limits, and no statutory withholding or surrender obligations on profit remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama does not require mandatory surrender, repurchase, repatriation, or conversion of export proceeds. Bunker export proceeds and other foreign receivable collections may be retained abroad or brought into Panama in USD without restriction. However, for accounting and tax territoriality purposes, the treasury should segregate invoices, vessel delivery logs, and remittance advices to substantiate the offshore or onshore sourcing of each transaction.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama does not maintain a central bank; the US dollar is legal tender and the monetary system is dollarized. Banking supervision is vested in the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), which licenses banks, exchange houses, and other financial intermediaries. No state exchange-control commission or foreign-exchange administration exists; external financing and consolidated fiscal monitoring are handled by the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. Panama does not have an EU-style electronic-money institution (EMI) licensing regime; e-money activities are absorbed by the banking licence framework supervised by the SBP. Offshore digital EMIs may provide ancillary USD-denominated wallets, dual-currency cards, or payment aggregation, but they cannot lawfully serve as the primary operative treasury, capital escrow, or trade settlement account for a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima engaged in ship bunkering. Legal and compliance risk is material; EMIs should be restricted to operational niceties and never used for AML-critical escrow flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital injections should be transferred in USD using the standard SWIFT MT103 message through an SBP-licensed Panamanian bank. The remitter must state the beneficiary as the Panamanian S.A. with its exact account number and SWIFT/BIC, reference the corporate business name exactly as registered, and include the payment narrative 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — SOCIEDAD ANÓNIMA'. Supporting documents should include a certified board resolution authorizing the capital increase, an updated shareholder register, and a source-of-funds statement. Since Panama is dollarized, the receiving bank will credit USD directly with no conversion or exchange-control paperwork. Where the parent or shareholder is foreign, the remittance should be labelled as 'direct investment' and, where applicable, reported internally for transfer-pricing and economic-substance documentation.Trade disbursements for bunker fuel procurement, lubricant replenishment, and port-service settlements should be executed using direct SWIFT MT103 credits for ordinary invoices and standby letters of credit (SBLC) or commercial documentary letters of credit issued under UCP600 for deferred or documentary shipments. The LC chain will normally include an MT700/MT707 issuance and amendment series requiring presentation of marine fuel delivery notes, quantity and quality certificates, invoices, statement of facts, and discharge port vouchers. Because Panama is a USD clearing centre, USD transfers are settled via US correspondent banks, typically through Fedwire or CHIPS, adding a one-to-two business day clearing cycle. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% flat corporate income tax (Impuesto sobre la Renta, ISR) on net taxable income derived from Panamanian-source activities; a 1% alternative minimum tax on gross taxable revenue (Tasa Única) applies where it exceeds the ordinary ISR liability.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding on gross dividend distributions paid from profits taxed under the ordinary 25% ISR regime; 20% withholding applies to dividends paid from profits that benefitted from the Tasa Única (1% minimum tax) regime. Distributions funded exclusively by foreign-source (untaxed) earnings fall outside the Panamanian dividend WHT where the company can trace the profit pool with audited accounts.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual ISR return must be filed within three months after the fiscal year-end; calendar-year companies must file no later than March 15 of the following tax year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Quarterly installments are due within 15 days after each quarter-end: April 15, July 15, October 15, and January 15.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 250,000 - annual aggregate related-party transactions exceeding this threshold trigger the mandatory transfer-pricing documentation and informative disclosure attached to Form 101 (Executive Decree 38 of 2018). A full Local File and Master File (functional analysis, comparability and benchmarking study) are required when aggregate controlled transactions exceed USD 2,000,000; group-wide Country-by-Country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least USD 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: ECONOMIC SUBSTANCE REQUIREMENTS (ESR) APPLY. Law 254 of 2021 imposes mandatory economic presence for Panamanian entities undertaking relevant activities, which expressly include international shipping-related operations, fuel distribution, and treasury/financing functions. A ship-bunkering S.A. must demonstrate Panamanian management and control (board meetings and strategic decisions in Panama), a local registered office and physical premises, qualified local employees sufficient to execute the core revenue-generating activities, and local operating expenditure proportionate to the profit booked. An annual Relevance Activity Declaration and ESR return must be filed with the DGI by the statutory tax-return deadline. Foreign-source exemption does not waive ESR; failure to satisfy substance results in loss of tax-residence benefits, treaty denial, and sanction under the ESR penalty ladder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion penalties: fines from 100% to 300% of the evaded tax, monthly surcharges of 1.5% on unpaid amounts, and imprisonment of two to five years under the Código Fiscal and Código Penal; DGI may seize bank accounts, vessels, and receivables and cancel operating licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct or knowingly tolerate the false declaration are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax; the corporate veil is pierced, personal travel bans are imposed, and conviction carries imprisonment of up to five years.
Unlawful Dividend Distribution and WHT Avoidance

Statutory sanction: DGI assesses the unwithheld tax against the S.A. plus a 100% surcharge under Article 712 of the Código Fiscal, mora interest at 1.5% per month, and denial of future tax clearance; repeated violations trigger criminal prosecution of the company's authorized signatories.

Director personal liability: Board members who approve unlawful distributions are personally liable for the unrecovered WHT and interest; they face civil enforcement against personal assets, exit bans, and criminal indictment for fraudulent profit partition.
Shell Company and Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Panama's Economic Substance Law 254 of 2021 imposes progressive fines up to USD 100,000 for false substance declarations, nullification of the entity's tax-residence certificate, DGI recharacterization of all foreign-source revenue as Panamanian-source, and potential dissolution of the S.A.

Director personal liability: Nominee and de facto directors face joint financial liability for the recharacterized tax, disqualification from holding directorships, travel restrictions pending payment, and criminal money-laundering charges where the sham entity was used to conceal ownership.
Money Laundering and AML Compliance Failure in Bunkering

Statutory sanction: AML fines up to USD 1,000,000 per violation against the S.A. and its compliance officer; criminal money-laundering conviction carries imprisonment of five to twelve years, forfeiture of vessels and fuel cargo, and administrative closure of the bunkering license by the maritime authority.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, and compliance officers are criminally liable for organized money-laundering acts; asset forfeiture without prior conviction is possible, combined with Interpol red notices, travel bans, and the collapse of corporate limited-liability protection for gross negligence.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Mispricing

Statutory sanction: DGI TP adjustment recharacterizing the transaction, a 10% penalty on the resulting underpayment, fixed fines up to USD 10,000 for omitted documentation per Executive Decree 38 of 2018, loss of treaty benefits, and audit extension to the previous three fiscal years.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors face civil restitution for the adjusted tax and penalty amounts; intentional TP mispricing is prosecuted as fraud, exposing signatories to travel bans, personal asset execution, and criminal imprisonment.
