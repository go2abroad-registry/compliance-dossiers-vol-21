Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas de la República de Panamá; electronic portal: Sede Electrónica ('e-Tributa') at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa and https://www.e-tributa.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or its locally incorporated Sociedad Anónima must first obtain legal existence or an effective branch registration with the Registro Público de Panamá. Upon registration, the entity must apply to the DGI for the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) through the Sede Electrónica, submitting the public registry certificate, sworn declaration of business commencement, proof of the legal agent's appointment in Panama, and a fixed fiscal address notice. A non-resident foreign entity acting without local incorporation may be required to appoint a resident fiscal representative and register for a RUC where it carries on a taxable activity or maintains an economic nexus in Panama. The RUC is the unified TIN/VAT ID used for income tax, ITBMS, and customs purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: the Panamanian Sociedad Anónima is taxed on Panama-source income at the entity level; foreign-source income is generally exempt from Panamanian corporate income tax. For an export wholesaler, income from goods physically delivered and sold outside Panama may be treated as foreign-source provided the offshore sale is properly documented and no title or beneficial ownership is transferred inside Panama. The entity is not a pass-through and is subject to entity-level taxation on Panama-source income only.

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election for Sociedades Anónimas. However, available elections and optional statutory regimes include: (i) the simplified small-taxpayer ITBMS regime ('Régimen de Tributación Simplificada') for taxpayers with annual gross receipts up to USD/B/.300,000; (ii) the Panamanian Investment Company (SIP) election for qualifying holding and treasury activities; (iii) the Multinational Company Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007; and (iv) an optional 25% corporate income tax rate regime applicable to Panama-source earnings. Taxpayers do not have an election to be taxed on a worldwide basis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no de minimis economic threshold for ITBMS registration; any individual or entity performing taxable acts in Panama (including preparing or executing export sales from Panamanian territory) must register for the ITBMS as of the commencement of business activity. An optional simplified small-taxpayer regime applies to taxpayers with annual gross income not exceeding USD/B/.300,000, but standard ITBMS registration remains mandatory for an export wholesale corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods, including Chinese baijiu supplied from Panama to foreign buyers, are zero-rated for ITBMS purposes where the goods are physically removed from Panamanian customs territory, legal title and risk pass to the non-resident buyer abroad, and the sale is supported by a valid customs export declaration. Offshore services and logistics charges linked to an export shipment are also eligible for zero-rating if performed for a non-resident recipient and consumed outside Panama. The exporter must maintain evidence that the transfer of possession, title, and risk occurred outside Panama; mere issuance of an invoice to a foreign person does not qualify unless the physical export and customs exit are proven.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Mercancías de Exportación / SIGA electronic export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (Conocimiento de Embarque / Guía Aérea) evidencing dispatch to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice (Certificado de Remesa del Exterior / Comprobante bancario de cobro) showing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the shipment, the ISIC 4632 export wholesale activity, and the baijiu product class
  • Certificate of Origin (if required by the foreign customs authority or to evidence non-Panamanian sourcing)

Panama mandates issuance of electronic invoices through the DGI's Sede Electrónica or through certified 'equipo emisor fiscal' / private electronic-invoicing providers. Real-time or pre-clearance transmission is required for online electronic invoicing systems, including assignment of a fiscal serial number ('serie fiscal'), digital signature application, and inclusion of a fiscal QR code for verification. For exporters, the electronic invoice must be cross-referenced to the customs export declaration and the foreign buyer's tax identification data. The DGI may have to validate the invoice before its final delivery to the foreign counterparty, and any discrepancies between the e-invoice, customs declaration, and forex repatriation records will trigger an automatic digital audit flag.

Export zero-rating is scrutinized by the DGI and the Aduana Nacional. Fraudulent use of zero-rating — including fictitious exports, diverted or returned goods, overstatement of foreign proceeds, or failure to repatriate foreign currency within 180 days — triggers immediate reversal of the zero rate, payment of the unpaid 7% ITBMS plus surcharges and late-payment interest, and monetary fines under the Panamanian Tax Code. The issuing exporter and its legal agent may also be subject to criminal tax-fraud penalties, suspension or cancellation of the RUC, seizure of goods, and disqualification of the taxpayer from contributing to the simplified ITBMS regime or obtaining new export endorsements. Retrospective clawback applies from the date of the original zero-rated transaction, and the DGI may aggregate all false export claims within a six-year statutory period for surcharge purposes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted currency convertibility. Panama does not impose exchange controls on either capital or current account transactions. Profit and dividend remittances are freely permitted, subject only to standard AML/KYC documentation and the 10% withholding tax on dividends distributed from Panama-source profits. Foreign-source income earned by a local S.A. is outside the territorial tax base.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory deadline for export receipts denominated in foreign currency. Exporters may hold U.S. dollars or any other freely convertible currency in domestic or offshore accounts.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no formal central bank. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) regulates banking, the Ministry of Economy and Finance oversees monetary policy coordination, and the U.S. dollar circulates as legal tender alongside the Panama Balboa at a 1:1 fixed peg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Panama licensed digital EMIs and payment fintechs under the SBP's Fintech Hub framework, but cross-border settlement via fully-digital non-bank EMIs remains limited in LC issuance and high-volume USD clearing. Offshore digital EMIs without an SBP banking license are not viable as primary clearing entities; they are acceptable only as ancillary e-wallet collection nodes subject to strict KYC and transaction-volume caps. A tier-1 Panamanian commercial bank must remain the core clearing rail.

The annual Form 105 is due within three (3) months following the close of the fiscal year; for a calendar-year S.A., the deadline is 31 March of the following year. Quarterly instalments are due by 31 March, 30 June, 30 September, and 31 December (or the corresponding quarter-end for a non-calendar fiscal year).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 3,000,000 (Balboa, pegged 1:1 with the US dollar): taxpayers with gross Panamanian-source income equal to or exceeding this threshold must file the annual transfer-pricing return (Form 205) and prepare a contemporaneous Local File; a Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue of USD 850,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Requirements (Law 254 of 2021, implementing Executive Decree 265 of 2022) apply to corporations conducting relevant activities, including distribution and logistics, intragroup services, and headquarters operations. The baijiu export wholesale S.A. must maintain premises in Panama, employ locally resident directors or staff qualified to direct the activity, keep core business records in Panama, and incur proportionate operating expenditures. Failure to satisfy ESR disqualifies the entity from treaty benefits, allows the DGI to recharacterize the Panamanian entity as a tax-reporting shell, and attracts fines up to B/. 50,000 plus potential administrative dissolution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Civil recalculation of tax at 25%, a surcharge of up to 100% of the evaded tax, interest, fines up to B/. 10,000, plus criminal imprisonment of 2-5 years (5-10 years where the evasion exceeds B/. 300,000 or uses an interposed foreign entity).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the fraudulent return are held jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax on a piercing-the-veil basis; personal bank account freezes, travel bans, disqualification from managing Panamanian companies, and imprisonment are legally available.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Fines of B/. 5,000 to B/. 50,000 per relevant activity under Law 254 of 2021; the DGI may disregard the entity and assess the beneficial owner directly at 25%; administrative dissolution for repeated non-compliance; referral to the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF) for money-laundering review.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face personal fines and disqualification; directors who knowingly permit a sham structure can be criminally prosecuted under Law 23 of 2015 and ordered to personally restore any public or third-party losses.
Anti-Money Laundering / Trade-Based Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Law 23 of 2015 fines up to B/. 1,000,000 for serious AML breaches, asset seizure and freezing orders, and criminal money-laundering penalties of 5-10 years' imprisonment (up to 12 years for aggravated cross-border or organized activity).

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance managers are personally prosecutable for the failure to implement AML preventive standards; the corporate veil is pierced to allow confiscation of personal assets and travel bans pending criminal trial.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The DGI imposes a transfer-pricing adjustment that increases taxable income and applies 25% tax, together with a penalty of 25% of the underpayment and monthly interest; failure to file Form 205 adds an independent fine of B/. 5,000 to B/. 10,000.

Director personal liability: Where the corporation is undercapitalized, directors are personally assessed on the DGI-adjusted tax; deliberate price manipulation or falsified benchmarking is referred for criminal tax evasion; directors face disqualification when the company is struck off after persistent TP non-compliance.
