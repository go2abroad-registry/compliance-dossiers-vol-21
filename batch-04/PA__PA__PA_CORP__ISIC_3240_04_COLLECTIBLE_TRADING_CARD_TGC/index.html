Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas); official e-portal: Sede Electrónica DGI — https://sede.dgi.gob.pa (e-Tax 2.0 / RUC online services). Customs matters are handled by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA).

E-government filing portal

The Panama S.A. (Sociedad Anónima) is incorporated via notarized articles of incorporation (Escritura Pública) recorded at the Registro Público de Panamá through a licensed registered agent. For tax identity, the company must (i) appoint a resident legal representative; (ii) obtain the Aviso de Operación (business operation notice) from the municipal mayor's office (Alcaldía) corresponding to the registered address; and (iii) apply at DGI for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes), the sole unified TIN/VAT ID, submitting the public deed, corporate bylaws, the Aviso de Operación, passports/personal identification of the legal representative and directors, and proof of the registered office (Certificado de Registro Público). The DGI issues a single RUC number covering both income tax (ISR) and ITBMS, typically within 5–10 working days; foreign non-resident entities may also obtain a non-resident RUC through a local authorized attorney for ITBMS and export transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with Territorial_Scope: a Panama S.A. is a separate juridical person taxed on Panama-source income only. Gross income derived from activities carried out in Panama (including local manufacturing and domestic sales of collectible trading cards and related printing services) is subject to corporate income tax (ISR) at the headline 25% rate; income from foreign sources and foreign operations is generally outside Panamanian tax jurisdiction for a territorially structured S.A. Distributions to shareholders attract a 10% dividend withholding tax (5% is applicable for dividends sourced from foreign-source profits under certain conditions). No pass-through eligibility exists at national level; check-the-box treatment is not available.

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a US-style check-the-box pass-through election. Available statutory elections and special regimes include: (i) optional cash or accrual accounting election for income tax and ITBMS purposes by small businesses; (ii) Régimen Especial de Pequeño Contribuyente (simplified small taxpayer regime) for taxpayers with annual gross turnover up to B/. 300,000; (iii) the SEM / Multinational Headquarters regime (Law 41 of 2007) granting reduced/zero ITBMS and ISR treatment for qualifying regional headquarter services; (iv) the Panama Pacifico Special Economic Zone regime exempting eligible operations; (v) the flat annual corporation tax (tasa única) of B/. 300 applicable to offshore S.A.s that do not derive Panama-source income, maintained by payment of the annual franchise fee to the Registro Público; and (vi) elections under the anti-avoidance and substance rules adopted in Panama's tax transparency reform (CFC/PE regime), none of which create hybrid or reverse-hybrid mismatches for OECD purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 0 (no general de minimis threshold): every person or entity habitually selling goods or providing services in Panamanian territory must register for ITBMS from the first taxable operation. A simplified small-taxpayer regime applies when quarterly gross turnover is up to B/. 75,000 (approximately US$75,000, i.e., annual B/. 300,000), allowing a fixed/margin-based ITBMS settlement; nevertheless, the entity must still be registered in the DGI RUC registry.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods, including collectible trading cards with security holographic and cold-foil printing, and exports of services are zero-rated (0% ITBMS) under Article 25 of Law 24 of 2005 when the goods are physically removed from Panamanian territory, the sale is made to a buyer resident abroad, and ownership of the goods passes to the foreign buyer without local use or consumption. Qualifying services must be provided to a non-resident recipient, effectively used or enjoyed outside Panama, and not connected to immovable property situated in Panama. The exporter must hold an active RUC, file periodic informative ITBMS declarations, and substantiate the foreign destination of goods/services; input ITBMS on manufacturing inputs (films, foils, inks, cardstock, machinery) is creditable against output VAT or refunded periodically via the DGI electronic refund procedure. Due to full dollarization of the Panamanian economy, no central bank exchange-control approval is required for export proceeds, but the DGI requires traceable payment evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Exportación / DUA) stamped and validated by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (non-negotiable copy, dated and properly endorsed)
  • Commercial Invoice (consularized or notarized where required for the destination country)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign-exchange receipt evidence — SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice, or foreign correspondent bank confirmation showing export proceeds credited to a Panamanian or offshore account
  • Exporter/importer contract or binding purchase order evidencing f.o.b. / c.i.f. commercial terms
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (when claiming free-trade or Colon Free Zone benefits)
  • ITBMS input tax credit schedule (for refund substantiation)

Panama operates a mandatory electronic invoicing system (Facturación Electrónica de Panamá) phased-in by DGI Resolutions starting 2022 and fully scaled to all general-regime taxpayers, including exporters, by 2025. Business invoices must be declared as XML structured documents presented electronically to the DGI platform (Sede Electrónica); the system validates the XML in near real time, assigns an electronic authorization number, and applies a digital fiscal stamp issued on the qualified digital certificate (firma digital) of the issuer. Taxpayers must use DGI-approved billing software or API-enabled ERP/edge devices integrated with the fiscal cloud; for exports, DGI cross-checks the declared e-invoice against the Single Export Declaration (DUA) and customs transit records. Transitional fiscal printers remain in use only where explicitly authorized.

DGI, together with ANA and the Financial Analysis Unit (UAF), conducts post-shipment audits of zero-rated export claims using cross-referenced DUA, e-invoice, and bank transaction data. If the export is unsupported, the border crossing is not proven, or proceeds are not received within 180 days (or the period justified under documented commercial credit terms), the DGI re-characterizes the supply as taxable at 7% ITBMS and assesses the evaded tax with a 100% surcharge, monthly interest of 1%, and fines ranging from B/. 500 to B/. 50,000. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion under the Código Fiscal is available for deliberately fabricated exports and fraudulent refund claims; clawback of any credited or refunded input VAT is retrospective within the statutory limitation period of five years (extendable in cases of criminal conduct), and the exporter's RUC and electronic invoicing authorization are subject to suspension.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama maintains full de facto and de jure dollarization. Both the current account and the capital account are freely convertible: there are no advance-approval procedures, no exchange-rate warranty taxes, no capital-export authorizations, and no restriction on the repatriation of dividends, royalties, interest, or liquidation proceeds. A Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) receiving export revenue or foreign investment may hold, transfer, and repatriate U.S. dollars without any central-bank permit. The two practical frictions are not sovereign but institutional: (i) bank-level source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership verification under Ley 23 de 2015, and (ii) because Panama applies a territorial income tax, dividends distributed from Panama-source profits must be supported by the S.A.'s tax declarations to the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) before the account-holding bank will allow a large outbound remittance without triggering an AML narrative query. For a trading-card publisher, cross-border processing volumes from Stripe and Authorize.Net are treated as ordinary export collections and are freely credited to the S.A.'s USD account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no surrender, no mandatory conversion, and no repatriation deadline on export proceeds. A Colombian-style, Chinese-style, or Argentine-style export-receipts régime does not exist under Panamanian law. All USD export proceeds from the sale of collectible trading cards, including card sales settled through Stripe, Authorize.Net, PayPal or other online payment service providers, may remain in the S.A.'s designated USD account indefinitely or be converted to other currencies at the S.A.'s discretion. The only continuing obligations are commercial and fiscal: the S.A. must record all export income in its statutory accounting books, support it with sales invoices, reconcile its e-commerce payment-provider statements, and file the annual income-tax return (ISR and ITBMS where applicable). No DGI or SBP reporting of the physical receipt of funds is required beyond standard suspicious-transaction reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Panama operates without a traditional central bank and without an official monetary authority possessing lender-of-last-resort or foreign-exchange-reserve powers. The prudential regulator for banks, finance companies, trusts and savings institutions is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), established by Decreto Ley 9 de 1998. Securities and investment-fund intermediation are supervised by the Superintendencia del Mercado de Valores (SMV). The Banco Nacional de Panamá, a state-owned commercial bank, serves as fiscal agent of the Republic and provides domestic cheque clearance through the Cámara de Compensación, but it does not conduct monetary policy, issue base money, or administer exchange controls. The U.S. dollar is the circulating legal tender of Panama; the balboa exists only as fractional coinage. Consequently, the relevant 'central bank' functional equivalent for treasury compliance is the SBP acting through its Directorate of Regulation and its AML/CFT oversight functions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Viability rating: Medium-Low. Panama does not have a standalone electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing category comparable to the European EMI Directive. A foreign fintech or digital bank (for example Airwallex, Payoneer, or Stripe Treasury) may maintain a treasury or settlement account with an SBP-licensed Panamanian bank, but it cannot lawfully take deposits from the Panamanian public or act as a money transmitter in Panama without an SBP banking license or a licensed remittance-company authorization under the financial-license framework. The legally robust blueprint for PA_CORP is to (i) hold the primary operating balance at a Tier-1 Panamanian bank, (ii) use offshore EMIs and PSP consolidators only as inbound collection channels, and (iii) execute a daily 'sweep-to-master' instruction so that all PSP and EMI balances settle into the SBP-licensed USD account within T+1. Any arrangement under which an unlicensed EMI holds the S.A.'s funds on an ongoing basis with balance-sheet float should be documented with a written money-settlement agreement, an AML/BEC (business email compromise) control review, and a qualified legal opinion confirming that the EMI is not performing regulated deposit-taking activity in Panama.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Execute equity injections and operating revenue collections exclusively in USD using SWIFT MT103 or the equivalent ISO 20022 pacs.008 corporate-to-bank message. The remittance instruction must use the ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (Investment in Equity Capital) for shareholder contributions or 'INTC' (Intra-Group Payment) for intercompany financing, with the 140-character remittance-information field stating: 'Capital increase per S.A. resolution number [X] dated [YYYY-MM-DD]', followed by the beneficiary name exactly as registered with the Public Registry of Panama. The beneficiary S.A. shall hold its USD master account at an SBP-licensed Tier-1 bank; the funding bank must route via the beneficiary bank's New York correspondent account (Fedwire/CHIPS) because Panama uses the U.S. clearing system for USD. Verification bundle required by the receiving bank: the account-opening corporate package, an updated Ficha from the Public Registry, the shareholders' resolution authorizing the capital increase, the subscribed-capital payment receipt, a notarized director certificate, the RUBF filing receipt, and the CRS/FATCA self-certifications. For operating collections from Stripe and Authorize.Net, configure the PSP payout engine to settle daily to the S.A.'s USD account at the Tier-1 bank by Fedwire or international ACH; retain the PSP settlement reports as the audit trail for the bank. Inbound funds become immediately available after OFAC and internal sanctions screening; no Panamanian governmental approval or conversion is required.Primary clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 cover or serial payment for direct supplier disbursements, with FEDWIRE/CHIPS used for same-day USD clearing at the New York correspondent level. For substantial purchases from security-printing suppliers (holographic cold-foil film, laminated card stock, polypropylene overlay, anti-counterfeiting inks), use an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 and confirmed by the beneficiary's U.S., EU, or Asian advising bank, especially when the transaction exceeds US$ 50,000; the MT700 must incorporate Incoterms 2020 (CIF or FOB), the commercial invoice, packing list, and the required UL/eco-compliance certificates. For long-term supply contracts, the Panamanian S.A. may issue a Standby Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT760) as a performance guarantee, accounted for as a contingent liability in the statutory ledger. Disbursement controls: dual-authorization (maker-checker) in the online treasury module, IP-restricted sessions, beneficiary-account pre-validation through a positive-pay database, and a self-imposed OFAC/sanctions screening policy mandatory for every counterparty. Where the supplier operates in a jurisdiction with limited FX convertibility (for example, Chinese foil producers), settle the LC or MT103 in USD and permit the counterparty to access the FX conversion through its own country's banking system; the Panamanian entity bears no obligation to invoice or settle in the local currency. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability rating: Medium-Low. Panama does not have a standalone electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing category comparable to the European EMI Directive. A foreign fintech or digital bank (for example Airwallex, Payoneer, or Stripe Treasury) may maintain a treasury or settlement account with an SBP-licensed Panamanian bank, but it cannot lawfully take deposits from the Panamanian public or act as a money transmitter in Panama without an SBP banking license or a licensed remittance-company authorization under the financial-license framework. The legally robust blueprint for PA_CORP is to (i) hold the primary operating balance at a Tier-1 Panamanian bank, (ii) use offshore EMIs and PSP consolidators only as inbound collection channels, and (iii) execute a daily 'sweep-to-master' instruction so that all PSP and EMI balances settle into the SBP-licensed USD account within T+1. Any arrangement under which an unlicensed EMI holds the S.A.'s funds on an ongoing basis with balance-sheet float should be documented with a written money-settlement agreement, an AML/BEC (business email compromise) control review, and a qualified legal opinion confirming that the EMI is not performing regulated deposit-taking activity in Panama.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% marginal corporate income tax rate on Panama-source taxable income (progressive schedule applied to the first USD 1,500,000 of taxable income: 4% up to USD 100,000; 10% on USD 100,001-500,000; 15% on USD 500,001-1,000,000; 20% on USD 1,000,001-1,500,000; 25% above USD 1,500,000).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on gross dividends paid or credited out of Panama-source profits (uniform statutory rate under Article 733 of the Fiscal Code). No corporate-level tax is imposed on retained earnings, and no CFC rules apply to a Panamanian S.A.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panama's transfer-pricing regime (Law 55 of 2019, Executive Decree 12 of 2020, OECD-aligned) applies at transaction level. Local File: required when annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed USD 1,000,000. Master File: required when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue reaches USD 50,000,000. Country-by-Country Report: required for groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least USD 750,000,000. All thresholds are in USD, Panama's legal tender.

Economic substance scope: Panama is not an offshore no-tax jurisdiction, but Law 254 of 2021 established Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) applicable to a Panama tax-resident S.A. that earns 'mobile business income' from foreign related entities (IP royalties, intra-group financing, pure equity holding, distribution and service-center activities). A trading-card publisher operating a local printing plant with physical premises, employees, and equipment satisfies the substance test. A pass-through S.A. that collects foreign card revenue while management decisions occur abroad must file an annual economic substance declaration and demonstrate adequate premises, full-time local operational staff, recurring board meetings held in Panama, and decision-making exercised in Panama. Non-compliance triggers administrative fines (generally USD 10,000-USD 100,000), potential loss of the tax-residence certificate, and the risk that DGI will recharacterize mobile income as Panama-source on a control-and-management basis.
