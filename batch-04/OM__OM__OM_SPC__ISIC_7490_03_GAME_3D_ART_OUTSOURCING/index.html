Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Authority of the Sultanate of Oman (formerly the Secretariat General of Taxation, Royal Decree No. 39/2020 establishes the independent Tax Authority), administered as a single national-level agency under the Ministry of Finance framework. The official e-services portal is the OTA Tax Portal at https://taxoman.om, which handles taxpayer registration, VAT filing, e-invoicing clearance and audit notifications.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Single Person Company (SPC) with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) via the InvestEasy portal to obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate and unique CR number. Step 2: Access the Oman Tax Authority (OTA) Tax Portal (taxoman.om) and complete 'Taxpayer Registration' using the CR credentials and constitutional documents (Memorandum of Association, CR certificate, valid passport copies of the foreign sole shareholder, board/manager appointment). Step 3: Obtain the corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) for income tax purposes, which serves as the primary tax identity for the SPC; the TIN is electronically validated against MOCIIP and civil registration databases. Step 4: Register separately for VAT once the SPC becomes liable, i.e., upon exceeding the mandatory threshold of OMR 38,500 taxable supplies/imports in any consecutive 12-month period; a distinct VAT ID is issued. Voluntary registration is allowed at OMR 19,250. For a newly formed foreign-owned SPC with zero local activity, immediate VAT registration is not mandatory, but income tax registration is compulsory from the CR date; if the SPC exclusively exports services, it may still register voluntarily to recover input VAT on Omani overhead costs. Step 5: Complete e-invoicing onboarding under the OTA's phased digital stamping mandate once the SPC's turnover qualifies or the mandate becomes universal. Non-resident suppliers of electronically supplied services to Omani consumers must register for Omani VAT regardless of threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (SPC is a resident juridical person under Article 12 of the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree No. 28/2009, as amended by Royal Decree No. 9/2017); the SPC is taxed in its own name on worldwide income at the statutory corporate rates of 3% on taxable income up to OMR 30,000 and 15% on the excess. No pass-through or transparent treatment is recognised for LLC/SPC structures even when 100% foreign-owned. Unless the SPC is exempt under a negotiated in-country economic-zone incentive, the entity-level tax regime always applies; territorial scope is reserved only for non-resident persons whose Oman-source income is subject to withholding of up to 10% for services, but this does not apply to the resident SPC's own profits).

Available election forms

Oman does not operate a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' pass-through election; LLC/SPC entities may never elect transparent status. Available statutory elections include: (i) VAT Group registration election under the Executive Regulations of the VAT Law (Royal Decree No. 121/2020), available to companies 90%+ commonly owned, permitting a single group VAT return; (ii) voluntary VAT registration election where taxable supplies exceed OMR 19,250 but do not reach OMR 38,500; (iii) election of a different financial year for income tax with OTA prior approval; (iv) election of instalment plans for tax assessments; and (v) voluntary disclosure (sālih) procedures for correcting prior filings. Oman's GAAR (Article 30bis of the Income Tax Law) is a general anti-abuse rule, not an elective hybrid-mismatch regime, and Oman has not domestically enacted BEPS Action 2 hybrid-mismatch statutory elections as of the current regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: OMR 38,500 of taxable supplies and/or imports in any consecutive 12-month period (approximately USD 100,000); voluntary registration: OMR 19,250 (approximately USD 50,000); non-resident suppliers of electronically supplied services to consumers located in Oman must register for Omani VAT with no minimum threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under Article 14 of the VAT Law where the supplier or the purchaser exports the goods from Oman to a destination outside Oman and outside the GCC within 90 days of supply; the zero-rating claim must be supported by a valid Omani Customs export declaration, transport documentation (B/L or air waybill), commercial invoice, and proof that the goods left Omani territory. Services: 'offshore services' are zero-rated where the recipient of the supply is a non-resident person outside the GCC, the services directly benefit a person outside Oman and the services do not relate to immovable property or movable goods located in Oman; digital 3D art sculpting, character rigging and cinematic asset files transmitted electronically to foreign game studios therefore qualify for zero-rating if place-of-supply rules place the supply with the non-resident customer and documentary evidence of non-residency is retained. Inter-GCC B2B supplies to a registered game studio in another GCC State remain outside Omani VAT via the recipient's reverse charge mechanism rather than being Omani zero-rated exports. To claim a refund of residual input VAT on local Omani purchases, the exporter must hold a valid VAT registration and file quarterly VAT returns via the OTA Tax Portal, claiming zero-rated export lines with attached evidence.

  • Valid Omani Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document/SAD) issued by Oman Customs at the point of exit (air, sea or GCC land border)
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) / Air Waybill (airfreight) / customs road manifest for GCC land-border transit evidencing physical departure from Oman
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed Packing List
  • Transport contract evidence (carrier invoice, charter party or freight booking confirmation) and delivery/shipment certificates
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT MT103 wire-credit advice from an Omani licensed bank proving receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer (required within the 180-day FX repatriation window)
  • For zero-rated digital services (3D art and rigging): signed service agreement or master services agreement, IP assignment/license confirmation, email/FTP/digital delivery logs, and the foreign customer's certificate of non-residency / foreign VAT ID or incorporation certificate
  • Value-chain substantiation: input VAT invoice ledger matching local Omani costs (rendering farm rentals, software licences, subcontractor invoices) to the zero-rated export contract to prove economic nexus and bona fide export intent

The Oman Tax Authority (OTA) has launched a phased national e-invoicing and real-time fiscal clearance mandate under the modernised integrated tax administration. Taxpayers above the applicable turnover cohort (phased by OTA decision, initially targeting large taxpayers with taxable turnover of OMR 5 million or more, then progressively lower bands) must issue structured e-invoices through OTA-approved electronic invoicing solutions integrated with the OTA clearance platform via authenticated REST APIs (JWT/OAuth2 secured). Each cleared invoice is stamped with a unique transaction UUID and a graphical QR digital stamp embedding the harmonised invoice data hash. The mandated formats follow OTA-declared XML (UBL 2.1-based) and JSON schemas; PDF-only non-integrated invoices will not satisfy the fiscal clearance requirement in the mandated phases. B2B, B2C, export, and intra-GCC invoices must all pass through the clearance mechanism where applicable; invoice data and digital audit trails must be archived for 10 years, and non-cleared invoices are treated as not validly issued for input VAT deduction purposes.

The OTA applies risk-based audit review to all zero-rated export claims. If the 90-day physical export window for goods or the documentary zero-rating conditions for offshore services are not fully substantiated, the supply is recharacterised as taxable at 5%, triggering assessment plus late-payment interest of 1% per month on the unpaid VAT balance. Where fraudulent zero-rating is established, OTA may retrospectively claw back all input VAT credits associated with the false export supplies and impose tax-evasion penalties under Articles 82–84 of the VAT Law: monetary fines of not less than OMR 5,000 and up to OMR 50,000, plus an additional penalty of up to 100%–200% of the evaded tax, computed as double-recovery of the VAT at stake. For egregious cases, criminal referral may lead to conviction of the SPC's directors/shareholders with imprisonment and director disqualification. The limitation period for reassessment is 5 years from the end of the relevant tax period; in fraud or wilful concealment cases, the OTA may extend the clawback period to up to 10 years, and voluntary disclosure protection is forfeited where the exporter acted with fraudulent intent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Oman exercises a fully convertible open-economy regime: current-account and capital-account transactions are liberalised, permitting 100% foreign ownership under the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2019) and unrestricted remittance of profits, dividends, and liquidation proceeds. The Omani Rial is pegged to the US dollar at 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD, eliminating USD-OMR conversion risk for a USD-revenue studio. There is no withholding tax on dividends, interest, or royalties paid to non-resident shareholders. Practical hurdles are limited to bank CDD documentation, CBO Balance-of-Payments statistical declarations, and the OTA compliance check; profit extraction requires audited financial statements and a valid corporate tax clearance certificate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline apply. A 100% foreign-owned SPC may retain all cross-border receipts from 3D art serviço exports in USD, EUR, or OMR accounts. Each credit must be matched to an issued invoice and a CBO Balance-of-Payments declaration filed by the commercial bank. Balances may remain offshore indefinitely in group treasuries, provided the OTA's transfer-pricing and anti-avoidance standards are met under the new Income Tax Law applicable to tax years commencing on or after 1 January 2024.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), operating under the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000), the Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism Law (Royal Decree 30/2016), and the CBO Payment Service Provider Regulatory Framework. The Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) issues the SPC Commercial Registration, and the Oman Tax Authority (OTA) must issue the Tax Registration Number before banking treasury services are activated.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Low feasibility. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer) are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Oman; CBO regulations require all resident-company treasury balances to reside in CBO-licensed banks. PayPal Business is permitted as a collection gateway for game-studio receipts, but PayPal holds no Omani banking licence, and the SPC must periodically sweep PayPal balances into a licensed Omani bank account for statutory revenue reporting; any attempt to use an offshore EMI as the primary clearance bank of record will trigger CDD refusal and potential breach of CBO payment rules.
