Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); electronic portal: Sede Electrónica / e-Tax 2.0 (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Sociedad Anónima, the DGI RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) is obtained by filing the Formulario de Inscripción RUC through a Panamanian resident agent, attaching certified articles of incorporation from the Registro Público, passport copies of officers/shareholders, evidence of registered fiscal address in Panama, and the appointment of a fiscal representative; registration must follow company formation and precedes any taxable transaction. A simplified RUC inscription procedure applies to non-resident digital service suppliers under Ley 176 de 2021.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election is available; the S.A. is mandatorily taxed at entity level on Panama-source income only (25% corporate income tax). Optional fiscal elections include adoption of full IFRS accounting basis with DGI approval, and election by foreign digital service suppliers to file under the simplified ITBMS registration regime; no fiscally transparent pass-through status exists for a Sociedad Anónima.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PAB - No general monetary turnover threshold: RUC and ITBMS inscription are mandatory from the first commercial or industrial activity in Panama. Non-resident digital service suppliers must register and charge ITBMS on supplies to Panamanian consumers with no de minimis threshold under Ley 176 de 2021.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) tangible goods physically exported from Panama with title and risk transferred to the non-resident buyer, evidenced by customs exit; (ii) services provided to non-residents that are effectively used or consumed wholly outside Panama; and (iii) offshore services benefiting non-resident recipients. The exporter must hold valid RUC status, issue the invoice with PAN-specific export documentation, and retain proof of departure within the period required by DGI.

  • Panama customs export declaration (Declaración Única de Exportación ratified by the Dirección Nacional de Aduanas)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill with Panamanian departure port/airport evidence
  • Commercial invoice (factura comercial) referencing the RUC and ITBMS zero-rated condition
  • Packing list and certificate of origin where claimed
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica) with phased DGI rollout: invoices must be issued through DGI-authorised electronic invoicing service providers (PES) with real-time transmission, digital validation/signing by DGI, and inclusion of a verification code/QR code; no traditional physical fiscal stamp is used. Taxpayers integrating through the DGI e-Tax 2.0 platform must transmit invoice sets and receive electronic confirmation for each document.

If DGI disallows zero-rating for missing or fraudulent export evidence, the ITBMS at 7% is clawed back on the gross invoice amount, with interest, late payment surcharges, and administrative fines under Articles 708-711 of the Código Fiscal (up to 100% of the omitted tax); deliberate falsification of customs or banking documents additionally triggers criminal tax fraud liability and disqualification of the RUC/export privileges, with liability extending to the resident agent acting in bad faith.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates under full dollarization with the US dollar as legal tender and the Balboa as a purely nominal currency unit. Current and capital account items are freely convertible, with no exchange control regime, no differentiated current/capital account treatment, no prior central bank approval for remittances, and no dividend or profit repatriation restrictions beyond standard corporate solvency and tax compliance requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement exists. Export receipts may be retained, freely used, or remitted abroad in US dollars without any mandatory percentage conversion into local currency.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) acting as the banking regulator; Banco Nacional de Panamá as the fiscal agent of the state; no central bank with monetary authority is present.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Panama permits regulated banks and certain supervised non-bank financial institutions to offer payment and e-money services, but no dedicated EMI licensing framework equivalent to the EU or UK is in place. Offshore digital EMIs are not permitted to conduct banking intermediation, take deposits, or clear funds for Panamanian customers without a local license or an authorized local bank partner. A remote-opening-only EMI without SBP authorization faces legal constraints; practical feasibility is limited to USD nostro-backed payment facilitators that use licensed Panamanian banks for underlying settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant incoming shareholder capital injections use the SWIFT MT103 wire protocol to a Panama USD nostro account. The wire must reference the corporate entity's name, tax ID (RUC), and account number. Panama imposes no exchange control declaration; however, banks require a purpose code or free-text explanation such as 'Capital Contribution pursuant to Shareholder Resolution' or 'Preorder Deposit for Commercial Sale'. Supporting documentation includes the shareholder resolution, board resolution, articles of incorporation, and proof of source of funds.Settlement of trade liabilities for raw materials and manufacturing services uses SWIFT MT103 (direct wire) for supplier payments and commercial documentary letters of credit (LC) for bulk procurement. Where an LC is used, it is typically an irrevocable at-sight LC confirmed by a US or Panamanian bank. For small supplier balances, MT103 is the approved clearing rail. All outbound disbursements require underlying commercial invoices, transport documents, and board approval, given the strict AML expectation for export-oriented manufacturing entities.No NDF or currency swap market is permissible or necessary because Panama's functional and settlement currency is the US dollar.Plain vanilla FX forwards may be offered by international banks outside Panama but require a genuine underlying commercial exposure in a foreign currency; if invoices are USD-denominated, no admissible forward is needed.Cross-currency swaps are permissible only for actual USD-to-foreign-currency operating exposure; purely speculative instruments are disallowed under standard Panamanian banking conduct rules.

Offshore digital EMIs are not permitted to conduct banking intermediation, take deposits, or clear funds for Panamanian customers without a local license or an authorized local bank partner. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% additional corporate dividend tax on distributions made from Panama-source profits that have already suffered the statutory 25% corporate income tax. If profits remain untaxed or are distributed under the small taxpayer/estimated-rate regime, the applicable rate is 10%.

Within 3 months after the fiscal year-end; for a calendar-year S.A., the statutory filing deadline is March 31 of the following year. Quarterly estimated payments of CITA are required on February 15, April 15, July 15, and October 15 based on projected annual taxable income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panama's transfer pricing rules apply to taxpayers with gross income of at least US$10 million or related-party transactions exceeding US$1 million in a fiscal year. Below the threshold, the taxpayer must still maintain contemporaneous evidence supporting arm's length pricing in intercompany transactions involving resin statue sales, IP royalties, and cross-border advances.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations, enacted under Law 254 of 2021 and its implementing rules, apply to Panamanian resident entities deriving active income. A polyresin statue S.A. must be able to demonstrate adequate physical premises, qualified local employees, operational costs proportional to its income, and local direction of core business activities. Foreign-owned S.A.s with no employees or premises but which claim Panamanian tax residency or hold valuable IP must file the economic substance declaration and must satisfy the applicable substance test or face substantial fines.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud penalties of up to 100% of the tax evaded, monetary fines, business closure during penal proceedings, revocation of the S.A.'s annual operational permit, and inclusion in the DGI's public tax default registry.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or instruct employees to conceal revenue are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, interest, and surcharges. Criminal tax proceedings may impose travel bans, attachment of personal assets, and imprisonment of the controlling director.
Shell Company Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations impose a minimum fine of US$100,000 for non-compliance with the substance declaration and annual notification rules. Continuing non-compliance can lead to monetary penalties per month, disqualification of the resident agent, and potential dissolution proceedings against the S.A.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for ESR fines where they authorized the failure to appoint a local substance supervisor, failed to maintain employment records, or caused the company to operate as a mere nominee facade. Courts may pierce the corporate veil and order director restitution of assets diverted to foreign beneficiaries.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Violations of Panama's AML Law No. 23 of 2015 and UAF regulations carry fines of up to US$1,000,000 per violation, suspension of the S.A.'s domicile capacity, blacklisting in the UAF public enforcement system, and criminal charges for money laundering with sentences of up to 12 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who tolerate AML deficiencies are subject to criminal prosecution, personal fines, travel bans, and confiscation of personal assets. Panamanian courts disregard corporate personality where the S.A. was used as a mere instrumentality for laundering payments, exposing the beneficial owner and directors to imprisonment.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Failure to withhold subjects the S.A. to a surcharge of 10% of the unwithheld tax plus monthly interest at the statutory rate. The royalty or interest expense may also be disallowed as a deduction, generating an additional 25% corporate income tax assessment.

Director personal liability: The director or administrator responsible for authorizing dividend distributions without payment of the complementary dividend tax is personally liable to the DGI for the unwithheld amount. In fraudulent withholding schemes, the director may also incur criminal liability and a personal travel ban pending tax collection.
