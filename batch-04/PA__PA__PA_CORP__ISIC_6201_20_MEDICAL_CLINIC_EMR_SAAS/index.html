Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas – Portal: https://www.dgi.gob.pa (also via Sede Electrónica: https://sede.dgi.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first appoint a resident legal representative and register with the Public Registry (Registro Público) to obtain corporate existence. Then, apply for a taxpayer identification number (RUC – Registro Único de Contribuyente) via the DGI's online portal or in person. The application requires the corporate charter, proof of registered agent, and identification of directors. The RUC is issued immediately upon completion of the online form and validation of documents. For VAT purposes, the same RUC serves as the VAT ID. Non-resident entities without a permanent establishment may need to register only for specific transactions, but for a local S.A. with business activity, full registration is mandatory.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — Foreign entities must first appoint a resident legal representative and register with the Public Registry (Registro Público) to obtain corporate existence. Then, apply for a taxpayer identification number (RUC – Registro Único de Contribuyente) via the DGI's online portal or in person. The application requires the corporate charter, proof of registered agent, and identification of directors. The RUC is issued immediately upon completion of the online form and validation of documents. For VAT purposes, the same RUC serves as the VAT ID. Non-resident entities without a permanent establishment may need to register only for specific transactions, but for a local S.A. with business activity, full registration is mandatory.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not offer check-the-box elections. However, entities may elect to be taxed as a Panama International Company (Sociedad Anónima) under the territorial regime, which taxes only Panama-source income. There is no hybrid mismatch election; Panama's tax law does not recognize hybrid entities. For transfer pricing, entities may elect to apply the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, but this is not a classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold; any person or entity engaged in taxable activities (sale of goods or services) in Panama must register for ITBMS. However, small taxpayers with annual gross income below USD 36,000 may opt for the simplified regime (Régimen Simplificado) with a reduced rate of 2% on sales, but this is optional. For standard VAT registration, there is no threshold; registration is mandatory from the first taxable operation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are zero-rated for ITBMS. For services, the service must be rendered to a non-resident client, and the benefit of the service must be deemed to occur outside Panama. For software/SaaS, the service must be provided to a foreign entity and not be used or consumed in Panama. The taxpayer must maintain evidence of the export, including the contract, proof of payment from abroad, and a declaration that the service is for foreign use. The DGI may require a certificate of non-residence of the client.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) – for goods, but for services, a 'Declaración de Servicios Exportados' is required
  • Commercial Invoice or Contract detailing the service
  • Proof of payment from abroad (e.g., SWIFT, bank transfer receipt)
  • Certificate of Non-Residence of the client (if applicable)
  • RUC registration and ITBMS registration certificate

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) for all taxpayers. The DGI's 'Sistema de Facturación Electrónica' (SFE) requires real-time transmission of invoices to the DGI via API or web portal. Taxpayers must obtain digital authorization (Aviso de Facturación) and use digital stamps (Código QR) on each invoice. The system requires integration with the DGI's web services (API) for validation and clearance. Since 2023, all taxpayers must issue electronic invoices; non-compliance results in fines and suspension of operations.

The DGI may audit export zero-rating claims. If the export is found to be fraudulent or not substantiated, the taxpayer is liable for the unpaid ITBMS (7%) plus interest (currently 1% per month) and fines up to 200% of the tax due. Additionally, criminal penalties may apply for tax fraud, including imprisonment. The DGI has a 5-year statute of limitations for assessments, but in cases of fraud, the period extends to 10 years. Retroactive clawbacks are common when documentation is incomplete.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized jurisdiction under which the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) is a nominal unit on par with the US Dollar (USD) and USD coin and paper currency circulate freely as legal tender. There are no capital controls, no exchange controls, and no differentiated current versus capital account restrictiveness. Profit repatriation, dividend distributions, and return of capital can be freely executed in USD through commercial banks without prior central bank authorization. The only material hurdle is corporate income tax compliance on Panama-source income, including a 10% dividend withholding tax on dividends declared from Panama-source profits and a 2% management fee tax on payments to non-resident directors, if applicable. The absence of an FX regime makes currency convertibility effectively 100% unrestricted for operational and treasury purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no statutory conversion, surrender, or repatriation requirements on export proceeds. Exporters, including software and cloud service providers, may receive invoices in USD or any other freely convertible currency, maintain offshore collections, and use export receipts for any lawful purpose. The only applicable duty is the obligation to keep proper books and records for tax purposes under the territorial income tax regime and to bank through SBP-licensed institutions when such funds are routed through Panamanian bank accounts.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama does not operate a central bank. Monetary and banking supervision is exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), while the official currency issuer is the National Bank of Panama (Banco Nacional de Panama) acting as the government's fiscal agent. For foreign exchange, there is no separate FX administration; the SBP regulates banking and forex market participants, and the Superintendencia de Sujetos no Bancarios supervises non-bank financial entities, including foreign exchange exchange houses.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Panama does not recognize the EU EMI (Electronic Money Institution) passport, and offshore digital EMI entities without an SBP banking or fintech license cannot establish a local settlement account. In practice, a licensed Panamanian digital bank or a fully authorized fintech payment processor (e.g., STRIKE-backed USD settlement rails, or a SBP-licensed 'red multibanco' participant) may be used as a local clearing agent, but this requires an SBP-filed outsourcing arrangement and a compliant anti-money laundering KYC file. The most practical route for a foreign-owned EMR SaaS is to maintain a Tier 1 commercial bank account and deploy Stripe Billing as a front-end payment rail while clearing through the licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing follows a normal USD wire transfer via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or investor account to the Panamanian entity's local account. Since Panama uses US dollar clearing, all inbound incoming wires settle through Fedwire, CHIPS, or correspondent banking networks, and the receiving Panamanian bank will typically request a Funds Receipt Form. The recommended payment purpose description is 'Equity capital contribution to Panamanian subsidiary — No services rendered' or 'Paid-in capital deposit under Law 32 of 1927, S.A. incorporation'. Supporting verification includes the board resolution approving the capital increase, a sworn statement before the SBP-compliant compliance officer, and identification of the ultimate source of funds. If the remittance exceeds existing paid-in capital limits, the entity must update the registry at the Public Registry (Registro Público) within the statutory corporate filing window and ensure the increase is reflected in the tax registration.Outbound trade disbursements for licensing fees, software vendor payments, and cloud infrastructure settlements are executed via SWIFT MT103 credit transfers for direct commercial payments and via MT202 for interbank reimbursements when routing through a correspondent bank. For high-value medical clinic software licensing to Panamanian clinics, the preferred instrument is a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) where the Panamanian buyer initiates a documentary LC through the issuing bank, with the beneficiary collecting against presentation of an electronic software license certificate or commercial invoice. Cross-border cash concentration may be achieved by using a single USD sweeping account in Panama with a US correspondent bank, thereby reducing the clearing time to same-day via Fedwire and enabling straight-through-processing to Stripe Billing vaults. No local FX conversion is required because both the remitting parent and the Panamanian S.A. operate in USD.USD/PAB spot and forward contracts (where the PAB is conceptually converted at 1.00, these are primarily notional).Cross-currency swaps (USD against EUR, CHF, or LATAM currencies) with SBP-licensed counterparties.FX forwards against non-convertible regional currencies (e.g., COP, MXN) for head-office settlements.FX options (plain vanilla call/put) available from large international banks through the Panamanian branch.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on currencies with offshore restrictions, permitted when arranged through a global bank and settled in USD outside Panama; although PAB is fixed, NDF instruments are not prohibited by Panamanian law.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends paid by a Panama S.A. out of Panama-source profits. Since risk profits carry an effective pre-dividend cost of 25%, the combined corporate-plus-dividend burden on a fully distributed Panama-source profit equals 32.5% of pre-tax profit (25% CIT + 10% WHT on after-tax dividend).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Balboa (PAB) / USD par: Local File must be prepared when related-party transactions exceed B/.5,000,000 in the tax period; Master File applies when group consolidated revenue exceeds B/.50,000,000; CbC reporting applies to significant groups with consolidated revenue above B/.850,000,000. The annual TP Informative Return (Form 380) is additionally triggered when Panama-source gross revenues exceed B/.12,000,000. For the current EMR SaaS business, the realistically critical threshold is B/.5,000,000 of intra-group clinical licensing/hosting flows.

Economic substance scope: Panama imposes Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) under Law 70 of 2019 and Executive Decree 582 of 2021 only on 'relevant activities' (banking, insurance, IP holding, distribution, shipping, logistics, HQ, etc.). An active EMR clinical software vendor is outside the ESR perimeter. However, for territorial tax certainty and against the 'zero-tax shell S.A.' archetype, the company must demonstrate continuous Panamanian management, board meetings, medical-support staff, cloud procurement decisions, and an actual registered office. If the S.A. owns the EMR IP, all critical IP decision-making and exploitation functions must be in Panama; otherwise a foreign taxing authority or DGI can recharacterise the entity as a sham, re-source the SaaS income, and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Fraud / Evasion

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 50% to 100% of the omitted corporate income tax plus 1% monthly interest; criminal tax-fraud charges punishable by imprisonment from 2 to 5 years and fines from two to ten times the tax covered.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign fraudulent returns are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes, interest and penalties; courts may issue additional travel bans (prohibición de salida) and attach personal assets for civil restitution.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: DGI adjusts taxable income to arm's length, imposes a 100% surcharge on the tax undercharged for abusive related-party transactions, and levies documentation fines of B/.1,000-B/.5,000 per omitted TP report.

Director personal liability: Managers/tax directors who sign non-arm's-length contracts are personally responsible for the DGI adjustment if the company is insolvent; in fraud cases, personal criminal liability and travel restrictions follow.
Shell Company / Sham Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: DGI and courts may disregard the entity for tax purposes, re-source all EMR SaaS income to a taxable permanent establishment, impose the 25% ISLR plus recargo of 50%, suspend the corporate licence, and order dissolution of the S.A.

Director personal liability: Under the doctrine of 'levantamiento del velo societario', directors and shadow owners are personally liable for corporate tax debt; criminal sanctions for fraud and court-imposed travel bans may accompany the civil recovery.
AML / Beneficial Ownership (FATF)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to B/.500,000 under Law 129 of 2020 and Law 23 of 2015 for UBO/AML omissions; the S.A. may be included in the public beneficial-ownership non-compliant list, incurring commercial-banking restrictions and director disqualification.

Director personal liability: De facto directors and compliance officers can be criminally prosecuted for money-laundering facilitation (5 to 12 years imprisonment), personal fines, seizure of foreign assets, and travel bans while under investigation.
Dividend WHT / Unlawful Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent is liable for the 10% unpaid WHT plus a recargo of 10% to 25%, interest at 1% monthly, and, if the distribution was made in resort of fraud, criminal fines; the dividend payment may be clawed back for creditor protection.

Director personal liability: Directors authorising unlawful dividends are jointly and severally liable to the tax authority for the WHT and to creditors for the distributed value; serious or repeated violations expose them to criminal securities/fraud liability and travel bans.
