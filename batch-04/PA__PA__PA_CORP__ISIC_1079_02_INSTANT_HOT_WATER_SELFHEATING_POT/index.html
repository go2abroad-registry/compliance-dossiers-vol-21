Authorized capital of at least US$10,000 divided into 100 common shares of US$100.00 par value is the customary Law 32 de 1927 structure. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) – official portal: https://www.dgi.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must (i) appoint a local resident agent and legal representative; (ii) file incorporation or registration documents with the Registro Público de Panamá; (iii) apply for RUC through DGI's SISTEMATIZA e-services portal or in person at a DGI office; (iv) submit certified copy of the public registry certificate, the article of incorporation, and the legal representative's identity documents; (v) obtain the RUC and, if engaging in taxable activities, register for ITBMS by filing the corresponding fiscal registration form (SIF) with the DGI.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope — A foreign entity must (i) appoint a local resident agent and legal representative; (ii) file incorporation or registration documents with the Registro Público de Panamá; (iii) apply for RUC through DGI's SISTEMATIZA e-services portal or in person at a DGI office; (iv) submit certified copy of the public registry certificate, the article of incorporation, and the legal representative's identity documents; (v) obtain the RUC and, if engaging in taxable activities, register for ITBMS by filing the corresponding fiscal registration form (SIF) with the DGI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Panamanian law. A Sociedad Anónima is a corporation subject to entity-level taxation on Panama-source income only. Foreign-source income remains outside the Panamanian tax base. No hybrid mismatch election is available; taxpayers may elect the simplified small taxpayer regime (pequeño contribuyente) if annual gross turnover is below the statutory threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 0 – no minimum threshold for corporate entities; any person engaged in taxable activities in Panama, including foreign entities with a local distribution or fulfillment presence, must register for ITBMS. Small taxpayers with annual gross income below USD 36,000 may opt for a simplified regime, but such election is not available to a fully constituted S.A. engaged in manufacturing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (including self-heating ready meals manufactured in Panama) are subject to a 0% ITBMS rate provided the seller qualifies as the exporter and the goods physically leave Panamanian territory. Services rendered to non-residents, or performed and used entirely abroad, may also be zero-rated if the economic benefit is located outside Panama. The zero-rate applies through the normal invoice mechanism with the corresponding export evidence; no prior DGI approval is needed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA – Declaración Única de Aduanas)
  • Bill of Lading and/or Airway Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (comprobante de remesa o entrada de divisas)

Panama has a mandatory electronic invoicing (e-factura) regime for all ITBMS taxpayers. Invoices must be generated and validated in real-time through DGI's SISTEMATIZA platform using the approved API/web services. Each invoice must carry a digital stamp (código de barras or QR code) from DGI. No independent physical fiscal clearance is used, but electronic data must be transmitted within 15 days after issuance. The factory sale of self-heating meals will require e-invoicing from the ERP to the DGI system.

The DGI may deny export zero-rating and impose a 25% surcharge on unpaid ITBMS plus annual interest (currently 1% monthly) if export documentation is incomplete. Administrative fines may reach 100% of the unpaid tax (Código Fiscal Article 709). Fraudulent export claims, including falsified bills of lading or artificial FX remittance, expose the entity to criminal tax fraud penalties under the Código Fiscal (Articles 310‑G and related provisions) and retroactive clawback of all ITBMS input credits claimed in connection with the non-qualifying export, with interest from the date of filing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama uses the US dollar as full legal tender. There is no separate capital-account or current-account convertibility regime and no foreign exchange statute requiring Central Bank approval. Profit repatriation, dividend remittances, and liquidation proceeds are freely transferable through the banking system. The only practical hurdles are standard AML/KYC documentary controls and the territorial-source taxation of Panamanian-derived income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion timeline applies to export receipts. Exporters may retain, hold, or deploy USD and other foreign currency proceeds freely. Banks submit only mandatory suspicious-transaction reports under Panama's AML/CFT regime; there is no obligation to force conversion into any local currency.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Superintendency of Banks of Panama) as the prudential banking regulator; Banco Nacional de Panamá as the Government's fiscal agent. No domestic central bank with monetary authority exists as Panama is fully dollarized with no local currency issuance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate feasibility through licensed local fintech or banking-as-a-service sponsors. Pure offshore digital EMI accounts unconnected to a Panamanian regulated entity are not viable for corporate treasury clearing. Foreign EMIs require either a Panamanian fintech license, a local commercial banking sponsor, or an association with a licensed payment processor to obtain USD clearing and local IBAN-like settlement capabilities.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% on dividends paid out of profits already subject to Panamanian corporate income tax; 10% on dividends paid out of exempt or non-taxed profits. A final withholding is required on all dividend distributions, irrespective of shareholder residence.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is due within 3 months after the fiscal year-end; for a calendar year-end company, the statutory deadline is 31 March. A one-month extension is available when a prepayment of 10% of estimated tax is made.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 500,000 (aggregate related-party transactions in the fiscal year) triggers mandatory Transfer Pricing Local File and Form 070 disclosure. A Master File is required when the multinational group consolidated revenue exceeds USD 50 million. Taxpayers with annual gross revenue above USD 3 million must maintain a contemporaneous TP study even without a DTA.

Economic substance scope: Panama has not enacted formal Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, Panama's tax residence rules, Law 23/2015 beneficial ownership registry, Decree 406/2019, and OECD/MLI compliant provisions require a Panamanian S.A. performing food manufacturing or self-heating meal assembly to maintain an actual physical office, staff, local decision-making, and real economic activity. A company without substance but claiming Panama-source or treaty benefits is exposed to anti-abuse recharacterization, denial of DTA relief, and shell company sanctions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine equal to 100%-300% of the omitted tax, mandatory default interest, and imprisonment of 2 to 5 years under Articles 720-724 of the Código Fiscal.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil pierceable when the S.A. is a mere instrumentality. Directors and officers may be jointly liable for the unpaid tax, subjected to asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Abuse of Territoriality

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of foreign-source income as Panamanian-source by the DGI, imposition of 25% tax plus 10% surcharge, denial of treaty benefits, and exclusion from the Beneficial Ownership registry with fines up to USD 750,000 under Law 23/2015.

Director personal liability: Directors and registered agents face civil liability for resulting tax deficiencies, pecuniary sanctions, disqualification from acting as directors in Panama, and criminal liability for false declarations.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of USD 10,000 per infraction under Decree 406/2019, freezing of bank accounts, and revocation of the commercial license. The S.A. may also be struck from the Public Registry as a sanctioning measure.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent may be personally fined, barred from practicing in Panama, and held criminally liable for money laundering where the corporate vehicle is used to mask illicit funds.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: DGI adjustment under OECD-approved methods, penalties from 20% to 100% of the adjusted tax liability, default interest, and publication as a non-cooperative taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors participating in the pricing arrangement may be jointly liable for adjusted tax and penalties; flagrant disregard of arm's-length standards can trigger criminal tax fraud prosecution and civil restitution.
