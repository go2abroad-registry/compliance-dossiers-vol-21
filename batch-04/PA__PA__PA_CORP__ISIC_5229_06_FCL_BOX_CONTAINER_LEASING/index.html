Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; official portal: Sistema Integrado de Tributación (SIT) at https://sit.mef.gob.pa; customs oversight: Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Panamanian S.A. is automatically assigned its RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) upon registration with the Registro Público de Panamá under Law 32/1927; activation requires the registered agent to file the initial RUC data in SIT, obtain a digital tax mailbox (Casillero Fiscal), and procure a digital signature (Firma Electrónica) from AIG or an accredited certifier; no physical presence or minimum capital is required; foreign shareholders provide only a notarized power of attorney and passport copies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under Panamanian law. Available statutory elections: (1) transitional 50/50 tax-credit acceleration on qualifying movable assets (including container boxes) acquired in 2025-2026 under Law 365/2024; (2) voluntary ITBMS registration as 'Responsable' when below the B/.36,000 threshold to enable export zero-rating; (3) election to apply the alternative minimum tax regime under Article 699-A C.F.; (4) exporter-authorization election enabling ITBMS input-credit recovery.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.36,000 Balboas (pegged 1:1 to USD) of annual gross turnover from taxable activities; compulsory registration for habitual sellers exceeding the threshold and for Gran Contribuyente; voluntary registration allowed for exporters of container-leasing services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and offshore services qualify for 0% ITBMS. Leased ISO containers departing Panamanian ports on international voyages qualify as exported goods when supported by a customs DUM; container-lease revenue to non-resident shipping lines using the boxes outside Panama qualifies as services used and enjoyed abroad under Article 1056 C.F.; the S.A. must hold authorized exporter status and file the corresponding zero-rated electronic invoice in SIT; input ITBMS on related costs may be recovered through the exporter credit-refund mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Mercancías - DUM) filed in the SIA customs system
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent multimodal transport document evidencing international transit of the container
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) or SWIFT/ACH evidence showing lease payment credited to Panama
  • Zero-rated FEV/FES electronic invoice containing the SIT security code
  • Exporter registration certificate and SIT Responsible-taxpayer status proof
  • Packing list and Equipment Interchange Report (EIR) where applicable

Mandatory electronic invoicing via SIT (Facturación Electrónica) for all taxpayers; exporters claiming zero-rating must issue FEV/FES invoices validated through API-based real-time 'convalidación' with SIT, producing an alphanumeric security code and QR code; deferred batch clearance is limited to small taxpayers and must be transmitted within 5 calendar days; lease transactions must be electronically matched to customs export declarations.

DGI and Aduanas conduct joint audits of zero-rating claims; failure to substantiate exports or to repatriate funds within 180 days triggers clawback of the full ITBMS at the current rate, monthly interest of 10% per annum, fines of 10-30% for late payment, and a 100% surcharge for false documentation; willful evasion incurs criminal penalties of 3-5 years' imprisonment under Article 757-O C.F. plus inclusion in the public Lista de Deudores Morosos.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full currency convertibility with no separate legal tender issuance. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible, and there are no exchange control restrictions on inbound investment, ongoing funding, or profit remittance. Container-leasing revenue earned outside Panama is generally treated as territorial-source income and may be repatriated without Panamanian tax, provided proper corporate filings are maintained. Dividends derived from Panamanian-source income are subject to a 10% withholding tax; foreign-source income dividends are non-taxable. No prior central bank approval or exchange allocation requirement applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender rules exist. Exporters and service providers, including container leasing companies, may retain proceeds in U.S. dollars or any other currency without mandatory conversion. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation or forced sale of foreign currency. All foreign earnings can be held offshore at the discretion of the company.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no central bank. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the principal banking and financial-system regulator. Exchange control is functionally absent because the U.S. dollar is legal tender and the Panamanian Balboa is a fractional currency pegged 1:1 to the U.S. dollar. International monetary relations are governed under a monetary agreement with the United States.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Mercury Fit: High

Moderate feasibility with legal constraints. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not directly licensed by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá. A Panama-domiciled corporate entity may open accounts with regulated foreign EMIs only if those EMIs comply with correspondent banking relationships and AML/CFT protocols. For cross-border container leasing operations, U.S.- or EU-licensed EMIs (e.g., Payoneer, Mercury, Currencycloud) can be used as collection/disbursement channels, but they are not a substitute for a Panamanian banking account for statutory capital maintenance or legal-merchant registration. The onshore regulatory requirement is that any entity carrying out financial intermediation in Panama must be licensed by the SBP; using an EMI for treasury purposes triggers no Panama licensing but exposes the company to the EMI's jurisdictional KYC/AML requests and potential access restrictions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfers for shareholder capital injection must be made via SWIFT MT103 with precise narrative. Recommended purpose code: 'Capital Contribution / Equity Injection' and, for statistical reporting, reference the local investment code 'Inversión Directa' (Panama does not have a formal FX approval requirement). Receiving bank must be a Panamanian licensed bank. The sender's name and account number must match the shareholder of record. For capital from a related company, a loan agreement is required to support a debt injection, while for equity, board minutes from the Panamanian corporation authorizing the issuance of shares must be filed with the bank. Inbound funds are verified via the bank's Know-Your-Depositor process; the bank may request a certificate of seal and incorporation, the corporate ID card, and a sworn statement of first capital contribution.Clearing rails for trade settlements are primarily through the SWIFT network using MT103 for direct wire transfers and MT700/MT707 for Commercial Letters of Credit (LC). For recurring lease payments, the company may also use Transmittal Letters and direct deposits. Panama's high-value clearing system (ACH) is available for local payments in U.S. dollars, but cross-border payments require correspondent banks (typically U.S. dollar clearings via the Federal Reserve or through a Panamanian intermediary). The company should maintain an operational account with a bank that provides trade finance desks, enabling issuance of standby LCs or performance guarantees for container depot operators. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding applies to dividends paid out of Panamanian-source profits. A 5% additional surcharge applies to dividends from CAIR-taxed profits. Dividends paid from foreign-source profits are not subject to dividend withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before 31 March following the fiscal year-end for calendar-year taxpayers; entities with a different fiscal year must file within three months after fiscal year close.

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall

Late filing incurs a 10% surcharge on the tax due plus monthly interest equal to the prime rate plus 10 percentage points. Failure to file for two consecutive years is a criminal tax offense. Administrative fines for inaccurate or non-filed transfer pricing forms range from USD 250 to USD 50,000. The statute of limitations for non-filing is 8 years.

Non-resident corporations with no Panamanian-source income may have paid USD 0 advance tax; otherwise claim credit for actual installments. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and transfer pricing affidavit (Form 290) are triggered when the aggregate related-party transactions exceed USD 1 million per fiscal year. A Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue over USD 15 million. The documentation must be in Spanish, prepared under the arm's-length principle, and filed with the DGI within 6 months after the return due date.

Economic substance scope: Although Panama has no formal Economic Substance Regulations identical to other Caribbean jurisdictions, its income tax law and the SEM requirements incorporate an economic substance and source-based test. A container leasing S.A. that maintains a physical office, employees, and central management control in Panama while leasing to Panamanian users will be treated as a Panamanian taxpayer. Pure letter-box operations suspect of being a sham are vulnerable to reassessment, fines for incorrect filing, and potential criminal exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to 50%–200% of the omitted tax under Article 816 of the Fiscal Code, plus interest. Criminal tax fraud under the Criminal Code (Law 14 of 2007) carries imprisonment of 1 to 5 years and a fine. Loss of tax refund offsets and mandatory reassessment with 25% rate.

Director personal liability: A director who signs a false return may be prosecuted as a co-author of tax fraud, subject to fines, civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years. Panama permits 'piercing the corporate veil' under Article 52 of the Civil Code where the corporation is a mere instrument of fraud.
Shell Company (Lack of Economic Substance)

Statutory sanction: DGI may disregard the corporate entity and assess tax at 100% on attributed non-resident income; fines under the Tax Code for sham invoicing and improper accounting; revocation of the resident agent license; blacklisting as a high-risk entity by the DGI; inclusion in negative public registries.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered by courts to pay unpaid corporate taxes from personal assets; potential criminal sanction for using the company for money laundering or tax evasion; travel restrictions and asset freezing orders can be imposed.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering (Law 23 of 2015)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to USD 5 million against the entity; criminal seizure of container fleets if linked to laundering proceeds; referral to the UAF and criminal indictment for money laundering under Articles 254 and 255 of the Criminal Code; imprisonment from 5 to 10 years; cancellation of the entity registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who knowingly participate in laundering funds face imprisonment up to 10 years, personal fines, forfeiture of bank accounts, travel bans, and disqualification as corporate officers.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failures

Statutory sanction: Fine for failure to file: 10% of the undocumented transaction amount or up to USD 50,000; a 5% to 100% penalty adjustment may be applied to the intercompany charge plus interest of the avoided tax; the DGI may impose an estimated deemed profit and disallow foreign tax credits.

Director personal liability: Officers who execute documents knowing them to be false may be personally prosecuted for perjury, have a qualified criminal fine, and be barred from serving as officers of Panamanian corporations for 5 years.
