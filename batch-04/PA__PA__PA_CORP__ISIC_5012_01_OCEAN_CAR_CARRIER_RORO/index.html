Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance of Panama; online portal and electronic filing system: DGI e-Tax 2.0 (https://etax.dgi.gob.pa) and institutional site https://www.dgi.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first be validly incorporated or admitted in Panama (or have a resident agent and a local branch) and registered in the Registro Público de Panamá. The appointed local resident agent then files the DGI taxpayer registration application ('Aviso de Inscripción de Contribuyente') and supporting notarized/charter documents via the e-Tax 2.0 portal or in person at a DGI office. The DGI issues the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente), which is the compulsory tax identification number for both income tax and ITBMS purposes; an electronic user/password or digital-signature credential for e-Tax 2.0 is issued simultaneously.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope. A Panama Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) is a separate juridical person taxable at the entity level on Panamanian-source income at the 25% corporate income tax rate. Foreign-source income is outside the Panamanian taxing jurisdiction unless specifically recharacterized. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope. A Panama Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) is a separate juridical person taxable at the entity level on Panamanian-source income at the 25% corporate income tax rate. Foreign-source income is outside the Panamanian taxing jurisdiction unless specifically recharacterized. The entity is not eligible for pass-through treatment, and no partnership-style flow-through classification is available for an S.A.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Panamanian domestic law; tax classification follows the entity's legal form. For eligible taxpayers, Panamanian law offers specific elections: (i) SEM (Multinational Headquarters) regime, Law 41 of 2007, taxing qualifying headquarters services on a 5% deemed base; (ii) special tonnage tax election for shipping vessels registered under Panama's Merchant Marine fleet, which replaces income tax on international maritime transport; (iii) Panama-Pacifico Special Economic Zone employment and investment incentives; (iv) small-taxpayer simplified ITBMS/income regime for annual gross income not exceeding B/.36,000; and (v) deferred partial taxation for free-zone operations (Zonas Francas). Elections are made before the DGI and require prior authorization where indicated.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Panama uses the balboa (PAB) at a fixed 1:1 exchange rate with the U.S. dollar. There is no monetary threshold for ITBMS registration: any person selling movable goods or providing services in Panamanian territory, or otherwise making taxable supplies in Panama, must register for the RUC/ITBMS before commencing operations. A simplified regime is available for small taxpayers with annual gross income not exceeding PAB 36,000 (approx. USD 36,000).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and offshore services are zero-rated (0% ITBMS) where: (a) the recipient of the goods/services is outside Panama; (b) goods are physically removed through an authorized Panamanian customs office and the export declaration is filed by the seller; (c) for Ro-Ro ocean vehicle transport, the carried vehicles depart from a Panamanian port and are released abroad, or the service is provided wholly between foreign ports, so the income is from international transport; (d) consideration is paid from abroad and remitted to Panama; and (e) the exporter is duly registered with RUC and, where required, in the DGI export registry. Input ITBMS related to zero-rated exports is creditable and may be refunded by DGI if credits exceed taxable activity liabilities.

  • Declaración Única de Aduanas (DUA)/Single Export Customs Declaration, electronically filed in SISCOM
  • Original or telex-released Ocean Bill of Lading evidencing the carriage contract and destination
  • Ro-Ro cargo/vehicle manifest (Manifiesto de Carga) and stowage plan for the vehicle shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a Panamanian bank (or SWIFT MT103/MT202) showing receipt of export proceeds in Panama
  • Final commercial invoice and packing list issued by the Panamanian exporter
  • Charter party or booking confirmation if the Ro-Ro vessel is operated under charter or slot charter

Panama mandates electronic invoicing for all registered taxpayers through DGI Resolution No. 201-1414 of 2021 and its amendments. Taxpayers must use DGI-authorized invoicing software or the DGI's free online 'facturador' to issue electronic receipt (comprobante) transactions. The DGI pre-authorizes invoice series and, in the standard clearance model, receives each XML over the e-Tax 2.0 API in near-real-time for validation; a digital fiscal stamp/authorization code is generated online. No private printed fiscal stamps are accepted. API integration requires an active RUC and digital signature; invoices must also be linked to the export customs system (SISCOM/DUA) for zero-rated export evidence.

DGI and Panama Customs cross-check zero-rating claims against customs exit records, bills of lading, and bank remittance trails. False or unsupported zero-rating gives rise to: (i) reversal of the zero rate with ITBMS at 7% plus 1.5% monthly interest; (ii) surcharges and fines under Articles 740-741 of the Código Fiscal (up to B/.10,000 and up to 100% of the omitted tax); (iii) criminal tax-evasion penalties of 3 to 7 years' imprisonment under Law 70 of 2019 if intentional; (iv) customs smuggling penalties if the export declaration is false; and (v) loss of the exporter registration and electronic invoicing license. The statute of limitations for reassessment is generally three years, extended to six years in cases of fraud or omission of declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Panamanian balboa (PAB) is nominal legal tender at par with the United States dollar (USD), which circulates as actual currency. There are no exchange controls, no capital account restrictions, and no current account restrictions. Profit remittances, dividends, and return of capital may be freely and swiftly executed in USD, subject only to Panamanian income tax on Panama-source earnings and the standard 10% dividend withholding tax on distributions paid by a corporation domiciled in Panama.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None are imposed. Panama has no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no forced repatriation period for export proceeds. Shipping revenue earned in USD or other currencies may be retained offshore, deposited in any foreign bank account, or held in an approved panamanian bank without central bank reporting beyond normal AML/beneficial-owner obligations.

Central bank supervision: Panama does not operate a central bank. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) is the banking regulator, and Banco Nacional de Panamá (National Bank of Panama) acts as fiscal agent and banker for the Republic, while the Ministry of Economy and Finance supervises monetary integration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Panama does not yet issue full digital bank licences to foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs). Offshore fintechs and EMIs can operate only through a licensed Panamanian bank or licensed payment agent, and they must comply with the SBP's Payment Systems Law and Comprehensive AML/CFT regulation. A foreign EMI cannot open a core settlement bank account in Panama without a local corporate presence and regulator-approved outsourcing arrangement. For a vessel-owning RoRo carrier, an EMI is advisable only as a supplementary FX-processing channel, never as the primary treasury account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard Panamanian corporate income tax (ISR) under Article 699 of the Código Fiscal; Law 113/2019 applied transitional reduced rates of 15% (FY2020), 18% (FY2021) and 20% (FY2022), reverting to 25% from FY2023 onward.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% standard dividend withholding tax on distributions by Panamanian S.A.s to shareholders; 5% preferential statutory rate applies when the distribution is sourced from earnings already subject to the 25% corporate ISR; undistributed profit at the S.A. level bears no tax (no CFC taxation) until declared.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD/Balboa (Panama uses the Balboa at 1:1 parity with USD): taxpayers with related-party transactions exceeding USD 250,000 annually must file the transfer-pricing informative annex with Form 1200 and maintain the Local File/TP study; Master File and CbCR apply to MNE groups with EUR 750 million consolidated revenue (Law 52/2016 implementing BEPS Action 13).

Economic substance scope: Panama Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in force from fiscal year 2019 (implementing decrees under Código Fiscal and MLI commitments; OECD/EU-aligned). Shipping (maritime transport of vehicles by Ro-Ro vessels) is a 'relevant activity': the S.A. must demonstrate in Panama (i) strategic management and board meetings with local quorum/minutes, (ii) resident employees or qualified outsourced managers, (iii) physical premises, and (iv) proportionate operating expenditure. An annual ESR declaration must be filed with the DGI; the Panama Maritime Authority registry file must show the vessel's commercial management office in Panama, and intra-group bareboat or time-charter contracts must be benchmarked at arm's length.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fiscal Fraud)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fiscal fraud under Código Fiscal Articles 711–718 and Código Penal Article 254: fines up to USD 200,000 and imprisonment from 5 to 10 years; the DGI concurrently imposes a 10% surcharge, 1% monthly interest, and 100% re-assessment of undeclared income with penalty tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent are jointly and severally liable for the suppressed ISR and WHT; criminal conviction triggers asset forfeiture, travel bans, disqualification from acting as Panamanian fiduciary, and extradition risk for non-resident beneficial owners.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Breach

Statutory sanction: ESR non-compliance fines of up to USD 100,000 per fiscal year, mandatory rectification within 30 days, and cancellation/suspension of the DGI RUC and the Panama maritime registry flag; re-characterization of the foreign-source revenue as Panamanian-source taxable income with back taxes plus interest (10% surcharge).

Director personal liability: Statutory deregistration of the S.A. and public blacklisting; directors/officers face disqualification and personal liability for the ESR declaration and unpaid taxes; the corporate veil may be lifted to reach shipping shareholders' personal assets.
AML / Concealment of Beneficial Ownership (Law 23/2015)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to USD 1,000,000 per violation under Law 23/2015; freezing of the Panamanian bank account, independent AML audit at the corporation's cost, and criminal money-laundering prosecution with imprisonment of 5 to 12 years (Código Penal Articles 254–256).

Director personal liability: Directors, nominees, and the resident agent incur personal criminal liability for knowingly masking UBOs; travel bans and provisional deposition of passports are standard pre-charge measures; extradition treaties (including MLATs with the US and EU) enable cross-border prosecution of the ultimate Ro-Ro shipowner.
Nominee Abuse / Corporate Veil Piercing

Statutory sanction: Corporate Law (Law 32/1927) sanctions and DGI anti-abuse re-characterization: fines of USD 10,000–50,000, nullification of treaty benefits and deduction disallowances, plus DTA/MLI principal-purpose-test denial (nullifying the 5% dividend rate), converted into a 10% dividend WHT levy on deemed distributions.

Director personal liability: Panamanian courts apply 'levantamiento del velo corporativo' where the S.A. is a mere alter ego; directors become personally liable for the full ISR, WHT, penalties and third-party claims, with restraint orders over vessels/personal assets, travel bans, and custodial sentences for perjury before the maritime and tax authorities.
