UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Law 23 of 27 April 2015 establishes the national AML/CFT framework and designates the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) as the Panamanian financial intelligence unit. The beneficial-ownership (UBO) disclosure regime is governed by Law 129 of 2020 (Registro de Beneficiarios Finales), as modified by Law 254 of 2021. Complementary instruments include Law 41 of 2007 (tax information exchange), Executive Decree No. 363 of 2013 (know-your-client obligations and suspicious-transaction reporting), and UAF/regulatory guidelines applicable to designated non-financial businesses and professions (DNFBPs).
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A statutory registered office is mandatory and the corporation must maintain a resident agent in Panama. For this industry, a non-licensed fintech personal-wealth analytics and portfolio-tracking cloud application, the registered agent's physical office fully satisfies the registered-office requirement. A virtual office is permissible because no on-the-ground staff, server infrastructure or banking/securities license is required; contractually, the registered agent must accept service of process and retain statutory records in Panama.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; electronic portal: SEDE (Sistema Electrónico de Declaraciones de Impuestos) at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

For a Panama Sociedad Anónima: (1) Execute public deed through a licensed Panamanian attorney and register the company at the Registro Público de Panamá; (2) File the Aviso de Operación (business operation notice) with the DGI through SEDE; (3) Obtain the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) and the DGI registration certificate; (4) Elect ITBMS taxpayer obligations and apply for electronic invoice authorization; (5) Appoint a resident agent and fiscal representation with local address; (6) Open a Panamanian bank account. For a non-resident digital service provider supplying SaaS/fintech services to Panama consumers: register remotely with the DGI under Ley 67 de 2022 to obtain a simplified RUC before commencing taxable activities, charge ITBMS at the statutory rate, and appoint a local tax representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election exists in Panama. Available elections and regimes include: (i) territorial-source income allocation method under Article 711 of the Código Fiscal; (ii) ITBMS group registration (Grupo de Interés Económico) for related taxpayers; (iii) optional Simplified Small-Taxpayer Regime where annual gross receipts do not exceed USD 36,000; (iv) special economic regimes (SEM headquarters, ETE, City of Knowledge) if the entity obtains the relevant license; (v) Remote DGI registration and simplified compliance for non-resident digital service providers under Ley 67 de 2022.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 0 — No general de minimis ITBMS registration threshold applies: any person engaged in taxable transfers of goods or services, including corporations, must register upon commencement of activity. Non-resident digital service providers must register in Panama before their first B2C digital sale (Ley 67 de 2022) irrespective of turnover. Only small individual taxpayers may optionally elect the Simplified Regime (annual gross receipts ≤ USD 36,000), which is not available to a standard S.A.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Panama zero-rates (0% ITBMS) the export of goods and services. A software-based financial portfolio tracking service qualifies as an exported service where: (i) the recipient is a non-resident entity; (ii) the service is effectively used or consumed abroad; (iii) the consideration is paid by a non-resident; and (iv) the supplier maintains contemporaneous export substantiation. The Panamanian exporter should register in the DGI service-exporter registry and, where applicable, obtain the Certificado de Compra de Servicios al Exterior or equivalent sworn certification. Panama does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline (the USD is legal tender and there are no exchange controls); the 180-day benchmark is applied by DGI auditors as the prudent documentary window for receipt of external consideration in the Panamanian banking system, after which a documented justification is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación filed through VUPEA/SIGA)
  • Commercial Invoice with export-specific reference and zero-rating indication
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing physical departure (for goods)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT/MT103 or bank credit advice for services)
  • Service contract, dated and executed, evidencing foreign use and non-resident beneficiary
  • Certificate of Exportation of Services (formulario DGI) where required

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Documento Electrónico / Factura Electrónica) through the DGI e-fiscal infrastructure: taxpayers must obtain DGI-authorized invoice ranges, apply digital signature and QR stamping, and transmit XML invoice files (DataInt) to the SEDE platform on a periodic near-real-time basis. Higher-volume and obliged taxpayers must use approved e-invoicing software with API integration to the DGI clearing system. Non-resident digital service providers must issue an accredited electronic invoice for each transactional ITBMS remittance. Failure to use the fiscal clearance channel invalidates input credits and triggers fines.

DGI may reject a zero-rating claim where the export documentation bundle is incomplete, the service is demonstrated to have been effectively consumed in Panama, or consideration is not traceable from abroad. Penalties include: retrospective clawback of all zero-rated ITBMS refunds plus interest on unpaid tax (approximately 10% per annum), fines ranging from 50% to 300% of the evaded tax for deliberate misdeclaration (Código Fiscal Articles 760–762), suspension and revocation of RUC status, and criminal tax fraud prosecution with imprisonment for aggravated falsification of export documents. The review period is extended to up to 10 years where fraud is alleged.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized; the balboa is a fiduciary unit at par with the US dollar, but only US dollars circulate in physical form. There are no general exchange controls, no restrictions on current or capital account convertibility, and no profit remittance hurdles. Corporate profits of a Panama S.A. may be freely repatriated and converted without central bank or government approval. The SBP, however, requires banks to enforce internal client due diligence and UAF reporting thresholds on large cross-border movements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender regime exists. Panama does not impose mandatory repatriation, holding limits, or conversion ratios on export receipts. A Panama corporation may retain foreign-currency receivables offshore or bring them into Panama without conversion requirements because the local monetary system is already dollar-based and capital is deemed freely mobile.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP), the national banking regulator and supervisory authority; Panama has no issuing central bank. The financial intelligence unit for AML/CFT supervision is the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Panama law recognizes non-bank financial companies and has a fintech innovation sandbox, but an offshore digital EMI without a licensed Panamanian banking relationship is not sufficient for treasury clearing. Local banks generally cannot open nostro accounts to an unlicensed EMI; foreign EMIs may be used only for client-side collections ancillary to the software platform. Full corporate treasury operations require a physical Panamanian bank account and compliance with UAF transaction-reporting rules.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Panama imposes a 5% corporate-level complementary dividend tax (Aporte de Ley) applied to retained earnings before dividend distribution, and a 10% withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders. Resident corporate shareholders may be exempt if certain conditions are satisfied. The total effective burden on distributed profits is approximately 15% (5% complementary + 10% WHT), assuming profits were subjected to the 25% corporate income tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form 701 must be filed within three months after the fiscal year-end. For calendar-year filers, the deadline is March 31. Fiscal-year entities may file by the last day of the third month following their chosen fiscal year close. Monthly provisional payments (Form 706) and quarterly advance income tax payments are generally due during the tax year, reconciliable in the annual return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panama does not maintain a traditional OECD-compliant Local File/Master File statutory threshold expressed in local currency. Panamanian TP rules apply to related-party transactions involving Panama-source income, irrespective of size, and the Tax Code requires taxpayers to maintain contemporaneous documentation in compliance with the arm's length principle. The DGI may request TP documentation where cross-border transactions with related parties exceed USD 1,000,000 per annum. In practice, a Master File and Local File should be prepared whenever aggregate related-party revenue exceeds USD 1,000,000 to avoid penalties for non-compliance during audit. Additionally, a TP declaration (Anexo de Precios de Transferencia) must be attached to Form 701 when related-party transactions exceed USD 1,000,000 (per taxpayer) or USD 200,000 per related party.

Economic substance scope: Panama's International Financial Centers Law (Law 254 of 2021 and its implementing regulations) imposes Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) on entities licensed as an International Financial Center (IFC) and on qualifying multinational headquarters (SEM) entities. A plain Panama S.A. (Sociedad Anónima) engaged in ISIC 6201 software/cloud services is not automatically within the ESR scope unless it obtains an IFC license, a SEM license, or operates under Panama-Pacifico. However, to avoid the characterization of a shell or conduit under DTA relief, foreign tax authorities (e.g., IRS, HMRC, or EU Member States) may apply their own economic substance doctrines (e.g., controlled foreign corporation rules and principal purpose tests) to the Panama company. Accordingly, the Panama S.A. should demonstrate substantive operations — physical office, local employees (or genuine externalized management), board minutes, bank accounts, and real decision-making power over the software subscription business.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 296 of the Código Penal and Law 42 of 2007, tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment from 1 to 4 years and fines of up to 50% of the defrauded tax, plus interest and penalties determined by DGI. Civil penalty: up to 150% of the omitted tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and/or resident agents who knowingly sign falsified tax returns or who direct the concealment of income face criminal liability as co-perpetrators or abettors; personal fines, travel bans, confiscation of assets, and imprisonment of up to 4 years apply. The corporate veil may be pierced in cases of fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Law 254 of 2021, failure to demonstrate economic substance upon DGI or UAF (Financial Analysis Unit) request can result in revocation of the license, administrative fines of up to USD 1,000,000 (for regulated entities), a strike-off of the company from the Registro Público, and the imposition of tax arrears plus 10% late payment surcharge. If the entity is deemed a tax-resident operative activity, territorial-source reclassification of the underlying income and double taxation exposure may arise in the shareholder's home jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Officers and resident agents face administrative sanctions under Law 254, including disqualification as directors or resident agents, personal fines of up to USD 50,000, and in flagrant cases criminal prosecution for fraud if they facilitate a false corporate facade.
Money Laundering / AML (Cross-Border Portfolio Proceeds)

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015 (AML/CFT) and Ley 70 de 2019, non-subject professional activities may still be fined for facilitating money laundering. The fine can range from USD 10,000 to USD 1,000,000 per offense, plus asset forfeiture. If the S.A. causes a breach in the payment chain (Stripe or Apple IAP), financial institutions may suspend or terminate merchant services.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who knowingly operate the portfolio tracking entity to evade AML controls face criminal liability, including imprisonment of 5 to 10 years, personal fines, confiscation of digital assets, and travel restrictions; the court may pierce the corporate veil to recover misappropriated client funds.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to 1% of the value of transactions not disclosed, with a minimum penalty of USD 10,000; adjustments to taxable income by the DGI with interest at the statutory rate and a 20% surcharge on the additional tax. The S.A. may also be subject to the 12.5% WHT on deemed royalties if the IP is mispriced.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable to the extent they execute or authorize mispriced intercompany contracts; if fraud is established, they incur criminal liability for tax evasion and are subject to co-obligor status for the assessed tax and penalties.
