The charter must state an authorized capital of no less than USD 10,000, typically divided into registered par-value shares or shares of no par value. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; online tax portal: Sistema Integrado de Tributación (SIT) at sit.mef.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

A foreign beneficial owner must instruct a licensed Panamanian resident agent to apply to the DGI for issuance of the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes), which is the sole national tax identification number. The resident agent files the official RUC application through SIT or at a DGI office, attaching: notarized and Public Registry-inscribed articles of incorporation and bylaws; a legal certificate from the Public Registry showing the S.A. is in good standing; the resident agent's acceptance and proof of appointment; a notarized board resolution identifying the local representative and ultimate beneficial owners; an executed municipal Aviso de Operación for the registered office address; and proof of the registered address. Upon review, the DGI issues the RUC certificate, which simultaneously enrols the company for corporate income tax and ITBMS (VAT) purposes. There is no minimum capital requirement and no residency requirement for shareholders.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not provide check-the-box or any other entity-classification election for a Sociedad Anónima; an S.A. is a per-se corporation. No hybrid mismatch or pass-through elections are available under Panamanian law. A pure equity-holding SPV may elect to maintain a particular fiscal year upon registration, though this does not alter its territorial-source classification. Special fiscal regime elections (e.g., legal stability contracts or SEM headquarters regime) are not relevant to a passive holding SPV and do not create pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No numeric geographic threshold for ordinary taxable activities. Any person habitually performing taxable transfers of goods or services in Panama is required to register for ITBMS using the RUC. A simplified small-taxpayer regime exists for annual gross turnover below PAB 300,000, but this does not exempt the entity from registration if it has Panama-source commercial activities. A foreign pure-equity holding SPV with no Panamanian-source operational activities generally has no ITBMS registration nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% ITBMS rate applies to: (i) exports of tangible movable goods physically departing Panama under a valid customs export declaration; (ii) services rendered from Panama to a non-resident recipient whose economic use and enjoyment occurs wholly abroad, provided the services are not directly tied to Panamanian real estate or movable goods present in Panama; and (iii) other transactions expressly zero-rated by the ITBMS Law. For a pure equity-holding SPV, foreign-source dividends and gains from transfers of foreign subsidiary shares are not 'exports' for ITBMS purposes and remain outside Panamanian territorial income tax. To substantiate zero-rating, the taxpayer must retain the full customs, freight, contract, and foreign payment documentation proving the overseas destination and actual flow of funds from abroad. Panama imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; nevertheless, the bank credit record evidencing settlement from abroad is the primary audit evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera, DUA), accepted and stamped by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / loading manifest showing physical departure from Panamanian customs territory
  • Commercial invoice issued on the company's letterhead
  • Foreign inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 confirming receipt of settlement from abroad
  • Export service contract defining the foreign recipient, scope of services, and place of economic use (for services)
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (for goods)

Panama mandates electronic invoicing through the SIF-E (Sistema de Facturación Electrónica), fully integrated with the SIT portal. All invoices and equivalent documents must be digitally signed and transmitted in near real time to the DGI for fiscal validation and stamping; each accepted invoice receives a unique DGI authorization code and QR matrix. The issuance of an invoice without prior DGI clearance, or outside the SIF-E channel, is legally defective and may trigger fines, establishment closure, and disallowance of input VAT credits.

The DGI may comprehensively audit zero-rated export claims, verifying customs departure, the existence of the foreign recipient, the place of use, and the wired payment. If a purported export is recharacterised as a domestic taxable transaction, the DGI will assess the 7% ITBMS due retroactively, plus monthly surcharges (1% per month or equivalent calibration), and apply penalties ranging from 50% to 200% of the omitted tax in cases of negligence or fraud. Fraudulent invoice issuance attracts separate civil fines, possible economic offence sanctions, and criminal liability under Panama's fiscal enforcement rules. The DGI may also claw back any input VAT credit claimed on export-related procurement where zero-rating is unwarranted, together with interest and full restoration of the credit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized, open-market economy. There are no exchange controls, no capital or current account segmentation, no administrative approvals for inbound or outbound transfers, and no restrictions on dividend remittances, profit repatriation, or proceeds of liquidation. Convertibility is effectively absolute for all lawful transactions in any currency; the only practical frictions are bank-level AML/KYC due diligence, the availability of correspondent banking relationships, and the willingness of counterparties to transact with a Panamanian intermediate holdco. The territorial tax system and the absence of a national currency eliminate any sovereign FX conversion bottleneck.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. Panama imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export or any other foreign receipts. Because the US dollar is the domestic legal tender, all proceeds retained by a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima remain freely disposable in USD or any foreign currency. The only applicable compliance duties are the general anti-money-laundering record-keeping and suspicious-transaction reporting obligations under Law 23 of 2015, administered by the Financial Analysis Unit (Unidad de Análisis Financiero).

Central bank supervision: The Republic of Panama does not maintain a central bank. The supervising authority for banking, treasury, and financial-system stability is the Superintendency of Banks of the Republic of Panama (Superintendencia de Bancos de la República de Panamá). The Banco Nacional de Panamá acts solely as the fiscal and clearing agent of the State, and the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) is a nominal unit circulating at par with the US dollar, which serves as legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low as the sole treasury rail and Medium-High as a supplementary collection channel. Panamanian law, including the fintech licensing regime introduced by Law 13 of 2018 and supervised by the Superintendency of Banks, does not prohibit a Panamanian S.A. from maintaining accounts with foreign-regulated payment institutions. However, foreign digital EMIs and neobanks generally impose jurisdictional restrictions, frequently decline or limit services to Panamanian corporate entities because Panama has historically been treated as a higher-risk financial center, and cannot access the local ACH-Panamá clearing rail. An offshore EMI account should therefore be confined to USD collections or disbursements in supported corridors, with automatic sweeps via SWIFT MT103 into a tier-1 Panamanian bank account. It must not be relied upon for capital escrow, dividend distributions, or trade settlement infrastructure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections must be routed as SWIFT MT103 or MT202 USD wire transfers from the investing entity into a Panamanian licensed bank account, with a corresponding New York correspondent clearing via CHIPS or Fedwire. Panamanian banks do not maintain a national FX purpose-code registry; instead, the remitter must complete the bank's economic-activity and purpose declaration and include in MT103 Field 70 an explicit remittance description such as SUSCRIPCION DE CAPITAL O APORTE DE CAPITAL SOCIAL together with the corporate RUC and the unique account number. The receiving bank will verify the credit against the corporate resolution, the share subscription minutes, and the UBO file, and will request a sworn source-of-funds declaration for amounts exceeding its internal reporting threshold. Funds arrive in USD without conversion, surrender, or regulatory pre-approval, and are immediately available in the S.A.'s operational or escrow sub-account.Outbound treasury settlements, including dividend distributions, intercompany loan repayments, and acquisitions of subsidiaries, are executed as SWIFT MT103 transfers settling through the local ACH-Panamá system for domestic USD items and through New York CHIPS or Fedwire correspondents for international transfers. Panama's licensed banks issue documentary commercial letters of credit under UCP 600 and standby letters of credit under ISP98 for trade-related disbursements, with sight drafts financed against the USD credit line of the S.A. No foreign-exchange license or central-bank approval is required because the domestic legal tender is the US dollar; where the recipient requires EUR, GBP, JPY, or a Latin American currency, the SPV may purchase the foreign currency spot from the serviced bank at the prevailing market rate, with settlement conducted through the correspondent network of the Panamanian institution. All disbursements must be matched against board-authorized payment records and the AML file of each beneficiary to preserve the compliance trail.OTC deliverable foreign exchange forwards in USD/EUR, USD/GBP, and USD/JPY executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Panamanian licensed bank or a regulated international dealer.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in USD/BRL, USD/COP, and USD/MXN with international counterparties, used where the local Latin American currency market imposes convertibility or settlement restrictions.Cross-currency swaps and FX swaps converting USD into EUR or into select Latin American currencies for medium-term parent-funding or acquisition liabilities, subject to bilateral credit approval and, where required, a Credit Support Annex.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than 31 March following the fiscal year-end for calendar-year taxpayers; for off-calendar fiscal years, the last day of the third month after the fiscal year close; no blanket extension exists, only DGI-granted individual deferrals.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within the first 10 days of the month following the taxable month; mandatory where the average net taxable income of the preceding two fiscal years exceeded USD 250,000.

Deadline: · Frequency:

TP informative return due within 6 months after fiscal year-end; ESR declaration due within 8 months after fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and sworn TP return (Form 150) trigger where aggregate related-party transactions exceed USD 2,000,000 (Panama Balboa at parity with USD) in a fiscal year; Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above USD 85,000,000; CbC reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Panama ESR (Executive Decree 358 of 2021, effective 1 January 2022) applies to pure equity holding SPVs as a covered relevant activity with a reduced substance test: a registered office in Panama, adequate physical premises, a qualified resident director or registered agent, accounting records maintained in Panama, and board-level oversight of subsidiary management; annual ESR declaration must be filed within 8 months of the fiscal year-end; non-compliance triggers fines up to USD 100,000, loss of good standing, and automatic exchange of beneficial ownership information.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Surcharge of up to 30% of the omitted tax plus 1% monthly default interest; criminal tax fraud under the Criminal Code is punishable by 2 to 5 years imprisonment and fines of 1 to 3 times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under the Código Fiscal where returns reflect fraudulent intent; directors and the registered agent become jointly and severally liable for the tax debt, and travel bans and precautionary asset seizures may be imposed pending final assessment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR fines up to USD 100,000 per instance, deregistration from the Registro Público, cancellation of tax residency certificates, and exclusion from the territorial-exemption regime for foreign-source dividends.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident registered agent face professional discipline and monetary fines; directors may be disqualified from serving in Panama and the ultimate beneficial owner loses access to Panama banking and good standing certificates.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015 and Decree Law 2 of 2018, money laundering is punishable by 5 to 10 years imprisonment, fines up to USD 1,000,000, and asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are charged individually for laundering; preventive freezing orders, travel bans, and extradition cooperation apply, and the predicate offense expressly includes tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Mandatory primary adjustment to Panama-source income, secondary recharacterization, 1% monthly default interest, and fines of 5% to 10% of the adjusted amount.

Director personal liability: Knowingly certifying false TP documentation exposes directors to joint and several liability for the adjusted tax, penalties, and criminal liability for document forgery.
Fraudulent Treaty Relief

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, 50% penalty on the under-withheld tax, accrued default interest, and mandatory disclosure to the OECD harmful-tax-practices framework.

Director personal liability: Signing officers face up to 5 years imprisonment for forgery; professional deregistration applies to the local counsel or registered agent who facilitated the fraudulent claim.
