Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); online tax portal: DGI e-Tax 2.0 / SITIO (Sistema Integrado de Tributación en Línea), accessible via the official DGI website at www.dgi.gob.pa.

E-government filing portal

A Panama S.A. must procure a RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) before carrying on any taxable activity. Procedure: (1) incorporate the Sociedad Anónima in the Registro Público de Panamá with a licensed resident agent (agente residente) and a resident legal representative (representante legal), and file beneficial-ownership information under Law 23 of 2015; (2) file the RUC application with the DGI through the resident agent, attaching the incorporation certificate (testimonio), corporate bylaws, resident-agent consent, registered-address evidence, and identification of directors and officers; (3) obtain the RUC certificate and a recognised digital-signature certificate (firma digital) for online filings; (4) activate the DGI online account and register the company as an ITBMS taxpayer with the relevant economic-activity code; (5) obtain the municipal aviso de operación (business permit) at the registered office and commence DGI-compliant electronic invoicing. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope; Entity_Level_Taxation. A Panama S.A. is a juridical person taxed at entity level exclusively on Panamanian-source income under the territoriality principle; foreign-source revenue, including ad-network payouts from Google AdMob and AppLovin derived from offshore platforms, falls outside the domestic income-tax base. ITBMS (VAT) applies to taxable transactions performed in or enjoyed within Panama, not to foreign-source export revenue. A standard S.A. is not a pass-through entity and cannot be disregarded for Panamanian tax purposes.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election mechanism exists in Panamanian law. Statutory options available to the S.A.: (a) opt-in to the simplified small-taxpayer regime (Régimen de Pequeño Contribuyente) where annual gross turnover is at or below approximately PAB 36,000, replacing the standard income-tax and ITBMS compliance burden with a simplified payment method; (b) election to register and operate as an export-services platform under Law 41 of 2007, which secures ITBMS zero-rating, territorial income-tax treatment and an enhanced export-documentation framework; (c) election of the monthly or quarterly filing calendar for ITBMS and income-tax advance returns according to the DGI duty roster; (d) no group or consolidated income-tax filing election is available for separate S.A. entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PAB (Balboa, at par with USD) – There is no de minimis turnover threshold: any person habitually carrying out taxable transfers of goods or provisions of services in Panama must register for ITBMS before commencing operations, irrespective of revenue volume. A simplified compliance track is available for small taxpayers below approximately PAB 36,000 of annual gross income, but registration itself remains compulsory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and exported services are subject to ITBMS at 0%. A service qualifies as exported when rendered to a non-resident person for use, benefit, or enjoyment abroad. The S.A.'s revenue-share stream from AdMob and AppLovin (both foreign ad networks) qualifies: the contractual payees are non-resident, the game-publishing and ad-inventory services are delivered to offshore platforms, and the economic benefit is realized outside Panama. To claim 0% the taxpayer must evidence the foreign recipient, the offshore consumption of the service, and inbound remittance. Certification as an export-services platform under Law 41 of 2007 strengthens eligibility and provides a rebuttable presumption of export status.

  • Customs export declaration and bill of lading (required only for physical-goods exports; not applicable to pure digital service exports)
  • Executed digital services agreement or platform terms with Google (AdMob) and AppLovin identifying the foreign payer and revenue-share mechanics
  • Law 41 of 2007 export-services platform registration certificate (if the election is made)
  • Ad-network payout reports (AdMob and AppLovin statements) mapping gross revenue, deductions, and foreign remittance
  • Inward remittance certificate from a Panamanian bank evidencing receipt of USD from abroad
  • ITBMS sales register (libro de ventas) and periodic ITBMS returns presenting zero-rated lines separately
  • Annual income-tax return declaration classifying the revenue as foreign-source under the territoriality rules

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) is phased in by the DGI and applies to the general taxpayer population. Invoices must be issued as digitally signed XML/UBL documents bearing DGI-authorized sequential numbering and transmitted to the DGI online validation platform; taxpayers must use DGI-certified billing software or the DGI free web issuer. High-volume issuers integrate through API/web-service clearance; a DGI acknowledgement (acuse de recibo) constitutes fiscal clearance. There is no physical fiscal stamp; legal validity depends on DGI digital validation. Non-validated invoices do not support input ITBMS credits and expose the issuer to fines.

Panama is a dollarized economy with no exchange controls; there is no statutory FX repatriation deadline. However, DGI auditors routinely expect inward-remittance evidence within a 180-day window for each zero-rated invoice, and failure to demonstrate offshore receipt and foreign-source character can lead to denial of the 0% rate. On reassessment, the S.A. is liable for the full 7% ITBMS on gross payouts plus monthly default interest and fines generally ranging up to 100%–300% of the omitted tax, with higher sanctions for recidivism; fabrication of export documentation or sham foreign contracts triggers criminal tax-fraud charges under the Código Fiscal and AML liability under Law 23 of 2015. Retained foreign earnings not supported by payout reports may also be scrutinized for undisclosed Panamanian-source revenue.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto currency convertibility. Panama is a fully dollarized jurisdiction: the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) is a purely nominal unit pegged 1:1 to the United States dollar (USD), and the USD is the legal tender used in all domestic transactions. There are no exchange controls, no mandatory conversion ratios, no central-bank approval for remittances, no FX licensing requirement, and no restriction on repatriation of dividends or return of capital; profits may be remitted freely through the SBP-regulated banking system. Under the territorial tax model, foreign-source gaming revenue derived from ad-monetization platforms (AdMob and AppLovin) is outside Panamanian source-income taxation pursuant to Article 694 of the Fiscal Code; dividend withholding tax of 5% applies only to distributions made out of Panamanian-source taxable profits. The only practical profit-remittance hurdles are documentary in nature: formal board and shareholder resolutions, RUA beneficial-ownership filings, and the annual fiduciary income-tax declaration (Form R068).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Panama imposes no statutory timeline, no percentage surrender obligation, and no mandatory conversion requirement on export proceeds, digital ad-monetization receipts, or any other foreign-exchange inflow. Because the domestic settlement currency is the USD, export receipts are immediately usable in-country without any repatriation formalities. The only compliance requirements in the vicinity are transaction reporting by the receiving bank to the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF) under Law 23 of 2015 when a single wire or an aggregate series exceeds USD 10,000, and the maintenance of ordinary commercial invoicing documentation to support territorial-source income characterization for the annual corporate tax file.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP, Superintendency of Banks of Panama) — the sole banking conduct-of-business supervisor of the Republic of Panama; there is no central bank issuing domestic currency; the Banco Nacional de Panamá (National Bank of Panama) functions as fiscal agent for the Panamanian Treasury rather than as a monetary authority, while monetary stability is effectively delegated to the United States Federal Reserve through the statutory dollarization regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High, subject to material legal constraints. Panama's Fintech Law (Law 21 of April 20, 2021) created SBP-authorized e-money and innovation-sandbox licensing; offshore digital EMIs such as Payoneer, Airwallex, Wise, and similar wallet providers do not hold Panamanian banking licenses and may not establish branches or accept local deposits in Panama. A Panamanian S.A. may lawfully use such EMIs as settlement pass-through rails for AdMob and AppLovin payouts provided that (i) the EMI's contractual relationship is with the S.A. itself under an express treasury resolution, (ii) funds ultimately land in the SBP-licensed USD corporate account, and (iii) the UAF reporting thresholds are preserved through bank-side monitoring; nonetheless, the treasury blueprint must designate an SBP-licensed Tier-1 bank as the sole final custodian of corporate liquidity and must never leave the EMI as the primary repository of gaming proceeds.

wires exceeding USD 10,000 are automatically reported by the receiving bank to the UAF; the injected funds should be parked in a bank-designated escrow sleeve until the bank completes its sample forensic screening, after which the funds are swept to the operational account without any FX conversion because the deposit is already USD-denominated.The primary clearing rail is the SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfer for revenue-share payments and third-party studio licensing commissions; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High, subject to material legal constraints. Panama's Fintech Law (Law 21 of April 20, 2021) created SBP-authorized e-money and innovation-sandbox licensing; offshore digital EMIs such as Payoneer, Airwallex, Wise, and similar wallet providers do not hold Panamanian banking licenses and may not establish branches or accept local deposits in Panama. A Panamanian S.A. may lawfully use such EMIs as settlement pass-through rails for AdMob and AppLovin payouts provided that (i) the EMI's contractual relationship is with the S.A. itself under an express treasury resolution, (ii) funds ultimately land in the SBP-licensed USD corporate account, and (iii) the UAF reporting thresholds are preserved through bank-side monitoring; nonetheless, the treasury blueprint must designate an SBP-licensed Tier-1 bank as the sole final custodian of corporate liquidity and must never leave the EMI as the primary repository of gaming proceeds.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% (dividend tax) applies to distributions made out of profits not previously subject to ISR at the corporate level, including distributions of foreign-source profits; 0% if the profits have borne the 25% ISR at the S.A. level. Dividends paid to residents and non-residents are subject to the same rule; the Panama payer withholds the final tax at the moment the dividend is declared or paid. Profits distributed to shareholders of a conducting company under the small-enterprise regime are typically subject to a 5% rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Fiscal year generally follows the calendar year (1 January – 31 December). The annual Form 201 is due on or before 15 March of the year immediately following the tax year end; quarterly advance ISR payments are due in April, June, September and December during the tax year (exact dates per DGI resolutions, normally the fifteenth day of the month). A company that merely holds a RUC but reports no taxable activity must still file and submit a nil or informational declaration to avoid administrative cancellation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 3,000,000 (US$3,000,000) of annual gross revenue or B/. 1,500,000 of aggregate related-party transactions in a fiscal year; taxpayers exceeding the thresholds must prepare a Local File, Master File (when the consolidated group exceeds the DGI threshold) and file the annual transfer-pricing schedule with the Form 201 documentation package. Even below the threshold, the DGI can require analysis for specific controlled transactions, and the documentation must be delivered within 45 business days of a formal request.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not operate a BVI/Cayman-style Economic Substance (Amendment) regime for the standard S.A. However, the territorial-source claim and the 10% dividend tax create a de facto substance test: the DGI will scrutinize whether management and control, personnel, decision-making, servers, and the geo-location of end-users support a foreign-source characterization. For a hyper-casual gaming S.A., creditable substance includes: substantive technical staff and development activity of the group outside Panama, centralized treasury and commercial management away from Panamanian territory, and no Panamanian employees, premises or signatories with authority to execute material contracts. Additionally, Law 129 of 2020 and its implementing Beneficial Ownership Register (RBF) require the S.A. to identify natural-person beneficial owners; RBF non-compliance results in restriction of corporate rights, suspension of dividend distributions, and administrative fines.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False or misleading ISR declarations

Statutory sanction: Administrative: fine up to 100% of the omitted tax plus moratory interest and the 25% late-filing cap; civil claim for the total tax shortfall. Criminal: evasion of taxes through false declarations constitutes defraudación tributaria (Código Penal, Article 289-A, as amended in 2019), punishable with imprisonment of 2 to 4 years, increased to 4 to 6 years where the evaded amount exceeds B/. 100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors, administrators and signatories who knowingly approve or sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and exposed to criminal prosecution, patrimonial embargo, travel bans and disqualification from serving as corporate officers or obtaining public contracts in Panama.
Shell Company / Lack of Gapless Substance

Statutory sanction: RBF non-compliance: fines in the range of B/. 1,000 to B/. 50,000 per filing violation and temporary suspension of the shareholder dividend rights; Aviso de Operación non-renewal leads to cancellation of the RUC and eventual dissolution proceedings. The DGI may disregard the corporate form and recharacterize foreign-source profits as Panama-source, assessing 25% ISR plus interest; undistributed untaxed profits become subject to the 10% dividend tax.

Director personal liability: Where the veil is pierced for fraud or abuse, directors are personally liable for corporate tax debts and fines. The Tax Court (Tribunal Administrativo Tributario) may order travel bans and embargo of personal assets during concealment audits, and criminal prosecution for falsification of books can result in imprisonment.
Money Laundering / AML-FT Violations in Cross-Border Payment Flows

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under Law 23 of 2015 (AML/CFT), which can reach B/. 1,000,000 for serious violations; criminal money laundering (Código Penal) carries imprisonment of 5 to 10 years for simple laundering, increased to 5 to 15 years where organized structures or repeated transactions are proven; instrumentalities and proceeds are subject to confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly design opaque payment structures face personal criminal liability, asset forfeiture, travel bans, and 5-year disqualification from managing financial or corporate entities in Panama; banks may also blacklist the officers individually.
