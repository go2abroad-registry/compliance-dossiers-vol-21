Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas de la República de Panamá; electronic portal: SICAR (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa / SICAR e-filing platform).

E-government filing portal

A Panamanian Sociedad Anónima obtains its RUC/NIT by filing the public deed of incorporation with the Registro Público, through its resident agent, and submitting the electronic SICAR application to DGI. The DGI assigns a single RUC that serves for both income tax and ITBMS (VAT) registration. For foreign entities without a Panama corporate charter, the non-resident corporation must appoint a resident legal representative, file notarized and apostilled constitutional documents, register a Panamanian tax domicile, and submit the DGI Form for taxpayer registration through a tax attorney; the same RUC covers income tax, ITBMS, and customs obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation under Territorial_Scope (Panama-source income only; the Panamanian S.A. is not a pass-through entity and is subject to corporate income tax at 25% on net Panama-source income, plus dividend tax upon distribution). — A Panamanian Sociedad Anónima obtains its RUC/NIT by filing the public deed of incorporation with the Registro Público, through its resident agent, and submitting the electronic SICAR application to DGI. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation under Territorial_Scope (Panama-source income only; the Panamanian S.A. is not a pass-through entity and is subject to corporate income tax at 25% on net Panama-source income, plus dividend tax upon distribution).

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Panama; a domestic S.A. is per se taxable and cannot elect pass-through treatment. Available statutory elections include the simplified small taxpayer regime for gross income not exceeding US$36,000, optional accelerated depreciation, large taxpayer periodic prepayment methods, and foreign-sales exclusion regimes where expressly codified. Hybrid mismatch or transparent entity elections are not permitted under Panamanian domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

US$0.00 — no de minimis threshold; any person engaged in taxable supplies within Panama must register for ITBMS. An optional simplified regime applies to small taxpayers with annual gross income up to US$36,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods qualify for 0% ITBMS when the goods are physically transported from Panama to a foreign destination and sold to a non-resident purchaser for use or consumption abroad. Services may be zero-rated when rendered to non-residents and effectively used outside Panama. The exporter must be registered with DGI, issue an electronic invoice marked 'exportación', maintain complete customs and shipping evidence, and substantiate receipt of payment from abroad. Input ITBMS attributable to zero-rated exports may be credited, and any residual credit is refundable upon application through SICAR.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera de Exportación)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT / transfer receipt evidencing payment from abroad)

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) under DGI Resolution; authorized taxpayers must issue invoices in XML format through DGI-certified software, incorporate a digital fiscal stamp and QR code, transmit invoice data to the DGI's SICAR/Facturación Electrónica service within 24 hours, and obtain prior validation of credit/debit notes. The system does not operate as a real-time pre-clearance model but imposes contemporaneous electronic transmission and digital stamping requirements.

DGI examines physical export exit, authenticity of foreign payment, and proper issuance of the export invoice. Failure to substantiate zero-rating leads to re-characterization as a taxable domestic supply, triggering 7% ITBMS, 25% corporate income tax, plus late-payment surcharges and interest. Fraudulent zero-rating claims or forged documentation expose taxpayers to fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the omitted tax, potential closure of operations, and criminal tax-fraud liability under Panama's Código Fiscal. Panama imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline, but the taxpayer must maintain contemporaneous bank evidence of receipt from abroad; unexplained delay may intensify audit scrutiny and cast doubt on the export character of the transaction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates under full dollarization. There are no exchange controls, no currency conversion licensing, no capital account restrictions, and no current account licensing requirements for import payments or profit remittances. Inward wires in USD can be credited directly to domestic accounts without central bank registration. Profit distribution may trigger Panamanian territorial dividend tax obligations, but there is no currency-control hurdle to repatriating funds abroad.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama imposes no export proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory repatriation period, and no statutory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters and importers may freely maintain USD and foreign-currency accounts onshore or offshore. Panamanian customs and SBP AML rules apply only to documentary integrity, sanctions screening, and beneficial ownership disclosure; no foreign-exchange administration approval is required for import settlement.

Central bank supervision: Panama does not have a traditional central bank. Monetary and banking supervision is exercised by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); the Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent of the State and provider of treasury services; the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) is responsible for external credit and sovereign debt policy. The Panamanian Balboa is pegged at 1:1 to the US Dollar, and USD is fully legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury node; acceptable only as an ancillary payment channel. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed Panamanian banks and cannot issue Panamanian IBANs or provide insured demand-deposit accounting. A non-resident EMI must contract with an SBP-licensed local bank or obtain a special license under Panamanian banking law. For a frozen beef and seafood importer, principal payables and LC operations should remain on tier-1 bank rails; fintech EMIs are suitable only for low-value supplier expense cards or supplementary payment testing.

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends paid by a Panamanian S.A. out of taxed profits; the 10% also applies to distributions from exempt or foreign-source profits, with no credit attaching to the corporate ISR.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File documentation and Form 708 disclosure are triggered when the aggregate value of related-party transactions exceeds B/.250,000 (USD 250,000) per fiscal year; a Country-by-Country Report is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least USD 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Panama maintains Economic Substance Rules (ESR) for entities conducting relevant activities, which expressly include distribution and import/export of goods. The frozen-beef and seafood importer must demonstrate Panamanian management (board meetings in Panama), physical premises (cold-chain warehousing), adequate employees, and expenditure proportionate to the volume of trade, and must file the annual substance declaration with the DGI. Territorial tax exemption does not remove the substance obligation; failure exposes the entity to administrative fines and RUC cancellation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudacion Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the evaded tax plus 10% surcharge and 1% monthly interest; administrative fine of 50%-150% of the evaded amount; criminal conviction carries imprisonment of 2 to 6 years under the Criminal Tax provisions of the Codigo Fiscal.

Director personal liability: Signing officers and directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt where they directed the falsification; criminal conviction triggers impedimento de salida (travel ban), disqualification from management, and seizure of personal assets.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to B/.50,000, RUC cancellation, compulsory dissolution, and re-attribution of income to the ultimate beneficial owner with recomputed 25% ISR and 10% dividend withholding; adverse EU/OECD listings may follow.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face personal fines and regulatory disqualification; failure to file UBO declarations triggers separate AML penalties and criminal exposure for fronting.
Trade-Based Money Laundering / AML

Statutory sanction: Decree-Law 31 of 2015: freezing and confiscation of assets, fines up to B/.5 million, and imprisonment of 5 to 10 years; the bank relationship is terminated and the entity is blacklisted by the UAF.

Director personal liability: Directors, signatories and compliance officers are personally prosecutable; courts routinely impose travel bans, personal asset seizure, and 5-10 year custodial sentences for structured trade transactions.
Dividend Withholding Evasion (Remittance Leakage)

Statutory sanction: The S.A., as withholding agent, is assessed the full 10% un-withheld tax plus 10% surcharge and 1% monthly interest; fines of B/.50 to B/.500 per omitted declaration apply.

Director personal liability: Officers who instructed the disguised distribution are personally liable for the un-withheld tax and may face criminal prosecution for breach of withholding-agent duties.
