UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Article 1 to Article 40 of Law 23 of 2015 (prevention of money laundering and terrorist financing), Articles 1 to 20 of Law 254 of 2021 (Beneficial Ownership Registry, Registro de Beneficiarios Finales), Executive Decree No. 442 of 2022 (regulations on beneficial ownership disclosure), Article 10 of Law 32 of 1927 (Sociedad Anónima statutory source), Decree No. 166 of 2018 (UAF AML supervision framework), and Cabinet Decree No. 3 of 2002 (mandatory UBO identification for financial activities).
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

No virtual office is permissible for the statutory registered office of a Sociedad Anónima in Panama. Law 32 of 1927, Article 18 requires the registered office to be the real physical address of the licensed registered agent and must be a Panamanian street address publicly available in the Registro Público. For an online auto-parts e-commerce business, a virtual office cannot constitute the statutory registered office; however, it is permissible as a back-office or administrative address provided the statutory residence of the entity is located in the licensed agent's premises. The operational business may be run remotely and the e-commerce warehouse may be located outside Panama without establishing a permanent establishment, provided sales are not attributed to the Panamanian territory.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas, Republic of Panama; official online portal: e-Tax 2.0 / SINCO (https://dgi.mef.gob.pa) and the electronic invoicing platform under the same portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Panamanian Sociedad Anónima must: (1) incorporate with the Registro Público de Panamá and maintain a resident agent; (2) file the Aviso de Operación through e-Tax before commencing taxable activities; (3) obtain the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) through the SINCO/e-Tax application, submitting the certified corporate charter, residency agent consent, and sworn financial estimate; (4) register for ITBMS and obtain an electronic invoicing certificate once a taxable turnover exceeding the statutory threshold is projected or an export zero-rating right is claimed; (5) appoint a local fiscal representative where the S.A. has no physical presence in Panama

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope — A foreign-owned Panamanian Sociedad Anónima must: (1) incorporate with the Registro Público de Panamá and maintain a resident agent; (2) file the Aviso de Operación through e-Tax before commencing taxable activities; (3) obtain the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) through the SINCO/e-Tax application, submitting the certified corporate charter, residency agent consent, and sworn financial estimate; (4) register for ITBMS and obtain an electronic invoicing certificate once a taxable turnover exceeding the statutory threshold is projected or an export zero-rating right is claimed; (5) appoint a local fiscal representative where the S.A. has no physical presence in Panama

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No United States-style check-the-box election exists under Panamanian tax law. The Panamanian S.A. is a limited liability company subject to entity-level taxation solely on Panama-source income. Available elections include: (i) application to the Multinational Company Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007 for eligible administrative and re-invoicing activities; (ii) optional registration as a Gran Contribuyente for centralized compliance; (iii) voluntary ITBMS registration without crossing the ordinary threshold for exporters of goods or offshore services; (iv) election to be governed by the Panama Pacifico Area tax regime for licensed operations inside that zone; and (v) the applicable tax treatment of dividends or branches is governed by the territorial source rules, with no hybrid mismatch election available under domestic law

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PAB 300,000 (at parity with USD 300,000) in annual gross taxable supplies becomes the compulsory ITBMS registration threshold once exceeded; below this amount a seller may remain under the simplified consumption tax regime, but auto parts e-commerce exporters are strongly recommended to register voluntarily to claim 0% export treatment; imported digital services paid through platforms such as Stripe and PayPal attract a 7% ITBMS withholding administered by the financial intermediary in Panama

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of automotive replacement parts and tuning accessories qualify for 0% ITBMS when: (a) the sale is made to a non-resident customer under Ex Works or Free on Board terms with title and risk passing outside Panamanian territory; (b) the goods are physically exported within six months of dispatch using a customs export declaration through the SICA/e-Export process; (c) payment is received through banking rails or payment platforms (Stripe, PayPal) traceable to foreign-issued cards or offshore accounts; and (d) for digital services ancillary to aftermarket parts (e.g., remote tuning consultations), the recipient must be a non-resident and the benefit of the service wholly consumed outside Panama, supported by an Exportación de Servicios form and a sworn affidavit

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación vía SICA / e-Export DUA)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing international transportation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) or banking SWIFT credit advice confirming foreign exchange repatriation
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Order or contract with the non-resident buyer and, where applicable, proof of delivery to the foreign warehouse or foreign port

Mandatory electronic invoicing under Decision Law 31 of 2018 and DGI Resolution, effective for all ITBMS-registered taxpayers; every invoice must be generated as a Factura Electrónica (E-DFD) through the DGI web portal, certified private software, or an API integration; DGI validates the XML batch and issues a digital stamp and QR code; validation is near-real-time batch clearance rather than real-time pre-clearance; all e-invoices must be submitted to the DGI through nightly or immediate API transmission, and the taxpayer must retain the full audit trail (PEPPAP) for at least five years

Unsubstantiated or fraudulent zero-rating is reversed by DGI and reclassified as a domestic taxable supply, triggering 7% ITBMS liability plus a late-payment surcharge of 10% per month and statutory monthly interest (currently 10% per annum); monetary fines of USD 500 to USD 20,000 apply per invalid e-invoice, and a further penalty of 50% to 100% of the tax evaded applies where intent or fraud is established; criminal tax fraud proceedings may be initiated under Ley 71 de 2019; the DGI audit window covers the last five fiscal years (ten years where registration was omitted); automatic clawback is triggered where export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days without a valid extension approved by the DGI or the customs authority

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama has full convertibility for both capital and current account transactions, as the US Dollar is legal tender and no central bank forex intervention exists. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and capital repatriation are unrestricted; however, payment flows must comply with Panamanian corporate income tax (territorial basis, but CFC/PE rules apply to foreign-source income remitted through Panama), withholding tax obligations, and AML/KYC reporting requirements. No exchange control or prior central bank authorization is required for cross-border transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio apply to export receipts in Panama. There is no requirement to surrender export proceeds or convert foreign currency, as the domestic monetary system is fully dollarized. Exporters may retain proceeds in any convertible currency, provided that banking records are maintained for tax and AML compliance.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as the primary banking and treasury regulator; Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the official fiscal agent. Panama has no formal central bank and operates a fully dollarized monetary system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Panama permits non-bank electronic payment institutions under SBP-regulated regimes and the Fintech Hub framework (Law 23 of 2015 for AML; Decree 55 of 2016 for fintech). Offshore digital EMIs not licensed in Panama can only be engaged as processor channels, not as local clearing agents. A Panama-licensed EMI/fintech requires an SBP operational license, a local compliance officer, and AML software; viability is medium for an auto-parts e-commerce operator without a full banking license.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital flows from shareholders must be filed as 'Capital Increase / Paid-in Capital' with the Panama Public Registry (Registro Público) and reported to the DGI for tax certificate purposes. Wires must be sent via SWIFT MT103 using the Panama-incorporated entity's USD account; the beneficiary bank will apply purpose codes consistent with Direct Investment (BOP code 5201) or Equity Capital (BOP code 5221).
