Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama — taxpayer portal and e-services: SIME (Sistema Integrado de Mejora de Ingresos), accessible via https://dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Sociedad Anónima obtains a Panamanian RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) by: (1) incorporating at the Registro Público de Panamá and recording the directors, resident agent, and secretaries; (2) appointing a Panamanian resident agent and fiscal/legal representative (mandatory for a foreign-controlled company) to interface with the DGI; (3) submitting the RUC application via SIME/DGI with: apostilled/consularized articles of incorporation and public deed, registered bylaws and power of attorney, identity documents of shareholders/directors (cédula or passport), the ISIC 4791 e-commerce activity descriptor, the registered-office or resident-agent address, and bank references; (4) receiving the DGI RUC certificate and assigned ISR/ITBMS obligations; (5) enrolling in the electronic-invoicing system (Factura Electrónica) and activating the SIME digital-certificate account; and (6) where the company maintains no physical Panamanian establishment for pure offshore e-commerce, the DGI registers the S.A. on an ITBMS/invoicing-only footing with the fiscal agent as authorized recipient of DGI notifications. Registration must be completed before taxable operations commence and no later than the statutory deadline (generally 30 days from the start of activities).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope — a Panamanian S.A. is a limited-liability juridical person taxed on its Panamanian-source income (ISR at the standard 25% corporate rate for internal-source profits); foreign-source income, including export e-commerce turnover earned and performed outside Panama, is statutorily outside the Panamanian tax base under the territoriality principle. The entity is not eligible for pass-through treatment; no CFC or worldwide-income aggregation applies in the absence of a Panama permanent establishment for the foreign sales channel.

Available election forms

Panama does not maintain a US-style check-the-box regime and has not enacted OECD hybrid-mismatch elections. The available statutory mechanisms for the S.A. are: (a) an advance tax ruling (consulta fiscal) before the DGI confirming the foreign-source/export character of cross-border web-retail turnover; (b) election of the simplified small-taxpayer regime (Régimen Especial de Pequeños Contribuyentes) for gross turnover within the legal ceiling (currently up to B/. 150,000 per annum) — available for ISIC 4791 retailers qualifying as 'pequeño contribuyente'; (c) election for the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime under Law 41 of 2007, as amended, where the Panamanian entity performs qualifying regional headquarters/co-ordination services for the cross-border group; (d) designation as a registered exporter with the foreign-trade authorities to secure 0% ITBMS and customs facilitations; and (e) tax-neutral classification elections for investment funds or trusts are not applicable to this S.A. structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No numeric registration threshold: any person habitually transferring movable goods or rendering services in Panama must register for ITBMS from the first taxable transaction (Código Fiscal and Ley No. 13 de 1995). For non-resident e-commerce sellers, ITBMS registration is triggered by habitual sales to Panamanian consumers (activity-based nexus); a separate numeric VAT registration threshold has not been codified. A simplified quota regime (Régimen Especial de Pequeños Contribuyentes) applies to taxpayers with annual gross turnover not exceeding B/. 150,000, but export sellers and importers are generally excluded from that election. Postal/courier import de minimis of B/. 100 applies to goods entering Panama by mail/express.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero per cent ITBMS applies to: (i) tangible goods physically exported from Panama, evidenced by the DUA exit stamp, transport document, and payment evidence; (ii) services sold to non-residents and effectively used wholly outside Panama; and (iii) goods supplied to the Colon Free Zone and other qualifying free-zone/licensed warehousing regimes. For the outdoor-camping e-commerce model, the S.A. may zero-rate only the portion of turnover physically shipped from Panama to foreign customers under matching DUA/B-L records; drop-ship orders fulfilled from third-country warehouses to customers outside Panama are treated as non-Panamanian transfers and fall entirely outside the ITBMS base rather than being 'zero-rated export supplies'.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Aduana - DUA) filed through the SISDUA electronic customs platform with proof of the effective exit of goods from Panama
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (DGI-stamped e-invoice coded 'Exportación' at 0% ITBMS)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or courier despatch proof) showing the foreign destination and physical movement
  • Exporter enrolment (Registro de Exportadores) and any transport/export permit required for the goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT credit advice, or bank deposit evidence showing collection of the export proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • E-commerce platform sales records, marketplace payout reports, and order-shipping trace logs substantiating each zero-rated web order

Mandatory national electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica para Facturas Electrónicas 'FELe') under Ley No. 9 de 2021 and DGI implementing resolutions. Registered businesses must: (1) issue sales documents only through DGI-certified software or the free SIME billing portal; (2) transmit each e-invoice in XML to the DGI in near real-time before the document may be delivered to the buyer; (3) obtain from the DGI a Sello Digital (digital stamp) and Código de Seguimiento (tracking code); (4) complete the invoice with electronic signature and QR; (5) for exports, issue the 'Factura de Exportación' with the 0% rate code and attach the customs DUA reference; (6) integrate ERP/marketplace billing through certified APIs (SIME REST/TLS) for high-volume e-commerce; and (7) retain all stamped e-documents in the SIME repository. Invoices lacking the DGI stamp are legally invalid and input ITBMS credits are denied.

The DGI and the Dirección General de Aduanas jointly audit export zero-rating claims through data analytics that cross-match DUA exits, Bills of Lading, shipping manifests, bank remittances, and platform/payment-rail records (PayPal/Stripe settlement data). Circular payments, shell addresses, mismatched quantities, over-invoicing, or fictitious franking trigger recharacterization of the supply as Panamanian-source taxable turnover. Consequences include: (i) reassessment of 7% ITBMS together with any ISR due on the reclassified income; (ii) a monthly surcharge of 1% of the omitted tax up to the legal cap and moratory interest at the statutory rate; (iii) administrative fines under the Código Fiscal ranging from B/. 100 to B/. 50,000 and up to 200% of the tax prejudiced; (iv) forfeiture of export input credits and suspension of the exporter registration; (v) criminal liability for tax fraud (defraudación tributaria — imprisonment of 3 to 6 years) and customs document fraud; and (vi) extension of the statutory clawback/assessment window where returns were not filed or evidence is fraudulent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de facto dollarization (U.S. Dollar legal tender; Balboa only a nominal unit). No exchange-control apparatus exists. Current-account transactions (e-commerce collections, trade payments, services) and capital-account transactions (equity injections, shareholder loans, dividend remittances) are freely convertible without prior approval. Profit remittances face no withholding, timing, or repatriation hurdles under the territorial tax model, provided the S.A. remains in good standing and files informative returns with the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or export-proceeds repatriation requirement. USD and foreign-currency e-commerce receipts may be retained overseas or remitted at the entity's discretion; the only applicable obligations are bank AML/KYC documentation of source and purpose.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as banking regulator; Banco Nacional de Panamá (BNP) as state fiscal agent and clearing bank; Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) as fiscal policy authority. Panama has no conventional central bank and does not issue domestic fiat currency.

Medium as complementary rail; Low as primary account. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) originator board resolution authorizing equity subscription or shareholder loan; (2) SWIFT MT103 transfer in USD from the shareholder's bank to the S.A., using the Panamanian bank's SWIFT BIC and ABA correspondent routing through New York (BNY Mellon, JPMorgan, or Citibank) via Fedwire/CHIPS; (3) reference line coded 'Capital Contribution – BPM6 Code 810 (Direct Investment Equity)' or 'Shareholder Loan – BPM6 Code 824'; (4) inbound credit verified by the bank's compliance team against the approved KYC file before funds are made available; (5) retained documentation: debit/credit advices, SWIFT MT103 confirmation, notarized capital-escrow accounting, and updated corporate ledger entries.SWIFT MT103 for routine supplier and logistics disbursements in USD (and selected EUR/GBP payouts through correspondent banks); MT700 documentary commercial letters of credit for high-value inventory purchase orders from Asian, U.S., or EU manufacturers; MT202 cover payments for interbank settlements. All outbound payments pass through bank-side anti-fraud filters and are tagged with 'GROS' (goods/services) or 'TRAD' trade purpose codes. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium as complementary rail; Low as primary account. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Mercury, DLocal) may serve as ancillary collection and payout rails for Stripe/PayPal settlement corridors, but cannot substitute for a licensed Panamanian bank account because deposit-taking from the public requires a license under Banking Law (Decree-Law 9 of 1998, as amended by Law 79 of 2019). SBP and correspondent banks classify unlicensed EMIs as higher-risk pass-through accounts, generating enhanced scrutiny and potential 'unlicensed banking operation' concerns under FATF/GAFILAT expectations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File obligations arise when the taxpayer's annual gross income exceeds B/.1,500,000 (US$1,500,000) and the taxpayer conducts controlled transactions with related parties. The Dirección General de Ingresos may also require documentation below the threshold for transactions involving low-markup distributions, buy-in of goods, or persistent losses.

Economic substance scope: Law 254 of 2019 and its regulatory decrees impose Economic Substance Requirements on Panamanian legal entities undertaking 'Relevant Activities' within the Republic. An internet retail operation in ISIC 4791 is likely a distribution activity if inventory is held, processed, or delivered from Panama. In such cases, the S.A. must prove physical premises, adequate personnel, executive management in Panama, and annual local expenditure proportional to the income derived from that relevant activity. If all online sales are generated and executed outside Panama with no Panama-source income, the ESR filing is dormant, but the DGI and financial authorities retain audit powers over actual substance versus paper registration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Surcharge of 50% to 150% of the underpaid tax, monthly interest penalties, and loss of deductions. Criminal liability under Article 714 of the Código Fiscal and Law 23 of 2017 can expose the company to fines of up to US$250,000 and imprisonment of responsible parties for up to five years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced when the S.A. is a mere conduit. Directors and officers can be held jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and penalties, may face criminal prosecution, travel bans, asset freezes, and debarment from holding directorial roles in Panama.
Shell Company / Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties under Law 254 of 2019 range from US$75,000 to US$1,000,000 per infraction, plus potential cancellation of the corporate license and dissolution of the entity. False or incomplete ESR filings carry additional fines and may trigger criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false ESR declarations assume statutory responsibility; proven participation in a sham structure allows DGI to assess the unpaid taxes directly against the directors, impose civil restitution, obtain travel bans, and seek imprisonment in aggravated money-laundering cases.
AML/CFT Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Panama's Law 23 of 2015 and related AML regulations, serious breaches can result in fines up to US$1,000,000, suspension of bank relationships, revocation of payment acceptance authorizations, and administrative closure of the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate money laundering risk criminal conviction with imprisonment of up to 12 years, seizure of personal assets, and prohibition from future financial or corporate management roles.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DGI may recharacterize the controlled transaction, compute taxable income under the arm's-length principle, disallow excessive deductions, and apply a 20% penalty on the additional tax determined, alongside interest and potential criminal penalties for fraudulent documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false intercompany pricing or maintain deficient TP files can be held personally liable for the resulting tax adjustment when the S.A. cannot pay; civil restitution, travel restrictions, and director disqualification may follow.
