Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); sole national tax authority for ISR and ITBMS. Digital services: 'Sede Electrónica de la DGI' at dgi.gob.pa (registration, filings, payments), the 'Facturación Electrónica' module for e-invoicing, and the portal of the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA) for export declarations.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident principals incorporate a Panama S.A. under Ley 32 de 1927 through a resident agent, register the public deed at the Registro Público de Panamá, appoint a Panamanian resident legal representative, and obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación plus MIDA sanitary registration for animal feed. The statutory tax identifier is the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente). Procedure: (1) file the Solicitud de Inscripción de Contribuyente at the DGI Sede Electrónica attaching the apostilled public deed extract, Registro Público certificate, officer appointment, proof of local address, resident representative identity document, and banking reference; (2) DGI issues the numeric RUC covering ISR, ITBMS, and employer obligations; (3) activate Sede Electrónica credentials and register with ANA in the SISCA customs system for DUCA export declarations; (4) once operational, file ISR and ITBMS declarations under the assigned calendar schedule.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — the S.A. is an entity-level corporation (not pass-through) paying 25% ISR only on net income from Panamanian sources; profits from export sales perfected abroad, foreign-source dividends received, and foreign capital gains fall outside the Panamanian tax net; 100% non-resident ownership is permitted without CFC or shareholder-level anti-deferral for foreign-source earnings.

Available election forms

Domestic law fixes the S.A. as a corporation under Ley 32 de 1927; there is no Panamanian check-the-box election. For U.S. federal tax purposes, the Panama S.A. is not on the Treas. Reg. §301.7701-2(b)(8) per-se list and is an eligible entity that may be classified via Form 8832 (e.g., disregarded entity or partnership). Panamanian statutory elections available to the S.A. include: (i) simplified ISR/ITBMS SME regimes for gross turnover not exceeding USD 200,000; (ii) monthly versus quarterly ITBMS filing cycles; and (iii) optional accelerated depreciation methods for industrial plant and freeze-drying equipment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory monetary threshold (B/.0); ITBMS registration attaches at the first taxable event (importation or local supply in Panama) without de minimis turnover. A pure foreign exporter with no Panamanian establishment, no local sales, and no goods physically imported into Panama has no ITBMS registration obligation; if the S.A. imports raw meat or sells into the domestic pet food market, RUC registration as an ITBMS taxpayer and periodic filings become mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated (0%) under the ITBMS: the freeze-dried pet food must physically leave Panamanian customs territory against a formal ANA export declaration, title and risk must pass to a foreign buyer, and the sale consideration must be paid from abroad. Tolling/manufacturing services rendered exclusively to non-residents and consumed abroad are likewise outside the charge. The exporter credits input ITBMS (raw meat, packaging, utilities, machinery inputs) against its ITBMS obligations and may claim a cash refund (devolución) or credit transfer of the excess export-generated credit through DGI proceedings. Although Panama is fully dollarized and has no statutory exchange control, the standard banking practice and DGI audit expectation require repatriation and traceability of export proceeds within 180 days from the bill of lading date to preserve the zero-rating and refund position.

  • Formal customs export declaration (DUCA) filed in ANA's SISCA electronic system with customs exit validation
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exit from Panamanian customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching DUCA values and the ISIC 1080 product description
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 confirmation of USD payment by the foreign buyer
  • MIDA authorized sanitary export certificate for pet feed and, where applicable, certificate of free sale

Mandatory e-invoicing ('Facturación Electrónica') under DGI Resolution No. 201-3922 of 2018, as amended: all RUC/ITBMS-registered taxpayers must issue electronic invoices through DGI-authorized channels (DGI web portal, mobile application, or certified invoicing software connected via API) carrying a DGI-validated 'código de seguridad' obtained in real time at issuance; invoices are pre-cleared digitally before delivery to the buyer, and the electronic sales ledger is transmitted to the DGI. Export transactions must be typed as 'Exportación' with the foreign buyer's tax identification and a zero-rated ITBMS line; an invoice without a valid digital clearance code is legally ineffective for tax credit purposes.

DGI and ANA cross-check the DUCA, the e-invoice security code database, vessel and airline manifests, and inward remittances; the zero-rated 7% ITBMS is clawed back with late-payment interest and penalties (up to 100% of the tax on fraudulent reversals) where goods are diverted into the domestic market, export invoices are simulated ('exportación ficticia'), prices are under-declared, or proceeds are not repatriated. The exporter must retain the full documentation bundle for the statutory limitation period and prove lot-level traceability from raw material import to outward manifest. Intentional schemes are prosecuted as 'defraudación tributaria' under Law 70 of 2019, exposing responsible officers to imprisonment, fines, RUC suspension, and debarment from the exporter registry.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility on both capital and current account. Panama operates under complete dollarization with no exchange controls, no required prior authorization for cross-border fund transfers, and no restrictions on profit repatriation, dividend distribution, or capital redemption. The SBP only imposes standard AML/KYC reporting thresholds, not FX approval requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or holding-period requirement for export proceeds. Exporters may retain 100% of invoice value in USD or any freely convertible currency and may maintain balances in foreign bank accounts without SBP authorization. Statutory obligations are limited to export customs declarations via the Panamanian Single Window (VUPA) and general anti-money-laundering reporting for transactions above USD 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Panama maintains no traditional central bank or Monetary Authority with note-issuing power. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) serves as the banking and AML/CFT regulator, while Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as the State's fiscal agent and clearing settlement bank. The Panamanian Balboa (PAB) circulates exclusively at a fixed 1:1 parity with the US Dollar, which is legal tender for all transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-Low. Offshore digital EMIs cannot operate independently in Panama absent a local banking or payment-service-provider license under Law 186 of 2020 (Innovation Law) and SBP sandbox rules. Non-regulated foreign EMIs must route transactions through a licensed Panamanian correspondent bank; direct account opening for a Panamanian S.A. is generally rejected unless the EMI holds an SBP-sanctioned payment provider (Proveedor de Servicios de Pago) or sandbox authorization. Legally compliant structure: local cash-management account with a Tier 1 bank + SWIFT TT and Platform_Pay settlement overlays.

Within three months after the fiscal year-end. For a calendar-year taxpayer: 31 March. For a non-calendar fiscal year: the last day of the third month following the close of the fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/.1,000,000 (US$1,000,000) aggregate value of related-party transactions in a fiscal year triggers the mandatory Local File. A Master File is required where the multinational group consolidates revenues of B/.20,000,000 or more and the Panamanian entity undertakes related-party transactions above the local threshold. All documentation must be prepared and filed with the annual tax return (Form 202, TP annex).

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) under Law 254/2021 and Regulatory Decree 532/2023 apply to relevant activities, including distribution and service centres, financing, leasing, intellectual property, and headquarters. A freeze-dried pet food manufacturer must demonstrate in Panama: (i) physical local premises; (ii) full-time employees proportionate to the activity; (iii) director and board meetings with real control; and (iv) all core income-generating activities performed and managed in Panama. If the entity operates as a shell or mere backup, the DGI may deny treaty benefits, reclassify foreign-source income, and apply penalties; a Form 509 annual substance filing is required for qualifying entities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fines between B/.10,000 and B/.20,000, plus 2 to 5 years imprisonment under the Panama Tax Code and Criminal Code; confiscation of assets and full restitution of the tax debt plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; criminal prosecution may include preventive custody and a travel ban pending trial.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Administrative derecognition of the corporate structure; denial of territorial tax treatment; reclassification of all income as Panama-source; penalty of up to 100% of omitted tax and potential loss of corporate registration.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors can be personally prosecuted for tax facilitation; courts may pierce the corporate veil and order civil restitution of distributed profits plus accrued penalties.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment to taxable income; imposition of a 40% surcharge on the adjustment; monthly interest of 5% (compounded); administrative fine up to B/.50,000 if documentation is absent.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise TP positions without economic basis may be required to reimburse the company for the additional tax and penalties; if conduct amounts to fraudulent or intentional non-compliance, criminal liability may follow.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23/2015 and its regulations, fines of up to US$1,000,000 or 20% of the value of the transaction; imprisonment from 5 to 10 years for officers or employees found guilty of money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable for knowingly accepting illicit funds; travel bans, asset seizure, and a personal fine up to US$500,000 may be imposed; the corporate veil will be pierced for restitution.
