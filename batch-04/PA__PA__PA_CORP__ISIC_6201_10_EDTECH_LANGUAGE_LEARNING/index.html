Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Panama; online portals: DGI Sede Electrónica (https://sede.mef.gob.pa), DGI institutional portal (https://www.dgi.mef.gob.pa), SFE e-invoicing system (https://sfe.dgi.mef.gob.pa).

E-government filing portal

For the newly incorporated Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (PA_CORP), the registration workflow is: (1) complete public-registry inscription at the Registro Público keeping the registered agent; (2) the legal representative or the fiduciary files the DGI RUC application (Formulario RUC-1) accompanied by the articles of incorporation, certificado de registro, domicile certificate, and a sworn statement of economic activity (ISIC 6201; digital EdTech services); (3) a Panamanian Contador Público Autorizado (CPA) must certify the registration; (4) obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación (business license) in the district of the registered address; (5) activate digital credentials at the Sede Electrónica and register in the SFE e-invoicing module; (6) for foreign-owner identification, shareholders' passports or entity documents must be filed for the Registro de Beneficiario Final under AML Law 23 of 2015. A foreign entity without a local S.A. does not obtain a Panamanian RUC unless it maintains a permanent establishment in Panama; no threshold-based VAT registration exists, so every taxable operator must register regardless of turnover, while the USD 36,024 threshold only elects the Pequeño Contribuyente simplified regime.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Panama does not maintain a check-the-box, LLC-transparent, or hybrid-mismatch election: an S.A. is mandatorily a corporation taxed at entity level (ISR 25%) and as an ITBMS taxpayer on Panamanian-source income only. Available statutory elections: (i) Pequeño Contribuyente regime under Law 82 of 2011 for annual gross revenue not exceeding USD 36,024, paying a final 3% gross-receipts tax in lieu of ISR and ITBMS; (ii) quarterly ISR/ITBMS filing election for taxpayers with annual gross revenue below USD 300,000; (iii) for EdTech export income, the taxpayer may issue invoices as 'no causado' (0% ITBMS) when the service is supplied to Apple/Google non-resident marketplaces and exploited abroad; where ITBMS has been charged in error on export revenue, the taxpayer elects the refund or credit mechanism under the Fiscal Code; (iv) exporters may elect to accumulate and offset input ITBMS credits under the export reimbursement regime against their periodic ITBMS declarations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No exemption or de minimis threshold: any person habitually engaged in the transfer of chattel goods or the provision of services on Panamanian territory is obliged to register for ITBMS (Código Fiscal, Article 1061 et seq.); the only quantitative demarcation is the elective small-taxpayer line of B/.36,024.00 (USD 36,024.00) annual gross revenue, below which the 3% Pequeño Contribuyente gross tax may substitute for ITBMS; subscription digital services sold to final consumers in Panama remain taxable at 7% even when delivered electronically.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies are outside the territorial incidence of ITBMS ('no causado', effective 0%): (1) goods: merchandise must be physically removed from Panama and exported through the DUA export declaration in the SIGA customs system; (2) services and digital subscriptions: the service is supplied to Apple/Google marketplaces as non-resident resellers or directly to non-resident users, and the software use and exploitation occurs outside Panamanian territory; the taxpayer must document the foreign residence of the platform or customer, the offshore application usage, and the foreign-platform settlement proceeds. Eligibility is substantiated at invoicing time: the exporter must issue an SFE export invoice conspicuously marked 'Exportación – ITBMS no causado' and must retain proof of non-resident status of the buyer (Apple/Google legal-entity certificates, foreign IP addresses, user geolocation, platform seller reports). Input ITBMS borne on costs attributable to exports is creditable or refundable under the export reimbursement mechanism of the Fiscal Code.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DUA – Declaración Única Aduanera) filed in the SIGA customs clearance system with operative exit confirmation, required for goods exported as physical media.
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing dispatch of exported goods for physical exports; for pure digital exports through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing, the SFE export invoice, the platform settlement report, and the digital-content distribution agreement.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement statement (SWIFT credit advice, Apple/Google payout statement) evidencing the receipt of export proceeds before the applicable repatriation benchmark.

Mandatory real-time clearance under the DGI 'Sistema de Facturación Electrónica' (SFE) based on Law 95 of 2018 and the applicable DGI resolutions. All ITBMS-registered taxpayers, including EdTech ISIC 6201 providers, must issue XML electronic invoices in the SFE with DGI real-time validation: each invoice is transmitted immediately via DGI web services/API, and upon validation receives a unique DGI authorization code and a QR code printed on the PDF or customer copy. Paper or point-of-sale invoices are permitted only under SFE contingency mode (platform unavailability), and the contingency batch must be uploaded to the DGI within the deadline fixed by DGI resolutions, generally within 48 hours after restoration. The system also obliges digital emitters to accept electronic credit and debit notes, to issue e-invoices for each Apple IAP or Google Play Billing settlement as a B2B export sale, and to file periodic ITBMS returns electronically through the Sede Electrónica with automatic cross-matching of authorized invoices.

The DGI audit directorate (Dirección de Fiscalización) cross-checks SFE export invoices, SIGA customs outflows, and bank receipts of proceeds: invoices for services in fact consumed in Panama, or lacking evidence of foreign exploitation, are reclassified as local taxable supplies. Consequences include: reassessment of the omitted 7% ITBMS plus monthly surcharge and interest; fines of one to three times the evaded tax (Código Fiscal, Articles 760-761); professional sanctions against the certifying CPA; withdrawal of the SFE emitter authorization; re-opening of prior fiscal years within the five-year statute of limitations in cases of fraudulent concealment; criminal tax evasion exposure under Law 114 of 2004 with imprisonment of two to five years where the tax loss exceeds USD 36,024; and bank AML downgrades or suspension under Law 23 of 2015. Panama has no statutory FX repatriation deadline because the US dollar is legal tender; the 180-day benchmark is the monitoring convention used by DGI audits and AML/CFT reviews to test whether export proceeds were genuinely realized and traceable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Panama is a dollarized jurisdiction with no exchange control regime and no separate legal tender. Capital and current account transactions are free of any prior authorization or divestment restrictions. There is no statutory limitation on the repatriation of dividends, interest, or capital; the only practical requirements are standard AML/KYC verification by the banking intermediary and, for cash transactions above USD 10,000, reporting to the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement exists. Since Panama has no central bank and no foreign exchange control authority, export receipts (including EdTech subscription revenue from Apple and Google) are not subject to any surrender, conversion, or mandatory holding period. All receipts may be freely retained, reinvested, or remitted abroad.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). Panama does not have an issuing central bank; the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender under Decree-Law 4 of 2012 and the Monetary Agreement with the United States. The SBP supervises all banking activity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Low-to-Medium as a primary liquidity rail, Medium as a supplementary rail. Panama's SBP does not authorize offshore digital EMIs (e-money institutions) to provide deposit-taking or corporate payment clearing without a full Panamanian banking license. EMIs incorporated in third-country jurisdictions may operate only through a domestic partner bank's omnibus accounts and cannot directly access Panama's clearing infrastructure or settlement accounts. For the EdTech subscription enterprise, a regulated Panamanian bank account is mandatory as the main clearing account; offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex) may only be used for low-value outbound payouts to independent language tutors or to facilitate vendor remittances in non-USD currencies. Legal constraints: Operation of an EMI without a local license may violate the Panamanian Commercial Code and Banking Law; the company must ensure that any EMI partner does not solicit 'banking clients' in Panama and that the EMI's cross-border payment services are conducted through a licensed correspondent bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing documentation is expected when Panamanian gross revenue exceeds B/. 1,000,000 or related-party transactions exceed B/. 250,000 in a fiscal year; DGI may nonetheless require a Local File and Master File in any TP audit.

Economic substance scope: Panama applies a substance-over-form/territoriality doctrine for S.A. entities: foreign-source income characterization requires demonstrable offshore decision-making, active operation, personnel and bank accounts; passive collection of large platform revenues routed through Panama without local staff or offices is re-characterized as Panama-source trade or business, exposing the company to full 25% tax, penalties and director examinations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud (defraudación fiscal) that can be punished with imprisonment from 2 to 5 years, fines of ten to one hundred times the evaded annual income tax, and cancellation of tax registration.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or direct underpricing may be jointly and severally liable for evaded tax plus interest; a criminal judge may impose travel bans (impedimento de salida) and asset embargoes during the investigation.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Disregard

Statutory sanction: DGI can disregard the invoicing entity, reclassify all revenue as Panama-source, assess 25% corporate tax plus default interest and a 10% extemporaneous return surcharge; amounts may be increased as 'non-deductible' distributions to shareholders.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be required to prove economic substance; failure exposes them to imposed deemed taxable income and civil restitution for corporate losses; intentional use of the shell to hide true ownership also triggers AML sanctions.
Anti-Money-Laundering (AML) Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Under Law 23 of 2015 (as amended), fines from B/. 1 million for failure to maintain beneficial ownership records, and up to B/. 5 million for serious AML violations; commercial agents and resident agents can lose their licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors can be named in Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs); deliberate concealment of a politically exposed person or ultimate beneficial owner can lead to criminal prosecution, blacklisting on OFAC sanctions, travel bans and imprisonment.
