Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Autoridad Nacional de Ingresos Públicos (ANIP), created by Ley 366 de 2023 as the successor to the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) and the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA). Primary portals: institutional site anip.gob.pa and taxpayer e-filing platform Sede Electrónica (sede.anip.gob.pa, formerly sede.dgi.gob.pa).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Sociedad Anonima (S.A.) with a licensed resident agent at the Registro Publico (Public Registry); nominal registered capital suffices and no minimum paid-in capital is required. Step 2: Obtain the Aviso de Operacion (business operation notice) from the competent Alcaldia/Municipio and pay the municipal operating tax (patente). Step 3: Register for the RUC (Registro Unico de Contribuyentes) with ANIP through the Sede Electronica, declaring the ISIC/CIIU activity codes (4791 for e-commerce wholesale platform operations) and obtaining the definitive RUC certificate; non-resident shareholders may act through a notarized power-of-attorney holder. Step 4: Activate the ITBMS (VAT) taxpayer account and, if any local employees are engaged, register with the Caja de Seguro Social (CSS). Step 5: Obtain a digital signature certificate (certificado de firma electronica) and enroll in ANIP's electronic invoicing (facturacion electronica) environment before issuing invoices. Step 6: File monthly ITBMS returns (accounting-taxpayer forms), the annual corporate income tax return (Formulario 200), and, where applicable, the annual informative foreign-income schedule to evidence territorial-source treatment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope / Entity_Level_Taxation: The Panama S.A. is an autonomous juridical person taxed at entity level on income from Panama-source activities only; foreign-source income realized through cross-border B2B marketplace operations is outside Panamanian income tax, although a nil annual return remains mandatory. The standard corporate rate is 25% on Panama-source net taxable income; no pass-through or transparent treatment exists for an S.A.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box classification and no hybrid-mismatch election are recognized under Panamanian territorial law. Available elections include: (i) the Regimen Especial de Pequenos Contribuyentes (special small-taxpayer regime) available where annual gross income is equal to or below B/.36,000, substituting a fixed quota for full ITBMS administration; (ii) the election between the 10% tax on dividends actually distributed and the 5% deemed-dividend (dividendos presuntos) regime computed on book profits in the annual ISR return; and (iii) consolidated ISR filing for affiliated groups meeting the 90% common-control and ANIP authorization conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de-minimis threshold: any person habitually transferring taxable goods or rendering taxable services within Panama must register for ITBMS upon commencement of taxable activities. A special simplified regime ceiling of B/.36,000 in annual gross income allows election of the fixed-quota small-taxpayer regime. Non-resident digital-service suppliers are required to register through a simplified procedure when their B2B/B2C digital services are consumed or used in Panama.

Mandatory phased electronic invoicing under Ley 71 de 2019 and ANIP executive regulations. Registered taxpayers must issue Factura Electronica through ANIP's e-invoicing portal, transmitting invoices to the fiscal platform in real time (online mode) or at least once every 24 hours (offline mode) via the ANIP REST API, with XAdES digital signature certification, ANIP real-time validation, and a quick-response (QR) code. Legacy paper 'impresos fiscales' are abolished for mandated groups; marketplace operators, exporters, and large taxpayers must interface their ERP/POS systems with the ANIP fiscal module.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is a fully dollarized economy with an absolute and unrestricted legal framework for currency convertibility. There are no exchange controls, no capital account restrictions, and no current account barriers to profit remittance, dividends, interest, royalties, or loan repayments. Convertibility is functionally denominated in USD; the practical constraints are limited to correspondent banking AML/KYC screening and US Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) compliance, not Panamanian exchange regulations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirements exist. Panama does not impose any mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation timeline, or central bank registration on export proceeds. Exporters may retain, deploy, and wire foreign currency receipts freely, subject only to standard banking due diligence and suspicious transaction reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) pursuant to Decree Law 2 of 2008; Panama does not maintain a formal central bank. The Banco Nacional de Panamá serves as fiscal agent of the State, while the US Dollar (USD) is legal tender and the Balboa (PAB) circulates only as coins at a 1:1 fixed peg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for locally licensed fintech/EMI arrangements; Low for unlicensed offshore digital EMIs. Panama's Law 5 of 2021 establishes an innovation hub and fintech licensing regime administered by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). An offshore EMI without a Panamanian banking, fintech, or money-services license cannot legally custody client funds, open local payment accounts, or clear USD in Panama. A licensed local partner or a bank-operated payment gateway is required to channel marketplace receivables and payouts. Such arrangements are viable but require contractual liability allocation, sub-account segregation, and SBP AML/CFT compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the Panamanian S.A. are routed through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the foreign shareholder's or lender's bank to the beneficiary account held at a Panamanian Tier-1 bank. There is no Panamanian central bank notification, registration, or prior approval requirement. The transfer's purpose description should state 'Capital contribution - equity issuance' or 'Shareholder loan disbursion', accompanied by a board resolution, updated shareholder registry, and a source of funds letter. Amounts above USD 10,000 will automatically generate bank transaction reports under Panamanian AML law; amounts above USD 50,000 or unusual patterns may trigger enhanced due diligence but remain fully lawful. For a shareholder loan, the bank will request a signed facility agreement, an interest-rate analysis, and a repayment schedule; to ensure a clean deduction for withholding tax on interest, the lender should consent to Panama tax reporting under applicable double tax agreement if any.Trade settlement and payouts to B2B vendors can execute via several permissible rails: direct SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for equal or non-documentary payments; commercial letters of credit (LC), typically issued by the Panamanian bank through MT700 messages, with MT798/MT799 confirmation and modification traffic; MT202/MT202COV for interbank settlement covering underlying trade obligations; and local USD clearing via Panama's automated clearing house operated by Centro de Procesamiento Interbancario (CPI) for domestic payees, allowing next-day USD settlement. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within three (3) months after the close of the fiscal year per Article 714 of the Código Fiscal. For calendar-year taxpayers the deadline is 31 March of the following year. No general extension exists; delayed filing triggers surcharge and interest.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD/PAB 250,000 in aggregate annual related-party transactions triggers the mandatory Transfer Pricing Information Annex (Anexo de Operaciones con Partes Relacionadas) and the Transfer Pricing Report to be attached to Form 820; full Master File/Local File documentation under Executive Decree 194 of 2011 and DGI Resolution guidance is mandated for taxpayers with annual gross revenue above USD 4,000,000 concurrently engaged in related-party transactions above USD 250,000; the documentation must be contemporaneous and submitted upon DGI request.

Economic substance scope: Panama does not apply the OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations to ordinary local-activity Sociedades Anónimas, but formal ESR-type substance obligations exist for entities enjoying preferential regimes (free-zone operations, SEM multinational headquarters, ship/satellite registries). For this B2B marketplace S.A., the DGI and the AML authorities nonetheless apply a de facto substance doctrine: the company must demonstrate real personnel, physical premises, accounting records, and actual decision-making in Panama; a shell structure with nominee officers and foreign-only management will be recharacterized as an offshore conduit, subject to denial of territorial-source benefits, reattribution of income to the non-resident principal, and anti-immunity measures under FATF grey-list supervision.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declarations

Statutory sanction: Civil surcharge of 10% of the unpaid tax plus monthly interest of 1.5%; administrative fines between USD 50 and USD 500 for non-filing; fraud penalty of 50% to 100% of the evaded tax; criminal prosecution under Law 8 of 2010 and the Código Fiscal exposing the entity to fines of up to twice the evaded amount and imprisonment of the responsible officers of 2 to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing applies where directors, managers, or the resident legal representative knowingly sign false returns; directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, civil restitution of evaded amounts with interest, travel bans (impedimento de salida), asset embargoes, and criminal imprisonment.
Shell Company or Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Judicial dissolution of the corporation; cancellation of the commercial registration (Ficha) and the RUC; administrative fines under corporate and AML law ranging from USD 1,000 to USD 100,000; disqualification from obtaining banking and payment-processor onboarding; mandatory inclusion in the risk files of the DGI and the UAF.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors cannot shield themselves behind the corporate form; those who facilitated the shell for tax evasion or money laundering face civil restitution, travel bans, disqualification from acting as directors for up to 10 years, and imprisonment for participation in a criminal enterprise.
AML/CFT Failure and Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties under Law 23 of 2015 of up to PAB 5,000,000 for legal entities; suspension or revocation of the license to operate payment processing; RUC cancellation; aggravated exposure due to Panama's FATF grey-list status including debanking by correspondent banks; money laundering conviction carries imprisonment of 5 to 12 years and asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and the resident agent who concealed the true beneficiary or executed suspicious transactions are individually liable: civil restitution of laundered funds, freezing orders, travel bans, prohibition on leaving Panama, and criminal imprisonment without the possibility of corporate indemnification.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Mis-Invoicing

Statutory sanction: DGI recharacterization of the transactions and upward adjustment of taxable income, with unpaid tax assessed at 25% plus monthly interest of 1.5%; specific fines for non-filing or incomplete TP documentation of up to PAB 50,000; non-deductibility of the intercompany payments; inclusion in the DGI high-risk audit program.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the annual return and attached the false TP annex are personally liable for the additional tax, surcharges, and interest; flagrant mis-invoicing may be prosecuted as aggravated tax fraud leading to civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment of 2 to 5 years; group treasury officers abroad may be co-indicted through international cooperation.
