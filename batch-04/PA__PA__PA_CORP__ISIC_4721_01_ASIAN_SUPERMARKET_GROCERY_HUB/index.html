Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama; taxpayer portal: SISTEMA TRIBUTARIO (sede electrónica at https://www.mef.gob.pa and https://sistematributario.mef.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Sociedad Anónima by filing a 'pública escritura' (public deed) with the Registro Público de Panamá; a licensed resident agent is mandatory. Step 2: Obtain the municipal Aviso de Operación (business license) from the competent alcaldía (e.g., Municipio de Panamá) before commencing retail activity. Step 3: File the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) application online through the DGI's SISTEMA TRIBUTARIO portal, attaching the corporate deed, municipal license, and identification of directors/officers; a foreign entity may obtain the RUC through its resident agent without local presence beyond its registered agent. Step 4: Update registrations for ITBMS status and receive DGI authorization to issue fiscal invoices (electronic invoicing profile). Step 5: Secure the sectoral sanitary permit from MINSA (Ministerio de Salud) for food retail premises; the DGI cross-references this permit in its registration and inspection database. For foreign-owned S.A. entities, the RUC process takes approximately 5-15 business days with a resident agent's apostilled powers of attorney.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation | Territorial_Scope: The Panamanian S.A. is a separate corporate taxpayer subject to corporate income tax (ISR) at 25% on the rate applied to net income sourced in Panamanian territory only; worldwide income of non-resident activities is outside Panamanian tax jurisdiction. Foreign shareholders are not taxed in Panama on distributions or capital gains generated abroad. No pass-through treatment applies to the S.A.; the entity cannot be treated as a disregarded hybrid for Panamanian purposes.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Panamanian law. Available elections and regimes: (i) Pequeño Contribuyente (small taxpayer) optional regime for entities with annual gross income not exceeding B/.36,000, replacing ordinary ISR and ITBMS mechanics with a simplified fixed-rate quarterly tax; (ii) election to carry forward net operating losses under the revised ISR rules (subject to the statutory limitation periods); (iii) ITBMS input credit elections and accelerated recognition rules for capital goods; (iv) election to accrue income under the pre-2010 versus post-2010 territorial rules in limited transition scenarios. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; Panama applies a purely territorial sourcing rule and has not adopted OECD hybrid arrangement rules or GAAR-style election forms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for general registration: any person habitually carrying out taxable transfers of goods or rendering of services in Panama, including retail importers, must obtain an RUC and register for ITBMS regardless of turnover. A lower-tier alternative exists: taxpayers with annual gross income ≤ B/.36,000 may register as Pequeños Contribuyentes and apply the simplified fixed-rate regime instead of standard ITBMS filing and collection mechanics.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% ITBMS rate applies to (i) physical export of Panamanian movable goods, and (ii) certain international services expressly classified as exports by DGI rulings, provided: (a) the seller is an ITBMS-registered taxpayer; (b) the goods physically leave Panamanian customs territory within the statutory period and are evidenced by a formal export declaration (DUI - Declaración Única de Aduanas); (c) the transaction is concluded with a non-resident buyer; and (d) the exporter retains full transport and payment evidence. For a supermarket engaged in domestic retail (ISIC 4721), over-the-counter sale of imported Asian condiments and vegetables is domestic taxable supply at 7%; zero-rating applies only if the groceries are resold and physically exported from Panama under customs control, or supplied to duty-free/zona franca end-customers in the customs regime.

  • DUI - Declaración Única de Aduanas (customs export declaration approved and stamped by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas de Panamá)
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document issued to the non-resident consignee
  • Signed export contract or purchase order evidencing sale to the non-resident buyer
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT/MT103 proof of foreign exchange settlement

Mandatory electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica) applies progressively by taxpayer category; medium and large taxpayers must generate each Comprobante Fiscal Digital in the DGI-approved format, sign it with an X.509 digital certificate, and transmit the invoice file to the DGI's SISTEMA TRIBUTARIO platform for real-time validation. Only invoices receiving a DGI 'AUTORIZADO' status (or an approved contingency code in documented system failure) may be issued to customers. Invoice data must include the RUC, buyer identification, tax base, rate, and ITBMS amount, with unique sequential numbering. Small taxpayers may continue using fiscal printers or approved simplified receipts. Failure to transmit or obtain clearance is treated as issuing non-fiscal invoices and triggers daily fines and suspension of invoice authorization.

The DGI and Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas jointly audit export zero-rating claims through documentary cross-checks against the electronic export declaration system, transport manifests, and banking records. Failure to substantiate physical exit or to demonstrate receipt of foreign proceeds within approximately 180 days results in retroactive reversal of the 0% rate, with the ITBMS becoming payable together with monthly recargo (default interest) of up to 1.5% per month and surcharges of up to 25% of the tax due. Wilful misrepresentation or use of false export documents triggers civil fines ranging from 50% to 200% of the evaded tax, suspension of the RUC and invoice authorization, and criminal liability for tax fraud under Ley 8 de 2010 and Código Fiscal Articles 708-712, with imprisonment available for serious cases. For the target retail operation, the principal indirect-tax exposure is instead the 7% ITBMS charged on importation at customs (creditable as input tax) and the 7% output tax on consumer sales.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is de facto dollarized with the U.S. dollar as legal tender. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible and free of exchange-control approval. Profit remittances encounter no FX conversion or central-bank permit hurdle; the practical limitations are derived solely from AML/CFT source-of-funds verification and bank-level business-risk screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panamanian law imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no conversion ratio, and no prescribed holding timeline for export receipts. Export revenue may be retained in U.S. dollars, used directly for import settlement, or freely remitted, subject only to standard AML/CFT transaction reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Banco Nacional de Panamá (National Bank of Panama, fiscal agent) and Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (Superintendent of Banks); Panama has no conventional monetary central bank under the partially dollarized system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium feasibility. Panama does not license offshore digital EMIs as banks, and EMIs cannot lawfully accept deposits or participate directly in Panama's local clearing infrastructure. Offshore EMIs may be used as auxiliary payment-collection tools for card or tokenized flows, but all treasury liquidity must be consolidated and settled through a licensed Panamanian bank to preserve legal certainty and reporting traceability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on gross dividends paid by a Panamanian Sociedad Anónima; the tax is withheld by the company and constitutes a final liability of the shareholder, without imputation credit for the 25% corporate tax paid.

Line 2 (Ingresos exentos) Income from exempt sources, including foreign-source revenue qualifying under the territorial rule. Attach evidence of foreign-source character and business seat (sede de explotación) maintained in Panama. Late filing: fine of 10% of the tax due (minimum USD 100, maximum USD 500) plus moratory interest of 1.5% per month or fraction; false or fraudulent returns trigger criminal tax evasion sanctions.

Ensure intercompany purchase pricing complies with the arm's length standard if suppliers are related parties. Late filing: fine of 10% of the tax due (minimum USD 100, maximum USD 500) plus moratory interest of 1.5% per month or fraction; false or fraudulent returns trigger criminal tax evasion sanctions.
Line 4 (Gastos operacionales) Rent, payroll, utilities, depreciation, marketing, insurance, and other deductible operating expenses. Deductible only if incurred to produce taxable Panamanian-source income and supported by valid invoices. Late filing: fine of 10% of the tax due (minimum USD 100, maximum USD 500) plus moratory interest of 1.5% per month or fraction; false or fraudulent returns trigger criminal tax evasion sanctions.
Line 5 (Renta neta gravable) Taxable income after COGS, operating expenses, and admissible adjustments, before net operating loss application. Apply the 5-year loss carryforward in chronological order with supporting schedules. Late filing: fine of 10% of the tax due (minimum USD 100, maximum USD 500) plus moratory interest of 1.5% per month or fraction; false or fraudulent returns trigger criminal tax evasion sanctions.
Line 6 (Impuesto causado) Tax computed at 25% on net taxable income, or at the progressive 5%-20% scale for qualifying small taxpayers. Compute liability at entity level only; do not include shareholders' personal circumstances. Late filing: fine of 10% of the tax due (minimum USD 100, maximum USD 500) plus moratory interest of 1.5% per month or fraction; false or fraudulent returns trigger criminal tax evasion sanctions.

Reconcile DGI receipts for prepayments; carry forward unused credits with formal DGI authorization. Late filing: fine of 10% of the tax due (minimum USD 100, maximum USD 500) plus moratory interest of 1.5% per month or fraction; false or fraudulent returns trigger criminal tax evasion sanctions.

Late filing: fine of 10% of the tax due (minimum USD 100, maximum USD 500) plus moratory interest of 1.5% per month or fraction; false or fraudulent returns trigger criminal tax evasion sanctions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and TP informational return (Formulario Nota Técnica) triggered when related-party transactions exceed USD 250,000 (B/.250,000) in a fiscal year; Master File and Country-by-Country notification apply to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least USD 85 million.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (Law 70 of 2019 and Executive Decree 352 of 2019) apply to relevant activities, including distribution and service centers, financing, headquarters, insurance, IP holding, shipping, and pure equity holding. Domestic grocery retail itself is not a listed relevant activity; however, intra-group procurement and distribution performed within the Asian supermarket chain will qualify as a distribution and service center and demands management and control in Panama, local employees, premises, and expenditure commensurate with income. Reliance on the territorial exemption for foreign-source income also requires maintaining the business seat (sede de explotación) in Panama.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 3 to 6 years under the Criminal Code when the evaded tax exceeds USD 100,000, plus a fine of up to 100% of the omitted tax and moratory interest.

Director personal liability: Signing directors and legal representatives are jointly and severally liable; DGI may obtain asset freezes, preventive seizures, and travel bans (arraigo) against the individuals.
Shell Company / Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Fines up to USD 1,000,000 under the ESR regime, administrative strike-off or dissolution, and loss of the territorial exemption for foreign-source income.

Director personal liability: Directors may be fined individually for false ESR filings; courts may lift the corporate veil where the entity is used for fraud, exposing shareholders and officers to civil restitution.
Money Laundering / AML

Statutory sanction: Criminal money-laundering penalties of 5 to 12 years' imprisonment, aggravated to up to 15 years when the laundered amount exceeds USD 1 million; confiscation of proceeds and corporate fines.

Director personal liability: Courts routinely pierce the corporate veil in AML cases; directors face personal imprisonment, civil confiscation of assets, and a 10-year prohibition from holding commercial positions.
Withholding and Remittance Fraud

Statutory sanction: Fine of 10% of the unremitted amount plus 1.5% monthly moratory interest; criminal misappropriation charges for deliberate non-remittance.

Director personal liability: DGI enforces joint and several collection against directors and payroll officers; travel bans and bank garnishments are ordered for persistent defaulters.
