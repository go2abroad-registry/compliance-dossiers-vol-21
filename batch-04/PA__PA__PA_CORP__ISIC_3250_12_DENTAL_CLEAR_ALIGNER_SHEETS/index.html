Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital required by Law 32 of 1927 for a standard Sociedad Anónima; the authorised capital may be stated in the articles, and capital paid-in es la suma de $10,000.00 (diez mil balboas) or such lower amount as designated by the directors, which may be paid in cash or other property. No escrow account or deposit verification is required at incorporation; capital contribution is attested by the incorporating attorney and reflected in the Escritura Pública.

Funding & unlock path: The Benemérito Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá must issue a Fire Safety Certificate (Inspección de Seguridad contra Incendios) for the industrial facility, including verification of adequate fire extinguishers, emergency exits, an automatic fire alarm if the plant exceeds 500 m², and a fire evacuation drill record. The Dirección General de Catastro (Ministry of Economy and Finance) or the local municipality performs a structural load inspection confirming that the flooring and mezzanines can support thermoforming equipment and injection presses. The Ministerio de Trabajo y Desarrollo Laboral (MITRADEL) completes an occupational workplace clearance (Inspección de Salud y Seguridad Ocupacional) verifying compliance with the national occupational health regulations (Decreto Ejecutivo No. 502 de 2016), including machinery guarding, illumination level ≥ 300 lux, noise ≤ 85 dB(A) for 8-hour exposure, and provision of personal protective equipment (PPE). A workplace first-aid station and worker training records on machine safety are mandatory.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas; online portal: Sede Electrónica / Sistema Integrado de Tributación (SIT) at https://dgi.mef.gob.pa

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must (1) appoint a resident agent and maintain a Panama address; (2) incorporate a Sociedad Anónima or legally register a foreign branch in the Registro Público; (3) obtain certified corporate documents (notarized articles, bylaws, proof of registration); (4) file RUC registration with DGI using the applicable DGI form jointly with the Aviso de Operación (business license payable in the respective Municipality); (5) receive the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) identifying the entity for corporate income tax and ITBMS purposes; (6) complete the DGI electronic taxpayer activation and request authorization for issuance of electronic invoices; (7) comply with customs ANA registration if exports or imports are anticipated.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope (Panama-source income only; not pass-through) — A foreign entity must (1) appoint a resident agent and maintain a Panama address; (2) incorporate a Sociedad Anónima or legally register a foreign branch in the Registro Público; (3) obtain certified corporate documents (notarized articles, bylaws, proof of registration); (4) file RUC registration with DGI using the applicable DGI form jointly with the Aviso de Operación (business license payable in the respective Municipality); (5) receive the Registro Único de Contribuyente (RUC) identifying the entity for corporate income tax and ITBMS purposes; (6) complete the DGI electronic taxpayer activation and request authorization for issuance of electronic invoices; (7) comply with customs ANA registration if exports or imports are anticipated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope (Panama-source income only; not pass-through)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Panama; a Panama S.A. is per se a taxable corporation. Panama's domestic law permits an election to the Régimen Simplificado de Tributación for small taxpayers whose annual gross receipts do not exceed the statutory threshold (approximately B/.30,000). Qualifying multinational headquarters may elect the SEM regime under Law 41 of 2007. A group may apply to DGI for authorization to file consolidated income tax returns.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/.0 — mandatory ITBMS registration for any commercial operator; small-taxpayer simplified regime election may be available for annual gross receipts up to B/.30,000

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to (1) exports of goods physically removed from Panama under an approved customs export declaration; (2) sales to non-resident customers located outside Panama; (3) the exporter being a DGI-registered ITBMS taxpayer; (4) the transaction being evidenced by an authorized electronic commercial invoice, bill of lading/air waybill and packing list; and (5) the related foreign proceeds being received through Panama's banking system and, where applicable, repatriated within the statutory deadline.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) validated by Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Panama
  • Commercial Invoice issued through the DGI-authorized electronic invoicing system (Facturación Electrónica)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the export payment in Panama
  • Packing List and export price documentation supporting the customs valuation

Panama's DGI mandates electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) for registered taxpayers. Invoices must be transmitted to the DGI Sede Electrónica through authorized web services or API using the approved XML/PDF-A formats; each invoice receives a DGI-issued digital stamp and authorization code before use with the buyer or customs. For medium and large taxpayers, transmission occurs in near real-time (typically within 24 hours of issuance). Unauthorized or non-electronic invoices are not deductible and expose the issuer to formal penalties.

A fraudulent zero-rating claim authorizes the DGI to claw back full ITBMS at 7% from the date of export, together with statutory interest and surcharges of up to 100% of the tax omitted under the Código Fiscal. Customs Law 41 (ANA) imposes separate penalties for falsified export documentation, while Panama's tax evasion Law 6 of 2011 carries criminal fines and imprisonment of 2 to 5 years. The concerned invoices are disallowed, restricted, and subject to retrospective review for up to the full statutory limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility under a dollarized economy. Both current-account transactions (profit remittances, dividends, royalties, interest) and capital-account transactions (equity injections, intercompany loans, debt repayment) are freely convertible at the official peg of 1 USD = 1 Balboa, with zero requirement for central-bank approval. Panama adheres to IMF Article VIII, guaranteeing unrestricted international payments and transfers. Thus, no FX conversion hurdle exists for profit repatriation from manufacturing operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Panama has no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. The PA_CORP entity may retain 100% of export receipts in USD, may keep them in any domestic or foreign bank account, and may freely allocate them to operating expenses, debt service, dividends, or capital reserves. The only obligations are accounting- and AML-related documentation of source of funds and trade-invoice coherence.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP). Panama does not maintain a central bank; the US dollar is legal tender. The SBP regulates all Panamanian banking and financial activities, and the Ministry of Economy and Finance administers public debt. The national monetary architecture is built on full dollarization without any separate foreign-exchange management authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM for offshore EMIs (e.g., Mercury, Ramp, Clearco, Airwallex, Payoneer). These are not licensed by the SBP and cannot access Panama's local USD clearing infrastructure or open direct settlement accounts at the main Panamanian banks. An SBP-licensed Panama fintech or a bank's own digital platform is the only viable electronic-money channel. The correct treasury architecture is to use the Panamanian bank account as the primary USD home, with EMIs as supplementary collection gates only for card and platform fees, routed through the bank's correspondent network. Automated Clearing House (ACH) feeds from unlicensed offshore EMIs into Panamanian banks will usually be rejected or frozen due to SBP AML circular 004-2023 and Law 42 of 2001 risk-management requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity is received via USD wire on the SWIFT network, using the Panamanian bank's correspondent Nostro account in Miami or New York (typically Bank of America, JPMorgan, or BNY Mellon). Recommended routing: Remitting Bank -> Fedwire/CHIPS -> Intermediate Correspondent (Miami or New York) -> Panamanian Bank (Banco General or Banistmo) -> PA_CORP USD account. Since the Balboa is pegged at 1:1 to the USD, no FX conversion. The wire must include in Field 70 the narrative 'Non-repatriable capital contribution under board resolution dated [date] – accumulation of capital surplus for manufacturing plant construction'. Panama does not mandate purpose codes, but the beneficiary's RUC number, full legal name, and account number must be complete. For compliance, keep the approved board resolution, capital contribution agreement, and shareholder loan agreements on file in the bank's compliance portal.Principal clearing rails are (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct interbank USD payment to suppliers for raw materials (PETG and TPU pellets, thermoforming blades, packaging, dental scanners); (2) documentary commercial letters of credit denominated in USD issued by the Panamanian bank for high-value capital goods and payment guarantees to overseas machinery suppliers; (3) Standby L/C or bank guarantee for freight-forwarding and customs-bond obligations; (4) Panama local USD ACH (IRIS) for payroll, intercompany transfers, and domestic supplier settlements requiring immediate clearing. For European and Asian suppliers requiring cash-against-documents (CAD), utilise the bank's trade finance desk to handle title documents and expedite disbursement through SWIFT.
