Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá - Dirección General, Sección Mercantil (Mercantile Section of the Public Registry of Panama), Panama City.

Governing statute

Ley 32 de 1927 (General Law of Sociedades Anónimas of Panama), as amended, Articles 1-7 (formation and deed content), Article 19 (registered shares), Article 26 (resident agent); supplementary provisions of the Código de Comercio (Articles 1375 et seq.) and Ley 5 de 2015 (registered share regime and abolition of bearer shares).

Formation / constitutional filings

Escritura Pública de Constitución (Public Incorporation Deed) executed before a Notary Public of the Republic of Panama and inscribed in the Registro Público de Panamá.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies under Ley 32 de 1927. The Escritura Pública must state the authorized capital, commonly USD 10,000 divided into registered common shares; the capital may be fully subscribed with deferred payment and no escrow, deposit or banked capital account is mandated. Additional registry inscription fees apply if the authorized capital exceeds USD 10,000.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Certificado de Seguridad Contra Incendios) required from the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá for any physical office premises; structural pre-occupancy load inspection by the Dirección de Obras y Construcciones of the Municipalidad de Panamá; occupational health, electrical safety and workplace ergonomics compliance under MITRADEL (Ministerio de Trabajo y Desarrollo Laboral) and Caja de Seguro Social (CSS) regulations once employees are hired.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies under Ley 32 de 1927. The Escritura Pública must state the authorized capital, commonly USD 10,000 divided into registered common shares; the capital may be fully subscribed with deferred payment and no escrow, deposit or banked capital account is mandated. Additional registry inscription fees apply if the authorized capital exceeds USD 10,000.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Ministerio de Comercio e Industrias (MICI) - Dirección General de Actividades Comerciales; however, no industrial operating or manufacturing license is mandated for the ISIC 6201 no-code website builder activity, which is a service-type operation.

Category: Not applicable as an industrial license. Only the municipal Aviso de Operación (Municipal Business Operation Notice) issued by the Alcaldía de Panamá is required when the company maintains physical premises or hires local employees; no manufacturing permit applies.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF), Republic of Panama. Official portal: https://www.dgi.gob.pa; taxpayer e-services, RUC administration, tax filing and e-invoicing are accessed through the DGI e-Tax/SICAI portal and the separate Sistema de Facturación Electrónica de Panamá (FEP).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the Sociedad Anónima at the Registro Público de Panamá through a licensed attorney; a resident agent is mandatory. 2) File the municipal Aviso de Operación with the competent municipality, or use the Ventanilla Única de Registro de Empresas (VURE). 3) Apply for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) online at DGI e-Tax using Form Solicitud de RUC, attaching the Escritura Pública, Registro Público certificate, legal representative identification, municipal Aviso de Operación, and sworn statement of fiscal domicile; the ITBMS registration is activated on the same RUC by electing the Impuesto de Transferencia de Bienes Muebles y Servicios obligation. 4) Activate the electronic invoicing profile in the FEP portal and obtain the digital stamp certificate. 5) A non-resident foreign digital service provider without a Panamanian establishment must register under the simplified Proveedores de Servicios Digitales regime through a tax representative, obtaining a NIT/RUC for ITBMS collection and reporting.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (territorial scope: Panama-source income only; a Panamanian S.A. is a separate juridical person subject to corporate income tax at the entity level, but foreign-source income remains outside the Panamanian tax base. Pass-through treatment is not available.) — 1) Incorporate the Sociedad Anónima at the Registro Público de Panamá through a licensed attorney; a resident agent is mandatory. 2) File the municipal Aviso de Operación with the competent municipality, or use the Ventanilla Única de Registro de Empresas (VURE). 3) Apply for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) online at DGI e-Tax using Form Solicitud de RUC, attaching the Escritura Pública, Registro Público certificate, legal representative identification, municipal Aviso de Operación, and sworn statement of fiscal domicile; the ITBMS registration is activated on the same RUC by electing the Impuesto de Transferencia de Bienes Muebles y Servicios obligation. 4) Activate the electronic invoicing profile in the FEP portal and obtain the digital stamp certificate. 5) A non-resident foreign digital service provider without a Panamanian establishment must register under the simplified Proveedores de Servicios Digitales regime through a tax representative, obtaining a NIT/RUC for ITBMS collection and reporting.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (territorial scope: Panama-source income only; a Panamanian S.A. is a separate juridical person subject to corporate income tax at the entity level, but foreign-source income remains outside the Panamanian tax base. Pass-through treatment is not available.)

Available election forms

Panama has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity election; classification as a taxable S.A. is per se. Available statutory elections and special regimes include: fiscal group consolidation for corporate income tax; simplified registration and remittance regime for foreign digital service suppliers; advance pricing agreements for related-party cross-border service transactions; election of monthly advance payments with annual final ISR settlement; small-taxpayer ITBMS threshold regime for local micro operations; and the Multinational Company Headquarters (SEM) regime under Ley 41 de 2007 where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 300,000 of annual gross taxable turnover for domestic taxable persons (registration required once the threshold is exceeded); foreign non-established digital service providers must register and remit ITBMS when their annual supplies of digital/e-services to Panama exceed USD 150,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS export zero-rating is available where: (i) the recipient of the service is domiciled or established outside Panama; (ii) the no-code website builder service, visual templates and SaaS platform are delivered remotely and effectively used, enjoyed or economically exploited entirely outside Panama; (iii) payment originates from abroad and is received into a Panamanian bank account; and (iv) the Panamanian exporter holds a valid RUC/ITBMS registration and files the sworn Certificado de Exportación de Servicios / export declaration with the DGI. Sales to Panamanian customers or any local use of the software trigger the standard 7% ITBMS.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera / DUA) for physical goods; not required for pure digital no-code software services
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or original bank statement showing receipt of export proceeds from abroad through the Panamanian banking system
  • Certificado de Exportación de Servicios or sworn declaration filed with the DGI
  • Commercial invoice and signed service agreement identifying the foreign customer and the offshore use/benefit of the software
  • User-activity logs, IP-based customer localization report or server-access evidence demonstrating that the service is consumed outside Panama

Mandatory phased electronic invoicing under the Facturación Electrónica de Panamá (FEP) regime. All registered taxpayers must issue electronic invoices in XML/JSON to the DGI fiscal clearance platform. Online mode requires real-time submission to DGI and receipt of an electronic Autorización before the invoice is delivered to the customer, with API integration for software vendors. Offline mode requires a cryptographic Sello Digital and transmission of the signed XML file to DGI within the prescribed batch window, with QR-code validation. Foreign digital service providers may use the simplified e-receipt module of the FEP portal.

Export zero-rating is a high-risk DGI audit area. If the substantiation file is incomplete or the foreign-remittance evidence is inconsistent, the DGI may retroactively reverse the 0% rate and assess the standard 7% ITBMS on the gross invoice amount, plus statutory late-payment interest. Where fraud, forgery or fictitious exports are detected, the law authorizes fines of 100%-300% of the omitted tax, clawback of any refunded ITBMS with interest, and criminal tax fraud prosecution under Panama's delitos tributarios framework with imprisonment of up to 8 years. Directors and the legal representative may be held personally liable in cases of gross fraud. The DGI may reassess within the general five-year limitation period, which is extended where concealment or fraud is established.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Panamanian economy is fully dollarized; capital and current account transactions are freely convertible with zero exchange-control restrictions. There are no restrictions on inflow or outflow of foreign currency, no differential exchange rates, and no foreign-exchange licensing or approval requirements. Profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriation may be executed freely without government authorization, subject to standard bank compliance and corporate tax filings. Withholding tax obligations apply only if the distribution is solvent and properly documented.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Panama does not operate an export-proceeds surrender regime. There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio requiring exporters to sell or deposit foreign currency with any domestic bank. Receipts may be retained in any denomination, held offshore, or settled through any freely chosen correspondent bank. Financial institutions may still require documentary evidence of the underlying export transaction for AML/KYC purposes, typically a commercial invoice, bill of lading, and customs declaration, but they do not enforce any FX surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Panama has no independent central bank. The Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) regulates the banking sector, while Banco Nacional de Panamá acts as fiscal agent for the government. The U.S. dollar is legal tender, and Panama does not issue its own currency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Panama does not have a passportable EU-style EMI licence. Offshore digital EMIs and payment service providers can be used only as supplementary treasury rails, not as primary bank accounts, because they generally do not provide insured deposit custody, IBAN access, or full credit intermediation. Panama’s SBP regulates payment and settlement services under a 'Try-and-Adapt' fintech sandbox; a locally registered investment company or a Payment Service Provider licence (subject to SBP approval) is required for a regulated in-country fintech presence. For cross-border SaaS treasury, using a reputable U.S./EU EMI for operational collections is viable but must be layered with an SBP-regulated Panama bank for peak cash concentration, capital escrow, and corporate investor wire settlement.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident ownership / offshore legal form (Sociedad Anónima domiciled in Panama but controlled by foreign natural persons)

Root cause: Non-resident ownership / offshore legal form (Sociedad Anónima domiciled in Panama but controlled by foreign natural persons)

Defense evidence: Provide a full notarized and apostilled corporate registry record (Ficha Registral), certified copy of the public deed, current Personería Jurídica, updated UBO declaration with passport copies and original utility/bank statements, plus a signed sworn affidavit explaining the source of wealth and source of funds. Include a current Panama legal opinion letter confirming that the entity is not an 'offshore shell' but holds a genuine business operation (e.g., website template subscription platform) with identifiable employees and customers.
Expected inbound capital from crypto-adjacent or high-volume low-ticket payment rails (Stripe, Paddle)

Root cause: Expected inbound capital from crypto-adjacent or high-volume low-ticket payment rails (Stripe, Paddle)

Defense evidence: Submit a full treasury model showing expected monthly revenue, customer invoice exports, billing-platform settlements, and reconciliation statements. Provide independent traceability: Stripe/Paddle legal terms, tax-exemption certificate (or tax registration) if applicable, and a company bank-reference letter from the originating bank where the payment-rail settlement account is held. If any nuance of crypto or stablecoin settlement is reportedly involved, include a legal memo proving that all inbound settlement funds are fiat-denominated and that no unlicensed crypto exchange is used.
Minimum capital has not been contributed (Panama does not require paid-in capital for S.A., but banks may reject zero-capital structures)

Root cause: Minimum capital has not been contributed (Panama does not require paid-in capital for S.A., but banks may reject zero-capital structures)

Defense evidence: Execute a board-resolution authorizing a capital contribution (even if minimal, e.g., USD 10,000) and deposit it into a temporary internal account; present the notarized equity-transfer statement, the bank receipt proving capital custodia, and a post-contribution certified capital statement. Alternatively, show that the juridical person is receiving paid-in capital from verified shareholders via SWIFT MT103 with explicit purpose code 'Investment in shares (Código 1000)' or 'Capital contribution', with supporting shareholder agreements.
U.S.-based ultimate shareholder or founder with Panama corporate only (potential 'US person in Panama entity' red flag due to FATCA)

Root cause: U.S.-based ultimate shareholder or founder with Panama corporate only (potential 'US person in Panama entity' red flag due to FATCA)

Defense evidence: Obtain a Panama legal/audit opinion confirming that the Panamanian entity has no U.S. sourced income and is not a 'passive foreign investment company' under U.S. rules. Submit IRS Form W-8BEN-E so the bank can evidence beneficial owner's non-U.S. status. For U.S. citizens, provide a W-9 and explain the tax compliance status via an international tax attorney letter; the bank will open the account only after FATCA clearance and will include the CRS/FATCA legal-form package.
Lack of physical office / shared virtual address in Panama

Root cause: Lack of physical office / shared virtual address in Panama

Defense evidence: Provide a signed lease or official corporate registered-agent receipt (of the law firm acting as resident agent), current municipal license (Aviso de Operación) if the company has any local activity, and an on-the-ground compliance officer (the local legal counsel) who can represent the entity during bank site visits. If no physical office exists, use a 'virtual office' arrangement with a reputable third-party provider, and attach the service agreement and corporate resolution authorizing the use of a virtual office address for official correspondence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from shareholders must be executed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for high-value interbank settlement) through a reputable correspondent bank. The 'purpose of payment' must unambiguously state the nature of the funds: for equity subscription use 'Contribution of capital (Aporte de Capital)' or SWIFT field 72/77B note 'CAPITAL INJECTION'; for shareholder loans use 'Loan – intercompany financing' and reference a signed loan agreement. Panama has no exchange-control approval, but the receiving bank will require around USD 10,000+ to evidence source of funds: notarized/shareholder board resolution approving the capital increase, wire instruction from the shareholder’s owned account (not third-party), a corporate certificate listing shares issued, and a record of the internal capital movement (Asset and Liability register). Banks apply a 0.25%–0.50% commission for high-value inbound wires, and funds are typically available within 1–2 banking days. Wire transfers from offshore jurisdictions or fintech wallets may be blocked unless an 'Economic Substance Equivalent' note is attached; therefore, route all investments directly from the investor's bank account in the same legal name as the shareholder in the corporate documentation.For outbound trade and operational disbursements, Panama’s clearing rails are fully USD-based. Settlement is achieved via (a) domestic ACH/RTGS clearing within Panama's Cámara de Compensación Interbancaria (for local vendor invoices); (b) correspondent U.S. Dollar clearing via Fedwire or CHIPS for payments into the United States; (c) SWIFT MT103 for international transfers outside Panama; and (d) Commercial Letters of Credit issued through a Panamanian commercial bank under UCP600 for international best-way trade transactions. For software vendor payments and API platform obligations (e.g., Stripe, AWS), the issuer must pre-clear the vendor name on the bank's OFAC/fintech integration list; payment instructions should include a granular invoice reference, (preferably) remittance via a 'Expected Credit Application' (CTA), and a unique transaction ID (UTI). Bank settlement times for MT103 outside Panama are typically T+1 or T+2. For large cross-border disbursements exceeding USD 100,000, banks may impose an FX rate margin (if not in USD) and require an underlying contract or invoice for FX administrative purposes.Forwards (short-dated USD/foreign currency forwards) executed with an SBP-regulated bank or international counterparty, where the corporate has a documented foreign-exchange exposure (e.g., EUR, GBP, CAD).Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are rare but permissible where the currency of the operational currency (e.g., Argentine Peso, local-currency receivables) is not fully market-convertible; settlement in USD only, but Panama does not impose restrictions on buying hedging products from international banks.Cross-currency swaps (fixed-for-fixed USD/EUR or USD/GBP) available from top-tier global corporate banks with a Panama domicile, but the entity must demonstrate a treasury purpose, not speculative intent.Plain-vanilla FX option structures (call/put on EUR, GBP) with international investment banks, allowable for netting and risk management; no Panamanian exchange approval is required. Note: given that Panama is fully dollarized, and for a low-risk SaaS build, the simplest and most compliant hedge is to keep invoices in USD and only execute short-date forward trades via the clearing bank's treasury desk when a material contractual exposure arises in non-USD currency.

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends paid by a Panamanian S.A. out of Panama taxable income. Where dividends are paid from exempt foreign-source retained earnings, the territorial characterization must be fully documented to avoid DGI reclassification; if the source analysis fails, the 10% withholding may be asserted on the gross distribution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Panama applies arm's length transfer pricing rules to related-party transactions. Mandatory documentation, including Local File and Master File requirements, is triggered where aggregate related-party transactions equal or exceed PAB 1,000,000 (USD 1,000,000) in a fiscal year, or where gross revenue exceeds PAB 2,000,000 (USD 2,000,000) and related-party transactions are material. The DGI may request a transfer pricing study in any audit, regardless of threshold, where connected software development fees, IP royalties, or intercompany management charges are present.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations, principally Law 254 of 2021, require Panamanian legal entities to demonstrate real economic presence when undertaking relevant activities or claiming zero or foreign-source tax treatment. A no-code website builder must show that its Panamanian office, employees, board decisions, and operational control are proportionate to the revenue held in the company. The absence of local offices, local staff, or real business control while foreign-source income is booked through Panama will allow the DGI to classify the S.A. as a shell and recharacterize the income as Panama-source, with treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Restitution of the underpaid 25% corporate income tax, plus monthly interest and late-filing surcharges of up to 50% of the tax due. Criminal tax fraud can result in fines of multiple times the evaded tax and imprisonment for the individuals who sign or direct the false return.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign false declarations can be personally and jointly liable for the tax deficiency through piercing of the corporate veil; civil restitution, asset freezes, passport retention, travel bans, and imprisonment up to five years may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing / Related-Party Abuse

Statutory sanction: DGI adjustment of income to the arm's length standard, reversal of excessive deductions, assessment of back tax with monthly interest, penalties ranging from 10% to 40% of the adjusted tax amount, and fixed fines for failure to file transfer pricing informative returns.

Director personal liability: Directors approving nonsensical transfer prices without documentation may be jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax and penalties. In a fiscal audit, travel bans and seizure of personal assets can be ordered when willful misconduct is proven.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the shell company as a Panama tax-resident and taxable enterprise, reclassification of revenue as Panama-source, denial of treaty benefits, economic substance fines under Law 254 of 2021, and cancellation of the corporate registration where false declarations are found.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in a sham substance structure may be investigated under Panama anti-money laundering law (Ley 23 de 2015); they face civil restitution, confiscation of property, travel bans, director disqualification, and criminal imprisonment.
Dividend Withholding / Profit Repatriation Failure

Statutory sanction: DGI reclassification of the distribution as a dividend, assessment of the 10% withholding on the gross payment, late-payment interest, surcharges, and penalties for failure to file the corresponding withholding return.

Director personal liability: A director who authorizes the repatriation without required withholding is personally liable for the tax deemed collected by law and may face criminal prosecution for misappropriation of public revenues; the corporate bank account may be frozen during proceedings.
