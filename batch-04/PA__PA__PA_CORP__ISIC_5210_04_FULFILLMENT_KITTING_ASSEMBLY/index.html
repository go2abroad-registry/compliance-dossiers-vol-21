Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registro Público de Panamá – Sección de Mercantil (Mercantile Registry Section), Folio Electrónico Division, Avenida Justo Arosemena, Panama City.

Governing statute

Ley 32 de 26 de febrero de 1927 (Panama Companies Act for Sociedades Anónimas), as amended by Ley 5 de 2011 (authorized capital in freely convertible currency, shares without par value) and Ley 70 de 2017 (mandatory registered agent, abolition of bearer shares). Key provisions: Art. 2 (method of incorporation by public deed), Art. 5 (content of the articles of incorporation), Art. 7 (legal personality acquired upon inscription in the Public Registry), Arts. 13-31 (capital stock and share ledger), Arts. 38-48 (directors and first board governance).

Formation / constitutional filings

Escritura Pública Constitutiva (Public Deed of Incorporation) submitted for mandatory inscription as a Sociedad Anónima in the Mercantile Folio (Folio Electrónico).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 19 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital or escrow deposit is required by Panamanian law. The articles of incorporation commonly declare authorized capital of at least USD 10,000 divided into registered (nominative) common shares; such capital may be fully or partially unpaid, and subscribed shares must be entered in the Share Register. Bearer shares are prohibited under Ley 70 de 2017.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Visto Bueno de Prevención de Incendios) must be obtained from the Cuerpo de Bomberos de Panamá following on-site inspection of extinguishers, exits, emergency lighting and signage. Structural and seismic load clearance is evidenced by the municipal use-of-soil certificate and compliance with the Reglamento de Diseño Estructural (Law and F-200 building code provisions). Workplace clearance is granted by MITRADEL upon approval of the Occupational Health and Safety Program, including the accident registry, medical emergency plan and CSS work-risk contribution receipts.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital or escrow deposit is required by Panamanian law. The articles of incorporation commonly declare authorized capital of at least USD 10,000 divided into registered (nominative) common shares; such capital may be fully or partially unpaid, and subscribed shares must be entered in the Share Register. Bearer shares are prohibited under Ley 70 de 2017.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); online portal: Sede Electrónica DGI (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa)

E-government filing portal

A foreign Sociedad Anónima must first register its constitutive deed and registered agent with the Registro Público de Panamá. With that proof, the legal representative files Form RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) before the DGI, either in person or through the Sede Electrónica, submitting corporate by-laws, appointment of resident agent, proof of registered address, and valid passport of directors. The DGI issues a provisional RUC immediately and a definitive RUC after verification. Accelerated issuance applies for companies solely engaging in export services or holding activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: Entity-level tax imposed only on Panamanian-source income; foreign-source income earned by the S.A. is outside Panama's income tax net. The entity is not eligible for pass-through treatment and is subject to annual corporate tax return plus the annual franchise tax (USD 300).

Available election forms

Panama does not offer a US-style check-the-box regime nor hybrid-mismatch elections. Statutory elections available include: (i) consolidated income tax filing for groups with ≥75% ownership upon DGI authorization; (ii) election to use the Multinational Headquarters (SEM) regime tax incentives for qualifying companies; (iii) simplified ITBMS regime for small taxpayers with annual turnover ≤ B/.36,000. No other hybrid or transparent entity elections are recognized.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

B/. 36,000 (approximately USD 36,000) annual gross receipts triggers full mandatory ITBMS registration; however all businesses engaged in taxable supply chains (including foreign providers of digital services) are required to register for RUC/ITBMS regardless of turnover if no simplified regime is applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods from Panama to a foreign destination are zero-rated for ITBMS purposes provided ownership passes abroad and goods physically leave Panama. Export-related services (logistics, warehousing, assembly, kitting, fulfilment) supplied to foreign recipients for use outside Panama also qualify for 0% rate. The taxpayer must retain a contractual export commitment, commercial invoice, customs exit declaration (DUA), and transport documentation. Sales to Panama's Colon Free Zone and other tax-exempt zones are treated as exports for ITBMS purposes.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera/DUA)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from Panama
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or letter from a Panamanian bank confirming foreign currency proceeds credited to the exporter's account

Factura Eléctronica is mandatory. Taxpayers must obtain DGI authorization and transmit e-invoices in XML via the DGI-web service API, incorporating a digital signature, QR code, and prior validation (CUF). Selected large taxpayers are subject to near-real-time digital clearance from DGI; no physical stamps are required. Foreign suppliers must issue e-invoices through an appointed legal representative's RUC.

DGI audit teams cross-check export declarations, customs exit data, transport manifests, and bank statements. False zero-rating claims trigger retroactive assessment of the 7% ITBMS, surcharges (statutory interest), fines ranging from 50% to 100% of the omitted tax, and criminal tax fraud liability for the legal representative. DGI may also revoke RUC privileges and including the exporter in the public tax-delinquency registry.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama has unrestricted de jure and de facto currency convertibility under a full dollarization regime where the U.S. dollar is legal tender. The capital account and current account are both freely convertible. There is no foreign-exchange licensing requirement, no central bank exchange-rate peg, and no permit for inward or outward remittance. Profit remittance may be executed through commercial banks without FX approval; the only practical constraints are corporate solvency, bank-source-of-funds rules, and applicable dividend withholding or territorial income tax compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no legal obligation to surrender export proceeds to any Panamanian authority. Panama does not impose mandatory conversion ratios, repatriation timeframes, or settlement requirements for export receipts. Export proceeds may be held in U.S. dollars domestically or retained abroad at the exporter discretion, subject only to banking record-keeping and AML/CFT obligations.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as bank supervisory authority; Banco Nacional de Panamá as fiscal agent and designated government depositary; no central bank with monetary policy or foreign exchange administration exists because Panama is fully dollarized.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium with a strict licensing condition. Panama authorizes licensed electronic payment and electronic money issuers under SBP regulatory frameworks, and such regulated fintech institutions may offer cross-border collection and payout services. However, an unlicensed offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully maintain a Panamanian account or provide payment services to a Panama-incorporated operating company. A viable structure is to use an SBP-licensed EMI as a downstream payment conduit with the corporate operating account held at a tier-1 bank, or to use a licensed EMI that maintains a Panamanian banking custody relationship. A pure offshore EMI with no local license should not be used for treasury-critical disbursement rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are routed by SWIFT MT103 shareholder remittances from the originating bank to the Panamanian operating account. The bank will not require a foreign-exchange conversion because Panama uses USD, but the beneficiary bank will require the payment narrative to clearly state Equity Capital Contribution, Shareholder Loan, or Paid-in Surplus. The supporting file must include the shareholder board resolution, payment instruction, proof of corporate existence, and the banks source-of-funds form. Purpose-code fields are aligned to account-opening declarations rather than central-bank codes because Panama has no central-bank exchange-control reporting. Large or unusual injections may be held subject to compliance confirmation before posting to an available account.Eligible clearing rails for trade settlement include SWIFT MT103 customer transfers for direct supplier payments, MT101 for corporate-initiated batch transfers, MT202 for bank-to-bank interbank reimbursement, and commercial letters of credit issued by the Panamanian bank and advised through a foreign confirming bank. For domestic vendor disbursements the ACH Panama clearing system, RTP networks, and bank proprietary online-beneficiary systems can be used. U.S. dollar cross-border liquidity clears principally through the Panama bank correspondent relationships with U.S. Federal Reserve member banks and CHIPS or Fedwire. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends distributed from profits subject to the 25% ISR; 5% from profits taxed under the small-taxpayer simplified (CAIR) regime; 15.5% on distributions from non-taxed profits; a 4% annual provisional dividend tax may be payable on retained profits and is creditable against the 10% WHT on later distribution.

Late filing fines up to B/. 10,000; monthly surcharge of 5% on tax due (capped at 50%); interest of 1% per month; non-filing allows DGI estimated assessment and the loss of the company's good-standing status.
Box 4 (Utilidad Gravable) Net taxable income after cost of sales and allowable deductions. Where income is predominantly Panama-source, no territorial apportionment is available; entire net profit is taxable at 25%. Late filing fines up to B/. 10,000; monthly surcharge of 5% on tax due (capped at 50%); interest of 1% per month; non-filing allows DGI estimated assessment and the loss of the company's good-standing status.
Box 5 (Impuesto Causado) Corporate income tax computed at 25% on the net taxable income, subject to the alternative minimum CAIR test. A resident legal representative/authorized signatory must certify the return; DGI may require audited financial statements for gross revenues above B/. 3,000,000. Late filing fines up to B/. 10,000; monthly surcharge of 5% on tax due (capped at 50%); interest of 1% per month; non-filing allows DGI estimated assessment and the loss of the company's good-standing status.
Box 7 (Créditos y Pagos a Cuenta) Quarterly advance credits, foreign tax credits (only for Panama-source recomputation), and the 4% provisional dividend tax credit. Advance payments are mandatory even for non-resident-owned entities; underpayments trigger the 25% rate plus surcharges. Late filing fines up to B/. 10,000; monthly surcharge of 5% on tax due (capped at 50%); interest of 1% per month; non-filing allows DGI estimated assessment and the loss of the company's good-standing status.
Box 8 (Impuesto a Pagar / Crédito a Favor) Final net tax payable (or refund) resulting from the return. Any refund is processed through the DGI's verification procedure and requires full filing compliance and registered bank account in Panama. Late filing fines up to B/. 10,000; monthly surcharge of 5% on tax due (capped at 50%); interest of 1% per month; non-filing allows DGI estimated assessment and the loss of the company's good-standing status.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 250,000 (at par with USD) of related-party transactions in a fiscal year; beyond this threshold, the taxpayer must file the Form 351 related-party informative return and maintain a full transfer pricing study (Local File).

Economic substance scope: Panama applies an OECD-aligned economic substance doctrine: preferential regimes (Sem/SEF) were abolished (2010); a Panamanian S.A. claiming territorial exclusion or treaty benefits must prove real economic nexus (licensed premises, employees, local management, bank accounts, and books kept in Panama). An entity lacking local substance in the fulfillment/kitting business will be re-characterized as a pure invoice conduit, causing DGI assessment, denial of tax-residence certificates, and FATF/AML scrutiny.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to 10 times the evaded tax plus surcharges, monthly interest, and criminal conviction: imprisonment of 2 to 5 years; assets seized.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are individually liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax; conviction carries imprisonment, asset freezes, and travel bans.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of income, retroactive denial of territorial exclusions/substance offsets, DGI penalties up to 100% of recomputed tax, cancellation of the company's tax-resident status.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and shareholders may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax and exposed to criminal charges for aiding tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DGI adjustment of taxable income, additional 25% ISR plus surcharge (up to 50%) and interest; penalties up to 100% of the adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the TP policy face personal fines and, where the adjustment arises from deliberate under-taxation, criminal prosecution.
AML / UBO Registry Violations

Statutory sanction: Law 23 of 2015 AML penalties up to B/. 1,000,000; Law 70 of 2019 UBO Registry fines; money-laundering conviction carries imprisonment up to 12 years; freezing of funds.

Director personal liability: Civil and criminal responsibility attaches to directors, compliance officers, and legal representatives; travel bans and seizure of personal property are available remedies.
