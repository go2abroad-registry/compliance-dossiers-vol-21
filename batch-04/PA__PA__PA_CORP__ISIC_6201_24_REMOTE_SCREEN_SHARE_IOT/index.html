Governing statute

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) of the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); official portal: https://www.dgi.gob.pa with the Sede Electrónica de la DGI (https://sede.dgi.gob.pa) for filings, e-invoicing, and RUC administration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Panamanian Sociedad Anónima obtains its statutory Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC) as follows: (1) file Articles of Incorporation before a notary and inscribe the company with the Registro Público de Panamá; (2) obtain the Aviso de Operación (business license) from the Municipal Treasury of the operating district; (3) file the DGI registration form (formulario de inscripción) together with the public deed certificate, registered agent confirmation, and passport copy of the legal representative at the competent DGI regional office; (4) receive the RUC certificate, which automatically includes the ITBMS (VAT) registration where taxable activities are declared. Processing typically takes 3 to 10 business days; a provisional RUC can be issued immediately for customs and invoicing purposes pending final verifications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: the Panamanian S.A. is an entity-level taxpayer subject to 25% corporate income tax solely on Panamanian-source income; foreign-source income remains exempt under Panama's strict territorial tax system. There is no pass-through classification for a Sociedad Anónima; the entity is always the taxable person for domestic-source revenue.

Available election forms

Panama does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch pass-through election. Available statutory elections and regimes include: (a) SEM (Multinational Headquarters) license under Law 41 of 2007, electable to obtain a 5% effective income tax rate on qualifying services; (b) GKE (Knowledge Economy) regime under Law 41 of 2021 for qualifying software/technology services with reduced income tax treatment; (c) the simplified monthly turnover regime under Law 66 of 2017 for enterprises with annual gross income up to PAB 36,000; (d) cash or accrual accounting election for small taxpayers; (e) the foreign-source income exclusion is automatic under territorial principles and requires no election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No quantitative turnover threshold: any person engaging in taxable economic activity in Panama is required to register for ITBMS. For the simplified monthly regime, eligibility threshold: annual gross income up to PAB 36,000 (Law 66/2017); taxpayers above this threshold must file periodic ITBMS returns under the general regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS at 0% applies to goods physically exported from Panama when customs export formalities are completed and the goods leave within the stipulated statutory period (generally 90 days). Exported services are outside the territorial scope of ITBMS when supplied to a non-resident recipient and rendered or economically exploited outside Panama; qualifying exporters may credit and recover attributable input ITBMS. For remote desktop/IoT software services, eligibility requires that the beneficiary is located abroad, the contract designates foreign use, and the payment is received from abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Aduanera de Exportación processed through SIGA/SICG)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (master and house) evidencing physical departure of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (constancia bancaria de ingreso de divisas evidencing the foreign payment)
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract or service level agreement
  • Proof of performance and foreign use/exploitation for exported software or remote support services

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica) for all ITBMS-registered taxpayers, implemented through Law 41 of 2019 and favorable DGI resolutions. Invoices must be issued as XML electronic documents with a QR code, generated through the DGI Sede Electrónica platform or certified PSA (Proveedor de Servicios de Autorización), and transmitted in real time or near real time for DGI fiscal validation and digital stamping before delivery to the purchaser.

The DGI may scrutinize zero-rated export transactions and, upon finding false or insufficient substantiation, retroactively claw back all zero-rated ITBMS credits and exemptions within the 5-year statute of limitations. Law 76 of 2019 (Tax Procedure Code) imposes a 25% surcharge on the evaded ITBMS, monthly interest at the statutory bank lending rate, fines up to PAB 50,000, and suspension of the RUC. Where the conduct constitutes criminal tax fraud, personal liability may arise under Panamanian law with imprisonment from 2 to 5 years. Export documentation must be retained for at least 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama is fully dollarized and maintains no capital controls, no exchange-permit regime, and no distinction between current-account and capital-account convertibility. Non-resident shareholders may inject equity capital, extend shareholder loans, receive dividends, repay principal and repatriate sale proceeds freely in U.S. dollars without central-bank approval or exchange-form filing. The only practical barriers are bank-level compliance controls (source-of-funds verification, sanctions screening and tax-status validation) and Panama's territorial tax framework: income earned outside Panama is not subject to Panamanian corporate income tax, while dividends distributed by a Panamanian corporation out of Panamanian taxable profits are subject to a 5 percent withholding tax, increasing to 10 percent where the underlying profits have not borne Panamanian income tax. There are no profit-remittance registration or prior-authorization hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory repatriation timelines or minimum conversion ratios in Panama. A Panamanian exporter may retain 100 percent of foreign-currency receipts in any onshore or offshore account and may freely dispose of such proceeds for any lawful purpose. For ISIC 6201 software exports, invoices may be denominated in USD, EUR or any agreed currency; proceeds derived from services economically performed outside Panama remain outside DGI territorial tax reach, provided the exporter maintains contemporaneous documentary evidence of the place of performance and the corresponding client location.

Central bank supervision: Panama does not have a central bank issuing national fiat currency. The de facto banking and financial-system regulator is the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP); the securities market is supervised by the Superintendencia del Mercado de Valores (SMV); fiscal administration is vested in the Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF) through the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI); the Financial Intelligence Unit for AML/CFT is the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF). The U.S. dollar is full legal tender, and the Panamanian Balboa (PAB) circulates solely as fractional coins at a fixed 1:1 parity with the U.S. dollar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: Medium, with material legal constraints. Panama does not operate a dedicated Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence category; any deposit-taking or payment-account activity requires an SBP banking licence or an SBP digital-banking licence under Acuerdo No. 4 de 2019, as subsequently amended, which permits 100 percent digital banks. Offshore EMIs, including Mercury, Airwallex, Wise Business and Relay, generally exclude Panamanian corporate entities because of jurisdictional risk policies and correspondent-clearing restrictions; where accepted, onboarding is typically limited to natural persons domiciled in supported countries. Given the target payment rails (Stripe and Paddle), neither Stripe nor Paddle currently lists Panama as a supported merchant payout jurisdiction. The practical, compliant structure is a U.S. or U.K. intermediate holding vehicle acting as merchant of record for Stripe/Paddle, with the Panamanian Sociedad Anónima owning the software IP and receiving an arm's-length royalty via SWIFT MT103 into a Panamanian bank account. This preserves the Panama treasury hub while satisfying payment-facilitator country availability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: shareholder executes a board resolution of the Panamanian S.A. calling in the subscribed capital; remittance is made by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's foreign bank to the Panamanian bank's U.S. dollar correspondent account, with field 70 Remittance Information stating 'Aporte de Capital – Suscripción de Acciones' (capital contribution for share subscription). If the injection is instead a shareholder loan, field 70 must state 'Préstamo de Accionista', and a signed loan agreement with commercial terms plus the source-of-funds file must be retained for SBP and DGI inspection. Verification requirements at the Panamanian bank: updated share subscription schedule, entry in the Share Register, and a written issuer receipt acknowledging the paid-in amount. Single cash deposits of USD 10,000 or more within a 30-day period generate an automatic currency transaction report; cumulative movements exceeding USD 100,000 may trigger an enhanced source-of-wealth questionnaire. The bank will classify the credit as equity or debt based on the documentary bundle, not solely on the SWIFT message.Clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 wire transfer is the primary rail for all trade settlements, including software subscription fees, intercompany IP royalties, subcontractor payments, cloud infrastructure costs and regulatory fees, using the recipient's full account number, IBAN or account identifier and the receiving bank's SWIFT/BIC. (2) Commercial letters of credit under UCP 600 are available from Banco General and Banistmo but are economically unnecessary for intangible digital services; a commercial LC is used only for the physical hardware component of the IOT telemetry product stream and is issued as an irrevocable sight or usance LC with a U.S. confirming bank. (3) Merchant-of-record payouts from Stripe and Paddle are settled to the U.S. or U.K. intermediate entity and subsequently remitted to the Panamanian S.A. by MT103 as intercompany royalty payments supported by a written IP licensing agreement and a DGI-compliant invoice. Every outbound invoice must carry the corporate RUC and the underlying contract reference; supporting invoices, contracts and bank statements must be retained for at least five years for territorial-tax audit defence.OTC deliverable FX forwards (USD against EUR, GBP, CAD or CHF) offered by Banco General, Banistmo, Towerbank or a qualified international broker bank, with tenors typically up to 24 months under standard ISDA or bank master agreementsCross-currency swaps (plain vanilla USD/EUR or USD/GBP) for structural hedging of material multi-currency receivables generated by Paddle or international distributorsFX spot outright conversions for Stripe and Paddle settlement proceeds using the SBP reference rateEuropean-style FX options on a request basis from Banistmo or international counterparties, subject to a board-approved treasury policy and notional limitsNDFs are not applicable to USD/PAB because there is no local currency float or convertibility restriction; for EUR/USD or GBP/USD exposures, standard deliverable forwards are used in lieu of non-deliverable instruments

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Medium, with material legal constraints. Panama does not operate a dedicated Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence category; any deposit-taking or payment-account activity requires an SBP banking licence or an SBP digital-banking licence under Acuerdo No. 4 de 2019, as subsequently amended, which permits 100 percent digital banks. Offshore EMIs, including Mercury, Airwallex, Wise Business and Relay, generally exclude Panamanian corporate entities because of jurisdictional risk policies and correspondent-clearing restrictions; where accepted, onboarding is typically limited to natural persons domiciled in supported countries. Given the target payment rails (Stripe and Paddle), neither Stripe nor Paddle currently lists Panama as a supported merchant payout jurisdiction. The practical, compliant structure is a U.S. or U.K. intermediate holding vehicle acting as merchant of record for Stripe/Paddle, with the Panamanian Sociedad Anónima owning the software IP and receiving an arm's-length royalty via SWIFT MT103 into a Panamanian bank account. This preserves the Panama treasury hub while satisfying payment-facilitator country availability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: B/. 250,000 (Panamanian Balboa at par with USD) in aggregate related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File and Form 730 filing obligations; a Master File is required for multinational groups meeting DGI consolidated revenue thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations apply to relevant activities including IP holding, management, distribution, and service functions. A remote screen-share/IoT S.A. claiming Panama tax residence or DTA benefits must demonstrate locally directed management, adequate staff, office presence, and economic risk in Panama; a bare shell without substance will be disregarded and its income re-sourced or treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of unpaid tax, surcharge and statutory interest; criminal tax penalties of up to B/. 100,000 and imprisonment from two to six years under Panamanian criminal tax provisions; forfeiture of proceeds and account freezes.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency; criminal conviction triggers travel bans, disqualification from serving as directors, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: DGI may disregard the entity under anti-abuse doctrine, re-source income to Panama, deny DTA benefits, impose penalties up to 100% of unpaid tax, and request administrative dissolution of the company.

Director personal liability: Nominee and statutory directors remain exposed; beneficial owners may be identified through annual beneficial-ownership filings; travel bans and director disqualification can follow.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DGI adjustment to taxable income based on the most appropriate method, disallowance of deductibility of controlled payments, penalty up to 2% of controlled transactions, and statutory interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: CFO, tax director, and statutory directors may be personally liable for negligent certification of false TP documentation; professional disqualification and administrative fines apply.
Dividend Withholding and Profit Repatriation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Panamanian withholding agent becomes personally liable for the unremitted dividend tax, plus surcharge, interest, and a fine equal to the tax not withheld; DGI may freeze bank accounts and issue embargoes.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised an unlawful distribution are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding tax and can be subject to criminal tax prosecution and travel bans.
AML / Sanctions / Illicit Software Payment Flow

Statutory sanction: Under Panama AML Law (Ley 23 de 2015), fines of one to five times the laundered amount, asset forfeiture, and imprisonment up to twelve years for money laundering; Stripe/Paddle accounts and corporate bank accounts may be seized.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officer, and ultimate beneficial owners face criminal liability, personal asset forfeiture, travel bans, and extradition exposure in serious cross-border fraud cases.
