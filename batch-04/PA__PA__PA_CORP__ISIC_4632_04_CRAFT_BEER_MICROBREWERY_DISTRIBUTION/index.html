Governing statute

Ley No. 32 de 1927 (Law No. 32 of 1927, General Corporation Law of Panama), as amended by Ley No. 68 de 2003; Articles 1 through 12 governing incorporation, share capital, corporate purposes, directors and officers; complemented by the Registration Regulations of the Registro Público de Panamá.

Formation / constitutional filings

Escritura Pública de Constitución (Public Instrument of Incorporation) containing the Pacto Social and Bylaws, inscribed in the Registro Público de Panamá and evidenced by the Certificado de Inscripción (Registry Certificate).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) del Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF); all registrations, filings, and e-invoicing are processed via the SICE (Sistema de Información de Contribuyentes Electrónicos) internet portal at https://sice.dgi.gob.pa and, for customs matters, the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Panamanian Sociedad Anónima (S.A.) must first be inscribed in the Registro Público de Panamá and maintain a licensed resident agent. The taxpayer then applies for the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) through DGI-SICE or in-person at DGI offices, submitting: (i) certified copy of the public deed and inscription certificate, (ii) resident agent attestation, (iii) proposed fiscal address and Aviso de Operación, (iv) identification and ultimate-beneficial-owner/PEP declarations of all officers and shareholders. A provisional RUC is issued immediately upon filing; the final RUC, including activation of ITBMS registration and corporate income tax assessment, is issued within approximately 30 days. Non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the shares, but the resident agent is mandatory for service of process and tax notices.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope - the S.A. is a separate juridical person subject to entity-level corporate income tax (25% of net taxable profits) exclusively on Panama-source income under Articles 694-699 of the Código Fiscal. Export income from goods loaded at Panamanian ports is generally treated as Panama-source regardless of where title passes; consequently, a craft-beer exporter's foreign sales margin remains taxable in Panama, while genuinely foreign-source income (e.g., warehousing and resale wholly abroad) is excluded from the tax base but must be separately evidenced. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope - the S.A. is a separate juridical person subject to entity-level corporate income tax (25% of net taxable profits) exclusively on Panama-source income under Articles 694-699 of the Código Fiscal. Export income from goods loaded at Panamanian ports is generally treated as Panama-source regardless of where title passes; consequently, a craft-beer exporter's foreign sales margin remains taxable in Panama, while genuinely foreign-source income (e.g., warehousing and resale wholly abroad) is excluded from the tax base but must be separately evidenced. The S.A. does not benefit from tax-exempt-zone status unless domiciled in the Colón Free Zone, Panamá Pacífico, or another SEZ.

Available election forms

Panama does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification follows the Código Fiscal, and the S.A. is always a standalone income-tax taxpayer. Available elections and special regimes include: (i) Multinational Headquarters (SEM) license under Law 41 of 2007, which may exempt qualifying cross-border service and financing income from Panamanian tax; (ii) optional consolidated-group reporting under Article 699-7 of the Código Fiscal for 100%-owned Panamanian subsidiaries; (iii) application of the Multilateral Convention on Tax Treaty Related Measures (MLI) and double-tax relief under Panama's tax treaty network; and (iv) express allocation/transfer-pricing methodology elections required under Law 254 of 2021 for related-party transactions with hybrid or mismatch elements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold permitting deferral of ITBMS registration: any person habitually selling movable goods or rendering services in Panama must obtain the RUC and register for ITBMS before commencing operations. Wholesale distribution of beer in Panama attracts the differentiated 10% ITBMS rate applicable to alcoholic beverages, in addition to the excise/consumption tax levied at production or importation; monthly ITBMS returns are mandatory even if no taxable supplies occur. A small taxpayer whose annual gross revenue is at or below PAB 36,000 may elect the simplified annual ITBMS settlement regime, but an export-focused brewery distribution entity will normally exceed this threshold and remains in the general monthly regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods, including artisanal craft beer and cold-chain kegs, qualify for a 0% ITBMS rate under the Código Fiscal, provided the seller is an ITBMS-registered exporter, the goods physically depart Panama through an ANA-authorized customs regime, and the sale is made to a foreign customer at an export price. The exporter may credit input ITBMS paid on domestic raw materials (malt, hops, packaging, refrigeration and transport services) and may claim a cash refund or offset for the net credit position attributable to export sales. Zero-rating does not exempt the exporter from corporate income tax on the Panama-source export profit and does not eliminate the separate excise tax on beer production unless the beer is exported free of national consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única Aduanera, DUA) approved and endorsed by the Autoridad Nacional de Aduanas (ANA)
  • Bill of Lading (port-to-port or multimodal) or airway waybill evidencing physical exit of the goods from Panama
  • Commercial invoice with explicit reference to the DUA and the 0% ITBMS clause
  • Packing list and container/keg manifest per consignment
  • Export sales contract or binding purchase order from the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank credit advice showing receipt of the export proceeds
  • Certificate of Origin and health/sanitary export certificate for beer where required by the destination market

Panama has phased in mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) under the DGI-SICE platform. Large taxpayers, alcoholic-beverage producers/wholesalers, and exporters must transmit invoice data to the DGI in near-real-time through the SICE web services/API using digital signatures, with each invoice carrying a DGI-validated QR/security code; the ANA's export declaration (DUA) reference must appear on the export electronic invoice at dispatch. Medium and small taxpayers may use DGI-authorized fiscal printers or the free SICE e-invoicing portal. No separate physical tax stamp is fixed to export invoices, but domestic beer invoices are subject to digital clearance validation before leaving the issuer's system.

Panama imposes no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline; the 180-day reference is the documentary benchmark recommended by the DGI and ANA for matching export proceeds to the export declaration, and unexplained non-remittance may trigger transfer-pricing or fiction-of-realization adjustments. Under the Código de Procedimiento Tributario (Ley 76 de 2019, as amended by Ley 254 de 2021), the DGI may review export zero-rating claims retroactively for up to seven years (extended to ten where returns were not filed or fraudulent conduct is alleged). Inflated invoices, fake shipments, or circular remittance arrangements result in full clawback of ITBMS credits and refunds, statutory interest, fines of 50%-100% of the tax evaded, suspension or revocation of the exporter's RUC and ANA customs registry, and criminal tax-fraud penalties of two to five years' imprisonment; the DGI cross-verifies the DUA, bill of lading data, and bank remittance records automatically as part of its electronic risk-and-match system.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized monetary framework in which the U.S. dollar is legal tender and the Panamanian balboa circulates solely as a 1:1 coin subsidiary. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are entirely free of exchange-control restrictions: there are no prior-authorization requirements for foreign-currency remittances, no convertibility limits, and no restrictions on dividend distributions, profit repatriation, or return of contributed capital to non-resident shareholders. A Panamanian Sociedad Anónima with territorial-source income generally faces no Panamanian income tax on profits generated outside Panama and no withholding tax on outbound profit remittance, provided the relevant revenue is properly sourced outside the territory. The only practical hurdles are bank-level onboarding scrutiny, AML/CFT source-of-funds documentation, and OFAC and UN sanctions screening embedded in the U.S. dollar clearing system, rather than any statutory convertibility limitation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation exists. The craft-beer distributor is entitled to retain 100% of export proceeds in USD or in any freely convertible currency, with no mandatory conversion ratio, no prescribed repatriation timeline, and no obligation to sell export receipts to any public body or designated bank. The sole requirements are documentary: export invoices, customs declarations, bills of lading, and container temperature-monitor logs must be retained to evidence the underlying trade and to support the source-of-funds assessment performed by the receiving Panamanian bank.

Central bank supervision: Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as the sole bank and trust supervisor; Banco Nacional de Panamá as fiscal agent of the Republic; no traditional central bank exists, and no dedicated foreign-exchange administration authority is constituted under Panamanian law, consistent with the full dollarization system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for standalone use. Panama has no dedicated electronic-money-institution or payment-service-entity licensing regime comparable to the EU/UK EMI framework; an offshore digital EMI (FCA- or EMD-licensed) cannot clear USD domestically without a correspondent banking arrangement with a Panamanian licensed commercial bank, and the SBP generally expects a local physical presence or a regulated banking partner to host segregated sub-accounts. The viable structure is a hybrid: maintain the primary treasury account at a tier-1 Panamanian bank and engage an offshore EMI only for ancillary cross-border collection from retail or regional e-commerce distributors, with a written custody-opinion confirming the EMI is merely invoicing agent and not holding settlement funds on behalf of the Panama S.A. Any EMI relationship above a monthly volume of USD 50,000 should be flagged to the AML compliance officer and reconciled against the underlying export contracts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is delivered through the SWIFT network via a plain MT103 credit transfer (or MT202/MT202COV where the remitting party is a financial institution) from the investor's bank to the Panamanian operating account, using the purpose code P01 - Commercial/Business Investment as provided by the remitting bank. Panama imposes no pre-notification, licensing, or validation requirement for the inward transfer; the receiving licensed bank applies its internal source-of-funds verification, OFAC screening, and FATCA/CRS reporting. The contribution is recorded in the Libro de Acciones as paid-in capital and reflected in the statutory balance sheet; although the minimum capital threshold is zero under Panamanian law, a practical paid-in amount of at least USD 10,000 is recommended to meet bank underwriting standards and to substantiate the entity's commercial credibility.Primary trade settlements are executed by SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for direct supplier payments, complemented by documentary commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) opened through Banco Nacional de Panamá or Banco General for high-value procurement of hops and brewing equipment. Panamanian domestic liquidity runs through the local clearing infrastructure operated by the Cámara de Compensación de Panamá, supporting direct acreditación (ACH-type transfers), checks, and local electronic debit schemes denominated in USD with no currency conversion. Regional collections from Central American micro-distributors may be channelled through corresponding local ACH rails or the Central American short-payment system, with each inbound remittance balanced against export invoices and temperature-log evidence. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 3,000,000 of annual gross revenue for related-party transactions that trigger the obligation to prepare Local File documentation and file the annual transfer pricing return (Form 245).

Economic substance scope: Panama's Economic Substance Regulations (Law 254 of 2021 and Executive Decree 229 of 2021) require entities that pursue business activities in or from Panama, or that benefit from international or preferential tax regimes, to demonstrate sufficient local management, premises, employees, and expenditure proportionate to the income derived. A craft beer distribution S.A. must therefore have a genuine local operational presence; a shell S.A. whose distribution activities, treasury, procurement, and decision-making are entirely foreign is vulnerable to recharacterization, denial of tax benefits, and administrative dissolution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Penalties of up to 200% of the evaded tax, monthly surcharges on outstanding liability, default interest, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud punishable by imprisonment of 2 to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign or procure the fraudulent return face civil restitution of the underpaid tax, criminal vicarious liability when they knowingly participate in the fraud, and travel bans imposed pending criminal investigation; the corporate veil may be pierced when the S.A. is operated as a conduit for tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of taxable income on an arm's length basis, disallowance of related-party deductions, fixed fines up to USD 25,000 for non-filing, and additional penalty interests; materiality triggers criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or control the transfer pricing settings may be jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax and penalties; court orders can freeze personal assets and impose temporary travel restrictions while the administrative review is pending.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the entity as tax transparent, denial of the territorial regime, mandatory strike-off from the Public Registry, and fines up to USD 200,000 under Law 254/2021; subsequent business closure.

Director personal liability: Registered local directors and resident agents who vouch for fictitious substance may be jointly liable for the entity's tax debts, lose their license to act as resident agents, and be barred from leaving Panama pending regulatory investigation.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Breach

Statutory sanction: Money laundering sanctions under Panama's Criminal Code: imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, asset forfeiture, fines of up to 100% of the laundered value, and revocation of the commercial license by the relevant regulator.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners can be criminally indicted for failure to implement AML controls; personal travel bans, passport seizure, and prejudgment asset seizures are routinely imposed during Panamanian money-laundering investigations.
