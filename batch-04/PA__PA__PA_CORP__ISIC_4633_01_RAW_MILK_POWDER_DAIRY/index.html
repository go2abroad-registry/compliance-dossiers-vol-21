Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI), Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas, Republic of Panama; online portal: Sede Electrónica de la DGI at https://www.dgi.gob.pa; tax registry system: Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Panamanian Sociedad Anónima engaged in cross-border milk powder trading, the RUC is obtained as follows: (1) incorporate the S.A. by notarial deed and register the deed in the Registro Público de Panamá; (2) appoint a resident agent (legal counsel) with powers of attorney for tax matters; (3) obtain a municipal Aviso de Operación and, where relevant, the Ministry of Health/Ministry of Commerce licenses for dairy product trading; (4) file RUC application Form RUC-01 through Sede Electrónica, submitting the public deed, corporate and director identification, proof of local domicile/address, and notarized powers; (5) receive the temporary/permanent RUC certificate, VAT identification number, and tax obligations card. Foreign entities do not require a Panamanian bank account to obtain the RUC, but bank documentation is needed if applying for export-related authorizations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available for a Panamanian S.A.; entity-level taxation applies by statute to Panama-source income only. A qualifying small trader may elect the simplified small-taxpayer ITBMS regime if gross annual taxable turnover does not exceed B/.36,000. There is no general hybrid mismatch election; Panamanian law does not recognize transparent corporate treatment for S.A. entities. Foreign owners may choose a foreign-resident permanent establishment structure, but this does not alter the S.A.'s default opaque corporate tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

7%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general ITBMS registration threshold applies in Panama; every person or entity habitually performing taxable transfers of goods or services is mandatorily required to register for ITBMS. A simplified small-taxpayer regime is available for annual taxable turnover below B/.36,000, subject to eligibility and DGI authorization.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

ITBMS zero-rating applies to the physical export of goods from Panama, including whole and skimmed milk powder sold to foreign buyers, provided the goods leave Panamanian customs territory and are formally exported. Eligible transactions require the seller to be an ITBMS-registered exporter, the buyer to be a foreign resident, the goods to be shipped abroad, and the foreign currency payment to be made from abroad. Services rendered to external markets may also qualify as 'export of services' only when the beneficiary is located abroad and the services are effectively used or consumed outside Panama. Domestic sales, including deliveries made in Panama to free zones under certain conditions, are not automatically zero-rated and must be separately substantiated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración Única de Mercancías - DUA) validated by Panama Customs Authority (ANA)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill showing the exporting S.A. as shipper and a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list, cross-referenced to the ITBMS zero-rated invoice
  • Certificate of origin and export/sanitary license for dairy products (Ministry of Health registration where applicable)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank transfer/SWIFT evidence, demonstrating payment by the foreign buyer
  • DGI Form 355 (or current export VAT refund/officer form) and the electronically filed monthly ITBMS return

Panama mandates electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Compra y Venta, FECV) for all active RUC holders. Invoices must be generated and transmitted to DGI's Sede Electrónica validation system (real-time fiscal clearance) in XML format before delivery/supply, with digital signature and a unique DGI-assigned QR code/stamp. Resolution-compliant taxpayers use DGI-approved APIs and web services; export invoices for zero-rated operations must also be electronically transmitted. Non-compliant invoicing results in non-recognition of VAT credits and administrative penalties.

DGI and ANA jointly scrutinize export zero-rating claims, especially for commodity traders, through documentary audits, physical inspection, and cross-matching customs declarations with bank remittances. If a zero-rated export is recharacterized because of missing or inconsistent documentation, the exporter becomes liable for the 7% ITBMS on the entire sale, plus monthly late-payment surcharges (approximately 1.5% per month), penalties of up to 100% of the omitted tax, and retrospective clawback of any VAT refunds or tax credits claimed. Fraudulent or knowingly false export documentation, false e-invoices, or simulated customs declarations can lead to criminal tax fraud prosecution, monetary fines, and imprisonment, as well as AML violations if the scheme is used for money laundering.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Panama operates a fully dollarized economy with the United States Dollar (USD) as legal tender under Decree Law No. 84 of 1904 and subsequent monetary integration rules. The national currency, the balboa (PAB), circulates only as USD-denominated coins at par. There is no central monetary authority with discretionary issuance, and no separate capital account control regime exists; both current and capital account transactions are freely convertible without Central Bank licences. Profit remittances and dividend distributions from a Panama Sociedad Anónima are not subject to withholding tax or administrative exchange control approval. The practical hurdles are limited to compliance with bank onboarding standards, FATF-aligned anti-money laundering rules, and documentary support for underlying commercial transactions; the use of USD as the functional currency eliminates conversion risk for inbound and outbound treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender obligations in Panama. Panamanian law imposes no mandatory timeline for repatriation of export receipts and no forced conversion ratio into local currency, given the absence of a domestic fiat monetary authority and the full legal tender status of the USD. Dairy commodity exporters may retain receivables in USD in offshore or onshore accounts; however, corporate treasury best practice and banking KYC expectations require that export proceeds be credited to the named beneficiary account within a reasonable commercial settlement period, typically 30 to 90 days under the underlying sale contract. No surrender certificate is required, but the exporter must maintain complete shipping documentation, commercial invoices, bills of lading, and SWIFT credit advisories to substantiate the economic substance of cross-border milk powder sales and to satisfy tax authority and banking compliance reviews.

Central bank supervision: Banco Nacional de Panamá (BNP) acting as fiscal agent and clearing agent; Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá (SBP) as the banking regulator and prudential supervisor; Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) for external debt and public treasury functions

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium feasibility. Panama is not a member of the EU/EEA and therefore does not recognize European Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licences as passportable banking licences. Offshore digital EMIs, including UK FCA-authorised EMIs, Lithuanian and Irish EMIs, and other non-Panamanian payment institutions, cannot provide direct local clearing services or maintain Panamanian settlement accounts unless they obtain a licence issued by the Superintendencia de Bancos de Panamá under the banking law or the licensing framework for payment aggregators. Many global EMIs restrict onboarding of Panamanian corporates due to correspondent banking de-risking and FATF follow-up concerns regarding Panama. Legal constraints include the impossibility of using a foreign EMI to route payments that require a local bank confirmation for tax filings, customs clearance, or documentary letters of credit. A Panama-domiciled dairy trading company should use a local Tier 1 bank as the primary liquidity anchor and may utilize a foreign EMI only as a supplementary collection vehicle for small-ticket receivables, subject to the EMI's willingness to take Panamanian corporate clients and the availability of SWIFT-compatible payout rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the Panama corporation should be executed via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's account or a related corporate treasury account, with strict adherence to purpose codes relevant to trade and capital contributions. Acceptable purpose codes under correspondent banking practice include Article 5 of the IMF BPM7 classification for direct investment equity contributions (codes such as 6113 for equity capital other than reinvestment of earnings), or the ISO 20022 Ex post plausibility classification when the sending bank requires a transaction purpose code. The remitter must use a clear narrative: 'Capital contribution to [PA_CORP], beneficiary account holder at [bank], internal corporate treasury instruction'. For the dairy trading company, the most compliant routing is: originating bank in the shareholder's jurisdiction -> correspondent bank (usually a US money center bank, e.g., JPMorgan Chase, Citibank, Bank of America, or Wells Fargo) -> in Panama, either via Fedwire to the beneficiary bank's correspondent account or via SWIFT MT103 to the beneficiary account at Banco General, Banistmo, or BNP. The receiving Panama bank will require the beneficiary to identify the funds as equity injections and may request a copy of the corporate resolution authorizing capital increase, shareholder meeting minutes, and a certificate from the registered agent confirming the contribution. No exchange control approval is required because the injection is in USD; however, the receiving bank must verify the funding source against AML policies. This verification typically includes a source-of-funds letter, bank reference from the originating institution, and confirmation that the sender is the UBO or a known corporate parent.Outbound trade disbursements for the purchase of whole and skimmed milk powder will primarily be settled by SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for prepayments and open account transactions, and by documentary letters of credit (LC) for structured trade. The recommended clearing rails are: (a) SWIFT MT103 direct telegraphic transfer from the Panama operating account to the exporter's account, or via the exporter's correspondent bank, with a trade payment purpose code such as ISO 20022 purpose code 'PURP' if required; (b) irrevocable documentary LC issued by a Panama Tier 1 bank (Banistmo or Banco General) in favour of the dairy exporter, with SWIFT MT700 issuance, and settlement through the International Chamber of Commerce UCP 600 rules; (c) confirmed LC via a top European or US bank to mitigate bank-country risk, with MT742 and MT799 reimbursement messages as applicable; (d) standby LC (SBLC) for performance guarantees if required by the supplier. For all disbursements, the Panama corporation should maintain full documentation: purchase order, proforma invoice, final commercial invoice, bill of lading, certificate of origin, health and veterinary export certificates, weight and quality certificates, and LC or SWIFT remittance advice. The outbound disbursements should never be sent to an unverified shell account; the account name must exactly match the exporter's corporate name and the bank must provide a SWIFT BIC and IBAN or account number.
