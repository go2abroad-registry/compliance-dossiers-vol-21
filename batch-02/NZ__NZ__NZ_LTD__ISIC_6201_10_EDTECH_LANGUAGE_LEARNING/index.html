A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not categorically illegal for a New Zealand limited company, but major New Zealand trading banks, Inland Revenue, and AML/CFT reporting entities will reject a bare virtual mailbox for an EdTech subscription company because it fails the registered-office and commercial-substance test. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (New Zealand Inland Revenue Department); online electronic portal: myIR secure online services, accessed via www.ird.govt.nz; export-related customs coordination through New Zealand Customs Service (Trade Single Window).

E-government filing portal

For an NZ-incorporated limited company, incorporate at the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993 and obtain the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). Inland Revenue then allocates the company's IRD number automatically as the statutory TIN for income tax, GST, PAYE, FBT, and RWT. If the entity is a foreign non-resident not incorporated in New Zealand but required to register for income tax or GST, complete the non-resident entity registration through Inland Revenue's Non-Resident Tax desk / myIR, appoint a New Zealand tax agent or resident agent where required, and provide the foreign constitutional documents, director/officer information, foreign business registration details, and overseas tax identification number. Once the IRD number is issued, register for GST in myIR if the NZD 60,000 taxable-supplies threshold applies or if voluntary registration is desired, and use the same IRD number for GST filings, customs export entries, and e-invoicing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Statutory elections in New Zealand include the Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 for closely held companies, and the consolidated group taxation election for wholly-owned New Zealand-resident company groups. Hybrid and branch mismatch adjustment rules, enacted through the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018, may require or permit substitute adjustments where cross-border entity or payment classifications create mismatches. There is no US-style check-the-box election in New Zealand.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in taxable supplies made in New Zealand during a 12-month period; the same monetary threshold applies to non-resident remote-services suppliers making supplies to New Zealand resident consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11(1) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, goods are zero-rated where the supplier exports them at the time of supply or within 28 days thereafter, or where goods are supplied to a non-resident person and are subsequently exported under the statutory conditions. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed and the supply is not directly in connection with New Zealand land or moveable personal property. For the EdTech language-learning subscription platform, remote digital education and tutoring software sold to non-resident end consumers through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing qualifies as an exported remote service zero-rated for GST if the customer is resident and physically outside New Zealand and the effective use or consumption occurs offshore; platform payout statements and customer jurisdiction data substantiate the offshore supply. Because zero-rated exports are taxable supplies, related GST input credits are refundable through the GST return.

  • Customs Export Entry (export declaration lodged with the New Zealand Customs Service via the Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical export of goods from New Zealand
  • Customs clearance or export status report, or carrier certificate showing export occurred within 28 days of the time of supply
  • GST tax invoice annotated as zero-rated and containing the statutory particulars under section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985
  • Packing list or shipping manifest
  • Executed contract, order confirmation, or remote-service agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and the offshore delivery or consumption location, including Incoterms for physical goods
  • App-store payout ledger or statement, or bank inward remittance advice evidencing receipt of foreign-currency subscription revenue from the non-resident consumer; an FIRC-style certificate is not a statutory NZ Customs document

New Zealand has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, anti-fraud digital stamping, or pre-clearance tax invoice approval. Electronic invoicing via the Peppol network is voluntary. A GST-registered person must provide a compliant GST invoice within 28 days after a taxable supply and retain the statutory particulars required by section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, together with supporting export or remote-service evidence.

Inland Revenue scrutinises export zero-rating claims against transport, customs, contractual, and payment records. If goods are not exported within 28 days, or a service is supplied to a New Zealand-resident recipient or performed in New Zealand, the supply is taxable at 15% GST, the zero-rated refund is reversed, and the resulting tax shortfall attracts use-of-money interest and civil penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for failure to exercise reasonable care or an unacceptable tax position, 40% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or similar conduct; criminal prosecution may also be brought for knowingly providing false information or omitting material facts. There is no statutory New Zealand foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, so the mandatory FX repatriation days field is 0, but documentation of non-resident payment remains a practical evidentiary requirement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar is freely convertible across both current and capital account transactions. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not operate a foreign exchange control system, and there is no general requirement for prior central bank approval before inbound share capital injections, outbound dividend remittances, or intercompany loan repayments. Businesses may hold foreign currency accounts locally or offshore, convert NZD into any major currency, and repatriate profits without surrender or licensing requirements. The legal framework under the Companies Act 1993 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 focuses on verification and transparency rather than exchange restrictions. For an EdTech subscription business classified under ISIC 6201, the standard revenue streams from Apple and Google payment rails, B2B software licensing, and content procurement are all treated as ordinary commercial flows with no discriminatory currency treatment. The only relevant statutory screening mechanism is the Overseas Investment Act 2005, but it applies to sensitive land and fishing quota, not to a software company's corporate banking flows. Therefore the practical convertibility risk is negligible; treasury planning should simply maintain clean supporting documentation for source of funds, beneficiary identity, and commercial purpose in order to satisfy prudent New Zealand banks and preserve frictionless convertibility for settlement and profit remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirements exist. Exporters and service providers may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts, hold them in onshore or offshore foreign currency accounts, or convert them freely. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory timeline for repatriation, although all transactions are subject to ordinary bank AML/CFT monitoring and standard balance-of-payments statistical reporting.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed as registered banks in New Zealand and cannot directly access the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's Exchange Settlement System. Under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989, deposit-taking from the public requires a New Zealand banking registration, which most foreign EMIs do not hold; instead they function through a partner bank. An NZ limited company may use a foreign EMI as a supplementary collection node for low-value recurring EdTech subscription revenue, but the EMI should not be relied upon as the primary treasury or clearing counterparty. The legal constraint is that an EMI operating into New Zealand must either hold the appropriate New Zealand financial service provider registration on the Financial Service Providers Register or structure itself so as not to trigger deposit-taking definitions. Feasibility rating: LOW for primary corporate treasury use; MODERATE only as an ancillary marketing and consumer payment rail, with strict customer money safeguarding and no material settlement balance retention.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border associated-party transactions triggers a mandatory contemporaneous Local File, and a Master File is recommended whenever any category of intercompany transactions exceeds NZD 10,000,000. Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding EUR 750 million, translated to approximately NZD 1.1 billion, with New Zealand local filing thresholds and notifications as prescribed by Inland Revenue.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) statute in the form used by the UAE and similar jurisdictions. However, the Income Tax Act 2007 (sections GC 13 to GC 16) and the OECD Multilateral Instrument apply the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) and general anti-avoidance doctrine. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are used for permanent establishment attribution and to determine whether a New Zealand EdTech company possesses real commercial substance. A company with no local employees, no physical premises, and no active decision-making in New Zealand is exposed to treaty denial and the anti-abuse provisions of section BG 1.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Return

Statutory sanction: Section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes a civil evasion penalty of 150% of the avoidable tax shortfall. Section 144 creates a criminal offence carrying imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to NZD 50,000 for an individual (up to NZD 250,000 for a company).

Director personal liability: Directors who cause, participate in, or sign a false IR4 are personally liable under section 148 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 for the civil shortfall and penalties. Criminal conviction may trigger personal asset forfeiture, disqualification under the Companies Act 1993, and a border alert or travel blacklisting for unpaid revenue debts while the director attempts to leave New Zealand.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 sections GC 13 to GC 16 permit Inland Revenue to substitute an arm's-length price and adjust the taxable income. Section 141E of the Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes an unacceptable tax position penalty of 20% to 100% of the shortfall depending on the level of care and culpability, plus use-of-money interest and withholding tax restoration on the adjusted cross-border payments.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve sham intercompany contracts expose themselves to civil penalty liability under section 148 of the Tax Administration Act 1994. In cases of deliberate concealment, criminal prosecution can lead to imprisonment for up to 5 years, personal liability for the corporate tax shortfall by piercing the corporate veil, and travel blacklisting where unpaid tax liabilities exceed NZD 10,000 and departure is attempted.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping and AML-Tainted Structure

Statutory sanction: Section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 cancels the tax benefit as a void tax avoidance arrangement; the Multilateral Instrument's Principal Purpose Test denies treaty relief; the Companies Act 1993 allows the Registrar to strike off an inactive company for failure to meet annual return obligations; and promoter penalty provisions under section 141K of the Tax Administration Act 1994 expose scheme promoters to fines of up to NZD 50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell company used for treaty shopping or improper profit shifting face personal liability for unpaid corporate tax and penalties where the corporate form is used as a facade. The Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009 permits civil asset confiscation; fraudulent trading charges can result in imprisonment for up to 5 years; and directors may be blacklisted or prevented from departing New Zealand while significant corporate tax liabilities remain collectable.
