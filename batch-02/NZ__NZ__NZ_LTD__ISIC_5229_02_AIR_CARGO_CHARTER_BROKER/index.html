Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Companies Act 1993 does not impose any minimum paid-up capital requirement on an NZ limited company. Paid-up capital may be nil and there is no blocked escrow account requirement. Non-resident ownership is permitted, subject to the Overseas Investment Act 2005 if the business acquires sensitive New Zealand land or significant business assets.

Funding & unlock path: Before occupying any office, depot or warehouse, the company must obtain a building warrant of fitness or building occupancy certificate from the territorial authority, a fire evacuation scheme approved by Fire and Emergency New Zealand, and a fire safety certificate or compliance schedule for the fire protection systems. WorkSafe New Zealand clearance is required for workplace safety, first aid arrangements, asbestos management and hazardous substance controls. If cargo screening or aircraft loading areas are used, the Civil Aviation Authority must approve the security programme and operational load limits, and the structural load capacity of the premises must be certified by a chartered professional engineer.

Paid-in capital rule: None. The Companies Act 1993 does not impose any minimum paid-up capital requirement on an NZ limited company. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) – myIR online portal (https://www.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity seeking to register for tax in New Zealand, the process is as follows: (1) Determine if the entity has a New Zealand tax presence (e.g., carrying on a taxable activity, deriving New Zealand-sourced income, or making supplies subject to GST). (2) Apply for an IRD number using the 'Non-resident company IRD number application' form (IR742) or via the myIR portal if an agent is appointed. The application requires the entity's legal name, registered office address, country of incorporation, details of directors, and a New Zealand agent or contact person if no physical presence exists. (3) If the entity expects to make taxable supplies exceeding NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period, it must also register for GST using the 'GST registration' form (IR322) or online via myIR. (4) Provide supporting documents such as certificate of incorporation, proof of identity for directors, and a declaration of the entity's tax residency status. (5) The IRD will issue an IRD number (which serves as the GST registration number) and a GST registration certificate. (6) For non-resident companies, a New Zealand agent may be required for service of notices and compliance. The entire process can be completed electronically, and the IRD typically issues the IRD number within 10 working days.

Available election forms

None. New Zealand does not have a check-the-box election for limited companies. However, hybrid mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 apply to cross-border arrangements, and entities may elect to be a look-through company (LTC) only if they meet specific criteria, but this is not applicable to a standard limited company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (annual taxable supplies threshold; applies to both resident and non-resident suppliers of goods and services in New Zealand, including remote services)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: zero-rating applies if the goods are exported from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply, and the supplier retains evidence of export. For services: zero-rating applies to services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the services are not supplied to a person in New Zealand and are not directly connected with land or goods in New Zealand. For an air cargo charter broker, services such as freight forwarding, charter brokerage, and logistics coordination supplied to non-resident clients may qualify for zero-rating if the recipient is outside New Zealand and the services are not physically performed in New Zealand. The supplier must obtain and retain sufficient evidence of the recipient's non-resident status and the offshore nature of the services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUSED) – submitted via the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (for air cargo)
  • Bank statement or SWIFT confirmation evidencing receipt of payment from the overseas customer

No mandatory electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance regime exists in New Zealand. The Inland Revenue Department supports voluntary e-invoicing via the Peppol network, but there is no requirement for digital stamping or API-based clearance. GST returns are filed electronically via myIR, but invoices may be issued in paper or electronic format without prior approval.

Under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and the Tax Administration Act 1994, incorrect zero-rating claims may result in shortfall penalties of up to 20% of the tax shortfall for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% for fraudulent intent. Additionally, interest on unpaid GST applies from the due date. The Commissioner of Inland Revenue may retrospectively adjust GST assessments and claw back any incorrectly claimed zero-rating, with a four-year time bar for assessments (extended to seven years in cases of fraud or evasion). Criminal prosecution may also be pursued for deliberate evasion, leading to fines up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully free-floating currency regime with no exchange-control statutes, no surrender requirements and no capital-account convertibility licensing. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand supervises banks and the broader financial system under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989, while the Overseas Investment Office may screen certain significant offshore share acquisitions and farm or sensitive-land transactions; however, ordinary trading income and capital for an international air cargo charter broker are freely convertible. Current-account transactions such as freight services, brokerage commissions, fuel purchases, landing fees and operating expenses settle in any convertible currency without foreign-exchange permission. Capital-account injections, intercompany loans and profit or dividend remittances require only standard bank AML/CFT due diligence rather than central-bank approval. Dividends to non-residents remain subject to New Zealand withholding tax at the applicable statutory or treaty-reduced rate, but this is a taxation consideration, not a currency restriction. There is no statutory timeline for converting export or inward foreign-currency receipts, and no obligation to surrender foreign currency to the Reserve Bank. Banks may request source-of-funds evidence and commercial rationale for large transactions, but once cleared the currency is fully fungible. Non-resident directors and shareholders may operate the NZ limited company freely, and brokerage margin accounts can be funded in NZD after conversion at prevailing market rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand imposes no export proceeds surrender requirements, no statutory conversion timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export and foreign-currency receipts may be held in offshore or domestic foreign-currency accounts, converted to NZD at market rate, or deployed directly for trade settlement.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Offshore fintech EMIs are not legally barred from servicing a New Zealand entity, provided they are appropriately registered under the New Zealand AML/CFT Act 2009 with the Department of Internal Affairs or operate through a New Zealand bank-backed e-money programme. They are viable as ancillary rails for multi-currency wallets, sub-ledger payments and low-value supplier disbursements, but they lack statutory scale, trade finance capability and the institutional credibility required for capital escrow, commercial letters of credit and charter hire settlement. The primary treasury and onboarding relationship should therefore sit with Tier 1 Australian-owned New Zealand banks; the EMI may supplement but never replace the clearing bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder or parent funding should be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the remitter's bank to the New Zealand company's NZD account at a Tier 1 local bank. The payment narrative must clearly identify the transaction as 'equity subscription' or 'shareholder loan to NZ_LTD', supported by a Companies Office share allotment return for equity or an executed shareholder loan agreement for debt. No central-bank approval or FX registration is required for NZD conversion; the receiving bank will perform source-of-funds verification and tax-status checks. Turnover: A New Zealand limited company is legally required to prepare audited financial statements if it meets one or more of the following quantitative thresholds: total consolidated assets at balance date equal or exceed NZ$60 million; total consolidated revenues/turnover equal or exceed NZ$30 million; or 100 or more full-time-equivalent employees. A compulsory audit is also required for any entity that is an FMC reporting entity under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, regardless of those size tests, including issuers, managers of managed investment schemes, banks, licensed insurers, and supervised non-bank deposit takers. The audit must be carried out by a qualified independent certified auditor licensed under the auditor licensing regime administered by the Financial Markets Authority, and the company must maintain proper accounting records sufficient to support true and fair financial statements.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; fully imputed dividends paid to New Zealand resident companies incur 0% additional tax. Treaty jurisdictions may reduce the NRWT rate to 15% (or 5% on certain significant shareholdings under specific DTAs).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File or Master File monetary threshold in New Zealand, but contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is strongly expected where the aggregate annual value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZD 1 million; comprehensive OECD-standard documentation is recommended where such transactions exceed NZD 10 million. A Country-by-Country Report is mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue is EUR 750 million or higher, filed through Inland Revenue.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in the UAE. However, a company incorporated in New Zealand is automatically a tax resident and is taxed on worldwide income. A foreign-incorporated company is resident if its head office, centre of management or control is in New Zealand. General anti-avoidance rules under section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and the business continuity tests require genuine commercial substance and a real business presence. A pure shell company with nominee directors and no local management will not be treated as a separate taxable entity for NZ purposes if effective control is exercised offshore; conversely, a New Zealand-incorporated shell used to shift profits may be subject to CFC attribution and anti-avoidance penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under section 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994, imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to NZD 50,000, together with a civil shortfall penalty of 100% of the evaded tax and late payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign, authorise or participate in a false return are jointly and severally liable. Courts may order director disqualification under the Companies Act 1993, issue departure prohibition orders for unpaid tax debts above NZD 10,000, and pierce the corporate veil to recover the tax from personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Sham Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may re-determine taxable income under the transfer pricing rules (sections GC 6-GC 14 ITA 2007), impose a 20% unacceptable tax position penalty or 100% abusive tax position penalty, plus interest. In sham findings, the entire arrangement may be void for tax purposes.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the mispricing or signed tax returns relying on contrived pricing face personal liability for the shortfall. Civil recovery actions may be brought against directors for amounts that cannot be collected from the company, and criminal sanctions under section 143A may apply for fraudulent provisioning.
Unpaid Withholding Taxes (PAYE, NRWT, RWT)

Statutory sanction: Under section 159 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, a director penalty equal to 100% of the unremitted amount is imposed on every director of the company, in addition to late payment penalties (1% then 4% monthly) and use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors become personally liable for the exact amount of unremitted withholding tax once a 30-day demand is ignored. The Inland Revenue can issue a statutory demand and bankruptcy proceedings based on the director penalty, and the company's corporate veil is pierced statutorily to allow recovery from the director's personal estate.
AML/CFT Breaches by Unlicensed Broker

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to NZD 2 million for a company and NZD 300,000 for an individual; criminal fines up to NZD 100,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years. The court may order forfeiture of assets that constitute proceeds of money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors who are wilfully blind or knowingly involved in AML/CFT failures are personally subject to criminal prosecution, fines and imprisonment. Directors may also be disqualified under the Companies Act and travel restrictions may be imposed pending outstanding civil penalty liabilities.
