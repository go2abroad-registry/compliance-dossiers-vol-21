A registered office service may be used for Companies Register purposes if it supplies a physical New Zealand street address and can receive legal documents; a PO Box alone is prohibited. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand (Te Tari Taake), electronic portal: myIR (myIR.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity intending to register as a New Zealand taxpayer must obtain an IRD number (New Zealand Tax Identification Number) by applying through the myIR online portal using the non-resident company registration application, attaching the certificate of incorporation from the home jurisdiction, constitutional documents, details of directors and significant shareholders or beneficial owners, and an authorisation for a New Zealand-based tax agent if appointed; the Inland Revenue Department issues the IRD number once identity and substance checks are complete. For indirect tax purposes, the entity must separately register for Goods and Services Tax (GST) in the same myIR account; GST registration is mandatory where taxable supplies in New Zealand exceed NZD 60,000 on a rolling 12-month basis, and a non-resident supplier of remote or digital services may register under the remote services regime to zero-rate B2B supplies and collect GST on B2C supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in New Zealand. A qualifying closely held New Zealand limited company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under the Income Tax Act 2007 for tax-transparent treatment of underlying income, and New Zealand applies comprehensive hybrid and branch mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 to prevent cross-border mismatch outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (including remote services supplied by offshore suppliers to New Zealand consumers), with voluntary registration permitted below this threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 zero-rates (0%) the supply of goods that the supplier physically exports from New Zealand within 28 days of supply, evidenced by customs export certification; supplies of services to a non-resident customer contractually outside New Zealand; and qualifying remote digital services supplied to non-resident GST-registered businesses under the B2B remote services zero-rating rules. A B2B cross-border marketplace operator must document the non-resident status of the buyer, the physical movement of goods or offshore location of services, and the payment consideration in order to substantiate the 0% rate and any corresponding input tax credit claims.

  • Customs export entry lodged via the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from New Zealand within 28 days of supply
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bank statement or SWIFT wire transaction record evidencing receipt of foreign currency consideration (FIRC-equivalent for New Zealand purposes)

New Zealand does not impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control regime; electronic invoicing operates on a voluntary basis through the Peppol interoperability framework, and taxpayers must retain complete GST records for the statutory seven-year audit period without any requirement for anti-fraud digital stamping or fiscal device clearance.

If the Inland Revenue Department or New Zealand Customs Service disallows export zero-rating due to inadequate documentation, failure to export within the statutory timeframe, or unverified foreign-exchange repatriation within 180 days, the supplier must return 15% GST on the supply, and the Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes use-of-money interest, civil shortfall penalties scaled from 20% for lack of reasonable care up to 150% for evasion or abusive tax positions, plus criminal penalties including fines and imprisonment for knowingly false statements or fraudulent export claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has no statutory foreign-exchange control regime; the Reserve Bank of New Zealand is responsible for monetary policy, financial stability, and the operation of a freely convertible currency. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) trades without capital-control restrictions on either current-account or capital-account transactions. Non-resident shareholders, including offshore corporate and trust investors, are generally permitted to hold 100% equity in a New Zealand company without any requirement to surrender export proceeds to the central bank. Profits, dividends, and loan repayments may be remitted abroad without prior exchange-control approval; the only relevant hurdles are tax obligations, such as approved issuer levy or non-resident withholding tax, and the Overseas Investment Act screening thresholds for sensitive land and fishing assets. Legitimate B2B marketplace revenues held offshore may also be repatriated to NZD and credited to a licensed New Zealand bank account without conversion restrictions. In practice, banks apply anti-money-laundering and Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) customer due diligence, classification of the payment purpose, and balance-of-payments coding, but this is statistical and compliance-driven rather than exchange control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio exist under New Zealand law. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts indefinitely, hold them in approved foreign-currency settlement accounts, or convert to NZD at any licensed bank rate. All receipts are subject to normal AML/CFT monitoring, sanctions screening, and balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand, but there is no compulsory surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital-only EMIs are mostly not viable as primary banks for a New Zealand corporate. EMI service providers do not hold a New Zealand banking licence and cannot provide RBNZ-regulated deposit-taking products unless they are registered as financial service providers under the Financial Service Providers Act and appear on the Financial Service Providers Register; they must also be registered as AML/CFT reporting entities with the Department of Internal Affairs. They can complement but not replace a Tier-1 domestic clearing bank for SWIFT settlement, capital escrow, and trade finance requirements. Overall viability for primary treasury routing is Low to Medium, restricted to ancillary payment aggregation such as Alibaba_Pay collection or sub-account settlement in non-core currencies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand does not impose a general statutory dollar threshold for Local File or Master File preparation, but Country-by-Country Reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue above NZD 1.1 billion. For ordinary cross-border related-party transactions, IRD expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation to exist for all material controlled transactions, commonly beginning at annual intercompany transaction values around NZD 1 million. The documentation must support the arm's length outcome and the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no separate Economic Substance Regulations in the manner of the UAE or Cayman Islands. However, the general anti-avoidance rule in s BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, together with the DTA permanence and source rules, requires an NZ Ltd conducting a B2B cross-border marketplace to demonstrate real managerial, operational, and commercial substance in New Zealand. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA), including buyer-seller matching, payment settlement oversight, merchant due diligence, and platform risk management, should be carried out in New Zealand if the company wishes to assert treaty residence, claim arm's length relief, and avoid recharacterisation of its intercompany arrangements. The absence of economic substance may permit IRD to disregard the corporate form, deny treaty benefits, or reconstruct transactions under ss GA 1 and GB 1.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Income Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 ss 141E and 143B: a 150% shortfall penalty on the evaded tax, a fine of up to NZD 50,000 (or greater under the Criminal Justice scales for certain offences), and imprisonment for up to five years for a knowing and intended evasion. IRD may also amend assessments indefinitely where fraud or evasion is proven, removing the usual statute bar.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise or participate in the false return may be prosecuted personally as principals of the evasion. The corporate veil can be pierced under the Companies Act 1993 ss 135 (reckless trading) and 136 (incurring obligations without reasonable belief) to seek civil asset restitution from directors personally. IRD may issue departure prohibition orders, effectively blacklisting non-resident and resident directors from leaving New Zealand while significant tax liabilities remain unpaid, and courts may order director disqualification.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 ss GC 6-GC 8 empowers the Commissioner to reallocate income and disallow deductions to reflect the arm's length consideration. The reallocation is reinforced by s BG 1 for arrangements lacking commercial substance. A shortfall penalty of up to 100% may be imposed for an abusive tax position under s 141D, and the original incorrect deduction may attract integrity and late-payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute a sham transfer pricing structure are jointly implicated in the company's tax offence. The corporate veil may be pierced where the transfer pricing penalties render the company insolvent, and a director may be ordered to contribute to the company's assets under the Companies Act 1993. Courts can impose personal liability for unpaid tax where the director acted as the controlling mind in the profit-shifting scheme, and criminal conviction may suspend directors from managing companies.
Dividend Stripping, NRWT Evasion, and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 ss RF 2 and RF 8 subjects gross distributions to NRWT at 30% (or 15% where fully imputed). Section BG 1 and ss GA 1/GB 1 allow the Commissioner to recharacterise the loan or fee as a dividend and to deny treaty relief. Additional shortfall penalties may reach 150% for evasion, and any attempt to rely on a DTA while failing the beneficial ownership or limitation-of-benefits test is treated as a fatal compliance infraction.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or effect the dividend-stripping arrangement are personally accountable where the company becomes unable to satisfy the NRWT liability. The courts may lift the corporate veil to recover the distributed property from directors and shareholders. Imprisonment terms apply to criminal evasion under s 143B; directors who are non-residents may also face asset forfeiture, travel bans, and blacklisting on the IRD debt enforcement register until the full tax arrears and penalties are settled.
