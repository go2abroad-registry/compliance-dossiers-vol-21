Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, a functional unit of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), operating the New Zealand Companies Register online at www.companiesoffice.govt.nz and register.companiesoffice.govt.nz, authenticated via the RealMe identity service.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No 105), as amended. Core provisions: ss 10–16 (registration and status of company), ss 12–13 (application and Registrar's duty to incorporate), ss 20–24 (company name rules), ss 31–34 (constitution), ss 124–126 (issue and allotment of shares), ss 151–153 (director eligibility, number, and consent), s 189 (registered office), and ss 366–372 (fees and enforcement). The Companies (Fees) Regulations as amended govern the incorporation fee.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic incorporation application commonly referenced as Form NZCN ('Apply to Register a Company'), submitted under s 12(1) of the Companies Act 1993, culminating in the statutory Certificate of Incorporation (Form CIR) issued under s 15.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake), Government of New Zealand; online portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz); associated registries: New Zealand Companies Office and New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) Register

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must: (1) register as an overseas company with the New Zealand Companies Office and obtain an NZBN; (2) apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number using form IR596 (Non-Resident Company Registration) or the myIR online onboarding process; (3) register for GST if taxable supplies connected with New Zealand exceed the NZD 60,000 threshold or if a voluntary/non-resident supplier election is made; (4) obtain a Customs Client Code from New Zealand Customs Service for electronic export declarations; (5) retain the IRD number confirmation and GST registration certificate for invoicing and banking counterparty verification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; New Zealand resident companies are taxed at 28% on worldwide income; non-resident companies are taxed on New Zealand-sourced income only (territorial scope); no state or provincial income taxes; no general tax-exempt zone applies in New Zealand, though specific statutory regimes (e.g., petroleum mining and certain offshore activities) carry bespoke fiscal treatment; corporate pass-through is unavailable by default, with the only elective pass-through model being the Look-Through Company (LTC) regime for qualifying close-held companies.

Available election forms

Look-Through Company (LTC) election under the Income Tax Act 2007 (available to close-held qualifying companies with at most five owners and no corporate shareholder, each owner holding 10% or more of the entity); hybrid mismatch elections and disclosures under the OECD/G20 BEPS hybrid rules, including optional 'disregarded entity' designations and ordering-rule elections (s FH 1 et seq.); non-resident GST registration election (voluntary registration prior to meeting the threshold); the s 11A non-resident services zero-rating election conducted via documentation of residency status; buyer-created tax-invoice election under the GST Act 1986.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (annual taxable turnover for supplies connected with New Zealand); non-resident suppliers of remote services must register for GST if total supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period, excluding supplies received by GST-registered New Zealand businesses under the reverse-charge mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under s 11(1) of the GST Act 1986 where the supplier exports the goods within 28 days after the time of supply (extendable by the Commissioner for cause, including up to 60 days where goods remain on a vessel or under Customs control); the goods must not be supplied while in New Zealand. Services to non-residents are zero-rated under s 11A where the services are supplied to a person who is non-resident, the services are not supplied in connection with New Zealand land or goods, and the supplier retains sufficient documentary evidence of the recipient's non-resident status. For reverse-logistics operations (ISIC 5229 / NAICS 488510), zero-rating applies to inbound return-merchandise quality-grading and refurbishment services rendered for overseas principals, provided the returned goods are re-exported and the service recipient is a non-resident GST-unregistered party; compliance requires cogent RMA records, customs export documentation, and evidence of overseas settlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUSPRS/Trade Single Window filing)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / House Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing FX settlement in New Zealand
  • Packing List and Quality-Grading Report
  • Return Merchandise Authorization (RMA) and related reverse-logistics instruction records
  • Zero-rated tax invoice and s 11/s 11A substantiation certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate is imposed by New Zealand. Electronic invoicing is optional, with the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE) and IRD promoting Peppol BIS 3.0 interoperability for B2G invoicing via accredited Peppol access points. GST-registered suppliers must issue a compliant tax invoice containing the supplier's IRD number, invoice date, supplier and recipient names and addresses, description and quantity of goods/services, price exclusive of GST, GST amount, and GST rate; buyer-created tax invoices and credit notes are permissible under specified conditions. Digital tax invoices are recognised; records must be made available electronically to the Commissioner of Inland Revenue on demand.

New Zealand imposes no exchange-control statutory deadline for inward FX repatriation; the 180-day figure is a pipeline monitoring convention for risk assessment. Substantive audit risk flows from the GST Act 1986 and Tax Administration Act 1994: if export occurs outside the 28-day (or extended 60-day) statutory window, or if documentary integrity fails to prove the export nexus, IRD will re-characterise the supply as taxable at 15% GST and issue a retrospective assessment, plus use-of-money interest on unpaid GST. Fraudulent zero-rating claims attract civil shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall (s 141D), further penalties of up to NZD 50,000 for knowingly providing false information (s 141I), loss of GST registration privileges, and criminal liability (including imprisonment) under s 143B for evasion. IRD also applies a six-year reassessment window for most returns (unlimited for fraud or evasion), enabling retrospective clawback of refunds across multiple periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has no statutory exchange-control regime. The New Zealand Dollar (NZD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no restrictions on the remittance of dividends, royalties, loan repayments, profits, or liquidation proceeds to non-residents. The sole material overlay is the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which applies to dealings in sensitive land and significant business assets above specified thresholds; ordinary cross-border reverse-logistics, trading, warehousing, and fulfilment operations do not require approval from the Overseas Investment Office (OIO). Banking execution remains subject to AML/CFT verification, FATCA/CRS reporting, and standard transaction purpose declaration, but not to any foreign-exchange permission regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand has not maintained mandatory export-proceeds surrender rules since the abolition of exchange controls. Export receipts need not be repatriated, converted into NZD, or deposited into a designated New Zealand account. A New Zealand reverse-logistics company may lawfully retain foreign currency offshore or in New Zealand foreign-currency accounts for working capital. The company must maintain proper accounting and tax records covering revenue recognition, zero-rated GST treatment for exported services and goods, and corporate income tax, but no surrender deadline or conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand - Te Pūtea Matua

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a primary account provider, and High feasibility as a supplementary payout and cross-currency clearing rail. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex and Wise are generally not licensed banks in New Zealand; they may offer New Zealand-rendered account services only via partnership arrangements with RBNZ-licensed banks or by holding a non-bank deposit taker licence under the Deposit Takers Act 2022. Such EMIs provide compelling multi-currency virtual IBANs, low-cost disbursements, and streamlined Asia-Pacific corridors, but they do not offer the same settlement finality, deposit-protection coverage, or regulatory comfort as a Tier-1 bank account. The optimal structure for a cross-border reverse-logistics treasury is a Tier-1 domestic anchor account for capital, GST, and payroll, paired with an EMI platform for operational payouts and FX conversions.

Direct SWIFT MT103 wire from the overseas parent or beneficial owner into either the company's NZD account or a designated foreign-currency account. No RBNZ exchange-control approval is required. The remitting bank should state the payment purpose as 'equity share capital contribution' or 'intercompany working capital loan'. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on all taxable distributions to non-residents. The rate is reduced to 15% when the dividend is fully imputed and the shareholder is resident in a country that has a DTA with New Zealand. Certain DTAs (e.g., Australia) provide a 0% rate for dividends paid to companies that own 80% or more of the paying NZ company and have held the shares for 12 months.

The IR4 must be filed by the 7th day of the second month after the company's balance date. For a standard 31 March balance date, the return is due by 7 June. A registered tax agent may have an extended filing deadline (commonly 31 July for a 31 March balance date).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand residents are exempt from transfer pricing documentation if gross income is below NZD 30 million for the income year. Where gross income exceeds NZD 30 million, a Local File must be prepared if the value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZD 5 million in aggregate for that year. A Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds NZD 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) akin to the UAE. Instead, economic substance is enforced through case law and the general anti-avoidance rule (Part GA of the Income Tax Act 2007), which recharacterises arrangements lacking commercial reality. The 'business continuity test' for loss carry-forward and the 'extra-statutory concession' for taxation of non-resident-owned seed companies both require real New Zealand managerial functions. A company with no office, staff, or clear decision-making in New Zealand may be deemed non-resident under the tie-breaker of a DTA, or an avoidance arrangement may be struck down with penalties of up to 100% of the tax shortfall.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil tax shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax (s 141E TAA 1994); criminal prosecution under s 143B TAA 1994 liable to a fine up to NZD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or knowingly assist in a false return may be jointly liable for penalties and ordered to pay restitution. Courts can impose travel bans pending resolution, and serious cases may apply the Companies Act 1993 to order director disqualification.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may reallocate income to the New Zealand company, issue a reassessment, and impose a net shortfall penalty of 40% (unacceptable interpretation) or 100% (abusive tax position). Interest accrues daily.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve tax misstatements reflecting false transfer pricing may be personally assessed for the shortfall penalty under s 149B TAA 1994. They may also be banned from managing a company for up to 5 years and prohibited from leaving New Zealand if tax debts exceed a threshold.
Shell Company Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may cancel the tax benefits under GAAR and deem the company non-resident under a DTA. Penalties up to 100% of the tax shortfall apply. The Companies Office can strike off the company for not carrying on business.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid company tax if they allowed the company to trade while insolvent (Companies Act 1993, s 135). Civil proceedings may freeze personal assets, and directors may be barred from leaving New Zealand without a court order.
Fraud in Reverse Logistics / AML

Statutory sanction: Under the Crimes Act 1961, obtaining a tax refund by deception carries a maximum imprisonment of 7 years. Penalties under the Tax Administration Act for evasion add 150% of the shortfall. The AML/CFT Act may apply if the entity provides financial services, creating fines up to NZD 2 million for companies.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally participate in a fraudulent scheme can be charged as parties to the crime. Civil claims for deceit may result in restitution and punitive damages. Foreign travel may be restricted, and extradition can be sought for tax fraud.
