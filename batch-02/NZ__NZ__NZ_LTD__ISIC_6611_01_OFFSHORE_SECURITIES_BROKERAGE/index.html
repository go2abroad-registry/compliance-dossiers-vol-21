Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A New Zealand registered office must be a physical street address in New Zealand and open for service of process during business hours; a PO Box or commercial mailbox alone is void. A serviced virtual office with a physical street address, lease, signage and local staff can nominally satisfy the Companies Act 1993, but for ISIC 6611 retail securities brokerage the Financial Markets Authority, Inland Revenue and NZ banks will reject a pure virtual-mailbox model because the place of effective management and client trust-account custody must be demonstrable in New Zealand. The brokerage must maintain substantive premises, local directors and staff, and auditable records before FMA licensing or bank onboarding.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand Companies Act 1993 does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up share capital for registration of a limited company. The directors may determine the share capital and issue shares for such consideration as the board considers fair and reasonable under sections 123-127 of the Act. No blocked escrow account is required by the Companies Office for formation. Where the Financial Markets Authority imposes prudential or regulatory capital requirements on the company as a licensed derivatives issuer or financial advice provider, those requirements arise at the licensing stage and are separate from the formation capital rule.

PRO-FORMA FIRST BOARD OF DIRECTORS RESOLUTION OF NZ_LTD

The undersigned, being the duly appointed director(s) of NZ_LTD (the Company), acting by written resolution under the Companies Act 1993 and the Company's constitution, hereby resolve and record as follows:

1. CONSTITUTION AND REGISTRATION. The constitution appended to the Company's incorporation application is adopted as the constitution of the Company in accordance with section 122 of the Companies Act 1993. The directors are authorised to file the registration application, to pay the prescribed Companies Office fee, and to make any minor amendments to the constitution as may reasonably be required by the New Zealand Companies Office to secure registration.

2. SHARE CAPITAL AND ALLOTMENT. The Company's authorised share capital is established as such number of ordinary shares, with such nominal value, as is stated in the incorporation application. The initial shares are allotted to the subscribers named in the application at a consideration determined by the directors in accordance with sections 123-127 of the Companies Act 1993. The consideration may be satisfied in cash, property, or services, and the shares may be issued as fully paid or partly paid as the board decides. The board confirms that no statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for formation and that no escrow account is required by the Companies Office.

3. REGISTERED OFFICE. The registered office of the Company is fixed at the street address set out in the incorporation application, being a physical address in New Zealand, and the directors are authorised to file the registered office notice under section 184 of the Companies Act 1993. The registered office shall be open for service of documents during ordinary business hours and shall be the address at which the Company's statutory registers are kept.

4. APPOINTMENT OF DIRECTORS AND OFFICERS. The persons named in the consent forms are appointed as directors of the Company. Each director has consented to act and has provided the required statutory declarations and identity documents. If applicable, the board appoints the person named in the resolution as company secretary, not being required unless the board determines otherwise. All directors shall exercise their powers and duties in good faith and with the care, diligence, and skill required by the Companies Act 1993 and the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013.

5. BANKING MANDATE. The directors are authorised to open and maintain bank accounts with the Company's chosen bank, including a general operating account and a segregated client money account if required by the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013. The bank mandate shall require the signatures of any two directors, or such other signatories and limits as the board determines, and the directors are authorised to provide specimen signatures, corporate authorisations, and any bank forms required.

6. CORPORATE GOVERNANCE AND ACCOUNTING. The financial year of the Company is fixed to end on 31 March. The directors shall ensure that financial records, the share register, the director register, and the register of charges are maintained and available for inspection as required by law. Board meetings may be held by telephone, videoconference, or other electronic means provided that all participating directors can communicate with each other.

7. REGULATORY AUTHORISATIONS. The directors are authorised to prepare and lodge all applications and registrations necessary for the Company's offshore securities brokerage business, including Financial Service Providers Register registration, Financial Markets Authority market services licensing, AML/CFT registration, tax registration with Inland Revenue, and any local authority or Fire and Emergency New Zealand approvals. The directors are authorised to appoint professional advisers and to pay all related fees.

8. GENERAL AUTHORITY. The Company may execute leases, engagement letters, compliance manuals, client agreements, insurance policies, and such other contracts as are reasonably necessary to commence business. Certified copies of this resolution shall be provided to bankers, regulators, auditors, and professional advisers on request. This resolution takes effect upon the registration of the Company and may be referred to as the First Board Resolution.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) - online portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir)

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand Limited Company (NZ Ltd), the entity must first obtain an IRD number (tax file number) by submitting the IR596 form (Company Registration) either online via myIR or through the Companies Office joint registration process. The application requires the Certificate of Incorporation, details of all directors and shareholders (including non-resident beneficial owners), and a description of the intended business activities. Once the IRD number is issued, the company must assess its GST obligations. If the company expects to make taxable supplies exceeding NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, it must register for GST using the IR356 form (GST Registration) or via myIR. For non-resident directors or shareholders, additional identification and proof of address may be required. The company must also register for PAYE (Pay As You Earn) if it employs staff, and for FBT (Fringe Benefit Tax) if applicable. All registrations are completed through the Inland Revenue's online portal, myIR, which serves as the primary electronic filing and compliance platform.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from New Zealand within 28 days of the supply are zero-rated. Services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, and where the services are not supplied to a person in New Zealand, are zero-rated. Certain financial services supplied to non-residents (including offshore securities brokerage) may be zero-rated under Section 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, provided the recipient is a non-resident and the services are not supplied to a person in New Zealand. The supplier must hold sufficient evidence to substantiate the zero-rating claim.

  • Export Entry (Customs Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Bank remittance statement or foreign currency receipt
  • Signed contract or agreement with the non-resident recipient (for services)
  • Evidence of payment (e.g., SWIFT confirmation)
  • Proof of non-resident status of the recipient (e.g., certificate of incorporation or tax residency certificate)

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and supported via the Peppol network. GST invoices must contain prescribed particulars under Section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, including supplier name and GST number, date of issue, description of goods/services, and amount of GST. Tax invoices must be issued within 28 days of the supply. Anti-fraud measures include mandatory record retention for 7 years and the requirement to file GST returns electronically via myIR. There is no digital stamping or API-based clearance requirement.

If zero-rating is incorrectly claimed, the supplier is liable for GST at 15% on the supply, plus penalties and interest under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Penalties include a late payment penalty of 1% per month and a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall for intentional or reckless errors. Additionally, if goods are not exported within the required 28-day period, the supply is subject to GST and the supplier may be required to claw back any input tax credits previously claimed. Inland Revenue conducts risk-based audits on export zero-rating claims, particularly for financial services, and may require detailed documentation to verify the non-resident status of recipients and the actual provision of services.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a freely floating currency with full conversion of both current and capital account transactions under a liberalised regulatory framework. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not impose exchange controls, and the New Zealand dollar is fully convertible for trade, direct investment, financial transactions, and profit remittance. There is no surrender requirement for export proceeds, no central bank approval threshold for inward or outward capital flows, and no administrative restriction on the repatriation of dividends, share capital, or liquidation proceeds. The primary regulatory constraints are not FX controls but prudential supervision, AML/CFT obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and certain screening requirements under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 for foreign ownership of sensitive land or material business assets. A securities brokerage company with non-resident shareholders must document that the brokerage itself does not hold such sensitive assets, and must file accurate Balance of Payments/International Transaction Reporting System (ITRS) declarations through its settlement bank. Remittance hurdles may arise only when a bank perceives elevated ML/TF risk for a high-risk financial-services entity; in such cases, enhanced due diligence and source-of-funds evidence must be supplied. Foreign investors may hold NZD or offshore foreign-currency accounts and may hedge freely using deliverable forwards and swap markets, subject to commonly observed market documentation and credit-limit requirements. Overall, the NZ regime is categorised as no exchange control or open capital account, with compliance-focused frictions only.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender regime exists under New Zealand law. Exporters retain full title to foreign currency receipts, may keep proceeds in New Zealand or offshore foreign-currency accounts, and there is no mandatory conversion ratio or timeline. All offshore receipts must merely be reported for Balance of Payments statistical purposes via the International Transactions Reporting System. The practical banking requirement is to provide the settlement bank with underlying export/transaction documentation to support the SWIFT purpose code and to facilitate transaction monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium for a licensed New Zealand retail securities broker. Fintech EMIs generally lack the segregated client money architecture mandated by the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2006 and often cannot provide acceptable independent trust attestations that satisfy New Zealand’s prudential banking expectations. The Financial Markets Authority requires licensed brokers to hold client funds in an authorised local bank on trust; unlicensed EMIs would be non-compliant for this purpose. Limited utility may exist as a payment-only rail for settlement of Visa/Mastercard flows, but the core treasury function must sit with locally incorporated registered banks. Legal constraints also include the requirement that a non-bank EMI servicing New Zealand entities must obtain a flat tax/AML/CFT registration under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, and may not legally operate as a deposit-taker without a banking licence. Therefore, a prudent sponsor should reject EMIs for primary liquidity and use them, if at all, exclusively for sub-ordinated disbursement channels with strict transaction caps.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection must be structured via a SWIFT MT103/MT202 wire using the explicit instruction 'equity contribution to NZ Limited for capitalisation', with an economic purpose code matching the incoming capital transfer under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's International Transactions Reporting System. The receiving bank will issue a foreign investment inflow declaration, and capital verification is completed after settlement through a statutory auditor's certificate to the effect that the funds have been credited to the NZD capital escrow account. No central bank pre-approval is required, but the bank will require the underlying legal agreement, shareholder resolution, and evidence of overseas banking source in order to apply the appropriate balance-of-payments classification. The final capital verification should also include an audit trail linking the capital account on the Companies Office register to the exact foreign inward funds, including all currency conversion confirmation tickets.For trade settlements, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for open-account payments, with all outgoing messages carrying full counterparty identifiers and accurate purpose codes. Commercial letters of credit issued by the settlement bank under UCP 600 are recommended for high-value physical commodity settlements or where the counterparty requires a bank guarantee. Documentary collections under URC 522 are acceptable for medium-trust relationships where underlying transport documents provide shielding. Client money disbursements to offshore brokers or liquidity providers must be separately tracked through a segregated client money account using SWIFT GPI with strict beneficiary verification, and must not be combined with proprietary capital. All outgoing transfers require the underlying trade agreement, invoice, FX transaction ticket, and beneficial-ownership confirmation, with records retained for at least five years.Deliverable forward foreign exchange contracts for NZD/USD, NZD/EUR, NZD/GBP, NZD/AUD, and NZD/JPY with maturities up to 24 months, provided they are booked under an ISDA or active own-funds collateral arrangement with a licensed New Zealand bank.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or offshore currencies such as CNY, INR, and IDR, only where the underlying exposure is a genuine commercial trade settlement and the NDF is transacted through a permitted Tier 1 bank or an authorised derivatives dealer.Cross-currency swaps solely for the purpose of aligning long-term foreign currency assets with foreign currency liabilities arising from client segregation trust balances, subject to FMA conduct guidance and board-approved treasury policies.Foreign exchange options used to hedge contingent brokerage settlement exposures, limited to cash-settled contracts with maximum maturity of twelve months and closed-out before any speculative use of client funds.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% NRWT on gross dividends; 15% NRWT for fully imputed dividends paid to non-residents; 15% AIL if elected for certain public or marketable securities. Non-resident companies cannot access imputation credits personally.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For standard 31 March balance dates, filing is due by 7 July of the same calendar year. For non-standard balance dates, the return is due by the 7th day of the month that corresponds with the end of the company's 12-month accounting period. Certain tax agents may obtain extensions to 31 March of the following year or another extended date per IRD practice.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1: Total Income Gross income from all sources, including commissions, spreads, CFD/equity brokerage fees, margin lending interest, and other trading income. New Zealand-sourced income only is taxable unless the company is resident; residence is governed by incorporation and centre of management rules. Non-resident companies with a New Zealand PE are taxable on PE-attributable income. Late filing penalty of NZD 250 for the first month and another NZD 250 after one month of continued non-filing (maximum NZD 500 for non-individuals). Late payment penalty applies at 1% immediately and 4% per six-month period on unpaid tax, plus accruing interest on unpaid tax at the IRD prescribed rate (currently 8.38% per annum).
Box 2: Total Expenses Deductible expenses: brokerage clearing fees, infrastructure, staff costs, regulatory fees, interest expense, and other ordinary business deductions. Expenses must be incurred in deriving New Zealand-sourced income; branch-specific allocations should be supported by transfer pricing documentation. Late filing penalty of NZD 250 for the first month and another NZD 250 after one month of continued non-filing (maximum NZD 500 for non-individuals). Late payment penalty applies at 1% immediately and 4% per six-month period on unpaid tax, plus accruing interest on unpaid tax at the IRD prescribed rate (currently 8.38% per annum).
Box 3: Net Taxable Income / Tax Loss Taxable income after deductions offset by tax losses brought forward. Non-residents carrying on business through a PE may offset PE losses against PE profits subject to continuity rules. Late filing penalty of NZD 250 for the first month and another NZD 250 after one month of continued non-filing (maximum NZD 500 for non-individuals). Late payment penalty applies at 1% immediately and 4% per six-month period on unpaid tax, plus accruing interest on unpaid tax at the IRD prescribed rate (currently 8.38% per annum).
Box 4: Tax Credits RWT, foreign tax credits, and imputation credits claimed to reduce corporate tax liability. Foreign tax credits may be claimed under NZ DTA provisions only if non-resident company is subject to NZ tax on the same income. Late filing penalty of NZD 250 for the first month and another NZD 250 after one month of continued non-filing (maximum NZD 500 for non-individuals). Late payment penalty applies at 1% immediately and 4% per six-month period on unpaid tax, plus accruing interest on unpaid tax at the IRD prescribed rate (currently 8.38% per annum).
Box 5: Income Tax Payable / Refund Final core tax at 28% after deducting credits and provisional tax payments. If no PE exists, no income tax return is needed and the company only remains subject to NRWT/AIL on New Zealand-source dividends, interest, and royalties. Late filing penalty of NZD 250 for the first month and another NZD 250 after one month of continued non-filing (maximum NZD 500 for non-individuals). Late payment penalty applies at 1% immediately and 4% per six-month period on unpaid tax, plus accruing interest on unpaid tax at the IRD prescribed rate (currently 8.38% per annum).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 1 million for cross-border related-party transactions requiring basic contemporaneous documentation; NZD 10 million for high-value transactions requiring robust Master File/Local File-style documentation; tax avoidance reassessment and penalties can apply below these thresholds where the arrangement lacks commercial substance.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have a separate ESR bureau; however, the Income Tax Act 2007 and Tax Administration Act incorporate arm's length provisions (Part GC) and GAAR (s BG 1) that require substance over form. A brokerage incorporated in New Zealand but managed and controlled offshore may be treated as non-resident under s YD 2, but if managed in NZ or purposely structured to avoid tax, income can be re-attributed; the CFC and FIF regimes also disregard artificial structures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraud

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 150% of tax shortfall plus criminal fines up to NZD 250,000 for a company; imprisonment up to 5 years under the Tax Administration Act 1994 s 143A (evasion).

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly concerned in evasion can be personally prosecuted and fined up to NZD 50,000 and imprisoned up to 5 years; civil action also allows recovery of unpaid tax from personal assets.
Shell Company / Lack of Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR cancellation of tax advantages (s BG 1); reassessment as agent or PE of offshore principal; penalties for abusive tax position up to 100% of tax avoided; Companies Office action for insolvent or misuse of company structure.

Director personal liability: Directors of shell companies may be personally liable for debts under Companies Act 1993 s 300 (reckless trading), disqualified for up to 5 years, and subjected to civil recovery orders.
AML/CFT Violations

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to NZD 2 million per breach for a reporting entity; criminal fines up to NZD 200,000 for individuals; revocation of financial services or broking licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers may be liable personally for knowing participation; imprisonment up to 2 years; travel bans can be imposed while investigations are ongoing.
Unlicensed Securities Brokerage Activities

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties up to NZD 1 million; statutory damages; confiscation of profits; client transaction voidability; mandatory compensation orders.

Director personal liability: Individuals directly engaged in unlicensed conduct face fines up to NZD 300,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years, plus unlimited civil restitution.
Failure to File / Transfer Pricing Documentation Defaults

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalties up to NZD 500 per return; shortfall penalties from 20% to 150% depending on culpability; TP adjustment to raise taxable income and interest accruals.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or tolerate transfer pricing manipulation can be made jointly and severally liable for corporate tax amounts under tax administration and general compensatory principles.
