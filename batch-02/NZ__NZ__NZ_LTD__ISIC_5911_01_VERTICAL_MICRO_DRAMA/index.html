Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a New Zealand limited liability company. The shareholders may subscribe with nominal consideration or nil paid-up capital; there is no blocked escrow, statutory capital deposit, or paid-up capital certification requirement before business commencement. Directors must still comply with the Companies Act 1993 sections 37-38 requiring lawful issue of shares and adequate consideration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) - online portal myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir/; company incorporation and registered-office filing through the New Zealand Companies Office (Companies Register) administered by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment; export clearance through New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS) via the Trade Single Window (TSW).

E-government filing portal

For an NZ-incorporated limited company (NZ_LTD) owned wholly or partly by non-residents, the statutory process begins with incorporation under the Companies Act 1993 through the Companies Office; the Registrar issues an NZBN and transmits the company details to Inland Revenue. The company then completes IRD-number registration (and, if taxable supplies exceed the threshold, GST registration) through myIR using the applicant company's NZBN, registered office, director/shareholder registry extract, and non-resident owner identification. The IRD number serves as the single New Zealand tax identification number for income tax and GST; no separate VAT identifier exists. A foreign non-resident company carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand without a local incorporation must first determine its permanent establishment / taxable activity nexus, register the overseas company with the Companies Office if it is carrying on business in New Zealand, obtain an NZBN, and then apply through myIR for an IRD number (and GST number if applicable). The IRD may require financial projections, supplier/customer information, a New Zealand address for service, and authority documents from the non-resident entity's governing body.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or an elective entity classification system. An NZ-resident close-held company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under subpart HA of the Income Tax Act 2007, but the statutory LTC conditions require all look-through counted owners to be New Zealand residents; therefore a company with non-resident shareholders, such as NZ_LTD, is not LTC-eligible. For indirect tax, a GST group election under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 may be available to certain affiliated registered persons. No domestic hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (GST Act 1985 s 51); a non-resident supplier of remote services to New Zealand residents is also required to register once its New Zealand taxable supplies reach or exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period, and voluntary registration is available below this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical goods, zero-rating is available under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the goods are supplied by a registered person and exported by the supplier, with the supplier retaining sufficient evidence of export. For the vertical micro-drama streaming service, zero-rating is available under section 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for services supplied to a person who is not a New Zealand resident and who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, subject to the statutory exceptions (e.g., services directly connected with New Zealand real property or moveable property). The NZ_LTD must substantiate each digital supply by reference to the subscriber's contractual status, app-store billing country, payment-gateway geolocation/billing address, IP-country logs, and the absence of New Zealand presence during the streaming or subscription period. Supplies to subscribers who are New Zealand residents or who are present in New Zealand when accessing the service are chargeable at 15% GST unless a specific zero-rating exception applies. Where Apple, Google, or Stripe act as an electronic marketplace or payment intermediary for remote services, contractual and supply-chain mapping must be maintained to identify the actual supplier for GST purposes and to avoid double taxation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export entry) lodged with New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window; the term 'Bill of Entry' is not used in New Zealand and the statutory record is the TSW export entry.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill together with packing list, commercial invoice, and transport insurance certificate for any physical exportation of goods, media, or promotional materials.
  • App Store Connect (Apple IAP), Google Play Console, or Stripe settlement reports reasonably identifying each non-resident subscriber, billing country, transacting currency, and settlement batch reference.
  • Customer contract, terms-of-service acceptance record, or subscription agreement together with IP-geolocation and session data evidencing that the service was supplied to a non-resident outside New Zealand at the time of supply.
  • Inward banking remittance evidence, such as SWIFT MT103/MT202 messages, bank statements in NZD or foreign currency, and foreign-exchange conversion records; New Zealand does not issue an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), and the IRD accepts banking records as the equivalent evidence of payment.
  • Zero-rated GST tax invoices, supply-chain contracts, and any electronic-marketplace or reseller agreements with Apple, Google, Stripe, or downstream distribution partners.

New Zealand has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance system, no government-authorised digital stamping platform, and no statutory requirement to pre-clear invoices before issue. GST-registered businesses must file GST returns and maintain tax invoices in accordance with section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and the Tax Administration Act 1994. Inland Revenue supports voluntary e-invoicing via the Peppol framework, but no API-based fiscal clearance is required for a myIR GST return. For app-store transactions, Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, and Stripe settlement data must be retained as supporting records; these payment rails do not substitute for the statutory tax invoice and record-keeping obligations.

Inland Revenue and New Zealand Customs Service may audit export zero-rating claims at any time. If the NZ_LTD cannot produce the export and residency evidence specified above, IRD will reassess the supplies at the standard 15% GST rate, recover any overclaimed refunds, and charge use-of-money interest from the original due date. The Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes shortfall penalties of 20% for failure to take reasonable care and for an unacceptable tax position, 100% for an abusive tax position, and up to 150% for evasion or similar offences. Knowingly false export declarations may also be prosecuted under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, leading to civil forfeiture, fines, and criminal liability for directors and managing agents. New Zealand applies no separate FIRC-style mandatory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the zero-rating is substantiated by the required documents and banking evidence at the time the GST return is filed, and retrospective clawback may occur up to the statutory time limits in the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand does not impose foreign exchange controls. The NZD is a G10 free-floating currency and the capital and current accounts are fully open. There are no surrender requirements for export earnings, no restrictions on the amount of capital that a foreign shareholder can invest, and no pre-approval for ordinary dividend or licence-fee remittances. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand conducts monetary policy and manages prudential stability but does not ration foreign exchange or require import/export licences for FX purposes. The primary constraint for a NZ limited company receiving or dispersing funds is bank-level AML/CFT compliance under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. In practice, inbound capital and profits can be freely converted to NZD and back, but remittance documentation must show the economic purpose (e.g. equity injection, royalty, trade settlement) to avoid de-risking. Because New Zealand is a unitary jurisdiction without provincial exchange controls, the entire treasury function can lawfully be run through domestic banks. For a micro-drama production and global distribution entity, licensing revenue from Apple, Google, and Stripe can be repatriated without central bank approval; however, corporate banking due diligence will require clear source-of-funds and source-of-wealth records for each inbound transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign currency receipts in New Zealand. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not require exporters to surrender or convert foreign currency into NZD within any period. Exporters may hold foreign currency in their own accounts, use it directly for foreign supplier payments, or convert it at commercial rates, subject only to normal banking, AML/CFT documentation, and tax reconciliation requirements.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability rating: Low as a primary treasury channel and Medium as an ancillary settlement utility. Legal constraints: an offshore digital EMI cannot hold a New Zealand banking licence and therefore cannot deposit funds in NZD or access the Reserve Bank exchange settlement account system. It is not automatically unlawful for an NZ Ltd to receive or pay through an EMI, but the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and FMA guidance require regulated financial service providers to be appropriately registered; an unregistered offshore EMI is not a compliant clearing partner. The stabilising blueprint is therefore to use only a Tier-1 New Zealand bank for structural liquidity, while EMI rails may be used for app-store payouts only if the EMI is regulated in its home country and does not take local NZD deposits.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital should be sent by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder own bank account to the NZ Ltd Tier-1 bank account. The payment narrative must clearly state equity contribution to NZ Ltd together with the company number, and the receiving bank should be advised through institution-issued KYC forms that the purpose category is inward direct investment or share capital. Because New Zealand does not operate a central bank FX approval system, there is no statutory capital verification certificate, but the company accountant should prepare a capital verification schedule attaching the bank credit advice, board minute, shareholder resolution, shareholder ledger, and source-of-funds documents. No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the Companies Act 1993 for this NZ Ltd, although the bank may set its own minimum relationship balance. Local NZD payments should clear through the New Zealand clearing system; foreign currency payments clear through correspondent banking. Before release, every beneficiary must be screened against sanctions and PEP lists, and each allocation must match an approved disbursement schedule. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 0 – No de minimis threshold under Income Tax Act 2007; contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation must be prepared for all significant cross-border controlled transactions. Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding NZD 1.3 billion (EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but tax residence requires company management and control to be exercised in New Zealand. The Inland Revenue may challenge arrangements lacking core income-generating activities (CIGA) under General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1). For the vertical micro-drama business, the New Zealand entity should hold actual employees, decision-making records, IP deregistration, and a commercial substance office in New Zealand; otherwise income may be reattributed to a non-resident principal or treaty benefits may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Suppression of Offshore Revenues

Statutory sanction: Violation of Tax Administration Act 1994 s 143B and s 141. Imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines of up to NZ$150,000 for the company and individuals; shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax; cancellation of any tax losses and imposition of non-deductible interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate are personally liable under TAA s 149A for the tax shortfall and penalties. The corporate veil may be pierced: personal assets are subject to civil recovery; directors may be blacklisted/travel-banned by Inland Revenue to enforce payment; criminal conviction may carry imprisonment of up to 5 years for each offence.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation via Related-Party Royalties

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 s GC 13-GC 17 authorise the Commissioner to reallocate income; shortfall penalty under TAA s 141E of 20% (not taking reasonable care) or 40% (dominant purpose to avoid tax) of the tax shortfall, plus UOMI. In egregious cases, criminal liability for tax evasion may be pursued.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed under TAA s 149A as persons involved in the arrangement; they must personally indemnify the company for penalties, have personal tax assets frozen, and may be disqualified from holding directorships under the Companies Act 1993. Civil recovery against personal assets is possible for unpaid tax liabilities.
Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Withholding Tax on Distributions

Statutory sanction: TAA s 140 makes the company liable for the unpaid NRWT. Failure to withhold is a criminal offence under TAA s 143A with fines up to NZ$50,000, and the unpaid NRWT remains a debt due. The payment is not deductible if NRWT is not withheld.

Director personal liability: Directors are statutory agents for the company and are personally liable for remitting the NRWT, including any penalties, as if it were their own obligation. The director's personal assets can be pursued via civil debt recovery; travel blacklisting and garnishment of bank accounts are common enforcement methods; wilful refusal can lead to imprisonment for up to 5 years.
