Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Wellington, New Zealand; electronic filing and taxpayer portal is myIR operated by IRD at ird.govt.nz. Company registration and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) are administered by the New Zealand Companies Office through the Register of Companies (companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz).

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand-resident limited company, the directors first incorporate under the Companies Act 1993 through the Companies Office; the Companies Office issues a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN), and the company's IRD number is normally obtained electronically during incorporation or immediately afterwards through the Inland Revenue registration process in myIR. A new NZ-resident company automatically falls within the IRD tax net and must provide its physical registered office, postal address, director details, principal business activity, financial reporting balance date, and intended tax obligations including income tax, GST, PAYE if employees are engaged, and fringe benefit tax if applicable. Once processed, IRD issues the single statutory IRD number used for both income tax and GST; there is no separate GST/VAT identifier in New Zealand. For a foreign non-resident entity that intends to carry on business in New Zealand, the entity must first register as an overseas company with the Companies Office if it has a New Zealand place of business, obtain an NZBN, and then apply to Inland Revenue for a non-resident IRD number through the designated non-resident company application channel in myIR. The foreign entity must provide the certified certificate of incorporation from its home jurisdiction, constitutional documents, board resolution, full register of directors, New Zealand service address, New Zealand agent details, foreign tax identification number, tax residence certificate, and, where GST registration will be sought, evidence of its taxable activity and projected turnover in New Zealand. For withholding on non-resident contractor income, a separate Inland Revenue certificate may be needed, but that is not a check-the-box election and does not replace the IRD number application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box regime. An eligible close-held company may elect look-through company (LTC) status under Part HA of the Income Tax Act 2007 so that tax-transparent treatment applies to shareholders. A wholly-owned group may elect consolidated group taxation under subpart IG of the Income Tax Act 2007. Hybrid mismatch and branch mismatch rules under subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 apply automatically and are not elective. A company electing LTC status remains subject to anti-hybrid limitations and must satisfy the statutory close-held and resident requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 for compulsory GST registration: a person carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand must register when taxable supplies in the past 12 months exceed NZD 60,000 or are reasonably expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in the next 12 months. Non-resident suppliers of remote services and of low-value imported goods are also required to register for New Zealand GST at the same NZD 60,000 consumer-supply threshold. NZ-incorporated companies are within the income tax net from incorporation regardless of turnover; separate PAYE registration is required if employees are engaged.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exports is available under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Goods exported by the supplier, or on the supplier's behalf, are zero-rated if exported within 28 days of the time of supply and the supplier retains documentary evidence of export. Services supplied directly in connection with exported goods, international transport of goods, or services reasonably incidental to international transport are also zero-rated. For a customs-bonded road feeder trucking operation feeding an international airport, the supply is zero-rated where the services are supplied in connection with goods that are being carried internationally and the supply is evidenced as furnished to a non-resident recipient or for the international transport of goods. Remote services supplied to a non-resident recipient outside New Zealand are zero-rated under section 11B when effective use and enjoyment lies outside New Zealand. Input GST incurred in making zero-rated supplies is fully creditable and is recovered through the normal GST return rather than a separate export refund claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a freely convertible currency regime with no exchange controls on current account or capital account transactions. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand supervises the financial system and the NZD is fully publicly traded. There are no mandatory surrender requirements for export proceeds, and foreign investors may freely remit dividends, loan repayments, and disposition proceeds subject to standard AML/CFT reporting and tax clearance procedures. Profit repatriation is not blocked, though payments made to non-residents are subject to Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) and any relevant double tax treaty relief documentation. Because the entity is engaged in customs bonded trucking, trade-related payments may require clear documentary evidence of the underlying trade to satisfy bank due diligence. Historically the jurisdiction has never imposed controls on the buy/sell spread, and foreign-owned companies may maintain both NZD and major foreign currency clearing accounts without central bank approval, provided the accounts are used for bona fide commercial activity. Capital account flows are free but large cash-like movements are reportable to the NZ Police Financial Intelligence Unit under the AML/CFT Act 2009. This convertible, open-market regime significantly reduces treasury planning friction and permits effective cross-border liquidity management through standard correspondent banking networks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender obligation exists in New Zealand. Exporters are not required to convert foreign currency earnings into NZD, and there is no legislated timeline for repatriation, retention, or conversion. All export receipts may remain offshore for working capital purposes, and any repatriated funds require only the standard AML/CFT identification of the source of funds if they exceed prescribed transaction thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand - Foreign Exchange Administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Non-bank financial institutions and cross-border digital EMIs are legally permissible in New Zealand under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the AML/CFT Act 2009, but they are not recognised as registered banks and cannot offer balance-sheet funded deposit accounts. Offshore digital EMIs may be acceptable for ancillary payment scarabing such as supplier payments or receivables collection in certain corridors, but they do not provide the clearing strength, foreign exchange pricing, or balance sheet security needed for a customs bonded truck fleet operating as an international airport feeder. The domestic Tier 1 banks offer direct SWIFT membership, RBNZ settlement accounts, and reliable trade finance infrastructure. Given the low-risk standard status and asset-heavy commercial model, the primary treasury relationship must sit with a New Zealand incorporated registered bank. EMIs can be used as secondary wallets for petty operational disbursements only if the EMI is licensed in a jurisdiction with mutual AML/CFT recognition and its platform supports real MT103-style tracking.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections for the customs bonded truck fleet should be routed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the overseas shareholder's bank account to the dedicated NZD capital escrow account held at a Tier 1 New Zealand bank. The remittance narrative must state the purpose as 'Share Capital Contribution' or 'Subordinated Loan for Commercial Vehicle Fleet Acquisition' and refer to the company registration number and the ISIC 5222 commercial scope. Under the NZ Foreign Investment regime, non-resident ownership is allowed and no exchange control approval is required, but the bank must complete standard customer due diligence on the originator bank and source of funds. A registered New Zealand chartered accountant should issue a capital verification certificate for any equity contribution, mapping the quantum of injected funds to the paid-up capital register and the asset acquisition contracts for the truck fleet. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File is expected where cross-border related-party transactions with the bonded truck fleet exceed NZD 10 million annually; a Master File may be requested for groups with NZD 100 million or more consolidated revenue; a Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for groups with consolidated turnover above EUR 750 million, approximately NZD 1.2 billion.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand applies the OECD substance-over-form principles and the statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007. A customs bonded trucking company must demonstrate real economic substance through employment of NZ-based drivers and logistics staff, active customs compliance, GPS asset management, and board management in New Zealand, being the core income-generating activities. Offshore entities must not carry out the core income-generating activities; otherwise profit allocation and treaty benefits will be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 section 141B imposes a 100% shortfall penalty for abusive tax positions; section 143B provides criminal fines up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years for individuals; for a company the fine may rise to NZD 500,000 for serious evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted personally as principals if they facilitated false filing. The corporate veil will be pierced under Companies Act 1993 sections 135 and 239 to recover assets; IRD may obtain freezing orders, impose travel bans and blacklist non-payment entities; directorial disqualification for up to 5 years is also possible.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 subpart GC empowers the Commissioner to re-adjust profits; Tax Administration Act section 141A imposes a transfer pricing penalty of up to 40% of the tax shortfall for valuation misstatements; interest and late-payment penalties accrue; Country-by-Country Report non-filing attracts daily penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the pricing with knowledge or reckless disregard can be held liable for the shortfall plus penalties. Courts will lift the veil where the subsidiary is used as a conduit to shift value; directors may face civil recovery, travel restrictions and, in blatant cases, criminal prosecution under Tax Administration Act section 143B.
Customs Bonded Fraud / Shell Company / AML

Statutory sanction: Customs and Excise Act 2018 sections 209 and 339 impose monetary penalties equal to 150% of duty evaded, forfeiture of vehicles and goods, and up to 5 years imprisonment; Tax Administration Act section BG 1 allows reconstruction of the intended tax outcome.

Director personal liability: Directors who control the bonded operation are jointly and severally liable for unpaid duty and GST; personal criminal conviction may follow where they knowingly permit diversion. IRD and Customs may blacklist directors and issue travel bans during investigations; Companies Act 1993 section 289 permits asset attachment and liquidation.
