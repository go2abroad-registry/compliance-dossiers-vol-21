Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand; online taxpayer portal is myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir/

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned New Zealand Limited Company: (1) incorporate with the New Zealand Companies Office and maintain a New Zealand registered office and, if required, a local agent; (2) apply to the Inland Revenue Department for an IRD number using form IR596 or through myIR/RealMe, providing the company constitution, directors, shareholders, registered office, business activity, estimated first taxable supply date, balance date, and bank account or payment intermediary details; (3) register for GST either in the same application if the NZD 60,000 taxable supplies threshold is expected to be exceeded, or voluntarily as a person carrying on a taxable activity; (4) register for employer PAYE if any New Zealand employees are engaged. An IRD number is issued immediately on successful electronic application, and the taxpayer record is maintained digitally in myIR.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a New Zealand incorporated company is tax-resident in New Zealand, is taxed at 28% on assessable income, and is not a pass-through entity. New Zealand operates a full imputation regime, and there is no sub-national income tax distinction in this unitary jurisdiction.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box entity classification election. Statutory elections relevant to a localization agency include: (a) election to maintain an imputation credit account under the Income Tax Act 2007; (b) wholly-owned group loss offset and intra-group asset transfer elections; (c) GST grouping elections for closely related companies under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; (d) GST accounting basis elections; and (e) elections relating to foreign tax credits and branch treatment. Hybrid mismatch rules are anti-avoidance measures and cannot be elected to create double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period, computed net of GST; voluntary registration is available for persons carrying on a taxable activity. Non-resident suppliers of remote services or digital goods to New Zealand consumers may also have liability under the offshore supplier registration rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST-registered localization agency may zero-rate supplies when goods are exported from New Zealand within 28 days of supply or when services are supplied to a non-resident client who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed. Digital translation, subtitling, dubbing, and content localization services delivered to offshore non-resident clients generally qualify as zero-rated exports under section 11(1) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, provided the supply is not connected with New Zealand land or a resident consumer. Contemporaneous evidence of the client's non-resident status, physical location, and commercial purpose must be retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration submitted through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window for physical goods or tangible media exports
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical removal of goods from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank payment receipt evidencing settlement in foreign currency from the non-resident client

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no mandatory API-based government clearance, and no digital stamping requirement. New Zealand operates a voluntary Peppol-compliant e-invoicing framework, and central government agencies require Peppol e-invoices from their suppliers. Private-sector suppliers may use standard commercial invoices provided they clearly disclose GST content. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR.

Inland Revenue may disallow zero-rating, reassess output GST, and impose use-of-money interest where export substantiation is inadequate. Shortfall penalties apply under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for failure to exercise reasonable care or an unacceptable tax position; 40% for gross carelessness; 100% for an abusive tax position; and 150% for evasion. Criminal prosecution may also be pursued, with fines and imprisonment available for fraudulent export zero-rating claims. The normal reassessment period is four years from the filing date, extended to seven years where evasion or fraud is involved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both the capital and current account. New Zealand has no exchange-control regime; neither the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 nor the Currency Act 1983 imposes any restriction on inward or outward remittances, profit repatriation, dividend distribution, or liquidation proceeds. Non-resident shareholders may freely hold NZD, maintain onshore bank accounts, and remit profits at any time. The only relevant legal filters are (i) the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (customer due diligence and record-keeping), (ii) the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 (financial services and derivatives licensing), and (iii) the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which applies only to sensitive land and significant business assets and is not engaged by a translation/localisation services company. Profit remittances are subject to non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) of 15% on dividends (reduced by imputation and double-tax-treaty relief) and 10-15% on interest, or the Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) of 2% where the borrower is an approved issuer; no central bank approval, FX conversion, or remittance licence is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand does not impose any statutory timeline for the repatriation of export proceeds, any mandatory conversion ratio, or any surrender obligation on New Zealand-dollar or foreign-currency receivables. A New Zealand exporter of services (including multilingual translation, subtitling, dubbing, and media localisation) may contract in any freely convertible currency, invoice in USD, EUR, or other foreign currencies, and maintain foreign-currency bank accounts (e.g., USD, EUR, GBP, AUD, JPY) both onshore and offshore without restriction. The only obligations are documentary: invoices, contracts, and bank statements must evidence the transactions for income tax purposes, and exports of services to non-residents are zero-rated for GST under section 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, with no requirement to demonstrate conversion or remittance within any time limit.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity subscriptions and shareholder loans from the non-resident parent or individual shareholder must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 (or equivalent) referencing the OECD BPM6 purpose code 'G01 - Direct Investment - Equity Capital' (banks may label it '2001 - Shares') and, for loans, 'G02 - Direct Investment - Loan' in bank-specific schemes. The wire must be directed to the receiving bank's NZD settlement arrangement using the beneficiary's 16-digit New Zealand bank-branch-account-suffix number; settlement occurs through NZClear, the Reserve Bank's exchange settlement account system. The bank must be provided with (i) the signed shareholder resolution authorising the injection, (ii) the share issue documents or a dated loan agreement with a stated interest rate and repayment schedule, (iii) confirmation that the Overseas Investment Act 2005 does not apply (services target below the significant business assets threshold and no sensitive land), and (iv) where the loan is interest-bearing, a completed RWT/NRWT election and Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) registration at 2% with Inland Revenue to cap withholding at source. Directors should also satisfy the Companies Act 1993 s 161 solvency certificate requirement before any distribution to a shareholder.For a multilingual translation and media dubbing business, supplier and royalty disbursements should be executed through the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 (with gpi tracking) for high-value supplier payments and rights-holder royalties, using purpose code '7091 - Business and professional services' and a clear remittance narrative naming the invoice number and contract; (ii) commercial letters of credit are not an appropriate custom for low-risk services; open-account settlement, purchase-order-backed wires, and MT103 transfers are the operating norm; (iii) domestic supplier payments are cleared through the bulk electronic clearing system (BECS/APC) on NZClear, and Inland Revenue payments for GST, Pay As You Earn (PAYE), and company tax are made via bank bill payment; (iv) Wise B2B is recommended for sub-NZD 50,000.00 supplier payments in cross-currency pairs, providing a full audit trail while minimising FX spread; (v) Payoneer is recommended for payouts to individual contractors and market-place clients where the client is already on the Payoneer network. The bank retains the right to request supporting commercial documents for any outbound transfer; a standing disbursement authority, an anti-fraud procedure, and an annual supplier audit will protect the account from being re-rated as high risk.Onshore deliverable outright forwards (plain vanilla NZD/USD, NZD/EUR, NZD/GBP, NZD/AUD, NZD/JPY, and NZD/CNH) executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or the New Zealand Bank Market Agreement with a licensed derivatives issuer (all tier-1 banks hold Financial Markets Authority derivatives issuer licences).Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for hedging emerging-market currency receivables (e.g., Indonesian IDR, Thai THB, Philippine PHP, Vietnamese VND, Indian INR, Brazilian BRL, and Colombian COP) where onshore deliverability is restricted; NDFs may be booked with an NZ licensed derivatives issuer or with an offshore dealer provided the company qualifies as a qualifying counterparty or wholesale client under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CIRS) for matching foreign-currency shareholder loans with NZD funding costs, thereby converting USD or EUR debt into NZD obligations; structured under ISDA/CSA with collateral, governed by a board-approved treasury policy.European-style FX options (cash-settled) for downside protection on USD revenue streams, including zero-cost collars and par-forwards, purchased from tier-1 banks only.Currency swaps (FX swaps and rollovers) for managing short-term intercompany liquidity mismatches between the New Zealand operating entity and the parent treasury.

They are not eligible to serve as the company's tax payment channel for Inland Revenue GST obligations, and their onboarding itself will trigger enhanced due diligence by the tier-1 bank when the platform is designated as a named beneficiary. Recommendation: use Payoneer/Wise for client collections and sub-threshold supplier payouts, with a daily or weekly sweep to the ANZ or ASB operational account for payroll, GST, NRWT, and long-term treasury management.
