Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not inherently unlawful for registration under the Companies Act 1993 as long as the registered office is a physical street address in New Zealand capable of receiving legal process and maintaining statutory records. However, for a cross-border matcha powder wholesale entity using Swift TT and wire payment rails, leading New Zealand banks and Inland Revenue require substantive commercial substance: physical or verifiable operational premises, local invoicing, documented trade contracts, and accessible records. A pure virtual mailbox with no staff, no inventory, and no local office is treated as a shell-structure red flag and is rejected by most licensed banks for non-resident account opening and by Inland Revenue for GST or place-of-effective-management determinations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must apply for a New Zealand IRD number before carrying on a taxable activity. For an NZ-registered limited company, incorporation with the New Zealand Companies Office results in automatic allocation of an IRD number. For an offshore non-resident entity, the non-resident company must submit the Commissioner-prescribed IRD number application via myIR or approved paper procedure, providing certified incorporation documents, registered office and overseas address details, expected business activities, projected turnover, and any GST registration election. Identity verification is completed through notarised documents and, where required, appointment of a New Zealand tax or customs agent. The same IRD number is used for income tax and GST. Mandatory GST registration arises once 12-month taxable turnover exceeds NZD 60,000, although voluntary registration is available for start-up activities; the GST registration confirmation is issued electronically through myIR.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods qualifies for zero-rating where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, including goods exported within the statutory timeframe and goods supplied to a New Zealand recipient for export in the ordinary course of business. Services supplied to non-residents located outside New Zealand may also be zero-rated to the extent the services are not supplied to a person in New Zealand and the services are not substantially performed in New Zealand. The claim is recognised on the GST return and must be supported by contemporaneous evidence of physical export, foreign buyer identity, and receipt of foreign consideration.

  • NZ Customs electronic Export Declaration submitted through the Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order identifying the overseas buyer and matcha powder commodity
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or New Zealand bank foreign exchange/TT advice evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Packing list and certificate of origin identifying organic matcha powder (HS heading 0902)

New Zealand has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory electronic invoicing, and no anti-fraud digital stamping regime. e-Invoicing is voluntary and operates through the New Zealand Peppol Authority framework; GST returns are filed electronically through myIR, and export zero-rating claims must be substantiated by underlying transaction and customs evidence.

Inland Revenue may reassess any improperly zero-rated supply as standard-rated at 15%, charge use-of-money interest from the original due date, and impose tax shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994, ranging from 20% for failing to take reasonable care or taking an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. Criminal prosecution and significant monetary penalties apply where export documentation is fraudulent, and zero-rating is retrospectively clawed back if goods are diverted, returned, or not exported within the statutory timeframe absent lawful Customs approval.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalized and convertible currency regime under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and the Foreign Exchange Control Regulations (repealed). There are no capital account or current account exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on acquisition or disposal of foreign currency by residents or non-residents. The New Zealand dollar is classified as a free-floating currency. Corporate entities may maintain foreign currency bank accounts, convert funds freely at market rates, and repatriate dividends, royalties and loan repayments without prior approval. Capital for investment, inter-company loans and trade financing flows across the border without licensing, although large cross-border transfers may trigger reportable thresholds only for statistical purposes by the Reserve Bank. Profit remittance by a non-resident-owned entity is subject only to standard corporate governance approvals and possible non-resident withholding tax on dividends; no administrative exchange control approval is required. Banking settlement occurs via the Exchange Settlement Account System and SWIFT corridors, with same-day NZD clearing through the Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system. Therefore the principal practical risk for a matcha distribution treasury is not convertibility denial but strict anti-money laundering documentation and bank counterparty due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender provisions exist in New Zealand. Under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Foreign Exchange Transactions) Exemption Notice, exporters may retain 100% of foreign currency export proceeds indefinitely, convert at will, or use them for overseas settlement without mandatory conversion timeline. There are no ratios.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

New Zealand does not legally bar foreign payments fintechs from servicing corporates if they are registered under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 as a financial institution or money changer; however, such EMIs are typically not licensed banks and do not offer local NZD Bank Settlement Account clearing codes, documentary letters of credit, or statutory capital verification vouchers. Viability is therefore Medium for ancillary receivables/payables but Low as a substitute for a tier-1 domestic bank in escrow or trade settlement. Engage only if the EMI maintains an agency arrangement with a New Zealand registered bank.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends is 30% at the statutory rate; a fully imputed dividend is subject to NRWT at 15% under domestic law. Double tax agreements may reduce the rate to 15% for portfolio dividends and in many cases to 5% for direct holdings of at least 10% of voting power, provided the beneficial owner satisfies treaty and residence requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File is mandatory where a New Zealand company or group has aggregate cross-border controlled transactions exceeding NZD 5 million in an income year. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds NZD 1.2 billion (approximately EUR 750 million). For smaller groups, contemporaneous records that reasonably support the arm's length result remain good practice and may be requested by Inland Revenue.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as found in certain offshore financial centres; however, the Income Tax Act 2007 contains robust general anti-avoidance rules (Subparts GB and GA), and the common law doctrines of sham and economic substance remain fully applicable. A New Zealand matcha distribution company must demonstrate genuine local management and control, maintain physical or verifiable business substance (e.g., an office or qualified staff, procurement contracts, board minutes and operational records), and ensure that profit attribution to New Zealand is not artificially displaced. Failure to meet these standards may cause Inland Revenue to disregard the entity's separate identity, to reattribute income, to deny treaty and NRWT benefits, or to impose shortfall penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under s 143 of the Tax Administration Act 1994; imprisonment of up to 5 years for a natural person; fines of up to NZD 50,000 for an individual and NZD 100,000 for the company; shortfall penalties may increase the total liability by up to 150% of the tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign, direct, or knowingly assist in filing false returns face individual prosecution, personal criminal fines, and imprisonment. Inland Revenue may issue a Departure Prohibition Order barring the director from leaving New Zealand while tax is unpaid, and the director may be disqualified under the Companies Act 1993.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may make an arm's length price adjustment under s GC 8 of the Income Tax Act 2007, denying deductions or increasing assessable income; a shortfall penalty of 20% to 40% applies for an abusive tax position, and a penalty of up to 150% applies where the avoidance is evasive or fraudulent; late payment interest is also charged from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute non-arm's length pricing can be treated as personally involved in tax avoidance; civil liability may extend to an equalisation of profit, a reinstatement of withheld tax obligations, and personal fines. Withdrawal of travel documents via a Departure Prohibition Order is a practical remedy available to Inland Revenue while a director remains personally liable.
Dividend Stripping / NRWT Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may apply the anti-avoidance provisions and reclassify the payment as a dividend subject to NRWT at 30% or the applicable treaty rate; unpaid NRWT is recoverable as a debt to the Crown, and a shortfall penalty of 20% to 150% may be imposed. Failure to withhold and remit NRWT is a serious criminal offence where done knowingly.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause or permit a distribution to be paid without the required NRWT deduction may be personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax, together with interest and penalties. A director could also be subject to a Departure Prohibition Order until the debt is settled.
Sham Shell Company / No New Zealand Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The arrangement may be void for tax purposes under s GB 1 or the common law sham doctrine; profits will be reattributed to the real controller, treaty benefits may be denied, NRWT at 30% may be imposed on hidden distributions, and penalties for false declarations may be assessed. In some circumstances the company may be treated as a resident of Hong Kong under the tie-breaker test, losing New Zealand tax residence benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign declarations of New Zealand residency or substance when none exists are exposed to criminal liability for false statements, disqualification, and personal liability for taxes, penalties, and interest arising from the reattribution of income.
DTA / Treaty Abuse Through Hong Kong Conduit

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue will deny treaty relief if the Hong Kong company is not the beneficial owner or lacks sufficient substance; the full 30% NRWT is then payable, together with interest and a shortfall penalty of up to 150% under the abusive tax position provisions. The transaction may also be challenged under the principal purpose test embedded in New Zealand's tax treaties.

Director personal liability: Directors of the New Zealand payer who knowingly process dividends through a conduit without verifying treaty entitlement may be personally liable for the unpaid NRWT, interest, and penalties; a departure prohibition order may additionally prevent them from leaving New Zealand.
AML / Trade-Based Invoice Fraud

Statutory sanction: Actions of this type are prosecutable as tax evasion and as money laundering under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and the Proceeds of Crime Act 1991. A reporting entity may be fined up to NZD 10 million for serious corporate non-compliance; fraudulent invoices can also trigger civil forfeiture and confiscation of the proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors or compliance officers who participate in or knowingly permit such activity face criminal prosecution, imprisonment of up to 7 years, personal fines, disqualification, and liability to confiscation orders; they may also be prohibited from leaving New Zealand pending the resolution of the tax and forfeiture matters.
