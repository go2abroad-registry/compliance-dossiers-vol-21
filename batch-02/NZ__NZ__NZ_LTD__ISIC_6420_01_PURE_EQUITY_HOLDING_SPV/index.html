Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

New Zealand law requires every company to have a physical registered office in New Zealand; a post-office box is not sufficient. A virtual office can satisfy the Companies Office filing only if it is a real physical location and the provider is contractually obliged to accept legal service and keep statutory records. For a passive equity-holding SPV, Inland Revenue and reputable New Zealand banks generally reject a bare commercial mailbox because it fails the economic substance and effective management tests; banks will deny an account where the address is not an accessible physical office used by the directors, where records cannot be inspected, or where the company has no local agent with enforceable service authority. A serviced law-firm or corporate-services office is acceptable; a mere virtual mail-drop is not acceptable for IRD tax residence mapping and may trigger enhanced AML due diligence or account refusal.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue, New Zealand); secure online portal: myIR (https://myir.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

For an overseas-owned New Zealand Limited company or a non-resident company carrying on a taxable activity or incurring New Zealand tax obligations, the statutory identifier is the Inland Revenue (IRD) number, which is also used as the base identifier for Goods and Services Tax (GST) registration. A New Zealand-incorporated company must be registered with the New Zealand Companies Office and, once incorporated, must complete Inland Revenue's registration procedure to obtain an IRD number. If the entity is a foreign non-resident company without New Zealand incorporation, the non-resident company must submit the prescribed Inland Revenue non-resident company IRD number application (IR596) through myIR or by paper, accompanied by certified evidence of legal existence in the home jurisdiction, a certified copy of the constitutional documentation, a board resolution appointing a New Zealand resident agent for service of process, particulars of all directors and ultimate beneficial owners, and a completed declaration of non-resident status. Inland Revenue verifies the information and issues the IRD number. No minimum paid-in capital is required. Once the GST registration threshold is met, or if voluntary registration is sought, the same IRD number is activated for GST; New Zealand does not issue a separate GST number distinct from the IRD number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a United States-style check-the-box election. An eligible close-held company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) treatment under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and Income Tax Act 2007, causing income, losses and tax credits to flow through to shareholders on a transparent basis; otherwise a limited company is taxed at the entity level. Hybrid mismatch recharacterisation rules in subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 apply automatically and do not offer elective participation. For a passive pure equity holding SPV, entity-level taxation remains the default classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of total taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any rolling 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value imported goods must register if their supplies to New Zealand-resident consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a supply of goods qualifies for 0% GST where the goods are exported by the supplier and are physically removed from New Zealand within 28 days of supply, unless Inland Revenue allows a longer period. Services, including qualifying offshore digital or distance services supplied by a New Zealand business to a non-resident recipient, may be zero-rated when the recipient is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, the services are not supplied to a person in New Zealand, and the services are not directly attributable to land or goods situated in New Zealand. By contrast, remote/digital services supplied by non-resident suppliers to New Zealand-resident consumers are subject to New Zealand GST at 15% under the offshore supplier rules and are not zero-rated. All zero-rating claims require contemporaneous documentary evidence of physical export and foreign exchange repatriation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EE) lodged with the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window (TSW) — the statutory New Zealand export entry mechanism, functionally equivalent to a Bill of Entry, identifying the supplier as exporter and including export tariff classification, quantity, value and destination.
  • Bill of Lading, or an air waybill / equivalent combined transport document, issued by the carrier evidencing that the goods departed from a New Zealand port or airport within the statutory 28-day export window.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) — in New Zealand practice, a bank foreign exchange credit advice, SWIFT MT103 confirmation, or inter-bank remittance advice evidencing repatriation of the export invoice value from the non-resident foreign buyer.

There is no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no government API invoice approval regime, and no anti-fraud digital stamping requirement for GST invoices. Inland Revenue supports the Peppol e-invoicing framework on a voluntary basis. Where a GST invoice is required, the supplier must issue a valid tax invoice within 28 days of a supply being requested, containing the prescribed particulars under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985: supplier name, IRD/GST number, invoice date, description of goods or services, amount excluding GST, GST amount, and total consideration.

If Inland Revenue determines that export zero-rating was claimed without substantiating customs or foreign-exchange documentation, or that the goods were not removed from New Zealand within the statutory 28-day window, the supply is recharacterised as a taxable supply at 15% GST. The unpaid GST is assessable immediately, together with use-of-money interest under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Shortfall penalties apply by culpability: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 150% for fraudulent intent. The general anti-avoidance rule may also cancel the zero-rating benefit if the arrangement has a dominant tax avoidance purpose. Inland Revenue may amend assessments outside the ordinary four-year time bar in cases involving fraud, evasion, or misleading statements. Supporting export and currency repatriation records must be retained for at least seven years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised and freely convertible currency regime under both the current account and the capital account. There is no general exchange control act, no approval regime for the acquisition of foreign currency, no restriction on the amount of funds that can be remitted into or out of the country, and no surrender requirement on export proceeds. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand supervises licensed banks and the financial system, but does not mandate exchange-rate controls because the New Zealand dollar floats freely. For a pure equity holding SPV, the main practical friction is not convertibility but proving the economic substance and tax purpose of the entity. A bank receiving inbound capital will require a purpose code identifying direct investment equity capital and will complete a balance of payments report to Statistics New Zealand. On the outward side, dividends and loans can be remitted after standard tax clearance validation: the bank may ask for a New Zealand resident withholding tax certificate, a tax residence certificate for the recipient, and a board resolution. There is no central bank pre-approval needed. The net assessment is that New Zealand's convertibility regime presents negligible liquidity risk.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand has no statutory export proceeds surrender rule. There is no required timeline for repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to sell foreign currency; exporters may retain export earnings in any currency, onshore or offshore, subject to the general anti-money laundering reporting requirements of the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 for international wire transfers.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Low for a New Zealand pure equity holding SPV to rely on an offshore digital EMI as its primary treasury rail. Under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989, only banks licensed by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand may undertake banking business in New Zealand, including the acceptance of deposits from the public. An offshore EMI cannot hold a New Zealand account for the SPV unless it is registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register and has a resident agent, but even then it is not a licensed bank and cannot provide settlement-grade corporate banking services. Most New Zealand Tier-1 banks will not accept an unlicensed EMI as a conduit for capital injections or dividend repatriation because the AML/CFT Act 2009 requires transparency of the end-to-end remittance chain. The legally safest structure is a domestic bank account with ancillary multi-currency sub-accounts; an EMI may be used only as a lower-volume payment front if the final leg is settled through a New Zealand licensed bank, but in practice this is uncommon and carries strong compliance risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a pure equity holding SPV, the compliant route is a direct SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's banking account to the SPV's NZD corporate account with a New Zealand Tier-1 bank. The wire must carry a purpose code corresponding to direct investment equity capital under the IMF Balance of Payments Manual and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's balance of payments reporting system. There is no mandatory prior approval, but the receiving bank will mark the funds as capital contribution and may place a temporary hold until the company secretary files the share issuance with the New Zealand Companies Office and provides a capital verification certificate. The full UBO, source of funds and tax residence certificate should be sent under separate cover to the bank's KYC team before the wire is initiated. If the originator is a trust, the bank will require a trustee resolution and a trust deed excerpt confirming the authority to advance funds.For a passive holding SPV, cross-border payments are primarily administrative expenses, dividends, intragroup loan repayments, or capital contributions to lower-tier subsidiaries. Dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders should be executed using an MT103 with the instruction payment of dividends and the relevant New Zealand withholding tax reference submitted to Inland Revenue if resident withholding tax applies. Trade-related disbursements, if the group's operating subsidiaries route commodity purchases through New Zealand, can be settled through a commercial letter of credit issued by a New Zealand bank, or through documentary collections on a delivery-against-payment or documents-against-acceptance basis. The main clearing rail is New Zealand's interbank wire and SWIFT network; collateralised trade finance is only needed when the SPV is actually named as importer or exporter of record.Deliverable forward FX contracts (NZD/USD, NZD/AUD, NZD/EUR) for up to 12 months, supported by an actual underlying exposure from a planned capital contribution, dividend receipt or loan repayment.Cross-currency swaps for converting an NZD-denominated intercompany loan into the parent company's functional currency, provided the intercompany loan is evidenced by audited financial statements.European-style FX options on NZD/USD for downside protection without sacrificing upside, subject to execution of an ISDA master agreement and credit support annex with the New Zealand bank.Non-deliverable forwards are not ordinarily permissible for NZD inside New Zealand because the New Zealand dollar is freely deliverable; offshore NDFs may be considered only through a Singapore or Sydney branch if the SPV maintains dedicated offshore Treasury banking lines, and only against a verified underlying transaction.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A statutory audit is compulsory under the Financial Reporting Act 2013 for companies classified as 'large'. A company is large when, at the end of each of the two immediately preceding accounting periods, the company together with any controlled entities (on a consolidated basis) has total assets exceeding NZD 60 million, or revenue exceeding NZD 30 million, or has 80 or more full-time equivalent employees. A company that is not large is exempt from statutory audit unless it is an FMC reporting entity under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, has shareholder-requested audit provisions in its constitution, or shareholders representing at least 5% of voting rights validly require an audit. For this pure equity holding SPV with no public accountability, the default presumption is that it is not large and therefore is not required to appoint an auditor unless a parent company or lender requires one.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% (Income Tax Act 2007, Schedule 1, Part A)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% domestic NRWT on gross dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, reduced to the applicable treaty rate (typically 15%) only where the recipient has supplied a valid Tax Residency Certificate and beneficial ownership declaration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border related-party supplies, loans, services, or royalties must be disclosed to Inland Revenue on Form IR607 when aggregate related-party transactions or loan balances exceed NZD 10 million per income year. Large New Zealand-resident multinational groups with turnover exceeding NZD 50 million are expected to maintain OECD-aligned Local File and Master File documentation contemporaneously.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute, but the OECD CIGA framework and the general anti-avoidance rule under Income Tax Act 2007 section BG 1 require a pure equity holding SPV to demonstrate real core income-generating activities in New Zealand: strategic investment decision-making, board governance, local administration, bank accounts, accounting records, dividend declarations, and compliance oversight. Absent such substance, the SPV may be recharacterised as a shell or agent, denied treaty benefits, and treated as a sham for veil-piercing purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Artificial Shell SPV

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Income Tax Act 2007 section BG 1, reconstruct the arrangement, disallow imputation credits and deductions, impose a 100% shortfall penalty under Tax Administration Act 1994 section 141D, and assess the SPV as agent of the non-resident owners, with interest accruing from the due date.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under Companies Act 1993 sections 135-137 and 494, making directors personally liable for unpaid tax, restitution, and damages. Criminal tax evasion proceedings under TAA sections 143A-143B can generate asset freezing, border-control travel blacklisting, and imprisonment for up to 5 years.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: The distributing New Zealand company is liable as withholding agent under Income Tax Act 2007 section RE 18. Inland Revenue may assess the unreported NRWT, impose late payment penalties, and apply shortfall penalties of 20% for unacceptable tax position or 100% if the omission is deliberate. Directors may be held personally liable for the unremitted withholding tax under director-penalty provisions in the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing exposes the signing director to joint and several liability for unpaid NRWT, penalties, and interest. Where the director knowingly caused the improper dividend flow, criminal proceedings may result in civil asset restitution, travel-ban blacklisting, and imprisonment for up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalisation Abuse

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 sections GC 6 to GC 14 authorise the Commissioner to adjust the consideration to the arm's length amount. Thin capitalisation rules in section FE 4 may disallow interest deductions for debt levels above the permitted ratio. Shortfall penalties of 20% for unacceptable tax positions, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% for evasion apply under Tax Administration Act 1994 section 141, together with interest and potential IR607 disclosure penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign false intercompany loan agreements, royalty contracts, or transfer pricing documentation are exposed to personal liability through veil-piercing and the director penalty regime for tax shortfalls. Criminal conviction can include confiscation orders, asset freezes, travel-ban blacklisting, and imprisonment for up to 5 years.
