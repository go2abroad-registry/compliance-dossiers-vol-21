Competent corporate registry

Formation / constitutional filings

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (New Zealand), administered by the Commissioner of Inland Revenue under the Tax Administration Act 1994; electronic filing and taxpayer portal: myIR at my.ird.govt.nz; entity registration infrastructure: New Zealand Companies Office and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) register.

E-government filing portal

A New Zealand limited company obtains an NZBN upon incorporation via the Companies Office and is registered for tax with Inland Revenue, which issues the statutory IRD number. For a foreign non-resident entity, the entity must first obtain an NZBN or register as an overseas company with the Companies Office if carrying on business in New Zealand, then apply to Inland Revenue through the myIR portal or the IRD non-resident company registration process. The application must provide proof of legal existence in the home jurisdiction, the certificate of incorporation or equivalent registration extract, constitutional documents, director and ultimate beneficial owner details, the intended New Zealand registered office or agent, and a statement of anticipated New Zealand tax activity. Inland Revenue will issue an IRD number used for income tax, GST, PAYE and other tax accounts. Separate GST registration is mandatory when the entity carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand or when a non-resident supplier of remote services to New Zealand consumers exceeds the NZD 60,000 GST registration threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None at general law; New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Eligible close companies may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under the Income Tax Act 2007 so that tax losses and income flow through to shareholders; an ordinary New Zealand limited company remains an entity-level taxpayer with a full imputation system, unless an LTC election is in force.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in a 12-month period for a taxable activity carried on in New Zealand; a non-resident supplier of remote services, including digital and automated payroll software, must register for GST if total supplies to New Zealand consumer recipients exceed or are likely to exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 to goods physically exported from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply and to services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, where the supply is not acquired by a New Zealand resident for use in New Zealand. For a payroll automation SaaS platform, exported digital and cloud-based services to non-resident offshore clients may qualify for 0% GST if the recipient is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the supplier holds contemporaneous evidence of the recipient's non-resident status and offshore usage.

  • Electronic export entry lodged with the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window, together with the approved Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing actual physical exportation from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, proof of supply contract and any electronic service delivery logs
  • Bank-documented foreign exchange repatriation evidence, such as SWIFT MT103 or international payment settlement advice, evidencing receipt of offshore proceeds in cleared funds; New Zealand does not operate an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) regime

New Zealand does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate for GST. Registered persons must issue and retain GST invoices containing all prescribed statutory details; electronic invoicing through the Peppol network is optional, interoperable with IRD systems, and satisfies Tax Administration Act 1994 record-keeping and GST invoice requirements.

Inland Revenue may require full documentation substantiating physical export, foreign exchange settlement and the non-resident destination of services. If the exporter does not maintain or produce adequate evidence within the audit timeframe, the zero-rating is denied and the supply is re-characterised as standard-rated at 15% GST, creating a tax shortfall. Statutory consequences include use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994, including 20% for an unacceptable tax position or failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion; criminal liability may also arise for knowingly providing false or misleading GST information.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates as a freely convertible, open capital account jurisdiction without blanket foreign exchange controls. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand prudentially supervises registered banks and clearing systems, while the Financial Markets Authority administers market conduct and anti-money-laundering rules. Current account transactions, including software subscription revenue and payroll service export receipts, benefit from unconditional convertibility under IMF Article VIII. Capital account flows, such as equity injections, shareholder loans, and acquisition of controlling interests, are generally unrestricted, although some direct inward investments may be screened under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 if they involve sensitive land or significant business assets. There are no statutory surrender requirements for export proceeds: an NZ company may keep receipts in a foreign currency account, convert to NZD at any time, or use the funds for direct offshore disbursements. Profit remittance is permitted through ordinary banking channels but is subject to non-resident withholding tax on dividends and interest, plus any relevant double tax agreement relief. Every cross-border movement is captured by the customer's banking anti-money-laundering reporting and Reserve Bank balance of payments statistics, but no exchange control permission is required. Therefore the treasury structure should maintain full NZD liquidity and an internal foreign currency ledger to monetise natural hedges between payroll billing and supplier payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion is imposed in New Zealand. Export proceeds may be held indefinitely in any foreign currency account or repatriated as the board directs, subject only to standard banking reporting and anti-money-laundering thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Direct viability is Low to Medium for overseas digital EMIs because they cannot offer a New Zealand bank account, cannot join the Payments NZ clearing infrastructure, and are not recognised as registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act. A foreign EMI may service the New Zealand entity only if it operates under a partnership with a New Zealand tier-1 bank or is registered as a money remitter and has an authorised agency arrangement in New Zealand. For a payroll SaaS platform that holds client funds and disburses salaries as a business model, bank compliance will regard the EMI as an unnecessary third-party intermediary and will impose enhanced due diligence, sanctions screening, and source-of-funds documentation for each inward transfer. The prudent treasury structure is direct tier-1 banking in New Zealand, with the EMI used solely for payment initiation and reconciliation, not as a treasury custodian.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident individual director and multi-country beneficial ownership chain fail to provide a New Zealand tax identification number or local nexus.

Root cause: Non-resident individual director and multi-country beneficial ownership chain fail to provide a New Zealand tax identification number or local nexus.

Defense evidence: Provide certified passport and proof of residential address for each director; 12-month bank and utility statements; current certificate of tax residence from each jurisdiction; a definitive register of ultimate beneficial owners; an IRD number application or non-resident exemption letter; and a statutory declaration describing the purpose and operational reality of the New Zealand entity.
Offshore trust or shareholder loan structures conceal the true source of the capital injection, raising suspicion of undeclared political exposure or indirect criminal acquisition.

Root cause: Offshore trust or shareholder loan structures conceal the true source of the capital injection, raising suspicion of undeclared political exposure or indirect criminal acquisition.

Defense evidence: Supply the notarised constituent documents and trust deed; verified chain of ownership from the trustee to the platform; a source-of-wealth report showing the economic origins of injected funds; audited financial statements or accountant's letter; a transaction trail from a recognised bank in the initiating jurisdiction; and a written legal opinion from New Zealand counsel confirming the structure does not breach the Overseas Investment Act 2005.
A payroll SaaS platform will initiate a large number of third-party credit disbursements to beneficiary employees across 13+ jurisdictions, creating a pattern frequently associated with layering and mule accounts.

Root cause: A payroll SaaS platform will initiate a large number of third-party credit disbursements to beneficiary employees across 13+ jurisdictions, creating a pattern frequently associated with layering and mule accounts.

Defense evidence: Submit a user risk management framework acceptable to the bank; a complete list of client employers and the contractual basis for salary handling; standard payroll files with unique employee identifiers and salary calculation logic; beneficiary identity care and verification records; the platform's sanctions, politically exposed persons, and adverse-media screening output; transaction monitoring and velocity rules; and an independent AML audit report of the platform from the last 24 months.
Inbound initial capital is sourced from a third-party payment processor, cryptocurrency exchange, or unregulated digital EMI instead of a regulated banking channel.

Defense evidence: Obtain 12-month bank statements from the original processing financial institution; identify and verify all connected wallet or ledger accounts; provide source-of-wealth documentation and notarised loan or gift agreements; include a bank confirmation of settlement rails from the first regulated financial institution to the New Zealand bank; and demonstrate the end-to-end fiat trail without any anonymity-enhancing break in the chain.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections should be routed as a SWIFT MT103 from the group treasury bank to the receiving tier-1 New Zealand bank using the company's dedicated account. The remittance information should be precise and avoid compliance ambiguity: for share issuance, 'Subscription for New Zealand ordinary shares'; for a shareholder loan, 'Shareholder loan under Companies Act 1993'; for intra-group subscription, 'Intra-group investment in New Zealand subsidiary'. All inbound transfers in New Zealand are free of exchange control approval but must be reported by the bank under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand for balance-of-payments statistical purposes. The bank will also capture a purpose code classification; the treasury should use 'direct investment - share capital' or 'direct investment - other capital - intercompany loan' to enable clear capital verification. Once received, the company must update the Companies Office ownership register and, if required, verify the subscribed capital in management accounts; there is no statutory minimum share capital, so quasi-capital under shareholder loans may be used to control liquidity. Settlement into the New Zealand dollar clearing system occurs through the Exchange Settlement Account System, and for amounts above the bank's internal threshold the treasury may be required to provide the original source-of-funds certificate before releasing escrow conditions.For cross-border trade settlement and payroll distribution, the company should use SWIFT MT103 with ISO 20022 remittance details for all international disbursements. For New Zealand domestic payroll, the appropriate rail is BECS (Bulk Electronic Clearing System) Direct Credit via a Payments NZ participant, automatically debiting the operating account and crediting employee accounts with a single batch. High-value same-day domestic settlements should be completed through ESAS. Trade settlements for clients or subcontractors are best executed under commercial documentary letters of credit (MT700/MT710) with a New Zealand bank advising or confirming; documentary collections (MT400) can be used for lower-risk trade flows where no letter of credit is required. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border associated-party transactions. Above this threshold, Inland Revenue expects contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation aligned with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines; Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more (NZD equivalent).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substances Regulations regime, but substantive local activity is required to avoid general anti-avoidance reconstruction under Income Tax Act 2007 sections BG 1 and GB 1. For a payroll automation SaaS company, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) under OECD principles include software development oversight, payroll processing control, client contracting, treasury management, employment decisions, compliance and AML/CFT functions, and assumption of operational risk. If CIGA are performed offshore without arm's-length reward, Inland Revenue will treat the NZ entity as a shell and reconstruct taxable income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - PAYE / NRWT Fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 sections 141 and 143B; civil shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax plus use-of-money interest, and criminal liability on conviction to a fine of up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment for up to four years. Director Penalty Notices can also be issued under section 145C for unremitted PAYE.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for PAYE and other withholding taxes deducted from employees where the company fails to pay those amounts to Inland Revenue. The corporate veil is pierced through Director Penalty Notices, allowing recovery against personal bank accounts and assets. Directors who knowingly facilitate payroll tax evasion face criminal conviction, civil restitution orders, director disqualification under the Companies Act 1993, and court-imposed travel bans or passport surrender conditions.
Transfer Pricing Profit Shifting and Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 sections GC 6 to GC 14 authorise Inland Revenue to substitute an arm's-length consideration, disallow excessive deductions, and reconstruct the NZ taxable profit. Civil shortfall penalties range from 20% to 150% under Tax Administration Act 1994 section 141, depending on whether the failure was careless, grossly careless, abusive, or evasion-related.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve transfer pricing schemes without economic substance can be held personally liable as knowingly concerned in tax evasion under Tax Administration Act 1994 section 143B. Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the NZ entity is a mere instrumentality or sham, ordering directors to restore diverted profits, pay restitution, and compensate Inland Revenue; criminal conviction can bring imprisonment, blacklisting by financial institutions, and travel bans preventing departure from New Zealand.
AML/CFT and Payment Rails Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 sections 75 and 76 permit the High Court to impose civil pecuniary penalties of up to NZD 5,000,000 for a body corporate and NZD 2,000,000 for an individual. Money-laundering conduct involving payroll proceeds can also be prosecuted under Crimes Act 1961 section 243, carrying imprisonment terms for serious offending.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause or are knowingly concerned in AML/CFT failures are personally exposed to pecuniary penalty orders, civil asset forfeiture and restitution of processed funds, and criminal liability for money laundering. The court may also disqualify directors, order surrender of passports and impose travel bans, and place the company and directors on financial-services blacklists that prevent continued operation of the payroll platform.
