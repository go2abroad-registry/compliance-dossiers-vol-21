Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand; electronic filing and registration through the myIR secure online portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity with a New Zealand tax nexus, whether deriving New Zealand-source income, carrying on a taxable activity, or making supplies subject to New Zealand GST, must obtain an IRD number from Inland Revenue. The entity must submit its statutory registration application through the myIR portal or by lodging the prescribed paper application with Inland Revenue, accompanied by foreign incorporation documents, certificate of good standing or equivalent, registered office and head office addresses, director and substantial shareholder details, the entity's foreign tax identification number, and a statement of the New Zealand activities giving rise to the tax nexus. Inland Revenue validates the identity and activity details and issues the IRD number, which serves as the single tax identification number for income tax, GST, payroll, and all other tax obligations. If the foreign entity incurs taxable supplies in New Zealand and is required to register for GST, the same IRD number is used as the GST/VAT registration number; no separate VAT identifier is issued. Entities that merely export goods from New Zealand without making taxable supplies in New Zealand may still voluntarily register for GST to claim input tax credits and substantiate zero-rating, subject to Inland Revenue approval.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in New Zealand. An eligible closely held company may elect look-through company (LTC) status, under which taxable income and losses flow through to shareholders. Hybrid and branch mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act 2007 (subpart FH) operate as mandatory statutory overrides rather than elective regimes.

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand consumers are also required to register when their supplies to New Zealand exceed NZD 60,000 in the same period

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the GST Act 1985, a supply of goods qualifies for the zero rate when the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand in the course of the supply, the goods are not supplied for consumption in New Zealand, and the supplier retains conclusive evidence of physical export such as customs-approved shipping documentation. Services supplied by a New Zealand supplier to a non-resident customer may qualify for zero-rating if the services are performed or enjoyed outside New Zealand and are not connected with New Zealand land or moveable property. For TikTok Shop cross-border retail transactions, the New Zealand seller must demonstrate that the goods were sold and dispatched to a foreign customer, left New Zealand under customs supervision, and that export proceeds were received from outside New Zealand.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Export Declaration (Export Entry lodged through the Trade Single Window)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list evidencing the overseas buyer and consignee
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill showing export from New Zealand to a foreign destination
  • Cargo and shipping documentation confirming customs exit from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank-issued foreign exchange receipt showing receipt of the overseas buyer's payment

New Zealand does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based per-invoice stamping, or anti-fraud digital sealing. Tax invoices may be issued in electronic form if they contain all compulsory GST information under the GST Act 1985. Peppol e-invoicing is optional and supported by Inland Revenue. GST-registered persons file GST returns electronically through myIR, but there is no per-transaction government clearance requirement.

Inland Revenue may audit export supply records and require the complete customs and foreign-exchange documentation bundle before accepting the zero rate. If zero-rating is not substantiated, the supply is reassessed to GST at 15%, with use-of-money interest running from the original returns date. The Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes shortfall penalties for evasion or lack of reasonable care, including penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall for fraudulent or intentional withholding, and criminal sanctions may apply. Any GST refund claimed on the basis of unverified or false export documentation is retrospectively clawed back and assessed as ordinary taxable supply output, subject to the ordinary reassessment time bar unless fraud or misrepresentation is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a liberalised, fully convertible currency regime. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) floats freely, and there are no foreign exchange controls, surrender requirements, or central bank approval gates on either current account or capital account flows. The current account is fully convertible: trade payments, service fees, royalties, interest and dividends may be remitted without Reserve Bank of New Zealand or Inland Revenue consent, subject only to non-resident withholding tax and anti-money laundering verification. The capital account is likewise unrestricted for ordinary commercial equity and debt, although the Overseas Investment Act 2005 creates consent thresholds for sensitive land, significant business assets, fishing quota, or media licences, and those must be screened before the transaction is settled. Profit remittance is blocked only by standard corporate law duties and the bank's KYC verification of a valid board resolution, distributable reserves, and the absence of liquidity concerns. There is no exchange-control licensing system, no import/export FX allocation, no requirement to channel conversions through official windows, and no differentiation between residents and non-residents for access to FX products. Surplus NZD may be invested abroad or converted to any major currency at resident banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender regime: exporters may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts without compulsory conversion, and no mandatory conversion ratio or surrender timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs that are not registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register and are not reporting entities under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 have low and practically unusable viability: they cannot lawfully open a New Zealand corporate account or operate settlement rails for the entity. Onshore or registered EMIs, in particular Wise B2B when appointed as the TikTok Shop payout rail, are feasible as a secondary, high-frequency collection rail, but they are not a substitute for a Tier-1 commercial bank because they generally lack documentary credit capacity, full Reserve Bank settlement access, and committed debt facilities. The prudent treasury structure is to use Wise B2B solely for platform payout capture and sweep every balance nightly to the Tier-1 bank, preventing aggregate exposure at any unbanked EMI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million aggregate cross-border related-party controlled transactions triggers the expectation of a contemporaneous transfer pricing Local File; Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting obligations arise for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of NZD 1.125 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations code comparable to low-tax offshore regimes, but economic substance is integral to the permanent establishment and transfer pricing rules. A TikTok Shop retail operation claiming to be a fully functioning NZ principal must demonstrate that the core income-generating activities, such as strategic inventory management, sales risk, platform contract negotiation, and customer-facing decision-making, are actually performed in New Zealand. Where the NZ entity is a shell that merely book-keeps live-commerce profits generated by foreign principals, the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and the reconstruction powers in the transfer pricing rules enable Inland Revenue to re-attribute the profits to the substance-locating jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Unreported Offshore Receipts

Statutory sanction: Sections 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994 criminalize evasion and the carrying on of business with intent to defraud, with fines up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment up to five years. The civil shortfall penalty for evasion is up to 150% of the tax underpaid under section 141D, plus use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or participate in the concealment are treated as aiding and abetting tax evasion. The corporate veil is pierced under the fraudulent trading provisions of the Companies Act 1993, making directors personally liable for the unpaid tax and creditors' losses. Inland Revenue may also issue a departure prohibition order under section 157 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, imposing an effective travel ban, and criminal conviction can lead to asset restraint and confiscation under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Substance Mismatch

Statutory sanction: Sections GC 1 and GC 6 of the Income Tax Act 2007 empower the Commissioner to reconstruct income using the arm's length principle. A transfer pricing shortfall penalty under section 141EB and an abusive tax position penalty under section 141E can be imposed at up to 50% of the resulting tax shortfall, with use-of-money interest; the documentation failure itself can lead to adverse inferences and evidentiary penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement the mispricing or fail to ensure the company's tax obligations are satisfied may be personally liable under section 149 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 for the company's tax liabilities. A breach of the directors' duties of care and good faith under sections 131 and 135 of the Companies Act 1993 exposes directors to compensation orders and restoration of diverted profits. Directors leaving New Zealand while such liabilities are outstanding may be subject to departure prohibition and arrest on departure.
Withholding Tax Evasion and Unlawful Dividend Distributions Through Shell Entities

Statutory sanction: Section RA 7 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes the withholding obligation; section 139B applies penalty tax for failure to comply; section 141D can impose a 150% evasion penalty. Sections 52, 55, and 56 of the Companies Act 1993 render unlawful any dividend paid other than out of profits and after the solvency test, and impose personal recovery obligations on directors.

Director personal liability: The Companies Act 1993 requires directors who sign or authorize an unlawful dividend to repay the full amount with interest, and this obligation survives the company's liquidation. Inland Revenue can invoke section 149 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 to recover unpaid NRWT directly from directors. Non-resident directors face a departure prohibition order and, where fraud is proven, criminal prosecution for evasion with imprisonment up to five years and confiscation of assets derived from the unlawful repatriation.
