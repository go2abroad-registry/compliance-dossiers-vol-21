Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake); online taxpayer portal: myIR secure online services; company incorporation registry: New Zealand Companies Office / Companies Register.

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand-incorporated limited company, the IRD number is obtained through the Companies Office registration workflow or by post-registration application via myIR. Required particulars include certificate of incorporation, New Zealand registered office address, directors and shareholders, constitution or articles of association, and intended tax registration dates. For a foreign non-resident company not incorporated in New Zealand, the entity must apply to Inland Revenue using form IR596 (Non-resident company IRD number application), providing foreign certificate of incorporation, board resolution, appointment of a New Zealand tax agent or resident contact for service of notices, proof of business address or fixed establishment in New Zealand, and financial projections demonstrating actual or intended taxable activity. Inland Revenue issues the same IRD number for income tax, GST, and employer obligations. A non-resident entity that has New Zealand taxable activity or that supplies remote services to New Zealand consumers above the GST threshold may apply for GST registration simultaneously.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 (section 51); non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand consumers have the same NZD 60,000 threshold under the remote services GST regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when they are exported from New Zealand by the supplier within 28 days of the time of supply. Services are zero-rated under section 11A when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not in New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are not directly connected with New Zealand land or goods. For remote services such as UI/UX design subscriptions supplied on a B2B basis to an overseas non-resident business customer, zero-rating is available when the recipient is not in New Zealand and the place-of-supply rules in section 8B do not treat the supply as made to a New Zealand consumer. The same service supplied to a New Zealand-resident consumer is standard-rated GST at 15%.

  • New Zealand Customs export entry filed through the New Zealand Trade Single Window or CusMod, identifying the exporter, destination, goods description, HS classification, and export date.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier evidencing that the goods physically departed New Zealand.
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract matching the GST tax invoice and confirming the overseas buyer.
  • NZ Customs cargo movement or outward export report showing exit from New Zealand.
  • Bank settlement evidence, including SWIFT MT103, New Zealand bank foreign-currency credit advice, or overseas payment receipt; an Inward Remittance Certificate is not a statutory instrument under New Zealand law.
  • For services-only exports: signed SaaS subscription agreement, one-time or recurring payment evidence, user records showing non-resident customer location, and IP deliverable acceptance records to substantiate the offshore recipient.

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance regime, government invoice API pre-approval, or anti-fraud digital stamping. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and supported through the Peppol interoperability framework. GST tax invoices must contain the statutory particulars required by section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and may be issued in paper or electronic form. Buyer-created tax invoices are permitted under section 24G. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR; transactional invoice clearance is not required, but zero-rated export invoices should state the zero-rating basis and be supported by the export documentation bundle.

Inland Revenue may assess GST on an export supply at the standard 15% rate, together with use-of-money interest, if the zero-rating is not substantiated by contemporaneous customs, transport, contract, and payment evidence. Records supporting zero-rated exports must be retained for seven years under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 include 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 50% where an unacceptable tax position has a dominant purpose of tax avoidance, and up to 150% for evasion or similar conduct. Fraudulent export documentation or intent to defeat GST may also expose directors and shareholders to criminal liability under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and penalties under the Crimes Act 1961. Inland Revenue can amend assessments within four years for non-specified matters, with no time limit in cases of evasion; clawback will include reversal of input credits previously attributable to the export supply and any refund obtained.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fundamentally liberalised foreign exchange regime characterized by full current account and capital account convertibility of the New Zealand dollar. There are no exchange control approvals, quantitative limits, or surrender-to-custodian requirements on the receipt, retention, or repatriation of foreign currency. Both capital inflows and outflows, including equity injections, intercompany loans, dividend repatriation, profit remittances, loan repayments, and trade-related settlements, may be freely routed through any registered commercial bank. Non-resident shareholders may own a New Zealand limited company without exchange-control attraction, provided the underlying business does not involve sensitive land or significant business assets that trigger the Overseas Investment Act 2005. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand is the central bank and prudential regulator, supervising banks and payment systems rather than operating a foreign exchange approval desk. The practical constraints on convertibility today are therefore not statutory exchange controls but bank-level AML/CFT onboarding, sanctions screening, source-of-funds verification, and the obligation on the company to meet New Zealand resident withholding tax and non-resident withholding tax rules when distributing funds. Profit remittance to shareholders is lawful and unrestricted once the entity has satisfied Companies Office filings, tax payments, and the bank's economic substance questioning about beneficial ownership. No special central bank permission is required for payment routing, forward hedging, foreign currency accounts, or multicurrency balances, making the New Zealand jurisdiction highly suitable for an international design-as-a-service treasury structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules in New Zealand. An exporter or design-subscription supplier may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts in any onshore or connected foreign currency account, may settle immediately into NZD at a free market price, or may hold the funds offshore without conversion; there is no mandatory conversion ratio and no surrender timeline imposed by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and fintech payment platforms receive a Low-to-Medium rating as the sole treasury bank relationship but a High rating as ancillary cross-border settlement rails for a low-risk design subscription business. The Banking Prudential Supervision regime in New Zealand restricts deposit-taking from the public to registered banks; an offshore EMI or e-money institution is not a compliant substitute for a domestic bank operating account, capital escrow relationship, trade finance facility, or foreign exchange hedging line. Wise is viable for receiving design-client subscription collections and for low-value disbursements to overseas designers because it is registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register and provides transparent multi-currency FX. Stripe is viable as a recurring payment gateway for UI/UX subscription invoices, but funds settled through Stripe and Wise must be swept into the prime New Zealand Tier 1 bank account to maintain a clear audit trail for capital verification, tax filings, and bank covenant purposes. An offshore EMI that is not registered as a financial service provider in New Zealand and does not maintain an NZD clearing relationship with a registered bank should be rejected for any balance above daily operating contingency thresholds. The primary liquidity hub should always be a locally incorporated commercial bank that can issue SWIFT instructions, accept inbound investor money, perform OFAC/sanctions screening, and support documentary trade instruments without regulatory ambiguity.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory NRWT on dividends paid by a New Zealand company to a non-resident is 15% where the dividend is fully imputed with New Zealand imputation credits, and 30% where the dividend is not fully imputed. Dividends paid within a wholly-owned group may be exempt from NRWT under the supplementary dividend rules only if specific administrative conditions are met. Under most NZ DTA protocols, the treaty dividend rate is 15% for portfolio shareholders and can be reduced to 5% for a corporate shareholder holding at least 10-15% of the voting power, subject to the limitation-on-benefits or principal purpose test.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For a standard 31 March balance date, the IR4 must be filed by 7 July following the end of the tax year. Provisional tax instalments are generally due in three instalments during the year, and terminal tax is due on the same 7th day of the fourth month after balance date unless a tax agent extension applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 1,000,000 for annual cross-border related-party transactions (each transaction category or aggregate related-party dealings above this level requires a Local File). A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue above NZD 680,000,000, following the OECD BEPS Action 13 template. New Zealand transfer pricing rules also require contemporaneous documentation, typically prepared within 30 days of the return filing date or before the tax position is taken.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate the Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but substance is a decisive legal factor in three areas: (1) tax residence – a company incorporated in NZ is resident unless its centre of management is elsewhere, and an offshore-managed NZ company may be non-resident if a tie-breaker treaty clause applies; (2) permanent establishment – a foreign parent of the NZ design agency must not create a taxable PE in NZ through employees or dependent agents; (3) DTA entitlement – the NZ entity must be the beneficial owner of the design IP and must perform core income-generating activities (CIGA) in New Zealand, including strategic design decisions, client contracting, risk assumption, and senior management of subscription operations. A shell NZ Ltd that merely holds the design subscription contract while all substantive activities occur offshore will be ignored for tax purposes, and the profits will be reallocated under the OECD BEPS rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Sham Substance and Artificial Profit Extraction

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1 and s GB 1 permit Inland Revenue to reconstruct the transaction and disregard the artificial company. Tax Administration Act 1994 s 141A-141EB impose a shortfall penalty of 100% for an abusive tax position and 150% for evasion, plus use-of-money interest. Under the Crimes Act 1961 s 144A, a person who evades assessment of tax is liable to imprisonment for up to 3 years; under s 144B, evasion of payment of tax carries imprisonment for up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the company is a facade for tax evasion. Directors who signed the sham service agreements or directed profit shifting are jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax shortfall to the extent they received a benefit or actively participated. Inland Revenue can issue a departure prohibition order, freezing exit from New Zealand, and can institute civil asset recovery under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009. Directors may also be disqualified from managing companies under the Companies Act 1993 for up to 10 years and face criminal imprisonment for fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Failure to Produce Contemporaneous Documentation

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 s GC 6 to s GC 18 require the Commissioner to adjust the taxable income to an arm’s-length amount. Failure to prepare contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation exposes the company to a 20% shortfall penalty for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% for an abusive tax position. The Tax Administration Act 1994 also imposes a separate penalty for failing to disclose cross-border transactions, and the group may be required to file a Country-by-Country Report; non-filing attracts $1,000 penalty for each failure and further penalties for each month of non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve a non-arm’s-length intercompany price are personally liable for the shortfall penalty if the company is used as a mere conduit and the transfer pricing adjustment exceeds the company’s ability to pay. The directors may be banned from undertaking management of the NZ company, their personal bank accounts may be subject to civil restraint orders, and if the arrangement involves false invoices or intentionally misleading the tax authority, they are subject to criminal prosecution under the Crimes Act 1961. Piercing the veil is available where the director has stripped the company of assets before payment of the adjusted tax liability.
False DTA Claim, Shell Company Treatment, and NRWT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue will deny DTA relief under both the OECD MLI principal purpose test and domestic anti-avoidance rules. The company is liable for NRWT at the statutory 30% (or 15% if fully imputed) on the entire dividend, plus a 100% shortfall penalty for the abusive tax position and use-of-money interest. The Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes a separate absolute-liability penalty on any company that fails to withhold NRWT from a dividend or interest payment, equal to the amount of unpaid NRWT, and the Commissioner may recover the penalty from the directors personally as persons responsible for the company’s obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors who arrange the dividend payment knowing the treaty claim is false are personally liable for the NRWT shortfall and penalties. The director may be blacklisted by Inland Revenue under the departure prohibition order regime and removed from the Companies Office directors register. Civil asset tracing may reach the director’s personal assets if the dividend was paid to a related offshore entity and then on-distributed to the director. Criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment for up to 7 years for evasion of payment of tax plus substantial restitution orders.
