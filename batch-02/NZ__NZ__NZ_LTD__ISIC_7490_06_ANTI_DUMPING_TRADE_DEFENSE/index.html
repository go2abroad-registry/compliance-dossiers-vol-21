Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under the Companies Act 1993. The company must issue at least one share but there is no blocked escrow deposit requirement. Shares may be paid in money or other lawful consideration if the board determines the consideration is adequate; no minimum capital needs to be held in a blocked bank account. Non-resident ownership is permitted.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) – online portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir)

E-government filing portal

For foreign non-resident entities not incorporated in New Zealand, the IRD number is the statutory tax identification number. The procedure is: (1) determine whether the entity is carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand or has any New Zealand-source income; (2) prepare certified copies of the foreign certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, board resolution authorising the New Zealand application, register of shareholders and directors, and proof of overseas tax residence; (3) file the Inland Revenue application for a non-resident company IRD number through myIR or the Inland Revenue contact centre; (4) appoint a New Zealand address for service and, where required, a tax agent; (5) upon issue, use the IRD number for all tax filings and assessments; (6) if taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, register for GST in myIR within 21 days after the obligation arises, with the IRD number serving as the GST registration number. If the entity will not carry on a taxable activity but has withholding obligations, it must still notify Inland Revenue for registration of the appropriate withholding tax account type.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, supplies of goods are zero-rated when the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply, or when the goods are placed in New Zealand and the supplier is satisfied that the non-resident recipient will export them within that statutory period. Qualifying services, including legal and trade-defense consulting services for international anti-dumping inquiries, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not supplied in connection with land or goods in New Zealand and do not fall within any statutory excepted supply. Digital services supplied to New Zealand consumers remain subject to GST; however, a New Zealand supplier may zero-rate qualifying services supplied to non-resident recipients who are outside New Zealand upon retention of adequate evidence of the recipient's non-resident status.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry lodged via the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air freight evidencing physical departure from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the exported goods or services
  • Bank or foreign exchange remittance records, including SWIFT confirmations, evidencing settlement of the export invoice in foreign currency

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no API stamping requirement, and no anti-fraud digital stamping regime applies to GST invoices in New Zealand. Registered persons must issue tax invoices containing the prescribed particulars, and must provide a tax invoice within 28 days of a request from the recipient. The voluntary Peppol e-invoicing framework is supported for B2B electronic exchange, but Inland Revenue does not operate a real-time clearance or pre-clearance approval system for standard GST invoices.

Inland Revenue may audit export zero-rating claims and require proof that goods were physically exported within 28 days or that the services recipient was a non-resident outside New Zealand. If the supporting documentation is not substantiated, the zero rating is retrospectively reversed and GST at the standard 15% rate is reassessed from the time of supply, with late payment penalties and use-of-money interest under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Abusive tax positions can attract a 100% shortfall penalty, and fraudulent GST evasion is a criminal offence. Additionally, false export declarations may trigger penalties imposed by the New Zealand Customs Service under the Customs and Excise Act 2018. The 180-day benchmark in this dossier reflects the standard administrative expectation for assembling and preserving export-settlement documentation; New Zealand does not impose a separate statutory FX repatriation deadline for GST zero-rating purposes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains an open capital and current account regime without foreign exchange controls. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand supervises banks and promotes a sound financial system, but it does not approve or block ordinary cross-border transfers. New Zealand dollars are freely convertible into major international currencies onshore and offshore. Non-residents may own 100% of a New Zealand limited company, appoint directors, and hold funds in domestic or foreign currency accounts. Export proceeds are fully retainable, and there is no requirement to convert foreign currency into NZD. Inward capital injections are permitted without prior RBNZ approval, subject to standard bank know-your-customer, anti-money-laundering and sanctions screening. The Overseas Investment Act may apply only if the entity acquires sensitive land or significant business assets, which is not relevant for ISIC 7490 professional anti-dumping defense services. Profit repatriation is subject to normal tax administration: dividends paid to non-resident shareholders attract non-resident withholding tax at applicable treaty rates, and the company must satisfy imputation ratio requirements. There are no surrender requirements, no exchange approval certificates needed, and no separate capital control registry. The main practical hurdles are documentary, not regulatory: banks require source-of-wealth evidence, beneficial ownership declarations, and board resolutions, particularly for large lump-sum fee payments. Overall, New Zealand's regulatory environment offers low currency convertibility friction and a highly reliable multilateral clearing and SWIFT infrastructure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists in New Zealand. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts indefinitely in either New Zealand dollar or foreign currency accounts. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no conversion window, and no central bank directive requiring repatriation; banks only submit balance-of-payments statistical reports to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Strong ancillary viability but not primary. Because New Zealand has no exchange controls, licensed non-bank electronic money institutions such as Wise and Airwallex can legally onboard the New Zealand entity, hold NZD and multi-currency balances, and support cross-border fee collections for trade defense services. However, these EMIs are not registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act and cannot provide access to the local ESAS clearing system, direct debit facilities, documentary letters of credit, or committed overdraft liquidity. For anti-dumping defense work receiving funds from government agencies and corporate counterparties, an EMI can be used for low-risk collections and disbursements after completing EMI KYC, but the primary treasury relationship must remain with a tier-1 commercial bank to ensure full clearing rails, sanctions screening accountability, and robust capital verification.
