Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox cannot satisfy the statutory requirement of a physical registered office in New Zealand under the Companies Act 1993. The registered office must be a real street address in New Zealand where documents can be served during business hours and from which the company's records may be inspected. For this light value-added fulfillment and kitting business, Inland Revenue requires a valid physical principal place of business for GST registration and income tax administration; a virtual office address is generally rejected by Inland Revenue as a place of business. Licensed New Zealand banks uniformly require evidence of physical operating premises, such as a lease or utility statement, and a registered office at a virtual mailbox will trigger elevated customer due diligence and very high likelihood of account rejection. A virtual office may only be acceptable as a secondary administrative mail forwarding address if the company maintains an actual operational warehouse or kitting facility, and even then the registered office must remain distinct and physical.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake) — online portal myIR; export and customs clearances are filed through New Zealand Customs Service (Te Mana Ārai o Aotearoa) using the Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand registered limited company (NZ_LTD), the Companies Office issues the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) upon incorporation. The company must then obtain an IRD number through myIR using its NZBN, legal name, registered office address, director details, and anticipated tax obligations. A non-resident company that does not have an NZBN but is carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand applies to Inland Revenue for an IRD number via the myIR application process or through an approved tax agent; the application requires the overseas entity's legal name, country of incorporation, registered office, directors/controlling owners, overseas tax identification number, and a statement of the NZ tax activities. A foreign non-resident entity that carries on business through a branch in New Zealand must register as an overseas company at the Companies Office to obtain an NZBN before applying for an IRD number. Non-resident suppliers of remote services may register for GST without forming a New Zealand company. The same IRD number is used for income tax, GST, and employer obligations; once registered, the entity must keep its details updated in myIR and may file returns electronically.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in New Zealand. A closely held New Zealand company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, making it fiscally transparent for income tax while retaining limited liability. Dividend imputation is operated through an imputation credit account (ICA), but no elective taxation-as-corporation mechanism is required because a company is taxable by default. The hybrid and branch mismatch rules are mandatory anti-avoidance overlays, not elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies (goods and services) in New Zealand in a 12-month period; a non-resident supplier of remote services and non-resident sellers of goods in New Zealand are also required to register once this NZD 60,000 threshold is exceeded or is expected to be exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11(1)(a) of the GST Act 1985, a supply of goods is zero-rated when the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days after the date of supply, or such longer period as the Commissioner allows if the supplier is not reasonably able to export within that period. Section 11(1)(b) zero-rates goods supplied to a non-resident who is in New Zealand at the time of supply and subsequently exports them. Services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply are zero-rated under section 11(1)(m), subject to exceptions for services connected to New Zealand land or moveable personal property in New Zealand; therefore, a separately invoiced kitting or fulfilment service performed on goods physically in New Zealand may be standard-rated. If the transaction is structured as a single supply of completed kitted goods that is exported by the supplier, the entire consideration can be zero-rated as an export of goods. Qualifying offshore digital services are zero-rated only when supplied to non-resident persons outside New Zealand and are outside the physical ISIC 5210 fulfilment scope.

  • New Zealand Customs Service export declaration lodged through the Trade Single Window
  • Customs-approved sea bill of lading or air waybill evidencing physical departure
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list for the export shipment
  • Purchase order or sales contract identifying the non-resident customer
  • Supplier's GST tax invoice containing a clear zero-rated export statement
  • Customs departure/export manifest record showing the consignment leaving New Zealand
  • Bank foreign exchange settlement or inward remittance statement showing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Warehouse inventory and kitting/assembly works order records supporting the export transaction
  • Customer's non-residence evidence, such as overseas registered address and tax identification number, when zero-rating is based on the customer's non-residence

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate. E-invoicing is voluntary and follows the Peppol framework through accredited service providers. GST invoices must nevertheless contain the prescribed information in section 24 of the GST Act 1985, including supplier name and GST number, recipient name, date, description, quantity, price, GST amount, and the basis of zero-rating where claimed. GST returns are filed electronically via myIR or GST-accredited software; Inland Revenue uses risk-based data matching rather than pre-transaction clearance.

If zero-rating is not substantiated with export and foreign-currency documentary evidence, Inland Revenue can reassess the supply at 15% GST, impose use-of-money interest from the date the GST should have been returned, and apply civil shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994; penalties range from 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for a not reasonably arguable tax position, and up to 150% for evasion. If goods are not exported within the statutory 28-day period, zero-rating is clawed back automatically and GST must be accounted for in the original adjustment period. Records supporting zero-rating must be retained for 7 years under section 16 of the Tax Administration Act 1994. New Zealand has no general exchange-control deadline, but an unexplained failure to settle export proceeds within the 180-day documentary benchmark will heighten Inland Revenue and Customs audit scrutiny. False or misleading statements made to New Zealand Customs are additionally sanctionable under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, and director liability may be asserted for serious non-compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised and open capital account with no general foreign exchange controls. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible on both current and capital account transactions. There are no statutory surrender requirements for export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, or capital for non-resident shareholders. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) does not administer exchange control approvals; instead, the oversight framework focuses on prudential regulation of registered banks, anti-money laundering supervision through the Department of Internal Affairs and the Financial Markets Authority, and the reporting of large cross-border cash transactions under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. In practice, a New Zealand limited company may open foreign currency accounts, receive export earnings in USD/EUR/AUD, and convert or retain those balances freely. The only practical frictions are standard banking due diligence, beneficial ownership disclosure, and the requirement for accurate tax and customs declarations. Foreign investors can inject equity capital and service external debt without prior RBNZ approval, though significant foreign direct investment in sensitive sectors may be screened under the Overseas Investment Act 2005. Overall, the convertibility risk is negligible, and treasury operations can be structured using ordinary offshore accounts and SWIFT rails without special waivers or reinvestment obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may hold foreign currency indefinitely in New Zealand bank accounts or convert to NZD at their discretion. Any foreign currency receipts are treated as ordinary business income for tax purposes, but no surrender to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand is required.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Highly viable, with established Airwallex and specialist non-bank lenders operating under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008. These entities are not licensed as banks, but they are permitted to provide payment and foreign exchange services to New Zealand entities under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and the National Payments System. Airwallex, in particular, offers local NZD bank account details, multi-currency accounts, and cross-border payment rails, making it a legitimate substitute for routine operational treasury. However, a regulatory distinction must be observed: fintech EMIs cannot hold capital escrow for a New Zealand company if the escrow requirement is imposed by a foreign counterparty under a loan or acquisition agreement that explicitly requires a registered bank as custodian. For standard trade settlement and vendor payments, EMIs are fully permissible. The legal constraint is not EMI prohibition, but contractual acceptance by counterparties and the absence of deposit-taking insurance (which is provided by the RBNZ statutory guarantee for registered banks only up to NZD 100,000 per depositor). Therefore, recommend using a Tier-1 domestic bank for long-term capital retention and capital verification, while routing daily operational payments through an EMI such as Airwallex to reduce FX spread and processing latency.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be routed via SWIFT MT103 using the legitimate foreign exchange purpose code 'Direct Investment – Equity Injection' (code 6000 or equivalent as mandated by the originating bank). The payment reference must include the New Zealand IRD number, the company registration number (NZBN), and a clear notation such as 'Share Capital Subscription – [Entity Name]'. The New Zealand receiving bank will treat the credit as non-resident foreign direct investment; no RBNZ approval is required. For capital verification, the company must maintain a dedicated Capital Escrow Account at a registered Tier-1 bank and deposit the share capital within 10 business days of execution of the subscription agreement. New Zealand does not require minimum paid-up capital for a limited liability company, but if the UK or Singapore investor insists on showing capital verification, the accountant must issue a capital certificate based on the SWIFT credit advice and bank statement. Any foreign currency conversion is optional and should be executed at a commercial exchange rate; the bank will issue an FX deal confirmation that serves as treasury evidence.For outbound trade settlements, the kitting entity may use standard SWIFT MT103 for simple supplier payments and ACH Direct for domestic New Zealand disbursements. For high-value international supplier payables (e.g., USD-denominated procurement from Asia), the recommended rails are: (1) Documentary Collections (DP/DA) for established vendor relationships, (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C at sight) for first-time suppliers above USD 50,000, and (3) SWIFT MT103 for raw materials under USD 20,000 with a pre-agreed invoice. The New Zealand bank will require an underlying commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading (if sea freight), and a custom declaration to evidence the trade. There is no restriction on making vendor payments in foreign currency, and no prior RBNZ approval is required. However, a regulatory distinction must be observed: fintech EMIs cannot hold capital escrow for a New Zealand company if the escrow requirement is imposed by a foreign counterparty under a loan or acquisition agreement that explicitly requires a registered bank as custodian. For standard trade settlement and vendor payments, EMIs are fully permissible. The legal constraint is not EMI prohibition, but contractual acceptance by counterparties and the absence of deposit-taking insurance (which is provided by the RBNZ statutory guarantee for registered banks only up to NZD 100,000 per depositor). Therefore, recommend using a Tier-1 domestic bank for long-term capital retention and capital verification, while routing daily operational payments through an EMI such as Airwallex to reduce FX spread and processing latency.

Use-of-money interest is also charged on unpaid amounts at the prescribed IRD rate. Continued failure to file GST returns can result in default assessments, deduction of liabilities from bank accounts, and ultimately prosecution or deregistration of the GST number; administrative strike-off as a company is not directly triggered by GST non-filing but may be linked to broader non-compliance before the Companies Office.
