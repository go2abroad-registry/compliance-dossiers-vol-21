Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A New Zealand company must maintain a registered office in New Zealand at a physical address, not a PO Box. A virtual office or commercial mailbox may satisfy the statutory service address if the provider is licensed and the street address is disclosed, but for a fast-casual self-serve food franchise business the Inland Revenue Department and New Zealand registered banks will normally reject a mail-only virtual office for GST registration and merchant/deposit account opening unless the company also produces a trading premises lease, a valid Food Act 2014 registration, local management, payroll records, and a New Zealand operating bank account. Accordingly, virtual office use should be limited to a supplemental administrative address; substantive commercial substance is mandatory.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Companies Act 1993 does not impose any minimum paid-up capital and New Zealand does not require a blocked escrow or trust account. Shares must be issued for consideration determined by the board, recorded in the share register, and all conditions on unpaid or partly paid shares must be stated in the constitution or resolution.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) requires an approved fire evacuation scheme and maintained fire systems where the building risk profile mandates it. The territorial authority issues building consent, code compliance, and any warrant of fitness under the Building Act 2004. WorkSafe New Zealand requires a workplace safety policy, hazard register, emergency procedures, first-aid equipment, public liability insurance, and safe systems for hot broth handling, gas safety, and electrical safety under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015. Gas fitting and electrical work must be certified by licensed practitioners and records retained.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; online portal: myIR (secure online services) at myIR.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must first assess whether it is carrying on a GST taxable activity in New Zealand or supplying remote services to New Zealand consumers under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. If carrying on business in New Zealand, the entity should first register as an overseas company with the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993. GST registration is effected through the Inland Revenue's myIR portal and the same statutory IRD number (the New Zealand taxpayer identification number / TIN / GST registration number) is issued by the Commissioner of Inland Revenue. For income tax purposes, a non-resident company not incorporated in New Zealand must apply using the prescribed non-resident company IRD number application required by the Commissioner under the Tax Administration Act 1994; the application must include certified corporate constitutional documents, certificate of incorporation, register of directors and shareholders, registered office or New Zealand agent details, evidence of New Zealand taxable activity or source income, and any overseas tax identification number. The IRD number is used for income tax, GST, PAYE and other tax liabilities. A New Zealand-incorporated company is normally issued an IRD number upon incorporation through the Companies Office and registers for GST through myIR.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A closely held New Zealand Limited Company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under sections HB 1 to HB 7 of the Income Tax Act 2007 if it has five or fewer qualifying shareholders and meets statutory eligibility tests; otherwise no US-style check-the-box election exists. Hybrid mismatch recharacterization elections are generally not available; the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 imposes statutory ordering and defence rules rather than optional taxpayer elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period; a non-resident supplier of remote services to New Zealand residents must also register when New Zealand supplies exceed NZD 60,000 per annum, and voluntary registration is available for any person carrying on a taxable activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST is zero-rated under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for goods exported by the supplier, and under section 11A for services supplied to a non-resident outside New Zealand where relevant statutory tests are satisfied. For this restaurant/franchise platform, domestic fast-casual food and beverage sales in New Zealand are standard-rated at 15%; franchise royalties, licence fees and offshore support services supplied to non-resident franchisees operating outside New Zealand may be zero-rated when the recipient is a non-resident and the services are supplied and performed outside New Zealand. The supplier must hold written evidence establishing the non-resident status of the recipient and, for goods, must show physical removal from New Zealand within a reasonable timeframe.

  • New Zealand Customs Service export declaration lodged through the Trade Single Window / electronic export clearance system
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Airway Bill (air) evidencing physical export of goods
  • Commercial invoice and GST tax invoice showing the New Zealand GST number, description, quantity, value and 0% GST code
  • Written contract or agreement with the non-resident customer identifying export terms, delivery terms and destination
  • Bank settlement records or payment evidence confirming lawful commercial payment; New Zealand has no statutory Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) regime and no mandatory foreign-exchange repatriation requirement

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or anti-fraud digital stamping. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates on the Peppol network using the NZ PEPPOL BIS format. GST-registered persons must issue and retain compliant tax invoices and file GST returns electronically through myIR or an approved gateway service provider.

If the Commissioner of Inland Revenue determines that zero-rating was claimed without sufficient export or non-resident evidence, the GST becomes assessable at 15% with use-of-money interest running from the original due date. Shortfall penalties under Part 9 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 may be assessed up to 150% for abusive tax positions and evasion, plus late payment penalties and potential criminal prosecution for wilful evasion under section 143B. Retrospective clawback applies to any goods not physically exported or services not actually supplied to a non-resident outside New Zealand, including fruitless or fraudulent zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised foreign exchange framework. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand, as the central bank and prudential supervisor, does not administer exchange controls, and the Financial Markets Conduct Act and Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act impose only conduct and integrity obligations rather than restrictions on convertibility. Both current account transactions, such as trade settlement, royalties, and service fees, and capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, shareholder loans, and equity injections, are freely convertible into NZD or any other currency. There is no statutory surrender obligation for export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency in external accounts or domestic foreign currency accounts. For a Chinese Malatang self-serve fast-casual franchise holder, inbound capital contributions and outbound royalty payments may be routed through standard banking channels without Reserve Bank of New Zealand approval. Profit remittances face no quota restrictions, provided the company satisfies ordinary tax clearance and the bank's know-your-client requirements. The only prudential hurdle is the bank's responsibility under the AML/CFT Act to validate economic substance and beneficial ownership, so documentary transparency is the operative compliance variable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists in New Zealand. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency or freely converted to NZD. There are no mandatory conversion ratios, no repatriation time limits, and no Reserve Bank of New Zealand approval triggers. Statistical reporting to Statistics New Zealand for balance-of-payments purposes is required, but it is not a regulatory exchange-control restriction.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand - Te Putea Matua

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for offshore digital EMIs. A purely offshore EMI is not registered as a New Zealand bank under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and cannot hold local accounts in the New Zealand banking settlement system. Such EMIs are also not licensed financial service providers for this NZ entity under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and generally cannot meet the AML/CFT, GST, and economic substance validation demanded by New Zealand banks. They are legally barred from acting as the entity's primary treasury rail and should be rejected in favour of tier-1 domestic commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection routing requires a Chinese outbound investor to complete SAFE outbound FDI registration and capital account payment approval before remitting via SWIFT MT103 from an account in the investor's legal name to the NZ entity's tier-1 settlement account. The payment must reference capital subscription, state a clear purpose code, and be converted to NZD at the receiving bank. The NZ company must file share issue resolutions under the Companies Act 1993, update the share register within twenty working days, and engage a New Zealand chartered accountant to perform capital verification. The bank issues a capital confirmation letter to the accountant evidencing that funds were received and credited to the capital escrow account. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory NRWT on dividends is 30%. A fully imputed dividend is not subject to NRWT. Treaty-protected non-resident shareholders may qualify for a reduced rate, commonly 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for substantial corporate holdings under relevant double tax agreements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The IR4 must be filed by the 7th day of the fourth month after the company's balance date. For a standard 31 March balance date, the filing deadline is therefore 7 July. A tax agent extension may defer the filing obligation to the subsequent 31 March, but interest on any terminal tax unpaid from the original due date continues to run.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 3,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions for documentation preparation; NZD 1,000,000 for related-party royalty, interest, financing or intangible flows. Large taxpayers are expected to hold Local File and, where applicable, Master File documentation prepared contemporaneously under OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a discrete Economic Substance Regulation regime comparable to UAE commercial companies legislation. However, the Income Tax Act 2007 general anti-avoidance rule, the BEPS-driven treaty principal purpose test, and anti-conduit rules require genuine commercial purpose, active board governance, local management capacity, physical premises and operating substance for a Chinese Malatang self-serve franchise master licence or royalty structure. A shell company without real operational substance will be disregarded for New Zealand tax and treaty relief purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994 sections 143A and 143B; conviction may attract imprisonment and a fine. Corporate shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the evaded tax, plus repayment of primary tax and use-of-money interest. False declarations may also trigger Crimes Act 1961 charges.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate are personally liable for criminal fines and restitution. Courts may impose travel bans, disqualification from management, personal recovery orders and imprisonment. Under the Companies Act 1994, directors can be held jointly liable for debts arising from fraudulent trading.
Transfer Pricing and Profit-Shifting Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may reprice the transactions at arm's length under Subpart GC of the Income Tax Act 2007. Shortfall penalties range from 20% for lack of reasonable care to 40% for an unacceptable tax position, and up to 100% if the position was dominant or abusive.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved false transfer pricing documentation are exposed to penalty assessments and civil recovery. Participation in a profit-shifting arrangement can support a criminal fraud prosecution and personal liability for resulting tax shortfalls; travel restrictions and director prohibition orders may apply.
Shell Company or Artificial Conduit Abuse

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rule cancels the tax benefit and the company is not treated as a tax resident or beneficial owner for treaty purposes. Sanctions include denial of DTA relief, abusive-position penalty of 100% of the shortfall, and potential strike-off or liquidation action by the Registrar of Companies.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell used for tax avoidance may be ordered under the Companies Act 1994 to repay corporate debts as a civil remedy. Where the shell is used to facilitate money laundering or tax evasion, directors face criminal prosecution, asset freezing, travel bans and imprisonment.
AML and Trust/Cash Infraction

Statutory sanction: Breaches of the AML/CFT Act 2009 attract civil penalties up to NZD 4 million for a body corporate and NZD 400,000 for an individual. Money laundering offences under the Crimes Act 1961 carry imprisonment up to seven years, asset forfeiture and confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors are senior managers for AML/CFT purposes and may be personally prosecuted for failing to report suspicious transactions or knowingly directing structurd payments. The court may impose personal fines, disgorgement, travel bans and imprisonment for those acting as controlling minds of the offending.
