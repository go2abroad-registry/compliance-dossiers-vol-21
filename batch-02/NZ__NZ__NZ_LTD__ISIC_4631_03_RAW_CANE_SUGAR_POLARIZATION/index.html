Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Companies Act 1993. New Zealand law does not impose a minimum paid-up share capital and does not require a blocked or escrowed capital account for ordinary limited companies. The Company may adopt no-par-value shares and issue shares for cash or non-cash consideration determined by directors as fair. A blocked account is not required; however, commercial lenders may require cash margins or pledge arrangements for commodity-trade revolving letters of credit. Non-resident ownership is permitted, subject to any applicable overseas-investment screening thresholds under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 if the business later acquires sensitive assets or significant business assets.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) / Te Tari Taake; online portal: myIR

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity intending to carry on a taxable activity or make supplies in New Zealand must first determine registration obligations under the Companies Act 1993 (including foreign-company registration with the New Zealand Companies Office if carrying on business in New Zealand). To obtain a New Zealand IRD number, the entity must apply to the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) through the myIR online service or via an approved tax agent, providing legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, registered office address or New Zealand agent address, director and ultimate beneficial ownership information, and a description of intended New Zealand activities. IRD will issue a unique IRD number once identity and tax obligations are verified. A non-resident entity also registers for Goods and Services Tax (GST) under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 if its taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period or if it chooses to register voluntarily to claim GST input credits; non-residents making only zero-rated supplies may remain unregistered or register voluntarily depending on input credit requirements.

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box classification regime. 15%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods sold by a New Zealand GST-registered supplier and actually exported from New Zealand qualify for the 0% GST rate under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, provided the exporter exports the goods within 28 days after the time of supply (or an extended period approved by the Commissioner) and retains evidence of export. For qualifying cross-border services, zero-rating applies when the services are supplied to a non-resident outside New Zealand. Vessel-shipped raw cane sugar qualifies only if the supplier or its agent executes the physical export and retains satisfactory export documentation.

  • Customs export declaration lodged through the Trade Single Window (TSW) with the New Zealand Customs Service
  • Ocean bill of lading or sea waybill evidencing shipment from New Zealand
  • Foreign exchange repatriation evidence such as a SWIFT MT103 credit advice or bank statement showing inward overseas payment

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control. New Zealand supports voluntary Peppol-based e-invoicing. Tax invoices must comply with content requirements under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; GST returns are filed electronically through myIR. There is no anti-fraud digital stamping or government pre-clearance API requirement.

If export evidence is missing, false, or insufficient to establish physical removal of goods, the export zero-rating is denied, and the supplier becomes liable for GST at 15% on the consideration, plus use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties for evasion or lack of reasonable care under the Tax Administration Act 1994. IRD may retrospectively claw back input tax credits attributable to denied zero-rated supplies. Evasion-related penalties can reach 150% of the tax shortfall, with gross carelessness at 40% and abusive tax positions at 100%.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a freely floating and fully convertible currency under the exchange control supervision of the Reserve Bank of New Zealand. There are no surrender requirements on export receipts and no restrictions on the repatriation of capital, dividends, interest, or fee streams for domestic resident entities. Current account convertibility is unrestricted for legitimate trade and service purposes, with payments executed through registered banks under SWIFT protocols. Capital account flows, including foreign direct investment, equity injections, intra-group loans, and portfolio hedging operations, are also convertible without central bank license, provided transactions comply with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, the Sanctions Measures, and the Incorporated Societies Act. Transformation of NZD to USD, EUR, or CNY occurs at floating market rates determined by interbank supply and demand through licensed dealers. Hedging instruments such as forwards, swaps, and options are freely available for treasury management. The only practical hurdles to profit remittance are standard onboarding documentation, corresponding bank due diligence inquiries, and real-time transaction monitoring for sanctions and terrorist financing. No exchange quota, prior approval, or retention ceiling exists. Consequently, cross-border liquidity flows are governed by AML/CFT rather than by FX custody regulations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules in New Zealand. Exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in offshore or onshore designated foreign currency accounts, convert them to NZD at any time, or use them directly for trade settlements. No mandatory conversion ratio or statutory timeline applies; only standard reporting under local banking and AML/CFT frameworks is required.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating is limited for standalone offshore digital EMIs operating directly in New Zealand. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not maintain a distinct EMI license; any non-bank deposit-taking activity would fall under the Non-Bank Deposit Takers Act and require partnership with a licensed local bank for settlement and clearing. Pure offshore EMI wallets cannot offer domestic NZD account infrastructure and are restricted to sending and receiving remittances under New Zealand's AML/CFT regime without holding client funds. Therefore, such EMIs should be used only as supplemental payout channels, never as primary treasury rails. Onboarding viability is Low for standalone EMIs; Moderate when integrated through a licensed New Zealand bank's API or custodial ring-fenced structure.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory rate: 30% for non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders. Fully imputed dividends may be subject to 15% NRWT under the standardised company imputation rules; for treaty jurisdictions, the rate may be reduced to 10% or 15% under the relevant DTA. For NZ-Australia DTA, the dividend rate is 15% for portfolio shareholders and 5% for substantial (75% or more) shareholding, but for most other treaties, 15% portfolio / 5% direct substantial shareholding. A supplementary dividend may be paid by the company to gross up the net dividend to the treaty rate, but the supplementary dividend is itself subject to NRWT.

Box 1 (IR4): Income class codes Total assessable income derived during the income year, including gross sales/turnover, interest, dividends, and other income; requires allocation to the appropriate income class code (e.g., C01 for wholesale trade, C01A for sales of goods). Report non-resident-sourced income only if assessable in New Zealand; an NZ-registered company is tax resident and subject to worldwide income tax except where a DTA tie-breaker or exemption applies. Direct sales of VHP raw cane sugar shipped physically from New Zealand to foreign buyers may be attributable to a New Zealand PE if selling activities are carried on in New Zealand. Late filing of IR4 incurs a late filing penalty: $250 for small/small-extra companies, $500 for medium, $1,000 for large, plus an incremental late payment penalty of 1% per month on unpaid tax (up to 10% cap after 6 months), and an initial late payment penalty of 1% immediately, then 4% after 7 days and 1% per month thereafter; use-of-money interest (UOMI) is charged at the prescribed rates (currently ~8-10% per annum) on unpaid provisional and terminal tax.
Box 2 (IR4): Deductible expenditure Expenses incurred in deriving assessable income, including CIF/freight costs, insurance, demurrage, storage, weighing, refining/polarization testing, brokerage, finance charges, and general administrative expenses. Only deduct amounts to the extent they are incurred in deriving assessable income and are not capital in nature. Payments to associated non-resident entities must be arm's length and supported by contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation; dividends, notional interest, or excessive management fees are non-deductible or may be subject to thin capitalisation and interest apportionment rules. Late filing of IR4 incurs a late filing penalty: $250 for small/small-extra companies, $500 for medium, $1,000 for large, plus an incremental late payment penalty of 1% per month on unpaid tax (up to 10% cap after 6 months), and an initial late payment penalty of 1% immediately, then 4% after 7 days and 1% per month thereafter; use-of-money interest (UOMI) is charged at the prescribed rates (currently ~8-10% per annum) on unpaid provisional and terminal tax.
Box 3 (IR4): Net profit/(loss) before tax Taxable surplus after adjustments for non-deductible items and exempt income; reconciles accounting profit to taxable income via the tax reconciliation statement. Ensure adjustments for foreign exchange differences on LC and Swift Wire payment rails, deferred tax, and imputation credits are correctly recorded; non-resident shareholders should treat the NZ company as a resident taxpayer. Late filing of IR4 incurs a late filing penalty: $250 for small/small-extra companies, $500 for medium, $1,000 for large, plus an incremental late payment penalty of 1% per month on unpaid tax (up to 10% cap after 6 months), and an initial late payment penalty of 1% immediately, then 4% after 7 days and 1% per month thereafter; use-of-money interest (UOMI) is charged at the prescribed rates (currently ~8-10% per annum) on unpaid provisional and terminal tax.
Box 4 (IR4): Taxable income and tax payable Apply the 28% corporate tax rate to net taxable income before tax credits; calculate provisional tax and terminal tax obligations. No special rate for non-resident-owned companies; but if the company is treated as a controlled foreign corporation (CFC) by a foreign parent, the parent's jurisdiction may tax the NZ profits under CFC rules. NZ withholding obligations on distributions are separate from the IR4 liability. Late filing of IR4 incurs a late filing penalty: $250 for small/small-extra companies, $500 for medium, $1,000 for large, plus an incremental late payment penalty of 1% per month on unpaid tax (up to 10% cap after 6 months), and an initial late payment penalty of 1% immediately, then 4% after 7 days and 1% per month thereafter; use-of-money interest (UOMI) is charged at the prescribed rates (currently ~8-10% per annum) on unpaid provisional and terminal tax.
Box 5 (IR): Imputation credit account (ICA) and supplementary dividend Record imputation credits attached to dividends; if the company pays a supplementary dividend to a non-resident shareholder, the additional dividend and related non-resident withholding tax credit must be tracked. A NZ company paying a dividend to a non-resident shareholder must withhold NRWT (or RWT when imputation credits are attached) and may choose to pay a supplementary dividend to gross-up treaty rates; ICA balances must not go negative at year-end to avoid further income tax (ICA debit/credit balance rule). Late filing of IR4 incurs a late filing penalty: $250 for small/small-extra companies, $500 for medium, $1,000 for large, plus an incremental late payment penalty of 1% per month on unpaid tax (up to 10% cap after 6 months), and an initial late payment penalty of 1% immediately, then 4% after 7 days and 1% per month thereafter; use-of-money interest (UOMI) is charged at the prescribed rates (currently ~8-10% per annum) on unpaid provisional and terminal tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local file / master file threshold: NZ$10 million (approx. USD 6.5 million) of related-party transactions for full TP documentation. A TP report is required for significant cross-border related-party dealings; on request from IRD within 30 days, contemporaneous documentation may be demanded. Simplified documentation exists for low-value intercompany services.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as such do not apply because they are a UAE/OECD standard; however, New Zealand applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under sections BG 1 and GA 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, which voids arrangements whose dominant purpose is tax avoidance. NZ also applies anti-conduit rules and controlled foreign corporation rules, and the IRD can treat a company as having a permanent establishment in NZ if substantive functions are exercised in NZ. The company must have real economic substance (physical office, employees, commercial risks) to avoid sham/anti-avoidance recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Tax Administration Act 1994, sections 143A, 143B, 143C)

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction: maximum fine of $250,000 for a company plus up to $50,000 per individual officer; imprisonment of up to 5 years for individuals; mandatory payment of evaded tax plus UOMI and shortfall penalties (up to 150% of the tax shortfall for abusive tax avoidance). IRD may publicly name the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for assisting in tax evasion or being knowingly concerned in the offending; civil restitution orders may require payment of unpaid tax, penalties and costs; a court may prohibit departure (travel ban) from New Zealand due to unpaid tax under the Tax Administration Act 1994; imprisonment applies to individual wrongdoers.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: IRD can recharacterize the arrangement as void under GAAR; assess tax as if the substance were located in the home jurisdiction; issue penalty of 100-150% for statutory avoidance; deregistration of the company under the Companies Act 1993 if the Registrar determines it is no longer carrying on business; denial of imputation credits and treaty relief.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for debts if trading while insolvent or for breach of director duties (Companies Act 1993, sections 135, 136, 137); they could be subject to disqualification (up to 10 years) and civil penalties; travel restrictions may apply for unpaid assessments.
Transfer Pricing / Thin Capitalisation Mismatch

Statutory sanction: IRD adjusts taxable income to the arm's-length amount; shortfall penalty up to 100% of the resulting tax shortfall if the taxpayer did not take reasonable care; interest and late payment penalties accrue; formal TP documentation failure carries a specific penalty (for failure to keep and preserve records) of $25,000 for a company.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign inaccurate TP documentation or issue false intercompany invoices may be liable as parties to the penalty; civil liability for the tax shortfall if they are alter ego; travel bans are possible after a tax liability is assessed and remains unpaid.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML/CFT Act 2009) and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to $2 million per contravention for a company (higher for criminal cases with imprisonment of up to 2 years); IRD's information-sharing (WHT) and Customs confiscation of cargo if the sugar is tainted by an overseas crime; a conviction can result in disqualification of directors for up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers can be individually fined up to $200,000 per contravention and may be jailed for up to 2 years if they knowingly conduct business with tainted funds; asset forfeiture order may apply; travel bans may be imposed to secure the presence of the accused.
False Invoicing / Trade-Based Money Laundering (TBML)

Statutory sanction: Fraud-related offenses: Crimes Act 1961, imprisonment up to 7 years; Customs export/import penalties; IRD penalty up to 150%; banks will freeze funds and may report to FIU; the company may be blacklisted for trade finance facilities.

Director personal liability: Directors subscribing to false documents face personal criminal liability under s 228 and s 229 of the Crimes Act; civil forfeiture of proceeds of crime; travel ban and passport surrender can be imposed by court order; compensation orders to victims.
