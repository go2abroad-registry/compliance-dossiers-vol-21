Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office in New Zealand will satisfy the Companies Office registered office requirement only if it is a genuine physical street address in New Zealand, staffed and capable of receiving official documents and service of process, and is not merely a post-office box, a document exchange number, or a bare mail-forwarding mailbox. Inland Revenue and New Zealand banks will usually reject a bare commercial mailbox as the company's principal place of business because the AML/CFT business risk assessment requires a physical premises, accessible records, and a substantive management presence. For the outsourced virtual CFO advisory industry, the appropriate structure is a registered office service provider that offers a leased desk or serviced office, a physical New Zealand street address, mail scanning, meeting rooms, and a responsible local agent, combined with documented commercial substance such as a service agreement, a New Zealand resident director or alternate director, local tax registration, and a real principal place of business. Use of a pure virtual address without staff or lease is likely to be rejected by banks for business account opening, may be challenged by Inland Revenue under residence and permanent establishment rules, and could undermine the AML/CFT risk rating. Best practice is to maintain a virtual office only as an overlay to a formal serviced office or executive centre with a local physical presence and full records access.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; online electronic portal is myIR at ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must first be established or recognised at the New Zealand Companies Office, then apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number via the myIR portal. If the entity is not incorporated in New Zealand, it must provide to the IRD certified evidence of foreign incorporation, the constitutional documents and memorandum of association, a completed non-resident company registration application as prescribed by the IRD, the names and tax residence details of all directors and ultimate beneficial owners, and a statement of intended business activities in New Zealand. Upon verification the IRD issues an IRD number which serves as the master tax file number for income tax, GST, PAYE, and other New Zealand tax obligations. The same IRD number is used as the GST registration identifier when the entity registers for GST, whether compulsorily upon breaching the taxable supply threshold or voluntarily.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Available election forms

A New Zealand limited company may make a Look-Through Company (LTC) election under the Income Tax Act 2007 to be treated as a transparent entity for income tax purposes, subject to eligibility requirements including a maximum of five shareholders. A GST grouping election is also available under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 to tax a group of related companies as a single registered person. No check-the-box election equivalent to United States Subchapter S exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 annual taxable supplies; registration is mandatory once turnover exceeds NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, and is available voluntarily to a business carrying on a taxable activity

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, a supply is zero-rated if the goods are exported from New Zealand within the statutory timeframe and the exporter holds sufficient evidence of export as required under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. For services, zero-rating applies under section 11A when the services are supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed, and the services are not supplied to a person in New Zealand or the appointment is not related to a registered recipient's business in New Zealand. Offshore digital services, including virtual CFO and bookkeeping services, qualify for zero-rating if the recipient is a non-resident and received the services while outside New Zealand.

  • Customs export entry lodged electronically and approved by the New Zealand Customs Service
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and contract or purchase order identifying the overseas buyer or non-resident recipient
  • Bank records or SWIFT MT103 payment confirmation showing foreign exchange receipt from outside New Zealand
  • Completed GST export evidence checklist if requested by the Inland Revenue Department

New Zealand does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital tax stamping regime. E-invoicing using the Peppol BIS 3.0 format is available on a voluntary basis through the IRD-approved network. Taxpayers must retain GST invoices and business records in accordance with the Tax Administration Act 1994, and the IRD may require electronic data exchange in audits, but there is no API-based pre-clearance of invoices.

If the IRD determines that zero-rating was applied to a supply that did not meet the statutory export or offshore supply criteria, the entity is liable for the unpaid GST at 15%, plus use-of-money interest. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply for incorrect or abusive tax positions: up to 20% for a lack of reasonable care, up to 40% for gross carelessness, up to 100% for an abusive tax position, and up to 150% for evasion or fraudulent intent. Willful or fraudulent claims may also lead to criminal prosecution, fines, and cancellation of GST registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully open and convertible currency regime. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) floats freely on global markets with no administrative exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no mandatory central bank approval for cross-border flows. Both current account transactions (trade in services, consultancy fees, interest, dividends) and capital account transactions (foreign direct investment, equity injection, intercompany loans) are liberalised under the OECD Liberalisation Code. There are no restrictions on the repatriation of profits or capital for a standard NZ Ltd, provided the company is not in a sensitive industry subject to the Overseas Investment Act. The only friction points are prudent compliance: banks require a source-of-funds narrative for large inbound transfers, and outbound dividend payments must be supported by board minutes, solvency certification, and an available earnings calculation under the Companies Act. Tax clearance from Inland Revenue may be requested for high-value distributions to non-resident shareholders to ensure the appropriate non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) is deducted at the rate under the applicable double tax agreement. For this fractional CFO advisory business, all service fees earned in foreign currency can be brought onshore without conversion constraints, and the treasury team may retain appropriate working capital in offshore settlement accounts without triggering regulatory penalties. The absence of surrender rules means no forced conversion into NZD, which is ideal for a multicurrency ledger business serving clients across Asia and the Pacific.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules exist. Exporters of services or goods are not required to convert foreign exchange earnings into NZD nor to repatriate them within any specific timeline. Companies may hold proceeds in approved foreign currencies in their domestic bank accounts or offshore accounts, subject to the entity's ordinary financial reporting and AML/CFT obligations.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High viability for payment collection and disbursement using Wise and Stripe as licensed financial service providers in New Zealand, but these rails cannot replace a regulated tier-1 bank account for statutory capital escrow, certified treasury operations, or regulatory custody. Non-bank EMIs are not subject to the Reserve Bank's prudential regulation as deposit takers and are legally barred from accepting statutory capital deposits or acting as the sole account for entity formation. They may be used as a settlement convenience for international consulting fees, provided the underlying contracts clearly map every inflow to an invoice and the company's NZBN is included on all payment references. Banks will require a reconciliation of all Wise/Stripe transactions to the tier-1 bank ledger, and a legal opinion may be requested if the EMI’s beneficial ownership structure is not transparent. Thus the domestic tier-1 bank remains the treasury anchor; the EMI rail is a compliant add-on rather than a substitute.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity injections must be wired via SWIFT MT103 quoting 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' in the purpose field and referencing the company's NZBN and incorporation number. A board resolution authorising the share issue and a signed shareholders' report must be delivered to the bank on request. Funds are first credited to the Capital Escrow Account and remain quarantined until the company files the share allotment notice with the Companies Office and obtains a tax-payer identification from Inland Revenue. For service fee income rooted in a recurring client contract, the wire should reference the invoice number and include a direct link to the engagement agreement to ensure the bank does not categorise the inflow as capital. No Reserve Bank approval is required, but the bank may require a bank statement from the remitting institution and a declaration of the source of the funds if the amount exceeds NZD 10,000.Trade settlements for the virtual CFO advisory business are executed via SWIFT MT103 for standard vendor payments and supplier fees, with purpose codes such as 'PAYMENT FOR SERVICES'. For high-value or contractually sensitive disbursements, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700) may be issued by the NZ corporate bank in favour of the beneficiary, or alternatively a documentary collection (D/P or D/A) may be used to mitigate settlement and freight risk. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Financial Reporting Act 2013 (New Zealand), a company is legally required to prepare financial statements and obtain a qualified certified auditor's report if it is a 'large' company. A company is 'large' if it triggers any two of the following three quantitative thresholds for each of the two immediately preceding accounting periods: (1) total assets exceed NZD 60 million (approximately USD 37.2 million); (2) total annual revenue exceeds NZD 30 million (approximately USD 18.6 million); (3) 100 or more full-time equivalent employees. Furthermore, any company that carries on a 'financial markets' activity or is otherwise a 'FMC reporting entity' under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 is compulsory audited regardless of size. For a private outsourcing company providing virtual CFO services, the default audit exemption applies unless the company is large or a shareholder or the Registrar demands an audit under section 207 of the Companies Act 1993.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZD 1 million in the income year. Master File mandatory where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZD 10 million in the income year. Safe harbour: cross-border related-party transactions of NZD 1 million or less do not require formal transfer pricing documentation. Low-value intra-group services may be remunerated under the simplified OECD approach. Documentation must be produced to IRD within 30 days of request.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply Gulf-format Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Instead, the anti-avoidance framework comprises: (i) the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1 and s GA 1 Income Tax Act 2007), which disregards tax avoidance arrangements lacking commercial substance; (ii) OECD/G20 BEPS Action 6 Principal Purpose Test in New Zealand's tax treaties, which denies treaty benefits where obtaining the benefit is a principal purpose; and (iii) substance-over-form and sham doctrines applied by IRD. Indicators of genuine economic substance for the NZ Ltd include: a physical office in New Zealand, substantive board and management decision-making in New Zealand, local employees providing the virtual CFO services, written service contracts, contemporaneous invoicing, and settlement through New Zealand bank and payment processor accounts (Stripe and Wise). A bare parent or conduit company with no employees, no office, and no operational control will be recharacterised and all income attributed to the ultimate beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under s 143B Tax Administration Act 1994: fine up to NZD 50,000, plus up to three times the tax shortfall, and imprisonment for up to 5 years; civil shortfall penalty of 100% of the tax shortfall (increased by 50% to 150% for obstruction of investigation); use of money interest accrues from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, consent to, or knowingly participate are personally liable for the company's tax shortfall via the directors' liability regime (ss 155A-156 TAA); director disqualification under ss 283-284 Companies Act 1993; departure prohibition orders enabling IRD to impose travel bans; property freezing and asset preservation orders; extradition proceedings for serious fraud; civil restitution to the Crown in insolvency.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Shell Company Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing recharacterisation and adjustment under ss GC 13-15 Income Tax Act 2007; shortfall penalty of 20% (unacceptable tax position), 40% (gross carelessness), 50% (abusive tax position), or 100% (evasion); documentation penalties of NZD 20,000 for failure to maintain or provide the Master File or Local File; denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot shelter behind the corporate veil where the arrangement is a sham or mere facade; personal liability for adjusted tax, penalties, and interest; clawback of fees received in insolvency; criminal liability for fraudulent transfer pricing documentation; travel bans and seizure of personal assets.
AML/CFT Reporting Entity Breach

Statutory sanction: Civil pecuniary penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009: up to NZD 2,000,000 for a company and NZD 300,000 for an individual director for serious non-compliance; criminal money laundering offences under s 243 Crimes Act 1961: imprisonment up to 7 years and/or a fine up to NZD 300,000; suspension or deregistration of the reporting entity; DIA compliance orders with audit costs.

Director personal liability: Directors must ensure the reporting entity implements an effective risk assessment and compliance programme; personal penalties of up to NZD 300,000; imprisonment for knowingly permitting client money laundering; disqualification from providing financial or accounting services; extradition and travel bans for cross-border laundering.
NRWT Repatriation and Unauthorised Treaty Relief

Statutory sanction: Personal liability of the company for the full 30% NRWT not withheld; use of money interest from the dividend payment date; late payment penalties of 1% plus 2% monthly; shortfall penalties of 20% to 100% for deliberate avoidance; denial of the dividend deduction in the shareholder's jurisdiction; reassessment of the distribution as a taxable dividend with penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise an unlawful distribution without NRWT are jointly and severally liable under the TAA directors' liability regime; recovery action against personal assets; disqualification for breach of Companies Act 1993 dividend provisions; criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion by the non-resident owner.
Phoenix Company, Reckless Trading, and Residual Tax Debt

Statutory sanction: Personal liability order against directors for unpaid PAYE, GST, FBT, and RWT under ss 155A-156 TAA; illegal dividends void and recoverable; voidable transactions clawed back by the liquidator; penalties of 100% of the shortfall; use of money interest; permanent director disqualification under the Companies Act 1993; Companies Office prohibition on managing companies.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil restitution to the Commissioner for the entire unpaid tax, plus penalties and interest; reckless trading liability under s 135 Companies Act 1993 for losses to creditors; travel bans through departure prohibition orders until the tax debt is secured; imprisonment for contempt where asset stripping is designed to defeat the Commissioner.
