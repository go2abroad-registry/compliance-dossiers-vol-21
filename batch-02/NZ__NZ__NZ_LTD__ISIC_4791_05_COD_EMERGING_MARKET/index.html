A virtual office is permissible only as a physical registered office address where staff accept service of documents, and only if it is a real street address in New Zealand, not a PO Box. Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), using the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) online registration portal and the Register of Companies.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No 105), particularly sections 12 to 20 (registration of companies), section 10 (company name requirements), sections 42 to 46 (constitution and incorporation of constitution), sections 52 to 58 (shares and share capital), section 66 ( considerations for issue of shares), sections 122 to 134 (duties of directors), and sections 151 to 157 (ceasing to hold office and disqualification), together with the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and the Companies (Fees) Regulations 2012.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form 1: Incorporation Document under section 12 of the Companies Act 1993, accompanied by the relevant consent to act forms and an integrated New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) application.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand company law imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement, no authorized capital ceiling, and no blocked escrow account for paid-up capital. Shares may be issued for nominal or non-cash consideration provided the board determines solvency and complies with sections 52 to 58 and section 66 of the Companies Act 1993. Cash-on-delivery e-commerce receivables must be recorded in accordance with applicable financial reporting standards, but no statutory escrow of issued capital is required.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable at industrial scale. The company must nevertheless ensure that any ancillary office, packing room, or fulfilment corner maintains a valid Building Warrant of Fitness if required by the territorial authority, installs fire detection and extinguishing systems, and has a fire evacuation scheme under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017. If the company directly employs workers, it must comply with the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including workplace hazard assessment, ergonomic controls, first-aid provision, and PCBUs health monitoring obligations. A Civil Defense clearance is not necessary for digital retail activity, but the registered office and logistics partners should maintain emergency continuity arrangements for physical parcels and cash remittances.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) / Te Tari Taake, New Zealand; online portal: myIR Secure Online Services. Company registration and NZBN issuance are administered by the New Zealand Companies Office; export declarations are administered by the New Zealand Customs Service (Te Mana Ārai o Aotearoa).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing NZ_LTD must first incorporate the New Zealand limited company with the Companies Office and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). The company must then apply for an IRD number through Inland Revenue by submitting the prescribed IRD number application for a company, either electronically via myIR or in the authorised paper format. Supporting documentation includes the certificate of incorporation, constitution, directors and shareholders registers, registered office address, and proof of identity and foreign tax identification numbers for all non-resident directors and beneficial owners. Once the IRD number is issued, it becomes the statutory TIN for income tax, GST, and all other Inland Revenue-administered obligations. If the company expects to make taxable supplies above NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, it must simultaneously register for GST; voluntary GST registration is also available if the company carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in New Zealand. A closely held eligible company may elect look-through company (LTC) status under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, which shifts tax liabilities and losses to owners, but the default statutory classification for NZ_LTD is entity-level corporate taxation. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are addressed under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of goods or remote services to New Zealand consumers must register at the same mandatory threshold, while voluntary registration is permitted when a taxable activity is carried on in New Zealand.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under section 11(1)(a) and (c) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for exported goods, including mail-order retail goods, where the supplier physically exports the goods within 28 days of the time of supply and retains reliable documentary evidence of export. Remote and digital services supplied to non-resident recipients may be zero-rated where the recipient is non-resident and the effective use and enjoyment occurs outside New Zealand. In the Cash-On-Delivery E-commerce model, every cross-border parcel must be shown to have physically departed New Zealand and to have been delivered as a COD shipment; collection of payment through logistics or payment-gateway partners does not by itself prevent zero-rating where the export evidence is complete.

  • New Zealand Customs export declaration lodged through the New Zealand Trade Single Window and approved by the New Zealand Customs Service
  • Commercial invoice showing itemised sale value, currency, shipping terms, and a statement that the goods are exported from New Zealand
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or logistics carrier manifest/consignment note evidencing physical departure from a New Zealand port or airport
  • Cash-on-delivery collection record from the logistics partner or payment gateway showing the foreign buyer's payment and settlement to the New Zealand seller
  • Foreign exchange settlement or repatriation evidence: bank credit advice, SWIFT MT103, or payment-service-provider settlement statement identifying the cross-border sale proceeds credited to the New Zealand entity's account

New Zealand does not operate a continuous-transaction-control (CTC) clearance regime, mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping. Tax invoices are self-issued: for every taxable supply exceeding NZD 50, the supplier must issue and retain a tax invoice containing the prescribed particulars under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR Secure Online Services, with filing frequencies and due dates calculated by reference to the registered GST period. PEPPOL-based e-invoicing is voluntary and supported by Inland Revenue, but is not a mandatory clearance channel.

Inland Revenue may disallow zero-rating and assess 15% GST on the full consideration, together with use-of-money interest from the date the GST return should have been filed, if the taxpayer fails to hold sufficient section 11 export evidence within the seven-year statutory record retention period. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply on a graduated basis: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 20% for taking an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, and 150% for evasion or deliberate misrepresentation. Fraudulent export documentation also triggers criminal and customs penalties under the Customs and Excise Act 2018. New Zealand imposes no separate statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for GST zero-rating, but where sale proceeds cannot be traced or are not repatriated at all, the claimed export may be recharacterised as a domestic taxable supply; therefore, robust internal controls should document repatriation within 180 days as a prudent governance standard.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a predominantly open capital account with no comprehensive foreign exchange control legislation. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not restrict the convertibility of the New Zealand dollar for current account transactions, including trade settlement, dividends, interest, or royalties, nor does it impose mandatory surrender requirements on export proceeds. Capital account transactions, such as inbound foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, or intercompany loans, are generally permitted but may be subject to screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 when the transaction involves significant business assets, sensitive land, or fishing quota. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal, provided that corporate governance documentation, tax residency certificates, and audited financial statements substantiate the distribution source. The key practical constraints are not legal convertibility barriers but bank internal compliance requirements, including source-of-funds declarations, beneficial ownership transparency, and verification of economic substance. Entities with cash-on-delivery e-commerce operations must additionally demonstrate that digital settlement platforms are integrated with auditable transaction trails to satisfy New Zealand Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 obligations, which apply to banks and payment service providers. Overall, the currency regime is liberal, but enhanced scrutiny applies to high-volume cross-border cash flows from emerging markets where the residual risk of layered laundering exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory statutory timeline or conversion ratio applies to export foreign currency receipts under New Zealand law. Exporters may hold foreign currency in dedicated offshore or onshore accounts, settle in trade invoices, or convert at commercially agreed rates through a registered New Zealand bank. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand requires reporting only for balance-of-payments statistical purposes, not approval or surrender. Banking counterparties may impose internal policies requiring timely repatriation of excess foreign currency holdings to the base operating account for liquidity management, but this is a commercial covenant, not a regulatory rule.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Wise, or licensed neobanking platforms can support cross-border payment technology, virtual IBANs, and trade settlement execution, but they are not a substitute for a primary corporate bank account in New Zealand. New Zealand lacks a domestic EMI license category analogous to the UK or EU; providers operate as registered financial service providers with limited prudential regulation, often partnering with a tier-1 bank for settlement. For a New Zealand limited company, it remains legally viable to use offshore EMIs for payments from supported corridors, but the statutory AML/CFT obligation and capital escrow functions should sit with a Reserve Bank of New Zealand registered bank. The viability rating is Medium-High for payment orchestration, but Low for statutory treasury custody due to the absence of local deposit-taking authorisation and the inability of EMIs to independently hold New Zealand dollar deposits without a banking partner. Therefore, the recommended architecture is a domestic bank anchor account with a technology layer feeding from selected EMIs for cash-on-delivery logistics remittance.

Annually by the company's annual return date, being the anniversary of the date of incorporation. Filing may be submitted at any time up to the due date, with the Companies Office reminding the registered office one month prior.

Statutory government annual fee

$28.68 USD

Late filing penalties

If the annual return and the NZD 47.80 government filing fee (converted to USD at a prevailing rate of 0.60) are not received by the due date, the company is recorded as a defaulting company on the public register. A late filing penalty of NZD 52.80 applies. If the company remains in arrears for two consecutive annual return cycles, the Registrar may issue a notice of intended removal and, after 20 working days, strike the company off the register under section 318 of the Companies Act 1993, extinguishing its legal capacity, with assets forfeiting to the Crown.

Turnover: An independent certified audit is legally mandatory for: (i) a "large company" as defined in section 45(1) of the Companies Act 1993 - i.e., at the end of each of the two preceding accounting periods, the company meets any two of the following thresholds: total assets exceeding NZD 60 million, total revenue exceeding NZD 30 million, or 100 or more full-time equivalent employees; (ii) any FMC reporting entity under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, irrespective of size (e.g., quoted securities issuer, managed investment scheme, or licensed market service provider); (iii) any company whose own constitution or shareholder agreement contracts an audit, or where shareholders holding at least 5% of voting rights validly request an audit under section 199 of the Companies Act 1993. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The New Zealand statutory non-resident withholding tax rate on dividends is 30% for non-imputed dividends and 15% for fully imputed dividends. A double tax agreement may reduce the rate to as low as 5% to 15% for a qualifying resident shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand does not statutorily require Local File or Master File documentation. Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding NZD 1,100,000,000 (equivalent to EUR 750,000,000). For all other related-party cross-border transactions, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is strongly recommended when the annual value of connected transactions exceeds NZD 1,000,000, as the documentation protects against subsequent shortfall penalties.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain offshore Economic Substance Regulations as found in certain financial centres. However, the general anti-avoidance rule under sections BG 1 and GA 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, together with anti-treaty-shopping jurisprudence, requires that a non-resident-owned company carrying on e-commerce, logistics, and cash-collection functions in New Zealand demonstrate real economic presence, local decision-making, arm's length staffing, and substantive operational control. A shell company with no local substance will be disregarded for tax purposes and profits reattributed to the actual economic owners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through COD Revenue Suppression

Statutory sanction: A shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax, plus criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994 with a fine of up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment of up to three years for knowingly evading or defeating a tax liability; all unpaid tax remains collectible with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause or permit revenue suppression are jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax shortfall if they personally participated in the evasion. They may also be prohibited from managing a company under the Companies Act 1993, subjected to travel bans during active tax fraud investigations, and imprisoned for criminal tax evasion.
Shell Company and Artificial Substance Absence

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may apply the substantive anti-avoidance provisions to strike through the shell, reassess the true resident entity, deny treaty benefits, allocate income on an arm's length basis, and impose shortfall penalties of up to 150% for evasion or 40% for taking an unacceptable tax position.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for debts incurred through reckless trading under the Companies Act 1993, including tax liabilities. Beneficial owners relying on nominee directors risk having the corporate veil pierced, with civil recovery powers, asset freezing orders, and in serious cases criminal charges for abusive tax structuring.
Disguised Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The deduction is disallowed, the payment is recharacterized as a dividend or non-deductible deemed distribution, NRWT is assessed on the gross amount, and a shortfall penalty of 20% to 40% applies. Wilful arrangements attract the 150% evasion penalty and additional civil interest from the original date of filing.

Director personal liability: Directors and group finance officers who sign withholding declarations are personally liable for unpaid NRWT obligations under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Travel restrictions and departure prohibition orders can be imposed where the non-resident controlling shareholder has stripped assets while related-party liabilities remain outstanding.
AML and Cash Logistic Red Flag

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a company may receive penalties up to NZD 2,000,000 for serious AML compliance failures; involved individuals are liable for fines of up to NZD 300,000 and imprisonment of up to 12 months for knowingly facilitating money laundering. Associated criminal proceeds may be confiscated under the Criminal Proceeds Recovery Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for wilful blindness in cash logistics operations and can be named in criminal proceeds recovery proceedings. The court may impose management bans, issue travel bans pending forfeiture orders, and make personal restitution orders requiring the director to restore misappropriated or laundered value to the company or the Crown.
