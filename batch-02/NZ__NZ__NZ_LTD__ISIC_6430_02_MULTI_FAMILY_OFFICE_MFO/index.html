Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office or commercial mailbox addresses are generally not permissible for a multi-family office (MFO) in New Zealand. The AML/CFT Act requires reporting institutions to have a registered presence and to maintain records at a physical address. The Inland Revenue Department and the Companies Office require a physical registered office address that is not a PO Box or virtual office. Banks and financial institutions will reject a virtual office address for account opening and will require a physical business address for due diligence purposes. Therefore, a physical office in New Zealand is mandatory for this industry.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Companies, New Zealand Companies Office, Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), operating through the New Zealand Companies Register online portal.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), in particular sections 12-23 (incorporation, constitution and certificate of incorporation), sections 24-36 (share capital and issue of shares), sections 151-153 (appointment of directors), section 189 (registered office), sections 191-201 (address for service and registers), and section 362 (rights of appeal). The Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 govern the licensing of the multi-family office operations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Online Incorporation Application via the New Zealand Companies Register (the electronic equivalent of the statutory incorporation application and Form 2 shareholders and directors consents), together with the proposed constitution, consent to appointment of directors, consent to shareholding, and registered office details.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up share capital, no minimum issued share value, and no escrow or blocked capital account requirement. The company must have at least one shareholder and one issued share, and each first director must certify that the company will be able to pay its debts as they become due in the ordinary course of business. Separately, an FMA-licensed Discretionary Investment Management Service provider must demonstrate adequate financial resources and operational capacity to the satisfaction of the Financial Markets Authority.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for civil defense fire permits and industrial structural load certification because no industrial premises or hazardous goods storage will be operated. The company must nevertheless comply with general workplace law, maintain a safe office under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, hold a current building warrant of fitness for any leased premises under the Building Act 2004, and observe Fire and Emergency New Zealand egress, alarm, and evacuation requirements for its professional office.

Paid-in capital rule: None. New Zealand law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up share capital, no minimum issued share value, and no escrow or blocked capital account requirement. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department of New Zealand (Inland Revenue, IRD); online electronic portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir). Company registration is administered by the New Zealand Companies Office (https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz).

E-government filing portal

A New Zealand incorporated limited company is formed under the Companies Act 1993 via the New Zealand Companies Office. On incorporation, the Companies Office electronically transmits the company particulars to Inland Revenue, which automatically allocates a New Zealand IRD number, the statutory tax identification number for income tax, GST, employer obligations and other tax purposes. The IRD number is made available to the company through the Companies Office registry and myIR. A non-resident company not incorporated in New Zealand but carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand must separately apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number through the non-resident company registration process in myIR and must provide certified certificate of incorporation from its home jurisdiction, constitutional documents and ultimate beneficial ownership details. The IRD number is required before any GST registration can be activated and must be quoted on all tax returns and correspondence with Inland Revenue.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available. A closely held eligible New Zealand company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) treatment under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, causing income and expenses to be attributed to shareholders; however, the LTC election is generally unavailable to a multi-family office owned by corporate trustees or with more than five shareholders and is therefore not assumed. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 apply and are not elective; there is no hybrid mismatch check-the-box election in New Zealand.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies (excluding GST) supplied in New Zealand in any 12-month period under section 51 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Exempt financial supplies, including many discretionary portfolio management services supplied by a multi-family office, are not taxable supplies counted toward the threshold, although voluntary registration is available. Non-resident suppliers of remote services into New Zealand must register for GST when their New Zealand remote-services turnover exceeds NZD 60,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, exports of goods may be zero-rated where ownership in the goods is transferred before or at the time of removal from New Zealand and the supplier retains evidence that the goods were exported from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply. Services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of performance are zero-rated where the services are not supplied directly in connection with land or movable property in New Zealand and are not otherwise treated as supplied in New Zealand. For a multi-family office, the supply of discretionary portfolio advisory services to a non-resident client must first be classified: if the services constitute GST-exempt financial services under section 3 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, no GST is charged and there is no zero-rating claim; if the services are taxable, the export service criteria in section 11 must be evidenced by the client's non-resident status, the performance out of New Zealand or receipt outside New Zealand, and absence of any direct connection to New Zealand property. Refunds of input tax attributable to exported taxable supplies are claimed through the normal GST return; overpayments are refundable by Inland Revenue, but the zero-rating evidence remains subject to audit.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Export Entry (electronic declaration filed through the Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and export-related carriage documents
  • Bank foreign exchange credit advice or inward remittance advice showing the export goods or services proceeds credited to the New Zealand company's bank account

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance, API stamping or anti-fraud digital stamping regime exists in New Zealand. GST invoices are required for taxable supplies of NZD 50 or more and must contain the prescribed particulars in section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and operates under the New Zealand National E-Invoicing Framework using the Peppol network; GST returns are filed electronically through myIR. Records must be retained for at least 7 years and made available to Inland Revenue on request.

Inland Revenue may scrutinise export zero-rating claims during GST audits and refund reviews. If the supplier cannot substantiate the export within 28 days for goods or cannot prove the non-resident, offshore supply criteria for services, the supply is recharacterised as taxable at 15% and the resulting GST shortfall is assessed with use-of-money interest and late payment penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Deliberate or fraudulent misstatements attract shortfall penalties under sections 141A to 141D of the Tax Administration Act 1994, including up to 150% of the tax shortfall in the case of evasion and 100% for an unacceptable tax position taken without reasonable care, and may give rise to criminal liability. Record-keeping for the substantiation documents is mandatory for at least 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains an open, freely convertible current and capital account regime. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand is responsible for currency issuance and prudential regulation but does not impose exchange controls, licensing for inward/outward remittances, or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. Both residents and non-residents may transact in NZD and foreign currencies through authorised banks. The Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 focus on conduct and verification rather than quantitative foreign exchange restrictions. Capital injections and profit repatriation require appropriate underlying documentation, including shareholder resolutions, trust distributions, audited financial statements, and standard bank customer due diligence. There are no surrender requirements nor forced conversion ratios. Certain real-property and significant-business acquisition transactions by overseas persons may require screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005, but that is not a foreign exchange restriction. Dividend, interest, service fee, and liquidation distributions can generally be remitted at bank buying and selling rates; banking counterparties may require a tax confirmation such as a New Zealand IRD certificate or certificate of residence. Overall, New Zealand is a high-convertibility, low-friction jurisdiction for treasury operations, with market-based pricing and deep NZD/USD, NZD/EUR, and NZD/CNH liquidity through the domestic interbank market. The key practical friction is not foreign exchange control but KYC/AML evidence and business substance verification for a high-risk multi-family office.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists under New Zealand law. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in approved overseas accounts or convert freely. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not prescribe timelines or conversion ratios. Reporting obligations are limited to AML/CFT transaction records, tax declarations, and OECD country-by-country reporting where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMI cross-border viability is legally constrained. New Zealand does not have a dedicated EMI licensing framework for standalone e-money institutions; non-bank payment service providers must register on the Financial Service Providers Register and comply with the AML/CFT Act, but they cannot take deposits or participate directly in the Exchange Settlement Account System. Such providers typically act as payment facilitators and require a sponsor bank for settlement. For a licensed multi-family office requiring custody-grade cash management, segregated client funds, and high-value cross-border wires, pure fintech EMIs rank Low in viability. A reputable Tier-1 bank is required for capital subscriptions, fiduciary holdings, and trust disbursements. Fintechs can supplement expense payments and FX comparison but cannot replace domestic clearing rails or provide legal banking credit and custody services.

Defense evidence: For each funding source, provide bank statements and transaction narratives from the originator banks; incorporation and tax records of underlying companies; audited financial statements; IRD or tax compliance certificates; traceability reports for digital assets; and an independent source-of-wealth report prepared by a certified accountant or forensic firm.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity capital injection into the NZ Ltd, route SWIFT MT103 from the ultimate parent or beneficial owner's bank to the company's designated NZD account at a New Zealand Tier-1 bank. Use Field 50K to show the actual beneficial remitter, Field 59 for the NZ Ltd beneficiary with the account number, and Field 70 with the exact purpose: SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION - NZD or DIRECTOR'S LOAN - NZD. New Zealand does not require resident banks to obtain Reserve Bank approval for inward remittances, but the receiving bank will perform sanctions and source-of-funds screening and may request a shareholder resolution allotting shares, a statutory declaration, and a board resolution confirming the capital injection. After receipt, prepare a capital contribution verification file that includes the bank confirmation, FX dealing ticket if any currency conversion occurred, board minute, and the share allocation filing on the New Zealand Companies Register. If the equity owner is an overseas person acquiring sensitive land or media assets, the Overseas Investment Act screening must be assessed before completion; otherwise no foreign exchange consent is required. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions in an income year triggers mandatory preparation of both Master File and Local File (New Zealand TP rules effective 1 July 2022). A Country-by-Country Report is required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds NZD 1,150,000,000. Documentation must be prepared by the IR4 filing deadline and provided to Inland Revenue within 30 days of request.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code. Instead, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under s BG 1 Income Tax Act 2007, the business continuity test for loss carryforwards, and the 'central management and control' residence test require an MFO Ltd to possess genuine commercial substance in New Zealand. A land-owning shell company with no directors, employees, or meetings in NZ risks being treated as non-resident (exposing its foreign income to NZ tax) or having its cross-border transactions recharacterised at fair market value. Inland Revenue applies OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and may impose shortfall penalties of 100% of the recharacterised tax adjustment (150% for abusive positions) if transactions lack commercial rationality.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Under s 143A and s 143B Tax Administration Act 1994: civil shortfall penalty up to 150% of the evaded tax. Criminal prosecution: imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to NZ$50,000 per offence (s 108 Crimes Act 1961 for forgery if applicable).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the company's tax debts arising from evasion under s 139 and s 140 Tax Administration Act 1994; reckless or fraudulent decisions can trigger director penalty notices, disqualification under the Companies Act 1993, and criminal liability for aiding or abetting tax evasion (s 143 D and 148).
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue will recharacterise the arrangement under the GAAR (s BG 1); the tax shortfall is assessed at 28% on all worldwide income if the company is held non-resident, plus a shortfall penalty of 100% (150% for abusive tax position). Groundless DTA claims attract criminal fines under s 143A.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign declarations of residence or treaty eligibility without factual substance face personal liability for penalty tax and interest; they may be prosecuted for 'taking an abusive tax position' under s 141D, leading to imprisonment up to 5 years.
AML/CFT Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to NZ$2,000,000 per offence for corporate entities; non-compliance with an audit or enforcement order attracts criminal fines up to NZ$10,000,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years for responsible officers.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and directors are criminally liable for 'wilful blindness' to money laundering red flags; they face personal fines up to NZ$300,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years under s 90 of the AML/CFT Act. They can also be sued for civil compensation by affected clients.
NRWT and Dividend Withholding Evasion

Statutory sanction: The company is assessed for unreported NRWT plus use-of-money interest. A shortfall penalty of 100% of the NRWT not withheld (150% for intentional evasion) applies. Directors who knowingly caused the non-payment are personally liable for the full NRWT amount under the director penalty regime.

Director personal liability: Directors may be issued a personal notice to pay unpaid NRWT; failure to rectify within 30 days makes them personally liable for the entire withheld amount, and further prosecution under s 143 may result in imprisonment up to 5 years.
