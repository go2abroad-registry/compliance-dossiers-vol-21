Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered office in New Zealand is a statutory requirement under the Companies Act 1993. A virtual office or commercial mailbox may satisfy the bare registered-address rule if it is a physical street address in New Zealand where documents can be served, but mainstream banks, the Financial Markets Authority, and Inland Revenue generally reject a pure mailbox-only virtual office as adequate for a commercially substantive entity. For an ISIC 5912 AI post-production and subtitle studio, a virtual office will be accepted only where there is a dedicated mail-handling agreement, physical access during business hours, a written service provider agreement appointing the address as registered office, and a New Zealand GST registration demonstrating real administrative management. The safer and more bankable structure is a serviced office or a physical office in Auckland or Wellington.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand, operating through the myIR online portal (https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir/); customs-related export procedures are administered by the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident company must file the prescribed non-resident company IRD number application with Inland Revenue, including the entity's certificate of incorporation, registered office and mailing address, full name, date and place of formation, principal business activity, New Zealand business address, a New Zealand resident contact agent or director contact, and, where the company expects to make taxable supplies in New Zealand, an application for Goods and Services Tax (GST) registration. The IRD will issue a single IRD number which also functions as the GST registration number. A non-resident entity not carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand may still obtain an IRD number for withholding tax or non-resident contractor purposes. Registration must be completed before invoicing for New Zealand taxable supplies, and an approved tax invoice must display the supplier's IRD/GST number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a New Zealand incorporated limited company is a separate tax-resident company taxed at 28% on worldwide income; no additional state or provincial income tax; GST is separate and applies at supply level).

Available election forms

None comparable to the United States check-the-box regime. A closely held New Zealand tax-resident company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) treatment under the Income Tax Act 2007 so that income, expenses and tax credits flow through to shareholders, subject to eligibility restrictions, particularly for non-resident shareholders. Hybrid mismatch and BEPS anti-avoidance rules are mandatory and cannot be elected out of.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; registration is compulsory once the 12-month turnover reaches NZD 60,000, or when there is a view to making taxable supplies in excess of that threshold; voluntary registration is available to entities carrying on a taxable activity even below threshold. Non-resident suppliers of remote services or cross-border digital services to New Zealand consumers are also subject to the same NZD 60,000 annual registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated when they are exported by the supplier from New Zealand and the supplier holds evidence of departure of the goods within the prescribed period; services are zero-rated when the supply is made to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, and the services are not supplied directly in connection with land or goods that are in New Zealand. For AI subtitling and audio-dubbing services supplied to overseas clients, the supply must be genuinely provided offshore, the client must be resident outside New Zealand, and all contract negotiation, performance, and payment evidence must be consistent with an offshore supply. If a service is effectively rendered in New Zealand or used in New Zealand, zero-rating is denied and 15% GST applies.

  • New Zealand Customs export entry (Trade Single Window export declaration) or overseas cargo notice; for services, the equivalent statutory evidence is the service agreement and invoices, as no customs declaration exists
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or courier/cargo consignment documentation proving physical export
  • Commercial tax invoice issued by the NZ Ltd supplier stating 'zero-rated export' and the supplier's IRD/GST number
  • Bank-issued foreign exchange inward remittance credit advice or SWIFT deposit/settlement record confirming receipt of payment in foreign currency from the non-resident client (the New Zealand banking equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate)
  • Documentary evidence of the non-resident customer's address and tax residency, and, for services, a contract or purchase order showing the place of supply and the jurisdiction in which the output is consumed

New Zealand does not operate a mandatory real-time electronic invoicing clearance, central fiscal stamping, or transaction-control API regime. GST invoices must be issued within 28 days after the tax point and must state the supplier's name/address, IRD/GST number, invoice date, description, quantity, price, GST amount, and total amount. The supplier may issue tax invoices electronically, including PDF and Edifact, and may participate voluntarily in the New Zealand Peppol e-invoicing network. Inland Revenue uses periodic GST returns (1-, 2-, or 6-monthly) and risk-based data matching rather than pre-clearance.

New Zealand has no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for exports, but Inland Revenue may audit the full documentary chain. If zero-rating is incorrectly claimed, the original supply is reassessed at 15% GST, with use-of-money interest running from the original due date, and shortfall penalties may apply under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and up to 150% for fraudulent or intentional evasion. Directors may incur personal civil penalty or recovery liabilities for unpaid GST where a company fails to comply. Repeat or artificial circular fund repatriation structures can also be attacked under the general anti-avoidance rule (section BG 1 and Part 5 of the Income Tax Act 2007) and the anti-money laundering / counter-terrorism financing obligations for banking and payment providers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a liberalised, fully convertible currency regime with no centralised foreign exchange controls. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) floats freely against all major currencies, and both current-account payments (trade, services, dividends, royalties) and capital-account transactions (equity injections, share transfers, intra-group loans, repatriation) can proceed without prior Reserve Bank approval. There are no surrender requirements on export proceeds, no limitations on foreign-currency bank accounts, and no obligatory conversion timing for export receipts. Profit remittance is straightforward via dividends, but a non-resident shareholder will encounter a standard resident withholding tax on dividends, usually 30% unless reduced under a relevant double-tax treaty; there are no currency-related hurdles. Banks treat foreign investments as normal commercial transactions, subject to AML/CFT customer due diligence and, where the investment reaches statutory thresholds, the Overseas Investment Act 2005. Because the NZD is fully convertible, NDF-based synthetic hedging is unnecessary for NZD; orthodox forward and swap instruments are available. Internally, tax depreciation and transfer-pricing rules may be reviewed by Inland Revenue for cross-border related-party transactions, but no exchange-control licence is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion requirement exists in New Zealand. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts in any offshore or onshore foreign-currency account, choose their own conversion timing, and remit proceeds to New Zealand at their commercial discretion. No mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) is the central banking authority; the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) separately administers foreign investment consent but does not regulate currency convertibility.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech EMIs and payment processors such as Wise (Borderless) and Stripe are legally available in New Zealand through the Financial Service Providers Register and AML/CFT registration. They are viable for collections, subtitling-platform settlements, and low-risk cross-border disbursements, but they are not registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and cannot serve as primary banking rails for capital verification, trade finance, or documentary credit operations. A legal constraint is that they must not hold themselves out as banks, and they cannot accept deposits or make loans in the ordinary banking sense; funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts rather than covered by the New Zealand Crown deposit guarantee. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A New Zealand limited company is legally required to appoint licensed independent certified public accountant to audit its financial statements if it is a 'large company' as defined by New Zealand financial reporting legislation. A large company means an entity that in each of the two preceding accounting periods meets at least two of three quantitative thresholds: (i) total assets exceeding NZ$10 million (USD ~$6 million); (ii) total revenue exceeding NZ$20 million (USD ~$12 million); or (iii) 50 or more full-time-equivalent employees. A statutory audit is also mandatory for issuers and public accountability entities under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, for a company whose shareholders holding 5% or more votes pass an audit requisition, or where a shareholder holding 10% or more shares requires audit.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% statutory NRWT on outbound dividends paid by a New Zealand company to a non-resident shareholder; treaty relief may reduce the rate to 15% or lower depending on the applicable DTA.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a standard 31 March balance date, the IR4 must be furnished no later than 7 July of the following income year. If the company uses an IRD-approved tax agent with extension arrangements, the filing deadline can be extended to 31 March of the following calendar year. Non-standard balance dates carry due dates prescribed by the Commissioner.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 1,000,000 aggregated cross-border related-party transaction threshold requiring contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File / Master File as applicable). CbCR filing generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding NZD 1.14 billion.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a zero-tax Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like some Caribbean or Gulf jurisdictions, but economic substance is central to tax residence, source, and treaty benefits. Under the Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1 general anti-avoidance rule, arrangements lacking commercial substance or created to defeat the intent of tax laws can be voided or reconstructed. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for an AI subtitle and voice-cloning studio include the local deployment of AI models, voice-library data curation, quality control, subtitle editing, client contracting, and the exercise of management control over revenue-producing assets. If these CIGA are performed by non-resident owners while the New Zealand entity is a bare title-holder, Inland Revenue may attribute the income to the actual economic owner or deny treaty protection, exposing the company and directors to reassessments and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Deliberate Understatement of Digital Service Revenue

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994, s 141B (abusive tax position shortfall penalty of 150% of the avoided tax), s 120A (use-of-money interest), and s 143A (evasion offence). On conviction for tax evasion, the penalty is a fine of up to NZD 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to five years. Inland Revenue may also reassess the full arm's length revenue, add NRWT, and demand immediate payment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false IR4 returns or direct the underpricing of related-party services are jointly and severally liable as active participants in the evasion. The corporate veil will be pierced where the company is a mere façade; directors may face civil asset restitution, a departure prohibition order (travel ban), disqualification from managing companies for up to ten years under the Companies Act 1993, and criminal imprisonment for tax evasion.
Withholding Tax Failure – Mischaracterisation of Royalties as Tax-Free Technical Services

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007, s RF 2 (royalty source), s RA 18 (withholding obligation), and the general NRWT collection provisions. Failure to withhold exposes the payer to recovery of the 30% NRWT, shortfall penalties up to 150%, and use-of-money interest. The deduction may be denied entirely, and the arrangement may be reconstructed under s BG 1.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false service agreements or knowingly sign withholding declarations without the necessary TRCs are personally liable as accessories. They may be required to compensate the company for the tax liability, suffer civil forfeiture of assets and travel bans, and face prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and Companies Act 1993 for fraudulent trading. Imprisonment is available for those who orchestrate the sham.
Shell Company Arrangement – Abusive Treaty Shopping and AML Substructure Failure

Statutory sanction: The arrangement is void under Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1, causing all revenue to be attributed to the New Zealand company on a look-through basis, with tax at 28%, NRWT at 30% on deemed repatriation, shortfall penalties, and interest. Additionally, the AML/CFT Act 2009 requires customer due diligence and beneficial ownership records; wilful non-compliance attracts civil penalties of up to NZD 2 million for companies and NZD 300,000 for individuals. The company may be struck off under the Companies Act 1993 for failing to operate as a genuine going concern.

Director personal liability: Directors, including foreign-chartered directors, are jointly and severally liable for tax debts when they allow the company to operate as a shell. The Court may order restitution of diverted assets, impose a travel ban or blacklisting by Inland Revenue, and disqualify the directors. Reckless trading under the Companies Act 1993 s 320 carries personal liability for the full amount of company debts, and criminal conviction on fraud or tax evasion charges can lead to imprisonment for up to seven years.
