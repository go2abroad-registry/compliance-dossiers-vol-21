Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / IRD) for income tax and GST; online service portal: myIR. For export goods, New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS) via the Trade Single Window is the border authority.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident company, apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number using the non-resident company application procedure via myIR or paper application (IR596) where no New Zealand bank account exists. Submit the company's Certificate of Incorporation, registered office details, full particulars of directors and shareholders, the foreign address, and a statement explaining the reason the IRD number is required (e.g. GST registration or filing obligations). Once the IRD number is allocated, apply for Goods and Services Tax registration in myIR with the intended registration date and expected taxable turnover. A non-resident supplier of remote services to New Zealand consumers must register for GST if its New Zealand taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period. There is no minimum capital requirement and non-resident beneficial ownership is permitted. A physical presence in New Zealand is not required to obtain an IRD number or to register for GST, although the entity must provide its overseas address and non-resident status accurately.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists for New Zealand tax purposes; a company is taxed at entity level under New Zealand domestic law. An eligible close-held company may elect look-through company (LTC) status under the Income Tax Act 2007, which provides tax transparency for qualifying owners, but this does not alter GST classification. There is no hybrid mismatch election or US-style classification regime; New Zealand applies its statutory entity classification rules only.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period for supplies made in New Zealand; non-resident suppliers of remote services must register when their taxable supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period and they are not already required to be registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated where they are supplied under a contract and exported by the supplier, the purchaser, or an agent of either party from New Zealand within 28 days of the supply date, and the supplier holds sufficient documentary evidence of export. Services may be zero-rated when physically performed outside New Zealand, supplied in relation to movable personal property located outside New Zealand at the time of supply, or supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand and does not acquire the services for use in New Zealand. For data-centre colocation or rack-space leasing, the classification depends on whether the supply is a supply of real property or a supply of services; zero-rating is available only if the supply qualifies as an exported or remote service under section 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, otherwise the standard 15% rate applies.

  • New Zealand Customs Export Declaration (lodged electronically through the Trade Single Window and accepted by New Zealand Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the goods from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract identifying the New Zealand exporter, overseas buyer, and the precise goods/services description
  • Customs departure confirmation, port manifest, or export entry clearance record showing export occurred within the statutory 28-day window
  • Foreign-exchange payment evidence such as a SWIFT MT103 or bank foreign-currency receipt showing remittance of export proceeds (FIRC is not a New Zealand statutory document)

No real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping is mandated. New Zealand operates a voluntary Peppol e-invoicing network; e-invoices may be exchanged bilaterally or through certified access points. GST tax invoices must contain the prescribed particulars under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and invoices must be retained for seven years. Recipient-created tax invoices are permitted in defined cases. There is no requirement to seek pre-approval or fiscal validation from Inland Revenue before issuing an invoice.

Inland Revenue may retrospectively deny zero-rating if the supplier cannot produce contemporaneous export and payment evidence. Any GST originally zero-rated would be reassessed at 15% with use-of-money interest from the original due date. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 may be imposed at 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. Knowingly false export declarations may also trigger offences under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 and related enforcement actions. Although New Zealand does not impose a separate statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, a 180-day repatriation window is the strict administrative benchmark used for audit substantiation; longer periods require clear documentary confirmation of payment terms and collectibility.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised foreign exchange regime with no exchange controls, capital account restrictions, or mandatory surrender obligations. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) maintains financial stability and registers commercial banks, but it does not vet individual currency conversions. Both current account transactions, including trade invoicing, dividends, interest, and software royalty flows, and capital account transactions, including equity injections, shareholder loans, and asset sales, are freely convertible. The only formality is customer due diligence under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act). Non-resident investors may hold NZD and offshore currency ledgers simultaneously; profit repatriation requires no central bank approval, though the paying bank may ask for a solvency resolution from the board and an IRD tax clearance when outbound remittances exceed NZ$50,000. The Overseas Investment Act 2005 applies only where a foreign person acquires sensitive land or significant business assets above NZ$100 million; a typical data centre colocation entity leasing rack space and importing servers remains below or outside the consent threshold unless it owns the land. Thus, the treasury blueprint treats NZD as convertible with zero surrender risk and no FX licensing friction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no export receipts surrender pool. Exporters may retain all foreign currency receipts in offshore or onshore foreign currency accounts; if converted to NZD, conversion occurs at client-approved spot rates without RBNZ reference or reporting burden except aggregated balance-of-payments statistics collected by Statistics New Zealand.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Medium-High as an ancillary payment rail, but Low for core treasury mandates. Airwallex, Wise Business, and OFX hold New Zealand FSP/Money Service Operator licences and are not legally barred from servicing an NZ resident limited company; they can deliver fast MT-type virtual wires, virtual IBANs, and monthly ACH staleness-free disbursements. However, because the model entity is a Tier-3/4 data centre colocation lessor that must place lease security deposits, hold capital escrow, open documentary letters of credit for imported server infrastructure, and demonstrate clean sponsor evidence to a Tier-1 lender, an unlicensed EMI cannot provide the required debt facility, ISDA/master service agreement, or cash settlement guarantee. The underwriting committee will therefore approve one regulated EMI for low-value operating disbursements under NZ$20,000 per wire, while all capital movements and trade settlements remain pinned to ANZ/BNZ/ASB/Westpac clearing rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed as a SWIFT MT103 from the parent investor's bank to the ANZ Capital Escrow Account. The settlement instruction must state a genuine BPM6 foreign exchange purpose code — 'DIA' for direct investment equity, 'INL' for intercompany shareholder loan, or 'OPR' for operational services — so the Reserve Bank's data collection, done via the clearing bank, can classify the flow correctly. On receipt, the escrow is swept into an internally earmarked ledger and an external auditor issues a Capital Verification Certificate (CVC) within ten business days, matching the credit note against the board's share allotment resolution. Only after the CVC confirms paid-up capital is a MT101 transfer to the operational currency account permitted. Where the total foreign ownership passes the Overseas Investment Act threshold for significant business assets or sensitive land, the OIO screening outcome must be obtained before the injection is settled.Outbound vendor payments use four clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for supplier invoices below NZ$500,000 with full strike-through of trade invoice references; (ii) irrevocable sight Commercial Letters of Credit opened by BNZ/ANZ for high-value server and cooling infrastructure imports, advised through the beneficiary's bank; (iii) Documentary Collections (D/P, D/A) for ongoing rack and network gear supply from reputable vendors; and (iv) local NZD clearing via the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) for domestic utilities. International wires must include the entity's IRD number and a purpose code 'T' for trade in goods, 'D' for distribution of dividends, or 'S' for services. The treasury overlay keeps an MT101 sweep from the escrow account to the operational account only when matching payment against approved invoices.Deliverable NZD/USD forward contracts up to 12-month tenor with ANZ/BNZ/ASB onshore dealing desks, available only against the underlying data centre trade and colocation lease receipts.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permissible solely where the counterparty cannot physically clear NZD; the standard NDF join is a tactical USD leg referencing the SNZ/USD exchange rate, strictly non-speculative.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) on infrastructure debt, subject to ISDA/CSA collateral annex and a mandatory 20/20 netting election between the NZ treasury and the clearing bank.Plain-vanilla purchased FX options (EUR/USD or USD/NZD) for committed capex orders, with a prohibition on writing naked options and quarterly reporting of the hedge ratio to the bank compliance committee.

Turnover: A mandatory independent audit is imposed when the NZ limited company is large under the Financial Reporting Act 2013. The large test is satisfied, on a consolidated group basis where applicable, when total assets exceed NZD 60 million, OR total revenue for the accounting period exceeds NZD 30 million, OR the company has 100 or more full-time-equivalent employees. An audit is also compulsory where the company is an FMC reporting entity, regardless of the monetary thresholds, or where shareholders holding 5 percent or more of the voting shares validly require an audit under section 199 of the Companies Act 1993.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 1,000,000 (for related party transactions exceeding this threshold, a Local File and Master File must be prepared)

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under Section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 applies. Transactions must have genuine commercial substance; otherwise, the Commissioner may recharacterize them. For data center colocation, the company must demonstrate real operational presence, including physical assets, employees, and decision-making in New Zealand.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall; criminal prosecution with fines up to NZD 50,000 for individuals and NZD 250,000 for companies, plus imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for tax debts if they knowingly allow the company to evade tax; may face civil penalties, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions under GAAR; denial of deductions; penalties up to 100% of the tax avoided; potential deregistration of the company.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid tax and penalties if they are involved in the sham arrangement; may face disqualification as directors and criminal charges.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to NZD 2 million for companies; criminal penalties up to NZD 10 million and imprisonment up to 2 years for individuals; suspension or revocation of business registration.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML breaches if they were knowingly involved; may face fines, imprisonment, and prohibition from managing companies.
