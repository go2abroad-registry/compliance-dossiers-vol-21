Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake, IRD) — the national tax and customs administration; secure online portal: myIR (www.ird.govt.nz/myir)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned NZ limited company must first obtain an IRD number by registering online via myIR using the company's NZ Business Number (NZBN) and certificate of incorporation; after incorporation, Companies Office automatically issues the NZBN and an IRD number is requested via Form IR596 (Non-Resident/Registration) or through the myIR portal. GST registration is a separate step: complete Form IR356 or online GST registration in myIR once the NZD 60,000 taxable-supply threshold is met or on a voluntary basis; the IRD issues a GST number with the format 000-000-000 and a GST return filing frequency (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly). Foreign directors/security holders with no NZ tax file number must also apply for an IRD number before the company registration is finalized. All applications are processed electronically within 5-10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand does not recognize the US check-the-box election. The principal statutory elective mechanism is the Look-Through Company (LTC) election under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, available only to closely held companies with five or fewer owners who are individuals or LTCs; an LTC election is filed via Form IR868 and makes the company fiscally transparent. For hybrid mismatch purposes, New Zealand applies the OECD hybrid mismatch and interest limitation rules, and taxpayers may file voluntary disclosure and elective adjustments (e.g., de minimis thresholds, CFC attribution elections) under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018; there is no non-resident-owned company preferential election apart from the LTC regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 gross taxable supplies in any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services (including digital/electronic services) are required to register when total supplies to NZ-resident consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period; registration is voluntary for persons carrying on a taxable activity even below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of goods applies under Section 11, Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the GST-registered supplier exports goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply and retains the statutory export documentation. Zero-rating of services applies under Section 11A where the services are supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, and the services are not supplied in connection with land in New Zealand or goods situated in New Zealand at the time of supply (subject to limited exceptions for physically performed services). For a parcel consolidation hub, the service of consolidating and line-hauling non-resident customers' parcels is zero-rated only if the recipient is a non-resident, the recipient is outside New Zealand when the service is performed, and the service is not supplied directly to a person in New Zealand; the exporter must hold documentary proof of physical export and of the non-resident status of the recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

New Zealand imposes no mandatory real-time electronic fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no government-run continuous transaction control (CTC) API clearance. GST is self-assessed and remitted periodically via GST101 returns filed through myIR. PEPPOL-based e-invoicing is voluntary and primarily used in B2G procurement; there is no legal obligation to obtain pre-clearance from IRD for individual invoices, but standard commercial e-invoicing (e.g., Xero, MYOB) must preserve full audit trail integrity for GST records.

The Inland Revenue Department audits zero-rating claims through GST matching against Customs export movement data, freight-forwarder records, and banking/fx inflows. If zero-rating is incorrectly claimed, IRD will retrospectively reassess the output GST at 15% and impose use-of-money interest from the original return due date. Civil shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 range from 20% of the tax shortfall for failure to take reasonable care, to 40% for evasion or an abusive tax position; late payment penalties accrue at 1% per month on unpaid net GST. Knowingly providing false export evidence can trigger prosecution with criminal penalties of up to NZD 50,000 for individuals and NZD 250,000 for companies, and the Customs and Excise Act 2018 separately penalizes false export declarations with seizure of goods and additional evidential penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. No distinction is made between current and capital account transactions for FX purposes; both current and capital transactions may be freely executed without central bank approval. Profit repatriation is unrestricted, subject to tax obligations only (e.g. non-resident withholding tax on dividends, applying up to 30% unless reduced by tax treaty) and standard SWIFT clearing requirements. There are no surrender, priority allocation, or counterparty concentration rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Export proceeds need not be surrendered, converted, or repatriated within any statutory timeline. Export contracts may be invoiced in any freely convertible currency. For NZ tax and accounting, revenue must be reported in NZD, and any foreign-currency receivables/balances must be translated to NZD at the balance-sheet exchange rate in accordance with NZ IFRS.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) is the central bank and prudential supervisor; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) regulates FX derivatives. New Zealand maintains no separate foreign exchange administration or exchange-control department; the AML/CFT supervisor for non-bank financial institutions is the Department of Internal Affairs.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High for a low-risk parcel consolidation hub. Licensed digital cross-border providers such as Airwallex (NZ) Limited are registered as financial institutions under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act) and may offer multi-currency wallets, FX conversion, and local clearing access. Unlicensed offshore digital EMIs may not hold NZD balances or provide payment services to NZ entities unless properly registered domestically. An EMI-only structure without a local NZ bank partner is operationally viable but should be supplemented by a clearing bank account for settlement finality.

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall

Box 6 Imputation credit account balance: retained imputation credits and dividend credits available to shareholders. Non-resident shareholders must calculate dividend withholding tax (NRWT) on any fully imputed distribution separately; attach an IR4J imputation processing schedule. Late filing of the IR4 attracts a Late Filing Penalty of NZD 250 per month for small taxpayers and NZD 500 per month for large taxpayers, capped at NZD 1,000 and NZD 2,500 respectively; late payment incurs an initial Late Payment Penalty of 1% immediately plus an incremental 4% after 7 days, together with Use-of-Money Interest (currently 8.34% on underpaid terminal tax).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File are required where aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZD 10 million per income year; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue above NZD 1.14 billion. For an ISIC 5229 parcel consolidation hub, contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is also recommended for any lower-value intercompany linehaul, warehouse management fees, or cost-sharing arrangements with overseas related parties.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime analogous to Gulf jurisdictions; however, economic substance is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under s BG 1 and s GA 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, the Principal Purpose Test inserted by the Multilateral Instrument into NZ DTAs, and the 'managed and controlled' test which establishes NZ tax residence. The e-commerce parcel hub must demonstrate physical warehouse premises, employed operational staff, genuine linehaul decision-making, and board control exercised in New Zealand to avoid sham recharacterisation, treaty-shopping denial, or loss of residence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under s 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994: a fine of up to NZD 50,000 for an individual or NZD 250,000 for a company, plus a civil shortfall penalty of 100% of the evaded tax, late payment penalties, Use-of-Money Interest, and imprisonment of up to 5 years for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise, participate in, or recklessly fail to prevent evasion are jointly and severally liable for unpaid withheld taxes under s 149 TAA and may be prosecuted as parties to the offence; consequences include civil restitution orders, director disqualification under the Companies Act 1994, travel bans, asset confiscation under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2009, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under Part GC and ss GC 1 to GC 18 of the Income Tax Act 2007 requiring the arm's length price to be substituted; a shortfall penalty of 50% of the resulting tax shortfall for an unacceptable tax position (and 100% where evasion is established), plus Use-of-Money Interest.

Director personal liability: A director who signs false transfer-pricing documentation or implements a scheme with a dominant tax avoidance purpose risks a personal penalty of up to 100% of the shortfall where the arrangement is held to be evasion; reckless conduct supporting insolvency may trigger personal liability for debts under s 135 (reckless trading) of the Companies Act 1994.
Shell Company / GAAR (Economic Substance Failure)

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may void the arrangement under the GAAR (s BG 1 / GA 1 Income Tax Act 2007) and reconstruct the transaction on the basis of substantive operations; shortfall penalties of 50% (abusive tax position) or 100% (evasion) apply, and the company may be re-domiciled as tax-resident in New Zealand under s YD 2; persistent shell inactivity can lead to Companies Act strike-off.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors knowingly involved in a sham may be personally liable for the avoided tax via civil restitution; courts may impose director disqualification, manage-company bans for up to 5 years, travel restrictions pending criminal proceedings, and compensation orders against the directors of the empty shell.
AML/CFT and Cross-Border Remittance Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 of up to NZD 2 million for a body corporate and NZD 200,000 for an individual per serious non-compliance, plus remedial orders and enforcement actions; associated customs fraud can trigger the Crimes Act 1961 money-laundering offence of up to 7 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Officers who knowingly assist or are wilfully blind to AML breaches face pecuniary penalties, prohibition orders, personal criminal prosecution for money laundering, and confiscation of illicit proceeds under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2009; directors may also be held civilly liable in tort for losses arising from failed compliance systems.
