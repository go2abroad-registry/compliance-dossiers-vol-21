Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally permissible for registered office purposes under the Companies Act 1993, provided that the address is a physical location in New Zealand and not a PO Box. However, for a franchise business engaged in food and beverage operations, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and commercial banks may require a physical operational presence for tax registration and bank account opening. The franchise entity must have a physical place of business where records are kept and where the company can be contacted. Virtual offices are often rejected by banks for high-risk industries, but the bubble tea franchise is considered low-risk; nevertheless, banks may still require a physical address for the registered office and for the principal place of business. It is recommended to lease a physical office or use a serviced office with a physical presence to avoid any rejection.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand, electronic portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand incorporated limited company (including those with non-resident shareholders), the IRD number is automatically issued upon incorporation via the Companies Office. The company must then register for GST through myIR if annual taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 or if it intends to claim input tax credits. For a foreign non-resident entity without NZ incorporation, the procedure involves completing the IRD application form (IR742) and submitting it to the IRD with supporting documents such as certificate of incorporation, proof of identity of directors, and a New Zealand physical or postal address. The IRD will issue an IRD number, and the entity must separately register for GST if it makes supplies in NZ exceeding the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated if exported from New Zealand within 15 days of supply, with evidence of export. Services (including franchise licensing and royalties) are zero-rated if supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, and the services are not supplied in connection with land or goods in New Zealand. Syrup and other physical goods exported as part of the franchise supply chain qualify for zero-rating if the export documentation is retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Foreign exchange receipt evidence (bank statement or equivalent showing receipt of payment in foreign currency)

No real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and may be conducted via the Peppol network. The IRD does not require API-based real-time reporting for GST; standard periodic GST returns (filed monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly) are sufficient.

Under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and the Tax Administration Act 1994, incorrect zero-rating claims may result in penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall, plus interest. Fraudulent claims or deliberate misrepresentation may lead to criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. The IRD may retrospectively claw back zero-rated treatment if the required export documentation is not retained for at least 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a genuinely liberalised and fully convertible foreign exchange regime for both current and capital account transactions. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not impose general exchange control restrictions, pre-approval requirements, or quantitative limits on the purchase, sale, or transfer of foreign currency. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) floats freely in the global marketplace, and both residents and non-residents can freely repatriate dividends, royalties, loan repayments, and capital proceeds without requiring Central Bank consent. This statutory openness is rooted in the Currency and Bank Notes Act and the Foreign Investment regime, which focuses primarily on national security and sensitive land/assets, not on current account remittance restrictions. For a global franchise treasury, there are no surrender obligations on export receipts, no mandatory conversion into NZD, and no lock-up periods on inbound capital. The only practical hurdles are customer due diligence requirements under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and the Overseas Investment Act screening if the entity acquires sensitive land or significant business assets. Consequently, a New Zealand limited company can maintain multi-currency accounts and deploy cross-border liquidity efficiently, subject only to standard banking compliance and tax clearance protocols.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender regime exists. New Zealand does not require exporters or franchise licensors to convert foreign currency receipts into NZD within any prescribed timeframe, nor does it mandate any minimum conversion ratio. All export proceeds may be retained in offshore accounts, swapped, hedged, or remitted internally at the company’s commercial discretion, provided the underlying transaction is properly documented for tax and AML purposes.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs face significant structural limitations in New Zealand. The country does not offer a standalone EMI license; non-bank payment service providers must either become registered under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and partner with a licensed bank for safeguarding, or operate as a subsidiary of a registered bank. Payment service providers that are not registered banks cannot accept deposits or provide lending, and they are generally restricted from offering full corporate treasury services such as multi-currency escrow accounts or trade finance instruments. For the purpose of a global franchise holding company that needs capital escrow, royalty consolidation, and SWIFT-based disbursement rails, a direct relationship with a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank is strongly preferable. A reputable fintech EMI may be used solely as a supplementary collection node for low-value local POS payments, but not as the primary cross-border clearing backbone. Regulatory risk and reputational exposure make it inadvisable to rely on an unregulated offshore EMI for the core liquidity structure.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Offshore trust as ultimate parent with opaque beneficial ownership layers

Root cause: Offshore trust as ultimate parent with opaque beneficial ownership layers

Defense evidence: Provide a formal trust deed, a searchable register of beneficiaries, a current trust resolution appointing the New Zealand director, a certified copy of the trust’s tax identification number, a licensed professional trustee’s statutory declaration, and a written legal opinion from an NZ-licensed counsel confirming the trust is not a shell or conduit for third-party funds.
Franchise royalty income flows from multiple foreign jurisdictions creating a fragmented source-of-wealth picture

Root cause: Franchise royalty income flows from multiple foreign jurisdictions creating a fragmented source-of-wealth picture

Defense evidence: Submit a detailed royalty model showing franchise agreements, monthly royalty calculations, certified financial statements for the last two years, source-of-funds letters from each master franchisee, and an independent audit trail of the underlying sales-based royalty payment via POS software logs and bank statements.
Potential exposure to sanctions or politically exposed persons through international franchise partners

Root cause: Potential exposure to sanctions or politically exposed persons through international franchise partners

Defense evidence: Deliver a global sanctions screening report covering all franchisees and beneficial owners, a negative media search report from a reputable due-diligence provider, certified copies of passports for all material stakeholders, and a signed compliance attestation that no sanctioned entity or PEP is involved in the franchise chain.
Use of capital escrow account for initial franchise deposits that may be misclassified as trade finance or investment income

Root cause: Use of capital escrow account for initial franchise deposits that may be misclassified as trade finance or investment income

Defense evidence: Provide the master franchise subscription agreement, the escrow instruction letter, bank-to-bank verification of the source of the inbound capital, a full breakdown of the funds as either share capital or shareholder loan, and a statutory declaration from the director confirming there is no connection to money laundering, terrorism financing, or unlicensed deposit-taking.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections for the New Zealand franchise holding entity should be routed through the company’s primary operational account at a Tier-1 bank using SWIFT MT103 or MT202 cover payments, depending on interbank currency corridors. The remitter must clearly identify the purpose of payment as either “Share Capital Injection” (purpose code 100 – Investment in equity capital) or “Shareholder Loan” (purpose code 800 – Other investment) in line with the OECD Balance of Payments manual and New Zealand’s international transaction reporting standards. Capital verification is performed through the bank’s internal capital account statement, a statutory declaration from the director, and filing with the New Zealand Companies Office showing the allotment of shares or issuance of debt instruments. For franchise deposits from foreign sub-licensees, the incoming wire must be labelled as “Franchise Royalty / Initial License Fee” with the relevant franchise agreement number to avoid misclassification as trade or investment income. All inbound wires above the $10,000 threshold are automatically captured by the bank’s AML transaction monitoring engine, and the treasury department should maintain a matching ledger to reconcile each receipt to the executed franchise contract.Outbound disbursements for syrup supply, equipment procurement, and franchise royalty distributions should be executed via SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic transfers, commercial letters of credit for high-value inventory purchases, and documentary collections for standard trade settlements. For recurring low-value global payments, the company can use the bank’s host-to-host API to initiate batch MT103 messages with structured remittance advice. Bulk payments to franchisees in the same country can be settled locally through the bank’s high-value clearing system (ESIS) or through a dedicated multi-currency ledger if the bank offers virtual accounts.
