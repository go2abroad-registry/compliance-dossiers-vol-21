To obtain a tax identification number for a foreign-owned non-resident entity: (1) Incorporate a limited liability company with the New Zealand Companies Office, receiving a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). (2) Apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number using form IR596 (for non-resident companies) or via myIR online registration. The IRD number serves as the TIN for income tax and GST purposes. (3) If the entity is engaged in taxable activity supply in New Zealand and turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period, register for GST using form GST101A or through myIR. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under s11(1)(a) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 applies to supplies of goods exported by the supplier from New Zealand, provided the goods are exported within 28 days (or within the applicable timeframe) and documentary evidence of export is retained. Services directly tied to exported goods and delivered to non-residents may also qualify. The export must be a physical export from New Zealand to a foreign destination; merely selling to a non-resident without exporting the goods does not create a zero-rated supply.

  • New Zealand Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry/Export Entry) with validated CUS/MARS reference
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing export from New Zealand
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bank statement or Foreign Exchange Conversion record (e.g., inward remittance certificate) evidencing receipt of foreign currency from overseas purchaser

No mandatory e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance regime in New Zealand. E-invoicing (Peppol format) is voluntary and permitted. No anti-fraud digital stamping is implemented.

Incorrect or fraudulent zero-rating claims may result in retrospective GST adjustment (output tax being imposed at 15%), late payment interest under section 120 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, and civil penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall for evasion or gross carelessness. Serious fraud may be prosecuted criminally under the Crimes Act 1961, with imprisonment terms. Exporters must retain all export documentation for at least 7 years to substantiate claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Reserve Bank of New Zealand operates an open-market, floating-currency framework and does not maintain exchange controls or foreign-exchange rationing. New Zealand has undertaken IMF Article VIII obligations, so both current account flows and capital account flows are freely convertible in practice. The NZD is fully settlable through licensed commercial banks at market-driven spot, forward, swap and option rates without central bank approval or quota limitations. There is no surrender requirement for export proceeds, no mandatory conversion of foreign currency receipts to NZD, and no prescribed timeline for inward repatriation of trade earnings. Dividend and profit remittance from a New Zealand limited company is constrained only by ordinary solvency tests, director/shareholder resolution records and any applicable non-resident withholding tax; it is not subject to exchange-control consent. The material treasury constraint is evidentiary rather than regulatory: banks must verify source of funds, beneficial ownership, economic substance and transaction purpose under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. For the stated low-risk online retail of garden hydroponics equipment, current-account convertibility is effectively automatic once KYC documentation is complete.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules are in force under New Zealand law. Exporters may lawfully hold foreign-currency receipts in onshore or offshore accounts in any form, with no mandatory conversion to NZD, no repatriation deadline and no fixed sell-down ratio. Foreign currency may be retained for direct settlement of overseas supplier invoices, or converted at the commercial bank's prevailing market rate at the entity's discretion.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand - the sole monetary policy, prudential and bank-licensing authority under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs are not legally barred from New Zealand, but they are not a substitute for a Tier-1 domestic bank treasury account. A fintech EMI must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register and must be supervised under the AMLCFT Act if it provides financial services to a New Zealand entity; it may operate as a money remitter or digital wallet provider. However, it cannot issue a true local bank account with the New Zealand business sort code, cannot provide a commercial letter of credit or documentary collection rail, and generally cannot satisfy the capital verification expectations of auditors or downstream investors. Therefore viability as the primary treasury solution is Low; viability as an auxiliary e-commerce collection rail for Stripe/PayPal float consolidation is Medium, provided the EMI's AML compliance and jurisdiction profile do not introduce additional refusal risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the inbound equity injection, the subscribing shareholder should initiate a SWIFT MT103 from its own previously verified bank account with a clear purpose narrative such as SHARE CAPITAL / EQUITY CONTRIBUTION TO NZ LTD and an ISO payment purpose code identifying investment in equity capital. The funds may be remitted in NZD or in a major convertible currency such as USD, EUR, GBP, APAC or AUD; on arrival the receiving New Zealand Tier-1 bank will either credit the capital escrow account in the same currency or convert at the spot rate after the bank's dealer confirms the purpose and source of funds. There is no foreign-exchange approval gate, but the bank will require the following capital-verification bundle before allowing onward use: board resolution authorising the share issuance, a completed Companies Office share allotment notice, a certified share register extract, evidence of the transaction ledger, source-of-funds documentation for any amount above the bank's prescribed threshold, and if applicable an accountant's capital-verification letter confirming that the payment is recorded as share capital and not as sale revenue. Annual Return (Companies Office AR30/AR12) updating company details and confirming the company is still entitled to remain on the New Zealand Companies Register Due during the company's annual filing month, being the calendar month corresponding to the original incorporation month; the return must be filed online on or before the last day of that month. $29 USD Failure to file by the last day of the filing month triggers arrears notices from the Companies Office; continued non-compliance leads to a Companies Act 1993 section 341 notice of intention to remove the company from the New Zealand Register, and if the default is not cured within the statutory period (commonly 20 working days from notice), the Registrar may strike the company off the Register, after which company assets may vest in the Crown and directors may be subject to enforcement and disqualification.
