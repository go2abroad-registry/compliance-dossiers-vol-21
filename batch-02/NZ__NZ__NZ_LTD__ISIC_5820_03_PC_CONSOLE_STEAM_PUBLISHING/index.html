Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is permissible only if it provides a physical street address in New Zealand, is not a PO Box, is staffed during normal business hours, and is capable of receiving service of legal process. Under section 187 of the Companies Act 1993, the registered office must be accessible to the public. For a digital video game publisher using Steam and PlayStation Store, a virtual office with mail handling and meeting room facilities will generally satisfy the Companies Office, but the Inland Revenue Department and New Zealand banks will reject a purely nominal virtual office for tax residence and bank account purposes if the board and majority of management operate offshore. The company must maintain one local director, a local registered agent, adequate minutes and director decisions, and financial records physically in New Zealand. A commercial mailbox without human presence is not an acceptable registered office.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None; New Zealand has no check-the-box entity classification election. An NZ limited company is statutorily taxed at entity level at 28%, cannot elect pass-through treatment, and the hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act 2007 are mandatory rather than elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in actual or estimated taxable supplies in any 12-month period; the same NZD 60,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of remote services/electronically supplied services sold to New Zealand consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a supply is zero-rated when the statutory export test is met. For this video game publishing business: (i) services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed are zero-rated; (ii) remote or electronically supplied digital publishing services provided to offshore platform operators such as Valve/Steam or PlayStation Store are zero-rated where the supplier does not know or have reason to believe the recipient is present in New Zealand and the service is performed outside New Zealand; (iii) physical goods exported from New Zealand, such as promotional or collector physical merchandise, are zero-rated only if there is a valid Customs export entry and evidence of actual departure from New Zealand; and (iv) supplies made to New Zealand consumers or to any person that acquires the service for direct consumption in New Zealand remain taxable at 15%. The mere selection of a foreign digital store platform does not by itself create zero-rating; the recipient, place of supply, and consumption location must be substantiated.

  • Customs Export Entry accepted by the New Zealand Customs Service under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, lodged through the Trade Single Window or Integrated Cargo System, for physical goods exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of goods from New Zealand and delivery to a foreign destination, for physical exports only
  • Commercial invoice, digital publishing agreement, or platform revenue statement with Valve/Steam or PlayStation Store identifying the non-resident platform operator and the consideration for the exported offshore service
  • Bank credit advice, SWIFT MT103, or foreign exchange settlement advice from a New Zealand bank showing receipt of the overseas platform payout; New Zealand does not issue a formal Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)
  • Recipient non-resident status evidence for digital services, including registered business address, jurisdiction of incorporation, payment billing address, IP address geo-location data, and platform user or merchant metadata supporting performance and consumption outside New Zealand

New Zealand does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing, or an anti-fraud digital stamping regime. Valid GST tax invoices must include the prescribed details under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and be issued within 28 days of supply; electronic tax invoices are permitted. Voluntary PEPPOL e-invoicing may be used for business-to-government transactions. GST returns and payments are filed electronically through myIR, with no mandatory transaction-level API clearance.

New Zealand does not impose a general statutory 180-day foreign exchange repatriation deadline for GST purposes; the 180-day figure should be read as an internal audit-evidence benchmark. Inland Revenue may claw back a zero-rated export if the supplier cannot produce the required export entry, contemporaneous transport documents, non-resident service evidence, or foreign exchange receipt evidence. Unsubstantiated zero-rating is reassessed as taxable at 15% at the time of supply, with use-of-money interest under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Civil shortfall penalties may be imposed from 20% for failure to take reasonable care up to 150% for evasion; false export declarations and materially incorrect records can also be prosecuted under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 and the Tax Administration Act 1994. All export evidence and underlying transactional records must be retained in New Zealand for at least seven years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised capital and current account. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 define the regulatory perimeter, but the Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not issue exchange-control permits for ordinary trade, service royalties, or equity flows. The New Zealand dollar is freely convertible; no central-bank prior approval is required for inbound share capital, outbound dividends, intercompany loan repayments, or Steam and PlayStation royalty remittances. The Overseas Investment Act 2005 applies only to sensitive land, significant business assets, and fishing quota; a pure digital games publishing entity without such assets will not trigger OIO consent. Profit repatriation is governed by Companies Act 1993 solvency and dividend distribution rules, with non-resident withholding tax at the applicable double-tax agreement rate. Banks perform source-of-funds and sanctions checks but do not enforce conversion ratios or repatriation timelines. Unlike jurisdictions with administered FX controls, New Zealand simply has no surrender requirement, no registration queue, no BOP coding approval, and no statutory matching of export receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export foreign currency receipts. Exporters may retain proceeds in onshore or offshore foreign currency accounts, convert to New Zealand dollars at their own discretion, or redeploy the proceeds for future platform payouts, provided the underlying transactions remain compliant with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and standard bank commercial terms.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital e-money institutions such as Wise, Airwallex, and similar providers are not legally barred in New Zealand, but they are subject to the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. To service a New Zealand company, an EMI must register on the Financial Service Providers Register and maintain an approved dispute resolution scheme. However, an EMI should be treated only as a complementary clearing rail for high-frequency Steam and PlayStation payouts, not as the primary treasury backbone. A registered Tier 1 domestic commercial bank remains mandatory for capital verification, Companies Office evidence, IRD tax payments, RBNZ settlement liquidity, and the robust source-of-funds audit trail expected by auditors and the Inland Revenue Department. Feasibility rating: high as a payout-side convenience rail; low as a substitute for a New Zealand licensed bank account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File are required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding NZD 1.2 billion. In addition, any cross-border related-party transaction with an aggregate value exceeding NZD 10 million in an income year triggers a statutory obligation to prepare and retain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation evidencing arm's length pricing. Even below the NZD 10 million threshold, a taxpayer must still be able to demonstrate arm's length compliance on request.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations akin to offshore financial centres, but the 'economic substance over legal form' principle drives the general anti-avoidance rule (Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1) and the place of effective management (POEM) test for tax residency. NZ Ltd must have real staff, office premises, decision-making powers, and active game-publishing functions in New Zealand. If the board of directors and core income-generating activities (CIGA) for publishing, platform contracts, and IP exploitation are located outside New Zealand, Inland Revenue may re-characterise the entity as non-resident or attribute profits to the foreign head office, and a non-resident company using a New Zealand shell without real substance may be ignored for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Undisclosed Offshore Digital Revenue

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Tax Administration Act 1994 ss 141 and 143B (evasion or similar act); shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax evaded; criminal conviction under s 143A carries imprisonment for up to 5 years and fines up to NZD 50,000 for the company, with liability for the tax, interest, and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed, authorised, or acquiesced in the concealment can be held jointly and severally liable under s 149 of the Tax Administration Act for the company's unpaid tax and penalties; civil asset restitution and freezing orders may issue; directors face individual criminal prosecution, disqualification from managing companies under s 383 of the Companies Act 1993, and imprisonment for up to 5 years. Departure prohibition orders can prevent overseas travel pending resolution.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation in Cross-Border Royalties

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 ss GC 6 to GC 14 empower the IRD to adjust taxable income to arm's length; transfer pricing shortfall penalties range from 20% (failure to exercise reasonable care) to 40% (unacceptable tax position) and up to 150% if conduct amounts to evasion or fraud; late payment interest accrues; the adjustment eliminates any tax benefit and may also trigger dividend recharacterisation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation or knowingly understate taxable income are personally liable for the tax shortfall and penalties; under the Companies Act 1993, they may be liable for reckless trading if the arrangement deprives creditors of assets; criminal liability for tax evasion can result in imprisonment up to 5 years, blacklisting for director appointments, and joint and several recovery from personal assets.
Improper Avoidance of Non-Resident Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: Non-resident withholding tax rules under Income Tax Act 2007 ss RD 10-RD 27, RE 13, and RF 2 require withholding on dividends, interest, and royalties. Failure to withhold triggers a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the unpaid NRWT plus interest; the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1) can recharacterise the payment as a dividend; the company is liable for the unpaid NRWT as a deemed trustee, and criminal sanctions apply for deliberate evasion under s 143A of the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for NRWT the company fails to deduct and remit, because the amounts are held in trust for Inland Revenue; Inland Revenue may issue notices to directors for asset attachment and personal recovery; failure to comply can lead to contempt of court, travel bans (departure prohibition orders), civil restitution, disqualification as a director, and imprisonment for up to 5 years for fraudulent evasion. Piercing the corporate veil is permitted where the company is a mere façade for the shareholder/director's tax avoidance scheme.
