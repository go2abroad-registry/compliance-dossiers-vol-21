Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or blocked bank account requirement for an ordinary limited liability company. The initial shareholders must, however, resolve the number of shares to be subscribed, and directors must ensure that the consideration for the allotment is received in full or that the allottee is liable to pay in full in accordance with section 75 of the Companies Act 1993. Capital protection rules and insolvency tests under the Act continue to apply.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand, administered through the myIR secure online portal (myIR.ird.govt.nz); export customs processing is administered by the New Zealand Customs Service.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a New Zealand limited company first incorporates through the New Zealand Companies Office, obtains an NZBN, and registers the overseas/constituent director details. The company then applies to the IRD for an IRD number using the prescribed application channel for non-resident companies (online through myIR where available, or the prescribed paper application for a non-resident company IRD number). The application must include the New Zealand registered office address, the company incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, director and ultimate beneficial owner details, and, where applicable, a New Zealand resident agent or tax agent appointment. The IRD performs a nexus review under the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; an IRD number is issued for income tax if the company is a New Zealand tax resident, carries on a taxable activity, or derives New Zealand-sourced income. GST registration must be completed separately, before exceeding NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period, using the IRD GST registration process; non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand residents must also register in New Zealand if the NZD 60,000 remote-services threshold is exceeded. If the non-resident has no New Zealand taxable activity and no New Zealand-sourced income, no IRD number is required, but the company should document that analysis to avoid penalties for failure to register.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None equivalent to the United States check-the-box election. New Zealand offers a statutory Look-Through Company (LTC) election for eligible close-held companies qualifying under the Income Tax Act 2007, which passes taxable income/losses to shareholders while preserving limited liability; the election is made via the company tax return and myIR profile. The GST Act 1985 also permits GST group registration elections for related companies, treating the group as one registered person. Hybrid mismatch rules apply under the Income Tax Act 2007, but no separate hybrid election is available; affected cross-border arrangements must be disclosed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of gross taxable supplies (standard-rated and zero-rated, excluding exempt supplies) in any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services supplied to New Zealand residents are subject to the same NZD 60,000 registration threshold in respect of those remote services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, goods physically exported from New Zealand are zero-rated when the supplier exports the goods under an agreement and holds the required customs evidence. Cloud LegalTech document automation and e-signature services supplied to a non-resident customer are zero-rated as exported services when the customer is not a New Zealand resident, the customer is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, the services do not relate to tangible property or land in New Zealand, and the customer is not carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand at the time of supply. The supplier must obtain and retain commercial and payment evidence showing the offshore recipient and offshore service delivery, as mere assertion of export without documentation is insufficient for the 0% rate.

  • New Zealand Customs export declaration (electronic filing through the Trade SingleWindow system) and export entry documents
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and pre-shipment export records evidencing the overseas buyer and terms of sale
  • Foreign exchange repatriation and payment records evidencing receipt of proceeds from the non-resident customer, including SWIFT MT103 or a bank foreign exchange conversion statement; an inward remittance certificate style document is not issued on a statutory basis in New Zealand and bank/IRD records are used instead
  • For digital services only: the signed SaaS/API agreement, service recipient tax-residence certificate or self-declaration, IP-geolocation or remote-delivery logs, and the overseas customer's billing and payment history

New Zealand does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate. E-invoicing is voluntary and may be exchanged via the Peppol network (Peppol BIS 3.0) or other electronic channels. Every GST-registered supplier must, however, issue a tax invoice containing the statutory particulars for taxable supplies above NZD 50 (including the supplier name, GST number, description, quantity, amount excluding GST, and GST component); recipient-created tax invoices may be used only under IRD-approved arrangements. Invoices and underlying transaction records must be retained for the statutory record-keeping period for tax audit purposes.

The IRD may audit export zero-rating claims and, where the export/customs evidence is incomplete or the required payment is not received within the applicable period, reassess the supply as a taxable supply at 15% GST. The resulting shortfall is increased by statutory interest and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for failure to exercise reasonable care, 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. False or misleading GST returns can also attract criminal or civil penalties, director-level liability, and loss of the zero-rating status for related transactions. Export substantiation records are generally required to be retained for seven years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible under both the current and capital accounts. New Zealand has no general foreign exchange control legislation restricting inward or outward remittances, dividend distributions, intercompany loan repayments, or capital repatriation by foreign-owned companies. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) performs prudential supervision and oversees the settlement and clearing infrastructure, but it does not operate as a classic exchange-control authority. The Overseas Investment Act 2003 only applies to sensitive land, quota, or significant business-asset transactions; the ordinary receipt of share-subscription capital for a LegalTech SaaS entity is not notifiable unless asset thresholds are triggered. Profits, royalties, and management fees may be remitted abroad via standard SWIFT banking channels without prior RBNZ approval, provided the underlying financial statements, board resolutions, and tax documentation are current and defensible. Export proceeds need not be converted into NZD and may be retained in the invoiced currency. Banks will still apply AML/CFT due diligence under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and may request source-of-wealth, source-of-funds, and tax-residency evidence for every shareholder and ultimate beneficial owner.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists in New Zealand. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no prescribed surrender timeline, and no prohibition on retaining foreign currency receipts in onshore or offshore treasury accounts. Export/contract revenue earned by the LeyTech entity may remain in USD or be voluntarily converted to NZD at market-determined exchange rates. Only ordinary tax invoicing, transfer-pricing documentation, and AML/CFT transaction-reporting obligations apply.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech EMI viability is Medium for low-value recurring SaaS subscriptions but Low for the core treasury, escrow, and capital-preservation mandate. Non-bank electronic money institutions such as Wise Business, Airwallex, and Stripe Treasury are legally permitted to operate as AML/CFT reporting entities under the New Zealand Department of Internal Affairs regime, but they are not RBNZ-licensed commercial banks. A LegalTech entity using Stripe, Credit Card, and other digital rails can use a fintech EMI as a collection gateway for payment cards and local payout automation, but the statutory capital injection, board-approved escrow, and cross-border liquidity buffers must be anchored in a tier-1 domestically incorporated bank account. EMI platforms should be limited to segregated operational sub-ledgers with daily sweeps into the primary NZD account to avoid concentration, lending, or deposit-taking risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections should be settled by SWIFT MT103 using the primary beneficiary NZD account at the chosen tier-1 bank. The remittance purpose field should be strictly tagged as 'subscription for ordinary shares in NZ Ltd' and include the company's NZBN and jurisdiction code to avoid misclassification. RBNZ approval is not required, but the funds will pass through a standard balance-of-payments reporting round. The New Zealand bank will screen counterparties and require confirmation that the transaction does not trigger the Overseas Investment Act 2003; for a small LegalTech SaaS company the threshold is normally not exceeded. Post-settlement, the entity must file an allotment return with the Companies Office, complete the accountant's capital verification statement, and retain the SWIFT confirmation trail. For conversion from USD or EUR to NZD, use the bank's same-value spot trade only after a treasury policy on timing and rate tolerance is documented.Cross-border disbursements for cloud infrastructure, sub-processors, legal counsel, and software development are most efficiently cleared using SWIFT MT103 from the primary NZD/USD accounts. For transactional SaaS purchases, Stripe and credit-card rails remain acceptable for low-value subscriptions. For large supplier payments, banks require the underlying invoice, signed service agreement, and a purpose code such as 'software subscription' or 'professional services'. Commercial letters of credit are not recommended because the business sells digital services rather than physical goods; documentary collections are possible where a software escrow release is required, but the preferred rail remains a straight MT103 with full invoice reconciliation.
