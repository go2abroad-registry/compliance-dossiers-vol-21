Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A New Zealand company must maintain a registered office in New Zealand. A virtual office or commercial mailbox can satisfy the bare registered-office rule, but for an ISIC 6619 cross-border payment gateway that requires a statutory licence and AML/CFT registration, the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA), the Financial Markets Authority (FMA), and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand will reject a pure virtual address as evidence of genuine commercial substance. The Inland Revenue Department and New Zealand banks will also decline to open corporate bank and merchant acquiring accounts where there is no physical office, local management, or employee presence in New Zealand. Accordingly, a virtual office is not permissible for the licensed PSP operation; a physical leased office with local directors and compliance staff must be established before the licence application is submitted.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand; official electronic portal: myIR (Inland Revenue Secure Online Services)

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand limited company, the IRD number (tax identifier) is automatically issued by Inland Revenue upon registration through the New Zealand Companies Office. A foreign non-resident entity without a New Zealand incorporation must complete Inland Revenue's non-resident company registration application, providing the foreign certificate of incorporation, certified constitutional documents, NZ registered office or tax agent authority, director/principal officer details, and a statement of the intended NZ business activity. GST registration is applied for through the same myIR registration workflow; the IRD number is issued before a GST number can be activated.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a New Zealand limited company, the IRD number (tax identifier) is automatically issued by Inland Revenue upon registration through the New Zealand Companies Office. A foreign non-resident entity without a New Zealand incorporation must complete Inland Revenue's non-resident company registration application, providing the foreign certificate of incorporation, certified constitutional documents, NZ registered office or tax agent authority, director/principal officer details, and a statement of the intended NZ business activity. GST registration is applied for through the same myIR registration workflow; the IRD number is issued before a GST number can be activated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, exported goods are zero-rated under section 11 where the supplier exports them within 28 days of supply (or such further period as the Commissioner allows) and holds acceptable evidence of physical export. Services are zero-rated under section 11A where supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed and the supply is not in connection with NZ land, NZ-situate tangible personal property, or otherwise received in NZ by an NZ-resident recipient. For a cross-border payment gateway service fee, zero-rating can apply when the merchant recipient is a non-resident outside NZ and the service is a section 11A qualifying supply; fees of a non-qualifying or financial-service-only character may be exempt rather than zero-rated.

  • New Zealand Customs export declaration (customs export entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing physical export from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or New Zealand bank foreign-currency credit advice for export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract/purchase order evidencing the foreign counter-party
  • Packing list and port/airport verification records (where goods are shipped)
  • For services: contract and evidence proving the recipient is a non-resident outside New Zealand at the time of performance

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital-stamping anti-fraud system. E-invoicing is voluntary and uses the PEPPOL-based New Zealand e-Invoicing network; where used, conformant interoperability standards apply. GST returns and payment summaries must be filed electronically via myIR for taxpayers registered for and using Inland Revenue's mandatory electronic filing channels (particularly large taxpayers).

New Zealand has no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds; the requirement is evidentiary. If Inland Revenue determines zero-rating is not substantiated, output GST at 15% is reassessed under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, with use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and up to 100% for evasion. Fraudulent tax evasion is a criminal offence under section 143 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, and the Commissioner may also issue an amended assessment clawing back the previously returned zero-rated supplies.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates an open and fully convertible currency regime. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely traded in global foreign exchange markets, and there are no surrender requirements, central bank exchange-control permits, foreign-exchange approval thresholds, or compulsory conversion ratios applicable to current-account trade flows, capital-account investment, dividends, interest, profit repatriation, or merchant settlement funding. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not ration foreign currency and would intervene only to smooth disorderly market conditions, not to impede convertibility. A cross-border payment gateway may therefore route USD, EUR, AUD, GBP, or NZD funding without needing a central bank licence solely to access FX. The practical compliance gatekeepers are the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, the FMA conduct and disclosure regime, and RBNZ prudential expectations for participant banks. Inbound share subscriptions, outbound beneficiary payments, and profit distributions are not subjected to pre-approval by the Reserve Bank, but each cross-border wire must carry a purpose classification acceptable to the reporting bank. Inland Revenue requires the entity to report taxable income in NZD using acceptable FX rates, and the bank may request quarterly foreign-direct-investment survey data. Overall, convertibility is effectively unlimited, with AML/KYC and corporate documentation forming the real treasury bottleneck.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules in New Zealand. Exporters and cross-border merchants may retain foreign currency receipts in offshore or onshore accounts indefinitely, convert them to NZD at any commercially determined rate, and use them for any lawful purpose. No mandatory conversion ratio or surrender timeline applies; the only imposed obligations are accurate tax accounting, AML/CFT record retention, and correct reporting of the underlying transaction purpose to the New Zealand bank for RBNZ statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), with the Financial Markets Authority for conduct and disclosure and the Department of Internal Affairs for AML/CFT supervisory roles across payment and remittance activities

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMI viability is high as a treasury overlay but not as a substitute for a licensed New Zealand bank. New Zealand does not issue a dedicated EMI licence comparable to the United Kingdom; instead, an offshore or local fintech must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register, comply with the AML/CFT Act 2009, and may handle payment processing, FX conversion, and multi-currency sub-ledgering. However, for NZD clearing and settlement through the Reserve Bank Exchange Settlement Account System, the entity must use one of the registered New Zealand banks as the underlying settlement partner. Lawful offshore EMIs are therefore viable for routing card transactions, vendor disbursements, and treasury reporting, but they cannot legally remove the NZ onshore banking relationship and cannot strip the surveillance architecture expected of a High-Risk PSP.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be routed via SWIFT MT103 using the bank's approved foreign-exchange purpose classification for inbound direct investment into New Zealand equity capital. The remitter must be the actual statutory shareholder; third-party wires are not acceptable without proof of agency and economic benefit. The bank will require the New Zealand Companies Office share allotment resolution, an updated share register, an external auditor or chartered accountant capital verification certificate, and a clear narrative in the wire such as Subscription for Ordinary Shares in NZ_LTD. Since no exchange control exists, funds may be credited to the Capital Escrow Account immediately, but disbursement from escrow requires signed bank-approved compliance clearing and matching of the source-of-funds evidence.Outbound trade and beneficiary disbursements should use SWIFT MT103 for standard cross-border credits, MT700 and MT710 series for commercial letters of credit, and MT400 and MT410 series for documentary collections. Local NZD legs clear through the Reserve Bank Exchange Settlement Account System using bank and branch account numbers; USD high-value legs may use Fedwire through the settlement bank's US routing, while Visa/Mastercard Direct is available for card acquiring and gateway settlement. Every message must include a valid purpose code, an exact NZD or foreign currency amount, and the beneficiary bank's BIC and account details. Turnover: A New Zealand company has a compulsory independent audit if it is (i) a large company under the Companies Act 1993 size tests applied over two consecutive prior accounting periods - broadly, at least two of: total assets greater than NZD 30 million, total revenue greater than NZD 60 million, or more than 50 full-time-equivalent employees; (ii) publicly accountable, an FMC reporting entity, an issuer, a manager of a registered managed investment scheme, a licensed insurer, a bank, or a non-bank deposit taker; (iii) otherwise required by a payment service provider licence, AML/CFT supervisor, or regulatory condition to have its financial statements or AML/CFT programme audited by an independent qualified CPA; or (iv) not able to access the small/medium company unanimous shareholder exemption. Even below the size tests, a licensed cross-border payment gateway should expect audit as a licence condition due to its banking/AML high-risk tier.
