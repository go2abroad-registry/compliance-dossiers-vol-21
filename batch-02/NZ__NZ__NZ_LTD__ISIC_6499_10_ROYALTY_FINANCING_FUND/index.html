UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act), as amended by the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Amendment Act 2023; Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism (Definitions) Regulations 2011; Companies Act 1993; Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013; Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008; United Nations Act 1946; Russia Sanctions Act 2022. New Zealand does not maintain a separate public beneficial ownership register; beneficial ownership information is captured through customer due diligence obligations under the AML/CFT Act and is accessible by the Financial Markets Authority, New Zealand Police Financial Intelligence Unit, Inland Revenue, and other designated supervisors. The PBOC/SAMR Order No. 3 Government_Only_Access regime is not applicable to a New Zealand domestic entity but the outcome of supervisory-only UBO access is substantively similar for AML/CFT purposes.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox may satisfy the Companies Act 1993 registered office requirement only if it is a physical street address in New Zealand, not a post office box, and has a person available to accept service of process during business hours. However, for an ISIC 6499 non-dilutive revenue-based financing entity operating at a Medium_Risk_Enhanced AML classification, a pure virtual office is commercially inadequate. New Zealand-registered banks will normally reject account-opening CDD if the registered office is only a mail-forwarding address without demonstrable management presence, board control, records, or operational substance. Inland Revenue and the Financial Markets Authority look to the principal place of business and economic substance, and may disregard the registered office if it is merely a letterbox on a shared service provider address. Therefore, the entity should maintain a substantive New Zealand office or a credible physical presence through a licensed chartered accountant or corporate services provider with genuine staff, records, and board activity.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital requirement under the Companies Act 1993. A company may incorporate with zero capital and no statutory escrow account is required. The application must state the number of shares being allotted and their nominal value, but payment may be deferred. Any financing received for share subscriptions may be held in a New Zealand bank account pending allotment, subject to general anti-money-laundering obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake), New Zealand — online portal myIR at www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned New Zealand limited company must first be incorporated with the New Zealand Companies Office, which issues a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). The company then applies to the Inland Revenue Department for a company IRD number (tax file number) using form IR596, attaching the NZBN, certificate of incorporation, registered office particulars and director details. IRD will register the company for income tax and, where annual taxable supplies are expected to exceed NZD 60,000 (or voluntarily below that threshold), for GST, issuing a separate 8-digit GST registration number. Non-resident shareholders and directors require no New Zealand physical presence; a registered agent and registered office in New Zealand suffice for registration purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a New Zealand resident limited company is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income at the 28% corporate rate, with an imputation credit system to relieve double taxation of distributed dividends. The entity is not pass-through eligible in the ordinary course; look-through company treatment is available only to closely held companies with five or fewer individuals or qualifying trusts as owners and is generally unavailable to a corporate-owned royalty financing fund. Non-resident shareholders are not taxed at entity level but are separately subject to Non-Resident Withholding Tax on dividends, interest and royalties sourced from New Zealand.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under New Zealand law. Available statutory elections and regimes include: (i) look-through company (LTC) election under Income Tax Act 2007 ss HB 1-HB 4 for closely held companies with qualifying individual/trust owners; (ii) consolidated group election for wholly owned corporate groups; (iii) functional currency election approved by IRD under s EC 6; (iv) GST payment-basis and six-monthly filing elections for qualifying taxpayers; and (v) mandatory rather than elective hybrid and branch mismatch adjustments under ss FH 1-FH 12 of the Income Tax Act 2007, which override contractual form where offshore jurisdictions produce deductible/non-includible or double-deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period, as measured on a prospective basis, is the mandatory registration threshold for GST. Non-resident suppliers of remote services (digital and intangibles) to New Zealand consumers must also register once their supplies to New Zealand non-business consumers exceed NZD 60,000 per annum, while business-to-business remote services are reverse-charged by the New Zealand recipient. Voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under ss 11 and 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Goods are zero-rated if physically exported from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply (or within an extended timeframe where goods are manufactured or assembled to order). Services are zero-rated under s 11A where supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not supplied in connection with land in New Zealand or goods in New Zealand for private use. Royalty financing and intangible licensing arrangements from a New Zealand fund to offshore licensees will generally qualify for zero-rating under s 11A where the place of supply is offshore and the recipient is a non-resident; the zero-rating claim must be supported by contractual proof of non-resident status and evidence that the services are performed outside New Zealand. Remote services supplied to a non-resident GST-registered business are also zero-rated on a B2B basis under s 8(4B), with the offshore recipient accountable for local reverse-charge obligations.

  • Customs Export Declaration — New Zealand Export Airway Bill / Sea Cargo Manifest clearance via the New Zealand Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and GST tax invoice showing the supply as zero-rated (GST-free)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-confirmed foreign exchange settlement statement evidencing that export proceeds have been recovered to New Zealand
  • Non-resident declaration and executed contract (for services/intangibles) evidencing offshore place of supply and offshore performance

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API pre-clearance system for invoices. e-Invoicing is voluntary and interoperable through the Peppol network. GST returns (GST-101) must be filed electronically through myIR on a one-month, two-month, or six-month cycle depending on turnover and registration election. The Inland Revenue Department requires taxpayers to maintain complete tax invoice records, debit notes, credit notes and audit trails, but does not perform transactional validation or live clearance; enforcement is retrospective through information requests, audits and non-filing assessments.

The Inland Revenue Department applies shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. Export zero-rating claims that fail the statutory evidentiary or timing requirements are recharacterized as taxable supplies, causing GST to be clawed back with use-of-money interest accruing from the original return due date. Knowingly false export documentation or fraudulent zero-rating claims attract criminal prosecution, a false-payment penalty under s 141EB, and joint and several liability for directors. Transfer pricing and general anti-avoidance rules under ss GA 1 and GC 1 may also reprice or reconstruct royalty and financing arrangements, generating additional GST, income tax and non-resident withholding tax exposure.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully convertible currency under a free-floating exchange rate regime. Both capital and current account transactions are generally unrestricted for non-residents, subject to notification or screening requirements only in limited circumstances such as large inward direct investments under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 (Overseas Investment Office screening thresholds). Profit remittances, including dividends, royalties, interest, and management fees, may be freely repatriated provided the underlying transactions are at arm's length and comply with New Zealand tax laws, including the approved issuer levy (AIL) regime for offshore related-party interest payments and non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) obligations on royalties and dividends. No express profit remission approval is required from the Reserve Bank, but banking counterparties will require standard tax residency declarations and a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) for entity verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no fixed percentage conversion obligation. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained in offshore bank accounts by New Zealand residents, provided any foreign currency balances are disclosed for tax purposes and attributable to the New Zealand entity in accordance with the Income Tax Act 2007's attributed foreign income rules. For a revenue-based financing fund structured as a New Zealand limited company, foreign currency receivables must be translated into New Zealand dollars for financial reporting under NZ IFRS, and any exchange gains or losses are recognized for corporate income tax purposes; no surrender or repatriation timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Limited. New Zealand has not enacted a localized electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime comparable to the EU's PSD2 EMI passport; overseas digital EMIs are not permitted to provide deposit-taking or payment services in New Zealand without a registered bank license under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989 or an approved non-bank deposit taker status under the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013 (unless operating under a recognized offshore banking license with a New Zealand Financial Service Provider (FSP) registration and licensed under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 for specific managed fund or crowdfunding services). An offshore EMI may be used only for non-NZ-domiciled treasury functions (e.g., collecting UK/EU inbound funds), but must not hold New Zealand-origin investor capital or effect domestic payments in NZD without local authorization. The primary compliant route is to partner with a licensed New Zealand bank or a registered non-bank deposit taker for local clearing, and use an offshore EMI solely as a foreign-currency collection wallet with full sub-ledger reconciliation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital routing should be executed via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's corporate bank account in the source jurisdiction to the NZ entity's NZD or USD operational account held at a tier-1 New Zealand bank. The remitter must state: (1) a clear purpose code, e.g., 'Financing of revenue-based financing operations' or 'Non-resident direct investment - subscription of capital' (for equity) or 'Intercompany loan' (if debt); (2) a precise corporate payment narrative referencing the investor's legal name, the NZ entity's NZBN, and the underlying agreement reference; (3) the tax treatment identifier if available (e.g., 'n/a' or 'not a personal payment'). New Zealand does not operate a purpose-code approval regime, but banks apply their own screening under AML/CFT; the funds must originate from a bank account in the investor's own name (third-party payments are a critical blocker). For equity injections, the NZ company must file an Overseas Investment Act notification if the investment confers 25% or more ownership rights in sensitive assets, or if the investor is a government-related foreign person. All documentation supporting the injection (signed subscription/loan agreement, board minutes, valuation, and source-of-wealth evidence) must be provided to the receiving bank; each inbound transfer of NZD 10,000 or above is subject to enhanced verification and recorded for CRS/FATCA reporting. If the entity is a close company, a Director's Resolution and updated shareholders' register must be lodged with the New Zealand Companies Office within 20 working days.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements and revenue-share distributions must be executed through: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct foreign-currency wires to overseas beneficiaries; (2) domestic NZD clearing via the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) using Bulk Electronic Clearing System (BECS) direct credits; (3) commercial letters of credit (LC) issued by the NZ bank in favor of overseas suppliers under ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600), with full compliance checked by the bank's trade finance desk; and (4) cross-border ACH-style payment platforms (e.g., Trans-Tasman Direct Entry, or Same-Day USD via correspondent banking). Each outward MT103 must include the correct ISAE/LT purpose code (e.g., 'Royalty payments', 'Financing services fees', 'Loan principal repayment'), reference the underlying agreement, and be accompanied by supporting invoices or statements. New Zealand imposes no exchange controls on outward payments, but banks apply self-regulatory FX and AML filters; payments to sanctioned jurisdictions, shell banks, or crypto-asset wallets will be rejected. For related-party outbound royalties or interest, the payer must withhold NRWT (15% standard, reduced under applicable DTT or 2% AIL for interest with an approved issuer levy election) and remit the withholding to Inland Revenue within the statutory deadline; wire narratives must reference the NRWT payment reference. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Limited. New Zealand has not enacted a localized electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime comparable to the EU's PSD2 EMI passport; overseas digital EMIs are not permitted to provide deposit-taking or payment services in New Zealand without a registered bank license under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989 or an approved non-bank deposit taker status under the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013 (unless operating under a recognized offshore banking license with a New Zealand Financial Service Provider (FSP) registration and licensed under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 for specific managed fund or crowdfunding services). An offshore EMI may be used only for non-NZ-domiciled treasury functions (e.g., collecting UK/EU inbound funds), but must not hold New Zealand-origin investor capital or effect domestic payments in NZD without local authorization. The primary compliant route is to partner with a licensed New Zealand bank or a registered non-bank deposit taker for local clearing, and use an offshore EMI solely as a foreign-currency collection wallet with full sub-ledger reconciliation.
