Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand law under the Companies Act 1993 imposes no statutory minimum paid-up share capital for a limited company. The board may issue share capital in any amount determined at subscription and recorded in the share register; no blocked escrow account is required by law. Non-resident shareholders and directors are permitted, but the company must maintain a physical registered office and an address for service in New Zealand.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and Emergency New Zealand approval of the fire evacuation scheme and supplementary Fire Safety Certificate for all premises; Building Act 2004 Building Warrant of Fitness for the main warehouse; structural load inspection for mezzanines, racking and vehicle docks; WorkSafe New Zealand notification for dangerous goods and hazardous substances storage; occupational clearance requiring certified forklift operators, site traffic management plan, and first aid and emergency response procedures under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; online electronic portal: myIR (ird.govt.nz); company incorporation is administered separately by the New Zealand Companies Office (companiesoffice.govt.nz).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity carrying on business in New Zealand must first register as an overseas company with the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993 if it has a physical presence, branch, or place of business in New Zealand. Following registration, the entity must apply to the Inland Revenue Department for a New Zealand IRD number through myIR or the prescribed paper registration process for non-individual entities. The IRD number is the sole statutory Tax Identification Number and simultaneously serves as the GST registration number; New Zealand maintains no separate VAT ID. The application must provide the entity's registered office or New Zealand agent details, director and ultimate beneficial owner identification, constitutional documents, the overseas jurisdiction of tax residence, and an estimate of expected New Zealand taxable supplies. The applicant must register for GST before commencing taxable activity or within the statutory time frame after the NZD 60,000 taxable supply threshold is exceeded. Inland Revenue issues the IRD number by myIR correspondence, after which the entity can file income tax, GST, and employer obligations under that single identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity classification election. A qualifying closely held New Zealand limited company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under the Income Tax Act 2007, which recharacterises certain income and deductions for income tax purposes, but this is not a hybrid mismatch election. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are counteracted by statute and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period, including supplies of remote services by non-resident suppliers to New Zealand consumers; registration becomes compulsory once the threshold is exceeded. Exported goods and international transport supplies are taxable supplies for threshold computation despite being zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a GST-registered supplier zero-rates the export of tangible goods where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand and retains statutory documentary evidence. International transport of goods and services supplied directly in connection with goods being exported from New Zealand, including express courier and freight services, are zero-rated. Services supplied to a non-resident recipient outside New Zealand and not performed in New Zealand may also qualify. The supplier must hold and preserve sufficient commercial, customs, transport, and banking evidence to satisfy the Inland Revenue Department that the supply was genuinely exported and paid for by foreign counterparties.

  • Customs export declaration lodged with the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window (TSW), including the unique declaration identifier and export tariff classification
  • Original or authenticated bill of lading for sea freight or air waybill for air freight issued by the carrying carrier for the outbound shipment
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the exporter, importer, goods description, value, and destination
  • Outbound courier shipment manifest or master consignment note evidencing physical departure of the consignment from New Zealand
  • Bank foreign exchange remittance advice (SWIFT MT103, exchange settlement record, or equivalent bank statement entry) evidencing receipt of foreign currency into the New Zealand bank account of the GST-registered exporter within the repatriation window
  • GST export substantiation file signed by the responsible officer, reconciling the customs declaration, transport documentation, commercial invoice, and foreign currency receipt

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or central electronic invoice stamping regime is operated by Inland Revenue. There is no lawful pre-clearance API requirement for ordinary B2B GST invoices. E-invoicing via the Peppol network is optional and primarily used for business-to-government procurement; Inland Revenue does not require transaction-level invoice clearance for GST purposes. Anti-fraud digital stamping does not exist in New Zealand.

Inland Revenue audits export zero-rating through customs movement data, bank records, and GST return data. If zero-rating is not substantiated by the required export and foreign currency receipt evidence, the supply is retrospectively clawed back to standard 15% GST, with interest and shortfall penalties assessed under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Penalty tiers for tax shortfalls are: unacceptable tax position 20%, lack of reasonable care 20%, gross carelessness 40%, abusive tax position 100%, and evasion or fraudulent conduct 150%. Late payment interest (use-of-money interest) accrues from the original due date. Criminal liability may also be imposed in cases of deliberate false export documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a freely floating foreign exchange regime administered by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ). There are no general exchange controls on current-account transactions: trade-related payments, service fees, dividends, interest, and royalties can be converted and remitted without prior RBNZ approval. The capital account is also broadly open for non-resident shareholders establishing a limited company; however, overseas investment in sensitive land, fishing quota, or significant business assets may trigger screening under the Overseas Investment Act. A cross-border express courier undertaking with no sensitive land interest is treated as an ordinary services business. The NZD is fully deliverable in both onshore and offshore markets; statutory surrender requirements do not exist. Dividends to foreign shareholders may be remitted after Board approval, subject to the company being able to satisfy the solvency test and after New Zealand non-resident withholding tax, resident withholding tax on interest, and any approved issuer levy consequences. Consequently, the practical hurdles are KYC/AML verification and tax obligations, not FX convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio applies in New Zealand. The RBNZ does not require export receipts to be surrendered or converted. A cross-border courier may hold foreign currency receipts indefinitely in a registered bank's foreign currency account, or convert them at the prevailing commercial rate. The only obligations are reporting under the AML/CFT Act 2009 for unusual transactions and translating foreign currency income to NZD for tax purposes at the RBNZ daily exchange rate.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI viability is Medium. Fintech EMIs are not legally barred from providing payment services to a New Zealand entity, but they are not RBNZ-registered banks and cannot offer full domestic NZD clearing, capital escrow, or lending. Any EMI must be registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register, appoint a compliant AML/CFT supervisor, and support the travel rule before serving a cross-border courier. For the ISIC 5320 model, an EMI may be used as a secondary collections rail for Card-POS or small ACH-Direct receipts, but the primary corporate treasury account and all customs duty settlements should sit with a Tier-1 New Zealand bank to avoid settlement risk and regulatory friction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident equity injections should be sent as an SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own bank, denominated in NZD or USD, to the company's Capital Escrow Account at the Tier-1 bank. The remittance should include the corporate legal name and the reference Equity Subscription for NZ Ltd. Use the bank's balance-of-payments purpose code for Direct Investment - Equity Capital; for trade payments use Other Business Services where applicable. The bank will source funds from the named beneficial owner, not from a third-party aggregator. The company must file the share allotment with the Companies Office, and the bank may require an independent certified public accountant's capital verification report before allowing the funds to be released to the operating account. No RBNZ authorisation is required for non-sensitive courier activities, but the payment will be reported as a direct investment for statistical purposes.Outbound cash management for the courier risk should prioritise SWIFT MT103 for agent settlements, customs duty prepayments, and high-value gateway invoices; the remitting bank should see a tax invoice or waybill identification. For recurring receivables and settlements, Card-POS and ACH Direct rails should be channeled through an NZD operation account and swept daily into an interest-bearing multi-currency account. Commercial Letters of Credit and documentary collections are acceptable only for capital purchases such as sorter machinery, delivery vehicles, or aircraft, and are not appropriate for high-volume parcel freight; for parcel-generated trade, MT103 with standardised corporate-to-bank API integration provides faster clearing and better audit trails.Onshore NZD/USD spot and forward contracts with a Tier-1 RBNZ-registered bank, limited to 12-month maturities matching forecast operational cashflows and signed under an ISDA or bank master treasury agreement.Cross-currency interest rate swaps between NZD and USD, EUR, or AUD for capital financing of aircraft, vehicles, and sorting equipment, subject to ISDA 2002 documentation, credit support annex, and board-approved treasury policy.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in offshore USD/CNH only when hedging CNY-denominated freight payables through a Chinese gateway; plain NZD NDFs are disfavoured because the NZD is freely deliverable onshore and a deliverable forward is more transparent.FX options and barrier strategies on USD/NZD with a bank-approved underlying exposure, permitted only for hedging committed courier revenues or duty payables and explicitly prohibited for speculative proprietary trading.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on gross dividends paid by a New Zealand company to non-resident shareholders. The rate is reduced to the fully imputed dividend rate of 28% where the dividend is fully imputed and paid to a resident of a jurisdiction with which New Zealand has an applicable DTA and certain conditions are met. For a wholly-owned non-resident subsidiary claiming treaty relief, the dividend rate under many New Zealand DTAs is 15% (or 0% where a 10%+ shareholding and head-office is in the treaty country exists).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$1,000,000 aggregate cross-border intercompany transactions per income year for goods, services, financial arrangements, and intangibles. For intercompany loans, documentation is required where the aggregate principal advanced to New Zealand borrowers exceeds NZ$1,000,000. A Master File and Local File must be maintained when the multinational group has NZ related-party transactions above this threshold, and the taxpayer must file the Inland Revenue International Questionnaire (IR395) when requested.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulation code (like UAE or BVI), but the substance doctrine is embedded in general anti-avoidance rules (Income Tax Act 2007, Part G), transfer pricing jurisprudence, and anti-conduit (ss GC 6-15) provisions. For a non-resident express courier company, economic substance in New Zealand is substantiated by actual deployment of key entrepreneurial functions (timed logistics management, crew and vehicle use, customs brokerage) that generate the core income-producing activity (CIGA) in New Zealand. Without real CIGA in New Zealand, profits cannot be attributed to a New Zealand permanent establishment and tax planning structures are exposed to challenge under the OECD BEPS Action 7 (PE avoidance) and NZ's equivalent rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Intentional Under-Reporting of Cross-Border Earnings

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act 1994 (TAA), evasion-like conduct triggers a shortfall penalty of 150% of the assessed tax shortfall (s 141D), plus use-of-money interest under s 120, and criminal prosecution under s 143C for wilful evasion and s 143B for knowingly providing false returns. The maximum criminal fine for a company is NZ$50,000 and for a director NZ$25,000; imprisonment for up to 5 years is possible for individuals.

Director personal liability: A director who participates in the tax evasion may have the corporate veil pierced under the Companies Act 1993 at s 300 (trading with intent to defraud creditors) and be personally liable for the company's unpaid taxes. Inland Revenue can recover the tax debt personally from the director, commence civil debt recovery, and issue a departure prohibition notice (travel ban) under s 157B of the TAA where the director has outstanding tax liabilities. Intent as breach of duty can also lead to disqualification from managing companies for up to 5 years and asset restraining orders under criminal proceeds legislation.
Abusive Transfer Pricing and Fabricated Intercompany Charges

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustments are enforced under Income Tax Act 2007, Part GC (ss GC 1-GC 24), allowing Inland Revenue to determine arm's length consideration and re-figure taxable income. A shortfall penalty of 100% under TAA s 141C applies for taking an 'abusive tax position' (dominant purpose of avoiding tax, less than 40% chance of success). Failure to provide documentation triggers a separate penalty of NZ$500 plus further daily penalties, and the tax shortfall penalty can be increased to 150% if evasion is proven.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held jointly and severally liable for transfer-pricing shortfall penalties where they signed or approved the tax return or knowingly allowed the arrangement to proceed. Inland Revenue's '1% de minimis’ does not apply. Civil asset recovery under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009 may be used to confiscate the director's personal assets if derived from the offending arrangement. A director may be prohibited from leaving New Zealand under TAA s 157B if the company's unpaid tax exceeds NZ$10,000. Criminal negligence causing the company to trade with intent to defraud creditors makes a director personally liable for all company debts under the Companies Act 1993, s 300.
AML/CFT Violations and Corporate Veil Misuse (Shell Company / Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Under the AML/CFT Act, ss 76-77 and s 95, a business that accepts funds as a money service business or remittance provider without registration and supervision commits an offence punishable by a fine up to NZ$2,000,000 for a company and NZ$300,000 for an individual officer. Failure to conduct customer due diligence and report suspicious transactions attracts further fines of up to NZ$200,000. Under the Crimes Act 1961, s 243, money laundering is a crime punishable by imprisonment of up to 7 years (NZ$20,000 fine); under high-value laundering, up to 14 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: A director is deemed a 'senior manager' under the AML/CFT Act and can be personally prosecuted if the company's AML program is deficient. The corporate veil will be pierced where the company is used as a sham to launder funds—the director becomes personally liable to compensate victims, and assets held in the director's name are subject to forfeiture. Courts may issue travel restrictions, revoke the director's ability to manage a company, and order civil asset restitution. The maximum personal fine is NZ$300,000 and imprisonment for up to 14 years for willful blindness or active participation in laundering.
