Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a New Zealand limited liability company. Shares may be issued for nil par value consideration; capital amounts are determined by the directors and recorded in the company register. Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake) — administered through the myIR online portal at https://www.ird.govt.nz.

E-government filing portal

A New Zealand limited company is first incorporated in the Companies Office, which issues the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) and normally auto-populates the Inland Revenue number through the integrated Companies Office/IRD business transformation registration process. If the IRD number is not automatically issued, the company must submit Form IRD 596 (Application for an IRD number for a New Zealand company) or register via myIR. Foreign beneficial owners require a New Zealand registered office and resident agent address for Companies Office purposes; the same address is used for IRD correspondence. Following IRD number allocation, the company must separately register for GST in myIR (or via the GST 2A application) where annual taxable supplies are expected to exceed NZD 60,000, or elect voluntary registration. The EMI must also obtain the applicable financial services licensing acknowledgement from the Reserve Bank of New Zealand before operating, although this is not a tax registration step.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — New Zealand resident limited companies are taxed at the entity level at 28% corporate income tax on worldwide income; tax losses may be carried forward and imputation credits attach to dividends. A non-resident owned NZ Ltd remains an entity-level taxpayer; distributions to non-residents may attract NRWT or be made under an approved issuer levy. No default flow-through treatment exists, although an eligible closely held company may elect Look-Through Company status.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not offer a full check-the-box election. Qualifying closely held companies (five or fewer owners and satisfying statutory restrictions) may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under the Income Tax Act 2007, causing income, losses, and credits to be attributed to owners. For cross-border hybrid structures, New Zealand provides the foreign hybrid entity classification regime at sections FA 2-FA 10 and related hybrid mismatch election and notification protocols that permit alignment of entity treatment with foreign jurisdictions. Portfolio Investment Entity (PIE) elections are available only for qualifying managed investment vehicles and are not relevant to a standard trading EMI subsidiary.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; compulsory registration applies once this turnover threshold is reached or exceeded. Voluntary registration is available below the threshold. Financial supplies such as electronic money and payment services are generally exempt from GST, so an EMI that makes only exempt financial supplies is not required to register for GST; however, registration may be elected to recover input tax where the entity also makes zero-rated exported financial services to non-residents.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for goods under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 requires that the supplier physically exports the goods from New Zealand, usually evidenced by a New Zealand Customs export entry and commercial transport documentation. Zero-rating for offshore services under section 11A is available when the services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are performed outside New Zealand, subject to exceptions where the services are for the direct benefit of an NZ resident. For an Electronic Money Institution (EMI), virtual IBAN issuance, digital wallet reserve management, and payment processing supplied to non-resident corporate clients are zero-rated as offshore financial services only if the recipient is non-resident, the services do not directly benefit a New Zealand resident, and the arrangement is not a GST-avoidance scheme. The EMI must maintain contemporaneous evidence of customer residence status, the offshore purpose of the supply, and the non-NZ deployment of the financial service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Customs Service electronic Export Entry filed through the Trade Single Window and cleared at the port of export)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or other contract of carriage demonstrating physical export from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank SWIFT/MT103 payment evidence for export proceeds where the 180-day FX repatriation directive applies
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice clearly describing the exported goods or offshore financial services and the zero-rating basis
  • Packing list, container/serial numbers, courier delivery confirmation, and foreign customs arrival evidence
  • Section 11A non-resident declaration forms, customer residence certificates, and officer sign-off for zero-rated financial services supplied to non-residents

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping regime applies in New Zealand. New Zealand has adopted the international PEPPOL e-invoicing framework; from 1 April 2022, central government agencies were required to be able to receive electronic invoices using PEPPOL BIS 3.0, and suppliers to government may be contractually obliged to send e-invoices through a PEPPOL access point. There is no IRD-operated invoice clearance API and no mandate for private-sector e-invoicing, although voluntary e-invoicing is supported by the IRD.

Inland Revenue rigorously scrutinises export zero-rating claims, particularly for financial services supplied to non-residents, requiring the EMI to hold full contemporaneous documentation. If a zero-rating position is reversed, the company becomes retrospectively liable for the GST shortfall, together with use-of-money interest from the original due date. Civil shortfall penalties range from 20% for lack of reasonable care to 150% for intentional evasion or hiding of documents, and late payment interest compounds. Fraudulent export zero-rating or intentional false statements constitute tax evasion offences under sections 143B and 143C of the Tax Administration Act 1994, carrying fines of up to NZD 75,000 for individuals (up to NZD 150,000 for a body corporate) and imprisonment of up to five years for natural persons. Directors and officers who knowingly participate may also face personal liability and disqualification-type enforcement under the Companies Act 1993 and, for licensed EMIs, additional regulatory enforcement by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand Dollar (NZD) is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. The capital account is liberalized subject to the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which requires consent for non-resident acquisitions of sensitive land, fishing quota, or significant business assets (generally NZD 100 million or more). Profit remittances do not require central bank approval; however, dividend distributions to non-residents are subject to non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at the treaty applicable rate, and banks may request an IRD certificate or tax clearance for exceptionally large outbound remittances as part of enhanced due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or repatriation timeline exists for export receipts in New Zealand. Exporters and digital wallet operators may retain sale proceeds in any freely convertible foreign currency indefinitely. There is no mandatory conversion ratio. If proceeds are credited to a New Zealand bank account, standard AML/CFT monitoring and IRD reporting obligations apply, and conversion to NZD occurs at prevailing market rates through the wholesale interbank market at no central bank direction.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — acting as the central bank and operator of the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS), while the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) administers foreign direct investment screening and the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) enforces tax-related remittance compliance; there is no distinct foreign exchange administration office in New Zealand.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium — Offshore digital EMIs may operate in New Zealand as registered Money Remitters under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, but they cannot hold NZD-denominated client funds directly and must partner with a locally licensed bank. Such partnerships are feasible but carry heavy compliance requirements, including AML/CFT Act 2009 obligations, restrictions on providing interest-bearing services, and enhanced scrutiny on 'money mule' systems. Viability is stronger for EMIs with a proven licence in an equivalent jurisdiction and a valid registration on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for large corporate transfers denominated in NZD, USD, or CNY, with purpose codes explicitly detailing 'Capital Injection' or 'Equity Subscription' for foreign direct investment. The receiving New Zealand settlement bank clears the funds via the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) operated by the Reserve Bank, or through a Foreign Currency Settlement Account (FCSA) for non-NZD liquidity. Verification entails SWIFT FIN MT103 acknowledgement (or MT103 RETURN/REJECT acknowledgment), the transacting bank's BIC and the recalling of wire-trace documentation. For a licensed EMI, investor capital must be locked in a capital escrow account until evidence of VAT/IRD classification, source-of-funds diligence and corporate extraction ratification is filed.Outbound trade and settlement disbursements fire over SWIFT MT103 for high-speed correspondent transfers, using the SWIFT gpi tracking service for end-to-end status monitoring. For documentary trade settlement, a Commercial Letter of Credit is issued via an MT700 series to the beneficiary bank and settled through MT780/MT756. Domestically, New Zealand-dollar clearances run over the same ESAS rails for real-time gross settlement (RTGS), while SEPACredit payments are bridged through a participating EEA partner bank that hosts a virtual IBAN for recurring 'pull' disbursements. The treasury suite reserves MT103 as the default rail because it preserves full traceability and meets the AML/CFT 'money trail' expectation of NZ banks.Traded OTC NZD/USD and NZD/AUD FX forwards with tenors up to 12 months, subject to documented underlying treasury exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) covering CNY, INR, IDR and other soft or restricted-convertibility currencies, settled against an independent rate fix and used strictly for hedging export or card-network receivables.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS) with 6 to 36 month maturity, used to align mismatched asset-and-liability currency bases and to manage balance-sheet interest-rate risk.Foreign exchange swaps (FX swaps) transacted with licensed New Zealand registered banks for short-term liquidity management across the settlement date.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For the standard tax year ending 31 March YYYY: return due 7 July YYYY+1 (non-agent) or 31 March YYYY+2 (registered tax agent). Terminal tax due 7 February YYYY+2 (non-agent) or 7 April YYYY+2 (tax agent). Provisional tax instalments fall due on 7 May YYYY, 7 September YYYY and 7 January YYYY+1, unless the GST-ratio or AIM payment option is adopted. Substituted balance dates shift each due date proportionately.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 / Line 1 Total income: gross assessable income of the company for the income year, including EMI service fees, interchange/network fees, float investment interest and foreign-sourced income attributable to the NZ business. Include only income derived from sources in New Zealand or from a business carried on through a fixed establishment/PE in New Zealand; income subject to final NRWT (interest/dividends/royalties) is generally excluded from the IR4 income computation. Late filing: NZD 250 late-filing penalty under s 139A Tax Administration Act 1994, increased by NZD 250 for each whole month or part month the IR4 remains unfiled. Late payment: 1% late-payment penalty immediately on the due date, a further 4% if unpaid 7 days after the due date, and 1% per month thereafter (s 139B TAA). Use-of-money interest under s 120 TAA accrues daily on unpaid terminal/provisional tax at the prescribed rate (underpayment rate approximately 10-11% p.a. in the 2025 rate cycle). Evasion offences under s 143B expose the company and officers to a fine of up to NZD 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Box 2 / Line 2 Total expenses: deductible expenditure incurred in producing assessable income (s DA 1), excluding capital items, non-deductible entertainment, fines, and expenses of exempt income. Deduct only expenses that are both incurred in deriving the NZ assessable income and consistent with arm's-length transfer pricing; head-office overhead allocations must be supported by a documented cost-pool allocation. Box 3 / Line 3 Net income or loss: Box 1 less Box 2, before deduction of tax losses and before offset of subvention payments. If Box 3 is a loss, the non-resident must still file the return when required; the loss may be carried forward only if the NZ-resident continuity rules and s IA 5 requirements are satisfied. Box 4 / Line 4 Tax losses carried forward and deducted: quantum of previously verified net losses offset against current-year net income (subject to the 49% continuity test and the business match test). For a non-resident company, losses are recognised only if derived through the NZ PE and can only be used in subsequent NZ PE years; the same continuity of ownership in the loss company must be maintained. Late filing: NZD 250 late-filing penalty under s 139A Tax Administration Act 1994, increased by NZD 250 for each whole month or part month the IR4 remains unfiled. Late payment: 1% late-payment penalty immediately on the due date, a further 4% if unpaid 7 days after the due date, and 1% per month thereafter (s 139B TAA). Evasion offences under s 143B expose the company and officers to a fine of up to NZD 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Box 5 / Line 5 Net taxable income: Box 3 minus Box 4; this is the amount to which the 28% company rate is applied. Tax is charged on NZ-sourced PE profit at 28%; a non-resident company is not taxed in NZ on non-NZ-sourced income unless it is attributable to the PE. Late filing: NZD 250 late-filing penalty under s 139A Tax Administration Act 1994, increased by NZD 250 for each whole month or part month the IR4 remains unfiled. Late payment: 1% late-payment penalty immediately on the due date, a further 4% if unpaid 7 days after the due date, and 1% per month thereafter (s 139B TAA). Box 6 / Line 6 Income tax payable on net taxable income (28%, subject to any rebate or special rate). If the company's only NZ income is passive (dividends, interest, royalties), the IR4 is not normally used — such income is subject to final NRWT and the company is not required to file an IR4 unless it has a PE. Late filing: NZD 250 late-filing penalty under s 139A Tax Administration Act 1994, increased by NZD 250 for each whole month or part month the IR4 remains unfiled. Box 7 / Line 7 Tax credits: provisional tax paid, resident withholding tax (RWT) credits, foreign tax credits, and imputation credits attached to dividends received. Non-resident companies may credit NRWT/AIL paid on NZ-source passive income against income tax only if such income is progressively assessed; otherwise the NRWT is final and no credit is claimable. Late filing: NZD 250 late-filing penalty under s 139A Tax Administration Act 1994, increased by NZD 250 for each whole month or part month the IR4 remains unfiled. Box 8 / Line 8 Residual tax due or refund: Box 6 minus Box 7; if positive, terminal tax is payable by the statutory terminal tax date; if negative, a refund is processed after the annual return is filed. Any residual refund is generally paid only to the NZ bank account of the company; refunds to non-resident companies require provision of a TRC and, for treaty purposes, confirmation of beneficial ownership. Line 9 / ICA Schedule Imputation credit account (ICA): opening balance, credits (income tax paid), debits (dividends credited), and closing debit balance per the company's imputation return (IR4J where separate format applies). Imputation credits attached to a dividend paid to a non-resident shareholder do not reduce NRWT; they may form part of the NRWT gross-up base, so the ICA reconciliation must distinguish resident vs non-resident dividend payments.
