Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; secure online taxpayer portal: myIR at https://myir.ird.govt.nz. Company incorporation and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) are administered by the Companies Office within the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE); export clearance and customs declarations are administered by the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window (TSW).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident investor forming a New Zealand limited company: (1) Incorporate the NZ_LTD entity through the Companies Office online register; the resulting NZBN is the master business identifier and Companies Office data is shared with Inland Revenue, which issues the company's unique IRD number for income tax and Goods and Services Tax (GST) registration. (2) If the foreign entity instead operates as an offshore non-resident company without New Zealand incorporation, apply directly to Inland Revenue for an IRD number through myIR using the non-resident company application route; provide certified constitutional documents, ultimate beneficial ownership information, registered office or service agent details, and a New Zealand address for service, and appoint a New Zealand tax agent if there is no physical presence. (3) To become GST-registered, use the GST registration function in myIR after the IRD number is issued; the applicant must certify that it carries on or intends to carry on a taxable activity in New Zealand and must satisfy the NZD 60,000 registration threshold, or register voluntarily. Provide New Zealand bank account details for GST refunds; a non-resident entity lacking an NZ bank account should arrange a designated account or tax agent trust account for refund purposes. (4) If goods are to be exported, obtain a Customs client code from the New Zealand Customs Service and lodge export entries through TSW. (5) Maintain all documentary evidence in English for IRD and Customs inspection.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand has no check-the-box regime. A standard NZ_LTD is taxed as a separate entity at the corporate income tax rate and may be eligible, if closely held (five or fewer shareholders and no corporate shareholder), to elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status through Inland Revenue; an LTC election makes income tax transparent but is not the default. No elective hybrid-mismatch election exists; New Zealand's hybrid and branch mismatch rules are mandatory anti-avoidance provisions under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2019.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in New Zealand in any 12-month period, or expected taxable supplies in the next 12 months; the same NZD 60,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of remote digital services and low-value imported goods selling into New Zealand consumers. Registration below the threshold is voluntary but becomes mandatory once the threshold is exceeded or is likely to be exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supply is an exported good or a qualifying offshore service. Goods must be exported from New Zealand by the supplier in the course of the supplier's taxable activity, or supplied for removal to an outside destination, and must leave New Zealand substantively unchanged within the applicable statutory timeframe. Qualifying offshore digital services, including the vertical ERP CRM cloud software subscriptions in scope, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, and the services have no direct connection with New Zealand land, goods, or a person present in New Zealand and are not consumed in New Zealand. The supplier should retain written contracts, orders, IP access records, and billing showing the offshore customer, overseas contract jurisdiction, delivery of access outside New Zealand, and GST-exclusive export invoices.

  • New Zealand Customs Service export entry (export declaration) lodged through the Trade Single Window and approved by Customs; this statutory export-clearance record is the functional equivalent of a Bill of Entry, which is not a New Zealand statutory document
  • Bill of Lading for sea cargo or air waybill/master air waybill for air cargo evidencing physical export from New Zealand, endorsed by the carrier, together with commercial invoice, packing list, and Customs-approved commodity/valuation data
  • Bank statement or SWIFT payment confirmation evidencing receipt of the foreign-currency or New Zealand-dollar remittance from the offshore buyer into the New Zealand entity's bank account, together with the underlying sales contract and records required by the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009; New Zealand does not issue an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), and the bank payment record is the accepted foreign-exchange repatriation evidence

New Zealand does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, anti-fraud digital stamping, or pre-clearance of invoices with Inland Revenue. GST-registered suppliers must issue tax invoices containing the prescribed particulars (supplier name, GST registration number, invoice date, supplier and recipient details, description, quantity and amount including GST) and retain them for the statutory record-keeping period. E-invoicing is voluntary and implemented under the PEPPOL framework (PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0); certified PEPPOL access points are used for interoperability with government and trading partners. There is no API-based fiscal pre-clearance obligation for ordinary B2B supplies.

New Zealand legislation does not impose a fixed 180-day export-proceeds repatriation deadline; the 180-day value in this dossier is the audit-readiness benchmark, and longer deferrals require documented commercial justification. Inland Revenue may challenge export zero-rating if the documentation bundle is incomplete or inconsistent, or if the goods or services are later returned to, restored to, or consumed in New Zealand. Retrospective GST clawback arises if physical export or qualifying offshore service is not substantiated: the supply is reassessed at 15% GST and input credits previously allowed on related costs may be adjusted. Penalties include GST shortfall, use-of-money interest from the original due date, late-filing penalties, graduated shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 from failure to take reasonable care through abusive tax positions and evasion, and criminal prosecution for intentionally false documents or fraudulent zero-rating claims. The standard four-year reassessment period applies to non-fraudulent supplies, with no statutory time bar for fraud or evasion.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a free-floating and fully convertible currency regime. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely traded in global markets, with no foreign exchange controls, capital account restrictions, or exchange surrender requirements imposed by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand. Current account transactions such as software export receipts, dividends, royalties, and service fees may be settled freely in NZD or any convertible currency. Capital account transactions, including direct equity investment, internal corporate loans, and repatriation of invested capital, are likewise unrestricted and do not require Exchange Control approval. The principal gateways are bank-level AML/CFT and sanctions due diligence, any applicable tax clearance for dividends or interest payments under New Zealand tax law, and the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which may screen certain non-resident acquisitions of significant business assets or farm land; a vertical SaaS company selling software services does not ordinarily trigger that regime. Profit remittance can be executed by instructing the bank to convert NZD to the target currency at a wholesale rate and issue a SWIFT MT103, or by remitting directly in NZD where the beneficiary can accept that currency. There is no prescription requiring a minimum local holding period, no forced conversion, and no black-market premium because official and market rates are identical. The real-time settlement infrastructure for NZD is the Exchange Settlement Account System operated by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand, while cross-border settlements clear through correspondent banks and SWIFT. In practice, the only hurdles are documentary: source-of-funds evidence, clean corporate ownership, and alignment with the bank's risk appetite.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand does not impose statutory timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export foreign currency receipts. Exporters may maintain foreign currency bank accounts offshore or with domestic banks, retain export proceeds indefinitely, and buy or sell NZD freely. For tax, exporters report accrual income in NZD using the applicable exchange rate, but this is a tax translation measure, not a surrender rule. If a transaction is processed through a domestic bank and exceeds the Financial Transaction Reporting threshold, standard AML/CFT record-keeping and, where applicable, suspicious transaction reporting apply.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech EMIs are viable but require registration on New Zealand's Financial Service Providers Register and compliance with the AML/CFT Act 2009. An offshore fintech EMI without a local Financial Service Providers Register linkage cannot lawfully provide payment services directly to a New Zealand entity. If the EMI operates through a local partner bank or is an approved non-bank payment provider, it may offer multicurrency collections and disbursements, but the underlying settlement is still supported by a registered New Zealand bank. For core treasury and capital escrow functions, a Tier-1 domestic bank is strongly preferred; an EMI may be used as a secondary collection rail for Stripe Billing and Direct Debit if it can demonstrate a valid local registration, client fund segregation, and clear audit trail. Overall feasibility rating: cautious supplementary use rather than primary banking infrastructure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The shareholder or parent entity should route equity injections via SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the company's designated capital account in New Zealand. In Field 70, the payment narrative should clearly state SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION or EQUITY INJECTION. If using ISO 20022 purpose codes, apply EQUI for equity or CAPI for capital injection. Because New Zealand does not operate a capital verification bureau, the company must maintain its own statutory capital register and reconcile the bank credit advice against the board resolution allotting shares, the shareholder subscription agreement, and the company register. The receiving bank may request a declaration of source of funds and, for material amounts, a Banker's Confirmation from the remitting institution. There is no requirement to convert inbound foreign currency to NZD; the company may retain the funds in the currency received or convert through the bank's FX desk at a wholesale rate. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech EMIs are viable but require registration on New Zealand's Financial Service Providers Register and compliance with the AML/CFT Act 2009. An offshore fintech EMI without a local Financial Service Providers Register linkage cannot lawfully provide payment services directly to a New Zealand entity. For core treasury and capital escrow functions, a Tier-1 domestic bank is strongly preferred; an EMI may be used as a secondary collection rail for Stripe Billing and Direct Debit if it can demonstrate a valid local registration, client fund segregation, and clear audit trail. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$10 million aggregated cross-border related-party transactions per income year; where the threshold is exceeded, the taxpayer must prepare contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File/Master File equivalent) and be able to provide it to Inland Revenue within 30 days of a request.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute comparable to the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and the doctrines of 'sham' and 'substance over form' enforce an economic substance requirement. A New Zealand Ltd must perform Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) locally—including management, contract negotiation, and risk control—in order to retain treaty benefits, secure imputation credits, and avoid the recharacterisation of arrangements by Inland Revenue.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return Filing

Statutory sanction: Sections 143A-143C of the Tax Administration Act 1994: evasion of tax is punishable by a fine not exceeding NZ$150,000 for a company and/or a director; individuals face a fine not exceeding NZ$75,000 and/or imprisonment up to three years. A civil shortfall penalty of 100% of the tax shortfall is also imposed under section 141D.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where a director signs or directly participates in the false return; the director is jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall, faces asset recovery actions, a High Court travel ban (asset freezing and departure prohibition) under section 173A of the Tax Administration Act, and criminal imprisonment of up to three years, plus disqualification from holding a directorship under the Companies Act 1993.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: The arrangement is void under section BG 1 (GAAR) and is recharacterised under section GB 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007; a 40% shortfall penalty applies for an abusive tax position under section 141A, plus use-of-money interest, and the denied treaty benefits result in the full 30% NRWT on dividends and 15% NRWT on royalties/interests being reassessed.

Director personal liability: Directors who benefit from or control the unreal activity are personally liable for the recovered tax shortfall under section 34 of the Tax Administration Act, exposing personal assets, a travel ban (blacklisting) for the unpaid amount, and criminal prosecution for aiding in tax evasion under section 143A, carrying imprisonment of up to three years.
AML/CFT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Sections 89 and 90 of the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009: a company is liable to a civil penalty not exceeding NZ$1 million for each contravention; directors and officers are liable to a penalty not exceeding NZ$250,000 and/or imprisonment for a term not exceeding two years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced on proof of a director's knowledge or wilful blindness; directors face criminal conviction with imprisonment up to two years, personal fines, asset restraining orders, travel bans, and disqualification from managing companies under the Financial Markets Authority enforcement regime.
