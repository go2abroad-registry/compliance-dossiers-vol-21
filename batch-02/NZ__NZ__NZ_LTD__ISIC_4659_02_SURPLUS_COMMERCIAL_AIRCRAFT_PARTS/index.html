Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox address is legally permissible for the Companies Office registered office requirement, provided the address is a physical location in New Zealand where service of documents can be effected and the company maintains a physical presence through its registered agent. However, for the purpose of GST registration and opening a business bank account with an authorized New Zealand bank, a virtual office alone will likely be rejected by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and banks, particularly for an aviation spares trading business under ISIC 4659, because the industry is expected to maintain an operational physical office, storage or inspection facility, or at least a documented commercial substance in New Zealand. The entity should therefore secure a physical registered office and, if practicable, a leased office or service arrangement with a licensed commercial agent to satisfy both the Companies Office and the due diligence expectations of correspondent banks.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand companies have no statutory minimum paid-up capital. A company may be incorporated with no issued capital or a nominal share capital, and shares have no par value under the Companies Act 1993. No blocked escrow account is required. Shares may be issued for cash or non-cash consideration, and the board must pass a resolution approving the issue and be satisfied that the asset or benefit received as consideration is adequate and fairly measurable under section 58.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and display of the Building Warrant of Fitness and Compliance Schedule are mandatory for the warehouse premises. Fire and Emergency New Zealand must approve the Fire Evacuation Scheme, means of escape, exit width, fire extinguisher placement, emergency lighting, and alarm systems. Structural load inspections must certify that racking and mezzanine storage do not exceed the building design limits. Worksafe New Zealand requires a documented health and safety management system under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including electrical safety, manual handling controls, forklift licensing, hazardous substances controls, incident reporting, and a worker participation system.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; online portal: myIR (secure electronic filing and registration portal accessible via www.ird.govt.nz).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine entity category and registration obligations for a foreign-owned New Zealand limited company. Step 2: Incorporate the company with the New Zealand Companies Office (under the Companies Act 1993) and obtain the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) which is automatically issued upon incorporation. Step 3: Register for income tax with Inland Revenue via the myIR portal or through the Companies Office data feed, providing the NZBN, registered office address, director details, and shareholder information; this results in issuance of an IRD number (often the same as the NZBN for companies). Step 4: If the company expects to make taxable supplies above the GST registration threshold, separately apply for GST registration in myIR electing a filing frequency. Step 5: If importing or exporting goods, apply for a Customs Client Code from the New Zealand Customs Service (via the Customs Client/Agent Interactive system) and obtain a Customs registered client identifier. Step 6: For non-resident directors or foreign entities, ensure a local agent or authorized myIR user is appointed under the New Zealand tax administration rules and file any required non-resident land withholding tax or financial product disclosures if applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A New Zealand GST-registered person may zero-rate the supply of goods that are exported from New Zealand if the supplier exports the goods within the time frames and documentary conditions set out in the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, including actual removal from New Zealand within 28 days of supply or earlier if required, the supplier retains the goods as trading stock until export, and the goods are not subsequently returned to New Zealand except as permitted; zero-rating also applies to certain internationally supplied services, transport services, and supplies of qualifying remote services to non-residents under defined conditions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Export Entry) submitted through New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window (for goods exceeding export declaration thresholds), including the registered exporter client code
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing physical departure of goods from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the surplus commercial aircraft parts and confirming the sale to an overseas buyer
  • Evidence of overseas payment or inward remittance such as a bank credit advice, SWIFT wire confirmation, or foreign exchange repatriation record (where applicable)

New Zealand does not mandate a real-time e-invoicing clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping regime. Peppol-based e-invoicing is voluntary and supported by the New Zealand Peppol Authority. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR, and records must be retained in accordance with the Tax Administration Act 1994, including sufficient detail for audit; there is no statutory requirement for a government-issued digital prior approval or real-time API clearance for invoices.

Under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and Tax Administration Act 1994, if zero-rating is applied but export documentation is insufficient or the goods do not physically leave New Zealand in time, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 15% GST, with interest and shortfall penalties for an unacceptable tax position or evasion; deliberate misstatements can trigger strict liability criminal penalties of up to NZD 50,000 for the first offense and imprisonment in serious cases, plus an automatic 100% or more shortfall penalty on the unpaid GST; Inland Revenue may reassess and claw back input tax credits claimed on procurement related to the export transaction when zero-rating is denied.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully floating, freely convertible currency under a comprehensive external account liberalization framework. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not restrict the conversion of New Zealand dollars for commercial trade or capital account transactions, and there are no comprehensive exchange control regulations analogous to those in emerging markets. Current account payments, including imports of surplus commercial aircraft parts, dividends, management fees, and aircraft maintenance disbursements, may be paid without requiring central bank approval. Capital account inflows and outflows, including shareholder equity injections into NZ_LTD, intercompany loans, and eventual repatriation of sale proceeds, are likewise permitted without surrender requirements or prior authorization. New Zealand's anti-money laundering regime, overseen by the Reserve Bank, the Department of Internal Affairs, and the Financial Markets Authority, focuses on customer due diligence and suspicious transaction reporting rather than foreign exchange rationing. The practical hurdles for profit remittance are limited to withholding tax obligations under New Zealand domestic tax law, including non-resident withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties, and the financial institution's own AML onboarding checks. New Zealand GST of 15% is recoverable through normal tax returns. No currency surrender or haircut is imposed, and the NZD/USD market operates deeply through local and global interbank channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists. Exporters may retain foreign-currency proceeds in offshore or onshore accounts, with no mandatory conversion ratio or compliance timeline. For ISIC 4659 surplus aircraft parts exports, proceeds must be reported through normal customs and Inland Revenue documentation, but the exporter may hold USD proceeds or convert them to NZD at its own discretion.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

New Zealand does not commonly issue elective money-license equivalents for digital EMIs; non-bank financial institutions can be registered as financial service providers and conduct money remittance under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, but they are not licensed to take deposits or issue commercial bank guarantees. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, and similar platforms may offer multi-currency payment accounts to New Zealand entities, but they cannot support documentary credit lifecycles, escrow arrangements for aviation parts, or dedicated trade finance facilities. Feasibility rating for this NZ_LTD surplus aircraft parts distribution roadmap is Low-Medium. Principal trade settlements must be routed through registered New Zealand commercial banks; EMIs may be used only as auxiliary disbursement rails for incidental operating expenses. No offshore EMI can provide equivalent FX hedging, capital verification, or syndicated credit support for cross-border aviation procurement. Legal constraints include NZ AML/CFT licensing for the EMI, sanctions screening obligations, and the absence of a deposit-taking authorization; therefore, an EMI cannot replace a tier-1 domestic commercial bank for escrow or operational treasury purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity or shareholder loan transfers for NZ_LTD must be routed through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 wire messages from the remitting bank. The wire instruction must clearly state a purpose code such as equity injection, shareholder loan, or capital contribution. New Zealand banks do not require central bank exchange-control permits, but they require source-of-funds declarations, supporting board resolutions, and remittance advices. For statutory capital verification, a New Zealand chartered accountant or auditor will reconcile the received funds against the share allotment ledger and issue a capital verification certificate or certificate of compliance. Non-resident shareholder injections are unconditionally permitted, though ultimate beneficial ownership information must be disclosed for non-resident withholding tax purposes. Funds may be held in NZD or USD, and no mandatory sale into New Zealand dollars is required.Outbound trade settlements for surplus civil aircraft parts will primarily clear over three rails: SWIFT MT103 for open-account supplier payments and intercompany disbursements; commercial letters of credit issued by the bank via MT700 or MT707 for larger procurement contracts; and documentary collections for buyer-based settlement where a letter of credit is not required. New Zealand banks route USD and NZD wires through SWIFT with delivery-versus-payment or free-of-payment settlement mechanics, and all correspondent banks must pass OFAC and United Nations sanctions screening. The company should maintain a USD operational account within the NZ bank to reduce costly conversion layers. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Non-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994, ss. 143A and 143B – evasion offence; conviction may result in a fine up to NZD 50,000 and/or up to 5 years‘ imprisonment. Civil shortfall penalties for evasion or abusive tax position under s. 141D can reach 150% of the tax shortfall, plus interest on unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed or authorised false returns can be prosecuted personally; the corporate veil is pierced where the company is a mere facade. Personal assets can be restrained or confiscated under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009, a travel ban and immigration blacklisting may be issued by IRD, and imprisonment of up to 5 years may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Unsupported Intercompany Charges

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 Subpart GC – the Commissioner may adjust the transaction to an arm‘s-length price, increasing taxable income. Transfer pricing shortfall penalties under Tax Administration Act 1994 ss. 141B and 141C range from 20% to 150% of the consequent tax shortfall, depending on reasonableness; failure to maintain required TP documentation shifts the burden of proof and incurs record-keeping penalties up to NZD 12,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused or permitted the pricing adjustments may be jointly and severally liable for the company’s unpaid tax and penalties; reckless trading under the Companies Act 1993 can lead to asset recovery orders. In extreme cases, making false or misleading TP records is a criminal offence (Crimes Act 1961) with imprisonment up to 7 years, and director disqualification.
Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) on Dividends, Interest or Royalties

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 s. 141E – a failure to withhold and pay NRWT results in a civil penalty equal to 100% of the unrecovered tax, plus statutory interest accruing at 7.9% per annum. Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly neglect withholding obligations are personally liable for the unpaid NRWT pursuant to current director penalty provisions; they may also be criminally liable for aiding and abetting tax evasion. Immigration restrictions (including departure prohibition orders) and personal asset seizure orders can be issued, and a director can be imprisoned for up to 5 years for deliberate non-compliance.
