Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; secure online filing and registration portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that is required to collect or file New Zealand GST must first obtain a New Zealand IRD number and register for GST through Inland Revenue. Procedure: (1) Determine whether GST registration is compulsory, voluntary, or based on the compulsory NZD 60,000 annual taxable supply threshold; (2) Confirm whether the entity supplies services to consumers in New Zealand or exports qualifying goods, because non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value imported goods may have special GST collection obligations; (3) Prepare the entity's overseas certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, directors' names and addresses, registered address, overseas tax identification number, and evidence of any New Zealand business connection; (4) Establish a myIR identity or appoint an authorised New Zealand tax agent; (5) Submit the non-resident company IRD number and GST registration application through the myIR portal, using the official GST registration service operated by Inland Revenue; (6) Receive the IRD number, GST registration number, filing frequency, and myIR reporting profile; (7) Appoint a New Zealand agent for service of process is permitted though not always mandatory, and the appointed agent contact details should be provided to Inland Revenue where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies made in New Zealand within any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers supplying remote services or low-value imported goods to New Zealand consumers also become liable to register when their New Zealand GST taxable supplies exceed the same NZD 60,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated when the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days after the time of supply and the goods are supplied to a recipient who is not resident in New Zealand or the goods are exported as an integral part of the supply. Remote services, including logistics technology SaaS and fleet telematics services, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident customer who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed and the services are not directly connected with land or movable property in New Zealand. The supplier must retain contemporaneous evidence proving the recipient's non-resident status, the offshore business address, the payment settlement, the customs export confirmation, and the physical export of goods.

  • New Zealand Customs Service export entry declaration in the Customs CUSEP/CUSED electronic export system showing the exporter, consignee, armed forces code, tariff classification, value, and overseas destination
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or other transport document evidencing shipment from New Zealand to the foreign buyer's destination
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt, inward remittance advice, or overseas payment record demonstrating receipt of sale proceeds in a foreign currency or via cross-border electronic transfer from the non-resident buyer

No real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate exists in New Zealand. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR on a monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly basis. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and operates through PEPPOL-enabled exchange models; there is no mandatory government API clearance gateway for invoices.

If zero-rated export supplies are not supported by valid contemporaneous export and foreign exchange documentation, or if goods are not exported within the required statutory time frame, Inland Revenue may retrospectively reverse the zero-rating and assess GST at 15% on the original supply. Shortfall penalties may apply at 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for abusive tax positions, and 100% for evasion, plus use-of-money interest on the unpaid GST. False documentation supporting an export zero-rating claim may lead to fraud investigations, criminal prosecutions, penalties of up to NZD 50,000 or higher on conviction, and loss of GST registration privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a free-floating exchange rate regime with full capital account convertibility. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) neither imposes exchange controls nor requires prior approval for cross-border capital movements, apart from reporting and sanctions screening. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible in both current and capital accounts. There are no surrender requirements for export proceeds, and repatriation of dividends, branch profits, loan repayments, or invested capital is unrestricted for both residents and non-residents. Companies may hold foreign currency accounts and deal in the global interbank market via domestic licensed banks. The chief practical friction is not statutory, but commercial: banks must satisfy Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 duties, including customer due diligence, source of wealth assessments, and beneficial ownership transparency. Remittance purposes must be accurately classified using standard BOP codes to enable the RBNZ statistical reporting. Baseline regulations also require compliance with United Nations Security Council sanctions and trade restrictions. For a typical logistics telematics software exporter, profit distributions and licensing fees can be paid abroad without additional regulatory clearances. Deficiencies emerge only if the operating entity lacks economic substance documentation, contrary tax indicia, or named sanctioned parties appear in the payment chain, which would trigger bank hold-offs. Overall, treasury planning is highly predictable and does not require complex conversion licences or central bank usance approvals.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists in New Zealand. Export businesses may lawfully retain 100% of foreign currency receipts in foreign currency accounts maintained at licensed local banks, convert to NZD at onshore market rates, or offshore at their parent treasury. No mandatory conversion ratio or time limit applies. All transactions require an accurate payment purpose code, source of funds declaration, and standard AML/CFT verification; failure to evidence the underlying export contract can cause a bank to delay clearing as a protective measure.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: Low for primary treasury banking; Medium for ancillary low-value payment services. Offshore EMI providers that are not incorporated in New Zealand and are not registered as financial service providers under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 are barred from offering virtual account services that mimic deposit-taking to New Zealand corporate entities. The RBNZ supervises deposit-taking under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989, requiring a registered bank licence. EMIs such as Wise can provide limited e-money wallets for minor operational disbursements, but they cannot serve as a legal substitute for a regulated corporate bank account used for capital escrow, trade settlement, FX hedging, or overdraft facilities. Any EMI must be licensed in New Zealand and registered on the Financial Markets Authority portal; full treasury reliance on a fintech EMI is legally and commercially untenable for a logistics SaaS exporter.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For injection of registered equity from a non-resident parent, the parent must execute a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the NZ_LTD capital escrow account using the local bank BIC and NZD account number. The payment purpose must be coded as 'Equity Capital Contribution' under the New Zealand International Transactions Reporting regime, using the standard BPM6 code for direct investment in an incorporated enterprise (E12). The bank will place cleared funds on hold until the company files the necessary share allotment form with the New Zealand Companies Office and presents a board resolution confirming the equitable receipt. A formal capital verification letter from the bank, together with the certificate of incorporation, share register, and shareholder resolution, completes the inbound capital gateway. The Overseas Investment Act screening is not engaged because the business is a software and logistics technology company, not a sensitive land or fishing quota acquisition.Outbound disbursements for license fees, subcontractor payments, and operational logistics costs should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the NZD operational account using the appropriate trade purpose code (e.g., 'Operational Expenses' or 'Licence Fee'). For larger deployments of fleet telematics infrastructure, a Commercial Letter of Credit, either at sight or usance, issued by ANZ, ASB, BNZ, or Westpac, provides secured settlement between NZ_LTD and its overseas vendors. Documentary collections (D/P or D/A) may be used when the parties hold open-account relationships, with title documents released against acceptance or payment. Domestic NZD clearing occurs via the RBNZ Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS), while USD, EUR, and GBP settlements settle through correspondent banking networks with strict adherence to sanctions screening and underlying invoice documentation.Deliverable FX forward contracts against USD, AUD, EUR, GBP, JPY, and CNY for maturities up to 12 months, available under bank-issued ISDA trade confirmations and subject to unsecured credit limits.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for non-convertible currencies such as INR, KRW, TWD, and CNY when settlement with a New Zealand bank is required to hedge a contractual exposure; NDFs are permissible where a deliverable market is unviable and must be traded with a licensed counterparty.Cross-currency interest rate swaps for hedging long-term foreign currency debt positions, subject to documented internal treasury authority and ISDA netting; tenors are typically confined to five years and notional limits set at 25% of the company net asset value.Plain vanilla FX options, both calls and puts, on NZD/USD and NZD/AUD for committed trade payments, requiring premium payment up front and bank approval under the internal derivatives product programme.

Turnover: A full independent statutory audit is mandatory for any company that is an FMC reporting entity under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 or that meets the 'large company' thresholds under the Financial Reporting Act 2013: total assets of NZD 60 million (approximately USD 36 million) or more, or total revenue of NZD 30 million (approximately USD 18 million) or more, in each of the two immediately preceding accounting periods, or 50 or more full-time equivalent employees. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 1 million aggregate cross-border related-party transactions per category is the IRD administrative benchmark requiring contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation; while New Zealand does not statutorily label a Local File or Master File requirement, IRD expects equivalent documentation at that threshold. The Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of NZD 1 billion or more, and material telematics royalty, service and financing arrangements involving non-resident related parties must be benchmarked and documented.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a nominal Economic Substance Regulation regime, but the statutory general anti-avoidance rule in ITA 2007 s BG1 and s GA 1, together with the OECD-aligned transfer pricing rules in subpart GC, recharacterise arrangements lacking commercial reality. For Logistics Fleet Telematics SaaS, the Core Income-Generating Activities include route optimisation and algorithm R&D, telematics data processing, software development, commercial contract negotiation, pricing and board decisions. If these activities are performed by an offshore related entity while NZ_LTD merely contracts with customers, profits can be reattributed to New Zealand, a permanent establishment can be established, and transfer pricing adjustments plus penalties will be imposed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing and Profit Stripping Through Offshore Dispatch and Royalty Fees

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue will adjust the transactions to an arm's length result under ITA 2007 subpart GC, specifically ss GC 6 to GC 14; the tax shortfall attracts a 20% unacceptable tax position penalty under TAA 1994 s 141D, and deliberate or egregious mispricing is treated as evasion under s 141E with a 150% shortfall penalty, use-of-money interest, and recharacterisation of the whole arrangement under s BG1 as void for tax purposes.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the offshore fee structure and sign the IR4 are exposed to personal civil liability where the payments leave the company unable to pay its New Zealand tax debt; the corporate veil can be pierced under Companies Act 1993 s 300 for reckless trading, s 301 for court-ordered asset recovery, and directors may face criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion under TAA 1994 s 143B, imprisonment for up to 5 years, fines up to NZD 50,000, asset restraint orders, travel bans and director disqualification.
Failure to Withhold and Remit NRWT on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: NRWT liability is imposed on the paying company under ITA 2007 s RF 6, and failure to withhold and remit triggers TAA 1994 penalties including an immediate 1% late payment penalty, a further 4% after 7 days, use-of-money interest, and a shortfall penalty of up to 150% for intentional avoidance; the unpaid NRWT remains a debt due to the Crown and cannot be discharged by company liquidation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participated in the disguised repatriation are personally liable for the unpaid NRWT and penalties under TAA 1994 s 149, may be ordered to pay compensation through Companies Act 1993 s 301, and are exposed to criminal liability under TAA 1994 s 143B for evading tax assessment or payment, carrying imprisonment up to 5 years, fines, blacklisting, border alert travel bans and seizure of personal assets.
False Returns, Inflated R&D Claims and Fraudulent Tax Statements

Statutory sanction: Knowingly providing a false or misleading tax return is an offence under TAA 1994 s 143A and s 143B, punishable by a fine of up to NZD 50,000 per offence and imprisonment for up to 5 years; the resulting tax shortfall attracts an evasion penalty of 150% of the underpaid tax under s 141E, rather than the lower 20% or 40% care and position penalties, plus daily use-of-money interest from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the false IR4 to be filed cannot rely on limited liability once fraud is established; personal civil restitution can reach their bank accounts, superannuation and trust assets, courts can order disqualification from managing companies for up to 10 years under Companies Act 1993 s 383, and cross-border investigations may trigger extradition, travel bans, blacklisting by Inland Revenue and criminal custodial sentences for tax evasion.
