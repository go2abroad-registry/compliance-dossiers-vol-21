Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand's national tax authority; principal portal is myIR Secure Online Services at https://www.ird.govt.nz, including IRD Gateway API services for tax return and GST filing.

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand limited company, the IRD number is procured automatically upon registration with the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993; the Registrar passes company details to Inland Revenue, which issues the company's tax file number (IRD number). A non-resident company deriving New Zealand-source income or making supplies subject to New Zealand GST must apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number using the appropriate non-resident company application form and must provide certified constitutional documents, director identification, registered office or care-of address details, and ultimate beneficial ownership information. Where a non-resident entity is carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand or supplying remote or digital services to New Zealand consumers, it must also complete GST registration through the Inland Revenue online GST registration portal. There is no standard fee for IRD registration, but all corporate documents in a foreign language must be accompanied by certified English translations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime. Taxation of a limited liability company occurs at the entity level under the Income Tax Act 2007, at the 28% corporate tax rate, with the New Zealand imputation credit system attached to shareholder dividends. Available statutory elections include: GST registration election once taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000; GST accounting basis election between cash and invoice basis; GST taxable period election; tax consolidation election for wholly-owned New Zealand resident company groups under Subpart IG of the Income Tax Act 2007; loss grouping and offset elections; and imputation credit account elections. Hybrid mismatch provisions under the Income Tax Act 2007 apply as mandatory anti-avoidance rules rather than elective entity classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in New Zealand in any 12-month period; applies to resident suppliers and to non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value imported physical goods under the New Zealand GST remote-services rules. Suppliers below the threshold may choose to register voluntarily but, once registered, must typically remain registered for a minimum of two years.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A New Zealand GST-registered supplier may zero-rate exports under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when goods are supplied by way of actual physical export from New Zealand and the supplier holds evidence sufficient to satisfy the Inland Revenue Department that export has occurred. Zero-rating also applies to services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not supplied to a New Zealand resident and are not directly connected with land or goods in New Zealand. Services performed on exported movable personal property and certain intellectual property supplies to non-residents may also qualify. The supplier must retain contemporaneous records proving the export, including customs declarations, transport documents, contract terms, insurance, and payment receipts, and must maintain such documentation for at least seven years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Customs Service electronic filing via the Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement proving receipt of foreign currency consideration
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list
  • Certificate of origin or regulatory export attestation where required
  • Insurance and freight documentation or transport contract

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory government-authorised clearance API applies in New Zealand. Inland Revenue accepts GST returns electronically through myIR and approved Gateway API channels. B2B e-invoicing using the Peppol framework is optional and encouraged for interoperability, but New Zealand does not mandate continuous transaction controls or pre-supply tax authority clearance. Large GST-registered persons with annual turnover exceeding NZD 24 million generally file monthly; smaller taxpayers may qualify for two-month or six-month filing cycles, and all GST payments are settled through electronic banking channels.

The Inland Revenue Department and New Zealand Customs Service apply rigorous scrutiny to claimed zero-rated export supplies. If goods are not physically exported or services fail the statutory zero-rating conditions, the supplier is liable to reassess GST at 15%, effectively clawing back the zero-rating, and must also pay use-of-money interest under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Knowingly or dishonestly making a false GST return, or knowingly assisting with an incorrect zero-rating claim, constitutes evasion and attracts civil shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the resulting tax shortfall, with criminal prosecution exposure of fines up to NZD 250,000 for companies and NZD 50,000 for individuals, and imprisonment in serious evasion cases. Auditors may recharacterise the supply as taxable if the exporter cannot produce the required customs and proof-of-export bundle, and any input tax credit previously claimed on the export supply is recoverable from the supplier where the zero-rating is invalidated.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised and unrestricted cross-border capital and current account regime. The New Zealand Dollar (NZD) is freely convertible with no exchange control legislation, no surrender requirements, and no approval gateways for profit remittance or dividend distributions. Non-resident shareholders may inject equity, receive dividends, and repatriate capital without RBNZ or Overseas Investment Office consent, provided the business does not involve sensitive land, significant business assets above the screening threshold, or fishing/quota interests. For a MedTech regulatory consulting firm (ISIC 7490) with no sensitive-asset exposure, convertibility is effectively unhindered for both capital and current account transactions. The only compliance overlay is the mandatory tax reporting under the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Financial Transactions Reporting Act (incorporated within the AML/CFT regime), requiring accurate classification of payment purpose codes for the recipient bank's statistical reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules in New Zealand. A resident exporter of services (including FDA 510(k) and CE Mark compliance consultancy) may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts in an approved New Zealand or offshore bank account, may convert to NZD at any time, and is under no obligation to repatriate. No minimum conversion ratio applies. The only mandatory obligations are (i) accurate recording of the transaction on the exporter's own books, (ii) potential GST zero-rating for exported qualifying services under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, and (iii) statutory record-keeping for income tax purposes and AML/CFT audit trails.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), acting in concert with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) as joint conduct regulator under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for ancillary collections and payments, but NOT a substitute for a licensed New Zealand bank account. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Currenxie, Revolut Business) are permissible as supplementary payment rails, subject to two binding constraints: (i) the EMI must be duly licensed in its home jurisdiction and pass the NZ bank's correspondent due diligence; (ii) where the NZ_LTD maintains its principal operating account at a licensed registered bank (as required by the Companies Act 1993 and the AML/CFT Act), the EMI may only process low-value operational receipts from overseas clients and must not be used for the segregated escrow account that holds client-mandated regulatory filing fees. The Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 requires any person providing financial services to be registered on the FSP Register, but an NZ limited company using an EMI as a pure collection vehicle is not itself deemed a financial service provider. Direct inbound transfers from the EMI to the NZ bank account are treated as ordinary cross-border wires subject to standard purpose-code verification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol: Non-resident shareholders remit equity contributions by SWIFT MT103 with the payment narrative of 'Inbound Equity Contribution - NZ_LTD Share Capital'. The purpose code under the RBNZ BOP statistical framework is 'Equity Capital' (Category 6.1 of the RBNZ standard). The bank requires the following verification evidence: (i) a board resolution authorising the share issue; (ii) the shareholder register entry updated under the Companies Act 1993; (iii) proof of source of funds (bank statement from remitting account); and (iv) the NZ Companies Office annual return confirmation of the new shareholding. There is no minimum capital requirement (the Companies Act 1993 imposes no statutory minimum), but for banking onboarding purposes a minimum working-capital injection of NZD 20,000–50,000 is commercially advisable to demonstrate solvency. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High for ancillary collections and payments, but NOT a substitute for a licensed New Zealand bank account. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Currenxie, Revolut Business) are permissible as supplementary payment rails, subject to two binding constraints: (i) the EMI must be duly licensed in its home jurisdiction and pass the NZ bank's correspondent due diligence; (ii) where the NZ_LTD maintains its principal operating account at a licensed registered bank (as required by the Companies Act 1993 and the AML/CFT Act), the EMI may only process low-value operational receipts from overseas clients and must not be used for the segregated escrow account that holds client-mandated regulatory filing fees. The Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 requires any person providing financial services to be registered on the FSP Register, but an NZ limited company using an EMI as a pure collection vehicle is not itself deemed a financial service provider. Direct inbound transfers from the EMI to the NZ bank account are treated as ordinary cross-border wires subject to standard purpose-code verification.
