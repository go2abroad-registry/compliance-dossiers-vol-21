Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an SPC under RD 18/2019. The share capital must be fully subscribed by the sole shareholder, paid in OMR into a licensed Omani bank account (or supported by an approved in-kind valuation report) and evidenced by a capital deposit certificate before the Commercial Registration Certificate issues. Funds are not held in escrow and become available for the company's lawful business purposes after registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety and Civil Defence Clearance Certificate from the Royal Oman Police – Directorate General of Civil Defence and Ambulance, based on an approved engineered fire suppression, evacuation and storage plan; municipal building/structural completion inspection for the production hall; and workplace occupational-safety clearance from the Ministry of Labour, including food-zone health screening for operatives.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (Al-Hay’ah Al-‘Ammah Li Al-Dara’ib), Sultanate of Oman; official e-services portal: https://taxoman.gov.om.

E-government filing portal

Step 2: Register the SPC for Income Tax with the Oman Tax Authority under Royal Decree 28/2009 as amended, obtaining the Tax Card and Tax Registration Number (TRN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Tax_Resident_SPC subject to Omani income tax on taxable profits computed under the Income Tax Law. The SPC is not a pass-through entity and cannot elect transparent taxation. Territorial scope follows Omani-source business income and certain repatriated or deemed-Omani-source income; non-resident shareholders may be taxed on Omani-source income and are subject to withholding where applicable. A licensed free-zone SPC may qualify for specific tax holiday or reduced-rate incentives, but such treatment is an incentive not a default classification.

Available election forms

No United States-style check-the-box election and no pass-through entity election is available in Oman. The SPC is mandatorily taxed as a separate legal person for income tax and VAT. A taxpayer may voluntarily elect VAT registration once the statutory voluntary threshold of OMR 100,000 in annual taxable supplies is exceeded, and may elect to apply for early registration before the mandatory threshold is reached under the conditions prescribed by the VAT Law. There is no separate statutory hybrid mismatch election under Omani domestic law; OECD BEPS Action 2 outcomes are applied administratively where relevant, but no elective regime is provided for domestic tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration threshold: OMR 500,000 of taxable supplies in a rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration threshold: OMR 100,000; imports of goods and services are subject to VAT without an importer registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically exported from Oman to destinations outside the GCC VAT territory are zero-rated where the exporter proves the goods left Oman within 90 days from the date of supply and retains contemporaneous export documents. International transport and directly related services may also be zero-rated where the transportation is from, to or across Oman and the statutory conditions are met. Goods supplied into an Omani free zone may be treated as exported or otherwise relieved where the free-zone regime and customs rules so provide. Export zero-rating is available regardless of the standard 5% rate and requires the supplier to account for the supply in the VAT return with supporting evidence of departure, ownership and consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan) duly approved by Oman Customs through the electronic single window
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or Road Consignment Note evidencing actual removal of goods from Omani territory
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List in the name of the exporting SPC
  • Certificate of Origin and any statutory food export license or health certificate required for frozen dim sum and dumplings
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) from a licensed Omani bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • VAT export ledger record with shipment dates, customer details, Incoterms and zero-rating narrative

Oman does not yet mandate a universal real-time e-invoicing fiscal clearance system. The Oman Tax Authority e-services portal supports electronic VAT filing, assessed invoices, and electronic invoice issuance through its digital channel; registered persons may issue electronic tax invoices where the invoice satisfies the authenticity, integrity and readability requirements of the VAT Law and maintains an audit trail through the VAT return. Real-time clearance, digital stamping and API-based integration akin to certain GCC counterparts are not currently compulsory for all domestic B2B supplies, but taxpayers must retain electronic records and customs-linked documentation for audit; phased adoption of e-invoicing is expected by the Authority.

The Oman Tax Authority may scrutinise zero-rated export claims retrospectively for up to five years. Missing, false or inconsistent export documentation will cause the VAT to be clawed back at 5% on the full consideration, together with reversal of any input VAT credits, late-payment interest and administrative penalties. Fraudulent zero-rating claims may attract monetary penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded, fixed fines under the VAT Executive Regulations, and potential criminal sanctions including imprisonment for directors and shareholders where evasion is proven. Exporters are also exposed to Indirect Tax audit findings and reputational enforcement action if export proceeds are not repatriated within the prescribed 180-day period unless the competent bank or Central Bank of Oman approval evidences a valid extension.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) operates a hard fixed-peg regime at 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD, contractually maintained by the CBO. Oman has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, ensuring unrestricted current-account convertibility for trade and services. The capital account is also substantially liberalized: 100% non-resident ownership of Omani SPCs is permitted, and there are no general CBO licensing requirements for inbound equity, dividend, or trade flows. Practical friction points are limited to: (i) full source-of-funds documentation and CBO-format UBO declarations at account origination; (ii) invoice–customs–bill-of-lading conformity checks for every trade wire; (iii) the need for an Oman Tax Authority clearance/zero-liability certificate on outbound dividend and large service payments to non-residents; (iv) a 10% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders unless reduced to 5% or 0% under an applicable double-tax treaty (e.g., the Oman–US FTA and relevant OECD-compliant treaties); and (v) bank-level de-risking practices that can delay same-day USD clearing by 1–3 business days when the beneficiary bank is not a CBO-recognized correspondent. The fixed USD peg effectively eliminates OMR/USD convertibility risk, but introduces US-dollar liquidity risk and requires USD-denominated cash-flow buffers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender obligation exists in Oman. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory deadline compelling exporters of frozen dim sum products to repatriate foreign currency. Exporters may retain USD or OMR balances offshore or onshore at their discretion. The compliance regime requires instead: (i) all export receipts entering Oman via the official banking system must be credited against the beneficiary SPC's current account with a truthful trade-purpose code; (ii) the bank must hold a completed export customs declaration, bill of lading/airway bill, commercial invoice, and, where applicable, the SPC's food-export health certificate; (iii) customs authorities require the exporter to register each consignment under the single-window customs e-declaration; and (iv) the annual corporate tax return must reconcile total export revenue, foreign receipts, and receivables aging. The CBO and the National Centre for Financial Information (through bank reporting) monitor currency-transaction records above statutory thresholds; deliberately underreported or routed proceeds trigger AML review and tax-audit exposure.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), incorporated as the supreme monetary and regulatory authority under the State's Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000), with statutory jurisdiction over commercial banking licensing, foreign-exchange monitoring, payment-system oversight, and AML/CFT enforcement; the Oman Tax Authority separately governs tax-clearance and withholding requirements applicable to cross-border profit remittances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW-MARGINAL with strict legal boundaries. Oman has no E-Money Institution licensing regime analogous to the EU; offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut, Airwallex) are not authorized to hold OMR deposit accounts, issue OMR-denominated payment instruments, or clear corporate trade payments in Oman. Under the National Payment System Law (Royal Decree 37/2019) and CBO Payment Service Provider regulations, all payment/remittance services provided into Oman require a CBO licence or an explicit commercial sponsorship by a licensed Omani bank. Practical usage is restricted to collecting B2C export receivables from foreign e-commerce end-customers: those remittances arrive as third-party EMT transfers subject to 3–10-business-day manual AML review, heightened UBO document requests, correspondent-fee stacking (USD 25–60 per receipt), and occasional rejection by the Omani receiving bank. For containerized trade settlements, commercial LC and MT103 bank-to-bank wires via a tier-1 Omani bank remain the only seamlessly compliant rails; an SPC should therefore not rely on an offshore EMI as a primary treasury channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections into the Omani SPC must be routed exclusively via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for intermediate settlement) using the receiving bank's designated foreign-direct-investment purpose code as defined by CBO unified reporting taxonomy (bank-specific classification, commonly 'Capital Contribution – Sub-Code K.402' or 'Direct Investment – Equity'). Compliance protocol: (i) the shareholder must presubmit the receiving bank's custody cover letter with the SPC's CR number, incorporation certificate, share-allotment table, and the bank's swift reference number; (ii) the remittance shall instruct 'Equity Contribution – No Goods or Services; Capital Exempt under Article 16; no WHT applies'; (iii) the beneficiary account must be the SPC's IBAN (OM form) at the sending bank's Oman branch; (iv) the shareholder resolution evidencing capital increase must accompany amounts exceeding authorized capital; (v) proof-of-funds and signed AML declarations are mandatory before credit; GCC-origin wires credit in T+1, non-FATF-jurisdiction wires in T+3–5 after enhanced review. Crypto-asset rails, virtual-asset intermediaries, and unlicensed money transmitters are categorically non-compliant with CBO and will trigger rejection and CBO notification.Primary clearing matrix for a frozen dim sum exporter in Oman: (1) SWIFT MT103 direct telegraphic transfer for repeated supplier payments (packaging, flour, seasoning, chilled logistics), each accompanied by commercial invoice, freight invoice, and the Omani import customs declaration for landed goods, with purpose codes under 'Merchandise Imports'. (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (Sight or Usance, UCP 600) issued via Bank Muscat/NBO/Sohar International trade desks for sea-freight consolidation, payable against a strict document set (commercial invoice, full-set B/L, Form A, phytosanitary certificate, health certificate, packing list). (3) Standby LCs or bank guarantees posted as customs security for export consignments. (4) Oman Clearing House (CBO-operated) transfers for OMR-denominated domestic payments to customs brokers, cold-storage operators, and freight forwarders. (5) Imports of food ingredients also trigger the Food Safety and Quality Centre (FSQC) sanitary permit verification; payments are suspended until the import permit number is included on the instruction. Offshore EMI-based trade disbursements are strongly discouraged: beneficiary banks frequently return or hold such payments for 10+ banking days on account of the non-bank originator.Deliverable OMR/USD spot and outright forward contracts (tenors up to 12 months) executed by CBO-licensed banks at the pegged rate of 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD, available for hedging export-receivable conversionOMR/USD FX swaps and rollover structures for matching cash-flow timing between USD export inflows and OMR operating-cost outflowsCross-currency swaps (CCIRS) between USD, EUR, and CNY legs for hedging machinery and ingredient import settlements with fixed-rate USD or CNY cash flowsUSD/CNY deliverable forwards with a CBO-licensed bank, linked directly to underlying Chinese ingredient purchase orders and permissible as a trade-hedge rather than open speculationOTC vanilla FX options (put/call) on OMR/USD permitted as collateral guarantees for foreign shareholder loans, subject to board resolution and bank credit approvalNon-deliverable-forwards (NDF) are declared unavailable and categorically excluded from the approved treasury product set because the OMR is pegged and not an NDF currency; any synthetic NDF is treated as an unauthorized speculative instrument.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: LOW-MARGINAL with strict legal boundaries. Oman has no E-Money Institution licensing regime analogous to the EU; offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut, Airwallex) are not authorized to hold OMR deposit accounts, issue OMR-denominated payment instruments, or clear corporate trade payments in Oman. Under the National Payment System Law (Royal Decree 37/2019) and CBO Payment Service Provider regulations, all payment/remittance services provided into Oman require a CBO licence or an explicit commercial sponsorship by a licensed Omani bank. Practical usage is restricted to collecting B2C export receivables from foreign e-commerce end-customers: those remittances arrive as third-party EMT transfers subject to 3–10-business-day manual AML review, heightened UBO document requests, correspondent-fee stacking (USD 25–60 per receipt), and occasional rejection by the Omani receiving bank. For containerized trade settlements, commercial LC and MT103 bank-to-bank wires via a tier-1 Omani bank remain the only seamlessly compliant rails; an SPC should therefore not rely on an offshore EMI as a primary treasury channel.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: OMR 1,000,000 in aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with related parties triggers the obligation to prepare and maintain contemporaneous Local File and, where the foreign parent is an MNE group member, a Master File; Country-by-Country Report notification applies to Oman-resident constituent entities of MNE groups whose consolidated group revenue reaches the OECD EUR 750 million equivalent; the Oman Tax Authority may require transfer pricing documentation even below the stated threshold in the course of audit

Economic substance scope: Oman does not impose a separate yearly Economic Substance Regulation filing of the UAE type on a domestic SPC conducting a physical factory; however, the Income Tax Law's permanent establishment, beneficial ownership, and substance-over-form doctrines require the plant to maintain real premises, active Omani payroll, operational freezing and cold-storage equipment, local management, and authentic production records. The prefabricated dim sum factory, by virtue of its regular payroll, utility consumption and manufacturing operations, automatically satisfies the factual substance test; the foreign shareholder's eligibility for treaty relief remains separately subject to anti-conduit examination

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Revenue Concealment

Statutory sanction: Fine of OMR 5,000 to OMR 50,000 plus payment of the evaded tax and a surcharge of not less than 50% of the additional tax assessed; referral to the Omani Public Prosecutor for criminal proceedings; freezing of bank accounts and seizure of factory assets under the tax collection powers

Director personal liability: The SPC's general manager and each director are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and fines; the execution court may issue a travel ban, order restitution, and impose imprisonment of up to 5 years where fraud is proven
Shell Company and Sham Substance Structures

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation and re-allocation of profits under the general anti-abuse provisions of the Income Tax Law; denial of treaty withholding relief; disallowance of loss carry-forwards; administrative strike-off and liquidation of the entity; penalty of up to 50% of avoided tax; referral under the Anti-Money Laundering Law (RD 30/2016)

Director personal liability: Directors and the authorised Omani manager face civil restitution for the tax loss, asset freezes, travel bans, and fines under the AML regime of up to OMR 100,000; individuals found to have operated a sham structure may be imprisoned for up to 7 years
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Cross-Border Misinvoicing

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's-length net margin, additional corporate income tax at 15% on the uplifted profit, monetary penalty of up to 50% of the additional tax assessment, statutory interest, and expanded audit examination of all related-party contracts and bank flows under the LC and TT payment rails

Director personal liability: Directors who approved sub-optimal intercompany pricing face joint and several liability for the adjusted tax and penalties; repeated intentional mispricing is prosecutable as tax evasion with fines and imprisonment, in addition to bona fide debt collection measures such as travel bans and seizure of export receivables
