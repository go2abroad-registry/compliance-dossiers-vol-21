Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

In New Zealand, a company must maintain a registered office in New Zealand pursuant to section 189 of the Companies Act 1993; the registered office must be a physical address, not a postal box or mailbox-only address, and must be open to the public or otherwise accessible for service of documents on ordinary business days. A virtual office service or commercial business centre is permissible only if it provides a genuine physical New Zealand street address, accepts service of documents on behalf of the company, retains the registered office records, is not merely a post-forwarding or virtual mailbox service, and is contractually documented as the registered agent service. However, New Zealand Inland Revenue and registered banks generally disfavor pure virtual offices for entities engaged in independent film licensing with international copyright royalty flows, because such arrangements increase the risk of a 'tax haven' characterisation and may trigger enhanced AML/CFT customer due diligence, non-resident financial account treatment, and denial or restriction of bank account opening. For a low-risk ISIC 5911 film licensing company, a physical office lease or a reputable serviced office with a dedicated meeting room and mail-handling protocol is strongly recommended; a virtual box address is permissible only as a licensed agent's registered office address and not as the company's commercial substance address, and should be supported by local employment or trade records.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up share capital for a limited company, and no blocked escrow account or capital deposit arrangement is required. The company may be incorporated with one or more shares of no par value, and the issued shares may be paid for in cash or by other valuable consideration as approved by the directors. Non-resident ownership is permitted without special capital sterilisation requirements.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no Civil Defence fire permit, building occupancy load certificate, or WorkSafe workplace safety licence is required. If the company later leases incidental office premises, the standard Building Warrant of Fitness and fire evacuation scheme under the Building Act 2004 will be handled by the premises owner, but no additional industrial or physical licensing approval is triggered by the licensed corporate activity.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand Government; online portal myIR at ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the foreign non-resident entity derives New Zealand-source income or carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand; an IRD number is required for any New Zealand tax liability, filing obligation or GST registration. Step 2: Apply for an IRD number via myIR using the non-resident company IRD number application, or by paper if myIR access is unavailable, supplying the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director details, registered office, ultimate parent information and a written explanation of the New Zealand business connection or offshore supply activity. Step 4: Await Inland Revenue identity verification and issuance of the 8- or 9-digit IRD number, which must be quoted on all tax returns, GST returns and correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A New Zealand limited company is taxed at entity level, but a close-held eligible company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) treatment under the Income Tax Act 2007, causing tax attributes to flow through to shareholders, provided all statutory eligibility tests are met and notice is given to Inland Revenue. Fully owned corporate groups may elect consolidated tax treatment. No check-the-box entity classification exists for foreign-owned companies; hybrid mismatch rules under subpart FH require self-adjustment but do not allow election out of entity-level taxation. GST grouping election is available to related companies under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand consumers must also register at NZD 60,000 of annual supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST-registered New Zealand supplier may zero-rate (0% GST) supplies of goods physically exported from New Zealand, evidenced by a New Zealand Customs export declaration and transport documentation. Services, including independent film copyright licensing, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are not directly connected with land or tangible personal property in New Zealand. The contract must demonstrate that the licensed film distribution rights are confined to markets outside New Zealand. Qualifying offshore digital and remote services follow the same place-of-consumption analysis: the supplier must hold evidence that the customer is a non-resident and that the IP-protection territory and exploitation are outside New Zealand. Input tax attributable to the zero-rated export may be recovered as a refund in the GST return.

  • Export declaration lodged with New Zealand Customs Service via the Trade Single Window for physical exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from New Zealand
  • Foreign exchange settlement advice or SWIFT MT103 evidencing repatriation of the licence fee or sales proceeds to a New Zealand bank account
  • Executed copyright licence agreement and GST tax invoice identifying the offshore recipient, restricted territory and zero-rating

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping or continuous transaction control regime. Registered persons must issue valid GST tax invoices under the GST Act 1985; e-invoicing via the voluntary PEPPOL network is accepted. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR.

Inland Revenue may audit zero-rating by testing the executed licence, tax invoice, payment routing, foreign exchange settlement, and any physical export documents. If the documentary evidence is inadequate or the rights are found to be exploited in New Zealand, the supply is recharacterised as standard-rated at 15%, with a GST shortfall assessment, use-of-money interest, and possible shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for failing to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. Fraudulent or knowingly false export evidence may result in criminal prosecution and loss of input credit claims. Inland Revenue may reassess within four years of the end of the return period, with no time limit where fraud or evasion is involved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates under a freely floating exchange-rate regime and is a high-income open economy. The New Zealand dollar is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account flows for residents and non-residents. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not maintain exchange controls, does not require prior approval for remittances, and does not impose surrender obligations on export receipts. A limited administrative framework exists for prudential reporting and AML/CFT monitoring, but there is no FX license required for ordinary cross-border payments, no requirement to channel transactions through state-owned intermediaries, and no restriction on profit remittance beyond normal tax withholding rules. Businesses must still satisfy bank-level KYC and beneficial ownership checks and may need tax residency information. Dividends and royalties remitted to non-resident shareholders are subject to applicable New Zealand non-resident withholding tax rates and any income tax treaty relief. This convertibility profile makes New Zealand a stable jurisdiction for escrow and treasury operations for an independent film licensing entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. There is no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no foreign-currency earning retention period imposed by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand. All export proceeds may be held in registered bank accounts as foreign currency or converted to NZD at commercial banks at market rates. Transactions above usual thresholds are subject to AML/CFT record-keeping and reporting obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Offshore digital EMIs are not able to offer full deposit-taking banking services in New Zealand unless they are locally registered as banks under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. A fintech EMI may operate as a payment service provider under the Financial Service Providers Register and the AML/CFT Act 2009, but it cannot legally accept call deposits or provide a comprehensive residential business treasury account. For a New Zealand limited company with cross-border film licensing revenue, the practical usability of an offshore EMI is therefore limited to ancillary payment collection through a licensed bank partner; the primary operating and capital escrow accounts should be held with a registered Tier-1 New Zealand bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Because New Zealand has no exchange control approval regime, inbound equity or shareholder loan funds can be routed by SWIFT MT103 directly from the overseas corporate bank to the NZ company's Tier-1 bank account. The wire must clearly state the business purpose and the bank's internal purpose code for capital injection or shareholder loan. For equity contributions, the NZ company must record the share allotment in its Companies Office records and provide the bank with a board resolution, updated share register, and a capital verification statement from its accountant confirming the subscription has been received and recorded. For shareholder loans, a formal loan agreement, board resolution, and certificate from legal counsel confirming the debt-to-equity treatment for tax purposes should be maintained. No Reserve Bank consent is required unless the underlying transaction otherwise triggers the Overseas Investment Act for sensitive land or significant business assets, which does not apply to ordinary motion picture copyright licensing.Outbound royalty payments, profit-participation remittances, and licence-fee distributions should settle through SWIFT MT103 clean payments for standard licensing fees. Where a distributor requires documentary proof of territory rights before payment, the appropriate rail is an irrevocable commercial letter of credit advised by a New Zealand bank, with the required documents including the signed licensing agreement, invoice, and certificate of film copyright ownership. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is expected for all non-book-value cross-border related-party transactions. Inland Revenue generally applies the OECD Local File / Master File documentation standard where New Zealand group turnover exceeds NZ$30 million, and Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more. For smaller taxpayers, any related-party international transaction above NZ$3 million in an income year should be backed by a full TP study using recognised OECD methodologies.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not administer a separate Economic Substance Regulations code, but substantive economic activity is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule (Income Tax Act 2007 s BG1), the business-continuity test for loss carry-forward, corporate residency rules, and anti-conduit/PBV limitation. An NZ_LTD in the film licensing sector must show real decision-making, local contracting capacity, risk assumption, and financial substance; a shell used solely to route film royalties to an offshore trust is vulnerable to reconstruction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994 constitutes an offence carrying a fine of up to NZ$300,000 for an individual and up to NZ$500,000 for a body corporate, with imprisonment of up to five years for natural persons. Civil shortfall penalties of 150% of the tax evasion amount and daily UOMI also apply, and the unpaid tax remains recoverable in full.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause or participate in the evasion may be treated as principals or joint offenders; the corporate veil may be pierced for civil restitution, assets may be frozen, travel bans may be issued pending prosecution, and imprisonment is possible on conviction.
