Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The Companies Act 1993 requires a registered office in New Zealand that is a physical street address where documents can be served; a PO Box or digital mailbox is not sufficient. A genuine serviced office or law-firm physical office used as the registered office is acceptable to the Companies Office, Inland Revenue, and AML/CFT regulated banks only if an authorised agent is present to receive and forward service, lease evidence exists, and the address is not a virtual mailbox. For the Amazon FBA online retail model, banks will likely require a physical trading, storage, or principal place of business address in addition to the registered office; exclusive virtual office arrangements are therefore resisted and may prevent bank account opening in New Zealand.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) is the principal tax authority for income tax and Goods and Services Tax (GST) registration; New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS) administers export declarations and border clearance. Electronic portals: myIR (IRD online portal) and New Zealand Trade Single Window (TSW).

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand incorporated Limited Company, register the company at the Companies Office and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). An IRD number is then obtained from Inland Revenue by registering the company for tax obligations through myIR, providing incorporation details, registered office, director/resident agent information, and business address. The same IRD number functions as the GST registration number once GST enrolment is approved. A foreign non-resident entity that is not incorporated in New Zealand but is carrying on taxable activity in New Zealand must apply for an IRD number through the myIR portal or a paper application to Inland Revenue, and separately register for GST under the remote-services or general GST registration rules if its New Zealand-taxable supplies exceed the NZD 60,000 threshold; no separate national VAT ID exists outside the IRD number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime. Companies are automatically taxed at entity level. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act 2007 and Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 may apply to prevent double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in a 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services and digital goods are also required to register when total New Zealand consumer supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

GST zero-rating applies to goods exported by the supplier from New Zealand, provided the goods are exported in the course of the supplier's business and the requisite evidence of export is retained. Services may be zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are not directly connected with land in New Zealand, physical goods in New Zealand, or performed on a person present in New Zealand. For an Amazon FBA seller, zero-rating is available only for goods actually shipped to overseas markets from New Zealand under a valid export transaction; domestic marketplace fulfilment in New Zealand is taxable at 15%.

  • Customs export declaration lodged via New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window
  • Bill of lading or airway bill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice/GST tax invoice stating a 0% GST rate and export destination
  • Purchase order or contract establishing the cross-border sale and overseas buyer
  • Bank settlement or foreign exchange receipt (SWIFT credit advice or inward funds record) evidencing timely receipt of export proceeds from the offshore buyer

New Zealand does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing approval, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is available on a voluntary basis through the Peppol network. GST-registered persons may issue conventional tax invoices provided they satisfy the GST invoice content requirements. Export entries must be lodged electronically through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window.

If Inland Revenue determines that zero-rating was claimed without sufficient export documentation or during audit finds the goods never physically left New Zealand, the supply is re-characterised as taxable at 15% GST and assessed as a shortfall, with use-of-money interest running from the original filing date. Penalties for not taking reasonable care, significant unsubstantiated positions, and abusive tax positions escalate from 20% to 100% of the tax shortfall under the Tax Administration Act 1994, while fraudulent or knowingly false export zero-rating claims may attract a 150% shortfall penalty and criminal prosecution, including imprisonment. New Zealand does not legally impose a fixed 180-day exchange-control repatriation requirement, but the 180-day benchmark is applied as the documentary settlement period for supporting export evidence; failure to provide timely repatriation evidence may trigger audit rejection and clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a floating exchange rate and does not impose exchange controls, surrender requirements, or central-bank approval for current-account or capital-account transfers. The NZD is fully convertible, and both residents and non-residents may hold, remit, and repatriate funds across the SWIFT network without prior RBNZ consent. This liberal regime nevertheless retains prudential oversight under the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act, the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2013, and the Overseas Investment Act as applied to sensitive land or fishing assets, not ordinary retail e-commerce. Profit remittances are taxed under the non-resident withholding tax regime or relevant double-tax treaty but face no FX barrier. Treasury must still satisfy RBNZ banking conduct expectations and FMA financial services conduct duties. The de jure absence of capital controls makes NZD straightforward for corporate treasury, but commercial banks independently underwrite source of wealth and beneficial ownership before wiring large dividends or intercompany loans. The same liberal convertibility applies to Amazon FBA revenue parked in USD or NZD, provided underlying trade invoices and sales records are traceable for AML/CFT purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio in New Zealand. Exporters may hold foreign currency in NZD or foreign-currency accounts indefinitely, convert at market rates, and use proceeds for trade settlement or investment. RBNZ merely requires supporting documentation for reporting and customer due diligence under AML/CFT, not surrender.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasible but ancillary. Financial technology providers such as Airwallex, Stripe, and Payoneer hold or use NZ-registered financial service providers under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and can legally service a New Zealand Amazon FBA entity. They are not Tier-1 banks, are not prudentially licensed by RBNZ as banks, and cannot offer NZD domestic payment settlement or credit facilities equivalent to a clearing bank. For inbound marketplace proceeds and low-value cross-border disbursements, fintech EMI rails are superior. For capital escrow, large FX forward contracts, and repo or swap lines, the primary account must remain at a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank. Legal constraints include AML/CFT reporting obligations, NZ remittance or relevant non-bank registration or authorised affiliate status, and restrictions on holding wholesale client money without proper trust arrangements. Overall viability is High for payment aggregation and Medium for treasury-grade liquidity.

Inbound non-resident equity injections for the NZ entity should be wired via SWIFT MT103 or equivalent in NZD from the shareholder's home bank to the Tier-1 NZ account. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

New Zealand imposes non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) of 30% on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders. Under applicable double tax agreements, the rate may be reduced to 15% generally, or to 5% for a corporate beneficial owner that holds a 10% or greater voting interest in the NZ company.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing documentation applies where the aggregate value of cross-border associated-party transactions exceeds NZD 1,000,000 in an income year; associated-party interest-bearing transactions require documentation at a threshold of NZD 100,000. The annual IR375 disclosure form must be filed with Inland Revenue for material related-party dealings exceeding the applicable threshold.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations filing regime comparable to OFC jurisdictions. However, the Income Tax Act 2007, together with general anti-avoidance legislation, mandates genuine business purpose and substantive commercial activity. A foreign-controlled NZ Ltd conducting Amazon FBA operations must demonstrate real physical presence, inventory storage, decision-making in New Zealand, and attributable functions; failure to do so may cause offshore recharacterisation, sham treatment, denial of treaty protections, and allocation of profits to the entity of resident economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Return

Statutory sanction: A shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax under the Tax Administration Act 1994; late payment penalties and use-of-money interest; criminal conviction for wilful evasion may result in a fine of up to NZD 50,000 per offence and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false IR4 declarations may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax under the Director Penalty Notice regime, leading to civil restitution orders, travel bans, prohibition from managing companies, and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Application of the general anti-avoidance rule (Income Tax Act 2007, section BG1) to reconstruct the transaction by disregarding the corporate form; denial of treaty benefits; 100% abusive tax position shortfall penalty on the resulting deficiency.

Director personal liability: Directors participating in a façade arrangement may have limited liability removed under the Companies Act 1994; court-ordered civil restitution, disqualification from directorship for up to 10 years, and statutory prohibition against self-dealing.
AML/CFT Breach and Undisclosed Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: For AML/CFT breaches, civil penalties for companies up to NZD 2 million; criminal penalties for serious or repeated breaches up to NZD 5 million and imprisonment for 2 years; forfeiture of illegally transmitted property.

Director personal liability: Directors of a reporting entity may be personally fined up to NZD 1 million and sentenced to 2 years' imprisonment for knowingly failing to implement compliant AML procedures and for creating deceptive payment structures.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustments under Part FG of the Income Tax Act 2007 reallocating taxable profits to New Zealand; 20% to 150% shortfall penalty depending on the taxpayer's degree of departure from the arm's-length standard; interest on deficiencies.

Director personal liability: Directors who actively devise or knowingly approve artificial pricing may be treated as shadow principals, with personal liability for unpaid adjustments; civil restoration to the corporate treasury and prohibition against exporting trader or director privileges.
