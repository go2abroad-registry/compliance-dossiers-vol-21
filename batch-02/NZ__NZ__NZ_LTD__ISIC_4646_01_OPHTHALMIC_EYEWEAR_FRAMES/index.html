Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) - New Zealand; online portal: myIR at www.ird.govt.nz. The IRD number is the sole TIN and GST/VAT identifier.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: For an NZ-limited company, incorporate or register with the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993. A foreign non-resident entity not already registered must first register as an overseas company under Part 18 of the Companies Act 1993. Step 2: Obtain or confirm the IRD number, normally triggered by Companies Office integration, or apply to Inland Revenue through myIR for a non-resident company or overseas company; a New Zealand address for service or New Zealand tax agent may be required. Step 3: Register for GST in myIR using the same IRD number; there is no separate GST/VAT registration number. Registration is compulsory once taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold, but voluntary registration is available for exporters to substantiate zero-rating and recover input tax. Step 4: Preserve the IRD confirmation, Companies Office registration details, and myIR account credentials for all tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None in the US check-the-box sense. A qualifying close-held New Zealand company may elect into the Look-Through Company (LTC) regime under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, but this election is not assumed and is separate from the standard default entity-level taxation. GST grouping elections are available under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months or expected to be exceeded in the next 12 months; remote services supplied by non-resident suppliers to New Zealand consumers are captured at the same NZD 60,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a supply of goods is zero-rated when the supplier exports the goods or supplies them in the course of export and the goods physically leave New Zealand within 28 days of supply; for this international wholesale optical frames business, the exporter must demonstrate delivery to a non-resident overseas customer and physical departure from New Zealand. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are not in connection with New Zealand land or goods and are not consumed in New Zealand. Remote and digital services supplied to New Zealand resident consumers are standard-rated at 15% under section 11A and are not eligible for zero-rating.

  • New Zealand Customs electronic export declaration, lodged through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, showing the optical goods, quantity, value, export date and overseas consignee
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air freight issued by the carrier and evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list cross-referenced to the customs declaration and the wholesale optical frames contract
  • Foreign exchange repatriation evidence: bank SWIFT credit advice, telegraphic transfer advice, or account statement showing settlement of export proceeds into the New Zealand exporter's account, functioning as the New Zealand equivalent of an inward remittance certificate

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API pre-clearance regime exists in New Zealand. Tax invoices must contain the prescribed particulars under section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Voluntary PEPPOL e-invoicing is available through the New Zealand e-Invoicing network. Inland Revenue accepts electronic records; GST records must be retained for seven years.

If the 0% GST rate is claimed without verified export documentation, Inland Revenue may recharacterise the supply as standard-rated, assess 15% GST, and charge use-of-money interest from the original filing date. Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, shortfall penalties apply at 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for an abusive tax position, and 100% for evasion or similar acts. False or knowingly incorrect export documents can trigger criminal prosecution under the Crimes Act 1961 and the Tax Administration Act 1994. If goods are diverted back into the New Zealand market, or export proceeds are not repatriated within the expected 180-day window, the zero-rating may be clawed back retrospectively and the full GST amount plus penalties becomes immediately due.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a freely convertible currency regime under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. There is no central bank-administered exchange control, no surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no limitation on current account transactions. Capital account convertibility is also unrestricted for ordinary cross-border equity and debt flows, provided the Overseas Investment Act 2005 consent thresholds are not engaged; for this wholesale optical frames distribution entity, no OIO consent is triggered unless the investment targets sensitive land, fishing quota, or significant business assets, which is not the case here. Profit remittances may be made freely in NZD or any convertible currency via licensed registered banks. The only barriers are standard tax compliance duties such as New Zealand IRD registration, non-resident withholding tax on dividends or interest if applicable, and AML/CFT source-of-funds diligence. The NZD has no dual rate, no rationing, and no administrative pre-approval for foreign exchange transactions. Accordingly, liquidity can be managed openly with no need for parallel-market buffers, though prudent treasury policy should still document economic substance and arm's-length pricing to protect treaty and transfer-pricing positions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory foreign exchange surrender requirement for export receipts in New Zealand. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign currency earnings in licensed bank accounts, settle them into NZD at any time, or use them directly for cross-border payments. No regulator-imposed conversion timeline or mandatory ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury clearing rail. New Zealand law does not formally classify EMI-style payment companies as 'banks' under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989, and none of the licensed non-bank payment providers hold direct settlement account membership in the Exchange Settlement Account System operated by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand. A fintech EMI could only provide pre-funded, pass-through payment initiation services, not genuine bank-grade clearing or FX settlement. Therefore, it cannot replace a Tier-1 registered commercial bank for statutory tax verification, capital verification, trade finance instruments, or direct SWIFT clearing. Use of an EMI is acceptable only as a supplementary wallet for low-value Platform_Pay transactions, subject to strict pre-screening and ECB-style e-money limits adapted to NZ AML/CFT rules; it must not be used for capital injection, dividend repatriation, or documentary trade settlements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$1,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions in a tax year; the Inland Revenue may request documentation for lower value transactions where significant transfer pricing risk is identified.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not impose a UAE-style ESR regime, but the doctrine of economic substance is entrenched in: (1) tax residence requiring the company's centre of effective management to be in NZ; (2) treaty entitlement requiring the recipient to be the beneficial owner of the income; and (3) the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG1 Income Tax Act 2007) which recharacterises shell structures lacking commercial substance. A mail-box entity or conduit used to access treaty benefits without real activity will be denied relief and the income will be re-attributed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 s 141: shortfall penalty of 150% of the tax avoided; s 143A criminal offence: fine of up to NZ$50,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years. The company is also liable for unpaid tax plus arrears interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or cause the filing of a false return are subject to civil asset recovery for the entire tax lost, a personal penalty up to 150% of the evaded tax, disqualification from managing companies under the Companies Act 1993, and criminal imprisonment for up to 5 years on conviction.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement – Shell Payments

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 s GC 13 (transfer pricing adjustment) and s GC 6 (reconstruction); shortfall penalty under TAA s 141 up to 150% if the adjustment is not a reasonably arguable position; interest on unpaid tax from the due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or implement the sham arrangement may be personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and penalties, ordered to pay equitable compensation to the company for breach of fiduciary duty, subjected to a travel ban (border alert) while tax debts are outstanding, and criminally prosecuted for evasion if the arrangement involved deliberate concealment.
DTA Treaty Abuse – Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: General anti-avoidance rule (s BG1) invalidates the tax advantage; the anti-treaty-shopping limitation in the relevant DTA denies relief; NRWT at 30% (dividends) or 15% (royalty) becomes immediately payable; shortfall penalty under TAA s 141 of 100% – 150% of the avoided tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the beneficial ownership declaration knowing it to be false are personally criminally liable under TAA s 143(1)(c) and s 143A, carrying a maximum of 5 years' imprisonment and a NZ$50,000 fine. Civil liability includes restitution of all underpaid withholding taxes and indemnification of the company against penalties, with worldwide asset tracing available to the Inland Revenue.
