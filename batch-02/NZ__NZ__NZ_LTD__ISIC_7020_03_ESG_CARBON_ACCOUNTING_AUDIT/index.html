Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under the Companies Act 1993 and no escrow or capital deposit account is required. Shares must be issued, but consideration may be satisfied by money, future services, or property as recorded in the constitution or share allotment resolution; the directors must certify solvency at the time of any allotment.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the Building Act 2004, comprising an annual fire evacuation scheme and building warrant of fitness for occupied premises; structural load and seismic compliance inspections against the New Zealand Building Code; and workplace clearance under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including a documented hazard register, emergency preparedness plan, and trained first aid and fire wardens.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake), Government of New Zealand; online filing portal is myIR (secure online services) at https://www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand limited company (NZ_LTD), the IRD number is automatically issued on incorporation of the company with the Companies Office, with the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) as the unique electronic business identifier; for a foreign entity not incorporated in New Zealand, the overseas company must register under the Companies Act 1993 with the NZ Companies Office, obtain an NZBN, then apply for an IRD number using form IR742 or through myIR; GST registration (where taxable activity exceeds NZD 60,000) is made on form IR742 or online in myIR, and the same IRD number is used as the GST number; no separate VAT/GST TIN is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a New Zealand resident limited company is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income; for New Zealand GST purposes, the company is a taxable person if carrying on a taxable activity; the business scope (ESG/carbon advisory) is a regular fully taxable GST activity, not within a territorial or tax-exempt zone.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not have a US-style check-the-box election; an eligible close-held company may elect to become a Look-Through Company (LTC) under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 (income and losses pass through to shareholders), and companies within a wholly owned group may elect to file a consolidated return; hybrid mismatch rules under Parts FH and GB of the Income Tax Act 2007 apply for cross-border arrangements, overriding such elections where mismatches arise.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period; voluntary registration is permitted if carrying on a taxable activity

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated under section 11 of the GST Act 1985 when the supplier exports the goods outside New Zealand within 28 days and the supplier is the exporter; services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed are zero-rated under section 11A (including remote services under s 11A(1)(a) where the services are not supplied to a person in New Zealand), subject to the supplier not knowing that the services are received by a New Zealand-resident person or used in New Zealand; carbon advisory, verification, and offset brokerage services will qualify as zero-rated exports if supplied to a non-resident outside New Zealand.

  • New Zealand Customs Export Declaration (approved CUSR/let export reference)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or other shipping/transport evidence showing the goods left New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and supplier contract showing consignee/ultimate destination outside New Zealand
  • Bank payment/foreign currency remittance evidence (or Inward Remittance Certificate where relied upon)
  • GST tax invoice or supplier's own documentation evidencing the export supply

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API pre-authorisation applies to GST invoices in New Zealand; e-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the New Zealand Peppol network (based on Peppol BIS 3.0); taxpayers may issue ordinary GST tax invoices electronically or on paper, and the standard payroll and e-invoicing channels are separate from IRD real-time clearance.

The IRD may audit export zero-rating claims and require supplier records to prove the statutory conditions (export within 28 days, non-resident recipient, no known New Zealand use); where zero-rating is wrongly claimed, GST is clawed back by reassessment for the relevant period under section 108 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 (4-year amendment period, with no time bar where fraud or misrepresentation is established), and the taxpayer is liable for use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties up to 150% of the tax shortfall (from 20% for lack of reasonable care to 150% for evasion), plus criminal penalties for knowingly evading GST; retrospective clawback applies with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand Dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. New Zealand has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations since 1982; there are no exchange controls, no restrictions on the purchase or sale of foreign currency, no documentary requirements for payments, and no prior Reserve Bank approval for any cross-border transaction. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and royalty or interest payments are unrestricted but subject to taxation: non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at the domestic 15% rate or a lower treaty rate under New Zealand's double tax agreements; shareholder interest paid to a non-resident lender may instead attract the Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) at 2% provided the lender is not associated and the AIL requirements under the Stamp and Cheque Duties Act 1971 are satisfied. Thin capitalisation rules (40% maximum debt-to-equity for non-resident-owned NZ companies) create an indirect constraint on excessive cross-border debt funding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable under New Zealand law. There are zero mandatory conversion ratios, no statutory surrender of export receipts, and no prescribed repatriation timeline. An exporter may retain proceeds in any foreign currency in onshore or offshore accounts, or convert freely into NZD. The only statutory obligations are general reporting duties: the Reserve Bank may require balance-of-payments and international investment position surveys, and Inland Revenue requires full disclosure of revenue in income tax and GST returns, including transfer-pricing documentation for exports to associated non-resident entities under New Zealand's BEPS-aligned rules.

Central bank supervision: The Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) is the central bank and prudential supervisor of all Registered Banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021. No separate foreign exchange administration exists in New Zealand: exchange controls were fully abolished in the 1984 financial deregulation and the Exchange Control Act 1953 was repealed. Residual regulatory duties relevant to cross-border flows are discharged by the Reserve Bank through the Banking Supervision Handbook (international claims and liabilities statistical reporting), the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for market conduct, and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) as AML/CFT supervisor for non-bank financial institutions under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium – viable as a supplementary cross-border disbursement rail, but insufficient as the primary treasury banking relationship. New Zealand does not issue a standalone e-money institution (EMI) licence; non-bank payment providers must be registered with the Department of Internal Affairs as reporting entities under the AML/CFT Act 2009 and may not take deposits or provide credit without Registered Bank status under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 and the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

A non-resident shareholder's equity injection or shareholder loan into NZ_LTD must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank financial institution transfer) to the beneficiary bank's NZ BIC and the NZD account of NZ_LTD. The payment must carry a balance-of-payments purpose code corresponding to 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' for shares, or 'Debt Instruments – Loan (Intercompany)' for shareholder advances, plus the company reference 'NZ_LTD' in field 70. The inward remittance is entirely free of exchange-control consent, but the company must hold the following documents for bank and Inland Revenue verification: share allotment resolution under the Companies Act 1993; updated shareholder register; full source-of-funds declaration; and a loan term sheet that ensures compliance with the 60:40 debt-to-equity thin capitalisation safe harbour. Where the non-resident investor acquires a 25% or controlling interest in the company, an Overseas Investment Act 2005 assessment remains required only if sensitive land or significant business assets above NZD 100 million are involved; for a pure advisory and brokerage entity these thresholds are not triggered. Interest on any shareholder loan must be priced at arm's length and either subject to NRWT at a 15% domestic rate (or treaty-reduced rate) or to the Approved Issuer Levy at 2% in satisfaction of the AIL election.Treasury disbursements for trade and services should use the following rails: (1) SWIFT MT103, preferably MT103 GPI with end-to-end tracking, for operating expenses, consultant invoices, registry fees, and brokerage payouts; (2) UCP600-compliant Sight Commercial Letters of Credit issued by the NZ bank and advised or confirmed through the seller's bank where the transaction involves high-value registered carbon credit purchases and document-vs-payment matching (invoice, registry transfer statement, retirement certificate, transport certificates); (3) ISO 20022 pacs.008 credit transfers for same-day, machine-readable cross-border payments initiated from modern treasury APIs; and (4) domestic NZD clearing for onshore payments via Payments NZ Bulk Electronic Clearing System and the Reserve Bank's Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) for same-day high-value clearing. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on fully imputed dividends paid to non-resident shareholders is 5% where the company has sufficient imputation credits; otherwise 15% (standard NRWT rate). The treaty rate under many NZ DTAs is 15% (or 5% for substantial shareholding of 10% or more where the recipient is a company), but a reduced rate may apply only if the recipient is the beneficial owner. An approved issuer levy (AIL) regime may substitute NRWT on interest and certain conduit arrangements but is not directly relevant to dividends.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a standard 31 March balance date, the annual IR4 return and terminal tax payment are due by 7 July following the end of the tax year (where the return is filed electronically by a tax agent, the due date may be extended to 31 March of the following year for non-individual taxpayers). Provisional tax is typically paid in three installments (e.g., 28 August, 15 January, and 7 May for a March balance date). Different due dates apply for non-standard balance dates.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand does not prescribe a single numerical threshold for mandatory Local File or Master File, but the IRD's transfer pricing guidelines require contemporaneous documentation where cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZD 1,000,000 in aggregate for services, NZD 2,500,000 for goods, and NZD 500,000 for intangibles and financing. In practice, any material cross-border related-party transaction in the carbon audit and offset brokerage industry should be documented. Large multinational groups (consolidated group revenue over EUR 750 million) must file a Country-by-Country Report and prepare Master File and Local File in accordance with OECD TP Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Instead, substance is assessed under the statutory general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and the common law 'substance over form' doctrine. An entity lacking commercial substance (no physical office, no employees, no actual management and control in NZ) is vulnerable to recharacterisation and treaty denial under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) in NZ DTAs. For the NZ_LTD, core functions (carbon audit engagement, offset brokerage contracts, client relationship management) must be performed in NZ with adequate personnel, records, and decision-making. The IRD may impose transfer pricing adjustments and attribute profit to a permanent establishment if economic substance is deficient. The OECD's revised guidance on permanent establishment applies, and the IRD may disregard a shell company arrangement and tax the foreign principal directly.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall for evasion (100% for abusive tax position, 50% for overt disregard). Criminal conviction under the Tax Administration Act 1994 (sections 143A, 143B) may impose on the company a fine of up to NZD 50,000 (first offence) or NZD 100,000 (subsequent offence) and on officers imprisonment for up to five years. The Commissioner may also issue an estimated assessment, recover the tax as a preferential debt, and apply daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable under section 149 of the Tax Administration Act for tax debts if they knowingly permitted the failure to file or pay. Under the Companies Act 1993, they may be liable for reckless trading (section 135) and for allowing the company to incur obligations without reasonable belief of performance (section 136). Criminal liability attaches to directors who participated in the evasion, with imprisonment up to five years. Civil restitution orders, personal recovery orders, travel bans, and asset restraint orders under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009 are available.
International Tax Evasion and Offshore Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The IRD may invoke transfer pricing adjustments (Income Tax Act 2007, sections GC 6 to GC 14) and the GAAR (section BG 1) to void the arrangement and reattribute income. Shortfall penalties of 20% to 100% apply, plus interest. If the arrangement is fraudulent, criminal penalties and prosecution may follow. The DTA may be denied under the Principal Purpose Test, resulting in full statutory withholding taxes. For CbC reporting failures, penalties for non-compliance with country-by-country reporting can reach NZD 100,000 for each failure to file, plus additional daily penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or direct the offshore profit-shifting may be personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall under section 149. Under the Companies Act, directors may be disqualified for up to five years for failing to keep proper records or for fraudulent trading (section 380). Criminal prosecution for tax evasion may result in imprisonment of up to five years, and for money laundering under the Crimes Act 1961 (if proceeds are laundered through a bank account), up to seven years. Travel bans can be imposed to secure tax recovery.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The IRD may disregard the corporate form under the GAAR and the 'sham' doctrine, assessing the foreign principal directly. The company may be deregistered under the Companies Act 1993 for non-compliance, and directors may be prohibited from managing companies. Treaty benefits are denied under the PPT. Tax penalties of up to 150% for evasion, plus interest, apply. In addition, a non-resident foreign principal may be deemed to have a permanent establishment in New Zealand, exposing the full attributable profits to 28% corporate tax and NRWT on repatriations.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell entity can be held personally liable for the company's tax liabilities and penalties. They may face disqualification orders, civil liability for fraudulent trading, and criminal conviction if they knowingly used the shell to defeat tax obligations, with imprisonment of up to five years. Asset confiscation orders and travel bans are possible.
AML/CFT and Fraudulent ESG Certification

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT), a reporting entity (including certain financial service providers) that breaches its AML/CFT obligations is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding NZD 500,000 for a body corporate and NZD 50,000 for individuals for each offence, with higher penalties on indictment (up to NZD 2 million for serious non-compliance). For misleading ESG claims, the Financial Markets Authority may impose civil penalties under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 up to NZD 1 million for a company, and the Fair Trading Act 1986 provides penalties up to NZD 600,000 per offence. The Climate Change Response Act 2002 imposes fines up to NZD 100,000 and imprisonment up to four years for fraudulent emission unit transactions. The IRD will also deny any false deductions or credits related to carbon offsets.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers of an AML/CFT reporting entity may be personally liable for offences committed with their authority, consent, or knowledge, facing penalties of up to NZD 500,000 and imprisonment for up to two years under the AML/CFT Act. Directors may also incur civil liability and disqualification under the Companies Act for reckless conduct. For fraudulent ESG certificates, individual directors may face personal fines up to NZD 200,000 under the Financial Markets Conduct Act and imprisonment up to five years. Travel bans and asset forfeiture may apply. Extradition may be sought for offences connected to international carbon credit fraud.
