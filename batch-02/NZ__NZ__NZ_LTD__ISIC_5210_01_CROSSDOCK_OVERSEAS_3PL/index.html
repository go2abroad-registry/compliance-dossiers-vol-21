Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

New Zealand law requires a registered office to be a physical street address in New Zealand, not a postal box. A virtual office that provides a physical street address and accepts government correspondence is permissible for registration and basic compliance, but local tax authorities (Inland Revenue Department) and banks will reject a commercial mailbox as the sole representation for a 3PL cross-dock e-commerce fulfillment operation. This industry demands demonstrable commercial substance: a leased or owned warehouse, dedicated staff, inventory management systems, and operational presence to support GST registration, payroll, customs bonded processing, and true economic activity. Thus, a virtual office may be used only as a registered address adjunct, never as the physical fulfillment site, and banks will require lease deeds and utility bills for the operational premises.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Companies Act 1993 and New Zealand regulations do not require any minimum paid-up capital. Ordinary shares may be issued at par or at a premium, and no blocked escrow or withheld capital account is mandated by law. Directors must ensure the company remains solvent before issuing shares or dividends in accordance with section 52 of the Companies Act 1993.

Funding & unlock path: Mandatory Building Warrant of Fitness under the Building Act 2004 for fire suppression systems, sprinklers, emergency warning systems, and fire doors; approved Fire Evacuation Scheme under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017; WorkSafe notification and prescribed workplace safety policies under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015; structural load clearance from a chartered professional engineer for racking, mezzanine floors, dock levellers, and heavy-duty storage areas.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Tāke), New Zealand, operating the myIR secure online tax portal; customs control is administered separately by the New Zealand Customs Service for export declarations.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity seeking a New Zealand IRD number and GST registration: (1) Determine New Zealand GST registration status using the NZD 60,000 annual taxable turnover test, including any obligation for non-resident suppliers of remote services or low-value imported goods. (2) Submit the prescribed Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application to Inland Revenue, providing certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, board and ownership details, and the entity's overseas tax residence information. (3) If appointed, name a New Zealand agent or authorised representative for service of notices and tax communications. (4) Inland Revenue issues an IRD number; use that number and myIR credentials to lodge a GST registration application under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, selecting the applicable return filing frequency (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for GST. New Zealand has no general check-the-box entity classification and no GST group election for cross-border passive holding companies; income tax carries a look-through company (LTC) election for qualifying New Zealand close companies, subject to strict New Zealand-resident ownership criteria, but this does not alter the indirect tax classification of an NZ Limited Company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in a 12-month period; non-resident remote-service suppliers to New Zealand consumers and non-resident suppliers of low-value imported goods are also subject to the same NZD 60,000 annual turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand, or where a customer receives the goods in New Zealand and exports them within 28 days, provided the export is evidenced to the satisfaction of Inland Revenue. Services related to exported goods and qualifying remote or offshore services supplied to a non-resident customer may also be zero-rated under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. The supplier must hold commercial and customs documentation proving the goods physically left New Zealand and that the transaction was a genuine cross-border supply; for cross-border e-commerce fulfilment, the contract must identify the overseas principal or buyer and the overseas final destination.

  • Customs export declaration (export entry) accepted by the New Zealand Customs Service, lodged through the Trade Single Window or approved integrated cargo system
  • Bill of lading or airway bill evidencing shipment from a New Zealand port or airport
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order identifying the offshore customer and final overseas delivery address
  • Packing list and courier or carrier proof of delivery records
  • Customs or shipping warehouse evidence showing the goods were not redirected back into New Zealand consumption
  • Bank and foreign exchange records, such as SWIFT MT103 or NZD inward remittance statements, showing payment from the offshore counterparty; an Inward Remittance Certificate is not a New Zealand statutory customs requirement

No real-time tax authority clearance, anti-fraud digital stamping, or mandatory electronic invoicing. New Zealand operates on a self-issuance GST invoice model. Taxable invoices must contain the supplier's name and NZ GST/IRD number, invoice date, supplier and recipient details, description, quantity, price, GST amount, and must be issued within 28 calendar days after supply. E-invoicing via the Peppol network is voluntary. GST records must be kept in either paper or electronic form for seven years.

Inland Revenue can deny zero-rating and assess GST at 15% on the original supply where export evidence is incomplete, false, or generated solely to obtain zero-rating. The taxpayer must then pay output tax, use-of-money interest from the original GST due date, late payment penalties, and shortfall penalties ranging from 20% for a failure to exercise reasonable care up to 150% for evasion. Criminal liability or prosecution may arise for knowingly producing false customs or export documentation, with mandatory record retention of seven years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised, free-floating foreign exchange regime under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989, with no restrictions on current account or capital account convertibility. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) trades freely in global interbank markets, and there are no exchange control approvals, no mandatory central bank notification for inward or outward transfers, and no surrender requirements on export proceeds. Profits, dividends, and capital repatriation may be remitted without prior regulatory consent. The only statutory obligations are general AML/CFT reporting under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and tax compliance. For a non-resident owned NZ limited company engaged in cross-border 3PL fulfillment, dividends can be declared under NZ GAAP and paid via SWIFT MT103 without FX allocation constraints. Inbound equity injections require Companies Office share allotment filings; no central bank permission is needed. The practical constraint is that cross-border payment rails must be priced against NZD liquidity in the offshore NDF market; APAC e-commerce settlement currency mixes create natural hedging opportunities. Overall, the jurisdiction poses minimal FX friction to treasury operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules apply in New Zealand. Export foreign currency receipts may be retained offshore or repatriated freely in full (100% maintainable) with no statutory conversion ratio and no mandatory deadline. The only duties are accurate accounting records for tax filing and standard AML/CFT transaction monitoring for inbound remittances.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Airwallex (NZ) Limited holds a New Zealand Financial Service Provider registration and is legally permitted to service NZ corporate clients for multi-currency settlement, local ACH direct debit, and global payout rails. Viability is rated Medium-High: EMIs are not barred in New Zealand, but they cannot act as the sole bank for statutory capital verification or provide committed lending facilities. The recommended treasury architecture is to pair a Tier-1 domestic bank (ANZ or BNZ) for the capital escrow and primary NZD operating account with a regulated EMI for high-frequency cross-border ecommerce settlement, subject to the EMI's own AML/CFT programme being approved by the relationship bank and mirror transfers documented in the operating agreement.

No central bank approval is required in New Zealand, but the bank will reflect the inward credit as capital and request the Companies Office share allotment filing (AR30) to complete capital verification. Turnover: A certified independent audit is mandatory unless the company qualifies as a 'small company' and shareholders unanimously resolve to waive the audit. Under the Companies Act 1993 and Financial Reporting Act 2013, a company is not small (and therefore audit is compulsory) when, in any two of the three immediately preceding accounting periods (or all periods, if fewer), it meets any two of: (a) total consolidated assets NZD 3,000,000 or more; (b) total consolidated annual revenue NZD 6,000,000 or more; (c) 12 or more full-time-equivalent employees. Audit is also compulsory for issuers, overseas companies, entities with public accountability, and where shareholders holding 5% of voting rights deliver a written audit demand under section 200 of the Companies Act 1993.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% flat corporate income tax rate (Income Tax Act 2007, Schedule 1, Part A, clause 3)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% statutory Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) on dividends paid by a New Zealand company to a non-resident shareholder (section NF 2, Income Tax Act 2007). Treaty relief reduces the rate to 15% on portfolio dividends and to 5% on substantial-holding dividends under the NZ-China DTA where the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 25% of the capital of the payer. Under the NZ-Australia DTA, the rate is 15% generally and 5% where the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 10% of the voting power.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand does not impose a statutory monetary threshold requiring preparation of a transfer pricing file, but Inland Revenue practice establishes administrative safe-harbour thresholds: related-party goods transactions of NZD 4 million or more per annum; related-party services of NZD 1 million or more per annum; and related-party financial arrangements of NZD 10 million or more in aggregate principal. Multinational enterprise groups with global consolidated revenue above NZD 1 billion are expected to hold Master File and Local File documentation aligned with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines for related party transactions exceeding these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute. Substance is nevertheless enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, the thin capitalisation rules in Part FE, the CFC and FIF attribution regimes, and the shareholder continuity rules governing loss utilisation. A cross-border fulfillment and cross-dock subsidiary must demonstrate genuine commercial substance: physical warehousing premises in New Zealand, locally engaged or supervised operations staff, executed logistics contracts with independent third parties, and substantive board decision-making in New Zealand. Absent such substance, Inland Revenue may recharacterise the arrangement, reattribute profit to New Zealand, disallow intercompany deductions and apply reconstruction penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Evasion shortfall penalty of 150% of the tax avoided; penalty for insufficiently maintained records of up to NZD 12,000; criminal conviction carrying a fine of up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who directed, instigated or assisted the evasion are personally liable for the company tax shortfall under section 149 of the Tax Administration Act 1994. The corporate veil will be pierced where the company is a mere facade; directors face civil restitution, asset freezing, travel bans pending offshore asset investigation, and imprisonment upon criminal conviction.
Shell Company / Defective Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may reconstruct the arrangement to deny deductions, reattribute profit to New Zealand, impose a shortfall penalty of 40% for an unacceptable tax position or 100% for an abusive tax position, and apply NRWT of 30% on deemed dividends arising from recharacterised payments.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for the reconstructed company tax under section 149 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, disqualified under the Companies Act 1993, and subjected to asset freezing orders and travel bans where offshore asset-stripping or phoenix company conduct is established.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Thin Capitalisation Breach

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue adjusts taxable income to the arm's length result, disallows interest deductions on the excessive debt component, and applies a shortfall penalty of up to 40% for an unacceptable tax position or up to 150% for evasion; imputation credit account penalties of 10% to 20% are separately imposed for incorrect credit allocations.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false transfer pricing declarations or directed the artificial funding structure are jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax shortfall; in insolvency, directors may be assessed for tax debts under section 300 of the Companies Act 1993 for reckless or fraudulent trading, with unlimited personal liability and disqualification.
