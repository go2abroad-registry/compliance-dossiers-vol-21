Virtual office and commercial mailbox addresses are accepted by the Companies Office for the statutory registered office under section 27 of the Companies Act 1993, provided a physical address for service is given. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the Companies Act 1993, and no escrow account or blocked capital account is required in New Zealand. The company must, however, issue at least one share to a subscriber shareholder. The board must record in the share register the number, class and consideration for each issued share. Share certificates must be executed and delivered in accordance with the constitution or the default provisions of the Act. Capital may be paid in cash, property, or for services, provided the consideration is lawful and is approved by resolution of the board. No capital duty is payable.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; online portal: myIR at www.ird.govt.nz; business master data coordinated through the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) register administered by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE) at www.nzbn.govt.nz.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a New Zealand limited company, or a foreign-incorporated entity carrying on business through a New Zealand fixed establishment, is taxed at the entity level on its assessable New Zealand-source income; profits are not passed through to shareholders and there is no automatic territorial exemption for portfolio offshore income.

Available election forms

None – New Zealand legislation does not provide check-the-box or entity classification elections; hybrid and branch mismatch counteraction provisions operate under the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Tax Administration Act 1994 without permitting elective entity reclassification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any 12-month period; registration is compulsory when that threshold is exceeded and voluntary below it. Non-resident suppliers of remote services, including digital voice streaming and social app subscriptions, are mandatorily required to register for GST in New Zealand from the first supply to a New Zealand resident consumer, irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for: (a) goods physically exported from New Zealand within the statutory timeframe as part of the supplier's taxable activity; (b) services supplied directly to a non-resident person outside New Zealand where the services are supplied and performed outside New Zealand; and (c) remote services, digital voice streaming supplies, and app subscriptions supplied to non-resident end-users whose resolved location data confirms residence outside New Zealand. The supplier must retain contemporaneous commercial and technical evidence, including app-store account country, billing country, IP geolocation, payment rail country, and recipient registered address.

  • Customs export goods declaration / export entry lodged via the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window
  • Marine or air bill of lading / airway bill proving physical removal of goods from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Digital supply records for the voice streaming app, including app-store reports and payment gateway settlement reports
  • Foreign exchange remittance evidence: bank SWIFT/telegraphic transfer credit advice, remittance confirmation, or an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by an authorised New Zealand bank
  • Certified bank statement showing conversion of export proceeds into New Zealand dollars and repatriation within the statutory deadline

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or mandatory digital stamping regime is currently in force in New Zealand. Inland Revenue supports voluntary e-invoicing using structured PEPPOL-standard messages through certified e-invoicing providers; GST returns must be filed electronically via myIR or approved software gateways, and taxpayers must retain transactional records for audit. Inland Revenue does not operate an API-based pre-issuance invoice clearance system for GST.

Inland Revenue may challenge export zero-rating if the non-resident recipient evidence, customs export proof, or FX repatriation trail is incomplete or inconsistent. Where a zero-rating claim is found to be invalid, GST at 15% is assessed on the full consideration, use-of-money interest is applied, and shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall may be imposed under the Tax Administration Act 1994 for evasion, gross carelessness, lack of reasonable care, or taking an unacceptable tax position. False or misleading export documentation may also result in prosecution under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, and any over-credited GST input tax can be clawed back by Inland Revenue under the assessment and recovery provisions of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a completely liberalised, floating currency regime with no exchange control restrictions under either the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 or the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act 1982 (since repealed). Both current account items (trade in goods and services, royalties, streaming revenue) and capital account items (foreign direct investment, equity injection, intercompany loans) are fully convertible into NZD and outbound to any jurisdiction without ex-ante regulatory approval. There are no surrender requirements, no forced conversion ratios, no negative interest penalties on foreign currency balances, and no prohibition on opening foreign currency accounts domestically. Profit remittances, dividends and liquidation surpluses can be freely transferred offshore once the company satisfies the solvency test under Part 4 of the Companies Act 1993 (shareholders equity not impaired) and withholds the correct non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) or approved issuer levy where applicable. The only practical constraints are the Overseas Investment Act 2005 consent thresholds for large assets or sensitive land, and routine bank-level KYC and AML proof-of-funds diligence, neither of which constitutes a foreign exchange restriction. For a New Zealand limited company engaged in a global voice streaming app, the freedom to price digital subscriptions in NZD or USD, hold settlement balances in pooled wallet accounts, and repatriate profits is effectively unlimited.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirement applies in New Zealand. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts indefinitely in any approved deposit account, and there is no deadline for conversion into NZD or any mandatory conversion ratio. The RBNZs Balance of Payments reporting regime only applies to statistical reporting thresholds for banks, not to individual exporters; the company is free to use export proceeds directly to pay overseas suppliers or convert via a licensed bank at market rates.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), functioning as the central bank and prudential supervisor for licensed banks and settlement systems.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

New Zealand does not issue full EMI banking licences; non-bank fintechs can be registered as financial service providers (FSP) under the FSP Act 2008 and may facilitate payment aggregation, but they cannot accept capital escrow or hold deposits on their own balance sheet without a New Zealand banking licence. For this voice streaming entity, a registered local non-bank payment processor may be used for Apple IAP / Google Play payout onboarding, but it is legally and structurally unsuitable as the primary corporate treasury or capital verification bank. Offshore EMIs (e.g. Wise, Airwallex) may be used for operational payout corridors only after passing New Zealand bank counterparty risk and sanctions due diligence; they remain supplemental to, not a replacement for, a Tier-1 domestic partner.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial share capital injection, the parent or subscriber should remit via SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pain.001 in the NZ clearing nexus) to the companys new-money escrow account at ANZ or BNZ, using the wire reason S01 – Share Capital Contribution and the recipient financial institution BIC. For NZD credits, the payment will settle through the RBNZ Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) in immediate central bank funds, and the bank will require a funds received / capital verification voucher. The companys auditors will perform capital verification against the issue price and Articles of Association and file an annual return to the NZ Companies Office; no RBNZ exchange control approval is needed. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand dollar (NZD) 10 million threshold for cross-border related-party transactions triggering mandatory contemporaneous documentation (Master File and Local File requirements for taxpayers with related-party revenues or expenses above that threshold).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply classic ESR regimes like the UAE, but imposes economic substance requirements through the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, thin capitalisation rules, and the requirement that a New Zealand company be managed and controlled in New Zealand for residency. A shell company without substantive business activities in New Zealand risks being non-resident or subject to aggressive reconstruction of income under transfer pricing rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty up to 150% of the tax shortfall plus criminal conviction with fines up to NZD 50,000 for individuals and NZD 100,000 for companies, and imprisonment up to 5 years under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the company's tax shortfall and penalties where they aided or abetted evasion; travel bans may be imposed under civil penalty procedures, and criminal conviction can result in imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: IRD may disregard the shell structure under GAAR (BG 1) and reconstruct income as sourced in New Zealand, imposing tax at 28%, shortfall penalties up to 150%, and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors risk personal liability for restructuring adjustments if they approved or implemented the arrangement without substantive commercial purpose; personal liability for penalties is available under Part IX of the Tax Administration Act.
AML Failure

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to NZD 2 million for a body corporate, NZD 100,000 per individual officer, plus compliance orders; criminal prosecution may result in fines up to NZD 300,000 for companies and imprisonment up to 2 years for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable and face travel bans, disqualification from managing companies, and imprisonment if they knowingly participated in AML breaches.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income by the arm's length amount, plus shortfall penalty of 20% or 40% (unacceptable tax position), and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed incorrect transfer pricing documentation may be personally liable for penalties if they did not take reasonable care or if they knowingly participated in a tax avoidance arrangement.
Late Filing and Non-Payment

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalties as described, daily penalties, late payment interest, and potential use of civil enforcement remedies including seizure of assets and bankruptcy proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid tax withheld from employees or dividends (e.g., DWT/NRWT) where the company is unable to pay, under the PAYE and withholding tax recovery provisions in the Tax Administration Act.
