Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) – online portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/my-ird)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a New Zealand limited company, the company must first be incorporated with the New Zealand Companies Office (NZCO). Upon incorporation, the company automatically receives a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). To obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) for income tax and GST purposes, the company must apply to the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) for an IRD number. The application is made via the IRD's online portal (myIR) or by submitting the relevant registration form (IR596 for non-resident companies). The company must provide its NZBN, certificate of incorporation, details of directors and shareholders, and the intended business activities. The IRD will issue an IRD number, which serves as the TIN for both income tax and GST. For GST registration, the company must separately register for GST if its taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period, or if it voluntarily chooses to register. The GST registration is also completed via myIR. Non-resident companies may be required to appoint a New Zealand resident agent for tax purposes if they do not have a fixed establishment in New Zealand.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: Zero-rating applies to goods exported from New Zealand within 28 days of supply, provided the supplier retains evidence of export (e.g., customs export documentation). Services: Zero-rating applies to services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the services are not supplied in connection with land or goods in New Zealand, and the non-resident is not a New Zealand resident. For intellectual property services (e.g., patent prosecution), zero-rating is available if the services are performed for a non-resident client and the benefit of the services is received outside New Zealand.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) – New Zealand Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency payment

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoicing mandate. New Zealand permits voluntary use of e-invoicing (PEPPOL standard) but does not require API-based clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping. GST returns are filed electronically via myIR on a periodic basis (typically monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly).

The Inland Revenue Department (IRD) may audit export zero-rating claims. If a zero-rated supply is found to be ineligible, the supplier is liable for the unpaid GST (15%) plus interest and penalties under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Penalties include a late payment penalty of 1% per month and a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall for fraudulent or intentional evasion. Additionally, if export documentation is incomplete or false, the IRD may impose a civil penalty of up to NZD 250,000 for individuals or NZD 500,000 for companies, and criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment. Retrospective clawbacks apply for up to four years from the date of the incorrect return.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised financial system. There is no exchange control regime; the Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not require prior approval for current account transactions such as trade settlements, service fees, or profit remittances, nor for capital account operations such as share subscription, loans, or repatriation. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible and floats freely against all major currencies. Capital and current account convertibility are effectively unfettered, subject only to general corporate law and AML/CFT documentation requirements. Profit repatriation is achieved through ordinary bank transfers after board approval; there is no remittance restriction, but proper tax withholding on dividends and non-resident interest may apply under double tax treaties. In practice, the only frictions are tax withholding rates and the banks' internal risk-based due diligence, rather than statutory FX approval. Export proceeds are not required to be brought onshore and no surrender ratio exists. Accordingly, treasury planning should focus on efficient SWIFT routing, transparent source of funds documentation, and compliance with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rules apply in New Zealand. Exporters may keep proceeds offshore or onshore in NZD or foreign currency; no mandatory conversion ratio and no prescribed timeline exist. Only standard tax record-keeping and AML/CFT obligations remain.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua); no separate foreign exchange administration exists because the country operates a fully liberal capital and current account system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech EMI viability is limited to ancillary payment services only. Wise is registered in New Zealand under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and can facilitate low-value cross-border collections and certain WIPO-related fee payments. However, a non-bank EMI cannot provide capital escrow-grade account safety, documentary collections, trade finance, or the same level of assurance as a locally incorporated tier-1 bank. Some New Zealand banks may treat incoming transfers from certain EMIs with enhanced scrutiny or require source-of-funds explanations. Therefore, the entity should establish a tier-1 bank relationship as the principal treasury rail and use a fintech EMI only for niche low-value outbound disbursements after the bank account is operational.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or loan capital should be settled via SWIFT MT103 from a regulated source bank directly into the entity's tier-1 New Zealand bank account. The payment must clearly reference 'Share subscription / capital injection for NZ_LTD' and include the company registration number. While New Zealand does not operate a formal foreign exchange purpose code system like certain other jurisdictions, banks expect the MT103 Field 72 or remittance information to specify the transaction nature as a direct equity contribution or shareholder loan. Capital verification should be performed by a New Zealand chartered accountant using the bank credit advice, executed shareholder resolution, deposit confirmation, and the company's registry extract.Outbound settlements for patent prosecution services can be structured through SWIFT MT103 for direct official fees to WIPO and foreign patent offices where the beneficiary bank details and currency codes are known. For more complex trade-related settlements, commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are available but rarely necessary for IP service fees. Domestic New Zealand payments such as associate fees and VAT-like operational expenses should use the local clearing rails operated through Payments NZ, principally the Bulk Electronic Clearing System or high-value clearing system, while international disbursements above the local threshold should use SWIFT. Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under section 45(1) of the Financial Reporting Act 2013, an independent licensed statutory auditor is compulsory when the company is 'large'. A company is large if at the end of the preceding accounting period: (a) total assets exceed NZD 60,000,000 (approximately USD 37,000,000); or (b) total revenue for the period exceeds NZD 30,000,000 (approximately USD 19,000,000); or (c) 100 or more full-time equivalent employees are employed. An audit is also mandatory under section 199(1) of the Companies Act 1993 if shareholders holding at least 5% of the voting shares deliver a written request, if the company's constitution so requires, if the company is an FMC reporting entity under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, or if the company is required to be audited by another Act. Small companies with no audit trigger may elect an independent review (a 'limited assurance engagement') but must still maintain proper accounting records.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million aggregate cross-border related-party transactions

Economic substance scope: New Zealand's tax administration will apply the substance-over-form anti-avoidance rule (Income Tax Act 2007 s BG1) and the OECD 2022 Transfer Pricing Guidelines. A mere direction as a PCT filing agent without local employees, office, decision-making functions, or risk assumption is not suitable for profit attribution. The company must prepare CIGA mapping to evidence that the core income-generating activities and economically significant risks are performed in New Zealand.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion by understating income

Statutory sanction: Evasion penalty of 150% of the shortfall (Tax Administration Act 1994 s 141E); criminal conviction under s 143A, with fines up to NZD 50,000 for individual directors and NZD 100,000 for the company; in addition, a civil penalty of up to NZD 20,000 per false statement.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil under s 34 TAA: a director can be made personally liable for the company's tax debt if the evasion was assisted, directed or acquiesced in. The director may be subject to a travel ban (s 107) and asset restraining orders under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009. Imprisonment of up to 3 years is possible.
Shell company used for dividend stripping and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: The arrangement is void under the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG1 ITA 2007): Inland Revenue reconstructs the amount to reflect the true economic position, plus a shortfall penalty of 100% of the avoided tax under s 141D.

Director personal liability: Directors who implemented the sham structure face civil liability for restitution of diverted profits under fiduciary duties; a blacklisting notification to the NZ Department of Internal Affairs may block re-entry; criminal liability for fraud when the arrangement was dishonestly misrepresented to the IRD; imprisonment up to 3 years.
Systematic failure to withhold NRWT on outbound royalties and dividends

Statutory sanction: The unpaid NRWT becomes a debt enforceable against the company, with monthly incremental penalties of 1.5% per month (s 139A TAA). A failure to comply with a notice can attract a civil penalty of up to NZD 20,000 (s 140A).

Director personal liability: Under s 34 TAA, directors are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid NRWT and penalties. In addition, the director who knowingly caused the omission may be jailed for up to 1 year; the court may issue a travel ban pending restitution, and a personal bank account of the director can be used to recover the underlying withholding.
