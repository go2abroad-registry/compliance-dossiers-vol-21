Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A New Zealand company must maintain a registered office in New Zealand at a physical address; a post-office box or mail-forwarding-only address is not acceptable. For an aircraft engine dry-leasing special purpose vehicle, a virtual office with a physical office address, a contractual local agent, and a physical record-keeping location may be accepted by the Companies Office, but Inland Revenue and New Zealand banks will generally reject a virtual office that is only a commercial mailbox because it lacks commercial substance. Banks will require a physical place of business or an authorized registered agent, original or certified board resolutions, the underlying engine lease, insurance certificates, maintenance records, and evidence of the lessor's or lessee's operational activity. Inland Revenue may disregard an artificial arrangement if the SPV has no staff, no local management, no bank account, and no physical presence, especially if claiming New Zealand tax residence, GST registration, or treaty benefits. Accordingly, a compliant virtual office is allowed only as a physical service address with substantive local agent functions and complete records, not as a letterbox façade.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

E-government filing portal

For a locally incorporated NZ_LTD, the Companies Office registration process enables the IRD number to be issued as part of incorporation; for any foreign non-resident entity the procedure is: (1) establish that the entity carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand or derives New Zealand-source income, which creates a legal duty to hold an IRD number; (2) prepare certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitution or equivalent constitutive document, board resolution appointing a New Zealand tax agent/contact person, identity documentation for directors and shareholders, and where applicable an overseas tax residency certificate; (3) complete the statutory application using Inland Revenue form IR596 (IRD number application - non-resident company); (4) file it through myIR if possible, or in paper to Inland Revenue if myIR access is not established; (5) include a valid power of attorney where an agent files; (6) upon IRD assessment, the entity receives its IRD number, and for GST purposes the same IRD number is used with a GST suffix; (7) if GST registration is required or elected, the entity must file the GST registration within myIR and select a filing frequency (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly).

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand has no check-the-box entity classification regime. A qualifying close-held New Zealand resident limited company may elect look-through company (LTC) treatment under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 if all shareholders are eligible persons. No elective hybrid mismatch classification is available; the hybrid mismatch rules apply mandatorily.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any 12-month period (GST Act 1985 s 51); non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand consumers are also subject to the same NZD 60,000 threshold before mandatory registration, but a voluntary election is permitted where the threshold is met for a taxable activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a zero rating applies under s 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand and the goods leave New Zealand within the statutory window (generally 28 days after the time of supply) with contemporaneous export evidence. Services: a zero rating applies under s 11A when the services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed and the services are not directly connected with New Zealand land or with movable property situated in New Zealand at the time of performance. For aircraft-engine dry leasing, the lease must be to a non-resident lessee, the engine must remain physically outside New Zealand for the lease period, and the contract must establish the overseas place of supply/performance.

  • Customs export declaration lodged with the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window (TSW), showing engine type/serial number, value, exporter IRD number, and foreign destination for any engine physically exported from New Zealand
  • Original or certified Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical export, or for an engine already outside New Zealand, foreign customs import documentation plus registration/maintenance logs confirming continuous foreign situs
  • Executed aircraft-engine dry-lease agreement and lease schedule defining the non-resident lessee, engine serial number, lease rentals in foreign currency, and the place-of-supply/performance terms
  • Bank-issued SWIFT MT103/MT202 or telegraphic-transfer credit advice from a registered New Zealand bank evidencing receipt of the foreign-currency lease rentals (New Zealand does not issue a statutory FIRC; bank remittance records are the accepted equivalent)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API pre-clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping exists under New Zealand law. Suppliers may issue manual or electronic tax invoices; the statutory content of a tax invoice is controlled by s 24 of the GST Act 1985 (supplier and recipient names/addresses, supplier IRD number, invoice date, description, amount, GST). Voluntary e-invoicing operates on the PEPPOL standard under the Electronic Invoicing Act 2021. GST returns must be filed electronically through myIR on the assigned monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly cycle.

Inland Revenue may upon audit demand the full export and FX evidence bundle. If the zero rating is unsupported or defeated, the full 15% GST is clawed back and assessed on the supply, reinforced by late payment interest, late filing penalties, and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for failure to take reasonable care or unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. Fraudulent zero-rating or false export documentation may also trigger criminal liability. Assessments generally remain open for amendment for four years from filing, with a longer period in cases of evasion or serious non-disclosure. Because New Zealand imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, the field is set to 0; however the FX receipt evidence remains mandatory to substantiate the zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has a floating exchange rate regime and does not operate import/export exchange controls. The New Zealand dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions, and the country has long adhered to Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion of export proceeds, and no prior central bank approval for repatriation of dividends, loan repayments, principal, interest, rental income, or capital arising from a cross-border aircraft engine dry leasing structure. Non-resident shareholders may receive dividends and repayment of share capital freely, subject only to ordinary corporate tax, non-resident withholding tax on interest and royalties, and transfer pricing documentation. The practical friction is not convertibility but compliance with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act. Banks and registered financial institutions must identity-verify all counterparties, sources of funds, and beneficial owners. For an aircraft engine leasing company, large wire receipts from foreign airlines will have to be supported by leases, invoices, ECSN payment schedules, and evidence that the engine asset is physically held or controlled in a lawful manner. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand may require statistical and balance of payments reporting for large cross-border flows, but this is an information requirement rather than an exchange licensing requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rules exist in New Zealand. All foreign currency earned by a New Zealand company may be retained in any currency, held in offshore or domestic foreign currency accounts, and used freely for operational needs. There is no mandatory conversion requirement and no timeline for repatriation.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium for the primary treasury mandate. New Zealand does not license stand-alone fintech EMI institutions to take deposits or issue commercial letters of credit. Offshore EMIs may be authorised payment service providers under the Financial Service Providers Act, but they must rely on partnership banks for settlement and cannot act as the principal clearing institution for a high-value aviation asset leasing company. They are legally viable for collection of small operating lease payments, multi-currency wallets, and low-value supplier disbursements, but they should not replace a New Zealand registered bank for capital escrow, letter of credit issuance, and audit-grade treasury clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections should be executed as telegraphic transfers through the Tier 1 bank's SWIFT MT103 field, using the exact legal name and the bank account number in New Zealand. For direct investments into a New Zealand company, the receiving bank will classify the transaction using the standard balance of payments code for direct investment equity capital. The remitter should state the purpose clearly as 'Equity capital injection for NZ_LTD' and reference the lease agreement if the capital is intended for acquisition of an aircraft engine. No prior Reserve Bank approval is required, but the bank's AML/CFT unit will request evidence of share issuance, the completed Companies Office share allotment statement, and a certified capital verification letter from a New Zealand chartered accountant before releasing the funds to operational accounts.Outbound payments for trade and operating expenses should be routed through SWIFT MT103 wherever immediate settlement is required, especially for maintenance, engine overhaul, spare parts, and insurance premiums. For the purchase of engines and high-value components, a commercial letter of credit under UCP 600 with sight drafts is recommended, while documentary collections under D/P and D/A terms can be used for scheduled lease disbursements and routine supplier invoices. Turnover: An independent certified audit is mandatory under the Financial Reporting Act 2013 for a New Zealand company that is clearly 'large' in two consecutive accounting periods. A company is large if consolidated total assets exceed NZD 60 million; or consolidated total annual revenue exceeds NZD 30 million; or the company has 50 or more full-time equivalent employees. An audit is also required if the company is an FMC reporting entity under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, if the company is an overseas-owned subsidiary meeting prescribed thresholds, or if shareholders holding at least 5% of voting shares validly request an audit under the Companies Act 1993.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand's transfer pricing rules (Part G of the Income Tax Act 2007, section GC13 and the Transfer Pricing Guidelines) require contemporaneous documentation where cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZD 10 million in aggregate annual value. For aviation leasing transactions, the threshold applies to lease payments, engine maintenance services, and interest on shareholder debt; failure to maintain such documentation carries a penalty of up to NZD 100,000.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a codified ESR regime comparable to UAE or Cayman, but the general anti-avoidance rule (section BG 1) and the associated reconstruction rule (section GA 1) impose a substance requirement on cross-border structures. The OECD base erosion and profit shifting (BEPS) minimum standards are incorporated through domestic rules, including the requirement that New Zealand companies claiming treaty benefits or tax deductions for intra-group payments must demonstrate genuine commercial rationale, physical presence, and actual decision-making. Thin capitalisation rules limit non-resident debt funding to 60% of assets (or 110% of the worldwide group debt ratio) and restrict interest deductions where the group's New Zealand assets exceed worldwide debt proportions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994; evasion penalty of 150% of the tax shortfall; criminal fine up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under section 149 for tax shortfalls where they knowingly participate in evasion; courts may impose civil restitution, travel bans, and management prohibition orders under the Companies Act 1993.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Application of the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1) and recharacterisation of the transaction under s GA 1; complete disallowance of interest and expense deductions; shortfall penalty of up to 100% (or 150% for abusive tax avoidance) under s 141D.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate a facade arrangement breach their statutory duties of care and diligence under s 135 of the Companies Act 1993 and may be ordered to pay equitable compensation; they may also be assessed as personally liable for the company's tax shortfall under s 149.
AML

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 of up to NZD 2 million for a reporting entity (company) and up to NZD 200,000 for an individual; infringement notices of up to NZD 20,000 per breach may also be issued.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers of a reporting entity are individually liable for failures to comply with AML/CFT obligations; a conviction can result in imprisonment of up to 2 years under s 111 of the Act, in addition to personal fines and disqualification from managing companies.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under s GC 13 recharacterising the lease income and disallowing excessive deductions; compensating interest under s 120; documentation penalty up to NZD 100,000; shortfall penalty of up to 40% for gross carelessness under s 141A and up to 100% for knowing tax avoidance.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incorrect returns with knowledge of the mispricing may be personally assessed for the resulting tax shortfall under s 149; recovery actions may compel personal restitution and debarment from directorship.
Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of the excess interest expense under section FE 4-FE 18; recharacterisation of excessive loans as equity; shortfall penalty of up to 40% where the non-compliance is deliberate or careless; interest and late payment penalties accrue until the assessed shortfall is paid.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for approving the debt structure who knowingly allow excessive interest deductions face personal liability for the company's shortfall, including civil recovery proceedings and a potential order for repayment of diverted company funds.
