Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, a business unit of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), operating the Companies Register and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) registry.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No. 102), especially sections 12-16 (incorporation and registered office), section 22 (name restrictions), section 37 and Part 5 (share allotment), sections 151-152 (director appointment and consent), section 24 (written resolutions), Part 11 (financial reporting), together with the New Zealand Business Number Act 2016 and the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 for any public offer of securities.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NZCN1 - Application for Incorporation of a Company (electronic lodgement through the Companies Office online portal, Companies Register, incorporating the reservation of company name, registered office particulars, director and shareholder details, and the constitution where adopted).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 127 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. The Companies Act 1993 requires only that at least one share be issued at incorporation; shares may have no par value and may be issued for cash, unpaid capital, or non-monetary consideration at the discretion of the board. No escrow account or statutory deposit is required by the Registrar of Companies; directors must satisfy the solvency test under section 4 and the duty in section 135 of the Act.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from Fire and Emergency New Zealand covering means of escape, suppression systems, emergency lighting, and hazardous goods storage; Building Warrant of Fitness and compliance schedule under the Building Act 2004; structural load inspection and cleanroom certification by a Chartered Professional Engineer; WorkSafe occupational clearance under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 including electrical safety, hazardous substance storage, and safe work method statements; annual testing of hydrants, extinguishers, and fire alarm systems; and approval of the Chemical Inventory and Emergency Response Plan.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital. The Companies Act 1993 requires only that at least one share be issued at incorporation; shares may have no par value and may be issued for cash, unpaid capital, or non-monetary consideration at the discretion of the board. No escrow account or statutory deposit is required by the Registrar of Companies; directors must satisfy the solvency test under section 4 and the duty in section 135 of the Act.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake); online portal 'myIR' at https://www.ird.govt.nz; corporate registry and NZBN issuance via the New Zealand Companies Office at https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident foreign entity establishing a New Zealand presence or a NZ-incorporated subsidiary: (1) Incorporate the company with the New Zealand Companies Office to obtain an NZBN and company number, or register the overseas company as a branch; (2) Apply for an IRD number via myIR online or by submitting form IR596/IR742 (non-resident company), and register for GST on the same application or via the GSTR form; (3) If hiring staff, register for PAYE employer obligations; (4) For physical export/import operations, obtain an importer/exporter client code from the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window; (5) A non-resident company without a New Zealand fixed establishment may separately register for GST using the 'non-resident remote services' registration process, generally through a nominated New Zealand tax agent.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (New Zealand resident companies are taxed at the flat corporate rate of 28% on worldwide income; non-resident companies are taxed only on New Zealand-source income attributable to a permanent establishment. Closely held qualifying companies may elect look-through company pass-through treatment.) — For a non-resident foreign entity establishing a New Zealand presence or a NZ-incorporated subsidiary: (1) Incorporate the company with the New Zealand Companies Office to obtain an NZBN and company number, or register the overseas company as a branch; (2) Apply for an IRD number via myIR online or by submitting form IR596/IR742 (non-resident company), and register for GST on the same application or via the GSTR form; (3) If hiring staff, register for PAYE employer obligations; (4) For physical export/import operations, obtain an importer/exporter client code from the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window; (5) A non-resident company without a New Zealand fixed establishment may separately register for GST using the 'non-resident remote services' registration process, generally through a nominated New Zealand tax agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (New Zealand resident companies are taxed at the flat corporate rate of 28% on worldwide income; non-resident companies are taxed only on New Zealand-source income attributable to a permanent establishment. Closely held qualifying companies may elect look-through company pass-through treatment.)

Available election forms

Look-Through Company (LTC) election: a New Zealand close company (five or fewer natural-person shareholders, no corporate shareholder) may elect to be tax-transparent, flowing income, losses, and credits through to shareholders. Portfolio Investment Entity (PIE) election: qualifying investment vehicles may elect a flat 28% entity-level tax. No general 'check-the-box' regime exists; however, the hybrid mismatch rules (Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018) automatically recharacterise cross-border payments involving hybrid entities/branches, including NZ branch elections that create hybrid mismatches, and deny deductions or impose countermeasures without elective relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 – compulsory registration applies to any supplier whose taxable supplies in New Zealand exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period (or is reasonably expected to exceed that amount in the next 12 months). The same NZD 60,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of remote services (cross-border B2C digital services and remote services), who may also voluntarily register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, exports of goods are zero-rated if: (i) the supplier exports the goods themselves, or on the supplier's behalf, within 28 days of the time of supply (within 12 months for goods supplied under hire purchase or conditional sale); (ii) the goods are supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the goods are subsequently exported before being used in New Zealand; or (iii) the goods are supplied for removal to an offshore installation or on a 'call-off' basis. Under section 11A, services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not supplied in connection with New Zealand land or physical goods in New Zealand, and are not supplied to a resident for use in New Zealand. Digital X-ray flat panel detector OEM supplies (goods manufactured in NZ and shipped to foreign medical device distributors) qualify for zero-rating if the 28-day export rule is met and documentary evidence is retained.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Export Declaration (filed electronically via the Trade Single Window under the Customs and Excise Act 2018)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of the goods
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (showing overseas buyer, HS/ISIC classification, quantity, and value)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by the New Zealand settlement bank confirming receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Certificate of Origin (where preferential tariff treatment under a free trade agreement is claimed)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of invoices exists. Electronic invoicing is voluntary, with a national Peppol framework facilitating B2B and B2G e-invoicing interoperability. GST returns must be filed electronically via the Inland Revenue 'myIR' portal (or through approved intermediaries/software) on a monthly or two-monthly basis, and payments settled by the due date. Physical export declarations are filed electronically through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window, not via a fiscal invoice stamping mechanism.

The Commissioner of Inland Revenue may reassess within 4 years (extended to 8 years for evasion) to claw back improperly zero-rated GST, recovering the 15% GST shortfall plus Use of Money Interest (UOMI) from the date the tax was originally due. Shortfall penalties under Part 9 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply strictly: 20% for lack of reasonable care; 20% for an unacceptable tax position (where a significant position lacks a reasonable basis); 40% for gross carelessness; 100% for an abusive tax position (dominant purpose of tax avoidance); and 150% for evasion or an equivalent act. Criminal prosecution under sections 143A–143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994 is available for knowingly providing false documentation or evading GST, with fines up to NZD 50,000 for the first offence and NZD 100,000 for subsequent offences, and imprisonment of up to 5 years for evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange-control laws, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on the repatriation of branch profits, dividends, or liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance is subject only to standard tax compliance, notably non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at 15% (subject to treaty reduction, e.g., 5% under the New Zealand–China double tax agreement where applicable), and to banking due diligence under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT). For a non-resident-owned NZ Limited manufacturing digital X-ray flat panel detectors, capital injections and trade settlements may flow freely in NZD and foreign currency accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies. Exporters are free to retain proceeds in foreign currency accounts (Foreign Currency Accounts or FCAs) without any Reserve Bank mandate to convert into NZD. Receipts may be held offshore or repatriated at the entity's discretion. The only obligations are documentary record-keeping for tax, AML/CFT source-of-funds verification, and, where export payments are routed through the New Zealand banking system, standard SWIFT/TT purpose-coding requirements.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) acting as the central bank and prudential regulator; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) supervises conduct and financial services licensing. Foreign exchange administration is liberalised, with no separate exchange-control authority or State Administration-style FX licensing requirement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational acceleration. Licensed digital financial service providers registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR), such as Airwallex (NZ) Limited and Wise NZ Limited, are viable for low-value trade disbursements, cross-border collections, and multi-currency wallets. However, they are not licensed as registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 and cannot provide NZD settlement account guarantees or letter-of-credit issuance. For a medical device OEM undertaking SWIFT LC and TT wire payments, the prescribed clearing rail remains a tier-1 locally incorporated bank; EMIs are legally constrained from operating unrestricted commercial credit products and must hold client funds in segregated trust accounts with an NZ prudentially regulated bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from the non-resident shareholder is routed via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) to the NZ subsidiary's NZD or USD account at a tier-1 New Zealand bank, citing OECD Balance of Payments purpose code 'ED51 – Direct investment: equity capital' or the bank-specific inward remittance purpose code for equity participation. The remitter must reference the share subscription agreement and the NZ company's IRD number in the payment narrative. Verification procedures comprise source-of-funds documentary checks, anti-money-laundering screening, and confirmation that the transaction does not trigger Overseas Investment Act 2005 screening (share subscription in a medical device manufacturing entity is not a sensitive sector, nor does it involve sensitive land or significant business assets, so no OIA consent is required). The funds are then recorded in the company's equity ledger and the share allotment is filed with the New Zealand Companies Office.Trade disbursements for component procurement and logistics settlement are executed primarily via SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for open-account suppliers, and via irrevocable commercial letters of credit (documentary credits governed by UCP 600) for high-value and first-time counterparties. The NZD clearing leg utilises the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESSA) operated by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand through settlement bank participants. USD and other foreign-currency payments are settled through the bank's nostro accounts and correspondent banking network. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: An independent statutory audit is required for a New Zealand company that qualifies as 'large' under the Financial Reporting Act 2013, i.e., meets at least two of the following thresholds in each of the two preceding accounting periods: total assets exceeding NZD 60 million, total revenue exceeding NZD 30 million, or 50 or more full-time equivalent employees. Mandatory audit also applies where shareholders holding at least 5% of voting shares requisition an audit, where the company's constitution requires it, or where the company is an FMC reporting entity. All other private companies are exempt unless a shareholder or creditor triggers the audit right.
