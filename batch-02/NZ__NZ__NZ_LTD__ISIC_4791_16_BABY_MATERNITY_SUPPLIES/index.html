Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under section 190 of the Companies Act 1993, a company must have a registered office in New Zealand to which communications and legal process may be sent. A virtual office or commercial mailbox alone cannot satisfy this statutory obligation because the office must be a physical address and the Registrar may reject or flag a registered office that is merely a postal forwarding address. For an online maternity/baby supplies entity using Stripe and PayPal, banks and payment processors typically require a substantive physical business address for KYC/AML compliance, and Inland Revenue and the Companies Office will expect documented commercial substance. A physical serviced office in Auckland, Wellington, or Christchurch with a dedicated desk, meeting room, and dedicated staff is permissible, but a pure mailbox rental is not acceptable. The registered office address must be distinct from any virtual mail forwarding address and must be displayed in the company's business communications. The company should also maintain a New Zealand bank account, GST registration (voluntary or mandatory), and basic payroll/administrative records at the physical location to satisfy economic substance expectations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) / Te Tari Taake of New Zealand, acting through the Secure Online Services portal known as myIR, in coordination with the New Zealand Companies Office for entity registration and the New Zealand Customs Service for export clearance verification.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the NZ Limited Company under the Companies Act 1993, the company first obtains a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) from the New Zealand Companies Office. The company must then apply to the Inland Revenue Department for an IRD number by enrolling online through myIR, using the NZBN and company registration details. Registration for GST is completed within the same myIR enrolment workflow; the company selects the GST registration start date, filing frequency, and taxable activity description. For a non-resident foreign entity not incorporated in New Zealand but supplying goods or remote services into New Zealand, the non-resident supplier must apply for a non-resident IRD number and, if liable, register for GST under the offshore supplier registration rules using the myIR portal submission process. The NZ Limited Company, being a domestic entity, obtains the standard IRD number and NZBN simultaneously; there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A New Zealand Limited Company is subject to entity-level corporate income tax by default. There is no US-style check-the-box election. A qualifying closely held limited company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under the Income Tax Act 2007, which disregards the company for income tax purposes and attributes taxable income to shareholders; however, under the standard entity structure described this election is optional and not assumed. The company may also elect to become an imputation group or maintain an imputation credit account for dividend distributions. No hybrid mismatch elections are available under New Zealand domestic law beyond the application of the OECD/hybrid mismatch rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period: a person making taxable supplies in New Zealand must register for GST when total taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period. A non-resident supplier of remote services or digital goods to New Zealand consumers must also register under the offshore supplier registration rules when the value of such supplies exceeds NZD 60,000 in 12 months. Volumetric registration is permitted and an overseas company not physically present in New Zealand may register for GST for cross-border e-commerce activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for goods is available under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when physical goods are exported from New Zealand in the course of or furtherance of the taxable activity and are exported within 28 days of the date of supply. For the online sale of maternity and infant care commodities to non-resident customers, the supply qualifies for a 0% GST rate only if the seller retains evidence proving physical removal of the goods from New Zealand, transfer of ownership to a non-resident customer, and settlement of payment from overseas. Services supplied to non-resident persons who are outside New Zealand at the time of supply may also be zero-rated except for services connected with New Zealand land or physically performed in New Zealand while the recipient is in New Zealand. Payment processor settlement records from Stripe or PayPal that identify the foreign payment account and transaction currency are essential supporting evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged electronically through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window for the outward shipment
  • Bill of Lading for ocean freight or Air Waybill for air freight naming the NZ exporter as shipper
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the baby and maternity supply goods, quantities, and stated export sale terms
  • Evidence of physical exportation within 28 days after supply, including vessel or aircraft departure confirmation and any customs endorsement
  • Non-resident customer purchase order, invoice, or online sales contract identifying the foreign buyer and delivery address
  • Foreign exchange repatriation record such as a bank telegraphic transfer confirmation, SWIFT credit advice, or payment processor settlement statement from Stripe or PayPal showing receipt of the proceeds in foreign currency into the New Zealand company's bank account

New Zealand does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping for e-invoices. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and is facilitated by the Peppol framework. GST returns must be filed electronically through myIR at the company's selected filing frequency (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly). Businesses must issue valid tax invoices showing the supplier name and GST number, invoice date, description and quantity of goods, amount payable, and GST content. For zero-rated exports, the tax invoice must clearly state the supply is zero-rated. Records must be retained in English for at least seven years under the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Inland Revenue will scrutinise export zero-rating claims by requiring production of customs declarations, transport documents, payment processor reports, foreign exchange settlement records, and customer details. If the required evidence of export or foreign currency receipt is not available or the export does not occur within 28 days of supply, the supply is reclassified as standard-rated and GST becomes payable at 15% on the full sale price. Inland Revenue may retrospectively reassess prior GST returns and impose use-of-money interest under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Shortfall penalties range from 20% of the resulting tax shortfall for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, up to 100% or more for fraudulent or evasion arrangements, and criminal conviction and imprisonment are possible for knowingly providing false or misleading documents. The recovery period is generally four years, extended to seven years in cases of fraud or evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates an open and floating foreign exchange regime with no exchange controls, no restrictions on current account transactions, and the New Zealand dollar (NZD) has full convertibility on both current and capital account under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no surrender requirements, no designated foreign exchange settlement accounts, and no central bank approval for ordinary trade-related receipts or payments. Profit remittance and dividend distribution are permitted freely, subject to normal company law solvency tests and tax withholding obligations; remittance forms such as NZ Inland Revenue non-resident withholding tax declarations and board minutes are commonly required by banks for audit trail purposes. Businesses may hold foreign currency accounts and settle cross-border trade in NZD, USD, EUR, or other convertible currencies without any net position limit. The only practical hurdles are bank-level AML/KYC verification and reporting under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, which requires customer due diligence but does not create convertibility restrictions. The lack of an explicit exchange control framework, combined with OECD membership and high sovereign rating, makes loss-organization funding, repayments of intercompany loans, and dividend remittance reliably executable through any licensed bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rule exists under New Zealand law; export receipts are freely retainable in any currency, and the only obligation is to report cross-border transactions to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand as part of balance-of-payments statistical reporting when a transaction value exceeds NZD 1 million or aggregated reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua) in coordination with the Financial Markets Authority for AML/CFT supervision; no separate exchange control administration exists, and the New Zealand Overseas Investment Office (OIO) reviews foreign investment ownership where material.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers with no New Zealand financial institution licence are legally barred from providing deposit services to entities in New Zealand; New Zealand banking regulation restricts deposit-taking business to registered banks unless exempt. An overseas EMI may only be usable as a payment gateway ancillary if it carries out a registered money-changer or money-remittance business under the AML/CFT Act, but banks generally disfavour EMI-linked funds due to chain-of-custody gaps. Therefore, the viability for this entity is Low for primary treasury operations. The recommended structure is a New Zealand registered bank account with direct Stripe and PayPal settlement through New Zealand clearing platforms, with any EMI restricted to a fully segregated disbursement pipeline for small e-commerce payouts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared in New Zealand dollars where the value of cross-border related-party transactions in an income year exceeds NZD 2 million, or where the company is part of a large enterprise group (annual turnover above NZD 50 million) and the cross-border related-party arrangement is significant. The documentation must support the arm's-length outcomes and be available within 30 days of an IRD request.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the general anti-avoidance rule in s BG1 and the business/PE sourcing rules require genuine commercial substance. For an ecommerce company, core income-generating activities (warehouse management, supplier negotiation, pricing, inventory risk, and payment-rail contracting) must be performed in New Zealand by local employees and decision-makers; mere legal ownership of brand/website by a foreign affiliate will not prevent the Commissioner from attributing income to New Zealand and may trigger transfer-pricing reallocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion - online turnover suppression

Statutory sanction: Commissioner of Inland Revenue may assess omitted income plus use-of-money interest, impose a 100% evasion shortfall penalty under section 141E of the Tax Administration Act 1994, and file a criminal charge under section 143B; criminal conviction carries a fine of up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under the Companies Act 1993 where the company is a mere façade or fraud; directors who signed incorrect returns or knowingly caused the omission are personally liable for the resulting penalty and can be made personally liable for unpaid tax under TAA s 296 where the company is liquidated; they may also be blacklisted by IRD debt-collection, subjected to international travel bans, and disqualified from managing companies.
Transfer pricing manipulation / thin capitalisation of related-party e-commerce supply chain

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may adjust the transaction consideration and assess increased taxable income under the transfer-pricing rules in subpart FA of the Income Tax Act 2007, and impose a 20% shortfall penalty for an unacceptable tax position (s 141D) or 40% for gross carelessness (s 141C), in addition to interest and late-payment penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors approving artificially inflated related-party invoices may breach duties under Companies Act 1993 ss 131-135 (act in good faith and not incur reckless obligations). If the company becomes insolvent as a result, creditors and the Commissioner can pursue directors personally under the insolvent-trading provision (s 300), and courts may classify unpaid tax as first-ranking preferential debt.
Cross-border withholding tax failure and undisclosed profit distribution to offshore parent

Statutory sanction: The company is liable to pay the uncollected NRWT under the Income Tax Act 2007 and Tax Administration Act 1994, plus penalties: a shortfall penalty of 20% for lack of reasonable care or 100% for deliberate evasion, late payment penalties, and use-of-money interest. The IRD may also treat the arrangement as void under s BG1 if the round-trip financing lacks commercial substance.

Director personal liability: Under the Tax Administration Act 1994 s 296, directors may be jointly and severally liable for withholding taxes that were deducted or were required to be deducted but are unpaid. The corporate veil is pierced where the company was used to strip profits while leaving the New Zealand entity with no assets; directors may face disqualification, asset recovery, criminal fines and imprisonment under s 143B, and restrictions on international travel while the personal tax debt is escalated.
