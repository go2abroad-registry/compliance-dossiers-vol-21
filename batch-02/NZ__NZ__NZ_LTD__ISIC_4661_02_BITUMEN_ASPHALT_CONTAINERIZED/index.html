Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; electronic portal myIR (https://myir.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A New Zealand limited company obtains its IRD number upon incorporation registered with the Companies Office; for a foreign non-resident entity that requires a New Zealand tax identity, the procedure is to apply through myIR using the non-resident company registration pathway, providing the foreign incorporation certificate (certified English translation where necessary), company constitution, director and shareholder details, address for service in New Zealand, and, if applicable, appointment of a New Zealand agent or tax representative. Inland Revenue will issue a single IRD number serving as both income tax and GST taxpayer identifier; no separate GST registration number is issued. GST registration is completed on the same IRD number using the GST registration application in myIR before taxable activity begins.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None; New Zealand has no check-the-box or US-style entity classification election. A NZ limited company is automatically taxable as a company resident in New Zealand, subject to 28% corporate income tax, and cannot elect pass-through treatment. Hybrid mismatch adjustment rules apply under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018, but no elective pass-through mechanism is available.

15%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, supplies of goods are zero-rated for GST when the GST-registered supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days after the time of supply, either by exporting the goods itself or by delivering them to a carrier or to a customs-controlled area for export. For containerized bitumen/asphalt products sold to non-resident wholesale buyers under FOB, CIF, or similar cross-border trade terms, the supplier must retain documentary proof that the drums/bags were shipped from New Zealand and cleared by New Zealand Customs. Qualifying remote services supplied to non-residents for final use outside New Zealand may also be zero-rated where the recipient is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are not supplied to a person in New Zealand.

  • New Zealand Customs export entry lodged through the Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bank confirmation or SWIFT MT103 advising receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Aircraft/vessel departure certificate or exporter's certification endorsed by the carrier

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, tax stamping, or government API e-invoicing mandate is in force. Use of the Peppol e-invoicing framework is voluntary. GST-registered persons must file GST returns through myIR, retain invoice and export substantiation records, and produce them on IRD audit.

Inland Revenue may reassess claimed zero-rated exports as taxable supplies at 15% GST if the statutory export evidence is incomplete or the goods are not exported within the statutory 28-day window, resulting in clawback plus use-of-money interest from the original filing date. Shortfall penalties apply under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for an unacceptable tax position and 40% for taking an abusive tax position, with up to 100% penalty for evasion, and criminal prosecution may follow for knowingly false GST returns. Supporting export and foreign-exchange records must be retained for at least seven years for IRD audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange controls, no central bank approval requirements for foreign currency transactions, and no requirement to repatriate or surrender export proceeds. The NZD floats freely and the market is deep and liquid. Inward investment and outward remittances can be made without exchange control licensing, though certain large or sensitive investments may require consent under the Overseas Investment Act 2005. Dividend and profit repatriation to a direct foreign parent is generally remittable without restriction, provided the New Zealand resident company complies with the Income Tax Act 2007, including tax payment, RWT/NRWT withholding obligations, and FATCA/CRS reporting. As a practical matter, banks apply standard customer due diligence to every wire, and tax authorities require contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for cross-border related-party transactions. Because the business is in wholesale bitumen/asphalt trading, there are no surrender rules and no withholding on principal repayments. The only hurdles are corporate, documentary, and tax-driven: a capital verification certificate, invoice and bill-of-lading evidence for trade-related wires, and good standing/company extract documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand statutory law imposes no export proceeds surrender requirement. Exporters may hold proceeds offshore or convert to NZD at their discretion. There is no prescribed timeline or mandatory conversion ratio. Receipts are fully tradable and may be lodged to any bank account or FX facility, subject only to ordinary AML/CFT record-keeping.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) acting under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013; foreign exchange policy is liberalized with no central bank licensing for ordinary trade or capital transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium for this entity. Offshore digital EMI providers that hold foreign e-money or payment institution licences are not New Zealand registered banks and generally cannot open a standard domestic operating capital account in NZD or provide documentary trade finance instruments such as import LCs. The Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 requires service providers to be registered reporting entities in New Zealand before providing financial services in the jurisdiction; most offshore EMIs are not RBNZ-registered non-bank deposit takers and are therefore legally barred from acting as a primary onboarding bank unless they are separately licensed and registered in New Zealand. They may be used only as a supplementary payment or FX card vendor if a domestic Tier-1 bank remains the operative treasury and settlement institution. Therefore, the corporate treasury blueprint should rely on ANZ, ASB, BNZ, Westpac or Kiwibank, and should never route capital escrow through an offshore EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from a foreign parent should be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from the parent's bank in the source jurisdiction using the beneficiary NZD/USD account at a Tier-1 bank. The remitter should specify an appropriate tax-purpose code for non-resident investment, typically capital contribution or equity injection rather than goods or services, and provide a local supporting schedule to the NZ bank such as the board resolution, share allotment return to be filed with Companies Office on completion, and anti-money laundering source of funds declaration. Because New Zealand has no exchange control approval, the main compliance gates are the verifying bank's own economic substance and CDD checks, SWIFT gpi transparency, and capital verification in the entity's annual financial statements. For non-resident investment in a wholesale bitumen trading company, Overseas Investment Act consent is not ordinarily required unless the company owns sensitive land, fishing quota, or significant business assets; for the target scope the investment is permissible without a foreign investment certificate. The capital can be held in a capital escrow account and converted to NZD at market rate; the company should document the actual FX rate through a standard currency conversion instruction from its treasury operator.Trade settlements for containerized penetration-grade bitumen and bagged asphalt aggregate should be executed through SWIFT MT103/TT wire for small by-volume purchases, irrevocable documentary letter of credit under UCP600 for sellers requiring a bank payment undertaking, and documentary collections where bills of exchange and shipping documents are remitted through banking correspondents. For goods import or export through New Zealand, the NZ bank will need the purchase order or proforma invoice, commercial invoice, weight list, container number, bill of lading, and customs entry evidence. If the trade is transit or merchanting and goods never enter New Zealand, the bank may classify it as foreign-to-foreign settlement and apply enhanced due diligence; the rails must include an economic purpose memo and a stable message format. Preferred rail for high-value lots is a confirmed letter of credit issued by a prime bank to avoid counterparty credit risk, while repetitive small-value transfers use MT103 with same-day or next-day corporate clearing paths through the SWIFT network and local high-value clearing system operated by Payments NZ.Plain vanilla NZD/USD forward outright contracts with a Tier-1 bank registered dealer for covered commercial trade exposuresFX swaps for rolling settlement of bitumen import/export payables and receivables when cash flows are separately documentedFX options (call and put) on NZD/USD or NZD/CNY for optional coverage where the underlying commercial exposure is confirmed by invoice and LC evidenceCross-currency interest rate swaps only where a matched non-NZD working capital loan exists and the swap is entered with a registered NZ bank dealing counterpartyNon-deliverable forwards and offshore NDFs are generally unnecessary and should be avoided because NZD is freely convertible; onshore deliverable forwards are clearly permissible
