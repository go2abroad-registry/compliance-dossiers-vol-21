A virtual-office PO box or commercial mailbox is not permissible as the sole registered office of a New Zealand company carrying on government affairs lobbying. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) - Te Tari Taake, online portal myIR; customs counterpart for export controls is New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS) under the New Zealand Trade Single Window (NZTSW).

E-government filing portal

The foreign non-resident shareholder must first incorporate the New Zealand limited company under the Companies Act 1993 through the New Zealand Companies Office, appointing a New Zealand registered office and maintaining a director or compliance agent acceptable to the Companies Office. Following incorporation, the company must apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number through myIR or by paper application, providing the certificate of incorporation, constitution, registered office address, details of directors and ultimate shareholders with certified passport/identity evidence, and a physical or electronic address for tax correspondence. Non-resident ownership is permitted, and no minimum capital contribution is required. For indirect tax, the company must submit a separate GST registration application through myIR once it either exceeds the registration threshold or elects voluntary registration for export zero-rating, selecting a filing frequency (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly) and a New Zealand bank account for refunds. Where a foreign entity has no New Zealand bank account, Inland Revenue may require a New Zealand tax agent to access myIR and manage payments/refunds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid entity classification election exists in New Zealand. A qualifying New Zealand limited company with five or fewer shareholders may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, which makes the company fiscally transparent, but the default is entity-level taxation at the 28% company income tax rate under the imputation regime. GST grouping elections are available under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for related companies. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 apply to cross-border arrangements; no tax-exempt zone election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month rolling period; non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value goods to New Zealand consumers must register when their New Zealand-facing taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period unless already registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a supply of goods is zero-rated where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within the applicable statutory timeframe and retains reliable evidence of physical export. Services, including cross-border trade policy analysis and government-affairs lobbying advisory services, are zero-rated where supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are not supplied to a fixed establishment of the recipient in New Zealand. The supplier must document the non-resident identity and offshore status of the client, the binding service contract, the actual performance of advisory/lobbying services from New Zealand, and the overseas application or consumption of the output. Zero-rated supplies allow input tax recovery, and net GST refunds are payable by Inland Revenue via myIR.

  • New Zealand Customs Service export entry lodged and accepted through the New Zealand Trade Single Window (NZTSW), being the functional equivalent of a Bill of Entry, for any physical goods accompanying the engagement
  • Commercial invoice and detailed service agreement identifying the non-resident recipient, the description of cross-border trade policy and lobbying advisory services, and the consideration in New Zealand dollars or specified foreign currency
  • Bill of lading or airway bill for physical export shipments where goods are involved; for pure cross-border services, this documentation is replaced by the signed service agreement and service-performance records
  • Inward remittance evidence in the form of a bank statement or foreign exchange receipt from a registered New Zealand bank confirming that export proceeds have been repatriated to the New Zealand entity bank account
  • Corporate ownership, director residency, and client-due-diligence records establishing the offshore status of the recipient for Inland Revenue and New Zealand Customs audit verification

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, government API connectivity for every invoice, or anti-fraud digital stamping. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Peppol interoperability framework, with certified access points and Inland Revenue as the Peppol authority. Taxpayers must retain invoice records and underlying export evidence for at least seven years for GST audit purposes; no government pre-approval or cryptographic stamp is required for invoices.

Export zero-rating is subject to Inland Revenue verification under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and the Tax Administration Act 1994. If the supply is not substantiated as exported or not supplied to a qualifying non-resident recipient outside New Zealand, the supplier must issue a GST adjustment returning output tax at 15% from the original application date, together with use-of-money interest. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% for an abusive tax position. Knowingly false GST returns or fraudulent export-credit claims may lead to criminal prosecution and fines. Inland Revenue may reopen assessments beyond the normal four-year time bar in cases of fraud, evasion, or significant misstatement, allowing retrospective clawback of refunds and reversal of zero-rated treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully floating exchange rate regime with no foreign exchange controls imposed on current or capital account transactions. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible, and there are no statutory surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios for export proceeds. Capital account liberalization is comprehensive, allowing foreign investors to repatriate capital and profits without prior central bank approval, subject only to standard tax clearance and anti-money laundering (AML) verification obligations. For a cross-border trade policy lobbying entity, inbound investment, retainer remittances, and operating expenses can be routed freely through licensed financial institutions. The only practical hurdles arise from bank-level risk appetite for politically exposed persons (PEPs) and the need to demonstrate legitimate source of funds. Profit remittance through dividends or management fees must comply with Companies Act solvency tests and Inland Revenue non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) obligations, but no exchange control permission is required. This open regime places New Zealand among the most accessible OECD jurisdictions for treasury operations, yet the absence of capital controls shifts the compliance burden to robust KYC evidence and transaction-purpose documentation rather than regulatory approvals.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules exist. Exporters may retain all foreign currency receipts in foreign currency accounts or convert to NZD at their discretion with no timeline or ratio mandates.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. New Zealand does not maintain a separate electronic money institution (EMI) licensing framework equivalent to the EU or UK. Non-bank payment providers must register as financial service providers under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and comply with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, but they cannot offer full corporate banking services such as credit, deposit-taking, or capital escrow accounts without a banking license issued by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand. Offshore fintech EMIs that operate without a local license are legally barred from servicing New Zealand-domiciled entities for core treasury functions, as they lack the capacity to provide NZD clearing infrastructure, inbound capital verification, and direct participation in the local high-value payment system (ESAS). Therefore, a statutory lobbying entity must establish banking relationships with Tier-1 domestic commercial banks for all material liquidity and settlement activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be received via SWIFT MT103 into a Tier-1 domestic bank capital escrow account. The wire must include a purpose code corresponding to 'equity injection in a New Zealand entity' (e.g., BOP code EO0 for equity capital) and full correspondent bank details to avoid intermediary friction. Under the Financial Markets Conduct Act and Companies Act, the bank will require a capital verification report from a qualified New Zealand chartered accountant confirming the paid-up capital amount and share allotment. All incoming wires from non-exchange-control jurisdictions are freely credited, but the bank's screening engine will automatically query the sending bank for originator information under travel rule requirements. Supporting documentation must include a board resolution approving the capital contribution, a share subscription agreement, and a source-of-funds declaration from the parent entity.Outbound trade disbursements for retainer fees, advisory payments, and expense reimbursements must be executed through SWIFT MT103 R-message protocols, with each transaction carrying a purpose narrative aligned to the Inland Revenue non-resident withholding tax classification. For high-value settlements, the bank may require the presentation of a commercial invoice and a service agreement. Licensed activities may also use documentary collections or letter of credit mechanisms, but given the advisory nature of the business, single-shot wire transfers are the preferred clearing rail. The bank will monitor repetitive payments to jurisdictions with higher corruption indexes and will require enhanced due diligence for any third-party beneficiary not named in the original mandate. All outbound wires must be pre-screened against OFAC, UN, and New Zealand consolidated lists. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under section 45(2) of the Companies Act 1993, an independent statutory audit by a licensed qualified auditor is legally compulsory for a New Zealand limited company if any one of the following quantitative thresholds is exceeded at the balance date: (a) total assets exceed NZD 60,000,000; (b) total revenue exceeds NZD 30,000,000; or (c) the company employs 50 or more full-time equivalent employees. Audit is also mandatory for for-profit entities that are FMC reporting entities under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, for issuers of securities to the public, and where the Registrar or shareholders holding at least 5% of the voting shares validly require an audit. A company that does not meet any threshold is not automatically exempt if it is required by deed, constitution, or an enactment to produce audited financial statements.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% statutory non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends paid to non-residents. Double tax agreement (DTA) rates commonly reduce the rate to 15% for portfolio dividends and to 5% for corporate shareholders holding at least 10% of the voting power.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Transfer pricing documentation is required where annual cross-border related-party transactions equal or exceed NZ$10 million. Country-by-country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least NZ$1.15 billion.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a distinct Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code for ordinary limited companies. Economic substance is nonetheless enforced through the Income Tax Act's general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1), non-resident withholding tax classifications, transfer pricing substance-over-form review, and financial arrangement rules. Real staff, premises, commercial functions, and local decision-making must support any treaty claim, cross-border fee, or related-party arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty for evasion or similar act of 150% of the tax shortfall; criminal prosecution may result in a fine not exceeding NZ$50,000 and imprisonment not exceeding 6 months, plus outstanding tax, penalties, and use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause, aid, or abet the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unremitted tax and penalties, may be disqualified under the Companies Act 1993, and may face imprisonment, asset seizure, travel restraints, or debt recovery procedures that pierce the corporate veil.
Artificial Shell Company / Negative Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1 Income Tax Act 2007) voids the tax benefit, reconstructs taxable income, and attracts shortfall penalties from 40% (unacceptable tax position) to 150% (evasion). Insolvent trading provisions may impose personal liability on directors for losses to the Crown and creditors.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the company is a mere sham, ordering directors personally liable for civil restitution, compensation orders, asset freezing, travel bans, and disqualification from managing companies.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, serious breaches attract fines up to NZ$2,000,000 for a body corporate and NZ$200,000 for an individual per breach, plus compliance orders, formal warnings, and confiscation under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and directors who consent or are wilfully blind may be held personally liable. Money laundering is punishable by imprisonment up to 7 years, and directors face asset forfeiture, travel restrictions, and personal financial penalties.
Transfer Pricing / Related-Party Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may re-price related-party transactions under ss GC 6 to GC 14 Income Tax Act 2007 using OECD transfer pricing methods and apply interest and shortfall penalties up to 150% for evasion or 40% for gross carelessness.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly prepare or sign false transfer pricing documentation may incur civil penalty liability; courts may disregard the foreign shareholder entity and pierce the corporate veil to recover assessments from controlling individuals.
Corrupt Lobbying / Government Relations Bribery

Statutory sanction: Corrupting bribery of an official is an offence under s 101 of the Crimes Act 1961, punishable by imprisonment for up to 14 years; the Secret Commissions Act 1910 also applies with fines, imprisonment, recovery of the secret commission, and asset confiscation.

Director personal liability: Individual directors who authorise or benefit from corruption face criminal conviction, asset confiscation, travel bans, exclusion from public procurement, and personal liability for restitution, court costs, and other civil compensation through the piercing of the corporate veil.
