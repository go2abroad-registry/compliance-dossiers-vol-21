Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A New Zealand limited company must have a registered office in New Zealand that is a physical street address at which documents can be served; a PO Box, virtual mailbox, or purely email-based address does not satisfy the Companies Act 1993. For commercial substance, holding the entity's only presence at a virtual office is highly likely to be rejected by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and by banks for a re-invoicing hub, because this structure lacks the real economic presence New Zealand requires to avoid being treated as a shell, and banks apply strict 'place of effective management' tests. A virtual office may be used only for auxiliary mail forwarding, while a serviced office lease with a physical boardroom and part-time staff is the minimum viable substance.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; online portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz; entity registry: New Zealand Companies Office and New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) registry.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity forming NZ_LTD: (1) incorporate the limited company with the New Zealand Companies Office through the Companies Register and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN); (2) submit an IRD number application using Inland Revenue's Form IR596 (Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application), either via myIR or postal channels, providing the company certificate, NZBN, registered office in New Zealand, and particulars of directors and shareholders; (3) for GST, separately register for Goods and Services Tax via myIR; the GST number is the same IRD number with GST registration status; (4) non-resident companies must also appoint a local agent for service and maintain a registered office. Domestic New Zealand companies normally receive an IRD number automatically upon incorporation, but this SOP applies to direct foreign-owned re-invoicing hub structures.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Eligible New Zealand companies may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, allowing income to flow through to shareholders for income tax while GST obligations remain at entity level. New Zealand does not have a general check-the-box election; hybrid mismatch rules under Part FM apply non-elective to cross-border arrangements, and no elective hybrid regime is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000; compulsory registration applies when taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any 12-month period exceed NZD 60,000 (or where there is a reasonable expectation of exceeding that threshold). Non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value goods to New Zealand consumers must also register when their NZD-inclusive supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period. Voluntary registration is available to non-residents carrying on a taxable activity with a New Zealand nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, goods are eligible for 0% GST when the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand and retains the prescribed proof of export within 28 days. Services (including re-invoicing hub commissions, trading intermediation, and logistics services) are zero-rated where the services are supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the benefit of the services is not received in New Zealand; remote services supplied to New Zealand residents are standard-rated. The place-of-supply and connected-services rules in sections 8 and 11 apply; no GST is charged on qualifying zero-rated cross-border exports.

  • New Zealand Customs Export Entry (electronic Export Declaration lodged through the Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and re-invoicing invoice chain matching the sale flow
  • Packing list, cargo manifest, and certificate of origin (if applicable)
  • Foreign exchange repatriation evidence, such as SWIFT MT103, bank inward credit advice, or TT wire confirmation demonstrating payment in NZD or foreign currency received by NZ_LTD

No statutory real-time invoice clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate applies in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Peppol network using internationally standardised formats; suppliers may transmit e-invoices via certified service providers. GST returns (IR 301) must be filed and taxes paid electronically through myIR or approved tax agent software, either monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly, depending on taxable supply volumes; no per-invoice government API clearance is required.

Inland Revenue may reassess a zero-rated export to standard-rated GST where the prescribed export evidence is not held, resulting in GST payable at 15% plus use-of-money interest from the original due date. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 range from 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% for evasion or abusive tax position; deliberate fraudulent conduct can lead to criminal prosecution. Export documentation must be retained for at least seven years to sustain a zero-rating position; missing documentation triggers retrospective GST clawback, interest, and penalty assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates an open capital account with full currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the Reserve Bank Act 1989 and the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely floating and not subject to exchange control restrictions, central bank approval requirements, or mandatory conversion thresholds. Foreign investors and trading intermediaries may freely remit profits, dividends, interest, service fees, and liquidation proceeds offshore, provided that underlying transactions comply with tax clearance under the Income Tax Act 2007 and anti-money laundering reporting obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. There are no surrender requirements, no exchange licences, and no restrictions on maintaining foreign currency accounts. However, the RBNZ as the central bank and prudential regulator requires banks to collect statistical purpose codes for the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) to compile balance of payments data. Banking institutions must conduct customer due diligence and file suspicious transaction reports. The major practical friction is not legal convertibility but the economic substance evidence required to support cross-border fund flows, particularly for re-invoicing hubs where funds may not correspond to physical goods passing through New Zealand.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirements. New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign currency receipts. Exporters and re-invoicing intermediaries may retain proceeds in any freely convertible currency or convert to NZD at commercial market rates. The only conditions are indirect: banking AML/CFT recordkeeping, tax disclosures to the Inland Revenue Department, and RBNZ statistical reporting.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary remittance and spot FX, Low for primary treasury and trade-finance operations. Legal constraints: Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, and Airwallex are not licensed as registered banks in New Zealand. They must either register as financial service providers under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Disputes Resolution) Act 2008 and become reporting entities under the AML/CFT Act, or partner with a locally licensed bank. The RBNZ does not grant banking licences to pure-finance technology remittance firms, meaning client funds may not be protected under the Deposit Takers Act 2022. EMIs also cannot issue letters of credit, provide overdraft facilities, or offer dedicated capital escrow services. For a re-invoicing hub, relying solely on an EMI is operationally risky due to lower counterparty limits, higher rate volatility, and the absence of robust trade finance rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan funds should be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the parent entity to the New Zealand entity's Tier-1 bank account. The bank must assign the appropriate balance-of-payments statistical purpose code (e.g., 'SI01' for direct investment equity or 'SH01' for shareholder loans) under the International Transactions Reporting System. As no FX controls exist, the deposit can remain in the original currency or be converted to NZD at the bank's commercial spot rate. Capital verification is conducted through a combination of the SWIFT inbound message, the bank's credit instruction, and a completion certificate signed by the local accountant. For tax registration clarity, the injection should be documented in the companies office annual return and reflected in the IRD tax returns, with each funding leg supported by board minutes and a signed loan agreement.For trade settlements, the preferred clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for high-volume working capital payments to suppliers, with purpose codes aligned to merchandise trade. For larger procurements, a Commercial Letter of Credit issued through one of the Tier-1 banks provides documentary protection; Documentary Collections serve as a lower-cost alternative for trusted counterparties. All disbursements must be supported by underlying invoices, bills of lading, packing lists, and customs documentation to withstand audit by the New Zealand Inland Revenue and AML/CFT supervisors. Maturing tax payments to the IRD are made through the standard electronic filing system.Outright FX forward contracts for up to 24-month tenors on NZD/USD, NZD/EUR, NZD/CNH, and NZD/GBP with registered Tier-1 banks, executed under a board-approved treasury policy and backed by known future foreign currency receivables/payables.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for non-NZD currency legs where onshore hedging is technically restricted (e.g., distant emerging-market currencies); never as a synthetic substitute for an avoidable NZD delivery obligation.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) are permissible under an ISDA/CSA master agreement where there is a genuine hedging need on long-term re-invoicing working capital lines; outright speculation or net short positions are prohibited by the entity's treasury risk charter.

Legal constraints: Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, and Airwallex are not licensed as registered banks in New Zealand. They must either register as financial service providers under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Disputes Resolution) Act 2008 and become reporting entities under the AML/CFT Act, or partner with a locally licensed bank. The RBNZ does not grant banking licences to pure-finance technology remittance firms, meaning client funds may not be protected under the Deposit Takers Act 2022. EMIs also cannot issue letters of credit, provide overdraft facilities, or offer dedicated capital escrow services. For a re-invoicing hub, relying solely on an EMI is operationally risky due to lower counterparty limits, higher rate volatility, and the absence of robust trade finance rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$10,000,000

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have a Gulf-style separate Economic Substance Regulation regime, but the IRD applies the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, supplemented by the source rules and treaty limitation-of-benefits principles. A re-invoicing hub claiming New Zealand treaty benefits and trading advantages must prove actual staff, premises, business decision-making and core income-generating activities (CIGA) in New Zealand. Without such substance, the arrangement may be recharacterised as a sham or tax avoidance scheme.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion through Artificial Re-Invoicing Margins

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 section GC 13 and section GC 16 empower the Commissioner to adjust the taxable income to reflect an arm's length price. Tax Administration Act 1994 section 141C imposes a 20% shortfall penalty for an unacceptable tax position; section 141D increases this to 40% for an abusive tax position; and section 141E imposes a 150% penalty under evasion or similar intent.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign inaccurate returns or supervise the false transfer pricing may be personally liable under the Tax Administration Act as persons knowingly concerned in the offence. Civil asset restitution may be ordered, directors may be disqualified from managing a company under the Companies Act 1993, and a travel blacklisting or departure prohibition order may be issued. Criminal imprisonment of up to five years can be imposed for tax evasion.
Tax Evasion and False Statement in Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 section 143A makes knowingly filing a false return a criminal offence, carrying a fine of up to NZ$50,000 and imprisonment of up to five years for each offence. A shortfall penalty of 150% under section 141E applies, together with use-of-money interest and late payment penalties. Criminal forfeiture of assets derived from the evasion can be sought under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where a director is the controlling mind of the evasion. Directors face full personal civil liability for the unpaid tax plus penalties, a prohibition order preventing management of any New Zealand company, blacklisting and asset freezing through the Serious Fraud Office, and imprisonment up to five years.
AML/CFT Compliance Failure and Operation as a Shell Artificial Conduit

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a body corporate may face a civil penalty of up to NZ$2,000,000 per contravention for failing to adopt, implement or maintain an AML/CFT compliance programme, or for failing to report suspicious transactions under section 90. Criminal liability can arise for knowingly or recklessly providing false information to the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable where the failure is attributable to their neglect, connivance or consent. Courts may order asset restitution, a director's disqualification, departure prohibition and travel blacklisting, and criminal imprisonment for money laundering or financing of terrorism under the Crimes Act 1961, with maximum sentences ranging from five to seven years for aggravated laundering offences.
