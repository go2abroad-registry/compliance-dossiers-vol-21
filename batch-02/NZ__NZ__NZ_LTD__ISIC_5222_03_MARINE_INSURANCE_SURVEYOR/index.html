Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Section 34 of the Companies Act 1993 requires a physical registered office address in New Zealand that is not a post-office box or virtual mailbox. A managed office supplied by a reputable corporate service provider can be treated as an acceptable registered office if it is a physical street address and accepts service of process. However, Inland Revenue and New Zealand banks will expect actual surveying substance: an operational workspace, records, claim survey equipment, and a person accountable for business activity. A pure virtual office is likely to be rejected by banks for account opening and may create IRD residency and GST registration challenges for the marine insurance surveying business.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. A New Zealand limited company may be incorporated with one or more shares issued for any lawful consideration fixed by the directors. There is no blocked escrow account requirement, no minimum bank balance, and no capital deposit certificate is required before incorporation. Shares may be issued for cash or non-cash consideration, and directors must act in good faith and ensure that the value of the consideration is not inadequate in all the circumstances. Non-resident share ownership is permitted.

Funding & unlock path: A Building Warrant of Fitness and a Fire Evacuation Scheme under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017 must be current for any fixed office, warehouse, or laboratory. The company must also register with WorkSafe New Zealand if it employs workers and carry out a risk assessment of confined-space entry, working near water, fall hazards, manual handling, and site security associated with surveying vessels. No separate Civil Defence emergency licence is required, but the primary duty holder must include emergency response and evacuation arrangements in the Health and Safety at Work system.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) — online portal myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must apply for a New Zealand Inland Revenue Department (IRD) number by lodging the applicable non-resident company registration application through myIR or by post to the IRD. The application package must include certified copies of the company constitution and certificate of incorporation (or equivalent overseas registration evidence), details of the registered office and physical principal place of business, particulars of all directors and shareholders, and confirmation that the company is not resident in New Zealand. The IRD will verify the information and issue the statutory tax identification number (IRD number). To register for Goods and Services Tax (GST), the entity separately files a GST registration application in myIR once turnover meets or is expected to meet the mandatory threshold or if voluntary registration is sought; the IRD will issue a GST registration number linked to the same IRD number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Available election forms

A New Zealand limited company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status for income tax purposes if it meets the statutory eligibility criteria, effectively permitting shareholders to be taxed on the company's income; however, New Zealand has no US-style check-the-box election. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Acts apply, but no elective hybrid mismatch classification mechanism is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in annual taxable turnover, measured over any 12-month period, or NZD 60,000 projected for the next 12 months; offshore suppliers of remote services to New Zealand consumers face the same NZD 60,000 registration threshold under the offshore supplier registration regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for GST applies to exports of goods where the supplier provides the goods and they are exported by the supplier or an agent within 28 days of the time of supply. Export services, including qualifying marine insurance survey and cargo claim inspection services supplied to a non-resident underwriter, insurer, carrier, or overseas principal, may be zero-rated if the statutory tests under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 are satisfied, including the supply being made to a person who is not resident in New Zealand and the services not falling within a standard-rated exception (such as services supplied in connection with land in New Zealand or certain moveable property physically situated in New Zealand unless a specific exclusion applies). The supplier must retain contemporaneous records evidencing the export transaction, the non-resident status of the recipient, the commercial purpose of the supply, and the cross-border foreign-exchange payment.

  • Customs export declaration in the New Zealand Trade Single Window (NZWarehouse) or equivalent customs clearance evidence showing goods exported
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export and delivery outside New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and underlying contract or survey engagement order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank foreign-exchange receipt evidencing payment in foreign currency from the overseas recipient
  • Marine survey report and cargo/vessel claim inspection certificate demonstrating the nature of the exported service supplied

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or anti-fraud digital stamping. E-invoicing operates on a voluntary basis using the New Zealand E-Invoicing framework built on the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 standard. GST returns and remittances must be filed electronically through myIR, and all GST records must be retained in accordance with the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and the Tax Administration Act 1994.

If a zero-rated export claim is overturned on audit through inadequate documentation, fraudulent documentation, or failure to meet the statutory export test, the IRD will assess GST at 15% and claw back any refunded or previously offset tax. Use-of-money interest applies from the original due date. Penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 include shortfall penalties of up to 100% for gross carelessness, substantial understatement, or taking an abusive tax position, and up to 150% for evasion or fraudulent intent, with additional criminal sanctions for knowingly providing false tax returns. Directors and controlling shareholders may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid GST and penalties where the company has failed to comply with tax obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalized financial system under IMF Article VIII obligations, meaning the New Zealand dollar is fully convertible for both current and capital account purposes. There are no restrictions on the purchase, sale, or transfer of foreign exchange, no surrender requirements on export proceeds, and no prior Reserve Bank approval for cross-border capital movements. The current account is completely free for trade in goods and services, including marine survey fees, insurance premiums, and claim settlement flows. The capital account permits foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, intercompany loans, share subscriptions, and profit distributions without exchange-control approval, although certain overseas investment transactions may require consent under the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which is a foreign-ownership screening regime rather than a currency control. In practice, profit remittances from a New Zealand limited company face only normal withholding tax obligations, non-resident withholding tax on dividends or interest, and bank-level AML/CFT documentation checks. There is no central bank approval queue, no repatriation delay, and no mandatory FX surrender. Corporate treasurers can freely convert NZD and retain export earnings in foreign currency. The only practical hurdles are regulatory compliance, tax filing, and standard banking due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no export proceeds surrender rules in New Zealand. Exporters are not required to convert export receipts into NZD within any statutory timeline and may hold foreign currency indefinitely in bank accounts. There are no mandatory conversion ratios. Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, the banking relationship must satisfy origin-of-funds and trade-evidence checks, but no exchange-control surrender deadline applies. The statutory position is full retention.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand - Te Putea Matua, central bank; New Zealand maintains no separate foreign-exchange-control administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech EMIs such as Wise are viable as auxiliary payment rails rather than principal banking infrastructure. Wise Payments NZ Limited is registered as a financial service provider under the Financial Service Providers Registration and Dispute Resolution Act 2008 and is covered by a dispute resolution scheme, but it is not a registered bank and cannot provide commercial lending, deposit-taking products, bank guarantees, or in-branch treasury services. EMIs are legally permitted to serve New Zealand entities for global payment collection and FX conversion, provided they are not marketed as banks. For a marine insurance surveyor, an EMI can be used for receiving premium refunds or claim disbursements in low volume, but capital verification, intercompany loan governance, and large marine insurance settlements should flow through tier-1 domestic banks. A significant constraint is that banks and underwriters may reject EMI statements as source-of-funds evidence because EMI transaction records do not always carry full SWIFT originator information. Therefore, the cross-border viability rating is Medium as an operational convenience and Low as a compliance-complete treasury or clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections should use SWIFT MT103 from the overseas remitting bank to the New Zealand bank SWIFT BIC, with the beneficiary account in the company name and reference clearly stating SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION or INTERCOMPANY LOAN. Since New Zealand has no exchange control, no Reserve Bank filing is required. The company must still issue shares and record the issuance in the shareholders register, file an annual return with the New Zealand Companies Office, and produce a board resolution authorizing the share issuance. For an intercompany loan, execute a facility agreement and account for non-resident interest withholding tax. Capital verification can be performed by a New Zealand chartered accountant reviewing the bank statement, share register, and business plan, producing a verification letter for underwriters and counterparties. If funds originate from a bank-secrecy jurisdiction, include a corresponding bank attestation and a complete pre-funding KYC pack.Outbound trade disbursements should use SWIFT MT103 for routine marine survey fees, insurer refunds, and claim payments, referencing the invoice and engagement contract. For larger cargo claim settlements, use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued by the counterparty bank and confirmed by a New Zealand tier-1 bank, or documentary collections with sight drafts under ICC URC 522 or UCP 600. The domestic NZD leg is cleared through the New Zealand Exchange Settlement Account System, while the cross-border leg is executed via SWIFT.
