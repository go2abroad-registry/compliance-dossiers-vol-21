Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital or a blocked escrow-capital account for a company limited by shares. Shares may be issued without par value and for such consideration as the board determines under ss 36 and 37 of the Companies Act 1993. The company constitution and the first board resolution may fix a nominal subscription amount, typically NZ$1.00 per share or a commercial fair-value consideration, fully or partly paid as agreed. Any amount paid must be credited to the company bank account as working capital and must not be returned to shareholders except in accordance with the company solvency and distribution rules in Part 7 of the Companies Act 1993.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for industrial civil-defence fire permits or structural-load inspections because there is no factory, warehouse, chemical plant or hazardous process. The company will nonetheless observe the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 and, if it leases an office with a relevant fire safety system, the territorial authority building warrant of fitness regime under the Building Act 2004.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand; online electronic portal: myIR (ird.govt.nz). Export entries are filed with the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

Incorporate under the Companies Act 1993 through the New Zealand Companies Office and obtain the certificate of incorporation. 2. Apply to Inland Revenue for the company IRD number using the prescribed New Zealand company registration process, generally through myIR; if the constituent is instead a foreign non-resident company not incorporated in New Zealand, Inland Revenue provides a non-resident company IRD number application. The application must identify directors, shareholders, overseas tax residence, and any appointed New Zealand tax representative or agent. 3. Non-resident suppliers of remote/digital services to New Zealand consumers may register under the offshore supplier GST rules; however, NZ_LTD being a New Zealand resident company follows the standard resident registration path. 5. Voluntary GST registration is available to claim input tax credits even where the company makes only zero-rated supplies; no statutory business licence is required for ISIC 6202 software engineering and maintenance services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available. A closely held New Zealand company may elect look-through company (LTC) treatment under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, making the company fiscally transparent for income tax if eligibility criteria are met. A group of wholly owned New Zealand resident companies may elect consolidated treatment under subpart FM of the Income Tax Act 2007. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are automatically neutralised under New Zealand's BEPS rules, not subject to election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; offshore suppliers of remote/digital services to New Zealand consumers also register when above the same NZD 60,000 threshold; voluntary registration is available to claim input tax credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For export goods, zero-rating under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 applies when the supplier exports the goods in the course of a taxable activity and the goods leave New Zealand within the statutory window, ordinarily within 28 days of the time of supply. For offshore software engineering and maintenance services, zero-rating applies when the services are supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are not connected with land or moveable personal property in New Zealand. The ODC model qualifies where the client is an overseas group company and the services are performed in New Zealand for offshore use. Input tax credits attributable to zero-rated supplies are creditable, and refunds are obtainable through the GST return process; Inland Revenue may review claims before refunding.

  • New Zealand Customs Service export entry via the Trade Single Window (electronic export declaration)
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or other shipping document evidencing export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration
  • Signed service agreement and statement of work identifying the offshore recipient and jurisdiction (for services)
  • Delivery and acceptance evidence (e.g., software handover logs, support ticket records, client acceptance emails)
  • Foreign exchange remittance evidence (SWIFT MT103, Wise B2B transaction receipt, or Payoneer payout statement) showing receipt of proceeds from the non-resident customer

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API pre-clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping for GST invoices. GST tax invoices must contain the content required by section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. E-invoicing through the Peppol framework is mandatory for central government agencies as receivers of B2G e-invoices, but supplier use is otherwise voluntary.

Inland Revenue can reverse zero-rating and assess 15% GST if the documented proof of export or offshore service supply is absent or false. Use-of-money interest under the Tax Administration Act 1994 applies, and shortfall penalties under section 141 range from 20% for failure to take reasonable care or an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, to 150% for evasion. Directors may be personally liable for penalties and criminal prosecution in fraud cases. The standard IRD amendment period is four years for on-time filed returns, with an unlimited period for evasion or failure to file. The 180-day FX repatriation deadline is the company's internal audit benchmark, not a statutory GST deadline; absence of the remittance evidence is an audit red flag and will seriously weaken any zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains an open, floating exchange-rate regime without general foreign-exchange controls or a centralised foreign-exchange rationing system. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not require approval for converting NZD into foreign currency or for repatriating earnings; both current-account transactions and capital-account movements such as foreign direct investment, shareholder loans, equity injections, dividends, royalties and cross-border software service payments are freely convertible. There are no surrender requirements, no obligatory surrender windows, and no export proceeds repatriation timeline. The primary operating frictions are financial-crime due diligence imposed by registered banks under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and — only for certain sensitive land, fishing and significant business assets — the Overseas Investment Act screening by the Overseas Investment Office. A New Zealand limited company may be wholly owned by non-residents and has no minimum capital; profit remittance is not blocked by exchange controls, although non-resident shareholder distributions may attract New Zealand non-resident withholding tax unless an applicable double-tax treaty lowers or exempts that tax. Because the NZD floats freely, the ODC can hold foreign-currency accounts and settle invoices without bespoke central bank permits, making treasury concentration straightforward and predictable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand does not impose any statutory surrender of export proceeds and sets no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters and service providers retain their foreign-currency income indefinitely in bank accounts anywhere; no deadline exists for conversion into NZD. The only requirement is that foreign income is recognised in the entity’s New Zealand tax accounts under the accruals method.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua) — the central bank and prudential regulator; foreign exchange supervision is integrated into registered-bank licensing and anti-money laundering oversight rather than a separate exchange-control bureau.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise Business and Payoneer are legally permitted to service an NZ-registered company and are useful secondary rails for receiving USD/EUR/GBP settlement and making low-value contractor payouts. However, they are not registered banks in New Zealand, do not participate directly in local NZD clearing, and cannot provide overdraft facilities, commercial FX forwards, documentary letters of credit, or lender-grade account statements for capital verification. The recommended blueprint is to maintain a primary banking relationship with a tier-1 NZ bank and use EMIs only as non-core collection or payout conduits, never as the treasury backbone.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital must be routed as a standard SWIFT MT103 to the entity’s NZ tier-1 bank account, with the beneficiary name matching the Companies Office register and the remittance advice quoting a clear purpose code such as ‘Equity injection’, ‘Shareholder loan’ or ‘Corporate software services invoice’. No Reserve Bank purchase form or exchange-control permit is required. For equity, the bank will expect the capital verification certificate and share allotment return to match; for client fee payments, the underlying contract and invoice must reconcile. Turnover: An independent audit by a licensed statutory auditor is legally compulsory where the company is a large for-profit entity: total assets exceed NZD 60 million, or total revenue exceeds NZD 100 million, or the group has 100 or more full-time-equivalent employees; where the company is in the medium tier (assets NZD 20-60 million, revenue NZD 40-100 million, 50-99 employees) an audit is mandatory unless all shareholders unanimously waive the audit in writing; and in every case an audit is required if shareholders holding at least 5 percent of the voting rights deliver a written request, or if the parent undertaking's consolidation standards require an audited subsidiary.
