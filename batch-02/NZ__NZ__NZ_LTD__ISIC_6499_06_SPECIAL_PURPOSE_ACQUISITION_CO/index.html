Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Section 188 of the Companies Act 1993 requires a New Zealand company to have a registered office at a physical address in New Zealand where service of documents can be accepted and the public can access; a post-office box or mail-forwarding service cannot satisfy this statutory registered-office requirement. A virtual office or commercial mailbox may be acceptable for general correspondence, but Inland Revenue and the banking sector will reject it as proof of commercial substance for a Special Purpose Acquisition Company operating under ISIC 6499/NAICS 525990. The company must maintain an on-the-ground physical suite, permanent signage, corporate records, and a responsible officer or agent to receive inspections; a nominee-only virtual address will create significant tax-residency and bank account-opening risks under the Income Tax Act 2007 and the AML/CFT Act 2009.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None: New Zealand has no statutory minimum paid-up capital. A company may have nil share capital until subscription funds are received. No blocked escrow account is required for incorporation. If the SPAC executes an initial public offering or NZX listing, the offering proceeds must be escrowed under the trust deed in a segregated account until a qualifying acquisition or return of capital.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable. No Civil Defense fire permit, structural load inspection or hazardous process approval is required by the formation of an ISIC 6499 SPAC. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Te Tari Taake, New Zealand; electronic return and registration portal: myIR (myIR.ird.govt.nz).

E-government filing portal

For an NZ Ltd incorporated under the Companies Act 1993, the Companies Office issues a certificate of incorporation and automatically notifies the IRD, which issues an IRD number. For a non-resident or foreign-incorporated entity with New Zealand taxable supplies or source income, the entity must register for an IRD number through myIR by completing the prescribed company tax registration application and providing the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, ultimate beneficial ownership schedule, New Zealand registered office or agent details, and expected New Zealand commencement date. The IRD will then issue an IRD number; the same myIR registration flow is used to apply simultaneously for GST, employer responsibilities, and other tax accounts where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under New Zealand tax law. An eligible NZ Ltd with five or fewer shareholders may elect look-through company (LTC) status under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, causing income to be attributed to shareholders; a qualifying company election is only available to pre-existing qualifying companies. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 apply to any double-deduction or deduction/non-inclusion outcomes across jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month rolling period; registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded, and voluntary registration is available for suppliers under threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under sections 11 and 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a supply is zero-rated when goods are physically exported by the supplier within 28 days of the time of supply; when goods are supplied to a non-resident for export and are exported within the statutory timeframe; or when services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of performance and the services are not directly enjoyed or supplied to a person in New Zealand. For an SPAC holding company, arrangement, listing-readiness and acquisition-advisory services can qualify for zero-rating only if the recipient is offshore and the benefit is not attributable to a New Zealand taxable activity; purely passive holding activities are outside the taxable sphere.

  • New Zealand Customs Service export declaration lodged electronically through the Customs Trade Single Window (export entry record)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and GST tax invoice issued in accordance with section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985
  • Proof of export dispatch, such as packing lists, container gate records, or terminal receipts evidencing export within 28 days
  • Bank SWIFT or inward remittance advice evidencing payment in foreign currency or NZD from the offshore counterparty
  • Zero-rating working papers retained in the GST return and tax file for IRD inspection

New Zealand has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance requirement, no continuous transaction control, and no anti-fraud digital stamping. E-invoicing is voluntary using the Peppol framework. GST returns and GST adjustment schedules must be filed periodically through myIR, and IRD may request the underlying electronic invoice data during an audit.

There is no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline in New Zealand; the evidentiary records must be retained for seven years. If the IRD determines that zero-rating was claimed without valid export proof, the output tax is clawed back by reassessment plus use-of-money interest. Penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply by reference to the shortfall and culpability: a lack of reasonable care or an unacceptable tax position attracts a shortfall penalty of 20%; an abusive tax position attracts 50%; and evasion or a similar wilful act attracts 100%. Criminal sanctions may also apply for knowingly false declarations made to the IRD or New Zealand Customs Service.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised and open capital and current account regime under IMF Article VIII, with the New Zealand dollar freely convertible. There is no foreign exchange control board, no central bank approval for opening foreign currency accounts, no prior authorisation for repatriation of capital, and no requirement to surrender export proceeds. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand is responsible for currency issuance, prudential supervision of registered banks, and oversight of wholesale settlement systems, but it does not administer foreign exchange rationing or convertibility licences. Current account payments for trade, services, royalties, dividends, interest, and management fees may be settled freely through any authorised bank. Capital account transactions are equally unrestricted for an ordinary New Zealand holding company, except that the Overseas Investment Act 2005 imposes targeted screening for acquisition of sensitive land, fishing quota, or significant business assets by non-residents; a special purpose acquisition company formed purely as an acquisition vehicle and holding cash pending an overseas target acquisition will generally not trigger OIO consent unless the target itself involves New Zealand sensitive assets. Profit remittance is taxed according to ordinary dividend withholding rules and the fully imputed New Zealand tax system, but there are no repatriation delays, no conversion quotas, no notional central bank swap requirements, and no administrative hurdles beyond standard anti-money laundering verification. Inward capital injections and outward acquisition funding flow through the CLS-settled NZD market at commercial rates without Treasury pre-approval, subject only to the counterparty due diligence of the relevant New Zealand bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand has no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion timeline or ratio. Exporters and holding companies may retain foreign currency proceeds indefinitely, hold them in domestic or offshore foreign currency accounts, and convert them to NZD or any other freely tradeable currency at commercial rates without central bank permission.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions and non-bank payment service providers are generally permitted to operate under New Zealand's Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. They may offer payment initiation, foreign exchange conversion, and limited stored-value or wallet products. However, they are not registered banks and cannot hold demand deposits, extend lending, or provide regulated trust-escrow services that would be acceptable for a blank-check SPAC's statutory capital verification. For the initial public offering proceeds, a special purpose acquisition company must normally maintain cash and securities in a segregated client trust or escrow arrangement at a regulated New Zealand bank with strong balance sheet strength, treasury dealing capability, and SWIFT connectivity. The practical viability of a fintech EMI for the central treasury mandate is therefore low; it may only be used as an ancillary payment rail for operational expenses in selected low-risk jurisdictions, and never as the primary repository for sponsor capital or escrowed IPO proceeds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital should be routed through the SGSS/CLS chain using a SWIFT MT103 payment from the sponsor's bank directly to the New Zealand bank account designated as the capital escrow account. The payment must include the company name, bank BIC, account number, and the purpose code or free-text description stating Capital contribution for subscription and allotment of ordinary shares in NZ_LTD. Since New Zealand imposes no exchange controls, the bank will convert the incoming USD/EUR/CNY into NZD at the prevailing commercial offering rate without requiring Reserve Bank approval. If the sponsor injects a convertible loan instead of equity, the bank should be given the loan agreement, intercompany resolution, and a suitability note confirming that the amount will be on-lent in compliance with ordinary thin capitalisation and withholding tax rules. The New Zealand auditor or registered chartered accountant will issue a capital verification report based on the bank statement, subscription letters, share allotment resolution, and the Companies Act 1993 register, with dividends or shareholder current accounts fully documented.Outbound acquisition and operational payments should be executed over SWIFT MT103 for straightforward wire transfers to the target company's principal bank, with full beneficiary bank details, remitter reference, and purpose code corresponding to the transaction. For trade settlements involving physical goods or deferred acquisition earn-outs, the New Zealand bank may issue an irrevocable commercial letter of credit in favour of the target or vendor, governable by UCP600, or use documentary collections under URC522 where the vendor accepts draft-on-documentary terms. All outbound disbursements require the board-approved payment schedule, original invoice or closing statement, sanctions pre-screening of all intermediaries, and a written compliance sign-off from the designated AML/CFT officer. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A certified independent audit is mandatory for: (i) an FMC reporting entity under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, which includes a SPAC that has made an offer of securities to the public or is listed on an exchange; (ii) a 'large' for-profit entity under the Financial Reporting Act 2013, defined as an entity with total assets in excess of NZ$10,000,000 at balance date or total annual revenue in excess of NZ$20,000,000; (iii) a company whose annual return shows capital or controlled assets requiring a statutory audit due to a court order or FMA direction; and (iv) any company where shareholders holding not less than 5% of the voting rights validly require an audit. The New Zealand statutory size test is balance-sheet and turnover based; there is no separate New Zealand headcount threshold that triggers an audit for a limited company, although a headcount of 50 or more employees remains a commercial indicator for group-reporting consolidation and stewardship analysis.
