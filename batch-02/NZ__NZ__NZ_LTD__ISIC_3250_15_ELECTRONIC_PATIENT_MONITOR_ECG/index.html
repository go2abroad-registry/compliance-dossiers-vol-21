Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed. The Company must issue at least one share, and the board must determine the subscription consideration and whether any amount is unpaid. There is no legal requirement for an escrow account or capital deposit for a standard NZ limited company.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (New Zealand) — Primary tax administration via the myIR secure online portal (https://www.ird.govt.nz); statutory business registry is the New Zealand Companies Office and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) register.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities: submit Inland Revenue application for a non-resident IRD number (form IR742) attaching the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, and the register of directors/shares, with authenticated copies of utilities and bank account statements; submission online via myIR or paper to the Auckland Compliance Centre. Domestic NZ-incorporated limited companies: the NZ Companies Office automatically issues a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) at incorporation and Inland Revenue issues the resident IRD number by default; for GST purposes, application must be made on form IR356 for the GST number once the NZD 60,000 registration threshold is met or voluntary registration in the first year is elected. The identification protocol requires the IRD number and NZBN to be reported on all inbound/outbound statutory filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an NZ-resident limited company is a separate taxpaying entity subject to 28% corporate income tax on worldwide income, with a fully imputed dividend tax system (imputation credits); the Territorial_Scope for non-resident shareholders is limited to withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties under the Income Tax Act 2007 and NZ double tax agreements; no pass-through for a standard Ltd unless a Look-Through Company (LTC) election is made, which is generally unavailable where non-resident shareholders hold interests.

Available election forms

Look-Through Company (LTC) election under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 (allowed for closely held NZ-resident-owned companies only; non-resident shareholder participation within permitted Australian/NZ resident shareholding limits assessed); hybrid or imported mismatch adjustments coordinated under Part FM (with taxpayer election to substitute a tax outcome or apply a mitigating adjustment); company imputation election to maintain an Imputation Credit Account and attach imputation credits to dividends; voluntary GST registration election under s 51(3) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; and the option to adopt a 12-month GST filing period with 'pay-as-you-go' instalment arrangements for registered persons with turnover below NZD 2 million.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in taxable supplies per rolling 12-month period (compulsory registration under s 51(1)); voluntary registration permitted from first supply when the entity has a carrying-on-business nexus in New Zealand, and non-resident suppliers of remote services may register voluntarily without the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating under s 11(1)(a) of the GST Act 1985 applies to a supply of goods (including the ECG patient monitors) where the supplier exports the goods and the goods are exported by the supplier within 28 days of the time of supply (time of supply being the earlier of invoice date or payment/consideration date), and the supplier holds proper export evidence. Services: zero-rating under s 11A applies where the services are supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed, and the services are not supplied in connection with land in New Zealand or movable property in NZ; electronic and remote assembly/calibration services supplied to offshore hospital distributors or healthcare group purchasing organizations qualify for 0% GST. The exporter must hold records demonstrating the goods left NZ (customs-approved export movement), foreign currency proceeds, and the non-resident status of the buyer; supply of installation and calibration services performed in NZ for a New Zealand-based buyer remains standard-rated at 15%.

  • New Zealand Customs Electronic Export Declaration lodged via the Trade Single Window (TSW) containing the tariff classification (HS 9018.19 for electro-diagnostic apparatus including ECG monitors), FOB value, and exporter/consignee details
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List matching the Customs declaration, with shipment number and INCOTERM 2020 rule (e.g., FOB/CIF)
  • Clean Ocean Bill of Lading or House/Air Waybill evidencing export and date of departure from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/TT confirmation proving foreign currency receipt from the off-shore buyer
  • Certificate of Origin (RCEP/CPTPP/NZ-FTA documentation) where the buyer claims preferential tariff treatment
  • Supplier's registration certificate (ISO 13485 / New Zealand Medsafe export certificate for medical devices) supporting the licensed export of ISIC 3250 medical devices

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate. New Zealand operates a voluntary e-invoicing framework aligned with the PEPPOL network (New Zealand Electronic Invoicing Standard, NZEIS); all government agencies must accept PEPPOL e-invoices, and E-invoices are accepted for IRD GST records. There is NO digital stamping, continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance, or API-based tax-authority approval requirement; GST returns are self-assessed and filed via myIR on a monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly basis. Registered persons must retain GST records, including electronic export evidence, for 7 years.

The IRD and New Zealand Customs jointly audit export zero-rating claims, cross-matching GST returns against Trade Single Window export declarations and bank foreign-currency receipts. A zero-rating claim is overturned if the goods are not exported within the 28-day window; the supplier must then account for GST at 15% plus use-of-money interest. Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, shortfall penalties for overstated zero-rating are 20% of the tax shortfall for failure to exercise reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 150% for evasion or fraudulent intent; fraudulent GST evasion additionally attracts criminal prosecution under s 143A with fines up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment. Customs false-declaration penalties under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 can reach NZD 20,000 per shipment, and the exporter may be suspended from the Accredited Exporter or Approved Exporter schemes. Foreign-exchange repatriation delays beyond the 180-day benchmark are reviewed under the international financial arrangement rules of Subpart FE of the Income Tax Act 2007, which can impute income on late-paying offshore receivables; documentary failures also trigger retrospective GST assessments, disallowance of input tax credits, and clawback of any refunds issued, with 100% of any fraudulent claim recoverable from the director personally in the absence of bona fide business purpose.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains an open current and capital account with full convertibility of the New Zealand dollar (NZD). There are no exchange control restrictions on trade-related payments, dividend distributions, loan repayments, or repatriation of capital by foreign investors, subject to standard banking documentation and tax clearance compliance. The Financial Markets Conduct Act and Overseas Investment Act impose targeted regulatory filters for certain offshore investment activities, but ordinary commercial remittances are not subject to FX approval. Profit remittances are permitted without central bank consent; however, non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at 15% or a lower applicable double tax agreement rate must be deducted from interest, dividends, and royalties, and a New Zealand branch or subsidiary must comply with Companies Office filings and IRD tax registration requirements before dividends are paid offshore. The sole practical convertibility hurdle is the availability of NZD liquidity at domestic banks for spot or forward transactions, which is deep and unrestricted for lawful commercial purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may freely retain foreign currency receipts in overseas accounts, sell proceeds forward, or convert to NZD at a bank-negotiated exchange rate. The only compliance obligations are standard Financial Action Task Force (FATF) record-keeping, shipping document retention for income tax evidence, and anti-money laundering (AML) verification of trade counterparties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. If export receipts are received after the relevant income year, accrual tax rules under the Income Tax Act 2007 will govern recognition; there is no central bank reporting or mandatory repatriation timeline for NZD-denominated or foreign-currency export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium (Conditional). Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) and non-bank payment service providers can supply cost-effective virtual IBAN/SWIFT-style multi-currency account rails, but they are not registered New Zealand banks and their balances are not covered by the RBNZ depositor protection regime established under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989 and the Depositor Compensation Scheme Act 2023. Legal constraints include the following: an offshore EMI must hold a payment service authorisation in its home jurisdiction; it cannot accept regulated deposit-taking business in New Zealand without a New Zealand banking licence or a relevant licence exemption; transaction flows between an offshore EMI and an NZ corporate must still route through a locally licensed bank for NZD settlement; and an NZ company using an offshore EMI as its main operating treasury account will encounter elevated KYC scrutiny at local banks, limited trade finance integration, and no guarantee of Commercial LC issuance. The EMI is therefore viable as a secondary liquidity rail for low-value supplier payments or FX conversion, but primary capital injection, LC-backed trade settlement, and statutory capital escrow functions should be anchored at an RBNZ-registered commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection from a foreign parent or investor into the NZ Ltd should be executed by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer denominated in NZD or in the original funding currency, using the New Zealand beneficiary bank's BIC (e.g. ANZBNZ22, ASBBNZ2A, BKNZNZ22, WPACNZ2W, or KIWINZ22), the NZD settlement account number, and the company name and registered address. The remitter must quote the purpose of payment as 'Equity Injection' or 'Shareholder Loan – Capital Contribution', and the NZ company must provide the receiving bank with the underlying share subscription agreement, board resolution, and UBO source-of-funds pack before opening or activating a dedicated capital escrow account. Bank officers will screen the transfer against sanctions and AML obligations and may reject any transaction lacking a clear commercial purpose. If the foreign investor acquires a significant business asset or sensitive land in New Zealand, an Overseas Investment Act consent may be required before the funds can be lawfully applied to the business; for an NZ manufacturing subsidiary with no more than NZ$100 million in New Zealand assets, no such consent is ordinarily needed unless the transaction involves land that is sensitive, adjacent to conservation areas, or otherwise regulated. After receipt, the CFO must ensure that the funds are recorded in the NZ Companies Office share register if issued as shares, and entered on the IRD financial statements as share premium or shareholder loan subordinated debt if retained as equity. All inbound remittances must align with the financial statements of the NZ company and be available for audit by the bank's financial crime prevention team upon request.The primary clearing rails for outbound trade disbursements are SWIFT MT103 (Customer Credit Transfer) for direct supplier payments and irrevocable Documentary Letters of Credit issued by the NZ corporate's bank in the form of SWIFT MT700/MT701 for larger or contractually governed supply shipments. For operating expenses, local tax, and salaries, disbursements are effected through NZD high-value settlement using the Reserve Bank's Exchange Settlement Account System or the Bulk Electronic Clearing System, while foreign currency disbursements involve a domestic bank's offshore correspondent network. Payment instructions must include the supplier's full legal name, account number, IBAN or BIC/ABA, and a purpose description consistent with the underlying purchase. Trade settlement documentation for Commercial LCs must include commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, packing list, quality certificates, and all documents required by the credit. The NZ company should maintain separate outflow sub-ledgers for component imports, calibration consumables, shipping and freight, professional fees, and royalties. For payments to high-risk or first-time suppliers, the bank may require a wire transfer authorisation letter signed by two directors, an evidence packet comprising contract and proforma invoice, and an undertaking that funds are not being diverted to sanctioned third parties. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium (Conditional). Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) and non-bank payment service providers can supply cost-effective virtual IBAN/SWIFT-style multi-currency account rails, but they are not registered New Zealand banks and their balances are not covered by the RBNZ depositor protection regime established under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989 and the Depositor Compensation Scheme Act 2023. Legal constraints include the following: an offshore EMI must hold a payment service authorisation in its home jurisdiction; it cannot accept regulated deposit-taking business in New Zealand without a New Zealand banking licence or a relevant licence exemption; transaction flows between an offshore EMI and an NZ corporate must still route through a locally licensed bank for NZD settlement; and an NZ company using an offshore EMI as its main operating treasury account will encounter elevated KYC scrutiny at local banks, limited trade finance integration, and no guarantee of Commercial LC issuance. The EMI is therefore viable as a secondary liquidity rail for low-value supplier payments or FX conversion, but primary capital injection, LC-backed trade settlement, and statutory capital escrow functions should be anchored at an RBNZ-registered commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border related-party transactions exceeding NZD 1,000,000 (tangible goods) or NZD 500,000 (services, intangibles, interest and financial arrangements) require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation aligned to the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines; Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least NZD 1,100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: No UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute in New Zealand; economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act 2007, s BG 1), the Principal Purpose Test embedded in NZ double tax agreements, the anti-conduit rules (ss GC 6-GC 23), and the CFC income attribution regime. A resident NZ company must maintain a registered office, accounting records, an independent local bank account, and locally exercised governance; 'letterbox' or sham entities will be recharacterised, denied treaty benefits, and subjected to shortfall penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abusive Tax Position

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty of 150.00% of the tax shortfall for evasion (TAA 1994, s 141E) and 100.00% for an abusive tax position (s 141D); criminal conviction for evasion under s 143B carrying a fine up to NZD 50,000 (individual) or NZD 250,000 (company) and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax under s 300 TAA 1994 where they knew or ought to have known the tax was unpaid; courts may order forfeiture of personal assets, travel prohibition via passport surrender under s 157 TAA 1994, and a knowingly participating director faces imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: Cancellation of the tax advantage under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s BG 1) with an additional 100.00% abusive-tax-position shortfall penalty; denial of treaty relief under the DTA Principal Purpose Test; deregistration and director disqualification under the Companies Act 1993 for failure to maintain a registered office or proper records.

Director personal liability: Directors who trade recklessly or incur obligations without reasonable grounds are personally liable to compensate creditors (Companies Act 1993, ss 135-136); directors may be disqualified for up to 5 years and courts may order civil recovery of funds extracted from the failed shell entity.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the AMLCFT Act 2009, civil penalty up to NZD 2,000,000 per breach for a body corporate and NZD 300,000 per breach for an individual; wilful money laundering under s 246 Crimes Act 1961 carries imprisonment up to 7 years; restraint and forfeiture of proceeds under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to pecuniary penalties and may be named in civil penalty proceedings; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed while proceeds-forfeiture proceedings are pending.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: IRD adjustment under s GC 7 Income Tax Act 2007 to the arm's length consideration with use-of-money interest; shortfall penalty of 20.00% for failure to take reasonable care and 40.00% where the taxpayer's position fails the 'more likely than not' standard for a material understatement exceeding NZD 50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or approve false transfer pricing documentation are personally liable under s 300 TAA 1994 for the resulting tax shortfall; serious cases are prosecuted as evasion with imprisonment up to 5 years and prohibition from managing a company.
