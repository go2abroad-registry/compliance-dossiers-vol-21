Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mail forwarding address may satisfy the bare registered office requirement for an ordinary trading company, but for a licensed captive reinsurer it is not sufficient as a sole physical presence. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand, acting under the Insurance Prudential Supervision Act 2010, requires a licensed captive reinsurer to maintain real commercial substance in New Zealand, including physical office premises, locally available books and records, local governance, board decision-making, and appropriate insurance reserves. The Inland Revenue Department will reject a mail-box-only structure for income tax residence and transfer pricing substance purposes. New Zealand banks will refuse to open underwriting reserve and SWIFT payment accounts for a captive reinsurer that has only a virtual office because the AML/CFT Act 2009 requires the bank to know the customer's true place of business and operational substance. Therefore, a virtual office is rejected by the tax authority, the prudential regulator, and banks for this specific industry.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Companies Act 1993; New Zealand imposes no minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow share capital account for a limited liability company. However, if the Reserve Bank of New Zealand grants a captive insurer licence under the Insurance (Prudential Supervision) Act 2010, the licence conditions may include minimum paid-up capital, capital adequacy margin, or statutory solvency reserve requirements that must be satisfied as a regulatory condition of that separate authorisation.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for a captive reinsurance SPV because no physical manufacturing, chemical handling, assembly, or warehousing occurs. The company will nevertheless occupy a leased commercial office and must ensure that the building has a current Code Compliance Certificate, that fire detection and evacuation procedures meet the requirements of the New Zealand Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017, and that the workplace conforms to the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015. No Civil Defence fire permit or structural load certification is required for purely administrative and financial services premises.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) – online portal: myIR (https://myir.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A New Zealand incorporated Limited Company automatically receives an IRD number upon incorporation through the Companies Office. For foreign non-resident entities not incorporated in New Zealand, the non-resident must apply for a New Zealand IRD number using the IR742 form (Non-resident company IRD application) or via myIR where a non-resident registration option exists. The application must include certified certificate of incorporation (if any), constitutional documents, and proof of foreign registration. Once the IRD number is issued, the entity must register separately for GST, PAYE, and other applicable taxes depending on its activity. For an NZ Ltd entity, the incorporation process via the Companies Office (New Zealand Business Number automatically issued) triggers the IRD number creation; subsequent tax registrations are completed through myIR, including GST registration if taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 or on a voluntary basis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Section 11(1) of the GST Act 1985 zero-rates exported goods and services supplied to non-residents who are outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the supply is not directly connected with New Zealand land or goods and is not made through a fixed establishment in New Zealand. For captive reinsurance services, which constitute financial services for GST purposes, section 11A zero-rates financial services supplied to non-residents so long as the supply is not to a person in New Zealand and is not an agreement for the importation of goods or services for use in New Zealand. The supplier must retain sufficient evidence of the non-resident status of the recipient and the offshore nature of the insured risks.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) — required for goods only; captive reinsurance typically qualifies as a service, so export entry may not apply
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill — for goods consignments, evidencing physical exportation from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency payment from the non-resident reinsurance counterparty
  • Executed reinsurance contract or placement slip identifying the policyholder as a non-resident entity and specifying the location of insured risks outside New Zealand
  • Zero-rated GST tax invoice issued to the non-resident client, containing the supplier's IRD/GST number and a statement that the supply is zero-rated under section 11 or 11A
  • Certificate of non-residency for the client (e.g., tax residency certificate) to support the zero-rating claim

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance electronic stamping, or mandatory API-based e-invoicing regime. Tax invoices must comply with the prescribed GST invoice content requirements under GST Act 1985 (e.g., issuer name, GST registration number, date, description, GST-exclusive/inclusive amounts). Electronic invoicing is voluntary and aligned with the Peppol framework; returns are self-assessed and filed via myIR using GST101 (standard) or GST102 (non-standard). No anti-fraud digital stamping is required.

Under the Tax Administration Act 1994 (TAA 1994), if IRD determines that a zero-rating claim is unsupported or false, it will reassess the supply at 15% GST, with late payment interest from the original due date. Shortfall penalties apply under TAA 1994: 20% for failure to take reasonable care in a taxposition, 40% for gross carelessness, and 150% for tax evasion or abusive tax avoidance. Criminal liability can arise under TAA 1994 s 143A with fines up to NZD 50,000 for knowingly misleading evidence. Reinsurance captive arrangements lacking commercial substance or documentation may also be challenged under general anti-avoidance rules in the Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1, leading to reframing of the transaction and imposition of penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised current and capital account under its IMF Article VIII status. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible, and there are no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no restriction on the repatriation of dividends, interest, capital, or reinsurance profits for a licensed captive reinsurance SPV. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand regulates the banking and insurance sectors and does not impose prior approval for cross-border transfers; instead, commercial banks perform their own due diligence and report under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. A captive reinsurance SPV may hold foreign currency balances with domestic banks without mandatory conversion, and may reinvest proceeds in hedged instruments. The only practical hurdles are non-resident withholding tax at 15% on dividend distributions (subject to treaty relief, for example under an applicable double tax agreement) and the requirement to obtain a valid New Zealand IRD number for tax clearance. Furthermore, insurance premium income from related entities must reflect an arm's-length commercial rationale to pass economic substance scrutiny under New Zealand tax and licensing rules. Overall, profit remittance is frictionless from an FX perspective provided the company complies with statutory tax filing and licensing obligations, alongside the prudential oversight exercised by the Reserve Bank on captive insurance entities. The Financial Markets Authority and the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment jointly administer licensing and conduct regulation for captive reinsurance activities, ensuring that policyholder risk is adequately ring-fenced and that premium income is not used as a mere conduit for tax leakage.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand does not impose any export proceeds surrender or mandated conversion requirement. Exporters and reinsurers may retain foreign currency receipts in offshore or domestic accounts without statutory timeline for repatriation; conversion is at commercial rates. However, for AML/CFT and tax reporting, material cash transactions exceeding NZD 10,000 must be reported to the Financial Transactions Reporting Act regime, and supporting trade/insurance documentation must be retained for at least 5 years.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs have limited viability in New Zealand. An entity licensed only in the EU or UK is not a registered bank under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand regime and cannot operate as a settlement or clearing institution for NZD; it would be treated as an unregulated money services provider. To service a captive reinsurance SPV lawfully, an EMI would need registration under the Financial Service Providers Act 2008 and a separate AML/CFT compliance programme accepted by the Department of Internal Affairs. Even then, most Tier-1 New Zealand banks will not clear payments through such EMIs due to downstream counterparty risk, and the absence of a physical New Zealand presence makes KYC and compliance oversight impractical. Therefore, the viability rating for fintech EMIs as primary treasury rails is Low, and they should only be considered as ancillary collection channels with pre-cleared counterparties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions to the captive reinsurance SPV are routed via SWIFT MT103/202 from the parent's corporate treasury to the SPV's designated capital account at a Tier-1 New Zealand bank. The payment instruction must reference an appropriate purpose code covering direct investment equity capital or intercompany loan disbursement, consistent with New Zealand's inward direct investment reporting standards if applicable. The bank treasury will verify the sender's KYC profile, screen the transaction against sanctions lists, and confirm the payment corresponds to a board resolution and a share subscription document. A New Zealand chartered accountant must issue a capital verification certificate for any recorded paid-up capital, and the funds should remain in the Capital Escrow Account until the FMA licensing approval and regulatory capital clearance are completed.Outbound disbursements for covered losses and reinsurance premiums are cleared through the SWIFT MT103 messaging network using ISO20022 structured narratives containing the policy number, claim reference, risk period, and reinsurance treaty code. For trade-related financial flows, the SPV may use commercial letters of credit issued under UCP600 or documentary collections with pre-approved bank listed trade instruments. Intra-group settlements must be supported by a master intercompany loan and reinsurance agreement, transfer pricing documentation, and a transaction-specific board resolution to prevent disguised capital export. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 1,000,000 in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions in an income year triggers the practical expectation of contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation. Large multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of NZD 1,300,000,000 or more are required to file Country-by-Country reporting and should maintain a Master File and Local File consistent with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to certain offshore financial centres. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, the specific transfer pricing rules in Part GC, and the insurance licensing regime require a captive reinsurance SPV to demonstrate genuine capital at risk, actual assumption of insurance risk, substantive local board control, locally retained documentation, and real economic activity in New Zealand. A captive with no physical presence, no local decision-making, no bank account, and no substantive reinsurance operations will be treated as a shell and its arrangements may be void against the Commissioner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty of 150% of the resulting tax under section 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994. Criminal conviction for evasion is punishable by a fine of up to NZ$100,000 for the company, a fine of up to NZ$50,000 for an individual, or imprisonment for up to five years. Use-of-money interest applies to all unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or knowingly participate in evasion are personally liable for civil penalties, may be criminally prosecuted and imprisoned, and may be jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts. Inland Revenue may obtain asset preservation orders, departure prohibition orders, and disqualification orders under the Companies Act 1993.
Transfer Pricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may recharacterise the controlled arrangements under section GC 7 of the Income Tax Act 2007, adjust income and deductions to the arm's length amount, and impose shortfall penalties of 40% for gross carelessness or 100% for an abusive tax position. Deductions may be denied and interest charged on the resulting reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or tolerate non-arm's-length arrangements can be made personally liable for penalties where the company is used to defeat tax. In serious cases, directors face criminal conspiracy charges, civil restitution, disqualification, and restrictions on departure from New Zealand.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The arrangement may be void under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and reconstructed by the Commissioner. Deductions can be denied, income reattributed to the ultimate parent, and penalties of up to 100% of the tax shortfall imposed for an abusive tax position. The Registrar of Companies may deregister the company for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors who permit the company to operate without substance may be liable under section 135 or 136 of the Companies Act 1993 for reckless trading and insolvent trading. They may also face director penalty notices for unpaid tax, disqualification, and personal asset tracing through civil recovery proceedings.
AML and Insurance Licensing Breach

Statutory sanction: Operating an unlicensed insurance business is an offence under the Insurance (Prudential Supervision) Act 2010, punishable by imprisonment for up to 12 months and fines up to NZ$500,000. Under the AML/CFT Act, a body corporate may be liable for a civil penalty of up to NZ$2 million and an individual up to NZ$200,000 for compliance failures, with additional criminal liability for intentional non-reporting.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and directors may be personally liable for civil penalties where they were knowingly concerned in the failure or did not take reasonable steps to ensure compliance. Directors may be disqualified from managing insurance or financial institutions, subject to freezing orders, and may face criminal conviction and imprisonment for serious financial crime.
