Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue, Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand; official electronic portal is myIR at https://myir.ird.govt.nz. Inland Revenue administers the Income Tax Act 2007, the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, the Tax Administration Act 1994, and related legislation.

E-government filing portal

Step-by-step procurement of the New Zealand tax identity for a foreign-owned NZ limited company: (1) Incorporate under the Companies Act 1993 and obtain the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) from the New Zealand Companies Office; the company number and NZBN are not tax identifiers. (2) Apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD Number using form IR596 (Non-resident company IRD number application) or online via myIR, providing the certificate of incorporation, NZ registered office address, director and ultimate beneficial owner particulars, tax residence of each shareholder, and the nature and place of the proposed business activities. (3) If the company will carry on a taxable activity in New Zealand or supply remote services to New Zealand consumers, register for GST (form IR356 or online in myIR) at the same time; the GST registration number is the same as the IRD Number. (4) Obtain a Customs client code from the New Zealand Customs Service for any physical import or export of goods; this code is not required for pure services or financial services. (5) For a regulated financial-services entity, complete the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) application and join an approved dispute-resolution scheme under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 before commencing lending activities; this is a licensing obligation, but Inland Revenue operationally cross-checks FSPR registration when processing GST and income-tax registrations. (6) Appoint a New Zealand tax agent or in-country director if the entity has no local operational presence; a non-resident company that is not carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand may still obtain an IRD Number for New Zealand-source income compliance, including withholding tax on interest. Issuance of the IRD Number is free of charge and is a precondition for claiming double-tax-agreement relief, filing returns, and registering for GST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with a territorial-source overlay. A company incorporated in New Zealand is a New Zealand tax resident (Income Tax Act 2007 s Y 2) and is taxed at the entity level at the corporate rate of 28% on its worldwide income. There is no automatic pass-through; company losses are quarantined in the entity and cannot be utilised by shareholders unless the company elects look-through status. The company may access the New Zealand dividend imputation regime (subpart OB). For a foreign-owned New Zealand limited company, New Zealand-source income (interest, fees, and lending profits sourced to New Zealand) is taxed on assessment; income sourced outside New Zealand is generally outside the New Zealand tax net for a non-resident company but inside the net for a resident company. As the operating lender platform for New Zealand consumers, all platform income from New Zealand borrowers will be New Zealand-source business income. There is no tax-exempt zone or offshore financial-centre classification available to a commercial micro-lending fintech in New Zealand.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box classification election exists in New Zealand; entity classification is determined by legal form and statute. Available statutory elections relevant to this NZ_LTD include: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, available to a company with five or fewer individual or eligible shareholders; the entity becomes fiscally transparent with income, deductions, and credits flowing through to shareholders, subject to shareholder basis limits for losses; the election is irrevocable for five years except with Commissioner consent. (2) Dividend imputation election, allowing the company to maintain an imputation credit account and attach imputation credits to distributions. (3) GST group election under s 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, allowing commonly controlled companies to register as a single GST group and simplifying returns and internal invoicing. (4) Hybrid and branch mismatch rules (ss FH 1 to FH 14) are mandatory and not elective; no check-the-box or pairing election exists for hybrid entities, but the legislation contains ordering and ratio rules and requires contemporaneous documentation of the offshore mismatch analysis. (5) Controlled-foreign-company elections, including the branch-equivalent election where the NZ_LTD holds offshore lending subsidiaries in grey-list jurisdictions; such elections must be made in writing to Inland Revenue before the relevant return due date.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in a 12-month period (GST Act 1985 s 51). Both a retrospective test (actual supplies in the preceding 12 months exceeded NZD 60,000) and a prospective test (reasonable expectation of exceeding NZD 60,000 in the next 12 months) trigger compulsory registration. A person making only exempt supplies, such as pure credit interest, is not liable to register and cannot claim input credits; the threshold is assessed only against taxable supplies. For non-resident suppliers of remote services, including online platform services to New Zealand consumers, the same NZD 60,000 threshold applies to supplies to New Zealand consumers; below the threshold registration is voluntary but unavailable where the supplier makes only exempt or non-taxable supplies. A P2P lender must map its revenue streams: interest and credit-facility fees are exempt financial supplies (Schedule 3 of the GST Act), while non-financial administration, software subscription, and default-recovery services may be taxable supplies subject to GST and counted toward the registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the GST Act 1985: (a) Goods are zero-rated under s 140 where the supplier exports the goods within 28 days of the time of supply in the ordinary course of business and retains documented evidence of physical export from New Zealand; the supplier must be the exporter of record on the customs declaration. (b) Services are zero-rated under s 11A where supplied to a person who is a non-resident and is outside New Zealand at the time the services are supplied, provided the services are not supplied for use directly in a taxable activity carried on in New Zealand and are not directly connected with New Zealand land or goods. For financial services, including the supply of credit, loan arrangement, and loan administration, s 11A zero-rates exported financial services where the recipient is a non-resident person outside New Zealand and the benefit of the service is obtained offshore; anti-avoidance s 11A(3) denies zero-rating if the arrangement has a purpose of avoiding GST and the services are in substance used by a New Zealand resident. The P2P platform must contractually identify the borrower's residence through a non-resident declaration supported by KYC/AML evidence, confirm that the proceeds are drawn and used offshore, and demonstrate that the loan is booked through its offshore lending channel rather than its New Zealand consumer channel. Exempt financial services supplied to New Zealand residents remain GST-exempt, not zero-rated; unsubstantiated zero-rating of such supplies is reassessed at 15%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic declaration lodged through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window, including exporter client code, tariff classification, valuation, and destination evidence) for goods exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (original or electronic transport document evidencing dispatch from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency receipt statement (bank-generated evidence that the export consideration was received into the entity's New Zealand bank account from the overseas customer, substantiating FX repatriation and source)
  • Non-Resident Services Declaration (for the lending platform: executed loan agreement, borrower KYC/UBO files, and a declaration or contractual warranty confirming that the borrower is non-resident and uses the funds offshore, in support of the s 11A zero-rating of exported financial services)
  • GST tax invoice and underlying platform fee schedule (demonstrating the classification of each revenue stream as exempt, zero-rated, or taxable and supporting the GST return position)

New Zealand operates no real-time fiscal clearance and no mandatory pre-clearance API or digital stamping for tax invoices. The GST compliance framework is periodic and self-assessed: the registered person accounts for GST on a monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly cycle and files form GST101/GST101A via myIR; payment is due on the filing date. Tax invoices must be issued within 28 days of the time of supply and contain the prescribed particulars, including supplier name, GST/IRD number, recipient details, description, date, consideration, and GST amount; debit and credit notes follow the same regime. E-invoicing is voluntary under the PEPPOL-based E-invoicing NZ framework (operated with support from the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment), and Inland Revenue accepts e-invoices as business records; there is no IRD interception or pre-clearance step. For exports of goods, the mandatory electronic export declaration is lodged through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window (customs.govt.nz), and that customs clearance is not a tax function. GST returns and payments must be filed electronically by registered GST entities.

Inland Revenue applies strict audit scrutiny to export zero-rating and GST refund claims. The taxpayer must retain all export and non-resident supply records for seven years (Tax Administration Act 1994 s 22); failure to do so exposes the entity to a best-judgement assessment under s 106. If zero-rating is not substantiated, the supply is treated as standard-rated at 15% GST, and the claimed refund of output tax and embedded input credits is clawed back together with use-of-money interest from the due date of each return. Shortfall penalties apply on the resulting tax shortfall: 20% for lack of reasonable care; 40% for gross carelessness; 100% for an abusive tax position; and 150% for evasion or an arrangement with a dominant purpose of defeating the tax. Criminal liability under s 143A exposes the company to a fine not exceeding NZD 250,000 and directors or officers to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years, with conviction implicating both the entity and responsible officers. For financial-services exports, Inland Revenue will test commercial substance, authenticity of non-resident declarations, actual cross-border payment flows, and the ultimate use of loan proceeds; a failed offshore treatment is reassessed at 15% on the full consideration plus penalties and interest, and the entity may also face taxpayer-name disclosure under s 77D and loss of GST registration privileges. New Zealand imposes no statutory exchange-control deadline for FX repatriation; the 180-day figure is the operational evidentiary benchmark, meaning the inward-remittance certificate should be obtained and filed within 180 days of the time of supply to satisfy Inland Revenue's export substantiation practice.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible. New Zealand maintains no general exchange-control regulations for either current or capital account transactions. Profit remittance is unrestricted, but dividends and interest paid to non-residents are subject to New Zealand non-resident withholding tax at treaty rates unless reduced under a Double Tax Agreement. Capital inflows and outflows, including equity injections, loan principal repayments, and trade payments, are not subject to prior approval unless the transaction triggers the Overseas Investment Act for sensitive New Zealand assets.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement exists for NZD. Export receipts may be retained in any currency. Entities must only maintain adequate records for tax returns and for Statistics New Zealand balance-of-payments reporting, including the official financial account balance and accompanying purpose of payment narrative when the local bank submits a regulatory statistical return.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua) under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989; the Financial Markets Authority also administers the licence for peer-to-peer lending services under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) cannot provide New Zealand settlement accounts without a New Zealand banking licence or avoidance of deposit-taking business. No European or UK EMI passporting applies to New Zealand. A licensed peer-to-peer micro-lending platform must hold client money in an independent, NLG-qualified trust account with a registered New Zealand bank, or maintain segregated operational and escrow accounts with a tier-1 registered bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: required where the New Zealand taxpayer's cross-border related-party transactions (including interest on related-party debt, management fees, software licensing, and lending platform royalty payments) exceed NZ$10 million in aggregate for an income year. Master File: required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding NZ$1.3 billion (approximately €750 million). Country-by-Country reporting applies at the same global revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code. However, economic substance is mandated indirectly by the Income Tax Act 2007: section BG 1 general anti-avoidance rule, transfer pricing rules in sections GC 1-GC 15, and the 'business continuity' test for loss utilisation. Non-resident lenders and platform operators must demonstrate real management, decision-making, and risk-taking in New Zealand to avoid a sham or GAAR assessment and to access treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under sections 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994; on conviction, a fine of up to NZ$250,000 for a company, plus a 150% shortfall penalty under section 141E for evasion or fraudulent intent. Interest on unpaid tax accrues from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in filing false returns are personally liable for penalties and may be imprisoned for up to 5 years. The corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent or sham conduct, exposing directors' personal assets and subjecting them to travel bans and forced civil restitution in civil recovery proceedings by the Commissioner.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustments under sections GC 1-GC 15, with taxable income recomputed at arm's length. Shortfall penalties range from 20% (failure to take reasonable care) to 150% (evasion); use-of-money interest applies. A minimum 10% penalty may apply for an unacceptable tax position without reasonable grounds.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's-length intercompany transactions without adequate transfer pricing documentation are personally liable for resulting shortfall penalties if they caused or allowed the company to take an unacceptable tax position. In serious cases, criminal liability for evasion may attach.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the relevant DTA's limitation-of-benefits or beneficial-ownership clauses; application of the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1. The entity may be wound up under the Companies Act 1993 if it is a sham or used to defeat creditors. Penalties include 150% shortfall penalties, refusal of refunds, and cancellation of the New Zealand Financial Service Provider licence.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the entity's unpaid taxes and penalties if the entity is a sham or façade, as the Courts will pierce the corporate veil. Directors can be subject to disqualification from managing companies, travel bans during investigation, and civil recovery of diverted funds.
AML/CFT and Consumer Credit Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, civil penalties for a company of up to NZ$2 million for each contravention; the Financial Markets Authority may revoke the financial service provider licence. CCCFA contraventions attract a maximum civil penalty of NZ$600,000 for a company, plus statutory damages to borrowers and loan write-off orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are personally liable for corporate AML/CFT failures if they consent or are wilfully blind; on conviction, imprisonment of up to 2 years and fines of up to NZ$300,000 per individual. Directors may also be subject to compensation orders and travel bans pending trial.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) Offshore Evasion

Statutory sanction: The payer is personally liable for the full amount of NRWT not deducted, plus an absolute-liability shortfall penalty of up to 150% under section 141D of the Tax Administration Act, late-payment penalties, and use-of-money interest. In addition, the transaction may be recharacterised as a dividend subject to further NRWT.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid NRWT where the company fails to pay and a director knew of the non-payment and did not have the company go into liquidation or administration (section 149A TAA). Travel bans may be imposed for substantial tax debt (over NZ$10,000) while the director is outside New Zealand.
Misuse of Imputation Credits / Dividend Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Imputation penalty tax of 10% of the company's debit balance at balance date, and a credit segregation and late filing penalties under sections 218-219 of the Income Tax Act 2007. The imputation credit account may be cancelled and NRWT recomputed at the 30% unimputed dividend rate.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false ICA reconciliation statements are personally liable for penalties; in liquidations, the Commissioner may recover from directors for fraudulent preferential payments through imputation debits.
