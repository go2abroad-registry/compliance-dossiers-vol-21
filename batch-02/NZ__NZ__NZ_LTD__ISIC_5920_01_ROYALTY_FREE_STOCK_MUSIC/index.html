Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None required. New Zealand imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital. A limited company may be incorporated with zero paid-up capital, and shares may be issued unpaid, partly paid, or fully paid as determined by the board. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand, electronic portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must apply for an IRD number (Tax Identification Number) by completing the 'IR742 – Non-resident company tax registration' form, available on the IRD website. The application must be submitted via post or through a registered tax agent, accompanied by certified copies of the company's certificate of incorporation, constitution, and proof of registered office address. The IRD typically processes applications within 10-15 working days. Once issued, the IRD number is used for all tax filings, including income tax, GST, and PAYE.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods (where the goods are exported within 28 days of supply) and to certain services that qualify as 'export services' under Section 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, including services supplied to non-residents who are outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the services are not supplied to a person in New Zealand and are not directly connected with land or goods in New Zealand. For digital services (e.g., royalty-free stock music licensing), zero-rating applies if the recipient is a non-resident business or a non-resident individual outside New Zealand, and the supply is not consumed in New Zealand.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry) lodged via New Zealand Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export of goods (if applicable)
  • Bank statement or foreign exchange receipt documentation evidencing receipt of payment in foreign currency from the overseas customer

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. GST invoices must be issued for all taxable supplies, and must contain prescribed information under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Electronic invoicing is permitted but not mandated; no API-based clearance is required.

Under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and the Tax Administration Act 1994, fraudulent or unverified zero-rating claims may result in penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall, plus interest. In cases of deliberate evasion, criminal prosecution may be pursued. Retrospective clawbacks of zero-rating are possible if the exporter fails to provide satisfactory evidence of export within the statutory timeframe, or if the goods/services are subsequently diverted to domestic consumption. IRD audits may require full documentation, and failure to maintain proper records can lead to additional penalties of NZD 250 per day.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a free-floating currency and has no legislative foreign exchange control act imposing permission requirements on current or capital account transactions. The NZD is freely tradeable in the global wholesale market, and resident entities can invoice, hold, and settle in any convertible currency. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) does not approve, allot, or restrict ordinary trade payments, dividend remittances, intra-group financing, or profit distributions. Capital account convertibility is equally open: debt and equity investment into an NZ Ltd may be effected through normal banking channels, subject only to bank-level KYC/AML checks and, in narrowly defined circumstances, the Overseas Investment Act 2005. Surrender requirements are absent; exporters and licensors can keep 100% of offshore receipts. Profit remittance obstacles are mainly tax-driven: non-resident withholding tax at the rate of 15% on dividends (subject to treaty relief), and foreign dividend exemption rules for NZ shareholders. There are no central bank permission barriers. For licensing revenue from royalty-free stock music, banks may request a copy of the licence, download counter, or merchant dashboard to ensure that the inflow is not a disguised third-party settlement. Overall convertibility is assessed as full for both current and capital items, with prudential and AML filters rather than exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender is imposed on New Zealand exporters. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no regulatory deadline; 100% of export receivable may be retained in the foreign currency bank account of the exporter or converted at the licensed bank's commercial rate. RBNZ only collects balance-of-payments statistical data. Trade receivables credited in foreign currency may be held indefinitely, although IFRS and IRD tax rules require translation to NZD for period-end reporting.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Viability rating: Medium-High, but only as an ancillary payment collection layer, not as a substitute for a licensed New Zealand bank. Stripe, PayPal, Wise, and Airwallex are permitted to service New Zealand entities under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and must comply with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. However, digital EMIs are not banks; they are not members of the RBNZ Fast Settlement System and cannot provide repo, FX hedging, or capital verification services. For large inbound shareholder injections, SWIFT settlement, and trade disbursements, a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank is mandatory. EMIs are legally viable for subscription royalty collection and franchise/store payouts, provided the AML risk tier is low and the overseas EMI holds or obtains the required New Zealand money services registration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a New Zealand company receiving foreign shareholder equity, use SWIFT MT103 from the ultimate beneficial owner's banking institution to a Tier-1 New Zealand bank, with the beneficiary field naming the NZ Ltd, the payment narrative stating equity subscription or share capital contribution, and the quantity reference quoting the board resolution and share allotment register. The NZD leg will clear through the RBNZ Fast Settlement System via the receiving bank. Purpose codes on the wire should identify income on equity or direct investment as required by the RBNZ balance-of-payments reporting framework. An overseas non-resident equity injection does not require RBNZ exchange approval; the company must file an equitable share allotment return with the Registrar of Companies within the prescribed statutory period and obtain a capital verification certificate from a registered NZ chartered accountant or external auditor before crediting distributable reserves. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions per income year requires a Local File; a Master File is required where the multinational group has consolidated global revenue of NZD 1.3 billion or more; all taxpayers must contemporaneously document transfer pricing for transactions with associated non-residents.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a separately labelled Economic Substance Regulations code; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007), the transfer pricing rules, and anti-treaty-shopping principles require the NZ LTD to have genuine substance in New Zealand: physical licensed studio space, employees handling licensing and rights administration, board decisions made in New Zealand, and bank accounts managed locally. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) test is applied when determining entitlement to DTA benefits: a foreign intermediary claiming treaty relief on music royalties must actually perform the development, enhancement, maintenance, protection, or exploitation functions itself. A passport-only shell will be disregarded, its income re-attributed to the real business, and the full statutory NRWT re-imposed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False IR4 / International Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 sections 141B to 141D impose shortfall penalties of 20% (lack of reasonable care), 40% (gross carelessness), 100% (abusive tax position), and 150% (evasion). Criminal conviction under section 143B carries a fine of up to NZD 50,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years. Section 143A imposes an additional NZD 50,000 fine for knowingly providing false tax information.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised or knew of the false return are personally liable to the penalty under section 149 of the Tax Administration Act 1994. IRD may issue a departure prohibition order under section 157A banning the director from leaving New Zealand until the tax debt is settled. On criminal conviction, asset restraint and forfeiture under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009 can strip personal assets, and director disqualification under the Companies Act 1993 may follow.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Treaty Shopping (Sham Structure)

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 sections GC 6 to GC 14 (transfer pricing) permit the IRD to substitute an arm's length outcome; sections BG 1 (general anti-avoidance) and GB 1 permit re-characterisation of the arrangement; interest apportionment applies under sections GC 15 to GC 23. Shortfall penalties of 100% to 150% of the assessed tax apply, and criminal fines of up to NZD 50,000 with up to 5 years imprisonment are possible under sections 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994 if the transfer pricing is a deliberate sham.

Director personal liability: The court will pierce the corporate veil where the NZ LTD is a bare puppet or sham: directors are jointly liable to restore misappropriated assets and can be sued for trading recklessly under sections 135 and 136 of the Companies Act 1993. Directors' personal bank accounts may be frozen under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009, and IRD can issue a departure prohibition order for unpaid assessments.
Shell Company Abuse / Economic Substance and Record-Keeping Failure

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 section 147 requires the retention of sufficient records for 7 years; failure is an offence with a fine of up to NZD 10,000. The sham arrangement can be disregarded under section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and DTA relief denied, leaving the full 30% NRWT on dividends and 15% on interest and royalties. Companies Act 1993 section 194 also obliges accurate accounting records.

Director personal liability: Directors breach their duty under sections 131 and 135 of the Companies Act 1993; on litigation, the corporate veil is lifted to impose personal liability for all company tax debts and creditor claims. The director may be disqualified under the Companies Act and, if involved in tax evasion, imprisoned for up to 5 years. A departure prohibition order (travel ban) and asset freezing order are the standard enforcement tools used by the IRD against such directors.
