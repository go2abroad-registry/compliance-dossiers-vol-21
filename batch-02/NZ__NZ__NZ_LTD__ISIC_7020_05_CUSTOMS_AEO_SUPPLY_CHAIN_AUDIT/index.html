Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox address may satisfy the Companies Act 1993 registration requirement only if it is a physical street address in New Zealand and not a post office box. However, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and commercial banks generally reject a pure virtual address for tax registration and corporate bank account opening in the management consultancy or AEO audit sector. A serviced office with a physical reception, lease agreement, and dedicated mail handling is the minimum acceptable standard. For AEO authorization and customs security auditing, the New Zealand Customs Service requires verifiable operational presence, so a complete virtual mailbox without a local business location will be deemed insufficient and will likely trigger enhanced due diligence or refusal of GST registration and banking.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Companies Act 1993 imposes no minimum paid-up or authorized share capital for a New Zealand limited company. No escrow or blocked capital account is required. Shares may be issued for nominal or non-cash consideration provided the board approves the allotment and satisfies the solvency test in section 4 of the Companies Act 1993 (asset test and cash-flow test) immediately after allotment.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department of New Zealand (Te Tari Taake), Wellington; electronic portal is myIR Secure Online Services for business tax registration and filing.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity obtaining a New Zealand tax identity must first ensure corporate legal existence under the Companies Act 1993 through the New Zealand Companies Office, including a New Zealand registered office and, where required, a local agent, to obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). The entity then applies to Inland Revenue for an IRD number through myIR using the prescribed non-resident company registration procedure. The application must declare the full legal name, NZBN, directors, shareholders, place of effective management, New Zealand and overseas addresses, nature of the business (ISIC 7020 supply-chain compliance audit), and the expected first income tax balance date. If GST registration is sought, the application must state whether NZD 60,000 taxable turnover threshold is met or voluntary registration is requested. Inland Revenue issues a single statutory IRD number used for income tax, GST, and other tax obligations; there is no separate VAT ID. Non-resident companies with no New Zealand-source income and no GST registration may not require an IRD number until registration is triggered.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand has no check-the-box election. A New Zealand limited company is by default an entity-level taxpayer under the Income Tax Act 2007. A close company with five or fewer shareholders may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and Income Tax Act 2007, making the company fiscally transparent for qualifying shareholders, subject to ownership and eligibility tests. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Arising from Hybrid Mismatch Arrangements) Act 2018 apply to cross-border structures and do not involve a voluntary election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies made in New Zealand or intended to be made in any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand consumers must register when their supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 to supplies of goods physically removed from New Zealand by the supplier or by an overseas purchaser before the time of supply, with contemporaneous evidence of actual export. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated where the service is performed outside New Zealand and is not supplied in connection with land or moveable property situated in New Zealand; remote services supplied to a non-resident customer are zero-rated, but services connected with New Zealand land or physical goods are standard-rated unless another specific zero-rating rule applies.

  • Approved Export Entry lodged with New Zealand Customs Service via the Trade Single Window (TSW), including the electronic Export Declaration and the outwards manifest evidencing physical departure from New Zealand
  • Original transport document such as a negotiable Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, Air Waybill, or International Forwarders' Cargo Receipt showing shipment from a New Zealand port or airport to the overseas destination
  • Proof of cross-border payment and foreign exchange settlement, including bank credit advice, SWIFT MT103 or MT202 records, and foreign exchange purchase or inward-remittance records accepted under the Tax Administration Act 1994; New Zealand does not issue an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) and the statutory evidentiary standard is satisfied by independent banking records

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-approval of invoices. Vendor invoices must contain the statutory particulars set out in section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and may be issued and stored electronically. Voluntary PEPPOL-compliant e-invoicing is available through the New Zealand e-Invoice Network. Inland Revenue provides application programming interfaces (APIs) and Gateway services for software-to-software filing through myIR, but e-invoicing clearance is not a tax administration prerequisite.

Inland Revenue and New Zealand Customs audit the complete export trail, from physical removal through customs control to delivery abroad. If export zero-rating is claimed without adequate evidentiary support, the supply is recharacterised as standard-rated and 15% GST is assessed with use-of-money interest from the original due date. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 may be imposed at up to 150% of the resulting tax shortfall for evasion or similar act, 100% for abusive tax position, and 75% for lack of reasonable care. In serious cases, criminal penalties and personal liability for directors can apply. AEO certification and supply-chain audit documentation must demonstrate continuous control over goods from the New Zealand supplier to the offshore recipient to protect the zero-rating, and any later contradiction of the export evidence can trigger retrospective clawback assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a genuinely open capital and current account framework. Since the 1984 liberalisation, the New Zealand dollar is floating and freely convertible; there are no RBNZ foreign exchange controls, no central bank surrender requirements, no forced prior approval for inbound or outbound capital transfers, and no restrictions on foreign ownership for incorporated companies. Current account payments for trade and services broadly require only standard banking due diligence, while capital account movements, including foreign equity injections, shareholder loans, M&A consideration, and profit remittances, may be made freely in NZD or any foreign currency through licensed commercial banks. The only practical hurdles are AML/CFT documentary friction and the economic substance expectations applied under tax anti-avoidance rules such as thin capitalisation and non-resident withholding tax criteria. Profit remittances are not subject to exchange control; they are governed by corporate tax and dividend imputation rules. Because New Zealand accepts IMF Article VIII convertibility obligations, the NZD is consistently strong in both trade and financial transactions. No sovereign FX auction, no multiple currency tiers, no import cover requirements, and no central bank approval certificates exist for any legitimate treasury operation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirements exist in New Zealand. Exporters are not forced to convert foreign currency receipts into NZD within any timeline, and there is no mandatory conversion ratio. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency domiciled onshore or offshore, subject only to ordinary AML/CFT record-keeping, suspicious transaction reporting, and tax disclosure obligations. The RBNZ does not monitor or approve the repatriation, holding, or reinvestment of export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) - Foreign Exchange, Cash, and Reserves Division

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not a substitute for a licensed New Zealand bank. An offshore EMI is legally barred from accepting deposits or providing trade finance in New Zealand unless it is registered as a financial service provider on the New Zealand FSP Register and maintains an approved AML/CFT compliance programme with the Department of Internal Affairs or another accredited supervisor. Feasibility is Low for primary corporate treasury clearing, as EMIs typically lack local correspondent banking capacity, NZD payment finality, and RBNZ oversight. Feasibility is Medium only for low-value remittances and supplementary expense payments through a locally incorporated, FSP-registered non-bank payment provider. For the primary corporate banking and trade rails, a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank is mandatory.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: An independent certified audit by a New Zealand licensed auditor is compulsory when the company is a large company under the Financial Reporting Act 2013, generally at or above any one of these quantitative thresholds: total assets exceeding NZD 60,000,000; total revenue exceeding NZD 30,000,000; or 60 or more full-time equivalent employees. Audit is also mandatory for an FMC reporting entity under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, for a company required by its constitution or a shareholders' agreement to be audited, and where shareholders holding at least 5% of the voting shares validly require an audit. The auditor must be independent and licensed under the Auditor Regulation Act 2011.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on gross dividends paid by a New Zealand company to a non-resident shareholder, unless a Double Tax Treaty reduces the rate. The standard OECD treaty limit is 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for qualified direct dividends usually involving at least 10% or 25% holding, depending on the treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$1,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions requiring contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) for the New Zealand entity; groups with worldwide revenue of NZ$1,000,000,000 or more are additionally subject to Country-by-Country Reporting and may be required to hold a Master File and make it available to Inland Revenue within 30 days of a request.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but economic substance is still central to tax residence, permanent establishment, and treaty access. The AEO compliance audit independently verifies physical premises, local staff, board control, record-keeping, and supply-chain security. These functional indicators mirror the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) concept: a New Zealand entity must actually direct and perform the audit, risk assessment, and customs-security functions that produce its income. A non-resident managed arrangement without real decision-making in New Zealand can be recharacterised under the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 and relevant reconstruction provisions of the Income Tax Act 2007.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through Hidden Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 sections 141A to 141E impose shortfall penalties escalating from 20% for lack of reasonable care to 150% for evasion; sections 143A and 143B create criminal liability for knowingly providing false tax returns or evading assessment with imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine of up to NZ$50,000 plus payment of all evaded tax and interest.

Director personal liability: A NZ director who knowingly authorises false returns or fails to account for NRWT can be personally assessed for the unpaid tax and penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994; the court may pierce the corporate veil under the Companies Act 1993 if the company is made insolvent by the extraction. The director risks civil asset restitution, disqualification from managing companies, border-control travel blacklisting, and imprisonment for up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalisation Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue can apply the Income Tax Act 2007 transfer-pricing rules in Part GD and the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 to reconstruct taxable income at the arm's-length level; shortfall penalties of up to 100% for abusive tax positions can be added, plus use-of-money interest. Failure to maintain the required transfer-pricing documentation is itself an exposure for accuracy penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing returns or cause the NZ company to enter artificial non-arm's-length contracts are personally liable for losses incurred by creditors if the company trades recklessly. The court can pierce the veil and order the director to repay the tax shortfall; criminal prosecution for false documentation can lead to a travel ban, disqualification, and imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Shell Company and AEO Customs Security Audit Fraud

Statutory sanction: The Customs and Excise Act 2018 and the New Zealand AEO Programme terms impose penalties for false or misleading customs declarations, with monetary penalties, forfeiture of goods, revocation of AEO status, and exclusion from future AEO participation. Fraudulent evasion of customs controls can be prosecuted as an offence carrying imprisonment and fines of up to NZ$50,000 or more depending on goods value.

Director personal liability: Directors who are knowingly concerned in AEO false certification are personally liable for the fraudulent declarations and can be prohibited from acting as directors under the Companies Act 1993. The Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009 allows confiscation of assets derived from the sham; the director may be blacklisted by New Zealand Customs, have a travel ban imposed by the court, be ordered to pay civil restitution, and be sentenced to imprisonment for up to 5 years.
