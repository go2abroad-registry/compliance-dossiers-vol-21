Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand Government – official portal myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

For NZ_LTD (a New Zealand-incorporated limited company), the Companies Office registration is completed first; the entity then applies to the IRD for an IRD number (the sole tax identification number used for income tax and GST) via myIR, submitting the certificate of incorporation, NZBN record, director details, registered office, and intended business activity. A non-resident company carrying on a taxable activity in NZ (or intending to do so) may also be registered by its approved agent using the IRD's non-resident company registration form (IR742) and GST registration application; the IRD issues the IRD/GST number, which must be quoted on all tax invoices. Registration for GST is compulsory when taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration is available to exporters so that input GST can be recovered.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. NZ_LTD is a separate legal and taxable entity under New Zealand law; as a New Zealand resident company it is taxed on worldwide income at the 28% corporate rate, while the same entity is the registered GST person. The entity cannot be treated as a pass-through or disregarded entity for New Zealand tax purposes.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election or hybrid entity election exists in New Zealand. Statutory classification is determined by the Companies Act 1993 and the Income Tax Act 2007. Limited elections available: voluntary GST registration under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, GST grouping election under section 55, consolidated income tax group election under subpart FM of the Income Tax Act 2007, and financial arrangement timing elections. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are mandatory under the BEPS hybrid rules and cannot be waived by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (including zero-rated exports); voluntary registration is allowed for any person carrying on, or intending to carry on, a taxable activity in New Zealand.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of baijiu from New Zealand are zero-rated under section 11(1) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when the supplier exports the goods (or delivers them to a carrier for export) within 28 days of the time of supply and holds sufficient documentary evidence of physical export. The zero-rating also requires the sale to be an unconditional supply to a non-resident customer, the goods must leave New Zealand, the supplier must retain customs and shipping records, and the proceeds must enter the New Zealand export stream. Domestic services, local warehousing, and any supply not proved to be exported are taxable at 15%; input GST credits can be refunded via the GST return once the zero-rated export is substantiated.

  • New Zealand Customs electronic Export Declaration (Trade Single Window clearance confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the goods as Chinese baijiu and the ultimate export destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds

There is no real-time fiscal clearance or pre-approval requirement for GST invoices. New Zealand has adopted voluntary e-invoicing over the PEPPOL network (PEPPOL BIS 3.0); no API or digital stamping is mandated by Inland Revenue. All GST-registered suppliers must issue a tax invoice within 28 days of a request, containing the IRD number, supplier name and address, date of supply, description, GST-exclusive amount, GST amount, and (for supplies over NZD 1,000) the buyer's name and address. Export documentation is transmitted to New Zealand Customs electronically through the Trade Single Window, not for GST clearance purposes.

Inland Revenue may audit zero-rating claims and require production of the export evidence bundle. If the goods are not exported within 28 days or the documentary trail is deficient, the supply is recharacterised as taxable at 15% GST, giving rise to increased assessments, use-of-money interest, and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for failing to take reasonable care or for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. Input credits previously claimed on export-related purchases are clawed back when the corresponding export zero-rating fails. Records must generally be retained for 7 years. False export declarations may additionally trigger penalties and seizure under the Customs and Excise Act 2018. There is no statutory FX repatriation requirement in New Zealand; the 180-day benchmark is a commercial and pipeline control, and failure to receive proceeds within that period may be treated by IRD as evidence that no genuine export occurred.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has full current- and capital-account convertibility, consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no general exchange controls, prior-use restrictions, or profit-repatriation impediments; dividends, interest, trust distributions and capital proceeds may be converted and remitted freely in New Zealand dollars or any foreign currency, subject only to bank KYC/AML/CFT checks, tax withholding compliance, and the Overseas Investment Act 2005 where a transaction implicates sensitive New Zealand land or significant business assets. For a wholesale liquor exporter, no RBNZ consent is required for ordinary trade payments, loans, or equity subscriptions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory timeline for export proceeds surrender and no mandatory conversion ratio. A New Zealand exporter may retain export receipts indefinitely in foreign-currency bank accounts, use them to pay foreign suppliers, or convert to NZD at market-determined rates. The only obligations are record-keeping under the AML/CFT Act, tax treatment of foreign-currency gains/losses, and disclosure to bankers for source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership verification.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — central bank and prudential regulator; no separate foreign-exchange administration. AML/CFT supervision of banks is jointly exercised by the RBNZ and the Financial Markets Authority under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium for primary NZD clearing but useful for secondary or fringe FX. Offshore digital EMIs and electronic-money institutions are not regulated deposit-takers in New Zealand and cannot provide local current-account or letter-of-credit facilities as if they were licensed banks. They may provide payment services only if registered on the Financial Service Providers Register and compliant with the FSP Act 2008 and AML/CFT obligations. Such providers are not suitable for the main NZD settlement or documentary trade operations; use a licensed New Zealand or Australian bank for core treasury. Non-bank EMIs such as global online payment firms can support ancillary supplier payments and low-value repatriations in CNY/USD/AUD corridors, but they are not a substitute for a regulated bank relationship for SWIFT MT103 and commercial LC transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound equity subscriptions from the Chinese shareholder must be routed by SWIFT wire (MT103) to the NZ_LTD NZD bank account with a clear purpose code such as '112 - Equity Investment' or the bank's equivalent BoP classification, using the exact legal name shown in the New Zealand Companies Register. The remitter must ensure the Chinese outward remittance is lawful under Chinese foreign-exchange law, including SAFE cross-border ODI registration if the Chinese shareholder holds 10%+ of the shares and is investing equity rather than making trade pre-payment. For the NZ receiving bank, deliver a board resolution authorizing the capital raise, updated share register and Companies Office filings confirming 'Shares Issued for Cash', share subscription letters, invoicing if applicable, source-of-wealth statements, and confirmation that no funds are remitted on behalf of unrelated third parties. Shareholder loans should be documented with written loan agreements, interest and repayment terms, and arm's-length pricing to prevent tax and thin-capitalization disputes. New Zealand does not require RBNZ approval for inbound equity, but the bank will apply AML/CFT verification before accepting principal.Trade disbursements in the Chinese-baijiu supply chain should settle through: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier and agent payments in CNY/USD/EUR, with payment references containing the commercial invoice number and product description; (ii) Commercial Irrevocable Letters of Credit using SWIFT MT700/MT720, with MT754 advising negotiation, for larger Chinese distiller or trading-house transactions; and (iii) documentary collections or electronic-BPO platforms for moderate-value repeat transactions.
