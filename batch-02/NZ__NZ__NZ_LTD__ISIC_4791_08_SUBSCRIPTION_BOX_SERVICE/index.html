Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A New Zealand company must have a physical registered office in New Zealand at which documents and records can be served; a PO Box alone is not permissible. A virtual office or commercial mailbox may satisfy the Companies Office if it provides a genuine physical street address, dedicated mail handling, and access to corporate records during business hours. For a subscription box merchant, however, Inland Revenue and most New Zealand trading banks will treat a directory-only virtual mailbox as insufficient evidence of commercial substance for tax registration, GST activation, and AML/CFT customer due diligence. If the virtual office provides meeting rooms, physical address signage, staffed mail scanning, and a credible business location, it can be acceptable for a low-risk online merchant. If no inventory storage, no staff, and no operational address exist, banks will likely reject the application as lacking physical substance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (Te Tari Taake), New Zealand's national tax authority, operating the myIR secure online portal; entity incorporation is separately administered by the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993.

E-government filing portal

For the NZ_LTD, a New Zealand-incorporated limited company, the company is registered with the New Zealand Companies Office and is allocated an IRD number by Inland Revenue through the integrated incorporation process. Thereafter, the company must separately register for Goods and Services Tax (GST) through the myIR portal. For a foreign non-resident entity that has not incorporated in New Zealand, the application process is more extensive: the non-resident entity must first establish whether it is carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand. If it is exporting goods from New Zealand or supplying taxable services into New Zealand, the entity must apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number using the non-resident entity registration pathway available in myIR. The application must include the overseas certificate of incorporation or equivalent constitutional document, registered office address, director and ultimate beneficial ownership details, the expected New Zealand turnover, and the purpose of the New Zealand tax presence. Where the non-resident entity's annual taxable supplies in New Zealand are expected to exceed NZ$60,000, GST registration is compulsory; voluntary registration is generally available only when the non-resident is already carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand. Once approved, Inland Revenue issues a unique IRD number and a separate GST registration number where applicable. Practical administration by a New Zealand tax agent or authorised representative is strongly recommended so that notices and tax returns can be validly served and lodged.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None comparable to the United States check-the-box regime. A New Zealand limited company may elect look-through company (LTC) status under the Income Tax Act 2007 for income tax flow-through treatment if all shareholders are eligible persons, but the LTC election does not change the entity's separate legal personality or its liability to GST. Hybrid mismatch rules under Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007 may apply to cross-border arrangements and deny deductions; however, no elective entity classification is available for indirect tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000: GST registration is compulsory once taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any continuous 12-month period exceed or are expected to exceed NZ$60,000. For non-resident suppliers of remote services, only supplies made to New Zealand resident consumers count toward this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical subscription-box goods qualify for 0% GST when the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand, provided the goods are physically exported within 28 days of the time of supply and the supplier retains contemporaneous evidence of export. The supply must genuinely constitute a supply of goods for export to an overseas subscriber, with title and risk passing outside New Zealand. Where the subscription includes a remote or digital component, that component may be zero-rated as a supply of remote services to a non-resident recipient if the recipient is outside New Zealand at the time of performance and no substantial use of the service occurs in New Zealand. Registered GST persons may also claim input tax credits on New Zealand procurement costs to the extent such costs are attributable to making zero-rated exports.

  • New Zealand Customs Service electronic export declaration (CusMod/Trade Single Window) or customs-approved export entry for the shipment
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or House/Standard Airway Bill for air freight evidencing dispatch from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and subscription order record showing the foreign subscriber's name and overseas delivery address
  • Authorised New Zealand bank foreign exchange credit advice or inward remittance advice evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds
  • Carrier proof-of-delivery documentation such as overseas courier tracking, customs certification, or carrier certificate showing arrival at the destination country

New Zealand does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoicing approval, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime. E-invoicing may be exchanged voluntarily through the Peppol network using PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0, but no transaction-level clearance with Inland Revenue is required before issuing an invoice. GST invoices must nonetheless satisfy the substantive tax invoice content requirements under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

Inland Revenue may challenge a zero-rating position where the required customs and shipping evidence is not retained, and will assess unrecovered GST at the standard 15% rate with use-of-money interest running from the original return due date. For inaccurate GST returns, the Tax Administration Act 1994 applies shortfall penalties in bands up to 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or an equivalent deliberate act. Fraudulent or knowingly misleading export documentation may also trigger criminal prosecution, fines, and director liability. The taxpayer bears the evidentiary burden to prove physical export, and failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day benchmark is treated as a strong audit flag for artificial schemes, potentially leading to reversal of zero-rating, reassessment at 15% GST, and clawback of any input tax previously claimed.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised foreign exchange and capital account regime. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible on both the current and capital accounts for residents and non-residents alike. There are no surrender requirements, no repatriation restrictions, and no requirement to obtain central bank approval for cross-border transfers, foreign direct investment, or profit remittances. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) acts as the primary monetary and financial stability authority, but it does not exercise exchange control approval powers; the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) and RBNZ jointly supervise anti-money laundering and counter-financing of terrorism (AML/CFT) obligations. For a New Zealand Limited company engaged in recurring physical subscription box merchandising (ISIC 4791), dividend distributions, shareholder loans, and intercompany payments may be freely remitted provided they meet standard tax compliance (e.g., withholding tax on director fees, non-resident withholding tax on dividends) and banks have no suspicion of structured laundering. In practice, banks require source-of-funds evidence, business purpose codes (e.g., NRA – non-resident alien? no, NZ uses IMD codes – but actually banks use SWIFT purpose codes such as 'T0300' for investment or 'T0100' for goods trade), and confirmation of tax residence for CRS/FATCA. The convertibility assessment is therefore categorised as 'full free convertibility with AML-triggered due diligence'. No foreign exchange licences are required to transact, but entities must maintain independent NZD accounts for operational liquidity and can hedge freely. The only statutory obligations relate to reporting cash transactions over NZD 10,000 to the New Zealand Financial Intelligence Unit (NZFIU) and maintaining audit trails for wire transfers in accordance with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. Overall, New Zealand poses minimal FX friction, and the key practical impediments are banking due diligence rather than state-level exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign currency receipts. Exporters may hold export proceeds in foreign currency accounts without restriction and convert to NZD or any other currency at their discretion, subject only to normal AML/CFT reporting and business-activity documentation requirements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, or Revolut cannot provide bank account services in the strict sense of licensed banking under the RBNZ's banking prudential framework. They are typically registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under the AML/CFT Act and supervised by the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA). They may legally hold customer funds as stored value and provide payment/collection services, including currency conversion, but they are not authorised to accept deposits or issue credit in New Zealand. For a New Zealand Limited company, using a non-resident EMI without a physical presence is legally permissible but operationally risky for KYC, because NZ banks often insist on local account arrangements for corporate underwriting and credit extension. An EMI may be used as an ancillary treasury tool for cross-border subscription collections, but the primary entity bank account should be with a prudentially-regulated Tier-1 domestic bank. If the EMI is headquartered overseas and not registered in New Zealand, the entity must still maintain a New Zealand operational bank account to satisfy tax and statutory compliance. Therefore, fintech EMI viability is rated as 'conditional support' – acceptable only for niche cross-border collections and disbursements, but not as the sole banking rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, or Revolut cannot provide bank account services in the strict sense of licensed banking under the RBNZ's banking prudential framework. They are typically registered as Money Service Businesses (MSBs) under the AML/CFT Act and supervised by the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA). They may legally hold customer funds as stored value and provide payment/collection services, including currency conversion, but they are not authorised to accept deposits or issue credit in New Zealand. For a New Zealand Limited company, using a non-resident EMI without a physical presence is legally permissible but operationally risky for KYC, because NZ banks often insist on local account arrangements for corporate underwriting and credit extension. An EMI may be used as an ancillary treasury tool for cross-border subscription collections, but the primary entity bank account should be with a prudentially-regulated Tier-1 domestic bank. If the EMI is headquartered overseas and not registered in New Zealand, the entity must still maintain a New Zealand operational bank account to satisfy tax and statutory compliance. Therefore, fintech EMI viability is rated as 'conditional support' – acceptable only for niche cross-border collections and disbursements, but not as the sole banking rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions for compulsory Local File, Master File, and IR International Questionnaire preparation; below-threshold transactions still require contemporaneous evidence that the price is arm's length.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not impose a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the general anti-avoidance rule in ITA 2007 s BG 1 and the transfer pricing rules in s GC 13 require actual employees, premises, unique functions, decision-making power, and risk-bearing capacity in New Zealand. A subscription-box entity that outsources core income-generating activities (procurement, packaging, quality assurance, customer contracting, and inventory risk) to an offshore related party without arm's length compensation will be recharacterized or reconstructed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Omission of Offshore Settlement Funds

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 s 139B(1)(c) imposes an evasion shortfall penalty of 150%; s 143B creates a criminal evasion offence carrying imprisonment for up to 5 years and a fine of up to NZD 50,000 for natural persons, with larger court-imposed fines for body corporates; use-of-money interest accrues under s 120A.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly directed offshore settlement or who signed falsified accounts are parties to the evasion under Crimes Act 1961 ss 66 and 71 and may be personally imprisoned. In civil proceedings, the corporate veil may be pierced under Companies Act 1993 s 135 (reckless trading) and s 136 (obligations performed without reasonable grounds), requiring directors to restore diverted assets and compensate creditors. Inland Revenue may also obtain a departure prohibition order and director blacklisting under TAA Part XA for unpaid assessed tax debts.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance / GAAR Avoidance

Statutory sanction: ITA 2007 s BG 1 invalidates the tax avoidance arrangement; reconstruction and recharacterisation follow under subpart GA and GB; transfer pricing adjustments apply under s GC 13; the abusive tax avoidance shortfall penalty is 50% under TAA s 139B(1)(b); and the Registrar may remove the shell from the New Zealand register under Companies Act 1993 s 364 for lack of real business activity.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for company assets lost through reckless trading, insolvent transactions, and failures to prevent serious company loss under Companies Act 1993 ss 135-137 and 288-289. A court may order asset restitution, disqualify directors under s 383, blacklist them through the Ministry of Justice, and impose criminal liability for false declarations or fraudulent use of the corporate structure.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Evasion / DTA Abuse on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The NRWT provisions of ITA 2007 make the NZ company liable for 15% on the fee or 30% on the dividend, even if it never withheld; false statements incur a shortfall penalty under TAA s 143A, escalation to a 150% evasion penalty under s 139B(1)(c), plus monthly late payment penalties and use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certified treaty entitlement or imputation credits for a non-beneficial owner face criminal prosecution under TAA s 143B. If the company has been stripped of distributable reserves through disguised distributions, the corporate veil can be pierced to recover assets from directors under Companies Act 1993 ss 52 and 135-137. Inland Revenue may issue departure prohibition orders and blacklist directors against whom personal assessments arise, and an insolvent dividend or transaction at an undervalue is recoverable from the directors personally.
