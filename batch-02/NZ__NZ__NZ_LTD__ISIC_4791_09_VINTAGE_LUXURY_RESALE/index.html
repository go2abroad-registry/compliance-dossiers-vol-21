Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; online electronic portal: myIR Secure Online Services at ird.govt.nz.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident company must first obtain an IRD number from the Inland Revenue Department. The application is made by lodging the standard non-resident company IRD number application with IRD, either through myIR or by postal submission accompanied by certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, company constitution, register of directors and shareholders, and proof of appointed New Zealand agent or tax representative where applicable. If the company will make taxable supplies in New Zealand exceeding the registration threshold, the same application must include GST registration; New Zealand administers GST under the same IRD number and does not issue a separate VAT identification number. For a non-resident company with no New Zealand banking presence, a New Zealand tax agent or registered agent must be engaged and confirmation of overseas address and officer details must be provided to satisfy CRS/FATCA and anti-money-laundering requirements before the IRD number is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand has no general check-the-box election. A closely held New Zealand company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status for flow-through income tax treatment if it satisfies the owner-count and eligibility rules; however, for a limited liability company carrying on an online resale business through non-resident shareholders, the default treatment is entity-level taxation. Hybrid mismatch and base-erosion rules apply to New Zealand entities with foreign hybrid constituent parts; relevant elections and recharacterisations must be made on return disclosure schedules with IRD. Otherwise, no entity-classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies (GST-exclusive consideration) supplied in New Zealand in any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services must register when the NZD 60,000 threshold is reached for supplies to New Zealand recipients, unless the supply is already zero-rated and the non-resident has no New Zealand establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of vintage luxury goods is zero-rated for GST where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand in the ordinary course of the sale and the goods are physically removed from New Zealand before the expiry of 28 days after the time of supply. The supplier must hold contemporaneous documentary evidence that the goods were exported and that the customer received the goods outside New Zealand. Qualifying digital or remote services supplied to non-resident customers are zero-rated where the customer is outside New Zealand and the services are not supplied in connection with New Zealand land or goods in New Zealand; service-supplier evidence may include the customer's residence, IP address, billing address, payment data, and foreign contract terms. Zero-rating cannot be claimed unless the supplier is GST-registered and the evidence requirements under the GST Act 1985 are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) filed through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the international carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank foreign exchange inward credit confirmation evidencing repatriation of the export sale proceeds to New Zealand

There is no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance API, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime in New Zealand. New Zealand operates a voluntary e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol network, and GST-registered persons may use commercial e-invoicing platforms. Tax invoices must contain all prescribed particulars and be issued within 28 days after the supply; GST returns are filed electronically through myIR with periodic payment, and the Commissioner may require specific invoice or record formats under investigation but does not pre-approve transactions.

The Inland Revenue Commissioner may disallow zero-rating and assess GST at 15% on the full consideration if the required export evidence is missing, late, false, or fails to show physical export and foreign exchange repatriation within the statutory or repatriation window. The Tax Administration Act 1994 shortfall penalties apply: 20% for failing to take reasonable care, 40% for an abusive tax position, and 100% for evasion or fraudulent intent, plus use-of-money interest for underpaid GST. Export documentation must be retained for at least seven years. Retrospective clawbacks may also arise if goods are returned, diverted back into New Zealand, or if proceeds remain unpaid beyond the 180-day foreign exchange repatriation deadline without a valid explanation; criminal sanctions may be imposed for knowingly providing false export or repatriation documents.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised foreign exchange regime. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible on both current and capital account transactions, with no general exchange control approvals, currency surrender obligations, or mandatory repatriation timelines imposed by the RBNZ. Payments and transfers for trade, services, loan repayments, dividends, and even repatriation of invested capital may proceed directly through the banking system using ordinary customer-initiated SWIFT payments. Current account transactions such as import/export settlement require no specific central bank authorisation, although banks require accurate trade documentation and AML/CFT identification of counterparties. Capital account items, including inbound direct investment to capitalise New Zealand Ltd companies, share transfers, and foreign-currency borrowing, are similarly unrestricted for most non-resident investors, subject to the Overseas Investment Act for sensitive land and fishing quotas; a standard online luxury-resale trading company is not sensitive. Profit remittance to overseas shareholders is permitted without withholding-tax clearance, but non-resident dividend withholding tax (NRWT) may be imposed at up to 30% unless reduced by an applicable double tax agreement, and the bank will request Form IR67 or a tax certificate. No export proceeds are required to be surrendered to any entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory or regulatory requirement exists in New Zealand for the surrender of export proceeds. Exporters may retain and use foreign currency receipts freely; no mandatory conversion ratio or timeline is prescribed. Any such retention must still satisfy the reporting bank's AML/CFT requirements and the company's own tax and treasury policy.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

New Zealand has no bespoke EMI licensing framework; non-bank payment providers must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register and comply with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. Offshore EMIs such as Revolut, Wise, or Airwallex can lawfully hold funds for NZ companies if they hold FSP registration and provide local settlement accounts; however, they are not registered banks and cannot offer a legally verified capital escrow account, direct RBNZ settlement, or a treasury-grade borrowing facility. Viability: Medium for low-value cross-border disbursements and operating expenses; Low as a sole treasury rail for a high-value luxury resale entity because of AML concentration risk, platform payment-rail limitations, and the need for structured documentary banking.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 1,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions in an income year requires contemporaneous Local File and transfer pricing documentation; a Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the worldwide group has annual consolidated revenue above NZD 1.125 billion.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations code for ordinary trading companies, but the Income Tax Act 2007 anti-avoidance provisions and the OECD agency-PE analysis require a New Zealand entity to perform core income-generating activities (CIGA) such as sourcing, authentication, pricing, inventory custody, risk assumption, seller onboarding, and payment settlement. A shell NZ Ltd managed entirely offshore and used only for invoicing, title routing, and DTA access will be disregarded and the profit reattributed to the non-resident principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 ss 141E and 143B: shortfall penalty equal to 150% of the evaded tax plus statutory late payment penalties; criminal fine up to NZD 50,000 per charge and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Limited liability is pierced where the director signs or directs the false return; the director is jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and shortfall penalties, must make civil restitution from personal assets, may face freezing orders, travel bans, and debtor blacklisting, and may be prosecuted as a party to the evasion offence with custodial imprisonment up to 7 years.
Shell Company / Double Tax Agreement and Permanent Establishment Abuse

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1 (general anti-avoidance rule): Inland Revenue may reconstruct the arrangement and disregard the New Zealand shell; transfer pricing adjustments under ss GC 1-13; an abusive tax position penalty of 100% of the tax shortfall; Companies Office strike-off or deregistration under the Companies Act 1993 for an inactive or defunct foreign-owned veneer entity.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute nominee resolutions, sign DTA documentation, or approve unsupported dividend declarations face personal civil liability under Companies Act 1993 ss 135 and 136 for reckless trading and incurring obligations without reasonable belief of performance; the share veil is pierced for restitution; director disqualification and travel blacklisting are available; criminal prosecution for knowingly facilitating an abusive tax avoidance arrangement can result in imprisonment.
AML/CFT and Payment-Rail Misuse in Luxury Resale

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009: civil penalties up to NZD 2,000,000 for a body corporate and NZD 200,000 for each involved director or officer; criminal money-laundering liability under the Crimes Act 1961 may carry up to 7 years' imprisonment; proceeds of crime forfeiture under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009 may confiscate tainted luxury inventory, payment balances, and personal director assets.

Director personal liability: A director who personally establishes, controls, or instructs the payment flow and conceals beneficial owners is treated as a principal money launderer; the corporate veil is pierced for civil restitution, the director may be blacklisted and disqualified from managing a company, travel restrictions may be imposed, and the director faces imprisonment up to 7 years for criminal participation in money laundering.
