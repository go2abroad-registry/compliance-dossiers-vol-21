Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is permissible for Companies Office registration because the Companies Act 1993 only requires a physical service address in New Zealand, but it is generally rejected by Inland Revenue and by New Zealand trading banks for a commercial stock photography and AI-asset company that will collect revenue through Stripe and PayPal. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand – online electronic portal myIR (ir.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

For foreign non-resident entities, the statutory Tax Identification Number is an IRD number issued by Inland Revenue. Non-resident companies carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand, or required to register for goods and services tax (GST) on remote supplied digital services, must apply for an IRD number using the prescribed application form for non-individuals (IR596) and, where required, register for GST using the prescribed GST registration form (IR356) or through the myIR online portal. The application must include certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, the entity's constitution, a letter from the registered agent, director and shareholder identities, and a completed New Zealand resident withholding tax declaration if applicable. If the non-resident entity has no physical presence in New Zealand, a New Zealand tax agent may be required; the Commissioner of Inland Revenue generally requires an application to be lodged via myIR or by mail, with original documents or certified translations if not in English. Once the IRD number is issued, the same number is used for income tax, GST, and employer obligations; no separate VAT ID exists.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. New Zealand does not provide a check-the-box elective classification; limited companies are taxed at the entity level as a matter of statute. Hybrid mismatch rules under Subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 apply automatically and do not involve taxpayer elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; the NZD 60,000 threshold also applies to non-resident suppliers of remote services (cross-border digital supplies) to New Zealand consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 applies where goods are supplied in the course of a taxable activity to a non-resident recipient and the goods are exported by the supplier within 28 days of the time of supply. Services: zero-rating under section 11A applies to services (including digital services) supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not physically performed in New Zealand and the benefit of the services is received outside New Zealand. For the stock-photography and generative visual assets business, downloadable digital content supplied to overseas customers will qualify for zero rating if the customer is a non-resident of New Zealand at the time of supply, is outside New Zealand, and the supply is linked to an overseas jurisdiction.

  • Export Entry lodged in the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window (export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and supply contract showing the non-resident purchaser and overseas destination
  • Bank inward foreign transfer evidence (SWIFT MT103 or banking credit advice) demonstrating repatriation of sales proceeds
  • Recipient non-resident status evidence for digital services (e.g., billing address, IP geolocation, or certificate of residence)

No mandatory electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance regime in New Zealand. Inland Revenue does not operate a pre-clearance API or anti-fraud digital stamping for individual GST invoices. Taxpayers may use voluntary e-invoicing under the Peppol framework. GST returns are self-assessed and filed electronically via myIR, with monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly filing cycles based on turnover.

If zero rating is claimed incorrectly or cannot be substantiated, the GST becomes payable (clawback). Inland Revenue may issue an assessment for the additional GST, plus use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties under Part 7A of the Tax Administration Act 1994: up to 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 150% for fraudulent intent. Repeat or fraudulent claims may also result in criminal prosecution under section 143 of the Tax Administration Act 1994. The standard four-year statutory assessment period may be extended to seven years in cases of fraud or gross carelessness.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised and freely floating foreign exchange regime with no exchange controls on either current account or capital account transactions. The Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, together with the repealed Exchange Control Act 1989, has removed all vestiges of mandatory surrender, conversion, or licensing for cross-border fund transfers. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible without restriction. Both residents and non-residents may hold foreign currency accounts domiciled with locally registered banks, and there is no statutory obligation to repatriate export earnings or seek Reserve Bank approval for profit remittances, dividend distributions, or intercompany loan repayments. The sole regulatory perimeter stems from anti-money laundering and counter-financing of terrorism (AML/CFT) obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, which require banks to verify the source of funds and the beneficial ownership of the entity. Additionally, the Overseas Investment Act 2005 may scrutinise foreign direct investment that involves 'sensitive' land or significant business assets, but for a digital stock photography platform engaged in non-sensitive commercial activity, the investment is generally not 'significant' under the asset thresholds. In practical terms, profit remittance is executed via straightforward bank instructions, subject only to standard tax withholding obligations (non-resident dividend withholding tax, for instance) and the bank's internal KYC/AML review. New Zealand's monetary authority does not impose a surrender ratio or timeline, and exporters are free to manage proceeds in their chosen currency, giving this jurisdiction a top-tier status for treasury flexibility and capital mobility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists in New Zealand. Exporters are not required to convert export proceeds into NZD, nor are there any mandatory timelines or ratios for the repatriation of foreign currency receipts. Earnings from global stock photo licensing and AI content subscriptions can be retained in USD, EUR, or any other currency, or converted at the entity's discretion via the prevailing wholesale banking market. The only compliance touchpoint is that all inbound and outbound remittances must be appropriately coded for financial reporting and AML/CFT record-keeping under the Financial Transaction Reporting requirements, but there is no central bank approval or conversion floor.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low and legally constrained. New Zealand does not issue a standalone 'electronic money institution' licence comparable to the UK or EU EMI frameworks. Non-bank financial institutions that wish to hold client funds or offer payment services must be registered as a financial service provider under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and simultaneously become a reporting entity under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, which requires a full AML/CFT programme and a nominated compliance officer. Digital-only EMIs domiciled outside New Zealand, such as offshore fintech platforms, are generally barred from clearing NZD through the local high-value payment system (Exchange Settlement Account System, ESAS) because they are not direct settlement participants and are not licensed deposit takers. They would need to route through a locally incorporated bank sponsor, which effectively negates the cost and agility advantage. More critically, most local banks require onboarding of the underlying entity directly, not through a second-tier EMI. As such, the treasury blueprint strongly mandates direct account relationships with one of the Tier-1 domestic banks listed above, while using payment orchestration platforms (Stripe, PayPal) purely as collection channels, with settlement ultimately settling into a primary bank account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: There is no statutory NZD threshold that automatically requires the filing of a Local File or Master File in New Zealand. However, New Zealand MNEs with consolidated group revenue exceeding NZ$1.3 billion are required to file a Country-by-Country Report notification and report. Inland Revenue expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for any material cross-border related-party transaction; practical audit scrutiny commonly intensifies where aggregate related-party turnover exceeds NZ$10 million, and the Commissioner can require documentation under the information powers in the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like the UAE or certain Crown Dependencies. Economic substance is nevertheless embedded in the tax residence, source, permanent establishment, and general anti-avoidance rules of the Income Tax Act 2007 and in the beneficial ownership and limitation-on-benefits articles of New Zealand DTAs. A New Zealand company with no office, employees, assets, or substantive board control in New Zealand will not be respected as the principal of the stock-photo AI business. Core income-generating activities, including contractual negotiation, pricing, content curation, model development, and risk assumption, must be performed in the jurisdiction claiming the profit. If effective management and control is outside New Zealand, the entity may be a non-resident for domestic law or treated as a shell for treaty purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Knowingly Filing a False IR4 and Concealing Digital Platform Revenue

Statutory sanction: Section 143B Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes criminal liability for evasion or similar offences, with fines up to NZ$50,000 for a first offence and imprisonment up to five years. The tax shortfall attracts a 100% evasion shortfall penalty under section 141E, plus use-of-money interest, late payment penalties, and a requirement to file accurate amended returns.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in preparing or signing a false IR4 can be prosecuted personally as principals or parties to the offence. Inland Revenue can invoke the serious-fault director liability provisions to pierce the corporate veil and recover the company's unpaid tax from directors' personal assets. A court may issue a departure prohibition order under section 157 TAA to prevent a director from leaving New Zealand while a tax debt is outstanding, and a criminal conviction can lead to disqualification under the Companies Act 1993.
Transfer Pricing and Artificial Profit Stripping - Sham Royalties and Offshore IP Ownership

Statutory sanction: Sections GC 13 to GC 19 Income Tax Act 2007 authorise Inland Revenue to reconstruct the consideration and adjust the New Zealand taxable income on an arm's length basis. Shortfall penalties under section 141A (20% unacceptable tax position) and section 141B (40% abusive tax position) apply, with further uplift where the position is not reasonably arguable. The reconstructed shortfall is also subject to use-of-money interest from the original due date.

Director personal liability: A director who approves a sham pricing structure or signs an IR4 that incorporates inflated offshore payments may be liable under the evasion ancillary-offence provisions. The corporate veil may be pierced where the arrangement is a sham or a mere cloak; directors can face civil recovery of diverted profits, personal liability under the Companies Act 1993 for reckless trading or breach of director duties, travel-ban orders, and, where fraud is proved, criminal imprisonment.
Shell Company Abuse, Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure and AML/CFT Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, non-compliance can expose the company to civil penalties up to NZ$2 million for serious contraventions, plus daily default fines. Under the Companies Act 1993, failure to maintain registers attracts default fines and potential deregistration. Inland Revenue can treat the entity as a sham and impose non-filing penalties, shortfall penalties, and criminal sanctions under the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who are involved in AML/CFT contraventions may be personally liable for penalties and damages. If the shell structure is used to launder proceeds of unlawful stock-photo or fraud activity, directors may be prosecuted under the Crimes Act 1961 money-laundering provisions, which carry imprisonment up to seven years per offence. Civil asset recovery, director disqualification, statutory demands, bankruptcy proceedings, and departure prohibition orders are all available to the Crown and creditors, effectively piercing the corporate veil where the company is a facade.
