Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox may be accepted as the statutory registered office in New Zealand only if it is a real, physical, street-addressed location in New Zealand, staffed during ordinary business hours, and capable of accepting legal service of process. A PO Box or mail-only virtual address is rejected for company registration. The New Zealand Inland Revenue Department and New Zealand registered banks generally require a physical place of business rather than a mailbox-only address for GST registration, bank account opening, and AML/CFT customer due diligence. For the off-price finished-garments wholesale industry, a pure virtual office without a substantive local operating presence is likely to be rejected by IRD and banks; the prudent structure is a law firm registered-office address plus a separate physical operating or warehousing address for commercial substance and bank verification.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital requirement under New Zealand law. A company may be incorporated with zero nominal share capital and without an escrow account. The only mandatory payment is the non-refundable NZD 74 registration fee. Share consideration may be cash, property, or services, subject to board approval.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certification: A Building Warrant of Fitness (WOF) is required for premises with an applicable building or fire safety system. An Evacuation Scheme and Fire Evacuation Procedure are required when more than 100 employees work at the premises, in accordance with the Fire and Emergency New Zealand (Fire Safety, Evacuation Procedures, and Evacuation Schemes) Regulations 2018. Structural Load Inspections: A Code Compliance Certificate from the relevant city or district council certifies that the warehouse floor loading, racking systems, and structure meet the New Zealand Building Code, particularly AS/NZS 1170.1:2002 structural loading. Racking systems must have a compliance inspection by a licensed engineer. Workplace Clearance: WorkSafe New Zealand requires a documented worker engagement and participation system under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, a hazard identification and risk assessment register, warehouse-specific orientation for all personnel, and emergency evacuation notices and first-aid kits in place prior to operations. If the premises are leased, the landlord's existing building compliance and WOF documentation must be verified and kept on file.

Wholesale distribution of finished garments is generally not subject to a separate industrial licensing board in New Zealand. The applicable authorities are: the New Zealand Companies Office for entity registration; Inland Revenue for wholesale supply taxation; the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE) for workplace safety and commercial regulation; and the relevant city or district council as territorial authority for land use, building compliance, and health licensing of warehousing and distribution premises.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

New Zealand Inland Revenue Department (IRD), including the myIR online tax administration portal. Customs export declarations and border export controls are administered separately by the New Zealand Customs Service.

E-government filing portal

A New Zealand limited company is incorporated with the New Zealand Companies Office and then applies to Inland Revenue for an IRD number if one is not issued through the incorporation process. The company should register through myIR or the prescribed IRD registration process, provide its Companies Office company number, registered office, principal business activity, directors, shareholders, authorized contact, bank details where requested, accounting period, expected turnover, and tax residency information. A nonresident company carrying on business in New Zealand, deriving New Zealand sourced income, or otherwise required to file New Zealand returns must apply directly to Inland Revenue for an IRD number and, where applicable, a nonresident taxpayer account. GST registration requires an application to Inland Revenue stating that taxable supplies exceed, or are expected to exceed, NZD 60,000 in any twelve-month period, or that voluntary registration is sought. The applicant must identify the effective registration date, taxable activities, filing frequency, taxable period, accounting basis, and authorized representative. Foreign entities should submit constitutional documents, proof of legal existence, ownership and control information, director details, registered address, business contracts or other evidence of New Zealand taxable activity, and certified identity documents if requested under Inland Revenue verification procedures. There is no separate universal VAT identification number distinct from the IRD number; a GST-registered person generally uses its IRD number as its GST identifier. Customs client registration and an importer or exporter client code may also be required for customs transactions and are separate from tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand has no United States style check-the-box election. A qualifying New Zealand company may elect to become a look-through company under the Income Tax Act 2007, subject to eligibility, shareholder, continuity, and filing requirements, with income and losses generally attributed to owners rather than taxed at company level. A qualifying company may also use other statutory regimes, such as qualifying company or imputation credit mechanisms, but these do not generally convert an ordinary company into a transparent entity. A New Zealand limited company is ordinarily subject to company income tax at 28 percent on taxable income. New Zealand applies anti-hybrid and branch mismatch rules based on OECD principles; these operate by statute and do not provide an elective hybrid mismatch classification. New Zealand tax residence and source rules determine whether income is taxable, and nonresident companies may be subject to New Zealand tax on New Zealand sourced income and business activities conducted through a New Zealand permanent establishment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding twelve months or expected taxable supplies in the following twelve months. Voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold where the statutory requirements are satisfied.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods supplied for export may generally be zero-rated where the statutory export conditions under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 are satisfied, including export by the supplier or export through an approved export arrangement and removal from New Zealand within the applicable statutory period, generally 28 days unless an extension or special rule applies. Goods sold to a purchaser who exports them are not automatically zero-rated merely because the purchaser intends to export them; the supplier must satisfy the relevant export documentation and timing requirements. International freight and certain export-related services may be zero-rated where the statutory conditions apply. Services supplied to a nonresident may be zero-rated only where the services are not physically performed in New Zealand for a person present in New Zealand and the statutory section 11A conditions are met. Services connected with land, goods situated in New Zealand, New Zealand events, or performance to a person in New Zealand may remain standard-rated. The supplier must retain sufficient evidence to demonstrate the nature of the supply, customer status, destination, export or delivery, contractual terms, and compliance with the applicable zero-rating provision. GST refunds are available where input tax exceeds output tax, subject to ordinary GST verification, documentation, and anti-fraud rules.

  • New Zealand Customs export entry or approved electronic export declaration
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, purchaser, description, quantity, value, delivery terms, and destination
  • Packing list and transport documentation, including bill of lading, sea waybill, air waybill, or courier export record as applicable
  • Evidence that the goods physically departed New Zealand, such as Customs export release, carrier departure confirmation, freight forwarder confirmation, or delivery evidence
  • Sales contract, purchase order, and proof of payment where relevant to substantiate the transaction
  • Evidence supporting any export timing extension or special zero-rating rule
  • For offshore services, contract, customer residency evidence, service location evidence, correspondence, deliverables, and records demonstrating that the statutory zero-rating conditions were satisfied
  • Inward remittance evidence may support commercial substantiation but is not generally a mandatory New Zealand GST zero-rating document, and New Zealand does not generally require an Inward Remittance Certificate for export GST treatment

New Zealand does not generally require real-time electronic invoice clearance, government pre-approval, digital fiscal stamping, or universal submission of invoice data to Inland Revenue. Businesses may issue electronic tax invoices if the required GST information is provided and records remain accessible and reliable. New Zealand supports electronic invoicing through the Peppol framework and has government adoption initiatives, but there is no general economy-wide mandatory Peppol or API clearance requirement for ordinary private-sector transactions. GST records, tax invoices, export evidence, customs records, and accounting data must be retained for the statutory record-retention period, generally at least seven years. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR or an approved intermediary at the assigned filing frequency.

New Zealand has no general foreign-exchange repatriation requirement or mandatory deadline for export proceeds. Incorrect zero-rating can result in reassessment of output GST at 15 percent, recovery of any overclaimed GST refund, use-of-money interest, and penalties. Shortfall penalties may generally be imposed at rates determined by the taxpayer conduct, including 20 percent for lack of reasonable care, 40 percent for gross carelessness, 100 percent for an unacceptable tax position in applicable circumstances, and 150 percent for tax evasion or a similar act, subject to statutory thresholds, reductions, and voluntary disclosure rules. Deliberate false or misleading statements, fraudulent refund claims, obstruction, or falsification of records may also create criminal exposure under tax administration and criminal legislation. Customs noncompliance may lead to seizure, forfeiture, administrative or infringement penalties, prosecution, and reassessment of duties or GST. Inland Revenue may require contemporaneous export, transport, contractual, customer-status, and accounting evidence and may deny zero-rating where the statutory conditions cannot be proven.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has an open, freely convertible foreign exchange regime. Current account payments (trade invoices, service fees, dividends, interest) and capital account movements (share capital subscriptions, intercompany loans, repatriation of funds) are not subject to general exchange controls or central bank approval. Profit remittance is lawful after the company satisfies the solvency test in the Companies Act 1993, files correct tax returns, and meets non-resident withholding tax or Approved Issuer Levy obligations. The main practical barrier to remittance is not convertibility but bank due diligence on source of funds and documentation, plus the Overseas Investment Act 2005 screening for non-resident acquisitions of sensitive land or significant business assets above statutory thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation of export receipts in New Zealand. Exporters may keep foreign currency in offshore or onshore foreign currency accounts, use it directly to pay suppliers, or convert it at any time. The statutory position is 'no surrender requirement, 0% mandatory conversion, and no time limit'; banks nonetheless expect export invoices, customs declarations, and shipping documents to classify receipts for balance-of-payments statistics and AML/CFT monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ). RBNZ is the central bank and prudential supervisor responsible for registered banks and payment system stability; New Zealand does not have a separate foreign-exchange control authority. The Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and the Overseas Investment Act 2005 provide ancillary compliance gateways.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as sole settlement bank, Medium as an ancillary cross-border payment channel. New Zealand has no dedicated e-money institution (EMI) licensing statute; an offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully hold deposits or call itself a bank without being registered under the Banking Prudential Supervision Act 1989 and supervised by RBNZ. It may operate as a limited-purpose financial service provider if registered under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and as a reporting entity under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, with the Department of Internal Affairs as anti-money laundering supervisor. For this NZ company, the practical model is to maintain a local bank operating account for domestic clearing and corporate credibility, while using an offshore EMI only for acceleration of small trade payments where counterparties accept virtual accounts; all major inbound and outbound treasury flows should clear through registered New Zealand banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from non-resident shareholders or holding companies must use bank-to-bank SWIFT MT103 messages, or local NZD clearing via Payments NZ Direct Entry where the send is domestic. The remittance instruction must state the true purpose, for example 'PAID-UP CAPITAL', 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION', or 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION', and include the ISO 20022 purpose category accepted by the receiving bank, typically 'TREA', 'CASH', or a bank-defined 'CAPT' code. The receiving NZ bank will require the NZ company to provide the share subscription agreement, board resolution approving the allotment, updated share register, Companies Office filings, and source-of-funds evidence for each beneficial owner. Exchange control approval is not required, but if the capital is connected to a transaction triggering the Overseas Investment Act 2005 screening, consent must be obtained before closing. Funds should be received in the same currency as the underlying shareholder loan or share position, and the company should reconcile the MT103 against its internal treasury records.Outbound trade payments for procuring finished garment overstock should use SWIFT MT103 for open-account telegraphic transfers, with a commercial letter of credit issued by the NZ bank (MT700) where supplier terms require documentary credit. For letter of credit settlements, the bank will use SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for the reimbursement claim, accompanied by compliant shipping documents, including commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or air waybill, and optional certificate of inspection. Urgent same-day NZD payments should use the high-value exchange settlement system via the bank; lower-value New Zealand-dollar obligations can be cleared through Payments NZ Direct Entry, often described as ACH Direct. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low as sole settlement bank, Medium as an ancillary cross-border payment channel. New Zealand has no dedicated e-money institution (EMI) licensing statute; an offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully hold deposits or call itself a bank without being registered under the Banking Prudential Supervision Act 1989 and supervised by RBNZ. For this NZ company, the practical model is to maintain a local bank operating account for domestic clearing and corporate credibility, while using an offshore EMI only for acceleration of small trade payments where counterparties accept virtual accounts; all major inbound and outbound treasury flows should clear through registered New Zealand banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$10 million in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions per income year (including purchases, sales, financing arrangements, services, royalties, management fees, and loan balances). Above this threshold, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation equivalent to Master File and Local File under the OECD TPG must be prepared before the IR4 is filed; below the threshold, a concise transfer pricing policy and arm's length justification are still required for material related-party dealings.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand applies a statutory general anti-avoidance rule in s BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 that vitiates arrangements whose purpose or effect is tax avoidance, plus the separate 'scheme' reconstruction power in s GA 1. Unlike coastal offshore ESR regimes, New Zealand does not administer a standalone economic substance filing, but the Commissioner assesses substance through premises, local employees, board decision-making, inventory custody, and risk assumption. For an off-price wholesale liquidation company, the physical storage of liquidation goods, New Zealand-based sourcing and pricing decisions, and local banking and warehousing operations are the critical indicia; an arrangement that is merely nominated in New Zealand while real control sits in an offshore affiliate will be voided and reconstructed for tax purposes. Thin capitalisation and the interest limitation rule (30% of EBITDA, with a NZ$1 million de minimis) further cap deductible debt from non-resident owners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994: imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines of up to NZ$50,000 per offence for natural persons and up to NZ$100,000 for a body corporate; civil shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax; statutory use-of-money interest on the full deficiency; mandatory disclosure to the Serious Fraud Office for amounts over the evasion threshold.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct false returns are prosecuted in their personal capacity, may be disqualified under the Companies Act 1993, and can be required to make civil restitution for the company's tax shortfall on a joint and several basis; the High Court may also grant freezing orders and travel restrictions pending fraud proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation & Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may adjust the pricing to an arm's length justified transfer price under the transfer pricing rules; the shortfall penalty is 20% for failure to maintain or produce documentation, escalating to 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion; interest limitation and thin capitalisation rules disallow excess related-party interest; GST adjustment may also be imposed on underpriced exports.

Director personal liability: Directors personally face assessment for unpaid NRWT and RWT arising from deemed distributions or excessive interest paid to the offshore affiliate; they are also exposed to void-dividend recovery where ICA debits were made without sufficient credit balances, and to criminal participation liability for knowingly executing a base-eroding scheme.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: Section BG 1 voids the tax avoidance arrangement as against the Commissioner; s GA 1 permits reconstruction on an economic-substance basis; 100% abusive position shortfall penalty or 150% evasion penalty applies; the company may be treated as non-resident where central management and control is offshore; penalties for false statutory declarations and breaches of director duties under the Companies Act 1993 apply.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who knowingly sign statutory declarations for a shell without any commercial information are personally liable for fines of up to NZ$200,000, may have the corporate veil pierced for fraudulent trading, and can be disqualified for a period fixed by the court; personal travel restrictions and compensation orders are available where shell operations facilitate tax fraud.
