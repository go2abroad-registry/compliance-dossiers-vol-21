Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permissible as the principal trading address for an airline inflight catering kitchen. New Zealand law requires an actual physical catering facility on or adjacent to an airport, with HACCP-based food safety controls under the Food Act 2014, a bonded storage and customs area under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, ramp access in accordance with Civil Aviation security rules, and adequate industrial machinery, refrigeration, and sanitation. The company may maintain a registered office with a professional service provider under the Companies Act 1993, but Inland Revenue will treat a mere mailbox as an invalid place of business and banks will reject it for Monthly ACH and aviation-contract payment rails due to the absence of physical substance, local payroll, and supply-chain oversight. For this ISIC 5621 industrial scope, a virtual office is therefore rejected as the primary compliance address.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Companies Act 1993. The Act does not prescribe a minimum paid-up capital or a blocked escrow account. The board may issue shares for any lawful consideration; where consideration is non-cash, an ordinary resolution and, in certain cases, an auditor's or independent chartered accountant's report on the consideration are required under sections 40-42. For the ISIC 5621 inflight-catering licence, MPI, the Civil Aviation Authority, and the airport lessor may impose bonding, working capital, or performance-guarantee conditions as commercial and sectoral licensing requirements, but no general statutory capital floor exists.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue) of New Zealand via the myIR online portal (myIR.ird.govt.nz); company registration through the New Zealand Companies Office and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) registry.

E-government filing portal

For an NZ limited company, incorporation with the Companies Office triggers automatic enrolment with Inland Revenue, and the company receives an IRD number for tax purposes and an NZBN for business identification. For a foreign non-resident company not incorporated in New Zealand, the statutory route is to apply for overseas company registration with the New Zealand Companies Office where the company is carrying on business in New Zealand, or otherwise to apply directly to Inland Revenue for an IRD number using the prescribed non-resident registration process and supporting documentation. The application must be supported by: certificate of incorporation (or foreign equivalent), certified constitutional documents, registered office address in New Zealand for service of notices, director and ultimate shareholder details with certified identity documents, and evidence of New Zealand taxable activity or expected taxable supplies. Inland Revenue issues the IRD number; where the entity carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand and exceeds, or intends to exceed, the statutory GST threshold, the entity must separately apply for GST registration. The GST registration application requires estimated annual turnover, chosen GST filing frequency (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly), bank account details, and tax agent or contact information.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in New Zealand. Eligible closely held companies may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status to become fiscally transparent for income tax, subject to strict ownership and eligibility conditions, and the election is irrevocable without Inland Revenue consent. Hybrid and branch mismatch entity classification elections, where relevant, must be notified to Inland Revenue under the hybrid mismatch rules; no other entity-level elections apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period, triggering compulsory registration within 21 days of becoming liable; voluntary registration is also available where the threshold is not met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a supply of goods is zero-rated when the goods are exported from New Zealand and the supplier holds the prescribed evidence of export. Supplies of goods loaded on an international aircraft or ship as stores, including in-flight catered meals physically taken out of New Zealand on an international flight, can qualify as zero-rated exports where the statutory export conditions and documentation are satisfied. Services supplied to a non-resident airline in connection with exported goods, or remote services supplied to a non-resident outside New Zealand, may also be zero-rated under the territorial rules. Domestic-flight catering supplies and services performed in New Zealand for consumption on domestic flights remain standard-rated at 15%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry) lodged through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing loading and physical export departure from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank foreign-exchange credit confirmation demonstrating payment repatriation from the overseas airline customer

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is voluntary under the Peppol framework. GST returns must be filed electronically through myIR or accredited software, and GST invoices must contain the prescribed statutory content and be retained for the required period. No Inland Revenue pre-clearance of invoices is required.

If export zero-rating is claimed without valid export evidence, or goods do not physically leave New Zealand within the permitted timeline, Inland Revenue will recharacterise the supply as standard-rated and assess 15% GST, with use-of-money interest running from the original due date. Shortfall penalties apply under the Tax Administration Act 1994 on a graduated scale up to 150% for evasion, including penalties for lack of reasonable care, gross carelessness, unacceptable tax positions, and abusive tax positions. Knowingly false export declarations may also be prosecuted under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 and Tax Administration Act 1994. The assessment time bar is generally four years, but no time limit applies where a return is fraudulent, evades tax, or the taxpayer has not filed a return.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates an open, lightly regulated capital account. The New Zealand dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital account, with no foreign exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no central bank approval threshold for inward or outward remittances. Resident companies may freely repatriate dividends, repay intra-group loans, and settle intercompany invoices. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not ration currencies or impose repatriation ceilings. Profit remittance is constrained only by general tax, thin capitalisation, transfer pricing, and Companies Act solvency tests, plus the ability to document the transaction as legitimate. In practical banking terms, funds transfers are settled through the CLS system for NZD, and partner banks use ordinary SWIFT messaging. The only meaningful regulatory filter is anti-money laundering and sanctions screening under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. Export proceeds are not required to be converted or surrendered. Capital injections and dividend distributions are treated as ordinary banking transactions once beneficial ownership, source of funds, and tax status are verified.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender requirements in New Zealand. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts offshore or convert to NZD at their chosen bank at market rates. No conversion timeline is mandated; funds can remain in foreign currency accounts indefinitely, and banks may require only transaction-specific source-of-funds documentation for export invoices or airway bills.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (the central bank), with the Financial Markets Authority acting as financial conduct regulator for banking and markets

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for primary treasury clearing; conditional viability as a supplementary receivable channel. Offshore digital EMIs are legally constrained in New Zealand because the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's prudential regime requires any party undertaking deposit-taking or issuing payment instruments to be a registered bank or a licensed non-bank deposit taker; there is no separate EMI passport from the European Union. An NZD balance maintained with an offshore EMI is not a protected bank deposit and generally cannot clear through the Exchange Settlement Account System. Therefore, the entity should rely on Tier-1 domestic banks for primary clearing, statutory capital custody, trade finance, and FX hedging, while EMIs may be used only for low-value collections or limited-volume supplier payments where regulatory-compliant and transparent to the domestic banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital should be remitted in NZD or USD via SWIFT MT103 from the investing shareholder's bank to the entity's Tier-1 bank account. The remitter must use a clear payment purpose description such as 'equity subscription for NZ Limited' or 'paid-up capital contribution' and reference the share subscription agreement. No Reserve Bank of New Zealand approval is required, but the receiving bank will perform AML screening and may request corporate authorisation, board resolution, and source-of-funds statements. Upon receipt, the company must issue shares and update the Companies Office registry; a chartered accountant can then conduct capital verification and issue a formal capital certificate. For tax efficiency, capital injections should not be structured as shareholder loans without proper documentation, to avoid thin-capitalisation and non-resident withholding tax complications.Supplier and aviation-related disbursements should be routed through SWIFT MT103 for high-value, time-sensitive payments, backed by approved purchase orders and supplier invoices. For large capital equipment entering the in-flight catering kitchen, an irrevocable, at-sight commercial letter of credit issued by the Tier-1 bank provides asset-backed settlement assurance. Documentary collections are suitable for repeat lower-risk suppliers where agreed invoice credit terms are in place. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 in annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers the mandatory maintenance of a New Zealand Local File and, for group revenue above the OECD threshold, a Master File and Country-by-Country Report; below NZD 10,000,000 the taxpayer must still retain bench-level arm's length documentation.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to certain offshore financial centres, but IRD vigorously applies the OECD-aligned transfer pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1, source provisions, and the non-resident companies' attribution rules. A catering entity engaged only as a shell booking vehicle without New Zealand kitchen premises, employed chefs, logistics control, risk-bearing, and board decision-making for menu planning, purchasing, and hygiene compliance may be disregarded and its profits re-attributed to the offshore principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994; on conviction, imprisonment for up to 5 years and/or a fine of up to NZD 50,000 for individuals and NZD 100,000 for a body corporate; civil shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the evaded tax plus use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false tax returns or direct staff to maintain dual invoicing systems face personal criminal liability, disqualification from managing companies under the Companies Act 1994, asset forfeiture, and departure prohibition orders preventing exit from New Zealand while tax debts are investigated.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: IRD adjustment of taxable income to the arm's length result; imposition of shortfall penalties of up to 150% for abusive tax avoidance; use-of-money interest accruing from the original balance date; potential six-year reassessment period extended to ten years in cases of evasion or non-disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors approving intercompany pricing solely for tax avoidance may be jointly and severally liable for the resulting shortfall penalty and interest; involvement in a deliberate transfer pricing scheme removes the usual director shield and exposes directors to criminal aiding-and-abetting charges.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the company as a conduit or sham; disallowance of deductions under section GB 1; GAAR reconstruction under section BG 1; penalties for failure to disclose avoidance; possible strike-off and deregistration under the Companies Act 1994.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow the company to carry on business without genuine substance or while insolvent risk personal liability for unpaid taxes as trading while insolvency is present; courts may make civil reparation orders, disqualification orders, and asset preservation orders; serious cases result in criminal conviction and imprisonment.
AML/CFT Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009; civil penalties up to NZD 2,000,000 for a body corporate and NZD 300,000 for an individual per contravention; infringement notices and forfeiture of tainted property; criminal prosecution for money laundering carrying imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and directors who know or suspect laundered funds are passing through the kitchen's payment rails face personal indictment, imprisonment of up to 7 years, and court-ordered restitution to affected parties; comfort directorships provide no defence where the director has acted with wilful blindness.
