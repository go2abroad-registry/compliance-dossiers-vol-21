Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office may satisfy the Companies Act 1993 requirement for a registered office in New Zealand if it is a physical New Zealand street address, not a post office box, at which documents can be served and received during business hours. However, for the recycling and physical consignment of shredded non-ferrous scrap copper wire under ISIC 4669, this structure will face material bank and Inland Revenue Department substance scrutiny. New Zealand banks and the IRD are likely to reject a pure commercial mail forwarding box without records of staff, a lease, board meeting facilities, physical inventory, and trade documentation. A serviced office with a dedicated physical suite, a named local registered agent, storage and inspection arrangements, and customs compliance infrastructure is more likely to be accepted. A non-physical virtual office alone is insufficient for AML/CFT customer due diligence and is an adverse indicator for cross-border B2B metals trading because of the high money laundering and trade-based money laundering risk in the scrap metal sector.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand law prescribes no minimum paid-up capital for a limited liability company. The board may issue shares for no paid-up capital or for such consideration as it determines, provided shares are not issued at a discount and the board complies with any solvency requirements under the Companies Act 1993. No statutory escrow or blocked share capital account is required, and the registered share capital will be shown in the company's annual return.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand; online portal: myIR (https://myir.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident limited company must first complete any applicable New Zealand Companies Register requirement (or register as an overseas company) only if it has a place of business in New Zealand. For tax registration, the entity must apply to the Inland Revenue Department for an IRD number. The application can be submitted electronically through the myIR portal after establishing an account as a non-resident company, or by completing the non-resident company IRD-number application form. The company must provide its certificate of incorporation (in English or translated), director particulars, a New Zealand registered address or agent's address, and a statement describing its proposed taxable activity (e.g., export of recycled copper waste and scrap). Once the IRD number is issued, a separate GST registration can be made through the same myIR profile, either before or upon exceeding the NZD 60,000 threshold; registration is advisable earlier if the company wishes to zero-rate exports and reclaim input GST on acquisitions used for export.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available; hybrid mismatch rules operate by statute rather than election. However, an eligible close-held limited company may irrevocably elect look-through company (LTC) status under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, subject to closely held criteria, and a group of wholly owned companies may elect consolidated tax group treatment under subpart IG of the Income Tax Act 2007. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

15%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11(1) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a supply of goods is zero-rated where the supplier has exported, or is responsible for exporting, the goods from New Zealand and the goods are physically removed from New Zealand within 60 days of supply (or within such longer period as the Commissioner accepts). For fragmented non-ferrous scrap (e.g., recycled copper wire), the zero-rating applies on the supply to the overseas buyer if the goods are containerised and shipped under documentary credit or telegraphic transfer, and documentary evidence demonstrates the goods left New Zealand.

  • Customs export entry lodged through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window (requires accurate classification, value, and destination details for the scrap copper wire)
  • Bill of lading (or air waybill) signed by the carrier and dated after export, evidencing ship or aircraft departure from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and contract of sale showing the foreign buyer and Incoterms (e.g., FOB, CIF)
  • Bank settlement confirmation (e.g., SWIFT MT103 or letter of credit presentation) if foreign exchange is repatriated; though not a statutorily required document for zero-rating, it should be retained as underlying evidence of an export sale

New Zealand does not operate a real-time transaction clearance, fiscal digital stamping, or anti-fraud API submission regime. Electronic invoicing is voluntary, with a PEPPOL-based e-invoicing framework available. GST returns (IR372) must be filed electronically via myIR at the filing frequency determined by IRD; invoices must contain the statutory details set out in the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

Inland Revenue may audit the substantiation of export zero-rating by requesting the documents in the bundle above plus associated weight, assay, and container tracking records. If the zero-rating is disallowed, GST at 15% is assessed on the supply, with use-of-money interest accruing from the original due date. Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, civil shortfall penalties apply: 20% for failure to take reasonable care or an unacceptable tax position; 40% for gross carelessness or blatant disregard; and 150% for evasion or an arrangement to defraud the revenue. There is no separate foreign-exchange repatriation deadline in New Zealand tax law, but consistent commercial payment records reduce the risk of reconsideration of the export character of the supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a freely convertible currency with no general exchange control restrictions. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) trades freely on the spot, forward, and swap markets. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) does not impose exchange controls under the Currency (Restrictions of Use) Act or the Overseas Investment Act, except for notifications on very large investments or fisheries/quota assets. Capital account transactions for resident and non-resident investors are fully liberalised. There are no surrender requirements for export earnings, and companies may hold foreign currency accounts at registered banks without RBNZ approval. Dividends and capital repatriations follow standard company law and tax clearance procedures, not FX authorisation. The practical hurdle is not convertibility but rather the exchange rate risk and bank AML/KYC requirements for high-risk commodity trades such as scrap copper. Profit remittance is straightforward with a completed IR4/IR7 tax form and bank FX contract. For a limited company engaged in recycling of non-ferrous scrap metal, the RBNZ expects all overseas direct investment to be reported for statistical purposes via Statistics New Zealand's overseas direct investment survey, but this is not a permission gate. Overall, the regime is one of the most open in the OECD, with no administrative ceilings on current or capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign currency receipts under New Zealand law. Exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in foreign currency accounts held with registered banks, or convert to NZD at commercial rates. The only compliance obligation is accurate record-keeping for GST, customs valuation, and tax purposes. For ISIC 4669 scrap metal trading, exporters should document the underlying export declaration and lodgement to Customs NZ to reconcile with FX receipts for banks' AML monitoring.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, or Revolut Business) operate in New Zealand either as money remitters under the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) or via banking-as-a-service partnerships with registered banks. They are legally permitted to service NZ-domiciled entities, but they are not Registered Banks and cannot issue documentary letters of credit, negotiate bills of exchange, or offer trade finance credit. For the specific scope of recycled scrap copper wire trading—which is a medium-risk enhanced commodity with high-value cross-border flows—the practical viability of an EMI as the primary treasury rail is Low to Medium. EMIs can provide an on-ramp for Fiat collection, debit cards, and basic SWIFT transfers, but they often apply strict velocity controls on high-value metals traders, request full downstream paperwork on every transaction, and may close accounts if the trade involves tier-3 jurisdictions. Moreover, offshore EMIs without an NZ FMA licence cannot provide a local NZD IBAN necessary for local clearing (NZ Clearing, ESAS). Therefore, for LC settlements and multi-million dollar consignment, a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank is mandatory. An EMI may be used only as a secondary collection tool for pre-payments under USD 20,000, with full audit trails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No hard statutory monetary threshold in the Income Tax Act 2007 automatically requires preparation of a Local File or Master File; however, Inland Revenue administrative practice and OECD-aligned transfer pricing guidelines require taxpayers to prepare contemporaneous documentation for all connected-party cross-border transactions, with an expectation of formal Local File and Master File documentation where annual related-party dealings exceed NZD 1,000,000. The Commissioner may require documents and particulars under the Tax Administration Act 1994 sections 16 and 17 where material related-party copper scrap transactions are present, and failure to substantiate the arm's length price shifts the evidentiary burden against the taxpayer.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate named Economic Substance Regulations comparable to zero-tax offshore jurisdictions; nevertheless, the Income Tax Act 2007 general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1, the transfer pricing rule in section GC 13, and double tax agreement substance doctrines require the NZ entity to prove real business purpose, physical premises, employees, board control, and core income-generating activities in the scrap copper purchase, grading, shredding, logistics, and resale chain. A company without substance that exists solely to collect treaty benefits or to re-invoice non-ferrous scrap transactions is treated as a sham and can be reconstructed, denied treaty relief, assessed on deemed profits, and subjected to director personal liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalties under Tax Administration Act 1994 sections 141A-141E of up to 150% of the evaded tax plus use-of-money interest; criminal prosecution under section 143B for evasion carries a fine of up to NZD 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. A false GST refund claim is prosecuted separately as tax fraud under the same statutory regime.

Director personal liability: A director who knew of or directed the fraudulent scheme is treated as a principal offender. The court may pierce the corporate veil under Companies Act 1990 section 588 to hold the director personally liable for all tax debts and trade creditor losses arising from dishonest trading. The director's personal assets can be frozen, alternative family trust assets traced, travel restricted by border alert/blacklisting while the case is pending, and upon conviction the director faces up to 5 years imprisonment, restitution orders, and disqualification from managing any New Zealand company.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reconstruction under Income Tax Act 2007 section BG 1 and section GB 1; transfer pricing adjustment under section GC 13; denial of DTA benefits; assessment of the offshore profit in the hands of the NZ company or the real controller; imposition of 30% NRWT on deemed distributions; and tax shortfall penalties for an unacceptable tax position up to 40%, rising to 150% if the shell arrangement is held to be evasive.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who lend their names without undertaking real functions are personally exposed where they knowingly participate in a sham. AML/CTF and Trade-Based Money Laundering / Export Misdeclaration
