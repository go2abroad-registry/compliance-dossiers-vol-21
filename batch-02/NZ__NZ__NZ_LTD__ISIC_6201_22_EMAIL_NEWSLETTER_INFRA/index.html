Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (Te Tari Tauhokohau) of New Zealand; online filing and entity registration portal: myIR (myir.ird.govt.nz). Company and NZBN registry: New Zealand Companies Office, Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE). Customs authority: New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate an NZ Ltd with the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993; the company automatically receives a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). Step 2 - Inland Revenue automatically issues an IRD number for income tax upon incorporation; the IRD number is the statutory TIN and is used for both income tax and GST, with no separate GST certificate number. Step 3 - For GST registration (mandatory if total taxable supplies including zero-rated exports exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, or voluntary if carrying on a taxable activity), log into myIR using the NZBN, complete the GST registration application, and Inland Revenue endorses GST registration on the same tax account. Step 4 - Non-resident ownership is permitted with no minimum capital; a foreign non-resident entity that has no NZ company presence but derives NZ-source income or supplies remote services to NZ consumers must apply for an IRD number through the non-individual IRD application via myIR or an approved agent and, where the NZD 60,000 remote-services threshold is met, register for GST.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies, including zero-rated export supplies, in the preceding or next 12 months; for non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand consumers the same NZD 60,000 threshold applies to total supplies made to New Zealand recipients. Voluntary GST registration is available to any person carrying on a taxable activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods - a GST-registered supplier may zero-rate exported goods under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the goods are exported by or on behalf of the supplier before, or within 28 days of, the time of supply, and the supplier retains contemporaneous export evidence. Services - under section 11A, services, including the email newsletter/API hosting platform, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, the services are not an excepted supply, and there is documented evidence that the recipient is an offshore non-resident (offshore contract terms, offshore billing address, non-NZ payment rail and/or IP/service-consumption logs).

  • New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window electronic export declaration/export entry (for physical goods; no New Zealand statutory Bill of Entry exists)
  • Commercial invoice or zero-rated tax invoice with supplier NZBN/IRD number, description of email infrastructure services or goods, offshore recipient, currency and consideration
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) together with carrier export manifest/port departure proof (physical goods only)
  • Signed offshore service contract or API subscription agreement and service-delivery logs confirming remote use outside New Zealand (digital services)
  • SWIFT MT103 credit advice, Stripe/Banking payout statement, or New Zealand bank foreign-exchange/foreign-currency remittance confirmation evidencing receipt from the overseas payer; Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) is not a New Zealand statutory customs form and is therefore not required

New Zealand has no real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, anti-fraud stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing; Peppol-based NZ e-Invoicing is voluntary. GST returns are filed electronically via myIR. For exported goods, New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window electronic export declaration is required for customs purposes, but this is not a tax clearance mechanism.

Inland Revenue may retrospectively assess 15% GST plus use-of-money interest if the zero-rating claim is not fully substantiated at audit. If exported goods are not exported within 28 days of supply, or if a service supply is proved to be enjoyed/consumed in New Zealand, the zero-rating collapses and output tax is clawed back from the original supply date. Shortfall penalties apply under the Tax Administration Act 1994, generally 20% for taking an unacceptable tax position or lack of reasonable care, rising to 40% for evasion; deliberate misstatement or fraudulent GST evasion may also lead to criminal prosecution and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised current and capital account. There are no exchange controls, no restrictions on conversion to or from foreign currency, and no surrender requirements on export receipts. Any person or company may hold, purchase, borrow, or sell foreign currency through registered banks and authorised financial institutions. Current account transactions, including subscription fees, software licensing, and cross-border SaaS revenue, may be settled freely. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, shareholder loans, and repatriation of dividends or branch profits, are permitted without prior regulatory exchange-control approval. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand supervises banks, clearing systems, and settlement systems for prudential and financial-stability purposes, but it does not approve individual convertibility transactions. The only material friction for profit remittance is the tax withholding regime: resident withhold tax on dividends, non-resident withholding tax, and any applicable double-tax-agreement relief require proper documentation from the beneficial owner. In addition, the Overseas Investment Act 2005 imposes consent requirements for transactions involving sensitive land, fishing quota, or significant business assets, although a pure email-infrastructure SaaS entity will normally fall outside those thresholds. Sanctions screening and AML/CFT customer due diligence remain compulsory but do not affect ordinary currency convertibility. Emergency exchange-control powers exist in statute but have not been used in modern times, so practical convertibility risk is assessed as low.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no forced timeline for export foreign currency receipts in New Zealand. Exporters may retain all foreign currency earnings in onshore or offshore foreign-currency accounts, may convert to New Zealand dollars at any time, and may use the receipts for operational expenditures without seeking regulatory permission. The only applicable discipline is tax and accounting compliance: New Zealand resident companies must report income in New Zealand dollars for Inland Revenue purposes, must use a consistent foreign-exchange translation method, and must retain documentation substantiating exchange rates under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Banks may impose contractual arrangements for currency conversion thresholds as part of credit risk or AML/CFT monitoring, but these are bank policy requirements, not statutory surrender rules.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Conditional Medium for supplemental payment routing, but Low for primary treasury custody. New Zealand does not have a distinct electronic money institution licensing class equivalent to the United Kingdom Financial Conduct Authority EMI regime or the European Union electronic money directive. An offshore EMI that wishes to provide financial services to or for a New Zealand resident entity must assess whether its activities require registration on the Financial Service Providers Register under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and whether it is captured by the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. In practice, a UK/EU EMI can provide multi-currency collection and payout capabilities to a New Zealand SaaS company, but it cannot hold a New Zealand bank settlement account unless it establishes a physical or agency relationship with a registered New Zealand bank. The EMI is not covered by any New Zealand depositor compensation regime, and local NZD clearing through Bulk Electronic Clearing System or the Exchange Settlement Account System is typically unavailable without a local bank sponsor. For the target payment rails of Stripe Billing and credit cards, the recommended architecture is to use a New Zealand Tier-1 bank as the treasury anchor, with Stripe payouts settling in NZD to the bank account, and to use an offshore EMI only for niche multicurrency payouts, provided the EMI is fully licensed, regulated, and compliant with New Zealand AML/CFT obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital and shareholder loans should be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the overseas investing entity to the New Zealand Tier-1 bank, with the payment narrative clearly stating the purpose, such as 'NZD capital subscription per board resolution dated...' or 'Shareholder loan per loan agreement dated...'. New Zealand does not require exchange-control approval for such wires, but the receiving bank will apply standard AML/CFT screening and will require supporting board resolutions, share allotment minutes, and a source-of-funds declaration. The company must file any share allotment with the New Zealand Companies Office within the statutory filing window and update its share register accordingly; should the injection be documented as debt, the loan agreement should state interest, repayment schedule, and subordination if appropriate. For capital verification, the bank may ask for an accountant's certificate confirming that the credited amount has been properly allocated to issued share capital or a shareholder loan in the financial records. If the investment contains assets that touch sensitive New Zealand land or significant business assets, Overseas Investment Act consent may be needed, but for a standard email-infrastructure SaaS holding entity with no such assets, no consent is required.Cross-border outbound settlements should be executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 payment instructions for vendor payments, hosting fees, cloud infrastructure, and intra-group service charges. For New Zealand domestic disbursements to payroll, contractors, and local suppliers, the company should use the Bulk Electronic Clearing System for low-value batch credits and the Exchange Settlement Account System for high-value real-time gross settlement transactions. Commercial letters of credit are appropriate only for physical goods procurement such as hardware infrastructure, not for core SaaS subscription services; where documentary collections are needed, New Zealand banks will require the presentation of invoices, packing lists, transport documents, and a certificate of origin to the negotiating or collecting bank. For the newsletter platform's affiliate and revenue-share payouts, the preferred architecture is to settle Stripe Billing revenues into the NZD operating account, then make outbound SWIFT MT103 payments or local BECS direct entries based on a formal payout report. The company should ensure that each outbound payment includes a meaningful purpose narrative, retains supporting invoices, and applies the correct withholding tax treatment for any fees paid to non-resident contractors.Spot NZD/USD, NZD/EUR, and NZD/AUD conversions with any registered New Zealand bank or authorised dealer for ordinary operational settlements of subscriptions, hosting bills, and software licensing fees.Deliverable outright foreign exchange forwards and FX swaps with tenors from one to twelve months, provided the underlying exposure arises from committed SaaS subscription contracts, cloud capacity agreements, or declared dividend obligations, and supported by an approved board FX policy and signed ISDA Master Agreement.Non-deliverable forwards are not necessary for NZD because the New Zealand dollar is freely convertible and deliverable; NDFs should be used only for exotic or restricted counterparty currencies where no genuine deliverable NZD market exists, and any such NDF must be rolled to spot at maturity rather than allowed to become a speculative position.Cross-currency interest rate swaps are permissible for matching offshore debt or asset exposures and converting foreign-currency liabilities into NZD, but speculative trading is not permitted under conservative bank treasury policy, and all hedging transactions remain subject to bank credit limits, transaction reporting, and the company's documented risk management mandate.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Conditional Medium for supplemental payment routing, but Low for primary treasury custody. New Zealand does not have a distinct electronic money institution licensing class equivalent to the United Kingdom Financial Conduct Authority EMI regime or the European Union electronic money directive. An offshore EMI that wishes to provide financial services to or for a New Zealand resident entity must assess whether its activities require registration on the Financial Service Providers Register under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and whether it is captured by the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. In practice, a UK/EU EMI can provide multi-currency collection and payout capabilities to a New Zealand SaaS company, but it cannot hold a New Zealand bank settlement account unless it establishes a physical or agency relationship with a registered New Zealand bank. The EMI is not covered by any New Zealand depositor compensation regime, and local NZD clearing through Bulk Electronic Clearing System or the Exchange Settlement Account System is typically unavailable without a local bank sponsor. For the target payment rails of Stripe Billing and credit cards, the recommended architecture is to use a New Zealand Tier-1 bank as the treasury anchor, with Stripe payouts settling in NZD to the bank account, and to use an offshore EMI only for niche multicurrency payouts, provided the EMI is fully licensed, regulated, and compliant with New Zealand AML/CFT obligations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and Local File are required when the New Zealand group has annual turnover above NZD 100 million and cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZD 10 million; Country-by-Country Reporting is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue is NZD 1.1 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime of the UAE type, but the OECD transfer pricing rules and the general anti-avoidance rule in s BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 require real functions, assets, and risks to be present in New Zealand; Core Income-Generating Activities are relevant because email infrastructure SaaS revenue must be allocated to the entity performing development, hosting, security, and client-management functions, and a registered limited company without staff or office cannot support the arm's length return.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent filing

Statutory sanction: The Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes a 150% evasion shortfall penalty under s 143B; criminal prosecution under s 143 may result in a fine not exceeding NZD 100,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the director uses the limited company as a sham; the director can be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, ordered to repay diverted profits, disqualified from managing companies, and restrained from leaving the jurisdiction by a court order connected to international asset movement.
Non-resident withholding tax evasion and dividend stripping

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue will apply anti-avoidance and recharacterisation rules to treat the payment as a dividend; a 30% NRWT assessment applies, plus use-of-money interest and a shortfall penalty of up to 150% under the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Director personal liability: A director who authorises an unlawful distribution or a scheme intended to defeat NRWT is personally liable to compensate the company under the Companies Act 1993; if the transfer occurred when the company could not satisfy its tax debts, preference recovery action may attach to the director's personal assets, and disqualification from directorship can be imposed by the Companies Office.
Abusive transfer pricing, thin capitalisation and shell company substance

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may adjust income to the arm's length amount under s GC 13 of the Income Tax Act 2007, disallow excessive interest under the interest apportionment rules, and apply a 100% abusive tax position penalty under s 141D; the adjusted tax is payable immediately and cannot be deferred through later audit responses.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign materially false transfer pricing declarations or cause the company to enter a scheme lacking commercial substance may be personally liable for reckless trading under s 320 of the Companies Act 1993; civil asset restitution, board-ban orders of up to 5 years, and criminal liability for false accounting are possible, while international travel restrictions may be sought in an extraditable tax fraud proceeding.
