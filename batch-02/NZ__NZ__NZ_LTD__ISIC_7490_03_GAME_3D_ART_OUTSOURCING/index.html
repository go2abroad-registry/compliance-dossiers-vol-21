Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department of New Zealand (Inland Revenue / IRD). Online portal: myIR Secure Online Services – https://www.ird.govt.nz. Administered under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

E-government filing portal

A New Zealand incorporated limited company is issued an IRD number (TIN) automatically upon incorporation through the Companies Register. For foreign non-resident entities needing a statutory New Zealand TIN or GST/VAT-type registration number, the SOP is: 1) access myIR at ird.govt.nz or use the IRD prescribed non-resident application channel; 2) submit the company foreign certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director and ultimate beneficial owner identification, registered office address, and the country-of-residence tax reference number; 3) state the intended New Zealand tax or GST activity, including expected turnover from New Zealand supplies; 4) await IRD validation, typically 5–10 working days, after which the IRD number is issued; 5) if GST registration is required, elect the myIR service ‘Register for GST’ using that TIN; 6) for non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand consumers, mandatory GST registration is required once NZD 60,000 of New Zealand consumer supplies is exceeded in a 12-month period.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A New Zealand incorporated limited company is issued an IRD number (TIN) automatically upon incorporation through the Companies Register. For foreign non-resident entities needing a statutory New Zealand TIN or GST/VAT-type registration number, the SOP is: 1) access myIR at ird.govt.nz or use the IRD prescribed non-resident application channel; 2) submit the company foreign certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director and ultimate beneficial owner identification, registered office address, and the country-of-residence tax reference number; 3) state the intended New Zealand tax or GST activity, including expected turnover from New Zealand supplies; 4) await IRD validation, typically 5–10 working days, after which the IRD number is issued; 5) if GST registration is required, elect the myIR service ‘Register for GST’ using that TIN; 6) for non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand consumers, mandatory GST registration is required once NZD 60,000 of New Zealand consumer supplies is exceeded in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 provides the statutory zero-rating tests. Goods are zero-rated when they are exported from New Zealand and evidence of physical export is retained. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the service is not directly connected with New Zealand land or goods situated in New Zealand, and is not supplied to a person physically present in New Zealand under certain visa or residence circumstances. For this 3D game art outsourcing scope, the supply of digital artwork, 3D sculpting, rigging and any related intellectual property to a non-resident game studio is a zero-rated exported service if the customer’s business establishment and end-use are offshore and the digital deliverables are transferred electronically outside New Zealand. Substantiation must include non-resident status evidence and delivery or end-use records. There is no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline under the GST Act; adopting a 180-day remittance benchmark is recommended to demonstrate commercial substance.

  • Signed master services agreement or purchase order identifying the offshore game studio and the 3D art, sculpting and rigging deliverables
  • GST zero-rated tax invoice issued to the non-resident customer containing the supplier’s New Zealand GST registration number
  • Electronic delivery receipt or signed acceptance certificate for the exported digital files (e.g., ZIP, FBX, OBJ, rig assets)
  • Bank SWIFT MT103 or equivalent foreign-currency remittance receipt showing receipt of payment from the offshore customer
  • Certified evidence of the customer’s non-resident status (foreign certificate of incorporation, overseas tax identification number, or statutory declaration)
  • If applicable, an IP assignment or licence agreement evidencing transfer of rights for use exclusively outside New Zealand

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction control API, and no anti-fraud digital stamping regime are currently imposed in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and aligned to the PEPPOL interoperability framework. Tax invoices must satisfy section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and may be issued and stored electronically under the Electronic Transactions Act 2002.

Inland Revenue may reassess a zero-rated export supply as taxable at 15% GST where the section 11 conditions are not met or the substantiation bundle is deficient. The resulting shortfall accrues use-of-money interest under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Shortfall penalties under section 141 Tax Administration Act 1994 apply at 20% where the taxpayer fails to take reasonable care, 40% for a substantially below correct tax position, 100% for an abusive tax position and 150% for evasion or similar. Knowingly providing false export zero-rating documentation can trigger criminal prosecution, significant fines and imprisonment. Retrospective reassessments may be made within seven years for non-fraudulent understatements and without a time limit in cases of fraud or evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has a liberalised, freely convertible currency regime under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021, with no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no mandatory conversion ratios. The New Zealand dollar floats freely, and both current account and capital account transactions may be settled without central bank approval. Dividend and profit remittances are subject only to standard corporate tax documentation and banking KYC checks, not to exchange control permission. Inward investment screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 is limited to sensitive land and significant business assets, and does not apply to ordinary operating capital for an outsourced game art studio. Foreign currency accounts can be held freely, and payment clearing can occur in NZD or major currencies such as USD, EUR, and AUD. The absence of exchange controls does not eliminate AML/CFT obligations; banks still require robust source-of-funds evidence, beneficial ownership disclosure, and transaction purpose verification. The practical consequence is that treasury structuring is driven by counterparty risk, settlement efficiency, and hedging needs rather than by state-imposed convertibility constraints.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion rules apply in New Zealand. Exporters and service providers may retain foreign currency receipts in offshore or onshore foreign currency accounts without any forced conversion timeline or ratio. Settlement occurs at freely agreed market exchange rates through authorised banking channels.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand - Te Pūtea Matua

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs without a New Zealand financial service provider licence and AML/CFT registration are not viable for corporate treasury purposes, as they would not hold a registered banking relationship under New Zealand law. NZ-licensed non-bank payment providers and digital banks are feasible for small-value receivables such as PayPal settlements, but they are not substitutes for Tier-1 commercial bank clearing rails for large inbound capital or trade disbursements. The recommended approach is to use local Tier-1 banks as principal clearing counterparties and keep EMIs restricted to low-limit ancillary payment collection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be settled by SWIFT wire transfers from the shareholder's or parent entity's bank to the New Zealand entity's Tier-1 bank account, using clear cross-border payment instructions and the correct beneficiary legal name. New Zealand has no exchange control approval requirement, but banks require the remitter to state the purpose of the payment and the recipient to provide supporting board resolutions, share allotment records, or shareholder loan agreements. If the injection is paid-up share capital, the amount is evidenced by the Companies Office share issue return and a bank confirmation letter. No statutory capital verification is required because New Zealand has no minimum paid-in capital for a limited liability company.For trade settlements, SWIFT MT103 wire transfers are the preferred clearing rail for recurring service fees, royalties, and contractor payments. Commercial letters of credit may be used for high-value imported hardware or software infrastructure purchases where supplier trust is moderate. Documentary collections are acceptable for smaller transactions with known counterparties where the cost of letter-of-credit issuance exceeds the risk. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% corporate income tax rate under Income Tax Act 2007, subpart MB, charged on the worldwide income of a New Zealand resident limited company.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% NZ NRWT on fully imputed dividends paid to non-residents and 30% NRWT on non-fully imputed dividends, unless a lower Double Tax Agreement rate applies; the NZ payer must withhold and account to Inland Revenue on the actual distribution, not merely on accumulated profits.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million aggregate cross-border related-party transactions requires the NZ Ltd to prepare contemporaneous Master File and Local File documentation; multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue equal to or exceeding NZD 1 billion are subject to mandatory Country-by-Country reporting in New Zealand.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand is not an Economic Substance Regulations jurisdiction in the Gulf-style sense, but the OECD substance doctrine, the New Zealand general anti-avoidance rule under section BG 1, and the Multilateral Instrument principal purpose test require the company to perform its core income-generating activities in New Zealand: actual 3D modelling, sculpting, rigging, art direction, contract negotiation and risk management must all be supported by local staff, premises, board decisions and accounting substance; a bare invoice conduit with foreign decision-makers will be treated as a sham or artificial arrangement for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 section BG 1 and Tax Administration Act 1994 sections 141, 143A and 143B impose a tax shortfall penalty of 100% of the evaded tax, daily penal interest, and criminal prosecution with a fine up to NZD 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years per offence.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under Companies Act 1993 section 300 for reckless trading; directors are personally liable for civil restitution of the tax shortfall and assets withheld, may be prohibited from leaving New Zealand under a travel prohibition order under section 157H of the Tax Administration Act 1994, and face criminal imprisonment up to 5 years for tax evasion.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 sections BG 1 and GB 1 permit Inland Revenue to disregard the structure, reallocate profits, deny treaty benefits under the MLI principal purpose test, impose shortfall penalties up to 100%, and reassess the company and its shareholders on a reconstruction basis.

Director personal liability: Directors bear personal civil liability for knowingly allowing the company to trade with no legitimate business substance; courts may pierce the veil and treat the NZ Ltd as an agent or sham, directors may be disqualified from managing companies under Companies Act 1993 section 383, listed on Inland Revenue's blacklisting/non-compliance register, subjected to asset restraint and travel bans, and prosecuted criminally for fraudulent trading.
AML/CFT and Money Laundering Violation

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 imposes civil and criminal penalties on body corporates of up to NZD 2 million per contravention; individuals and directors face fines up to NZD 300,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years, and the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009 permits forfeiture of assets traceable to criminal proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal money-laundering charges under Crimes Act 1961 section 243, which carries imprisonment up to 7 years, together with civil asset recovery, travel restrictions and border alerts, disqualification from company management, and blacklisting by financial institutions which serves as a de facto travel and banking ban.
