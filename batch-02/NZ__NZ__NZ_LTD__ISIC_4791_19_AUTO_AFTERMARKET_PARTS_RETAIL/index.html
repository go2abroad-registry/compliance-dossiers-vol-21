Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

New Zealand company legislation (s 187 Companies Act 1993) requires a registered office that is a physical location in New Zealand and open to the public at ordinary business hours, where documents may be served. A bare commercial mail-forwarding address or virtual mailbox is not a lawful registered office, and Inland Revenue and NZ banks reject it for GST registration and merchant account onboarding. For an ISIC 4791 online auto parts e-commerce operation, a 'virtual office' can be accepted only if it consists of a serviced office with a genuine physical address, a receptionist who can accept service, access to meeting rooms, and a dedicated telephone line. This must be distinguishable from a PO Box or customer-facing outlet. We recommend the client lease a serviced office in a recognized business centre and include the suite number in all correspondence. Stripe and PayPal acceptance policies similarly require a verifiable physical business address; they will offshore-review any mailbox-only address.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake), New Zealand; online portal myIR at ird.govt.nz. Company formation is administered by the New Zealand Companies Office through the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) registry.

E-government filing portal

For an NZ_LTD entity, incorporate under the Companies Act 1993 with the New Zealand Companies Office; the company will be issued an NZBN. Use the NZBN-linked business registration service in myIR to apply for the IRD number and to include a GST registration election at the same time, citing the principal business activity as online retail of automotive replacement parts and tuning accessories under ISIC 4791. If the operating entity is instead a foreign non-resident company, complete the Inland Revenue prescribed non-resident company IRD number application, attaching certified copies of the foreign incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, directors register, overseas tax residence certificate, and registered address details. After the IRD number is issued, use myIR to manage GST registration, confirm the NZD 60,000 registration threshold status, select a filing frequency, and update bank details for any GST refunds. The IRD number is the single tax reference for income tax, GST, and employer obligations; New Zealand does not issue a separate VAT identification number. A foreign-owned NZ Ltd with no physical New Zealand office should appoint a New Zealand tax representative for service of notices and Inland Revenue correspondence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies including zero-rated supplies made in New Zealand in any 12-month period; the same threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of remote or digital services supplied to New Zealand consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical automotive parts exported by a GST-registered New Zealand supplier are zero-rated if the goods are exported in the course of the sale and are removed from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply, with the supplier holding contemporaneous documentary evidence of export. Services and digital automotive content are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the supply is not directly connected with New Zealand land, goods in New Zealand, or performance physically rendered in New Zealand. A GST-registered exporter making zero-rated supplies may claim associated input tax credits, and excess credits are refundable by Inland Revenue after verification.

  • Customs export declaration or export entry lodged through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window where a formal declaration is required under the Customs and Excise Act 2018
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air freight showing the consignee and named foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order or export contract evidencing the overseas sale
  • Packing list and export manifest or cargo movement record
  • Carrier or freight-forwarder certificate of export where the customer or a third party arranges shipment and the supplier does not physically export the goods
  • Payment evidence where available, such as SWIFT credit advice, bank statement entry, or remittance advice; New Zealand does not issue a mandatory Inward Remittance Certificate for GST purposes
  • For offshore digital services, electronic evidence of the offshore recipient and place of consumption, including customer billing address, IP or geolocation data, and a written contract attesting non-resident status

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime. New Zealand operates a voluntary eInvoicing framework based on the PEPPOL network. A GST-registered supplier must issue a valid tax invoice for taxable supplies exceeding NZD 50 including GST; the invoice must contain the statutory particulars in section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, including supplier name and GST number, invoice date, nature and quantity of the supply, price excluding GST, GST amount, and total payable. Compliance is enforced through record retention, tax return reconciliation, and Inland Revenue audit rather than pre-clearance.

If zero-rating is not substantiated, Inland Revenue will assess GST at 15% on the supply, with use-of-money interest from the original taxable period and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994, including 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and up to 150% for evasion or intent to mislead. Inland Revenue may reassess within the normal four-year time bar; there is no time bar for fraudulent returns or unfiled returns. New Zealand has no statutory foreign exchange repatriation deadline applicable to GST export zero-rating; the critical substantiation requirement is removal of the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of supply and preservation of the complete export evidence bundle. Criminal sanction for tax evasion can include imprisonment under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised foreign exchange regime with no exchange control legislation. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. There is no requirement for prior central bank approval for foreign direct investment, repatriation of capital, dividend remittances, or servicing of intercompany debt, provided that the transaction is recorded accurately for tax and statistical purposes. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) does not impose surrender requirements on export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in offshore or onshore accounts and are free to convert them at any authorised dealer bank. Profit remittances by a New Zealand Ltd to non-resident shareholders are subject to New Zealand's non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) regime, but there are no foreign exchange hurdles. The absence of any 'convertibility restriction' means that treasury teams can execute cross-border payments and hedging strategies without prior regulatory filing, although banks must complete standard customer due diligence and suspicious transaction reporting under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. For a low-risk online auto parts retail entity, the practical constraint is not FX control but rather bank de-risking tendencies and the need to demonstrate legitimate e-commerce payment flows to satisfy correspondent bank and local bank compliance committees.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export foreign currency receipts. New Zealand does not require exporters to surrender foreign currency to the central bank or any designated authority. All export proceeds may be retained in NZD or foreign currency at the company's discretion, subject only to normal tax filing and transfer pricing documentation if transactions are with related parties.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

The viability of offshore fintech EMIs (electronic money institutions) for a New Zealand-registered company is Low-to-Medium for primary banking and Medium for supplementary payment routing. New Zealand does not issue EMI licences per se; any entity providing payment services must be registered as a 'payments provider' under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and must also comply with the AML/CFT Act by becoming a reporting entity. Major international EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Currenxie do, however, service New Zealand businesses via their licensing arrangements in other jurisdictions (e.g., UK FCA or Australian AUSTRAC/DCE), but they are generally not permitted to offer a full suite of regulated clearing and deposit services in New Zealand without a local banking relationship. For an e-commerce auto parts retailer, using offshore EMIs as primary settlement rails is legally risky because those EMIs often route NZD transactions through local partner banks, and New Zealand's own prudential regulation requires a New Zealand-based banking licence for deposit-taking. A practical structure is to maintain a Tier-1 NZD operational account with a local bank for statutory capital and tax obligations, while using a well-regulated offshore EMI (like Wise or Airwallex) strictly for cross-border vendor disbursements, FX conversion, and multi-currency receiving in USD/EUR/AUD. The bank's compliance department will require full transparency on any EMI account used, as correspondent banks treat flows from unknown third-tier EMIs as higher-risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection into the NZ Ltd's operational NZD account should be executed via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's overseas corporate or personal bank account. The wire must include a specific remittance information field stating 'Share Capital Contribution' or 'Contribution to Working Capital' and using New Zealand Reserve Bank's standard Business/Other purposes code 'C. Capital - direct investment - equity' (or the bank's equivalent code). For NZD 5,000 or larger, the receiving bank will request a copy of the board resolution authorising the capital injection and a capital verification certificate from a New Zealand Chartered Accountant (if required by the company's constitution or for a future credit facility). All cables should be pre-advised to the bank via the relationship manager to avoid freezing. If the capital arrives in a foreign currency, the bank's spot FX execution desk will convert at the prevailing mid-market rate plus a markup; no exchange control approval is required. For payments from related parties, a transfer pricing policy should be on file. The bank may require an annual certificate of solvency from the directors for the company's own corporate compliance.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: The viability of offshore fintech EMIs (electronic money institutions) for a New Zealand-registered company is Low-to-Medium for primary banking and Medium for supplementary payment routing. New Zealand does not issue EMI licences per se; any entity providing payment services must be registered as a 'payments provider' under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and must also comply with the AML/CFT Act by becoming a reporting entity. Major international EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Currenxie do, however, service New Zealand businesses via their licensing arrangements in other jurisdictions (e.g., UK FCA or Australian AUSTRAC/DCE), but they are generally not permitted to offer a full suite of regulated clearing and deposit services in New Zealand without a local banking relationship. For an e-commerce auto parts retailer, using offshore EMIs as primary settlement rails is legally risky because those EMIs often route NZD transactions through local partner banks, and New Zealand's own prudential regulation requires a New Zealand-based banking licence for deposit-taking. A practical structure is to maintain a Tier-1 NZD operational account with a local bank for statutory capital and tax obligations, while using a well-regulated offshore EMI (like Wise or Airwallex) strictly for cross-border vendor disbursements, FX conversion, and multi-currency receiving in USD/EUR/AUD. The bank's compliance department will require full transparency on any EMI account used, as correspondent banks treat flows from unknown third-tier EMIs as higher-risk.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 1,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions per income year triggers a mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) under IRD Transfer Pricing Guidelines (INV-278) and s GC 6-14 of the Income Tax Act 2007. Below this threshold, a simplified written commercial rationale must still be retained.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a dedicated free-zone economic substance regime, but the Income Tax Act 2007 requires that a company have real economic substance—employees, premises, assets, and risk control—to be considered the beneficial owner and to satisfy the arm's length standard. Companies engaged in pure e-commerce of aftermarket automobile parts must demonstrate that key entrepreneurial and management functions (pricing, brand, inventory risk, supplier negotiation) occur in New Zealand. If the NZ Ltd acts merely as a commissionaire or agent of a related foreign vendor, IRD may deny the profit allocation and attribute sales revenue to the foreign entity, and the taxpayer faces the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under s BG 1 and the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises and Tax Administrations. Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as known in some offshore centres are not a statutory requirement in NZ, but the 'substance over form' doctrine applies through anti-avoidance cases.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Section 141B of the Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes a 150% shortfall penalty on the tax evaded. Section 143A makes evasion a criminal offence punishable by a fine of up to NZD 250,000 and imprisonment of up to five years. In addition, under s 141A, an unarguable tax position can trigger a 40% slap penalty. The company, as a legal person, is liable for a fine of up to NZD 250,000, and if profits have been shifted abroad, a further reassessment under the transfer pricing rules, with gross interest on outstanding tax at 8.54% per annum.

Director personal liability: The directors are personally jointly and severally liable for the full tax shortfall, shortfall penalties, and interest under s 81B TAA 1994. The court may pierce the corporate veil under the Companies Act 1993 and s 364, ordering the personal bank accounts and house assets to be attached. A civil asset forfeiture action can freeze and confiscate property obtained through the crime. English/Common law principles permit a travel ban (blacklisting) preventing the director from leaving New Zealand; criminal imprisonment of up to five years is the maximum, and a director can be personally disqualified from holding a management role in any New Zealand company for five years.
Shell Company Abuse / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The DTA benefit is denied under the Principal Purpose Test (OECD MLI Art. 7) and under the domestic General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s BG 1 and s GA 1). The entire arrangement is void against IRD; the Jessup/Shadow corporation is reconstructed as a resident company of New Zealand, and the full 30% NRWT is assessed on the gross distributions. Section 141D imposes a 100% abusive tax position penalty. If the shell is later liquidated, the Commissioner can issue a personal liability notice to the directors for the outstanding company tax under the tax recovery rules, and may initiate a criminal fraud prosecution under s 143D.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly established and transacted through a shell entity to obtain treaty benefits are personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. Civil remedies include an order for restitution of the diverted profits, a property freezing order, and a travel ban (blacklisting) preventing departure from New Zealand. Criminal liability extends to a term of imprisonment of up to five years for evading NRWT and up to seven years for dishonest use of a document under the Crimes Act 1961. The High Court can order the directors' personal superannuation, shares, and vehicles to be sold to satisfy the tax debt.
AML/CTF Failure and Compliance Fraud

Statutory sanction: Section 90 of the AML/CFT Act 2009 imposes a civil penalty of up to NZD 2 million for each serious breach, and an additional NZD 1 million per day for continuing non-compliance. The company also breaches s 102 by carrying on a financial services business without being an AML reporting institution. Inland Revenue can impose a 150% penalty for unreported overseas income, and the police can apply for an asset forfeiture order under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009 covering the entire proceeds of the business.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal civil liability for up to NZD 2 million in AML penalties and are jointly liable for the company's unpaid taxes. The court may pierce the corporate veil to impose a personal repayment order for the laundered amount, impose a travel ban (blacklisting), and order director disqualification. Criminal liability includes imprisonment for up to two years under s 102 of the AML/CFT Act and up to seven years for money laundering under the Crimes Act 1961. The commissioner can also invoke the personal liability regime for unpaid GST and PAYE to recover directly from the directors' personal assets.
