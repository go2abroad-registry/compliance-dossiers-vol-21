Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue Te Tari Taake), the national tax authority of New Zealand; electronic filing and registration portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz); corporate registry: New Zealand Companies Office (https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A New Zealand limited company is incorporated through the Companies Office, which issues the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN); the IRD automatically allocates a company IRD number upon registration and sends the confirmation to the registered office. A non-resident company (including a foreign parent of NZ Ltd) must submit Form IR595 (Non-Resident Company IRD Number Application) with certified proof of incorporation, director and ultimate beneficial ownership details, and New Zealand contact particulars. GST registration is effected through myIR and must be completed within 21 days of the registered person becoming liable; non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand consumers may use the simplified myIR GST registration channel once the NZD 60,000 threshold is exceeded.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (a standard NZ Ltd is a resident company taxed on worldwide income at the 28% corporate rate; New Zealand has no tax-exempt zones and no general territorial exemption; pass-through treatment is available only by elective Look-Through Company (LTC) status, not by default) — A New Zealand limited company is incorporated through the Companies Office, which issues the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN); the IRD automatically allocates a company IRD number upon registration and sends the confirmation to the registered office. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a standard NZ Ltd is a resident company taxed on worldwide income at the 28% corporate rate; New Zealand has no tax-exempt zones and no general territorial exemption; pass-through treatment is available only by elective Look-Through Company (LTC) status, not by default)

Available election forms

Look-Through Company (LTC) election under Part HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 (available to companies with no more than five shareholders, all of whom are individuals, LTCs, or eligible trustees; income and losses flow through to owners); consolidated group and loss-offset elections under the Income Tax Act 2007; imputation group election for franking of dividends; Qualifying Company election (closed to new companies since 2011, grandfathered for existing electors); Australian resident company election to exclude CFC attribution; hybrid mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 apply automatically without elective check-the-box status, consistent with New Zealand's absence of a US-style check-the-box regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; compulsory registration once the threshold is met or expected to be met; voluntary registration permitted below the threshold; non-resident suppliers of remote services must register if supplies to New Zealand resident consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11 of the GST Act when supplied by a registered person to a non-resident for direct export or for removal from New Zealand, provided the goods are physically exported by the supplier within 28 days of the time of supply (extended to 365 days where goods are delivered to an approved storage facility for a non-resident). Services are zero-rated under section 11A when supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed and the services are not supplied directly in connection with land or goods located in New Zealand. The consumer-panel survey SaaS platform (market research subscriptions, survey design, data analytics, and panel management delivered online) qualifies for zero-rating on supplies to offshore non-resident clients as remote services; the supplier must retain evidence of export and of the recipient's non-resident status.

  • Customs export declaration lodged through the New Zealand Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal of goods from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Carrier or freight-forwarder certificate of export
  • Foreign buyer contract and purchase order evidencing a supply to a non-resident
  • Evidence of non-resident status for zero-rated cross-border services (overseas registered address, foreign business registration, IP address and telecom geolocation records)
  • Foreign currency payment settlement records (e.g., PayPal Mass Payout, Wise, or SWIFT receipts) evidencing inbound proceeds

New Zealand does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, IRD digital stamping, or mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime; participation in the voluntary PEPPOL e-invoicing network has been available since October 2023. GST registration, filing, and payments are administered through myIR on monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly cycles. GST tax invoices must contain prescribed particulars and be issued within 28 days of the time of supply; no pre-clearance by the tax authority is required.

The IRD maintains a focused compliance programme on zero-rating substantiation; where export conditions (including the 28-day export rule or the non-resident-recipient rule) are not met or cannot be evidenced, the supply is recharacterised as taxable in New Zealand, triggering a GST reassessment, use-of-money interest, and shortfall penalties: 20% for failure to take reasonable care or for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% where an unacceptable tax position is taken with a dominant purpose of avoiding tax, and 150% for evasion. Criminal liability may arise under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the Crimes Act 1961 for deliberate falsification. The standard four-year amendment period is extended to eight years in cases of evasion. New Zealand imposes no explicit statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is the recommended contractual standard for substantiating real offshore sales and as a compliance proxy consistent with the IRD's time-of-supply rules and anti-avoidance scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has full convertibility on both current and capital account. The New Zealand dollar is freely tradable for residents and non-residents, and there are no foreign exchange surrender requirements, no conversion quotas, and no separate capital controls on direct investment, loan repatriation, dividends, or commercial service payments. Profit remittance is unrestricted under foreign exchange law; the only substantive hurdles are company law solvency requirements for distributions, domestic tax withholding on certain payments, and AML/CFT source-of-funds documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. An entity may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency or convert to New Zealand dollars at its discretion. Export receipts may be held in Foreign Currency Accounts offered by New Zealand registered banks, and such balances are freely remittable for trade settlement or future operating expenses, subject only to standard tax record-keeping obligations.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand, with the Financial Markets Authority exercising secondary market conduct oversight; New Zealand maintains a fully liberalized no-exchange-control regime and does not require central bank approval for current or capital account transaction execution.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise and Airwallex are viable for ancillary payout functions if the provider holds an appropriate New Zealand Financial Service Provider registration and money remittance authorization. A pure offshore EMI without New Zealand registration cannot operate as a primary local settlement or deposit-taking banking rail. Legal constraint: New Zealand AML/CFT Act 2009 requires any money remitter servicing the New Zealand market to be supervised by a designated AML/CFT supervisor. Accordingly, the primary treasury account structure should remain with a Tier 1 New Zealand licensed bank, while fintech rails may be used solely for low-value consumer panel payout disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital or shareholder loan injections must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 or equivalent high-value interbank wire to the New Zealand operating account. 28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 5,000,000 in aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions; a comprehensive Master File and Local File are administratively expected where cross-border connected transactions exceed this threshold or the multinational group has annual global revenue of at least NZD 50,000,000 and significant New Zealand connected-party activity.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have standalone Economic Substance Regulations; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in section BG1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and associated business-purpose doctrines require real economic substance, including local board decision-making, physical governance, employees and operational capability in New Zealand; a mere registered shell company used to access treaty benefits or shift profits will be recharacterised and exposed to shortfall penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994, with imprisonment of up to five years and corporate fines of up to NZD 500,000; civil shortfall penalties up to 200% of the evaded tax; use-of-money interest on all unpaid amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed or knowingly facilitated the evasion are personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; courts may impose travel bans, asset restraint orders, disqualification from managing companies and imprisonment upon conviction.
Shell Company / Artificial Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Application of the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to cancel the tax advantage; recharacterisation of transactions; shortfall penalties of up to 100% of the resulting tax shortfall for an abusive tax position; disclosure of an unacceptable tax position under the mandatory disclosure regime.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for shortfall penalties if they signed or participated in the avoidance arrangement; continued deliberate conduct can lead to criminal tax evasion charges, travel restrictions and personal restitution orders.
AML / Counter-Financing of Terrorism Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Corporate fines of up to NZD 2 million per breach; public warnings and infringement notices; suspension or restriction of payment processing; criminal liability for serious or repeat non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who knowingly allow an AML/CFT breach are personally liable to prosecution with imprisonment of up to two years, disqualification as directors, and confiscation orders; travel bans may be imposed during active criminal proceedings.
Withholding Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: Immediate assessment of unpaid NRWT; late payment penalties of up to 1% plus 4% and 1% per month; shortfall penalties of up to 200% where the failure is intentional; imprisonment of up to five years for evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unremitted withholding tax under statutory director liability rules; they may face personal recovery actions, prohibition from leaving New Zealand while tax debts remain, and criminal sanctions if the failure is wilful.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's-length amount; secondary adjustment and recharacterisation; shortfall penalties of 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for an unacceptable tax position, or 100%-200% where abusive or evasive; use-of-money interest on all shortfalls.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for shortfall penalties where they cause or assent to the incorrect pricing; in aggravated cases, criminal prosecution, travel bans and imprisonment may apply.
