Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. New Zealand law does not prescribe a minimum paid-up capital for a company limited by shares. Shares may be issued for nil paid consideration, and no escrow block is mandated. The Board should, however, ensure the company is solvent and maintain adequate working capital for creditor protection.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand, Te Tari Tāke; all electronic tax registration, filing, and approval is administered through the myIR Secure Online Services portal (https://www.ird.govt.nz); entity incorporation and Business Number issuance is administered by the New Zealand Companies Office, and physical export declarations are lodged with the New Zealand Customs Service via the Trade Single Window (TSW).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the entity as a New Zealand limited company under the Companies Act 1993 through the New Zealand Companies Office online Business Register; incorporation automatically issues the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). For a foreign non-resident entity, register the foreign company as an overseas company on the same register and obtain its NZBN. Step 2: Apply to the Inland Revenue Department for an IRD number, using the myIR portal for the entity (or the approved paper-channel application for non-resident companies), and submit the certificate of incorporation, NZBN, registered office location, director identities, ultimate shareholder ownership, and intended business activities (ISIC 5610, specialty fresh bakery franchise and central kitchen operations). Step 3: Determine GST nexus; if the entity is carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand and its actual or expected taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, or if it supplies remote services or low-value imported goods to New Zealand consumers above that threshold, complete the IRD GST registration through myIR using the same IRD number, which is also the GST registration number. Step 4: If the company has employees in New Zealand, separately register for PAYE withholding through myIR. Step 5: Upon approval, use the IRD number/GST number and NZBN on all tax invoices and retain all supporting registration documentation for the statutory seven-year retention period under the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A New Zealand company is taxed at entity level by default and may elect look-through company (LTC) treatment under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 if all shareholders are qualifying persons, making the company's income and losses transparent for income tax; no United States-style check-the-box election exists, and New Zealand's hybrid mismatch and base erosion rules in the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 continue to apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period for taxable supplies supplied in the course of a taxable activity in New Zealand; non-resident suppliers of remote/digital services and low-value imported goods to New Zealand consumers are also required to register once their total relevant supplies to New Zealand exceed NZD 60,000, or if it is reasonably foreseeable that they will exceed that threshold in the next 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, principally section 11 for goods and section 11A for services. Tangible goods are zero-rated where they are physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier or by an agent/carrier on the supplier's behalf, in connection with a sale or other supply carried out by a registered person in the course of a taxable activity. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed and the supply is not one of the statutory exceptions for services directly connected with land or mobile property in New Zealand or services physically rendered in New Zealand. For exported digital/offshore services, the supplier must hold evidence of the recipient's non-resident status, including business registration records, billing address, IP address geolocation data, or payment account country codes.

  • Export entry / export declaration lodged with New Zealand Customs through the Trade Single Window (TSW) for physical exports
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, or Air Waybill evidencing shipment and export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice / GST tax invoice issued by the supplier
  • Packing list / cargo manifest
  • Customs release or port/terminal evidence confirming removal of goods from New Zealand
  • Bank credit advice, SWIFT MT103, or account statement evidence demonstrating receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds

New Zealand does not impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime for GST invoices. E-invoicing is voluntary and is implemented through the New Zealand e-Invoicing Service Specification using the Peppol framework. GST-registered suppliers must issue a valid tax invoice for taxable supplies exceeding NZD 50 (including GST) containing the supplier's name, GST registration number, invoice date, description, quantity, GST-exclusive amount, GST amount, and GST-inclusive amount; invoices can be issued on paper or electronically and must be retained for seven years. GST returns must be filed electronically through the IRD myIR portal on the applicable filing frequency, with no government API pre-clearance of individual sales invoices.

Where a zero-rating claim is not substantiated by contemporaneous export and exchange-control documentation, the Inland Revenue Department may reassess the supply at the standard 15% GST rate, impose use-of-money interest from the original due date, and apply statutory shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall for abusive tax position, gross carelessness, or evasion, as well as prosecution for knowingly providing false or misleading export evidence. The IRD will reopen zero-rating if goods are returned to New Zealand or diverted to domestic use, if the export proceeds are not received and repatriated through banking channels within a commercially consistent period (with 180 days applied as the evidentiary benchmark), or if the purchaser's non-resident status is shown to be false. Exporters must retain the full documentary bundle and foreign exchange records for at least seven years; fraudulent GST refund claims trigger immediate refund clawback with penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates under a fully liberalised and open capital account framework with no general foreign exchange control legislation. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand as the central bank does not impose exchange controls on current account transactions, capital account transactions, dividend remittances, royalty payments, or repatriation of capital. The New Zealand dollar is fully convertible for both residents and non-residents, and there are no restrictions on the holding of foreign currency accounts by locally incorporated companies. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are permissible, subject to standard tax withholding obligations under the Non-Resident Withholding Tax regime and the normal AML/CFT documentation requirements of the paying bank. New Zealand does not require foreign exchange receipts from export transactions to be surrendered, converted, or repatriated within any statutory timeline; exporters may lawfully maintain foreign currency proceeds in external accounts. The practical compliance burden is limited to satisfying the anti-money laundering and countering financing of terrorism obligations imposed on New Zealand financial institutions, including full disclosure of beneficial ownership, source of funds, and economic purpose. For a Chinese pastry chain master franchise, inbound subscription funds and ongoing franchise royalty flows can be routed freely through the banking system, and there is no official barrier to using third-party payment aggregators for point-of-sale collections, although serious treasury operations should use registered bank settlement accounts for reconciliation and capital preservation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement in New Zealand. No mandatory conversion ratio applies, and there is no prescribed deadline for repatriating foreign currency export earnings. Export receipts may be retained in designated foreign currency accounts with registered banks or may be converted to New Zealand dollars at commercial market rates. The only administrative expectation is indirect statistical reporting through the banking system for balance of payments purposes, and the requirement under the AML/CFT Act to maintain complete records of the underlying commercial transactions.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

New Zealand does not maintain a distinct e-money institution licensing regime comparable to the European Union, and offshore fintech EMIs are not recognised as registered banks for the purpose of holding safeguarded enterprise deposits. While a non-bank electronic money provider may lawfully offer payment services if it is registered on the New Zealand Dispute Resolution and AML/CFT supervision frameworks, the practical viability for cross-border treasury is low. Such entities cannot provide locally protected transaction banking, do not integrate directly with the New Zealand Clearing System, and are not appropriate for capital escrow, franchise royalty collection, or central kitchen disbursement flows. A compliant treasury blueprint using major Tier-1 domestic banks is strongly preferred, and EMIs should be used only as ancillary payment channels for small-value consumer POS collections if and only if they maintain segregated trust accounts and are supervised by an equivalent foreign regulator.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends is 30% gross; fully imputed dividends may be subject to dividend withholding tax (DWT) at 28%. Under the NZ–China DTA, dividends qualify for a 5% rate if the beneficial owner holds at least 25% of the capital, otherwise 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) is required when cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZ$1 million in aggregate for goods or NZ$500,000 for services, intangibles, and interest. Additional OECD Piller Two filing obligations apply to multinational groups with revenue ≥ EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: No specific economic substance regulations (ESR) as in offshore jurisdictions; however, section GB 51 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) apply. DTA benefits require the taxpayer to satisfy the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) and demonstrate commercial substance: a fixed place of business, local employees, and substantive management and control in New Zealand for residency.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to NZ$250,000 for a company; additional penalty of 100% of the tax shortfall (increased from 20% base penalty); criminal prosecution and imprisonment up to five years for individuals involved.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the 100% shortfall penalty and face criminal charges; the court may order compensation, issue travel bans, and impose a term of imprisonment up to five years.
Shell Company / Conduit Abuse

Statutory sanction: Cancellation of tax relief, reassessment as a non-resident, severe penalty of 100% of the resulting tax shortfall, and refusal of DTA benefits under the Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false declarations can be jointly and severally liable for the penalty amount and prohibited from managing a company for up to five years.
AML / Counter-Financing of Terrorism

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a body corporate is liable for a fine up to NZ$2 million; individual officers up to NZ$300,000; additional civil penalty orders may be applied.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers with knowledge or control of the failure are personally liable for the penalty; can be banned from serving as directors for up to 10 years and face imprisonment for up to two years for reckless non-compliance.
Repeat Late Filing / Non-Payment

Statutory sanction: Distress warrant authorizing confiscation of company assets, registration of tax debt as a charge on real property, and daily penalty of NZ$250 per day for every day the return is outstanding (up to NZ$10,000).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly caused the failure to withhold or remit tax can be made personally liable for the company's unpaid tax under section 46 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, with civil restitution and possible criminal prosecution.
