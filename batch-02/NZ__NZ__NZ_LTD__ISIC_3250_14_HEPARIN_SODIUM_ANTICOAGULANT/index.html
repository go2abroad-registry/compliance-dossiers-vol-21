Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered office must be a physical address in New Zealand under section 13 of the Companies Act 1993 and cannot be a post office box. A virtual office or commercial mailbox may satisfy the statutory registered office formality in low-risk cases, but for this ISIC 3250 active pharmaceutical extraction and heparin sodium anticoagulant business, the Inland Revenue Department and New Zealand banks will reject an arrangement that lacks real commercial substance. Tax authorities will question New Zealand tax residence and economic substance if there is no physical premises, responsible staff, plant or contract manufacturing oversight, quality control records, raw material inventory, or board decision-making in New Zealand. Banks will not accept such a shell arrangement for a medium-risk enhanced due diligence pharma exporter using letters of credit and SWIFT wires. Therefore the entity should secure a leased physical office, licensed manufacturing or contractual GMP facility oversight, local management, and a compliant registered office with a registered agent.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake), Wellington, New Zealand; primary online portal: myIR at myIR.ird.govt.nz; corporate registry interface: New Zealand Companies Office and New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) Register at companiesoffice.govt.nz.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned New Zealand limited company must first be incorporated with the NZ Companies Office to obtain a company registration number and an NZBN; immediately after incorporation, the company must register with the Inland Revenue Department for an IRD number (the statutory tax file number) using the IRD's online myIR registration or paper forms. Where the company is not otherwise required to file an income tax return, a non-resident company registration form (IR596) must be submitted to obtain an IRD number. No minimum capital contribution is required for incorporation. The IRD number is issued free of charge and without a statutory fee, and registration is completed in parallel with GST, PAYE (if employer), and other tax registrations as applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (New Zealand-resident limited company subject to corporate income tax at the flat 28% rate on worldwide income; non-resident company subject to tax at 28% on New Zealand-sourced income only; no automatic pass-through treatment for a standard Ltd, with the Look-Through Company election available only as a deliberate statutory option).

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. An eligible closely held New Zealand company (five or fewer underlying shareholders, all of whom are individuals or qualifying LTC-holding entities) may irrevocably elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, thereby achieving fiscal transparency of the company's income, losses, and credits. Hybrid mismatch rules under Subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 (implementing BEPS Action 2) limit deductions for hybrid financial instruments, hybrid entities, and reverse hybrid structures. In the GST context, a company may elect to register voluntarily below the NZD 60,000 mandatory threshold and may choose a monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly GST filing cycle consistent with its turnover profile.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; a person who exceeds this threshold must register for GST, while voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold and remains binding for at least two years.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, the supply of goods is zero-rated where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply, the supply is to a person who is not resident in New Zealand (or the goods are exported pursuant to a contract of sale to a non-resident), and the supplier obtains and retains the prescribed export evidence. For services, zero-rating under section 11A applies where the services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are supplied in connection with a business carried on outside New Zealand; services physically performed in New Zealand that directly relate to New Zealand land, or to goods in New Zealand, are excluded from zero-rating. Heparin sodium manufactured under ISIC 3250 and sold to an overseas buyer qualifies for 0% GST on export, provided the export documentary trail is complete.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic CUSR/New Zealand Customs Service export entry evidencing that the heparin sodium goods physically departed New Zealand within the statutory time frame)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing shipment from New Zealand to the foreign consignee on a named carrier and voyage/flight
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Currency Purchase/Sale record issued by a New Zealand registered bank evidencing settlement of the export sale proceeds

New Zealand does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or a continuous transaction control (CTC) model. Electronic invoicing is voluntary but supported through the New Zealand Peppol network, enabling standardized e-invoice exchange. GST returns (GST101A/GST101B) must be filed electronically through the myIR portal, and all tax records must be retained for seven years. There is no API-based pre-clearance requirement for invoices; however, exported goods must be supported by contemporaneous customs and shipping records acceptable to the IRD.

Zero-rating is retrospectively clawed back under section 11(3) of the GST Act 1985 where the goods are not exported within 28 days of the time of supply, or are subsequently diverted to local use or re-imported into New Zealand; the supplier must return the output GST originally zero-rated. Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, shortfall penalties escalate from 20% for lack of reasonable care, to 40% for gross carelessness, up to 150% for evasion, and are compounded by use-of-money interest computed under Part 7 of the same Act. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims may trigger criminal liability under sections 143 and 144 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, attracting fines of up to NZD 50,000 for a company (and NZD 25,000 for an individual), imprisonment for up to five years in the case of evasion, and concurrent charges for directors and officers. False customs declarations are additionally sanctionable under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, with forfeiture of goods and monetary penalties. IRD audit practice for zero-rated export claims requires strict documentary proof of physical export, foreign consignee identity, and remittance of proceeds; failure to substantiate results in reassessment, clawback of GST, shortfall penalties, and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions, reflecting New Zealand's standing as an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction. All exchange controls were dismantled in 1984 and the surviving Exchange Control Act was repealed. Profit remittance (dividends, royalties, interest, management fees) is freely permitted without central-bank approval, subject only to: (i) the solvency test in section 52 of the Companies Act 1993; (ii) non-resident withholding tax on the gross remittance; and (iii) the foreign-investment screening thresholds of the Overseas Investment Act 2005 where the remittance relates to the acquisition of a substantial New Zealand business or sensitive land. There are no restrictions on maintaining offshore bank accounts, no surrender requirements, and no balance-of-payments justification is required for a capital outflow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender obligation on export proceeds. An exporter of heparin sodium API may lawfully be paid into an offshore account, may retain settlement funds in foreign currency indefinitely, and may convert into NZD at any date and at any rate freely negotiated with an authorised bank. There is no statutory timeline for bringing export receipts onshore and no mandatory conversion ratio. The only compliance requirement is documentary: the receiving bank must be able to demonstrate the commercial underlying for each inward credit under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act), so the exporter should retain for each receipt the sales invoice, bill of lading, certificate of analysis and underlying purchase order.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the sole central bank and prudential regulator of the New Zealand banking system. New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised foreign-exchange regime; there is no separate Foreign Exchange Administration authority and no exchange-control approval body. The Overseas Investment Office (OIO) administers inbound foreign-investment screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005, but the Reserve Bank exercises no exchange-rate or capital-repatriation control.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium (viable as a supplementary payment rail; not viable as a primary banking relationship). Offshore digital EMI and fintech payment providers (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, OFX) may operate in New Zealand for payment processing, but they are not licensed banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 and cannot issue documentary letters of credit, provide cross-border trade finance, or open a true settlement account in the local clearing system. To act as a money changer or value-transfer service, an offshore EMI must register with the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) under the AML/CFT Act, appoint a local agent with substance in New Zealand, and comply with the New Zealand remittance reporting rules. For a pharmaceutical raw-material exporter that must settle transactions via commercial LC and SWIFT TT, a licensed commercial bank remains mandatory; the fintech EMI can be used only for smaller operational disbursements (freight, insurance, minor consultancy) where speed and lower fees outweigh the absence of documentary credit capability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing for an inbound capital injection into a New Zealand limited company is: (1) a SWIFT MT103 credit transfer from the offshore parent to the subsidiary's NZD operational account at an authorised New Zealand bank; (2) the remittance advice must reference the recognised treasury purpose code — PRIN (principal/equity contribution) or GINT (inter-company loan); (3) the receiving bank completes the credit without central-bank approval, as no exchange-control permission exists; (4) where the injected capital is applied to acquire a New Zealand business with total assets exceeding NZ$100 million or sensitive land, the transaction must first obtain Overseas Investment Office (OIO) consent under the Overseas Investment Act 2005; and (5) the treasury function must contemporaneously evidence the injection with a board resolution, a solvency certificate issued under section 52 of the Companies Act 1993, and an investor declaration for tax purposes. If the injection is structured as shareholder debt, a formal loan agreement must be executed and the interest rate must satisfy the New Zealand transfer-pricing and thin-capitalisation benchmarks under the Income Tax Act 2007.For trade settlement concerning heparin sodium API exports and payment of offshore crude-suppliers, the primary clearing rail is an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (LC) arranged through the bank's trade desk, issued as a SWIFT MT700 and, where the supplier demands a guarantee, confirmed by a first-class overseas correspondent bank. For amounts below the LC ticket threshold, the fallback rail is a direct SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer bearing the appropriate trade purpose code (e.g., GOOD for physical goods), accompanied by the full invoice description; this is particularly apt for freight, insurance and handling payments. The bank will require the company to present the standard trade document package for each disbursement — commercial invoice, certificate of analysis from the heparin fractionation plant, bill of lading or air waybill, insurance certificate, and any pharmaceutical export licence issued by the New Zealand Ministry of Health — and will operate a dedicated documentary-collection desk for delayed-settlement open-account flows. All outbound wires must pass the bank's sanctions filters and a transaction-monitoring ledger maintained under the AML/CFT Act.Outright deliverable FX forward (NZD/USD and NZD/EUR) — the standard permitted product under New Zealand's full-convertibility regime; executed under an ISDA Master Agreement or a bank proprietary terms sheet.Cross-currency swap (CCS) between USD and NZD for hedging the long-term capital structure, executed with an authorised registered bank and subject to applicable trade-reporting obligations under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013.FX options and zero-cost collar structures on NZD/USD, permissible as over-the-counter derivatives and documented under the Reserve Bank-approved bank framework.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) for hedging mismatched settlements in emerging-market currencies used to purchase heparin crude from a third-country supplier — permissible in New Zealand, although the NZD itself is freely deliverable and therefore NDFs are used only for the pass-through currency leg.Foreign-currency swap deposits (short-term currency swaps) at an authorised bank for converting surplus USD export proceeds into NZD operational liquidity without committing group cash to the open FX market.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium (viable as a supplementary payment rail; not viable as a primary banking relationship). Offshore digital EMI and fintech payment providers (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, OFX) may operate in New Zealand for payment processing, but they are not licensed banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 and cannot issue documentary letters of credit, provide cross-border trade finance, or open a true settlement account in the local clearing system. To act as a money changer or value-transfer service, an offshore EMI must register with the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) under the AML/CFT Act, appoint a local agent with substance in New Zealand, and comply with the New Zealand remittance reporting rules. For a pharmaceutical raw-material exporter that must settle transactions via commercial LC and SWIFT TT, a licensed commercial bank remains mandatory; the fintech EMI can be used only for smaller operational disbursements (freight, insurance, minor consultancy) where speed and lower fees outweigh the absence of documentary credit capability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is expected where aggregate related-party cross-border transactions exceed NZD 10 million. Country-by-Country Reporting and the OECD Master File obligation apply to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of NZD 1.3 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. Substance is assessed through the general anti-avoidance rule, judicial anti-avoidance doctrines, PE attribution rules, and treaty anti-abuse limitations. The active fractionation of low-molecular-weight heparin crude requires genuine New Zealand staff, plant, procurement, quality control, and risk management; a paper-only entity or contract-level arrangement without operational substance will be re-characterised and denied treaty or loss benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Intentional Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under section 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994, a company convicted of tax evasion is liable to a fine of up to NZ$500,000; an individual is liable to a fine of up to NZ$50,000 or imprisonment for up to 5 years or both. The civil shortfall penalty for evasion is 100% of the tax shortfall, plus use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise or sign false returns may be prosecuted as principals or parties to the offence. Courts can order restitution, impose director disqualification, and impose travel bans or surrender-of-passport conditions in serious tax fraud proceedings.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 and empowering provisions of the Tax Administration Act 1994 permit Inland Revenue to reconstruct the arrangement and assess the true profit-maker. Shortfall penalties up to 100% may be imposed. Under section 318 of the Companies Act 1993, a company that is not carrying on business may be removed from the New Zealand register.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability for reckless trading under section 135 of the Companies Act 1993 where the company continues to incur obligations with no genuine business. Disqualification orders under section 383 and personal recovery orders may follow where the company is a bare conduit for tax avoidance.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Non-Arm's-Length Cross-Border Transactions

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may re-compute taxable income under New Zealand transfer-pricing rules (subpart GC of the Income Tax Act 2007) and the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. Civil shortfall penalties of 20% for lack of reasonable care, 50% for an abusive tax position, and 100% for evasion may apply, together with use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause to be prepared false or incomplete transfer-pricing documentation can be personally liable for shortfall penalties and, in egregious cases, prosecuted for evasion. If the company becomes insolvent as a result of excessive fees, directors may be liable for insolvent trading.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) Defaults

Statutory sanction: The payer remains liable for the unpaid NRWT. Late payment penalties of 1% initial, 4% after 7 days, and 1% monthly thereafter apply, plus use-of-money interest. Shortfall penalties may also be assessed if the failure results from a dominant purpose of defeating the application of the withholding regime.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow the company to omit NRWT may be personally assessed for the withheld amount and may face criminal penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994, including fines and imprisonment for serious conduct. Travel restrictions may be imposed as bail conditions or on criminal conviction.
AML/CFT and Unlicensed Pharmaceutical Trading

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed pharmaceutical manufacturing and distribution carry fines and imprisonment under the Medicines Act 1981. Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, reporting entities that breach customer due diligence and record-keeping obligations are liable to civil penalties of up to NZ$2 million per contravention. Money laundering offences under the Crimes Act 1961 carry imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate reporting entity who knowingly permit AML/CFT breaches, or who participate in the unlicensed manufacture or distribution of pharmaceutical products, are personally exposed to criminal prosecution, personal fines, imprisonment, and disqualification from managing companies.
