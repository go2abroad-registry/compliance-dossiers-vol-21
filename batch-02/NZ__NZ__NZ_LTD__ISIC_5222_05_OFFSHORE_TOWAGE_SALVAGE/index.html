Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not per se prohibited for registration, but the registered office must be a physical location in New Zealand where the company's records are available for inspection. For this deep-sea towage and salvage entity, the IRD and the Companies Office will reject a bare post office box; a virtual suite with a physical street address, mail handling, and a resident agent may be accepted. However, maritime charterers, protection and indemnity clubs, insurers, and banks will typically demand real operational substance, a presence among the management team, and the ability to inspect the towage fleet. Therefore, a purely virtual office is assessed as high risk and likely to trigger enhanced CDD and physical inspection.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand has no minimum paid-up capital requirement under the Companies Act 1993. There is no blocked escrow account or capital-in-bank mandate. Shares may be issued as fully paid, partly paid, or nil paid as provided by the company's share allotment, and paid-up capital need not be deposited before incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) fire safety certification is required for any shore-side office, workshop, fuel storage or warehouse; this includes inspection of fire alarms, extinguishers, emergency egress and hazardous substance storage. WorkSafe New Zealand registration and clearance is required for workplace health and safety, hazardous substances locations, structural load assessment for mooring and quayside operations, and provision of a documented health and safety system. Vessel-side civil defence and fire protection must be evidenced by STCW Basic Firefighting certificates, vessel fire control plans, approved drill records, and solid emergency response coordination with the regional Civil Defence Emergency Management group. A Certificate of Survey issued by Maritime New Zealand or an authorised recognised organisation will confirm the vessel's structural readiness and compliance with New Zealand maritime safety standards.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue Te Tari Taake), Wellington, New Zealand; online filing and registration portal: myIR at www.ird.govt.nz. Export and customs declarations are made to New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS) through the Trade Single Window platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must first determine whether it is carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand or has any New Zealand-source income, GST, PAYE, or FBT obligations. The entity then registers for an Inland Revenue Department (IRD) number by submitting the prescribed non-resident company IRD number application through myIR, supported by a certified certificate of incorporation, registered office or principal place of business details, ultimate shareholders and directors information, and a statement of New Zealand business activities. Inland Revenue issues the IRD number, which also serves as the tax identification number; if the entity makes taxable supplies in the course of a taxable activity, it must separately activate GST registration in myIR using the same IRD number. Non-resident entities without a physical New Zealand presence must appoint and disclose a New Zealand agent for service of notices. If the entity exports physical goods from New Zealand, it must also obtain a Customs client code in the Trade Single Window and ensure that the registered customs broker or freight forwarder can lodge export entries on its behalf.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand does not have a US-style check-the-box election. A close-held New Zealand limited company may elect to be a Look-Through Company (LTC) under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, making income, deductions, losses, and tax credits of the company attributed to its shareholders. Eligible wholly-owned groups may elect consolidated income tax treatment under the consolidated groups provisions of the Income Tax Act 2007, and related GST-registered companies may elect GST group registration under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Hybrid and branch mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act 2007 operate automatically to deny or adjust tax benefits arising from hybrid instruments, hybrid entities, or imported mismatches, and no elective mechanism can override their application.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period is the compulsory GST registration threshold; non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value imported goods may have separate cross-border GST registration obligations; voluntary registration is available to a person carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods are zero-rated under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when the supplier exports the goods and the export is evidenced by timely removal from New Zealand. Supplies of services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are not supplied in connection with New Zealand land or certain personal property in New Zealand. For deep-sea towage and maritime emergency salvage on international voyages, zero-rating can also be supported by the place-of-supply treatment for international sea transport and by the service being supplied to a non-resident vessel owner, insurer, P&I club, or other eligible non-resident principal for use wholly outside New Zealand. The entity should obtain a signed contract, such as a towage or salvage agreement in Lloyd's Open Form or equivalent, the ultimate customer's non-resident status evidence, invoices, and payment records. New Zealand does not issue an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC); a bank inward remittance advice or SWIFT MT103 is the normal evidence of foreign exchange repatriation.

  • Electronic export entry lodged in the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window for physical goods exports
  • Bill of Lading or sea waybill evidencing removal of goods, vessels, or equipment from New Zealand to the foreign destination
  • Signed towage or salvage agreement (Lloyd's Open Form or equivalent) naming the non-resident principal and the operation
  • Commercial invoice and GST tax invoice issued to the non-resident customer
  • Bank inward remittance advice or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident customer
  • Vessel and equipment movement records, official logs, or incident and casualty reports supporting performance outside New Zealand territorial waters

New Zealand does not operate a mandatory real-time tax clearance, e-invoicing clearance, or anti-fraud digital fiscal stamping regime for GST. E-invoicing is voluntary and is transacted through the Peppol network. GST-registered persons must issue tax invoices containing the particulars prescribed in the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 within 28 days, retain sufficient business records to substantiate accounting treatments, and file GST returns electronically through myIR on a monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly basis depending on annual turnover and registration option. Inland Revenue provides APIs for integration with enterprise accounting systems, but such integration is not a statutory fiscal clearance requirement.

Inland Revenue can review the documentary chain underlying a zero-rated supply and, if the taxable supply is found not to have been exported or not supplied to an eligible non-resident, can reassess GST at 15% with use-of-money interest. Tax Administration Act 1994 shortfall penalties apply on the resulting GST shortfall: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for abusive tax positions, and up to 150% for evasion. The statutory time bar generally prevents reassessment after four years from the relevant assessment or return period, except in cases of fraud, evasion, or failure to file. New Zealand has no exchange-control law imposing a statutory 180-day inward remittance deadline; the 180-day figure is the internal documentation and repatriation benchmark used to demonstrate that the cross-border supply was genuinely made to, and paid for by, the non-resident customer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised capital and current account framework. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not impose exchange controls and has no authority to require approval, licensing, or prior notification of ordinary cross-border payments. As an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction, New Zealand permits unrestricted payments and transfers for current international transactions. Capital transactions, including equity injections, shareholder loans, and risk-based hedging, can be executed without forex surrender or conversion requirements. Exporters and service providers are free to invoice in foreign currency, retain collections offshore or onshore, and remit profits abroad after meeting corporate law and tax compliance. The only statutory friction arises from tax rules, non-resident withholding tax on dividends and interest, and AML/CFT customer due diligence implemented by banks. For an offshore towage and salvage company, salvage proceeds, towage fees, and security or award payments received in USD or other currencies are freely convertible to NZD. The absence of surrender obligations, the right to hold multi-currency accounts, and unrestricted foreign-currency borrowing mean the legal and regulatory overlay is light. The practical liquidity challenge is dependency on correspondent bank compliance, sanctions screening, and the quality of evidence supporting the commercial purpose of large irregular salvage receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio exists under New Zealand law. Export receipts and salvage/towage invoices denominated in foreign currency may be retained indefinitely in a foreign-currency account at a New Zealand registered bank or in an approved offshore account without surrender to the Reserve Bank. Optional conversion to NZD is permitted at market rates.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs, including digital-only payment platforms and e-money issuers, have constrained viability for primary corporate treasury services in New Zealand. Under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989, only registered banks may undertake banking business, including accepting demand deposits; non-bank EMIs cannot offer current accounts or deposit-taking facilities that resemble bank accounts. Overseas EMIs holding neither a New Zealand banking licence nor a money remittance registration under the AML/CFT Act should not be used for enhanced-risk maritime entities exposed to salvage, towage, and high-value cross-border payments. Locally registered payment providers may execute money remittance and foreign-exchange conversion as ancillary rails, provided they are registered and supervised for AML/CFT. For settlement of Lloyds Standard Form wires, salvage awards, and standby credits, a full commercial banking relationship with a Tier-1 bank is mandatory. Therefore, use a fintech EMI only as a supplementary low-value payment tool, subject to bank approval and connectivity testing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For inbound equity or shareholder loan funds, instruct the remitting bank to send a SWIFT MT103 using a USD, EUR, or GBP correspondent clearable to the New Zealand beneficiary bank. In field 70, use a clear payment reference such as EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION FOR NZ_LTD or SHARE LOAN NZ_LTD, and where the reporting bank requires, an approved international purpose category such as BPM6 equity securities classification. Since New Zealand does not apply exchange-control permission, no pre-approval from the Reserve Bank is required for the capital import. The receiving bank will perform AML/CFT screening and may request the underlying corporate resolution, share allotment form, or loan agreement before the credit is provisionally accepted. For capital verification, the company must file a share allotment notice with the New Zealand Companies Register where shares are issued, and maintain a board minute stating the number of shares, issue price, and settlement date. The bank may also issue a capital verification letter or audit confirmation, not as a currency-control measure, but to evidence the commercial purpose.Use SWIFT MT103 direct wires for bunker suppliers, subcontract tugs, port-state fees, and salvage award disbursements. Therefore, use a fintech EMI only as a supplementary low-value payment tool, subject to bank approval and connectivity testing.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends paid by a New Zealand company to a non-resident shareholder is 30% under domestic law (Income Tax Act 2007, Part RE). Treaty-reduced rates apply under double tax agreements: generally 15% for portfolio shareholders, and 5% where the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 10% of the voting power in the New Zealand company (e.g., under the NZ-Australia and NZ-China DTAs). Imputation credits attached to the dividend do not reduce the NRWT base for non-residents; the company must withhold on the fully imputed dividend at the applicable rate and is separately liable for NRWT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$10 million: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) is mandatory under section 78C of the Tax Administration Act 1994 where cross-border related-party transactions in an income year exceed NZ$10 million. Below this threshold, Inland Revenue still expects the taxpayer to be able to demonstrate arm's-length pricing on request. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) applies to New Zealand-resident members of multinational groups with consolidated group revenue equal to or exceeding EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute of the type found in zero- or low-tax jurisdictions. Substance is instead enforced through: (1) company tax residence rules - a company is a New Zealand tax resident if incorporated in New Zealand or if its head office and management/control are exercised in New Zealand, regardless of place of incorporation; (2) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ss BG 1 and GA 1, Income Tax Act 2007), which permits Inland Revenue to void any arrangement entered into for the dominant purpose of tax avoidance and reconstruct the arrangement in accordance with its commercial substance; (3) the 49% shareholder continuity requirement for loss carryforward, which requires real continuity of economic ownership; and (4) the treaty 'beneficial ownership' and permanent establishment tests, which deny treaty benefits to shell or conduit entities. A non-resident-owned New Zealand Ltd in the offshore towage and salvage sector must demonstrate genuine commercial substance - physical vessel assets, employed crew, third-party charter contracts, and board decisions taken in New Zealand - to withstand an IRD reconstruction or a DTA claim.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under section 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994: a fine of up to NZ$50,000 for a company and/or imprisonment of responsible officers for up to 5 years. Civil shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall (100% for evasion, 150% for evasion with deliberate obstruction), plus use-of-money interest on the full shortfall from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors, chief financial officers, and other officers who knowingly participate in evasion face personal criminal prosecution and imprisonment. The corporate veil may be pierced where the company is used as a sham or fraud is established. Inland Revenue may issue a Departure Prohibition Order (DPO) under s 157E of the TAA preventing any director from leaving New Zealand while tax debts in excess of NZ$10,000 remain outstanding.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the transaction to the arm's-length price under ss GC 6-14 of the Income Tax Act 2007; shortfall penalties of 20% for lack of reasonable care, 100% for an avoidance arrangement, and 150% for evasion with obstruction; all adjustments carry UOMI. Inland Revenue may also impose record-keeping penalties for failure to produce a Master File/Local File or CbCR information.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false transfer pricing documentation may be prosecuted under the TAA as parties to evasion. Where related-party payments render the company insolvent, directors incur personal liability for reckless trading under s 135 and wrongful trading under s 136 of the Companies Act 1993, and may be disqualified from managing companies. A DPO may also be issued to block departure.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Voidance of the arrangement under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s BG 1/GA 1) with income attributed to the actual economic owner; denial of treaty benefits for lack of beneficial ownership; deregistration of the company under the Companies Act 1993; and enforcement action by Inland Revenue for unpaid tax. Breach of AML/CFT registration and reporting obligations can attract civil penalties of up to NZ$2 million per breach for a body corporate.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly operate a shell structure can be held personally liable for fraudulent trading under s 320 of the Companies Act 1993 and for the company's debts. Senior officers may face criminal prosecution under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, with imprisonment up to 2 years and fines up to NZ$300,000. A director may also be prohibited from managing a company for up to 10 years and may be subject to a Departure Prohibition Order.
Unpaid Withholding Taxes and Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: The payer company remains liable for the full amount of NRWT or PAYE that was required to be withheld and remitted, plus late payment penalties (1% initial, 4% after 7 days, 1% monthly thereafter) and UOMI. Shortfall penalties of up to 150% apply where the failure is deliberate; criminal prosecution under s 143B is available. Interest paid to related non-residents may be recharacterised as a dividend if the loan is not genuinely at arm's length.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for PAYE and withholding taxes deducted from employees but not remitted to Inland Revenue under the director liability provisions of the TAA. The Commissioner can recover unpaid amounts directly from directors' personal assets. Travel bans via Departure Prohibition Orders apply for any tax debt over NZ$10,000, including withholding tax liabilities.
AML/CFT Breaches in the Medium-Risk Enhanced Environment

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009: civil penalties of up to NZ$2 million for a body corporate for each breach; criminal penalties for directors and senior managers include imprisonment of up to 2 years and fines of up to NZ$300,000; AML/CFT supervision by the Department of Internal Affairs can suspend or revoke registration, and the company can be named publicly.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are personally liable where they knowingly or negligently permit AML/CFT failures, or where a breach was committed with their consent or connivance. They may be subject to criminal prosecution, civil penalty orders, and disqualification as directors. The corporate veil is not a defence to a prosecutor where the individual was the directing mind of the breach.
