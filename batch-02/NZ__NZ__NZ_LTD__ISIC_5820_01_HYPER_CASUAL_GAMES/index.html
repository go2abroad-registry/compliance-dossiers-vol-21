Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; online portal: myIR Secure Online Services (https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir). New Zealand Customs Service operates the border and export clearance platform.

E-government filing portal

For NZ_LTD, a New Zealand resident limited company: (1) incorporate under the Companies Act 1993 with the New Zealand Companies Office and obtain the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN); (2) apply for the IRD number through the IRD using the myIR company registration channel, or receive it automatically from Companies Office data sharing; the IRD number is the single national TIN and is also the GST registration number; (3) register for GST at the same time if taxable supplies are expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period, or voluntarily before starting operations; (4) if the company is established by foreign shareholders, provide the ultimate shareholder and beneficial owner details for AML/CFT customer due diligence. For a foreign non-resident entity not resident in New Zealand but deriving New Zealand-source income or supplying remote services to New Zealand consumers, the entity must separately apply for a non-resident IRD number via Inland Revenue's non-resident registration procedures in myIR, supplying its foreign registration certificate, constitution, director and tax-residency details, and an agent for service in New Zealand if required. The IRD number must be quoted on all GST returns, income tax returns, tax invoices, and export documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand has no US-style check-the-box election. The default tax classification for a New Zealand limited company is separate entity-level taxation at the 28% corporate income tax rate on worldwide income. An eligible close-held company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) treatment under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, causing income and deductions to flow through to shareholders; this election must be made to IRD within the statutory timeframe and imposes strict eligibility requirements. There is no elective hybrid-mismatch treatment; New Zealand hybrid mismatch rules apply mandatorily. Under GST, related resident companies may elect group registration under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, treating the group as a single registered person.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies supplied in any 12-month period in the course of a New Zealand taxable activity; registration becomes compulsory when the threshold is met or expected to be met. Non-resident suppliers of remote services are also required to register when their supplies to New Zealand resident consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in 12 months. Entities may voluntarily register below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days after the time of supply and retains evidence of export. Services are zero-rated under section 11(2) when supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, excluding services supplied directly in connection with land in New Zealand or with tangible personal property located in New Zealand and not exported. For the hyper-casual gaming business, advertising inventory supplied to non-resident ad networks such as the AdMob and AppLovin payout entities, and digital content services supplied to overseas platform customers, may qualify for zero-rating as services to non-residents; free-to-play user access itself is not a taxable supply. The supplier must hold contracts, ad-network settlement and payout statements, user and platform location evidence, and records demonstrating the non-resident status and offshore consumption of the service. For pure digital services, a New Zealand Customs export declaration and physical transport documents are inapplicable; the digital substantiation package is the contract, platform payout statements, and foreign exchange credit evidence.

New Zealand does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate. Electronic invoicing through the Peppol network is voluntary; businesses may also issue PDF or paper tax invoices. GST returns must be filed electronically via myIR at the registered filing frequency. Tax invoices must contain prescribed particulars under the GST Act, and there is no IRD API pre-approval or stamping requirement for invoices.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates under a fully floating exchange-rate regime, and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not administer exchange controls, bilateral payments agreements, or capital-repatriation licensing. The current account is fully open: dividends, interest, royalties, profits, and loan repayments may be freely converted to any foreign currency and remitted offshore without central-bank approval or any surrender condition. The capital account is equally open for standard equity and debt injections, and non-residents may hold 100% of a local limited company; the only constraints arise under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 for acquisitions of sensitive land or significant business assets above statutory thresholds, neither of which applies to a hyper-casual mobile-game publishing entity owning intellectual property. There is no statutory obligation to convert export proceeds generated by AdMob or AppLovin payouts into NZD, no holding period for foreign receipts, and no income tax or withholding surcharge on distribution beyond normal imputation and Non-resident Withholding Tax on outbound dividends. The practical profit-remittance hurdle is therefore limited to tax-clearance documentation: directors must file accurate GST and income-tax returns and issue a shareholder and tax reconciliation to support the economic substance of the gaming operation before a bank permits a large one-off dividend wire. Overall, New Zealand scores as a zero-exchange-control, fully convertible jurisdiction for a standard software and intellectual-property treasury.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. New Zealand does not impose export-proceeds surrender or conversion requirements. A resident or non-resident-owned NZ Ltd may retain 100% of all foreign-currency earnings in any permitted currency, onshore or offshore, and may repatriate the balance of an AdMob or AppLovin settlement account to its NZD clearing account at a time of its choosing. The only statutory timeline is the balance-of-payments and financial-reporting deadline: a company must ensure that the Reserve Bank statistical collection via its bank automatically reports any inbound settlement wire within the same business day; no manual conversion window exists.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Limited but usable as a settlement funnel only. A fintech EMI that is not a Registered Bank under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 cannot lawfully accept deposits or provide NZD clearing-house access; such firms operate under a money-remittance and service-provider regime with disclosure duties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. Practically, an overseas EMI may hold variable-balance USD wallets for ad-network payouts and issue virtual cards, but it cannot issue New Zealand bank account numbers, cannot be named as the company's statutory banker for GST/IRD reconciliation, and cannot process inward correspondent flows on the NZ Clearing System. For a compliant treasury, the EMI should be used only to collect AdMob/AppLovin USD settlements before sweeping to a primary domestic ADI; the primary legal banking relationship must sit with ANZ, BNZ, ASB, or Westpac.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share-capital or shareholder-loan injections from non-resident shareholders should be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from an account in the shareholder's name to the NZ Ltd's domestic ADI account. The bank must be instructed to allocate the funds to the company's registered share-allotment ledger rather than to ordinary trade receivables; the MT103 field 70 should include a purpose text such as 'EQUITY INJECTION PER BOARD RESOLUTION DATED ...' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN AGREEMENT NO. ...'. Under the AMLCFT Act, the bank will match the debit side to the shareholder's bank statement and complete a source-of-funds check. For New Zealand purposes, no exchange-control approval or overseas-investment clearance is required unless the investment is considered a significant business asset, which is usually far above the target company's valuation. Capital verification is satisfied by an updated Companies Office register and an auditor's capital-confirmation certificate.Outbound payments for trade and licensing settlements should use the NZCH bank-to-bank clearing rails for NZD-denominated payments and SWIFT MT103 for foreign-currency wires. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File thresholds: Complete documentation is required for transactions with associated non-resident enterprises if the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZD 1,000,000 for acquisitions of goods and services (including advertising revenue sharing, platform payouts, and IP licensing), and NZD 100,000 for financing transactions. Entities above the threshold must prepare a Local File and, where the group's consolidated turnover exceeds NZD 250,000,000, a Master File and annual CbC report (threshold of NZD 250,000,000). Contemporaneous documentation must be in place at the time of the transaction or adoption of the return, and be available for the IRD within 45 days of request.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime as in Cayman/BVI. However, the IRD applies the general anti-avoidance rule (section BG 1) and the specific transfer pricing provisions (Part F, subpart GD) to disregard transactions that lack commercial substance. A New Zealand resident company must demonstrate real decision-making and operational substance in New Zealand; otherwise, the IRD may re-characterize or allocate profits to the jurisdiction of economic substance. For a hyper-casual games developer, substance is evidenced by board meetings in New Zealand, local game design and engineering staff, and the maintenance of patent/copyright ownership records. Thin capitalization rules (net debt to assets ratio of 60% for non-resident-owned groups) also impose an implicit substance requirement on debt levels.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction for tax evasion under sections 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994. Imprisonment up to 5 years and unlimited fine at the court's discretion. A further civil penalty of 150% of the tax shortfall if the evasion involved a dominant purpose of avoiding tax (shortfall penalty under section 141E).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns may be jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall under the Companies Act 1993, and also be personally prohibited from managing a company. Criminal conviction can lead to director disqualification for up to 5 years and cancellation of travel rights.
Shell Company Status

Statutory sanction: The IRD may disregard the corporate structure under section BG 1 and attribute the income to the actual recipient onshore. Additionally, the company may be liable for a shortfall penalty of up to 100% of the resulting tax, and the company name may be deregistered under the Companies Act for carrying on a 'zero substance' operation with a non-material place of business. Inland Revenue may also issue a notice under section 17 of the TAA to access bank records of platform payouts.

Director personal liability: Directors who permit the company to operate as a shell without genuine economic substance in New Zealand may be personally liable for the tax avoidance consequence under the 'abuse of corporate personality' doctrine. Courts may pierce the corporate veil and order the director to repay the full tax arrears, plus compound interest and penalties. In egregious cases, a director's travel is restricted by the High Court upon IRD application.
Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009: up to NZD 2,000,000 for a body corporate per offence, plus an additional NZD 10,000 per day for continuing violations by a reporting entity, if the entity performs a money or value transfer service. If a gaming publisher acts as a payment intermediary (which is atypical for pure advertising), it must register with the Department of Internal Affairs.

Director personal liability: Directors can be criminally liable for an AML/CFT offence if they are knowingly a party to a breach or fail to ensure the company's compliance. Penalties include imprisonment for up to 2 years and fines up to NZD 50,000 for individuals. A director can also be subject to a disqualification order under the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for up to 10 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may reassess taxable income using the arm's length price and impose a shortfall penalty of up to 40% of the underpaid tax for a merely tax-disloyal arrangement, and up to 20% if the taxpayer has not taken reasonable care. Interest at the long-term UOMI rate applies on the underpayment. The withholding of NRWT on deemed royalty payments may also be reassessed at 15% (or 0% if DTA relief is substantiated).

Director personal liability: Directors who actively set up the offending transfer pricing structure without proper TP documentation may be found personally liable as 'tax loss confirmation agents' under the Tax Administration Act, leading to an order to pay the entire shortfall and penalty. In cases of wilful TP fraud, criminal liability attaches, potentially including 5 years imprisonment and personal travel bans.
