Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA), the national tax administration established under Royal Decree No. 106/2020 (successor to the Secretariat General of Taxation, Ministry of Finance). OTA e-services portal: https://taxoman.gov.om; OTA institutional website: https://www.ota.gov.om. Customs administration for export declarations is the Oman Customs (Directorate General of Customs, Royal Oman Police) operating the Bayan single-window at https://www.bayan.om.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Oman SPC (OM_SPC): (1) Incorporate at the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MOCIIP) via Invest Easy and obtain the Commercial Registration Number (CRN); (2) Register the SPC with OTA for Corporate Tax by filing the constitutional documents, CRN, lease evidence, shareholder beneficial-ownership/KYC and bank details on the OTA e-services portal, receiving the OTA taxpayer file number/TIN — an Omani SPC is tax resident by place of incorporation under the Corporate Tax Law (Sultan Decree 26/2022), which replaced the prior Income Tax Law; (3) Apply for VAT registration on the same OTA portal to obtain the VAT Tax Registration Number (TRN): mandatory if taxable supplies exceed OMR 38,000 in any 12-month period (voluntary from OMR 19,000); an export-oriented manufacturing SPC should register from commencement to obtain zero-rating and input-VAT refunds, and non-resident suppliers are registrable from the first taxable supply; (4) Use the OTA portal for e-filing of CIT and VAT returns, VAT zero-rate schedules and payments; appoint a licensed tax agent for large-taxpayer or cross-border engagement. Registration certificates are electronically issued and must be updated upon corporate changes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Oman has no check-the-box pass-through: an SPC is taxed per se at the entity level. Available elections: (a) voluntary VAT registration election when annual taxable supplies exceed OMR 19,000 (and expedited registration to enable export zero-rating); (b) participation exemption treatment for exempt dividends and capital gains from qualifying shareholdings under SD 26/2022, subject to the statutory holding-period and substance conditions; (c) elections on accounting basis, depreciation, and foreign-exchange treatment under the Corporate Tax Law; (d) voluntary disclosure and penalty-abatement election before OTA assessment; (e) no hybrid-mismatch election is recognized, and GloBE/Pillar Two rules do not apply to this mid-cap SPC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration: OMR 38,000 (approx. USD 98,700) of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration: from OMR 19,000; non-resident suppliers and importers are registrable from the first taxable supply; registration is recommended from day one for this export-oriented SPC to secure 0% export treatment and input VAT credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the VAT Law and Executive Regulations where goods (freeze-dried pet raw-meat, ISIC 1080) are sold under conditions constituting a true export from Oman to a destination outside Oman, and for qualifying intra-GCC B2B cross-border supplies when both parties are registered. The SPC must (i) hold a valid supply contract with a non-resident buyer, (ii) dispatch the goods under a full customs export declaration, (iii) retain transport documents evidencing physical departure, (iv) comply with Omani veterinary and food-safety export licensing for products of animal origin, and (v) repatriate payment in foreign currency consistent with the Central Bank of Oman expectation and the 180-day standard. Only taxed inputs attributable to zero-rated supplies can be credited or refunded; OTA may require the SPC to present the export evidence file, the customs log, and the bank credit advice before approving input VAT refunds.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged through the Bayan Oman single-window system, with the validated export entry, HS classification and customs value
  • Commercial invoice with packing list issued by the OM_SPC in the name of the overseas buyer, stating Incoterms, quantity, unit price and total consideration
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / road CMR evidencing physical dispatch from Oman to the declared foreign destination
  • Certificate of Origin issued by the Oman Chamber of Commerce or other authorized certifier
  • Veterinary/Health Certificate and animal-origin export permit issued by the competent Omani authority for freeze-dried raw-meat pet food
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice) proving receipt of foreign-currency proceeds from the buyer
  • Supporting VAT export file (export invoice, vessel or aircraft details, cargo insurance and third-party loading evidence) retained per OTA record-keeping rules

No comprehensive real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoicing stamping regime is in force in Oman as of 2025. The SPC must issue VAT tax invoices containing all statutory particulars (parties, VAT numbers, invoice serial, Arabic/English invoice label, supply date, consideration excluding VAT, rate and VAT amount); simplified tax invoices are permissible for exempt or low-value supplies. VAT returns and payments are filed electronically on the OTA portal (taxoman.gov.om) on a quarterly basis (monthly for large taxpayers); export transactions must be lodged through the Bayan customs single-window and reconciled with VAT return export schedules. The OTA e-invoicing roadmap is in phased design and pilot — a future clearance/API mandate with digital stamping is anticipated and should be tracked via OTA public notices; currently no third-party fiscal API certification is imposed.

OTA audit scrutiny: if the SPC cannot substantiate zero-rating (missing or false customs, transport, veterinary or remittance documentation), OTA will claw back the 0% benefit by re-assessing output VAT at 5% on the export proceeds from the original due date, adding late-payment penalties and statutory administrative fines under Royal Decree No. 121/2020 and OTA decisions. Fraudulent zero-rating involving forged customs declarations, health certificates or bank advices extends the ordinary five-year assessment window to ten years and attracts criminal fines and imprisonment under Omani revenue and penal laws, plus parallel penalties under the GCC Unified Customs Law and veterinary/food-safety legislation. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the standard 180-day window also exposes the SPC to Central Bank of Oman exchange-control and AML review, trade-finance penalties, and denial of future VAT refunds until compliance is demonstrated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible. Oman has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations since 24 February 1974, and current-account payments and profit remittances are unrestricted. Capital-account convertibility is liberalized for foreign direct investment under the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2019) and the Commercial Companies Law (Royal Decree 18/2019). The OMR is pegged to the USD at a central rate of 1 USD = 0.3845 OMR (equivalent 1 OMR = 2.60076 USD) with a narrow trading band. No prior CBO exchange-control approval is required for capital injections or dividends; practical hurdles are purely documentary: audited financial statements, Oman Tax Authority filing confirmation, and underlying trade or investment contracts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. The SPC may retain 100% of freeze-dried pet food export proceeds in OMR, USD, or EUR accounts without a maximum holding period. All receipts must be credited through a CBO-licensed bank, which reports the transaction for national Balance-of-Payments statistics within five business days. Standard bank practice requires the export shipment bill of lading/airway bill, commercial invoice, and, for repeated flows, a standing export contract on file.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO) — Directorate-General of Banking Supervision; sole monetary authority and foreign-exchange regulator for the Sultanate of Oman.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs are not CBO-licensed banks and cannot hold OMR accounts, access local clearing (OmanNet/ACH), or serve as the primary collection account for export proceeds. They are viable only as a secondary expense-routeing rail for small EUR/USD supplier payments below USD 25,000 per month, and they are legally inadequate for capital injections, payroll, tax, customs duties, or any regulated treasury flow. Compliance with the AML Law (Royal Decree 30/2016) requires the SPC to concentrate main flows through a CBO-licensed bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% flat corporate income tax (CIT) on the taxable income of capital companies, including foreign-owned Single Person Companies (SPC), under Royal Decree (RD) 42/2024, the current Oman Income Tax Law.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Oman does not impose withholding tax on dividend distributions made by an Omani SPC to its non-resident shareholder; distributed profits leave Oman free of source-level dividend tax, and dividends are exempt income in the hands of the recipient under Omani law.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (contemporaneous documentation) is required when aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed OMR 750,000 in a tax year; Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated annual turnover exceeds OMR 30 million; Country-by-Country Report is required when consolidated group turnover exceeds OMR 300 million; the annual TP Disclosure Annexure accompanies the CIT return regardless of threshold unless the taxpayer is exempted as a small enterprise under the Executive Regulations.

Economic substance scope: Oman has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations law in the UAE style, but economic substance is embedded legal doctrine: (i) the Commercial Companies Law (RD 18/2019) obliges the SPC to maintain a genuine registered office, local management, payroll, and physical production premises, with the freeze-drying plant staffed by qualified operators and supported by audited books; (ii) RD 42/2024 introduces GAAR and Controlled Foreign Corporation (CFC) rules that recharacterize shell structures, deny treaty benefits, and attribute foreign passive income where no real business purpose exists; (iii) the licensing authorities (MOCIIP and the food-safety regulators) verify operational substance through factory inspections and food-manufacturing permit audits; (iv) in TP audits the Tax Authority disregards transactions lacking commercial rationale and re-qualifies them according to economic substance and the arm's length principle.

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Additional tax assessment up to 100% of the evaded tax, financial fines of OMR 43,000-50,000, seizure of assets, closure of the freeze-drying facility, and publication of the violation; the underpaid tax also attracts the 2% monthly late-payment surcharge.

Shell Company / GAAR / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of all related-party deductions and input credits, recharacterization under GAAR with full 15% CIT plus 2% monthly surcharge, denial of tax treaty relief, administrative penalties up to OMR 5,000, and potential dissolution and license revocation under the Commercial Companies Law.

Statutory sanction: Fines from OMR 5,000 to OMR 50,000 per violation under the Omani AML-CTF Law (RD 30/2016), freezing of bank accounts and payment rails, suspension or revocation of the food-safety commercial license, forfeiture of proceeds, and disqualification from export permits on repeat offense.
