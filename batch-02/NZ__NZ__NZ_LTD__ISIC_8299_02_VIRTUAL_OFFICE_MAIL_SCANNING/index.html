Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mail receiving address alone is not acceptable as the only registered office and service address for a New Zealand company under sections 190 and 191 of the Companies Act 1993 because a registered office must be a physical location in New Zealand at which documents may be served and defended. Because the entity's own business is a commercial mail receiving agency and digital scanning operation, an external virtual office is a red flag and will generally be rejected by the Inland Revenue Department for GST and company registration and by major New Zealand banks including ANZ, ASB, BNZ, and Westpac for a corporate bank account. Banks require a genuine physical site with the company name on signage, a lease in the companys legal name, permission to inspect, and operating records. A dedicated serviced-office suite with a formal lease and on-site staff can pass, provided the address is an actual suite number, the landlord issues an occupancy certificate, and the mail-scanning equipment is physically present. Using a PO Box or virtual mailbox without a physical office will trigger a high risk of refusal, and the New Zealand Companies Office may require a director's residential address as the registered office if no satisfactory physical registered office exists.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand law imposes no minimum paid-up share capital and no escrow or blocked capital account requirement. A company may issue shares for any lawful consideration, including nil or nominal consideration, but must have at least one share and one shareholder. The shareholder subscription is evidenced by entries in the share register rather than by a bank-blocked deposit.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no Civil Defence fire permit or structural load inspection is required for a pure virtual office and mail scanning operation. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; online electronic portal: myIR (secure IRD login for IRD number, GST registration, GST returns, and taxpayer records); business registration is coordinated with the New Zealand Companies Office via the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) registry.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a New Zealand presence, the entity first obtains an NZBN and, if carried on through a New Zealand registered company, that company is automatically issued an IRD number by the Companies Office and Inland Revenue integration upon incorporation. Where no automatic issue occurs, the non-resident company applies for an IRD number through Inland Revenue's myIR portal using the non-resident corporate application pathway and must provide: certified copies of the foreign incorporation certificate and constitutional documents; proof of the registered New Zealand address and any registered agent; identity documents for directors and beneficial owners; and a completed non-resident company IRD number application. The application is followed by enrolment for GST if the entity's taxable supplies are expected to exceed the GST registration threshold or if registration is voluntarily sought; the IRD will issue a New Zealand GST number, which in New Zealand is the same IRD number with a GST suffix, once the registration is accepted.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (New Zealand incorporated limited liability companies are taxed on worldwide income at the corporate tax rate, with elective look-through treatment available only for closely held eligible companies) — For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a New Zealand presence, the entity first obtains an NZBN and, if carried on through a New Zealand registered company, that company is automatically issued an IRD number by the Companies Office and Inland Revenue integration upon incorporation. The application is followed by enrolment for GST if the entity's taxable supplies are expected to exceed the GST registration threshold or if registration is voluntarily sought; the IRD will issue a New Zealand GST number, which in New Zealand is the same IRD number with a GST suffix, once the registration is accepted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (New Zealand incorporated limited liability companies are taxed on worldwide income at the corporate tax rate, with elective look-through treatment available only for closely held eligible companies)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in New Zealand. Available statutory elections include: election to become a Look-Through Company (LTC) under the Income Tax Act 2007 for companies with five or fewer shareholders; GST grouping election under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; and optional GST registration before the registration threshold is reached. Hybrid mismatch rules are codified under the Income Tax Act 2007 (subpart FH) and no elective hybrid mismatch accommodation is offered, though taxpayers may elect to apply the ordering provisions in accordance with those rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies per 12-month period for a person carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand; a lower or no threshold applies to certain non-resident suppliers of remote services in inbound B2C supplies, but for the NZ_LTD exporter the NZD 60,000 rule remains the core turnover trigger, subject to voluntary registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when the supplier proves the goods are physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier or by the recipient on behalf of the supplier, and the supplier retains evidence of export within the required time, usually by completion of a Customs Export Entry before removal from New Zealand. Services supplied by an NZ_LTD to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply may be zero-rated under the place-of-supply rules where the services are not directly connected with New Zealand land or goods and are not effectively supplied to a person in New Zealand; mail scanning and virtual office administration services to an offshore client fall into this category if the contractual recipient is a non-resident and the service is performed from New Zealand for a foreign destination. The supplier must obtain and keep contemporaneous evidence of the offshore recipient status, the service agreement, the delivery of the exported deliverables, the overseas payment, and any underlying physical export movement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry/CUSEXP) approved by New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window (TSW) with the Customs Client Number, consignor, consignee, goods description, and declared export value
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document such as an airway bill or express courier consignment note evidencing physical export departure from a New Zealand port or airport for any tangible mail or document items
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract and service brief for the CMRA virtual office and mail-scanning engagement, identifying the non-resident customer, the place of performance, and the exported output including scanned files, indexing notes, and mail summaries
  • Foreign exchange repatriation record consisting of an inward remittance advice or bank credit confirmation from a New Zealand financial institution showing the offshore client's foreign currency payment for the zero-rated supply and matching the invoice value

No New Zealand statutory mandate for real-time fiscal clearance, tax-valid e-invoicing API, or anti-fraud digital stamping exists. Businesses may issue tax invoices electronically, and GST information is reported periodically through myIR using standard 1-month, 2-month, or 6-month taxable periods. New Zealand participates in the Peppol network voluntarily for interoperable e-invoicing, but submission to a national clearance hub is not required.

Inland Revenue may audit export zero-rating claims and, where the evidence fails, reassess the supply as standard-rated, asserting GST at 15% and use-of-money interest from the original return due date. Exporters that fail to substantiate customs departure, offshore recipient status, or payment repatriation within the applicable timeframe must repay any GST refunded; shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 may range from 20% for lack of reasonable care, up to 40% for an unacceptable tax position, 100% for an abusive tax position, and up to 150% for evasion. Criminal prosecution for GST fraud or evasion is possible, and retrospective clawback periods can extend to the full limitation period applicable to tax avoidance and fraudulent returns. Additionally, if no physical export occurs, an attempt to zero-rate domestic services will be struck down and the full output GST becomes payable from the time of supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar is a freely convertible and independently floating currency. Neither the Reserve Bank of New Zealand nor any foreign exchange administration imposes exchange control approval, licensing, surrender or repatriation restrictions on current-account or capital-account transactions. A New Zealand limited company may receive inbound share capital, shareholder loans and trade receipts in foreign currency, convert those funds to NZD through a licensed bank at market rates, and remit dividends, interest, management fees or liquidation proceeds without central bank permission. Non-resident ownership is freely permitted, and there is no exchange-control minimum capital verification threshold, although the Companies Office requires share allotment filing and an annual return. The only practical constraints are tax-reporting duties, non-resident withholding tax on dividends and interest unless reduced by an applicable double-tax agreement, and banking KYC requirements that demand economic substance and source-of-wealth evidence. Export proceeds may be kept in foreign currency either onshore or offshore, and there is no requirement to convert foreign receipts into NZD. Therefore, the treasury environment is open-market and convertibility risk for this CMRA and virtual-office mail-scanning entity is negligible; all frictions arise from AML/CFT onboarding, tax compliance and bank risk appetite rather than statutory currency controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rule or mandatory conversion timeline exists for export or foreign-currency receipts. Exporters may retain sale proceeds in any currency in an NZD or multicurrency account at a registered bank without converting to NZD. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand only receives aggregate balance-of-payments and international investment-position data through reporting banks, and individual banks may apply internal reporting thresholds, typically for transactions equivalent to NZD 1 million or more. No portion of export proceeds must be surrendered to the central bank, and no government-mandated conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a principal banking rail and only medium as an ancillary collection rail. New Zealand does not have a European-style electronic money institution licence. A non-bank payment provider must be registered as a financial service provider under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, become a reporting entity under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and, if it accepts deposits or customer funds on a continuing basis, hold a licence under the Deposit Takers Act 2023 supervised by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand. Global fintech platforms such as Stripe, Wise and Airwallex may provide payment-facilitation, merchant acquiring, card acceptance and cross-border payout services, but they are not licensed to act as the primary deposit-taking bank for capital escrow, share-capital verification, or commercial lending relationships. They are inherently outside the core treasury structure for this entity and should be used only for recurring subscription collections and low-margin payment routing, with settlement flowing into a licensed New Zealand tier-1 bank account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% (Income Tax Act 2007, Schedule 1, Part A, Table 1, as applied to standard companies for the tax year corresponding to the balance date).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders (Income Tax Act 2007, Schedule 1, Table 1). Treaty relief commonly reduces this to 15% for portfolio shareholdings and 5% for substantial shareholdings of at least 10% of the voting power (e.g., under the NZ-Australia, NZ-US, and NZ-Singapore DTAs).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a standard 31 March balance date: the IR4 must be filed no later than 7 July following the end of the income year. Terminal tax is payable by 7 April, with an optional second instalment on 7 July for provisional taxpayers. Provisional tax instalments fall on 28 August, 15 January and 7 May. A tax agent with a valid extension may defer the filing date to 31 March of the following tax year, but terminal tax and provisional tax payment dates are not extended unless the company qualifies for payment concessions under the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border related-party transactions exceeding NZ$1 million per income year require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File / Master File package). Multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least NZ$1.3 billion (approximately EUR 750 million) must also prepare a Country-by-Country Report (CbC, form IR1047) and master file, and file the international questionnaire (IR1073) to disclose exposures.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation. Substance is assessed through statutory tax residence tests (incorporation; central management and control under s YB 4), the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1), IP/GAAR continuity tests, and the AML/CFT obligation of a 'trust and company service provider'. A virtual-office and mail-scanning company with no local employees, premises used for actual management, local board meetings, or income-generating activity in New Zealand risks recharacterisation as non-NZ-resident under DTA tie-breaker rules, or if treated as NZ-resident, reconstruction to reallocate profit to the place of effective management. The Inland Revenue may issue a 'shell company' warning and require an economic substance declaration where the company functions solely as a conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Tax Avoidance under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the total tax shortfall for evasion (s 141E), 100% for an abusive tax position (s 141D), 40% for gross carelessness (s 141C), and 20% for failure to take reasonable care (s 141B); plus use-of-money interest, late payment penalties, and full reassessment of the reconstructed profit. Criminal prosecution under TAA s 143A can expose the company to fines up to NZ$50,000 and imprisonment of responsible persons for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who knew of, directed or participated in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall (TAA ss 149 and 152). The Commissioner may apply for a Departure Prohibition Order preventing such directors from leaving New Zealand while the tax debt exceeds NZ$10,000. Courts may also disqualify directors for up to five years under the Companies Act 1993 s 382 and order civil restitution under s 301 for fraudulent or wrong receipt of company assets.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reconstruction of profits under s BG 1; denial of treaty benefits on residence grounds; cancellation of imputation credit account privileges; shortfall penalties of 100-150% on the reconstructed shortfall; and risk of the entity being re-taxed in its ultimate parent jurisdiction as a permanent establishment under an applicable DTA (Article 5).

Director personal liability: Directors risk personal liability for the company's tax debts under Companies Act 1993 ss 300-301 where they were knowingly parties to the sham; liability for reckless trading under s 135 if the shell structure leaves creditors (including the Crown) unpaid; and criminal liability for false statements to the Companies Office under s 358, with fines up to NZ$200,000 and imprisonment for up to ten years for forgery or false accounting under the Crimes Act 1961.
AML/CFT Compliance Failure for Virtual Office / CMRA as Trust and Company Service Provider

Statutory sanction: Civil pecuniary penalties up to NZ$200,000 for individuals and NZ$2,000,000 for bodies corporate for each contravention (AML/CFT Act ss 82-84); infringement offences for procedural failures; enforcement orders requiring remediation, suspension or revocation of the entity's registration; and criminal prosecution for wilful non-compliance or the tip-off offence.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are personally liable to pay civil penalties where they failed to exercise due diligence; they may additionally face criminal liability for money laundering (three years' imprisonment) or financing of terrorism (seven years' imprisonment). The Financial Markets Authority and the Department of Internal Affairs may name the individuals publicly, and the High Court can impose directorial disqualification.
Withholding Tax Fraud / Wrongful Treaty Relief

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the full NRWT shortfall (30% for dividends or 15% for interest/royalties), payment of supplementary dividends that should have been attached, shortfall penalties up to 150%, and use-of-money interest from the date the tax should have been remitted. Criminal prosecution under TAA s 143A applies where the payer knowingly misrepresented the foreign status or the payment character.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed incorrect declarations, directed the mischaracterisation, or caused the payment without deduction become personally liable for the withheld amounts and associated penalties under TAA s 149. The Commissioner can use tracing powers against nominee shareholders and bank accounts (TAA Part 10), and issue a Departure Prohibition Order if the aggregate outstanding liability exceeds NZ$10,000. Serious cases founded on dishonest conduct attract imprisonment terms under the Crimes Act 1961 ss 229-230.
