Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, an agency of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), Wellington, New Zealand; online service through the Companies Register at companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz using a RealMe login.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No 105), principally sections 10-13 (registration), 14 (effect of registration), 39 (issue of shares), 40 (no par value shares), 91 (board authority), 122 (written resolutions), 128-130 (financial assistance), 131-137 (director duties), 138-140 (director liability), 189 (company records), 221-225 (constitution); Financial Reporting Act 2013; Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008; Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009; Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 (applicable only to payment system access, not to registration).

Formation / constitutional filings

Form 5 - Application for Incorporation under the Companies Act 1993, filed electronically on the Companies Register; approval results in the issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation and automatic allocation of the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 45 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand company law does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital, and no blocked escrow or designated capital account is required for incorporation. A limited liability company may be incorporated with one or more shares issued for the consideration recorded in the share register. For the cross-border remittance business, the board must nevertheless maintain adequate operational capital and client fund segregation arrangements to satisfy banking and AML/CFT supervisory expectations, but such amounts are prudential and contractual, not statutory incorporation capital.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no civil defense fire permit, structural load certificate or industrial workplace licence applies to a technology-based remittance operation. If the company later leases an office, the building must hold a Building Warrant of Fitness and any required fire evacuation scheme under the Building Act 2004, and the company must comply with the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 and WorkSafe New Zealand workplace duties for all office staff.

Paid-in capital rule: None. New Zealand company law does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital, and no blocked escrow or designated capital account is required for incorporation. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand; online portal myIR at myIR.ird.govt.nz; company registration portal at the New Zealand Companies Office (companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Overseas company registration: before commencing taxable activities, the foreign non-resident entity must register as an overseas company with the New Zealand Companies Office under Part 18 of the Companies Act 1993, obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN), and appoint a New Zealand resident agent. Step 2 - IRD number application: submit the Inland Revenue non-resident company IRD number application through myIR or by the prescribed non-resident paper channel, attaching the home-country certificate of incorporation, constitution or equivalent governance instruments, certified registers of directors and shareholders, resident agent appointment evidence, ultimate beneficial owner details, and a projection of New Zealand-sourced activity; Inland Revenue issues the IRD number as the sole national tax identification number. Step 4 - Other payroll/tax registrations: register for PAYE and employee deductions if any employees are in New Zealand, and for fringe benefit tax or other statutory deductions where applicable. Step 5 - Regulatory prerequisites: because the entity is a consumer remittance agency, obtain the required AML/CFT registration and special licence from the competent New Zealand supervisor before operating the money transmission rails.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a New Zealand limited company is taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act 2007; a tax-resident New Zealand company is taxed on worldwide income, whereas a non-resident company in this structure is within territorial scope only for income with a New Zealand source).

Available election forms

None - New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime, and the limited company is per se an entity-level taxpayer. Available statutory elections relevant to the structure are limited to: (i) GST grouping under section 55 of the GST Act 1985; (ii) GST filing basis elections (invoice or payments basis); (iii) dividend imputation company grouping elections under Part OB of the Income Tax Act 2007; and (iv) documentation-based zero-rating for financial services supplied to non-residents under section 11A of the GST Act 1985.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 per rolling 12-month period for taxable supplies made in New Zealand; non-resident suppliers of remote services become registrable when supplies to New Zealand consumers reach NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, a 0% rate applies when the supplier in New Zealand exports the goods in the course of the supply or arranges delivery to a carrier for export (section 11(1)(a) of the GST Act 1985), with the export normally expected to be completed within 28 days of supply unless the Commissioner accepts a longer arrangement. For services, a 0% rate applies when the services are supplied to a recipient who is a non-resident and outside New Zealand at the time of performance and the services do not relate to land or goods in New Zealand or to employment in New Zealand (section 11(1)(m)). For the ISIC 6619 cross-border remittance rails, the applicable regime is the zero-rating of financial services (section 11A of the GST Act 1985): money transmission, payment processing and associated foreign-exchange services supplied to a non-resident outside New Zealand are zero-rated when the recipient is not acquiring them in connection with a New Zealand taxable activity and the supplier holds the prescribed evidence of non-resident status and offshore settlement. If the conditions are not met, the supply is an exempt financial service, not zero-rated, so output GST is not charged but input tax deductions for related costs are denied.

  • For physical goods: New Zealand Customs export entry filed through the Trade Single Window (the functional equivalent of a Bill of Entry), the corresponding Bill of Lading or Air Waybill proving export, and the commercial invoice for the goods.
  • For cross-border remittance services: executed customer contract/master agreement for money transmission, the customer due-diligence file maintained under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and the fee statement or commercial invoice identifying the non-resident recipient.
  • Correspondent bank settlement evidence: SWIFT MT103/MT202 or equivalent bank advice showing the foreign currency payment over the remittance rail, together with the New Zealand bank settlement account statement or treasury/FX confirmation evidencing receipt of the foreign currency.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) if voluntarily issued by the receiving bank, expressly noting that FIRC is not a New Zealand statutory export document and is supplementary to the bank and customs evidence.

New Zealand imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory pre-clearance approval of invoices, and no anti-fraud digital stamp. GST-registered businesses file returns electronically through myIR, and tax invoices may be issued electronically. Voluntary e-invoicing uses the Peppol operating model. New Zealand Customs accepts export-related electronic declarations through the Trade Single Window. Tax and supporting records must be retained for seven years under the Tax Administration Act 1994.

The Inland Revenue Department may retrospectively reverse any zero-rating claim where the exporter cannot evidence actual export, offshore recipient status, or receipt of foreign currency from outside New Zealand. If reversed, output GST at 15% is assessed on the supply, plus use-of-money interest from the original due date. Shortfall penalties under sections 141A-141EB of the Tax Administration Act 1994 are also imposed, at 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. False or misleading export declarations to New Zealand Customs are prosecuted under the Customs and Excise Act 2018. For remittance rails, the AML/CFT supervisor can separately enforce beneficial-ownership and transaction-monitoring failures under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, independent of the tax clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a non-restricted, fully convertible foreign exchange system under IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no capital controls, no administrative surrender requirements, and no waiting or repurchase periods for profit repatriation. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand supervises banks and the clearing system, while the Financial Markets Authority and Department of Internal Affairs oversee conduct and AML/CFT registration for non-bank remitters. Cross-border current account transactions, including trade settlements, service payments, remittance fee income, and dividend payments, can be executed freely through licensed banks and registered money remittance companies. Capital account flows, including inbound equity contributions, shareholder loans, and bond issuance, are also unrestricted provided the business does not trigger the Overseas Investment Act's sensitive land or significant business asset notification thresholds. Remittance payloads entering through the banking system must be accompanied by correct beneficiary details and should be tagged with business justification to avoid wire transfer misclassification. The New Zealand dollar trades without convertibility restrictions, and NZD/USD/EUR offshore deliverable liquidity is robust. However, for remittance activity, regulated reporting obligations under the AML/CFT Act and Payment Times Disclosure requirements require clearing to occur only via registered financial institutions to preserve correspondent banking access.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirements exist. New Zealand imposes no mandatory timeline for repatriation and no conversion ratio; exporters and remittance companies may retain foreign currency in offshore bank accounts or convert to NZD freely. The only relevant reporting arises under tax law: controlled foreign company and transactional documentation rules require business-need evidence when retaining receivable funds offshore.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Conditionally feasible for an NZ-LTD remittance agency. Full banking licenses cannot be held by EMI fintechs without Reserve Bank of New Zealand bank registration, but as remitters they can register under the AML/CFT Act and join accredited payment networks. Offshore digital EMIs are viable only when they have or obtain a New Zealand money remitter registration or partner with a registered reporting entity for funding and payout rails; unregistered fintechs are legally barred from providing cross-border remittance clearing as autonomous banks and will be rejected by Tier-1 correspondents. A licensing and contractual wall is required so customer funds stay in segregated operational accounts at an NZ bank.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Companies Act 1993 and the Financial Reporting Act 2013, a New Zealand limited company must have a compulsory statutory audit if: (1) it satisfies at least two of the following three quantitative thresholds in each of the two immediately preceding consecutive accounting periods: total assets at balance date exceed NZD 60 million (approx USD 36.6 million); total revenue for the period exceeds NZD 30 million (approx USD 18.3 million); and the company has 50 or more full-time equivalent employees; (2) it is an FMC reporting entity with public accountability under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, irrespective of size; (3) shareholders holding not less than 5% of the voting shares have validly required an audit under Part 11 of the Companies Act 1993; or (4) the financial statements are required to be audited by a sectoral regulator or licence condition, which for a cross-border remittance money service business may be imposed by the Department of Internal Affairs or other financial sector supervisor. The statutory audit must be conducted by a licensed chartered accountant auditor using International Standards on Auditing (New Zealand) (ISA (NZ)).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation are expected where aggregate related-party cross-border dealings exceed NZD 1 million; the IR289 International Tax Disclosure must be filed when that threshold is crossed. An OECD-aligned Master File is required where the multinational group has annual consolidated revenue above approximately NZD 1.2 billion (EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations code. However, the Income Tax Act 2007 general anti-avoidance rule, the OECD Principal Purpose Test, and central management and control rules require real commercial substance: physical premises, employed executives, local board decisions, risk-bearing capital, licensed AML/CFT operations, and bank accounts. A New Zealand remittance company functioning as an invoice shell or letterbox without such substance will be denied treaty benefits and taxed on a factual effective-management basis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty of up to 200% of the evaded tax plus use-of-money interest; criminal prosecution under section 144 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 with imprisonment for up to five years, fines up to NZD 50,000, and confiscation of proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: A director who signed or directed the false return is personally liable to prosecution; the corporate veil may be pierced for fraud or sham. The Commissioner may issue a departure prohibition order to prevent the director from leaving New Zealand until outstanding tax liabilities are secured.
