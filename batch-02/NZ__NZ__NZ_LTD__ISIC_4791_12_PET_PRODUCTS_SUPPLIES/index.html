Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue Te Tari Taake), operating the myIR secure online portal; company registration with the New Zealand Companies Office via the Companies Register

E-government filing portal

A non-resident limited company must first obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) if it incorporates in New Zealand or be recognized as an overseas company registered with the Companies Office; it must then apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number using the prescribed non-resident company application, providing certified certificate of incorporation or equivalent overseas registration evidence, directors'/shareholders' details, a New Zealand address for service or agent, and the intended New Zealand activities; the IRD number is issued by Inland Revenue and becomes the taxpayer reference for income tax, GST, and PAYE obligations; remote non-resident sellers of services or low-value goods may apply for GST registration through the same portal when they exceed the statutory threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; non-resident limited companies are taxed on New Zealand-source income, resident limited companies on worldwide income — A non-resident limited company must first obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) if it incorporates in New Zealand or be recognized as an overseas company registered with the Companies Office; it must then apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number using the prescribed non-resident company application, providing certified certificate of incorporation or equivalent overseas registration evidence, directors'/shareholders' details, a New Zealand address for service or agent, and the intended New Zealand activities; the IRD number is issued by Inland Revenue and becomes the taxpayer reference for income tax, GST, and PAYE obligations; remote non-resident sellers of services or low-value goods may apply for GST registration through the same portal when they exceed the statutory threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; non-resident limited companies are taxed on New Zealand-source income, resident limited companies on worldwide income

Available election forms

None; New Zealand does not operate U.S.-style check-the-box elections or elective hybrid mismatch regimes. Elective tax groupings (e.g. GST grouping, loss offset elections) do not change the entity's fundamental tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in New Zealand in a 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services or low-value imported goods must register if the NZD 60,000 threshold is exceeded, with no physical presence required

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically exported from New Zealand are zero-rated under the GST Act 1985 only if the goods are exported within 28 days of supply and the exporter retains sufficient shipping evidence. Supplies of services to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed may be zero-rated if the services are not supplied to a person in New Zealand for use/consumption in New Zealand; for pet products supplied via e-commerce, the sale to an overseas consumer plus proof of dispatch and export satisfies zero-rating. Related zero-rating applies to certain businesses involving international transport, toll, and exempt supplies.

  • New Zealand Customs export entry transmitted through the Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing New Zealand port/airport of export and overseas destination
  • Bank remittance evidence or overseas payment receipt showing settlement from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the pet supplies and export consignment
  • Freight forwarder or carrier instructions confirming physical export from New Zealand

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance API, or digital stamping regime exists in New Zealand. The GST return and payment are filed electronically through the myIR portal. Tax invoices must satisfy the substantive requirements of the GST Act 1985; Peppol e-invoicing is voluntary and not a fiscal clearance condition.

Inland Revenue may audit zero-rated exports and require production of the export and payment evidence during the standard 7-year record retention period. Unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating faces reassessment with GST clawback, use-of-money interest, and civil shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 of 20% for unacceptable tax positions, 40% for lack of reasonable care, 100% for gross carelessness or abusive tax positions, and 150% for evasion; serious evasion may also result in criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised open capital account and a freely floating, fully convertible New Zealand dollar. There are no statutory limits on current account conversions, no surrender requirements for export proceeds, and no approval requirement for ordinary dividend, interest, or trade remittances by a New Zealand Limited company. For a foreign-owned NZ Ltd engaged in electronic retailing of pet supplies, non-resident shareholders may subscribe share capital in any freely convertible currency, convert funds into NZD through a registered commercial bank or licenced foreign exchange dealer, and remit profits back to home jurisdictions after satisfying New Zealand tax obligations, particularly the prescribed dividend withholding tax framework. The only material regulatory overlay is not exchange control but the overseas investment screening regime administered by the Overseas Investment Office, which is unlikely to apply to passive e-commerce supply chain activity unless the company acquires sensitive land, significant business assets, or certain fishing quotas. Therefore, the New Zealand dollar is convertible across both current and capital accounts, with no quantitative restrictions or Central Bank pre-approval thresholds for ordinary treasury transactions of this entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no deadline for export foreign currency receipts. A New Zealand exporting entity may lawfully retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency accounts with registered banks or offshore accounts, and convert those proceeds to NZD at commercially selected rates and times.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua), with prudential supervision of registered banks and payment/settlement systems under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013; no separate foreign exchange administration exists because New Zealand operates no general exchange-control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs have Medium-Low viability as a primary treasury clearing rail and High viability only as a downstream collection/settlement overlay. New Zealand does not issue an EU-style EMI licence; non-bank payment service providers must be registered as financial institutions under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 if they provide money-changing, remittance, or payment services in or to New Zealand. An offshore EMI such as Wise, Airwallex, or Stripe Treasury cannot hold an Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) account at the Reserve Bank and is therefore not a direct participant in New Zealand high-value clearing. Any offshore EMI servicing this NZ Ltd would need to partner with a locally registered bank, maintain client funds in a trust account at a NZ Tier-1 bank, or operate only as a payment collection gateway for Stripe and PayPal settlements. For regulatory safety and clearing reliability, the treasury structure should retain a NZD-denominated operating account at a Tier-1 domestic bank, with Stripe/PayPal connected as acquiring rails, not as the primary corporate treasury settlement account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward shareholder capital is routed by SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the NZ Tier-1 bank account with the payment narrative identifying the purpose as share capital subscription or shareholder loan advance. The bank will treat foreign exchange conversion from USD, AUD, or GBP into NZD as a standard spot transaction with no Reserve Bank pre-approval. For capital verification, the entity must file the share allotment return with the New Zealand Companies Office, produce the executed subscription resolution, provide director identity and source-of-funds evidence, and maintain a bank certificate or letter confirming the inward credit and conversion rate. If the investor is acquiring or injecting funds through a lender or trust, the treasury should attach the underlying loan agreement and beneficial ownership register to prevent the funds from being frozen at the receiving bank. No Overseas Investment Office approval is required unless the capital is used to acquire sensitive land or significant business assets, which is not contemplated by this e-commerce treasury plan.Outbound trade disbursements for pet product inventory and logistics services are cleared through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for open account settlements, commercial letters of credit issued by the NZ bank using MT700/MT707 messaging for high-value supplier contracts, and documentary collections with draft and shipping documents for smaller documentary transactions. Domestic NZD supplier payments can be cleared through Payments New Zealand retail clearing rails via direct bank-to-bank transfers, Faster Payments style single debit/credit rails, or ordinary Direct Credits, while cross-border payments involving Asia-Pacific and European suppliers should be routed through the bank's international payments channel with ISBP and UCP600-compliant documentation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand mandates transfer pricing documentation for cross-border related-party transactions when the NZ group's global revenue exceeds NZD 100 million; a Local File is required when aggregate intercompany transactions exceed NZD 4 million in the income year, with separate thresholds applying to loans, services, intangibles, and inventory. Master File must be filed where the multinational group exceeds NZD 1 billion global revenue.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not impose a separate Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but the Commissioner will apply the general anti-avoidance rule (BG 1) and the OECD BEPS minimum standard to disregard arrangements lacking real commercial substance. A NZ e-commerce company must demonstrate CIGA through local employees, physical premises, execution of purchasing and supply contracts, board meetings, risk-bearing capacity, and profits attributable to actual functions performed. Offshore IP holding structures that shift profits without substantive decision-making personnel in NZ will be reconstructed under the latent CIGA principle.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Administration Act 1994, sections 143A and 143B, conviction on indictment may impose a fine not exceeding NZD 50,000 (company) and imprisonment not exceeding 5 years for officers; the evasion shortfall penalty is 150% of the unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where a director authorized or participated in the evasion; Inland Revenue may recover tax and penalties from the director's personal assets, including tracing and restitution orders, and may obtain a departure prohibition order or travel ban while the debt is outstanding. Directors may also be blacklisted or disqualified from managing companies under the Companies Act 1993 and face criminal imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company or CIGA Sham Lacking Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may apply Income Tax Act 2007, BG 1 to cancel the tax benefit, reattribute income to the true economic owner, impose a shortfall penalty of 100% to 150%, deny treaty benefits under the Limitation of Relief provision, and the company may be struck off the NZ register under the Companies Act 1993 for failure to carry on business.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for the full tax shortfall and penalties where they signed declarations knowing the company was a shell; civil recovery may attach personal assets and earnings; a travel ban or departure prohibition may prevent leaving New Zealand while tax debts are unpaid; knowing participation in tax abuse carries imprisonment up to 5 years and managerial blacklisting.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Base Erosion Payments

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007, sections GC 1 through GC 14 allow the Commissioner to adjust profits to an arm's length amount, deny deductions, impose shortfall penalties of 50% for unacceptable tax positions, 100% for gross carelessness, and 150% for evasion. Criminal liability arises under Tax Administration Act 1994 for obstruction or giving false evidence.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed intercompany agreements without independent economic analysis can be prosecuted as parties to tax evasion; civil liability includes personal asset restitution for profits stripped and penalties imposed; the Commissioner may obtain a departure prohibition order or travel ban and blacklist the director from holding directorships; imprisonment up to 5 years applies for knowingly false transfer pricing documentation.
