Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity: (1) Determine whether the entity is carrying on a taxable business in New Zealand; if so, register the overseas company with the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993 and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) where registration is required, using the foreign incorporation documents. (2) Apply to Inland Revenue through myIR for an IRD number as a non-resident company; if myIR self-service is not available for the entity type, submit the equivalent paper application to IRD. (5) For a New Zealand-incorporated limited company, the Companies Office forwards entity data to Inland Revenue, and the company must complete its IRD number and GST registration details through myIR before filing obligations commence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A New Zealand limited company is taxed at the entity level. Eligible close companies may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under the Income Tax Act 2007, subject to the maximum five-shareholder rule and eligibility criteria, causing income and losses to be attributed to shareholders. No US-style check-the-box election exists in New Zealand; hybrid mismatch treatment is governed by statutory anti-avoidance rules, not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in New Zealand in any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services and digital services to New Zealand consumers are also required to register if their total New Zealand supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in 12 months. Voluntary registration is permitted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11(1) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, goods are zero-rated when the supplier exports them, or a GST-registered recipient exports them, within 28 days of supply, with the supplier retaining statutory evidence of export. Under section 11A of the same Act, services are zero-rated when supplied to a person who is not resident in New Zealand and is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, excluding services directly connected with New Zealand land or New Zealand goods and certain services performed in New Zealand. For AI avatar video-generation SaaS, the digital service supplied to a non-resident recipient qualifies for zero-rating if the supplier holds reliable records showing the recipient's non-resident status, offshore presence or location (e.g., IP geolocation, billing country, business registration address), and the foreign delivery of the service. If the same supply is made through a merchant-of-record payment processor, GST characterisation should be reviewed against the underlying commercial agreement. New Zealand GST law does not impose a fixed foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure recorded in this dossier is a conservative internal audit benchmark and should not be treated as a statutory condition.

  • New Zealand Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry) lodged through the Trade Single Window for exported goods
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or postal/courier export documentation evidencing physical removal from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice, supply contract or purchase order, and proof of technical or digital service delivery
  • Foreign exchange remittance evidence from the non-resident customer (e.g., SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice, or Stripe/Paddle settlement records); New Zealand does not issue a separate Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), so bank and FX records are the FIRC-equivalent
  • For digital services: non-resident status declaration, IP geolocation logs, customer billing address, and payment processor records showing offshore consumption

No real-time fiscal clearance system, no mandatory API invoice stamping, and no anti-fraud digital stamping mandate exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates on the PEPPOL network. GST returns must be filed through myIR on a monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly filing cycle depending on turnover.

Inland Revenue may reassess unsubstantiated zero-rating and claw back GST at 15%, plus use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Standard assessments may generally be amended within four years, with no time limit for fraudulent, wilfully misleading, or evasive returns. Shortfall penalties include 20% for lack of reasonable care or an unacceptable tax position, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or similar. Knowingly false GST returns can also give rise to criminal prosecution and fines. To defend a zero-rating claim, the exporter must maintain the full documentary bundle and, for goods, evidence of export within the 28-day statutory period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates an open, freely convertible currency regime. There are no blanket exchange controls, no licensing of ordinary FX transactions, and no requirement to obtain central bank approval for dividend remittances, service royalties, intercompany loan repayments, or equity injections under the current or capital account, provided the underlying transaction is lawful and counterparties satisfy Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 verification requirements. The New Zealand dollar is a free-floating, fully deliverable currency. The RBNZ does not set exchange-rate targets, does not require export proceeds to be converted, and does not impose a surrender ratio. Profit repatriation is unrestricted, subject to withholding tax documentation and standard anti-money-laundering due diligence. The principal transactional controls are prudential and regulatory rather than FX: banks must sight commercial substance for high-value wires; transactions with sanctioned parties or unexplained ownership structures may be declined; and Overseas Investment Act 2005 consent can be required for certain foreign investments involving sensitive land, fishing quota, or significant business assets. This regulatory posture is well-suited to a global software-as-a-service treasury for AI avatar video generation, allowing multi-currency invoicing, rapid reinvestment, and clean dividend payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio exist in New Zealand. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts indefinitely or convert them at their chosen bank as commercial market conditions dictate. For bank onboarding, the underlying sales invoice or contract must be retained, and if the proceeds exceed the bank's reporting thresholds, an AML/CFT onward reporting threshold report may be filed. There is no central-bank approval, no conversion certificate, and no requirement to remit export earnings to a designated government account.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand - Te Putea Matua (RBNZ)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Tier-2 collection-only viability. Offshore EMIs and payment facilitators, including Stripe and Paddle, are not RBNZ-registered banks and cannot legally hold demand deposits or operate as a full corporate banking relationship in New Zealand. They are not legally barred from servicing this entity for SaaS card collection and subscription invoicing, but they cannot replace a licensed bank for equity escrow, payroll, PAYE tax payments, treasury hedging, or physical custody of share capital. The recommended structure is to use Stripe and Paddle as the front-end collection engines and to sweep settlement balances daily into an NZD operating account at a licensed tier-1 NZ bank. Multi-currency EMIs such as Wise or Revolut Business may be used as supplementary conversion and sub-ledger rails, but the primary treasury, capital verification, and liquidity buffers must remain with a domestic registered bank. The legal boundary is defined by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989, the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, and the AML/CFT Act 2009; any EMT without a New Zealand banking licence must clearly act as a payment intermediary, not as a deposit-taker.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Financial Reporting Act 2013, an independent audit by a licensed CPA or CA (Chartered Accountants Australia and New Zealand) is mandatory for (a) any New Zealand 'issuer' company, and (b) any 'large' non-issuer company. A non-issuer company is 'large' for a financial year if it meets any two of the following three thresholds in both the current and the immediately preceding financial year: (i) total assets at balance date exceed NZD 60 million (approximately USD 37 million); (ii) total turnover or revenue exceeds NZD 30 million (approximately USD 18.6 million); (iii) 50 or more full-time-equivalent employees at balance date. In addition, even if below these thresholds, shareholders holding at least 5% of the voting shares, the board, or Inland Revenue may lawfully require an audit. Any reporting entity under the Financial Reporting Act 2013 or a company in an industry with statutory audit requirements (e.g., financial services, insurance) is also subject to mandatory audit.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% NRWT on all dividends paid by an NZ-resident company to a non-resident shareholder, unless a DTA reduces the rate. Fully imputed dividends may, in certain circumstances, be subject to a lower rate or to the approved issuer levy alternative, but a payment to a treaty-shopping channel without a proper certificate is assessed at 30%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a standard 31 March balance date, the IR4 return must be filed on or before 31 March of the following income year unless a valid extension has been granted. Provisional tax installments are ordinarily due on 28 August, 15 January and 7 May, and terminal tax is payable at the same filing due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 in cross-border related-party transactions (aggregate consideration) triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation in the form of Master File and Local File under IRD's enforcement practice.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no separate Economic Substance Regulations register, but a New Zealand-resident company seeking treaty benefits or using a parent company in a low-tax jurisdiction must demonstrate core income-generating activities (CIGA) in New Zealand--shareholder discipline, board control, product research, AI model development, and substantial staff presence. A paper-only shell will be disregarded and its transactions recharacterised under the general anti-avoidance rule and anti-conduit principles.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer pricing / corporate tax evasion

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner will invoke the arm's length adjustment under the Income Tax Act 2007 (Subpart GC, ss GC 13-GC 15) and reassess taxable income at 28%. The resulting shortfall triggers a 150% evasion shortfall penalty under Tax Administration Act 1994 s 141C, use-of-money interest under s 120, and potential prosecution under s 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994, punishable by imprisonment for up to 5 years and/or a fine up to NZD 50,000 (individual) or NZD 250,000 (corporate).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve the underpriced cross-border sale or destroy TP evidence become personally liable for the company's unpaid tax; the Crown may pierce the corporate veil and obtain a civil restitution order. The director may be sued for the full tax shortfall, disqualified from holding office under the Companies Act 1993, prohibited from leaving New Zealand by a departure prohibition order until the tax debt is settled, and, on conviction, sentenced to imprisonment as a person concerned in the company's management.
Treaty abuse / sham corporate distribution

Statutory sanction: The IRD will disallow treaty relief and assess NRWT at 30% on the full dividend, with the NZ payer held directly liable for the unrecovered withheld tax. The company will incur a shortfall penalty of 100% under s 141B (or 150% under s 141C if deliberate), interest, and potential criminal fines. The arrangement, if artificial, is void as a sham and the payments are recharacterised as assessable income of the shareholder in NZ.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign dividend declarations without ensuring NRWT compliance are jointly and severally liable for the withheld tax and any penalties; the veil will be pierced to enforce personal asset restitution. They may be served with departure prohibition orders, banned from acting as directors, ordered to pay compensation under the Companies Act 1993, and prosecuted for assisting in tax evasion, carrying a maximum prison term of 5 years.
Economic substance / AML and beneficial ownership fraud

Statutory sanction: The R&D tax credit is denied (Tax Administration Act 1994 s 141A/141B penalties up to 100%), the zero-income return is reassessed, and the AML/CFT Act 2009 breach carries civil penalties up to NZD 2 million for continued non-compliance. The company may be removed from the New Zealand Companies Register, and ring-fenced profits are recharacterised as taxable in the hands of the true controller.

Director personal liability: Directors/shareholders who mask ownership or permit the company to operate as an empty conduit face director disqualification under Companies Act 1993 ss 382-385, personal liability for reckless trading under s 383, anti-money-laundering criminal penalties including imprisonment up to 7 years, travel bans, restraint orders over foreign assets, and forced repatriation of funds under the reciprocal tax recovery measures.
