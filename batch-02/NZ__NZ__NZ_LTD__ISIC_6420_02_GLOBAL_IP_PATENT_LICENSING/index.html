UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: The principal framework is the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, including the customer due diligence, beneficial ownership, enhanced due diligence, politically exposed person, record keeping, and suspicious activity provisions; the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Regulations 2011; the Companies Act 1993, including company registration, director, shareholder, registered office, and company-record requirements; the Financial Transactions Reporting Act 1996 where applicable; the Terrorism Suppression Act 2002; the United Nations Act 1946; the Russia Sanctions Act 2022; and regulations and orders made under the New Zealand sanctions framework. Beneficial ownership must nevertheless be identified and verified by reporting entities and other regulated counterparties, and ownership and control information must be maintained in company records and made available to competent authorities where legally required. There is no general statutory UBO filing decree imposing a universal 25 percent filing threshold on all New Zealand companies.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A New Zealand company must maintain a registered office and an address for service in New Zealand and must comply with the Companies Act 1993 record-access and availability requirements. A commercial virtual office may be used for an intellectual property holding and cross-border licensing company if it is a genuine physical location, is authorized for registered-office use, can receive official notices, maintains required company records or provides lawful access to them, and is staffed or accessible in accordance with applicable requirements. A mere post office box, mail drop, accommodation address, or nominee address without genuine access and record-control capability is unsuitable. The company should also maintain a New Zealand resident director or otherwise satisfy the statutory director-residency rule, unless the director qualifies under the Australia-based exception.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Companies, Companies Office, Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), Level 9, 33 Bowen Street, Wellington 6011, New Zealand; electronic portal: New Zealand Companies Register (companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz)

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), ss 5 (definition of company), 10-16 (incorporation by registration, certificate of incorporation, legal personality), 17-23 (constitution and company name), 24-33 (shares and shareholder rights), 83-89 (directors and officers), 128-129 (registered office), 151-152 (director residency and consent to act), 200-205 (company records); Companies (Registration) Regulations 1994; Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 (exemptions for non-issuer proprietary companies); Income Tax Act 2007 and Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for post-incorporation revenue registrations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form 1 — Application for Incorporation of a Company (prescribed by the Companies Act 1993 and the Companies (Registration) Regulations 1994), filed electronically together with Form 2 — Consent to Act as Director for each director.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

New Zealand has no statutory minimum paid-up capital. A limited company may be incorporated with one or more ordinary shares of no par value (Companies Act 1993, ss 24-25). No escrow account or capital deposit is required for a non-banking, non-licensed intellectual-property holding company. The board must record the consideration received for each allotted share in the share register (ss 27, 29-31); unpaid share capital, if any, is enforceable only as a debt of the shareholder and does not affect the Registrar's certificate.

Funding & unlock path: For a virtual registered office or shell IP HoldCo, no civil-defence clearance, fire-safety certificate, or workplace licence is required. If a New Zealand office with personnel is subsequently opened, the following clearances must be obtained from the territorial authority, Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ), and WorkSafe New Zealand under the Building Act 2004, the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017, and the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015: (i) building consent or certificate of acceptance for any fit-out; (ii) compliance schedule and annual building warrant of fitness (BWOF) covering means of escape, fire alarms, emergency lighting, and smoke control; (iii) earthquake-prone building (EPB) compliance notice under the Building (Earthquake-prone Buildings) Amendment Act 2016, where applicable; and (iv) a documented workplace health-and-safety system with a hazard register, worker engagement procedures, first-aid facilities, and, on any notifiable incident, notification to WorkSafe New Zealand within 24 hours.

Paid-in capital rule: New Zealand has no statutory minimum paid-up capital. A limited company may be incorporated with one or more ordinary shares of no par value (Companies Act 1993, ss 24-25). No escrow account or capital deposit is required for a non-banking, non-licensed intellectual-property holding company. The board must record the consideration received for each allotted share in the share register (ss 27, 29-31); unpaid share capital, if any, is enforceable only as a debt of the shareholder and does not affect the Registrar's certificate.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable. No industrial licensing board has jurisdiction over a non-manufacturing, non-asset-holding intellectual-property licensing company operating under ISIC 6420 / NAICS 533110. Should physical premises or a technical laboratory be established in the future, the competent authorities would be the relevant Territorial Authority (district or city council) for building consents under the Building Act 2004, and WorkSafe New Zealand for workplace health and safety jurisdiction under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015.

Category: No operating permit, industrial licence, or manufacturing authorisation is required for the ownership, registration, renewal, enforcement, and cross-border sub-licensing of patents and trademarks. The company may lawfully commence its licensing activities immediately upon issue of the Certificate of Incorporation. If the company later employs workers, it must comply with the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 and, only if it undertakes prescribed hazardous industrial activities (none are within the current scope), apply for a high-hazard facility licence from WorkSafe New Zealand.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD; Te Tari Taake) — online portal 'myIR' (ird.govt.nz), integrated with the New Zealand Companies Office and New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) registry for entity and tax registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned NZ Limited Company must first be incorporated with the Registrar of Companies (Companies Office) and obtain an NZBN. The company must then apply to the IRD for an IRD number using the IR596 form (or online via myIR), providing the NZBN, certificate of incorporation, registered office address, director/shareholder details, and a declaration of anticipated New Zealand taxable activity. Where the company will make taxable supplies exceeding NZ$60,000 in a 12-month period, it must simultaneously register for GST (Form IR356). Where it will pay royalties to non-residents, it should register separately for Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) deduction obligations (Form IR67). Non-resident directors or ultimate beneficial owners must also provide a New Zealand IRD number or a foreign tax identification number for applicable deemed disposition and land/supply rules.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — New Zealand resident companies are taxed on worldwide income at 28% corporate rate; however, a close-held NZ company (five or fewer owners, all being individuals, eligible trustees, or other look-through companies) may elect pass-through look-through company (LTC) status; a foreign corporate parent is not an eligible LTC owner, so this structure defaults to entity-level taxation. Territorial scope: worldwide income for tax-resident companies; non-resident companies are taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income (including royalties for the use of IP in New Zealand). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — New Zealand resident companies are taxed on worldwide income at 28% corporate rate; however, a close-held NZ company (five or fewer owners, all being individuals, eligible trustees, or other look-through companies) may elect pass-through look-through company (LTC) status; a foreign corporate parent is not an eligible LTC owner, so this structure defaults to entity-level taxation. Territorial scope: worldwide income for tax-resident companies; non-resident companies are taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income (including royalties for the use of IP in New Zealand). No tax-exempt zone applies to a standard NZ Limited Company.

Available election forms

Look-Through Company (LTC) election under sections HB 1–HB 4 of the Income Tax Act 2007 (available only to close-held companies with eligible owners); Qualifying Company (QC) election (grandfathered and closed to new elections); hybrid and branch mismatch elections under the OECD BEPS-aligned rules (Income Tax Act 2007, sections FH 1–FH 11) to neutralize double non-taxation; active income election for CFC attributable income; optional GST grouping elections and joint GST registration; and specific zero-rating elections for cross-border supplies. There is no US-style 'check-the-box' regime; entity classification is determined by New Zealand tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD $60,000 of taxable supplies in New Zealand in any 12-month period (GST Act 1985, s 51); non-resident suppliers of 'remote services' (including cross-border intellectual property licensing and digital services) supplied to New Zealand-resident consumers must also register where total supplies exceed NZ$60,000 in 12 months (s 54B); a separate NZ$60,000 threshold applies to physical services performed in New Zealand.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated where the supplier removes the goods from New Zealand within 28 days of the supply (or within 12 months where the goods are exported under a supply-and-install or hire/purchase arrangement). Services (including the supply, granting, or assignment of patents, trademarks, and other intellectual property rights) are zero-rated where supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the services are not supplied in connection with land in New Zealand, goods in New Zealand at the time of supply, or supplied to a non-resident GST-registered person who acquires the services for the purpose of making taxable supplies in New Zealand (and the supplier knows or ought to know that fact). Royalty income from offshore patent sub-licensing is therefore zero-rated where the licensee is a non-resident acting outside New Zealand; the supplier must hold contemporaneous documentation of the licensee's non-resident status.

  • Customs Export Declaration (New Zealand Customs Service 'Trade Single Window') and approved export entry confirmation
  • Signed license/sub-license agreement and royalty calculation working papers
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or other shipping/transport evidence of physical export (for goods)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/telex credit confirmation evidencing receipt of the foreign-currency royalty payment
  • Certificate of Tax Residence or equivalent evidence of licensee's non-resident status
  • Delivery and service performance records evidencing that the IP is exploited offshore

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates on the Peppol framework (NZ e-Invoicing network) coordinated by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE). IRD's 'myIR' and Business Transformation systems accept machine-readable GST returns and electronic records; taxpayers must retain sufficient records for audit but no mandatory government pre-clearance API clearance of invoices is required.

IRD and New Zealand Customs may disallow zero-rating and claw back GST (plus use-of-money interest) where goods are not exported within the statutory window or where services are in fact enjoyed or used in New Zealand, or where the supplier failed to obtain sufficient evidence of export or non-residence. Shortfall penalties apply under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 20% for taking an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion/fraud with a false or misleading GST return. Fraudulent or abusive zero-rating claims are subject to no time-bar and criminal prosecution; bona fide errors are generally time-barred after four years from the end of the relevant return period. Cross-border royalty structures also face transfer-pricing adjustment, non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) recharacterization, and the hybrid mismatch rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both the capital and current accounts. New Zealand maintains no exchange control regulations, no central bank approval requirement for inward or outward remittances, no restrictions on profit repatriation, and no differentiated treatment for capital vs current account transactions. Dividends, royalties, interest, and intercompany loan repayments may be remitted freely from New Zealand, subject only to standard documentary verification by the bank and statutory tax deduction obligations (Non-Resident Withholding Tax on payments to non-residents). The Overseas Investment Act 2005 requires consent for acquisitions of significant business assets (generally NZD 100 million or above), sensitive land, or fishing quota; ordinary global IP patent-licensing royalty flows and standard treasury operations are not notifiable unless an equity or asset acquisition of that magnitude is structured.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline exist in New Zealand. Royalty and licensing receipts earned by a New Zealand entity from global IP patent exploitation may be retained in foreign currency in onshore foreign-currency accounts, converted to NZD at the entity's discretion, or kept offshore with licensed banking partners. The Reserve Bank does not require any reporting or escrow of trade or royalty receipts.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), constituted under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 2021 as the prudential regulator and monetary authority; foreign exchange administration is decentralized and the Reserve Bank does not operate exchange-control machinery. The Overseas Investment Office (OIO), operating under the Overseas Investment Act 2005, screens foreign investment in sensitive New Zealand assets, and the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) supervises market conduct, while the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) and the Reserve Bank jointly administer the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 regime for reporting entities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for cross-border payment facilitation, Low for full local clearing replacement. New Zealand does not maintain a standalone Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing category; offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business, etc.) operate as non-bank payment service providers and must partner with an RBNZ-registered bank to access local NZD settlement via the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS). An offshore EMI may lawfully provide multi-currency collection accounts (virtual IBANs) for inbound royalty receipts and execute onward SWIFT disbursements, but the entity should register on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 if services are offered to New Zealand-based persons. For capital-contribution escrow integrity, audit trail, and final settlement in NZD, a direct Tier-1 domestic bank relationship is strongly preferred; EMIs should be used only as an ancillary liquidity-collection rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections and intercompany shareholder loans are routed as SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202/MT202 COV (large-value bank-to-bank transfer) from the parent's or affiliate's bank to the NZ receiving bank's SWIFT BIC. The payment must carry a capital-account purpose code, generally 'EQUI' (Equity Investment) for subscription proceeds or 'INTR'/'LOAN' (Intercompany Loan), accompanied by a strict narrative referencing the board resolution date and the executed subscription or intercompany loan agreement. The bank's verification package requires the certified board resolution of NZ Ltd, an executed share subscription or loan agreement compliant with the Companies Act 1993 sections on financial assistance and capital maintenance, an updated share register, the NZBN and IRD number, and a Source of Funds declaration. Inbound funds are parked in the Capital Escrow Account until full documentary compliance is confirmed, after which they are released to the Operational Account; all clearings finalize through the RBNZ-licensed bank's settlement on the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS).Outbound royalty settlements and trade disbursements are cleared primarily via SWIFT MT103 across the NZ bank's global correspondent network (typically US Dollar clearance through CHIPS/Fedwire New York and Euro clearance through TARGET2 Frankfurt). Each MT103 must reference the governing license agreement and indicate the Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) treatment; where the payee is a non-resident licensor without a treaty-reduced rate, the bank applies NRWT under the Income Tax Act 2007 and remits it to Inland Revenue, and may accept an IRD 902 certificate from the withholding agent for rate confirmation. For structured trade settlements, an Irrevocable Confirmed Commercial Letter of Credit issued through the bank's trade-finance desk (tenor typically 30 to 180 days) is available, requiring presentation of the commercial invoice, an executed sub-license utilization certificate, and any documentary evidence specified by the underlying licensing agreement; alternative rails include virtual-IBAN facilitation through licensed EMIs for low-value recurring royalties.Deliverable Outright FX Forwards (NZD versus USD, EUR, GBP, AUD, JPY) up to 36 months under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or the bank's master FX trading terms, used for conversion-rate certainty on committed royalty flows.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CIRS) for matched-maturity hedging of intercompany loans and long-dated IP acquisition consideration.European-style FX Options (vanilla put/call structures) for downside protection on large, one-off lump-sum royalty receipts while preserving upside participation.FX Swaps and Tom-Next (Overnight) Rollovers for treasury liquidity management across time zones.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) permitted only on an exceptional, documented basis where the counterparty currency is non-deliverable due to sanctions or limited convertibility; NDFs are not used for NZD pairs because the NZD is fully deliverable and freely convertible.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium for cross-border payment facilitation, Low for full local clearing replacement. New Zealand does not maintain a standalone Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing category; offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business, etc.) operate as non-bank payment service providers and must partner with an RBNZ-registered bank to access local NZD settlement via the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS). An offshore EMI may lawfully provide multi-currency collection accounts (virtual IBANs) for inbound royalty receipts and execute onward SWIFT disbursements, but the entity should register on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 if services are offered to New Zealand-based persons. For capital-contribution escrow integrity, audit trail, and final settlement in NZD, a direct Tier-1 domestic bank relationship is strongly preferred; EMIs should be used only as an ancillary liquidity-collection rail.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder not carrying on business in New Zealand. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million in aggregate cross-border related-party revenue or expenses triggers mandatory transfer pricing documentation and an explicit IR4 disclosure; OECD-aligned Master File and Local File are expected for MNE groups, and Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory where consolidated group revenue exceeds NZD 1.3 billion (approximately EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no codified Economic Substance Regulations in the UAE/GCC sense. However, the general anti-avoidance rule (Income Tax Act 2007, s BG 1), the corporate residence rules (s YD 2), imputation continuity requirements, and the transfer pricing recharacterisation power (s GC 13) require genuine commercial substance, local board control, real IP management functions, and economic activity in New Zealand. An IP HoldCo with no staff, premises, or substantive decision-making is vulnerable to recharacterisation, denial of deductions, and penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall under s 141E of the Tax Administration Act 1994; criminal conviction liable to a fine of up to NZD 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years; unpaid tax, use-of-money interest, and penalties remain collectable.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in evasion face joint and several liability for the company's unpaid tax where a corporate sham is established; personal liability for unpaid RWT/NRWT and related penalties under s 149A; Departure Prohibition Orders under s 41C prevent directors from leaving New Zealand; imprisonment of up to 5 years may attach to individual officers.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement and GAAR

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may cancel the tax advantage under s BG 1/s GB 1, reconstruct the arrangement, reallocate income at arm's length under s GC 13, deny foreign tax credits, impose shortfall penalties of 20% to 150% under s 141, and assess interest from the original payment date.

Director personal liability: Directors aware of the sham may be personally liable for company tax defaults under s 149A; asset preservation and Departure Prohibition Orders may issue; directors face disqualification under the Companies Act 1993 for fraudulent conduct and personal civil claims for restitution by creditors, including Inland Revenue.
AML/CFT and Money Laundering Exposure

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under the AML/CFT Act 2009 of up to NZD 2,000,000 per contravention for reporting entities; criminal money laundering liability under s 243 of the Crimes Act 1961 with imprisonment for up to 7 years; asset forfeiture orders may be made.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and directors may be prosecuted individually; personal fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years may be imposed on individuals involved in laundering; reckless directors can be sued for the financial loss suffered by the company or affected third parties, and banking relationships will be terminated with cross-border blacklisting consequences.
NRWT Default and Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the disguised distribution as a dividend under s GB 1; NRWT assessed at 30% on the recharacterised amount; shortfall penalties for evasion of up to 150%; use-of-money interest; cancellation of imputation credits if the imputation account is in deficit, imposing a further 28% ICA charge on the NZ company.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to take reasonable steps to ensure the company deducts and pays NRWT are personally liable under s 149A; Departure Prohibition Orders bar directors from leaving New Zealand until the tax is secured; deliberate non-deduction is a criminal offence carrying imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines; corporate veil-piercing permits recovery against personal assets of directors.
