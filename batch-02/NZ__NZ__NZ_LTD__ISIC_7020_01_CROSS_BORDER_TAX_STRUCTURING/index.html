Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department of New Zealand; online electronic portal: myIR Secure Online Services (myIR).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident establishing a New Zealand Limited Company must first incorporate the company with the Registrar of Companies and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) from the NZBN Register. The company must then apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number under the Tax Administration Act 1994, submitting the prescribed non-individual application via myIR or paper lodgement, together with certified certificate of incorporation, constitution, registered office address, directors and ultimate beneficial owner details, and intended taxable activities. Where a non-resident foreign entity operates directly in New Zealand without a New Zealand incorporated subsidiary, the foreign entity must apply directly to Inland Revenue for an IRD number if it derives New Zealand-sourced income or is required to register for GST. Inland Revenue issues the IRD number, after which the entity must separately determine GST registration obligations and register for GST through myIR if the NZD 60,000 threshold is met or exceeded, or if voluntary registration is desired.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box entity classification system. An eligible close-held company may elect look-through company (LTC) treatment under the Income Tax Act 2007, subject to shareholder and eligibility restrictions, causing tax transparency; otherwise the New Zealand Limited Company is taxed at entity level. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act 2007 apply to counter cross-border deduction and classification mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 annual taxable supplies turnover; registration is mandatory once the supplier has carried on a taxable activity and the value of supplies exceeds or is likely to exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, and must be completed within 21 days of becoming liable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from New Zealand qualify for 0% GST under section 11(1)(a) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when the supplier ships the goods to a destination outside New Zealand and exports them within 28 days of supply. Services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of performance, including cross-border tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory services, may be zero-rated where the services are not performed in New Zealand or are not directly connected with land or goods in New Zealand; the supplier must retain written evidence of the recipient's non-resident status and the offshore delivery of the service. For both goods and services, substantial commercial reality must exist, and proceeds must be repatriated in accordance with New Zealand foreign exchange requirements.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) lodged with the New Zealand Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent air waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-currency credit advice evidencing FX repatriation from the offshore customer
  • Zero-rated tax invoice/commercial invoice identifying the supply, recipient, and 0% GST treatment
  • Packing list and commercial invoice matching the exported goods
  • Service contract and engagement records evidencing offshore recipient and place of supply for consultancy services

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or anti-fraud digital stamping. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR; e-invoicing following the Peppol framework is voluntary. Tax invoices must contain the prescribed information and be issued within 28 days of the supply, and zero-rated export invoices must be supported by export and foreign-currency payment evidence retained for Inland Revenue audit.

Claims of 0% GST export treatment are scrutinized by Inland Revenue and the New Zealand Customs Service. If the exporter cannot produce the required evidence, or if the arrangement is found to be artificial or fraudulent, the supply is recharacterized as standard-rated at 15% GST. Inland Revenue may assess arrears, impose late payment interest and late filing penalties, and apply shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994, including a 100% penalty for evasion or an abusive tax position; intentional false statements to Customs or Inland Revenue may also expose directors to criminal liability under the Crimes Act 1961 for dishonesty.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates an open capital and current account regime. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not administer exchange controls or maintain any convertibility restriction on the New Zealand dollar. The domestic currency floats freely, and all residents and non-residents may hold, convert, and remit NZD without central approval. Export proceeds are not subject to surrender or conversion requirements; earnings can be kept in foreign currency indefinitely. Repatriation of dividends, royalties, management fees, loan interest, or invested capital is lawful and immediate, subject only to standard tax withholding mechanics and bank source-of-funds verification under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. Inward foreign direct investment does not require exchange control permission, although the Overseas Investment Act 2005 may require consent for acquisitions of sensitive land or significant business assets above statutory thresholds. Profit remittance hurdles are tax-centric rather than currency-centric: non-resident withholding tax on dividends, royalties, and interest may apply, and certain payments might trigger non-resident withholding tax or approved issuer levy obligations. In practice, treasury operations face no administrative rationing, no multiple exchange-rate pricing, and no queues, making New Zealand a highly convertible jurisdiction for corporate bank accounts and cross-border clearing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Goods or service exporters may maintain proceeds in NZD or foreign currency accounts; the only obligations are commercial record-keeping and tax filing under the Income Tax Act and GST Act. For AML purposes, the exporting entity must provide underlying invoices, bills of lading or service schedules to the paying bank when the transaction is flagged for documentary review.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers have only limited viability in New Zealand as primary bank counterparts. New Zealand's registered-bank framework under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act causes deposit-taking to be a licensed activity; a foreign EMI cannot lawfully describe itself as a bank or accept corporate deposits unless it becomes a New Zealand incorporated registered bank or obtains a non-bank deposit-taker exemption. Under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, certain non-bank money transfer businesses may register on the Financial Service Providers Register and provide payment or e-money services, but they are not eligible to offer capital escrow, trade finance, FX forwards, or ESAS clearing rails. For a low-risk tax structuring company, an offshore EMI may be used only for limited ancillary collection of small advisory fees if it has a New Zealand FSP registration and an approved dispute resolution scheme; the primary corporate treasury should remain with a tier-1 domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Because New Zealand has no exchange control permit, inbound equity can be routed as a direct SWIFT MT103 transfer to the entity's NZD bank account with the payment field annotated as a capital injection or share issue. The bank will perform standard KYC and source-of-funds verification, and the company's auditor or a Chartered Accountant can issue a paid-up capital certificate for corporate records and the Companies Register annual return. If the overseas investor acquires 25% or more of shares and the business owns significant business assets or sensitive land, Overseas Investment Office approval must be verified before booking; for a pure advisory services entity, OIO consent is generally not required unless the aggregate acquisition exceeds statutory thresholds. No surrender requirement applies, and the NZD conversion occurs at the agreed spot FX rate.Outbound payments from the NZ entity for consultancy sub-contractors, dividends, royalties, or goods may be made by SWIFT MT103 through the domestic bank's direct clearing or correspondent network. Large NZD interbank payments settle in real time via the Exchange Settlement Account System maintained by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand. For physical goods trades, beneficiaries can present a Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the NZ bank or use Documentary Collections; these instruments require shipping documents such as bills of lading, commercial invoices, and certificates of origin. USD 18,000,000), or 50 or more full-time equivalent employees; (ii) the company is an 'issuer' under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, such as an NZX-listed entity, registered bank or licensed insurer; (iii) the company is an overseas-owned reporting entity (more than 25% overseas-owned) that does not qualify for the small-company exemption and therefore must file financial statements and an auditor's report with the Registrar; (iv) the company's shareholders or directors have appointed an auditor under the Companies Act 1993 ss 198-199, which overrides any statutory exemption; or (v) the company is subject to a financial reporting requirement imposed by the External Reporting Board or is a consolidated subsidiary of a parent required to prepare audited group accounts. A company that falls below all quantitative thresholds and is not otherwise disqualifying may still require an audit if its constitution or shareholders' agreement requires one.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File documentation is expected when the aggregate New Zealand-side cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZD 3,000,000, or when the group is otherwise of significant compliance risk. Country-by-Country Report filing is separately required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least NZD 1,185,000,000.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain separate Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to Caribbean offshore regimes. However, New Zealand's general anti-avoidance rule, sham transaction doctrine, controlled foreign company rules, and the OECD substance requirements applicable to treaty benefits demand economic substance in New Zealand. A company engaged purely as a conduit or with no real personnel, premises, commercial decision-making or risk-bearing activity will be disregarded or have transactions reconstructed, and cross-border treaty protection will be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the Crimes Act 1961, rendering the company liable to a fine of up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment of the involved officers for up to five years, in addition to a 150% accuracy shortfall penalty and civil interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in evasive conduct face personal liability for civil penalties, restitution of the evaded tax, border travel bans pending settlement, and imprisonment. Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the company is used as a shield for deliberate tax fraud.
Shell Company and Sham Structuring

Statutory sanction: Application of New Zealand's general anti-avoidance rule in the Income Tax Act 2007 permits Inland Revenue to reconstruct the arrangement, deny the claimed treaty benefit, and impose a 40% or 100% shortfall penalty on the resulting tax avoidance. The entire structure may be voided as a sham at common law.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or execute a sham transaction can be held personally liable for the company's reconstructed tax position and ordered to pay damages for reckless trading under the Companies Act 1993. Directors may face statutory bankruptcy, prohibition from managing companies, and personal extradition or travel restraint where fraud is established.
Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a body corporate may be subject to civil penalties of up to NZD 2,000,000 for each breach and criminal fines for egregious non-compliance, together with compliance orders, asset freezing, and disqualification from financial services activity.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly participates in or fails to prevent an AML/CFT breach is personally liable for a substantial portion of the civil penalty and may be imprisoned for a term of up to two years. Directors can also be personally barred from managing any financial reporting entity and subject to compromised asset recovery orders.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may reprice all related-party transactions, adjust the New Zealand taxable income, apply interest, and impose shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall where the position is taken without reasonable care or was intentionally concealed. The Commissioner is also entitled to draw adverse inferences from missing transfer pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for the transfer pricing policy and documentation process may be personally liable for the resulting shortfall penalties where they grossly neglect their duties or sign defective tax returns. The corporate veil may be pierced to recover unpaid tax where the entity has been used as a mere alter ego for the non-resident shareholder.
