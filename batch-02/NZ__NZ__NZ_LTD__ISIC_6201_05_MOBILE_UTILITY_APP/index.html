Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand; electronic portal: myIR (secure online services)

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident company (incorporated outside New Zealand) carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand, the procedure to obtain an IRD number is: (1) Complete the Inland Revenue form IR596 'Non-resident company IRD number application' and submit it via myIR or by post. (2) Provide supporting documentation including the certificate of incorporation, proof of identity for directors, and details of the New Zealand business activity. (3) Upon approval, Inland Revenue issues an IRD number. For a New Zealand incorporated company (as in this case), the IRD number is automatically issued upon incorporation with the Companies Office. For GST registration, the company must register if its taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period or if it wishes to claim GST credits; registration is completed via the myIR portal or by submitting the GST registration form (IR742).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a freely convertible currency under a floating exchange rate regime. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both current and capital account transactions, with no foreign exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on the repatriation of profits or capital. The current account is open for trade in goods and services, income, and transfers, while the capital account permits foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, and outward investment without exchange control approval. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not require any form of central bank approval for foreign exchange transactions, and authorised dealers (registered banks) may conduct FX operations freely. Resident companies may retain export proceeds in foreign currency accounts or convert them at market rates without mandatory conversion. Profit remittances are subject only to standard corporate tax compliance, non-resident withholding tax rules, and anti-money laundering obligations. The primary regulatory oversight for financial institutions is the Reserve Bank under the Banking Supervision Handbook, in conjunction with the Financial Markets Authority and the Department of Internal Affairs for AML/CFT. Overall, there are no practical hurdles to convertibility or remittances for a compliant New Zealand limited company.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender requirements for export proceeds in New Zealand. Exporters are free to hold, use, or convert foreign currency receipts as they see fit, subject only to standard banking KYC/AML and reporting obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. New Zealand does not explicitly prohibit the use of offshore digital EMI service providers for inbound/outbound payments, but a New Zealand resident company with a GST number and Companies Office registration will face significant impediments with EMIs that do not hold a New Zealand banking license or are not registered as a financial service provider under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008. Most reputable offshore EMIs classify New Zealand as a supported jurisdiction and can open multi-currency accounts, subject to KYC. However, EMIs cannot provide a New Zealand domestic bank account in NZD with a local bank code, cannot issue direct debit or credit clearing through the Domestic Settlement and Clearing System (ESAS), and do not have access to the Payments NZ clearing rails. For treasury purposes, an EMI is viable only as an auxiliary collection or receivables conduit, not as a primary corporate banking relationship. Statutory audit and tax compliance require a physical relationship with a New Zealand registered bank for GST and income tax payment channels. Therefore, the viability rating is Low for primary treasury management, and Medium for supplementary cross-border payment routing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound equity injections should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank to the company's NZD or foreign currency account at a Tier-1 New Zealand bank. The payment must include a structured reference with the purpose description 'Share Subscription' or 'Equity Contribution' and, where relevant, the New Zealand Companies Office entity number. For funds above NZD 50,000 equivalent, the receiving bank will apply standard AML/Counter-Financing-of-Terrorism checks and may request the share allotment resolution. Where the investor holds more than 25% of shares, the Overseas Investment Office (OIO) screening is triggered only if the company owns sensitive assets or constitutes a significant business; a mobile utility app without land or sensitive assets generally does not require OIO consent. Capital verification is completed using the share allotment form filed with the Companies Office and a bank receipt certificate, which the auditor uses to certify paid-up capital. If the injection originates from a tax-haven jurisdiction, enhanced due diligence and a legal opinion on source of wealth are required.Outbound trade disbursements for a digital utility app consist primarily of third-party cloud hosting fees, software licensing, contractor payments, and developer remuneration. The standard clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for international supplier payments, while New Zealand Domestic Settlement and Clearing System (ESAS) is used for local payments. For higher-value infrastructure contracts with overseas vendors, a Commercial Letter of Credit may be used, although it is uncommon for SaaS or digital services due to the absence of physical goods and transport documents. Documentary collections are generally not applicable. For recurring disbursements such as AWS or Google Cloud invoices, the preferred method is automated clearing house (ACH) through the bank's online portal. All outbound payments require a valid purpose code under the Statistics New Zealand overseas foreign exchange transaction reporting guidelines and should be supported by supplier invoices, service agreements, and GST tax invoices where applicable.Plain-vanilla deliverable forward foreign exchange contracts with a registered New Zealand bank are freely available for NZD/USD, NZD/AUD, NZD/EUR, and NZD/GBP, and are permissible for hedging underlying commercial receipts and payments without regulatory approval, provided each forward is linked to a real underlying exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on NZD are permissible only when the hedging counterparty is offshore and the NZD is the non-deliverable leg; onshore authorised dealers do not routinely quote NZD NDFs against freely convertible currencies, and for a New Zealand resident entity NDFs are not recommended unless the counterparty is constrained from trading deliverable forwards.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps) are permissible for matching the currency and timing of known cash flows, but any swap beyond one year involving a resident company should be treated as an OTC derivative requiring compliance with the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, and will likely trigger the Derivative Issuer regime if structured as a separate financial product. The prudential boundary is to execute plain-vanilla cross-currency swaps only through an authorised bank as principal under a Chief Executive Officer-approved ISDA master agreement with a credit support annex.

Turnover: A New Zealand limited company is legally required to have an independent auditor and an independent certified audit if it is a large for-profit entity: total assets exceed NZD 60 million; OR total revenue exceeds NZD 30 million; OR it has 50 or more full-time-equivalent employees; OR it is an FMC reporting entity/listed issuer; OR it is required by shareholders holding at least 5% of the voting rights under the Companies Act 1993. The test applies on a group-consolidated basis where the company is a parent. A small/mobile utility app operating below all threshold tests is audit-exempt under New Zealand law.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million aggregate cross-border related-party transactions is the practical IRD expectation for preparing contemporaneous Local File / Master File documentation; Country-by-Country Reporting threshold is NZD 1 billion consolidated group revenue; all related-party transactions, however small, must nonetheless be supported by adequate records proving an arm's length outcome.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations code, but the general anti-avoidance rule and OECD MLI treaty defences require that the New Zealand company carry on substantive activity; the core income-generating activities for the mobile utility app must include actual product strategy, software development decisions, user-acquisition management, payment processing oversight and risk-bearing in New Zealand. A passive shell nominally owning the app while the true management occurs offshore may be recharacterised, denied treaty benefits, and subjected to NRWT on hidden deemed dividends or service income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via False or Incomplete Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 s 141D imposes a 150% shortfall penalty for evasion, plus use-of-money interest; s 143A creates criminal liability with up to 5 years imprisonment and substantial fines; knowingly producing false records may also be prosecuted as false accounting or fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise, sign, or deliberately cause the false return can be jointly and severally liable for the company tax shortfall, interest, and penalties where the corporate veil is pierced or where they are parties to the evasion. Courts may disqualify directors, order civil asset restitution, and issue departure prohibition orders or travel bans against tax debtors.
Abusive Transfer Pricing and Hidden Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing provisions in Income Tax Act 2007 subpart GC require an arm's length reconstruction; thin-capitalisation rules under subpart FE limit interest deductions; Inland Revenue may recharacterise excessive payments as deemed dividends subject to 30% NRWT, plus shortfall penalties ranging from 40% to 150% where conduct is grossly careless or evasive.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute or approve artificial intercompany pricing can be personally assessed for shortfall penalties if they are involved in the tax avoidance or evasion, and may face aiding-and-abetting criminal prosecution. New Zealand courts will pierce the corporate veil when the company is used as a facade to strip taxable profits, exposing directors' personal assets and imposing travel/enforcement restrictions.
Shell Company Operation and Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure / AML Failure

Statutory sanction: Companies Act 1993 imposes director and company fines for non-compliance with registers, disclosure, and filing duties; Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 imposes substantial civil penalties and remediation orders for failures in customer due diligence, suspicious transaction reporting, and compliance programme upkeep in respect of underlying payment flows.

Director personal liability: A director who allows the company to operate as a hollow shell while misrepresenting its activity can be personally liable for misstatements and for reckless trading under Companies Act 1993 s 135; courts may order compensation, blacklist the director for enforcement purposes, issue travel bans, and impose imprisonment for false statutory declarations or money laundering-related offences. The absence of genuine economic substance will also defeat every DTA relief claim, causing 30% NRWT to apply on all outbound payments and triggering severe reputational damage.
