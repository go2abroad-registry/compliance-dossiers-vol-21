Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake), Wellington, New Zealand; online portal myIR administered at https://www.ird.govt.nz; corporate registry interface operated by the New Zealand Companies Office and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) register.

E-government filing portal

An NZ limited company is first incorporated with the New Zealand Companies Office, which issues the NZBN and automatically notifies Inland Revenue; Inland Revenue then issues the IRD number to the registered company, normally within 10–15 business days. Foreign-owned NZ Ltd entities must ensure compliance with director residency rules under the Companies Act 1993 (at least one director resident in New Zealand or, for large overseas-owned companies, an approved legal or accounting firm acting as agent), and must apply separately for GST registration through myIR if taxable supplies exceed the registration threshold, are reasonably expected to exceed the threshold, or if voluntary registration is desired. Non-resident companies not carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand but deriving New Zealand-source income must apply for an IRD number using form IR 742 (Registration of non-resident company) or equivalent myIR online protocols; the application requires certified constitutional documents, director identity evidence, and details of the New Zealand agent or representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (New Zealand resident company taxed on worldwide income at the corporate rate of 28%, unless validly elected as a look-through company); Territorial_Scope (resident companies are taxed on worldwide income with a foreign income exemption for certain active foreign business income, foreign dividends, and grey-list CFC interests); Pass_Through_Eligible (an NZ Ltd may elect look-through company status so that income, expenses, gains, losses, and GST implications flow through to shareholders in proportion to their effective look-through interests); Tax_Exempt_Zone (not applicable—no general tax-exempt zone status in New Zealand, although certain development projects or specific statutory concessions may apply separately). — An NZ limited company is first incorporated with the New Zealand Companies Office, which issues the NZBN and automatically notifies Inland Revenue; Inland Revenue then issues the IRD number to the registered company, normally within 10–15 business days. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (New Zealand resident company taxed on worldwide income at the corporate rate of 28%, unless validly elected as a look-through company); Territorial_Scope (resident companies are taxed on worldwide income with a foreign income exemption for certain active foreign business income, foreign dividends, and grey-list CFC interests); Pass_Through_Eligible (an NZ Ltd may elect look-through company status so that income, expenses, gains, losses, and GST implications flow through to shareholders in proportion to their effective look-through interests); Tax_Exempt_Zone (not applicable—no general tax-exempt zone status in New Zealand, although certain development projects or specific statutory concessions may apply separately).

Available election forms

Look-Through Company (LTC) election under the Income Tax Act 2007 (form IR 433 or via myIR, subject to shareholder eligibility, no more than five owners, and all shareholders having voting/ownership interests between 5% and 100% of the company considered on a look-through basis); grandfathered Qualifying Company (QC) election for companies that held QC status before the repeal, with a 2011 proxy basis retained for eligible legacy entities; GST grouping election under s 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 to treat two or more related companies as a single group for GST purposes; GST payment basis elections (invoice basis, payments basis, or hybrid basis, and the six-monthly filing option); and statutory hybrid mismatch anti-avoidance rules under subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007, which prevent elective neutralisation of hybrid financial instruments and do not provide taxpayer election mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in taxable supplies (excluding exempt supplies and GST) in any 12-month period for suppliers of goods and services within New Zealand; a NZD 60,000 threshold similarly applies to remote services and digital suppliers above the threshold who must register under the non-resident remote services rules; once registered, GST is payable on taxable supplies and input tax credits are recoverable.

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping by Inland Revenue. The official New Zealand e-Invoicing network (Peppol-based, operated through accredited access points) is voluntary and available to GST-registered businesses. GST returns and payments are submitted electronically through myIR without an API-based transactional clearance requirement; all suppliers must maintain sufficient source documents to substantiate GST positions. No fiscal digital stamping regime is in force; however, IRD retains the power to require digital record production and access under the Tax Administration Act 1994, and records must be retained for at least 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalized and freely convertible currency regime for both current account and capital account transactions; the New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a free-floating currency with no statutory exchange control approvals, licensing, or transaction-level permissions for ordinary inwards or outwards treasury flows. Profit and dividend remittances from an NZ limited company are unrestricted under the Companies Act 1993 and the foreign exchange framework, but are subject to statutory Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) at 30% or a reduced treaty rate depending on the recipient jurisdiction, and to an anti-money-laundering verification of the underlying transaction purpose. For a corporate virtual card issuer, the genuine convertibility friction is not governmental exchange control but prudential characterisation: overnight holding of cardholder float or stored value can be re-characterised as deposit-taking under the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013 unless the funds are held with and settled through a licensed sponsor bank; all card-related intercompany funding must therefore be supported by board resolutions, signed treasury mandates, and contemporaneous purpose codes to demonstrate that funds are not disguised deposits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline, no surrender obligation, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; the Exchange Control Act 1973 has been repealed and exporters may retain foreign currency offshore indefinitely. The only administrative requirements are the periodic Balance of Payments statistical return thresholds administered through Statistics New Zealand, standard AML/CFT transaction record-keeping, and the banks internal sanctions-related screening policy. For the NZ_LTD virtual card treasury, this means inbound liquidity from the offshore parent can be structured in NZD, USD, EUR, or AUD without any Reserve Bank of New Zealand currency conversion approval, subject only to the receiving bank's internal FX policy, sanctions checks, and source-of-funds verification.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua), the apex monetary authority and prudential supervisor for banks, non-bank deposit takers, and payment system settlement infrastructure; foreign exchange administration is not separately delegated, with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) supervising conduct and disclosure and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) serving as the AML/CFT supervisor for non-bank financial institutions under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for a remote offshore EMI passporting into New Zealand; MEDIUM for a locally incorporated sponsor-bank agency structure. New Zealand has no EU-style electronic money institution (EMI) licence and no passporting regime; an entity holding customer funds or stored value must either be a registered bank or a licensed Non-Bank Deposit Taker (NBDT) under the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013, or alternatively must act purely as a program manager with all funds held at a licensed sponsor bank under a contractual trust or agency arrangement. The NZ_LTD virtual card issuer must register on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, join an approved dispute resolution scheme, and appoint a locally resident compliance officer. The Retail Payment System Act 2022 regulates access and pricing of retail card networks but does not create an EMI authorisation; therefore a foreign digital EMI lacking NZ regulatory substance, FSPR registration, and sponsor-bank backing will not be granted clearing or settlement access by tier-1 NZ banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial and subsequent equity or subordinated loan injections from the offshore holding structure should be routed as SWIFT MT103 customer transfers (or MT202 if interbank) to the NZ settlement account at the tier-1 sponsor bank, using the New Zealand Balance of Payments purpose code for direct investment equity or intercompany treasury funding; the payment must reference the legal registration number and full registered name of NZ_LTD. Funds received in NZD convert at the interbank spot rate without any Reserve Bank of New Zealand exchange-control approval. The receiving bank will require: a board resolution approving the capital call and investment purpose; certified director identification for the account authority; documentary evidence that the USD or other source currency originated from legitimate operational revenue or committed capital; and for amounts above the ordinary operational threshold, a capital-purpose letter addressing both New Zealand tax substance and screening under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 exemption for non-sensitive ordinary business securities.Card-as-a-service treasury settlement is executed daily through the sponsor bank to the network schemes using Visa Direct and Mastercard Network clearing and settlement rails, with net positions settled across the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Exchange Settlement Account System (ESIS) at end-of-day. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Transfer pricing documentation (local file/master file equivalent) is expected where cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZ$10 million per income year. Smaller transactions may still require documentation where they are high-risk or involve significant value. Inland Revenue follows OECD guidelines and may require documentation on demand.

Economic substance scope: No separate New Zealand ESR statute, but the general anti-avoidance rule (section BG 1), OECD Principal Purpose Test, and treaty beneficial ownership requirements force substantive operations. A virtual card issuer must maintain real employees, premises, decision-making, and risk management in New Zealand; otherwise income may be recharacterised and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under section 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994: fines up to NZ$250,000 for a company and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals. Evasion shortfall penalties up to 150% of tax shortfall. Unlicensed payment services offences under AML/CFT Act can attract fines up to NZ$3 million and 2 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns may be personally liable for the tax shortfall; courts may pierce the corporate veil and make civil restitution orders. Travel bans, asset freezing and imprisonment are available under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009 and Companies Act 1993.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may reallocate income and disallow deductions. Shortfall penalties of 20% to 150% of the adjustment apply. Thin capitalisation breaches cause interest deductions to be denied. Failure to provide documents on notice can result in statutory penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign false transfer pricing documentation may be jointly and severally liable; imprisonment up to 5 years for aiding tax evasion. If the company is a facade, the corporate veil can be pierced.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR and Principal Purpose Test will void the arrangement and reallocate income. Penalties up to 150% of tax shortfall; DTA relief denied. Company may be removed from Companies Register for failing to meet substance requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors behind the sham may be personally liable for corporate tax debts, disqualified from management for up to 5 years, and subject to travel bans and asset restraint orders.
AML/CFT Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to NZ$3 million for companies and NZ$300,000 for individuals plus 2 years' imprisonment per offence. Licenses may be cancelled and the business shut down. Pecuniary penalties recoverable by the FMA.

Director personal liability: Directors are individually liable under section 52 AML/CFT Act for failures to implement and maintain compliance programs. Personal pecuniary penalties up to NZ$2 million, imprisonment, and disqualification from directing financial services companies.
Unlicensed Financial Services Activity

Statutory sanction: Offences under the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013: fines up to NZ$1.5 million for a company (daily penalties apply). Under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, pecuniary penalties up to NZ$1 million. Consumers can seek restitution.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly participating in unlicensed activity are personally liable for penalties, can be disqualified for up to 5 years, and may face imprisonment up to 3 years where fraud is involved. Travel bans and asset freezing orders available.
