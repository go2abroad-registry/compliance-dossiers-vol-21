Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a limited liability company. Shares may be issued for cash or non-cash consideration as determined by the directors under sections 41 and 42 of the Companies Act 1993. No blocked escrow capital account or capital attestation certificate is required. The company must maintain a proper share register and, if no constitution is filed, the default rules of the Companies Act 1993 apply.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for civil defense fire licensing as a manufacturing operation. However, occupational clearance is mandatory: every workplace must comply with the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, include a worker participation system, first aid and emergency procedures. If ten or more workers are present, the office must have an approved evacuation scheme under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017. The building must hold a current building warrant of fitness where prescribed building systems exist, and electrical installations must meet the Electricity Act 1992 safety standards.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Tauhake Inland Revenue) - myIR online portal at https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir/

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand-incorporated limited company, the Companies Office issues an NZ Business Number (NZBN) at incorporation and Inland Revenue automatically allocates an IRD number when the company registers a New Zealand registered office. For a foreign non-resident entity not incorporated in New Zealand but carrying on a taxable activity or deriving New Zealand-sourced income, the entity must first register as an overseas company with the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993 (unless an exemption applies), obtain an NZBN, and then submit the Inland Revenue non-resident company registration application through myIR. The application requires proof of incorporation, registered office and principal place of business details, directors' personal details, New Zealand agent for service, tax residence certificate and BEPS-related self-certification, and bank account details. The IRD number is the single TIN used for income tax, GST, PAYE, and other tax types; a separate GST number is not issued. GST registration is added to the same IRD number via myIR when the entity has a GST taxable activity and exceeds the NZD 60,000 annual registration threshold, or elects voluntary registration. For an offshore non-resident supplier of remote services to New Zealand-resident consumers, the same IRD number is used to register under the GST remote-services supplier regime once the NZD 60,000 threshold is satisfied.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None comparable to a US check-the-box election; New Zealand has no check-the-box regime. Hybrid mismatch and branch mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act 2007 subpart FH apply to arrangements and are not elective. A qualifying closely held New Zealand company may elect look-through company (LTC) status under the Income Tax Act 2007 subpart HA, causing income, losses, and imputation attributes to flow through to shareholders; however, the default classification for an ordinary NZ Ltd is entity-level taxation at the 28% corporate income tax rate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; voluntary registration is available to any person carrying on a taxable activity. Offshore suppliers of remote services to New Zealand-resident consumers must register when taxable supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period (GST Act 1985 ss 51 and 54B).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11 of the GST Act 1985 when they are physically exported by the supplier within 28 days of the time of supply and the supplier holds appropriate customs export evidence. Services, including AI data-labeling and RLHF BPO services, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the services are not supplied in connection with land in New Zealand, moveable goods in New Zealand, or to a person in New Zealand. For digital and remote services, zero-rating is available where the supplier is satisfied the recipient is a non-resident business or non-resident consumer located outside New Zealand and the place of supply is outside New Zealand. Substantiation should include a written master services agreement with the offshore client, a tax invoice marked as zero-rated, evidence of the recipient's non-resident status, and service delivery logs demonstrating that the AI training/data-labeling services are performed for and used by the offshore customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry) lodged through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window - required only for physical exports; not applicable to pure service exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for any tangible goods consigned offshore
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Master services agreement and purchase order evidencing the non-resident recipient and scope of AI data-labeling services
  • Foreign-currency receipt evidence: SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice, or Wise payout transaction record; New Zealand does not issue Inward Remittance Certificates (FIRC)
  • Service delivery and use evidence: IP logs, task completion records, monthly service reports, and records demonstrating that the services were supplied to and consumed by the overseas client

New Zealand does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance system, does not mandate API-based preapproval of invoices, and does not require anti-fraud digital stamping. Tax invoices must contain the particulars required by sections 24 and 24B of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, including seller name, address and GST number, buyer name and address for supplies over NZD 1,000, invoice date, description of goods or services, quantity, price, GST-exclusive and GST-inclusive amounts, or a statement that the supply is zero-rated. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR; Peppol e-invoicing is available and voluntary in New Zealand, but it is not a tax clearance or pre-authorisation platform.

If Inland Revenue determines that a zero-rated export supply has not been substantiated, the supply is reassessed at the standard 15% GST rate on the full consideration and use-of-money interest applies from the due date of the original return. Shortfall penalties apply under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for failing to take reasonable care or taking an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and up to 150% for evasion. Criminal prosecution may also be pursued for tax evasion under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994, carrying fines and imprisonment. The standard four-year reassessment time bar is removed where fraud or evasion is established, allowing retrospective clawback beyond the usual period. Records must be retained for at least seven years under section 22 of the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a freely convertible current and capital account with no exchange control approval requirement for cross-border settlement. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand and the Financial Markets Authority do not impose foreign currency surrender, prior licensing, or repatriation approval on ordinary trading companies. A New Zealand limited company may receive FDI equity injection, open multi-currency bank accounts, convert NZD/USD/EUR in any amount, and remit dividends, royalties, service fees or principal repayments offshore without a currency commission or a Central Bank permit. The profits remittance channel is blocked only by standard prudential, sanctions, and AML/CFT filters plus IRD non-resident withholding tax and dividend imputation obligations. The practical hurdles are bank-level CDD/EDD for high-volume BPO payouts, the maintenance of beneficial ownership registers, and compliance with Overseas Investment Act screening if the activity ever involves sensitive land or significant business assets, which is unlikely for a pure data labelling BPO. Balance of payments reporting to Statistics New Zealand/RBNZ remains statistical rather than permissive. Thus convertibility is unrestricted while electronic wire handling is fully delegated to supervised banks and money service businesses.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. New Zealand has had no mandatory export proceeds surrender requirement since exchange controls were dismantled in the mid-1980s. Exporters may negotiate settlement terms freely, keep receipts in foreign currency with any registered bank, or convert to NZD at their discretion. No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies. For balance-of-payments and tax alignment, export invoices should be issued on the entity's New Zealand IRD tax file and overseas receipts reconciled monthly; cryptocurrency or quasi-cash settlement is not accepted as valid proceeds under bank policy.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% (Income Tax Act 2007, company income tax rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% statutory NRWT on gross outbound dividends paid by a New Zealand company to a non-resident shareholder (Income Tax Act 2007 section RF 8). DTA relief commonly reduces portfolio dividends to 15% and direct substantial shareholding dividends to 5% or 10% depending on the applicable treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$5,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions in an income year triggers mandatory transfer pricing documentation preparation (Local File and Master File). Multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750 million have separate CbC reporting obligations in New Zealand.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. The IRD applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in section BG1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, together with Part FV transfer pricing rules and DTA permanent establishment concepts, to require economic substance. The NZ_LTD entity must demonstrate that its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for data labeling, RLHF curation, annotation quality management, client contracting, payroll, and day-to-day decision making occur in New Zealand with real staff, premises, bank accounts, and governance records. A pure shell office passing invoices through a foreign parent is recharacterisable and subject to penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / deliberately false IR4 return

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 section 141D imposes a shortfall penalty of 150% of the tax avoided. Section 143B makes tax evasion a criminal offence carrying fines up to NZ$250,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals; body corporates may be fined up to NZ$500,000 upon conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or authorise the false return are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and shortfall penalties under TAA section 149A and Companies Act 1993 section 300. The court may pierce the corporate veil and order personal asset restitution, property seizures, IRD blacklisting, travel bans, company director disqualification, and imprisonment up to five years.
Transfer pricing manipulation / base erosion and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 Part FV (sections GC 13 to GC 24) allows the IRD to adjust income to the arm's length price, deny deductions for non-arm's length payments, and impose penalties under TAA section 141C (20%-40%) or section 141D (150%) for abusive tax positions. Failure to provide documentation when requested is a separate information-penalty offence.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's length intercompany contracts are treated as parties to the avoidance arrangement. They may face civil liability for shortfall penalties, equitable compensation for breach of fiduciary duty under Companies Act section 131, personal liability for company debts in insolvency-based transactions, and criminal liability if false transfer pricing documents are created or certified.
AML/CFT structuring and shell-company abuse

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 sections 74-78 impose civil penalties up to NZ$300,000 for bodies corporate and NZ$60,000 for individuals for record-keeping, reporting, and due-diligence failures. Proceeds of Crime Act 2009 permits confiscation of assets derived from money laundering. Income Tax Act section BG1 voids the sham arrangement for tax purposes.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the company is a mere façade. Directors face personal civil restitution, fines, director disqualification, travel bans, blacklisting, and criminal imprisonment. Knowingly structuring transactions to evade AML/CFT obligations can lead to imprisonment of up to two years; where the same conduct constitutes tax evasion, the imprisonment exposure increases to up to five years.
