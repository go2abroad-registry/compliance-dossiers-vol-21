Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a licensed legal-service entity (ISIC 6910) in New Zealand, a bare virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permissible as the sole registered office. Inland Revenue requires a physical business address for GST and income tax registration, and the New Zealand Law Society's trust-account rules require a physical office where client trust funds are administered. Banks conducting AML/CFT customer due diligence will generally decline an application if the entity uses only a virtual office or shared mailbox for a law practice. A managed physical office with an active presence, separate meeting space, and named responsible person is permitted, but must be disclosed and approved by the Law Society.

None. New Zealand law imposes no minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account rule. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake), New Zealand, with secure online portal myIR at www.ird.govt.nz; export and customs clearance is administered by New Zealand Customs Service (Te Mana Arai o Aotearoa) through the Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident corporate entity (an overseas company under Part 18 of the Companies Act 1993): (1) determine whether the entity is carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand, has New Zealand source income, or will make taxable supplies in New Zealand; (2) if it establishes a place of business or commences a taxable activity, register the overseas company with the New Zealand Companies Office and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN); (3) apply for the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) number using the prescribed non-resident company application form (IR742), either by paper application or through an authorised New Zealand tax agent lodging via myIR; (4) provide certified entity formation documents, certificate of incorporation, global register of directors and shareholders, registered office information, and details of the intended New Zealand business operations; (5) if annual taxable supplies will exceed the Goods and Services Tax (GST) registration threshold, complete the GST registration election as part of the same IRD number registration or later through myIR; (6) the same IRD number serves as the income tax TIN and the GST/VAT registration identifier; no separate VAT ID exists.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - a New Zealand limited company (Ltd) is a separate taxable entity resident in New Zealand; it is subject to New Zealand corporate income tax on worldwide income under the Income Tax Act 2007, with the New Zealand full imputation regime applying to shareholder distributions. No subnational income tax applies. — For a non-resident corporate entity (an overseas company under Part 18 of the Companies Act 1993): (1) determine whether the entity is carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand, has New Zealand source income, or will make taxable supplies in New Zealand; (2) if it establishes a place of business or commences a taxable activity, register the overseas company with the New Zealand Companies Office and obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN); (3) apply for the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) number using the prescribed non-resident company application form (IR742), either by paper application or through an authorised New Zealand tax agent lodging via myIR; (4) provide certified entity formation documents, certificate of incorporation, global register of directors and shareholders, registered office information, and details of the intended New Zealand business operations; (5) if annual taxable supplies will exceed the Goods and Services Tax (GST) registration threshold, complete the GST registration election as part of the same IRD number registration or later through myIR; (6) the same IRD number serves as the income tax TIN and the GST/VAT registration identifier; no separate VAT ID exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a New Zealand limited company (Ltd) is a separate taxable entity resident in New Zealand; it is subject to New Zealand corporate income tax on worldwide income under the Income Tax Act 2007, with the New Zealand full imputation regime applying to shareholder distributions. No subnational income tax applies.

Available election forms

None (no US-style check-the-box regime). Statutory elections available under New Zealand law include: (a) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Tax Administration Act 1994, making an eligible close-held company transparent for income tax purposes; (b) imputation company election to establish and maintain an imputation credit account; (c) hybrid mismatch rules under subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 are mandatory anti-avoidance provisions, not elective classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; registration is compulsory once the threshold is exceeded or is expected to be exceeded, including for non-resident suppliers of remote or digital services supplied to New Zealand consumers under the remote services GST regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a supply is zero-rated where the supplier exports goods from New Zealand within the statutory time frame (generally 28 days after the time of supply), or supplies services such as cross-border commercial arbitration, legal advice, and asset-tracing services to a non-resident client who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and who does not receive the services directly in connection with New Zealand land or goods physically located in New Zealand; qualifying remote and digital services supplied to non-resident customers may also be zero-rated. The supplier must hold adequate documentary proof of export and of the customer's non-resident status.

  • Export Entry (customs export declaration) lodged through New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window, together with the approved customs clearance record
  • Ocean Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill or Air Waybill / Air Cargo Manifest evidencing physical export from New Zealand and the overseas destination
  • Commercial invoice, service agreement, or professional engagement letter identifying the non-resident client and describing the cross-border legal, arbitration, or asset-tracing services
  • Bank or SWIFT payment transfer advice evidencing foreign-currency consideration, accepted as substantiation of the export or service proceeds
  • Evidence of non-residency of the customer, such as a tax residence certificate, passport, registered office record, or statutory declaration, and, for goods, packing list and inventory records

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-clearance API, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime exists in New Zealand. Inland Revenue accepts electronic records and GST returns filed through myIR; voluntary e-Invoicing is supported through the PEPPOL-based e-Invoicing network administered by the New Zealand Electronic Invoicing Board and promoted for business-to-government transactions.

New Zealand has no foreign-exchange control regime and no statutory FX repatriation deadline or Inward Remittance Certificate requirement. However, export zero-rating is audited by Inland Revenue using myIR data, New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window records, and bank/payment-rail information. If export zero-rating is not substantiated, the supply is re-assessed as taxable GST at 15%, with use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: up to 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. The Commissioner of Inland Revenue may revisit returns generally within 4 years, or within 10 years in cases of fraud or evasion, and records supporting zero-rated exports must be retained for 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised foreign exchange regime. The New Zealand dollar is freely convertible for both current-account items such as legal retainers, expert witness fees, dividends, interest, and trade payments, and for capital-account items such as equity injections, intercompany loans, and asset acquisitions. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not require exchange surrender, prior approval, or a foreign-exchange licence for ordinary commercial transactions. Profit remittance from a New Zealand Limited company is not subject to FX conversion limits; however, non-resident withholding tax and double-tax-agreement documentation will apply to dividends and interest, and the bank will require standard tax declarations. Large international transfers are captured by the AML/CFT Act 2009 and by RBNZ balance-of-payments statistical reporting, but these do not operate as controls on convertibility. The only material inbound investment screen is the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which is relevant only for sensitive land or significant business assets; a litigation chambers company holding no sensitive New Zealand assets will fall outside that screening threshold. Overall, capital and current account convertibility are unrestricted, the NZD market is deep and liquid, and regulated hedging is available onshore.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. New Zealand has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Foreign currency receipts may be retained in domestic or offshore foreign-currency accounts, converted at negotiated commercial rates, or used as the documented underlying exposure for bank-provided FX hedges. Only balance-of-payments statistical reporting obligations apply to the resident receiving bank.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. New Zealand's solicitors' trust account regime requires client funds to be held with a New Zealand registered bank or an entity expressly authorised under the Lawyers and Conveyancers Act 2006 and relevant trust account regulations. Offshore fintech EMIs are not registered banks in New Zealand and cannot legally serve as the depository for IOLTA-style client trust accounts, litigation retainers, settlement proceeds, or disbursement funds. EMIs may only be considered for ancillary operating expenses, and even then with caution because the core treasury architecture of a cross-border litigation firm must remain bank-anchored in New Zealand.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership opacity: the litigation firm is backed by an overseas discretionary trust or holding company, making the ultimate beneficial owner unclear for the bank's customer due diligence.

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity: the litigation firm is backed by an overseas discretionary trust or holding company, making the ultimate beneficial owner unclear for the bank's customer due diligence.

Defense evidence: Provide the full corporate org chart; trust deed and letter of wishes; register of beneficial owners; Companies Office certificate and NZBN extract; board resolution authorising account opening; passport copies and proof of residential address for every natural-person controller; signed UBO declaration; and a formal tax or legal opinion on the trust and the source of wealth of each effective owner.
Large inbound retainer or litigation-funding transfer from an opaque or higher-risk jurisdiction without an evidentiary link to the engagement.

Root cause: Large inbound retainer or litigation-funding transfer from an opaque or higher-risk jurisdiction without an evidentiary link to the engagement.

Defense evidence: Provide 12 months of sending-entity bank statements; executed litigation funding agreement; signed retainer or engagement letter; itemised pro-forma invoice; sworn affidavit from the director or trustee explaining source of funds and source of wealth; forensic accountant report where funds originate from prior settlements or family wealth; and written confirmation that no sanctioned jurisdiction is involved in the payment chain.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route inbound equity or retainer payments by SWIFT MT103 to the New Zealand bank account. For equity capital, instruct the sending bank to classify the credit as an equity injection into a New Zealand professional services company and use the appropriate balance-of-payments/ITRS purpose code provided by the receiving relationship manager; for legal fees and retainers, use a service-related purpose code such as legal services or professional advisory fees in SWIFT field 70. The receiving bank will require a statutory declaration, accountant's capital verification certificate, and board resolution if the injection is recorded for Companies Office or tax purposes. For FX conversion, execute the spot transaction with the New Zealand bank at the onshore NZD rate, and ensure the SWIFT reference contains the RBNZ balance-of-payments reporting code before settlement.For NZD-denominated local payments, use the ESAS high-value clearing system via the bank. For cross-border disbursements of settlement proceeds, witness fees, foreign counsel costs, or court-mandated payments, use SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary details and a reference to the judgment or settlement agreement. MT202 COV may be used for interbank transfers where the beneficiary is a bank. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are generally not the standard clearing rails for litigation service fees, but they may be acceptable in ancillary trade-dispute settlements where a standby letter of credit is lodged as security or a documentary collection is used to release shipping documents under a court-approved arrangement. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory non-resident withholding tax rate on dividends is 30%. A fully imputed dividend paid to an Australian resident may attract a 0% rate under the New Zealand-Australia DTA. Other applicable DTA rates generally range from 5% for substantial shareholdings to 15% for portfolio investors.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 aggregate cross-border associated-party transactions per income year triggers the expectation of contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation. Below this threshold, documentation is not statutorily mandatory but is strongly recommended to support arm's length pricing and penalty relief.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations code of the offshore-hub type. However, the New Zealand tax residence definition, the general anti-avoidance rule, CFC rules, and the licensing and conduct requirements of the Lawyers and Conveyancers Act 2006 impose a real economic substance test. A New Zealand litigation company must maintain physical premises, staff, governance, client files, and active decision-making functions in New Zealand. An entity lacking commercial substance may be disregarded, treated as tax transparent, denied treaty benefits, or recharacterised under Part G of the Income Tax Act 2007.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty up to 150% of the tax shortfall under section 141E of the Tax Administration Act 1994. Criminal conviction under section 143A carries imprisonment for up to 5 years and fines up to NZD 50,000 for individuals, or up to NZD 250,000 for a body corporate. Inland Revenue may also issue amended assessments, impose late payment penalties, and charge use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise or participate in evasion may be prosecuted as parties to the offending. The corporate veil is pierced in cases of fraud; courts may order restitution of diverted assets, disqualify directors for up to 10 years under the Companies Act 1994, and impose asset freezing orders and bail conditions that effectively restrict international travel.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may reprice transactions, deny interest deductions, and recharacterise excessive debt as equity under the transfer pricing and thin capitalisation rules. Shortfall penalties range from 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for an unacceptable tax position, up to 150% for evasion. Interest deductions on excessive non-resident debt may be wholly or partially disallowed.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation may be personally liable for penalties as parties to tax avoidance. If the arrangement is a sham, the corporate veil is pierced and directors may be required to make restitution. Serious cases are referred to criminal prosecution, and departing directors may be subject to travel bans while under investigation.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Abuse / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 and specific anti-avoidance provisions in Part G allow the Commissioner to cancel tax benefits, reconstruct the arrangement, and deny DTA benefits under the principal purpose test. Shortfall penalties up to 100% for abusive tax positions may apply, and loss offsets are denied unless the 49% shareholder continuity test is met.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for company tax liabilities where the shell company is used as an instrument of fraud. Liability may also arise for fraudulent trading under the Companies Act 1994. Courts may issue civil recovery orders and travel restrictions pending investigation or prosecution.
AML/CFT and IOLTA Trust Account Misuse

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 up to NZD 2 million for a reporting entity. Criminal liability for money laundering under the Crimes Act 1961 carries imprisonment up to 7 years. Professional discipline includes fine, suspension, or strike-off under the Lawyers and Conveyancers Act 2006, together with compensation orders from the Lawyers Fidelity Fund.

Director personal liability: Principals and directors who knowingly permit misuse of trust money are personally liable to repay the shortfall. The corporate veil will be pierced for theft by a person in a special relationship. Asset restraint orders and forfeiture proceedings may be brought under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009, and bail conditions may include surrender of passports, effectively imposing travel bans.
