Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Companies Act 1993 requires a registered office that is a physical address in New Zealand, open to the public and accessible for service of documents; a PO Box alone is not a registered office. A virtual office or commercial mailbox can be used only as a service address if a separate physical New Zealand business address exists and is disclosed to the Registrar and Inland Revenue. For an ISIC 6201 web-scraping and proxy API business, local New Zealand banks will generally reject a mailbox-only arrangement for account opening and will require director presence or a local office address. Inland Revenue will also treat an offshore-controlled virtual office as weak evidence of tax residence and may re-characterise income as managed from offshore, so a virtual office is not commercially advisable for this company if it seeks a New Zealand corporate identity and tax treaty protection.

None. The Companies Act 1993 imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement for a New Zealand limited company; shares may be issued for nominal or nil consideration and there is no escrow, blocked capital account, or bank statement verification requirement for incorporation. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake) - IRD - operating through the myIR Secure Online Services portal (myir.ird.govt.nz); company registration is administered by the New Zealand Companies Office with the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) register.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident limited company, the IRD number constitutes the statutory Tax Identification Number. The non-resident entity must first obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) via the Companies Office where a local registration is created, then apply to Inland Revenue through the myIR online tax registration workflow or an IRD paper application pack. The application must include the certificate of incorporation, constitution or equivalent charter documents, registered office details, director and ultimate beneficial owner identification, non-resident ownership schedule, and a declaration of the New Zealand taxable activity or offshore remote-services nexus. If the entity has no physical presence or employment connection in New Zealand, a New Zealand tax agent or legal representative is expected to be appointed to receive IRD notices and service documents. IRD will issue the IRD number and, where warranted, a GST registration number after the taxable activity nexus under the GST Act 1985 and Income Tax Act 2007 is established.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. New Zealand does not recognise a US-style check-the-box election or any general ordinary-income pass-through election; a limited liability company is mandatorily taxed as a separate entity under the Income Tax Act 2007. The base erosion and profit shifting hybrid mismatch rules in the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 apply automatically and are not election-based.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of GST taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any 12-month period; for a non-resident offshore supplier of remote services or digital products, registration is mandatory when supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period. Voluntary registration is available below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11 of the GST Act 1985, goods exported from New Zealand are zero-rated where the supplier exports the goods within 28 days of supply and retains sufficient evidence of export. Services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand are zero-rated when the services are not directly connected with New Zealand land, property, or other goods in New Zealand. Qualifying offshore digital services, including public commercial web scraping, proxy API gateways, and data pipeline services, may be zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident business outside New Zealand and the supply is not consumed or exploited in New Zealand. The supplier must substantiate the cross-border supply with customs, logistics, contractual, and foreign-exchange settlement evidence. Digital services supplied to New Zealand consumers are taxed at 15% under the offshore supplier remote-services rules and are not export zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Entry lodged through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window for physical exported goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the exporting carrier
  • Outward cargo manifest and port or airport export report
  • Commercial invoice and export or API service contract identifying the non-resident buyer and the offshore destination or consumption point
  • Bank foreign-exchange settlement or remittance credit advice evidencing actual receipt of offshore consideration

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, mandatory tax-authority API filing, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime applies in New Zealand. GST tax invoices must be issued in accordance with the GST Act 1985 and retained in records; e-invoicing may be exchanged through the voluntary Peppol interoperability framework supported by Inland Revenue. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR or accredited gateway software without transaction-level pre-clearance.

Inland Revenue may reassess the supply at 15% GST and claw back refunds if export zero-rating is not fully substantiated, goods are not exported within the statutory 28-day window, services are in fact performed in New Zealand, or the documentation evidencing export and payment is false. The Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes shortfall penalties up to 150% for evasion or fraud, with lesser penalties for failure to take reasonable care, unacceptable tax positions, and abusive tax positions; use-of-money interest also runs on unpaid GST from the original due date. Knowledge-based false refund claims or deliberate false statements in GST returns may additionally give rise to criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand administers a fully open capital and current account regime. The New Zealand dollar is freely convertible and trades without exchange control restrictions. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not require approvals for cross-border equity injections, trade receipts, dividend repatriation, or loan repayments. The Financial Markets Authority and the Reserve Bank supervise licensed institutions but do not impose FX surrender or convertibility restrictions. Banks may apply individual transaction vetting under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, sanctions screening, and tax information exchange protocols, but these do not obstruct legitimate treasury flows. Dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are taxable via non-resident withholding tax or approved issuer levy, but funds can be remitted in the currency of choice. The absence of exchange controls and the absence of an official exchange rate band create a stable setting for multi-currency treasury operations. Companies can hold foreign currency accounts, transact in any approved currency, and hedge currency risk through dealers without requiring central bank permission. The only practical hurdles relate to bank onboarding documentation, beneficial ownership disclosure, and ordinary commercial KYC, all of which can be satisfied with proper legal entity records. Overall convertibility is best assessed as unrestricted and at a low compliance burden by global standards.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement or fixed conversion ratio exists in New Zealand. All exporters may retain one hundred percent of export proceeds in licensed foreign currency accounts or convert to New Zealand dollars at market rates. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not mandate any timeline for repatriation. Exporters are nonetheless required to maintain accurate invoices and tax records for Inland Revenue, and may be sampled by Statistics New Zealand for balance of payments statistical reporting; reporting, if requested, is limited to data collection and is not a control or approval mechanism. The target entity's API subscription receipts in USD or EUR can thus be collected via Stripe or direct wire and held or converted with no mandatory FX switching window.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs are not licensed as full New Zealand banks for taking deposits; only registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 can offer core transactional or deposit accounts. Non-bank financial service providers may be registered on the New Zealand Financial Service Providers Register and provide niche payment services if licensed, but offshore digital EMIs without an approved local branch or FSP registration are largely barred from servicing local entities as their primary clearing bank. For the target entity, a fintech EMI is rated Low for primary treasury clearing because most offshore EMIs cannot issue ledger entries that satisfy New Zealand Companies Office capital verification and will not provide corporate credit, FX hedging, or LC issuance. Use an EMI only as a secondary collection gateway, such as Stripe or a regulated stablecoin on-ramp, while placing all capital inflows and outbound liquidity through a Tier 1 domestic commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border related-party transactions of NZ$5.0 million or more in an income year trigger mandatory transfer pricing documentation (Local File). A group with consolidated annual revenue of NZ$1.125 billion or more is required to prepare a Master File and Country-by-Country Report. Even below these thresholds, the New Zealand taxpayer must hold contemporaneous records sufficient to demonstrate arm's length pricing for API subscriptions, data licensing, hosted services, and intercompany management charges.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not administer a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations statute; instead, substance is evaluated through the Income Tax Act 2007 general anti-avoidance rule s BG 1, business test in s DA 1, permanent establishment attribution under double tax agreements, and the New Zealand source rules. A company holding the nominal right to web-scraping API income but lacking Core Income-Generating Activities in New Zealand, such as board meetings, employee operators, client contracting, and central risk management, will be treated as a conduit: profits will be reallocated to the jurisdiction performing the true functions, imputation credit benefits may be denied, and NRWT will be imposed on payments made to the offshore principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Sham Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1 disregards the tax avoidance arrangement; transfer pricing rules in ss GC 1-GC 20 impose an arm's length reallocation of profit. Tax Administration Act 1994 s 141(2C) imposes a 100% abusive-tax-position shortfall penalty, and s 143 imposes criminal fines of up to NZ$50,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years for deliberate evasion.

Director personal liability: Under Companies Act 1993 s 189, the court may pierce the corporate veil if the company is used for fraud or tax evasion. Directors are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the unpaid tax plus interest and penalties. Inland Revenue may obtain a travel-ban order preventing directors from leaving New Zealand until the debt is settled, and criminal conviction can disqualify the director from managing a company and trigger asset forfeiture of proceeds held in Stripe accounts or USDC wallets.
Withholding Tax Evasion and Abusive DTA Relief

Statutory sanction: NRWT is imposed under Income Tax Act 2007 ss RF 8 and RF 9 and is payable by the payer under s RNA 8. Failure to deduct results in the New Zealand company being assessed for the full NRWT, plus a 150% evasion shortfall penalty under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and late payment penalties. Making a false treaty claim is an offence punishable by imprisonment for up to 5 years and a fine of up to NZ$50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct the non-payment of NRWT are personally liable for the unremitted foreign withholding tax. Civil remedies include personal assessment, garnishment of USDC assets, and restitution of the tax shortfall. Criminal liability extends to imprisonment, travel bans, director disqualification, and forfeiture of the benefit obtained from the fraudulent treaty claim.
Anti-Money Laundering Breach and Digital Payment Anonymisation

Statutory sanction: The Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 imposes civil penalties of up to NZ$2 million per contravention on a body corporate, and directors or senior managers may be subject to individual civil penalties of up to NZ$300,000. Failure to register as an AML/CFT reporting entity is a continuing offence. If the disseminated funds are criminal property, prosecution under Crimes Act 1961 ss 243-245 for money laundering carries imprisonment for up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly or recklessly facilitates the AML breach can be held personally liable for civil penalties and for restitution of laundered funds. The corporate veil may be pierced under Companies Act 1993 s 189 where the company is used as an instrument of financial crime. The director may be subject to travel bans, seizure of digital assets, disqualification from directorship for up to 5 years, and criminal imprisonment for money laundering.
