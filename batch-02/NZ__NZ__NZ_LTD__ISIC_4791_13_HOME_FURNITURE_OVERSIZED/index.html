Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand Government, Wellington, New Zealand; online self-service portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A New Zealand incorporated limited company is assigned an IRD number as part of integrated registration with the New Zealand Companies Register. A foreign or non-resident company not already registered with Inland Revenue must apply for an IRD number using Inland Revenue form IR743 for non-individual organisations, supplying the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, and particulars of New Zealand-source income or taxable activity. The IRD number is the statutory taxpayer identification number; there is no separate GST or VAT identification number. GST registration is effected under the same IRD number either online through myIR or by paper application once the turnover threshold or voluntary registration conditions are satisfied.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No United States-style check-the-box election exists in New Zealand law. An eligible closely held company may elect look-through company (LTC) treatment under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, causing the company to be fiscally transparent and its income, deductions, and tax attributes to flow through to shareholders. GST grouping elections are available to related registered persons. Hybrid mismatch rules in subpart FM of the Income Tax Act 2007 apply to imported and structural mismatches; taxpayers may not elect out of their application except by lawful restructuring, and no elective safe harbour is available to override the statutory anti-avoidance consequences.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of gross taxable supplies made in New Zealand or to New Zealand consumers in any rolling 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services or low-value imported goods trigger the same NZD 60,000 registration threshold once their supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed that amount.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% GST export rate applies to goods that are genuinely exported from New Zealand by the supplier or supplied to a non-resident purchaser and subsequently removed from New Zealand within the permissible timeframe, with sufficient evidence. Zero-rating of services applies to supplies made to a non-resident recipient outside New Zealand at the time of performance, provided the services are not directly connected with New Zealand land or goods physically present in New Zealand; input tax credit recovery is available to the exporter, and excess credits are refundable by Inland Revenue.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export entry lodged with New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent ocean or air transport document evidencing physical export of the oversized furniture
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and/or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the offshore buyer

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital tax stamping, or continuous transaction controls. E-invoicing is voluntary and interoperable through the Peppol e-invoicing network. A GST-registered supplier may issue electronic tax invoices provided the invoice contains all legally required GST invoice details and remains accessible to the recipient; Inland Revenue does not require pre-approved or pre-stamped invoice formats.

Inland Revenue audits zero-rating claims by testing export documentation, shipping records, insurance documents, warehouse removal records, and payment flows. If the statutory export test is not met, the supply is recharacterised as standard-rated and the 15% GST is clawed back retrospectively, with use-of-money interest running from the original return date. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 range from 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or a similar offence; fraudulent claims also expose the company and its directing officers to criminal prosecution. The usual four-year assessment period is eliminated in cases of fraud or evasion, allowing unlimited retrospective assessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand has an open floating currency regime with full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. There is no general exchange control legislation requiring central bank approval for inbound capital contributions, outbound trade payments, dividend remittances, or repatriation. The New Zealand dollar is freely traded on global FX markets. Non-resident investors may hold accounts and remit profits without exchange control permits. Limited regulatory gateways apply only to sensitive land acquisitions and significant business assets under the Overseas Investment Act 2005, but ordinary treasury flows for an online furniture retail company are unrestricted. Tax clearance and AML/CFT source-of-funds verification are the operative barriers to remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no export proceeds surrender rules and no mandatory conversion timelines for export or online retail receipts. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency balances in offshore or New Zealand bank accounts, manage settlement timing at commercial discretion, and hedge currency exposure as needed. Only general tax record-keeping and AML/CFT transaction reporting obligations apply.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ), operating as New Zealand's central bank, together with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) for market conduct oversight and the New Zealand Police Financial Intelligence Unit for AML/CFT supervision under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary banking rail. New Zealand does not recognise the European Economic Area electronic money institution passport, and UK or EU EMIs cannot provide a licensed domestic New Zealand bank account. A non-bank deposit taker must hold a licence from the Reserve Bank of New Zealand under the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013. Offshore digital EMIs are useful only as supplementary card-issuing or payment-gateway rails, not as the primary settlement bank. The NZD clearing infrastructure requires participation through a locally incorporated licensed bank or a registered non-bank deposit taker.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan capital should be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from the originating bank to the New Zealand operational account. Reference the payment as 'Shareholder Capital Contribution' or 'Shareholder Loan'. No exchange control approval is required. The receiving bank will verify the payment description, the remitter's legal entity details, and the underlying source of funds. A board resolution authorising the capital injection, executed share subscription documents, and a funds flow statement should be held on file. If the capital exceeds materiality thresholds under the Overseas Investment Act, a legal assessment must confirm the transaction is not a notifiable acquisition of sensitive New Zealand business assets or land. Payment rails should use the beneficiary bank's SWIFT BIC and IBAN-equivalent New Zealand bank account number.Trade settlements for oversized furniture imports are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfers or MT202 cover payments for supplier-side nostro accounts. Commercial letters of credit may be issued as irrevocable documentary credits conforming to UCP600 when supplier financing and documentary compliance are required. For oversized freight logistics disbursements, direct wire transfers to freight forwarders and customs brokers are preferred rails. New Zealand-dollar clearing is settled through the Exchange Settlement Account System operated by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand, accessed via licensed settlement banks. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$10,000,000 in annual aggregate cross-border related-party transactions generally triggers Local File contemporaneous documentation; a Master File and Country-by-Country Report may become mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds NZ$1,125,000,000.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime analogous to UAE or Caribbean centres. Instead, the Income Tax Act 2007 and OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines require that intercompany contracts reflect actual functions performed, assets used and risks assumed. The general anti-avoidance rule under section BG1, thin capitalisation rules and transfer pricing reconstruction provisions may disregard artificial or substance-less arrangements operating in New Zealand.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994 with fines and imprisonment of up to five years; civil shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the evaded tax; use-of-money interest on outstanding tax; all assets traceable to the evasion may be subject to civil recovery orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers knowingly concerned in the evasion may be jointly and severally liable, with the corporate veil pierced in cases of fraud; criminal convictions can include imprisonment, director disqualification, and court-imposed travel restrictions while tax debts or proceeds of crime are litigated.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: IRD may apply the general anti-avoidance rule to void the arrangement, reconstruct profit to align with economic substance, and assess additional tax; penalties for abusive tax positions can reach 100% of the shortfall, plus interest. Treaty benefits may also be denied under the principal purpose test where applicable.

Director personal liability: If the company is found to be a sham or a façade, directors who directed or permitted the structure may be personally liable for the unpaid tax; civil claims may require restitution; serious cases can support criminal liability for tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may reassess taxable income using the arm's length principle, deny interest deductions, disallow transfer pricing adjustments, and impose shortfall penalties of up to 100% of the tax shortfall for abusive positions or 150% for evasion; late payment penalties and use-of-money interest also accrue.

Director personal liability: Directors approving or implementing non-arm's length arrangements without commercial justification may be personally liable for resulting tax debts under director penalty provisions; they may also face claims for breach of duty under the Companies Act 1993 for placing self-interest or group interests above the entity's creditors.
Dividend Repatriation and Withholding Tax Defaults

Statutory sanction: The NZ company is liable for all unpaid NRWT plus late payment penalties, use-of-money interest, and possible shortfall penalties up to 150% in cases of deliberate evasion; recharacterisation of loan or fee payments as dividends may trigger additional tax and penalty assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to ensure that withholding obligations are discharged can be personally liable for the unpaid amounts; Inland Revenue may issue director penalty notices and pursue civil collection against directors' personal assets.
AML/CFT and Payment-Rails Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a reporting entity that fails to implement an AML/CFT programme, report suspicious transactions, or apply customer due diligence faces civil penalties and criminal fines for body corporates; enforcement action can also lead to court orders freezing business assets and disgorgement of profits.

Director personal liability: Officers who knowingly participate in or knowingly permit AML/CFT breaches can be made personally liable and face civil penalties, criminal conviction and imprisonment; directors may also be removed from management and barred from acting as directors in future New Zealand companies.
