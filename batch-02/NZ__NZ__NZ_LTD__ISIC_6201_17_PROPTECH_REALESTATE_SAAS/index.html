Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand; online portal: myIR Secure Online Services via www.ird.govt.nz. For export physical clearance, the competent authority is the New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS), operating the Trade Single Window (TSW) for electronic customs declarations.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity, the statutory tax identifier is the New Zealand IRD number, which also serves as the GST registration number; there is no separate VAT/GST ID. The standard procurement procedure is: (1) determine whether the non-resident entity has a New Zealand tax obligation, such as carrying on a taxable activity, making supplies to New Zealand consumers exceeding the GST registration threshold, deriving New Zealand-sourced income, or being required to withhold New Zealand tax; (2) complete the Inland Revenue Department non-individual IRD number application, denoted in Inland Revenue practice as form IR596, for a company or other non-individual entity; (3) provide certified copies of the entity constitution, certificate of incorporation, directors and shareholders register, registered office address, tax residence jurisdiction, and, where the document is not in English, a certified English translation; (4) where the foreign entity has a fixed place of business or continuous business activity in New Zealand, first register the foreign company with the New Zealand Companies Office and appoint a resident New Zealand agent for service of tax documents and notices, as required by New Zealand tax administration practice; (5) submit the application and supporting documentation through myIR or, for non-resident entities without myIR access, through the prescribed Inland Revenue paper application channel; and (6) retain the IRD number confirmation in the entity's permanent tax file. The same IRD number is used for income tax, GST, withholding tax, and other New Zealand indirect tax obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A New Zealand limited liability company is a separate tax-paying entity subject to company income tax on a residency basis; a New Zealand incorporated company is resident and taxed on worldwide income, while a foreign-incorporated non-resident company is taxed only on New Zealand-sourced income unless its management is effectively in New Zealand.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box election. Qualifying closely held New Zealand companies may elect look-through company (LTC) treatment under the Income Tax Act 2007, subject to strict shareholder and eligibility conditions. There is no elective hybrid mismatch mechanism; OECD/great hybrid mismatch and anti-avoidance rules apply mandatorily where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period for taxable supplies connected with New Zealand; a non-resident supplier of remote services or digital services, including software-as-a-service platforms, is required to register for GST when the value of supplies to New Zealand residents exceeds NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, with voluntary registration available below that threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods qualifies for 0% GST when the goods are exported from New Zealand by the supplier and the export is evidenced by the New Zealand Customs export declaration and transport documentation. A supply of services, including SaaS/PropTech platform access, qualifies for 0% GST under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the service is supplied to a person who is non-resident and outside New Zealand at the time the service is performed, and the service is not directly connected with New Zealand land, New Zealand moveable property, or a natural resource. The SaaS subscriber must be a non-resident with no New Zealand place of business using the platform for New Zealand domestic enjoyment. The supplier must obtain and retain contemporaneous commercial evidence of the recipient's non-resident status, foreign business address, offshore use, and foreign payment. GST-registered zero-rated exporters may also claim input credits and receive refunds from Inland Revenue for GST incurred on business inputs attributable to those zero-rated export supplies, subject to the same documentary substantiation.

  • New Zealand Customs export declaration (Export Entry) lodged electronically through the Trade Single Window, showing exporter IRD number, goods description, value, and overseas destination
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air freight evidencing physical export from New Zealand to the foreign consignee
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the exported goods or services and the overseas buyer
  • Signed SaaS platform subscription agreement, contract, or order form identifying the non-resident customer, their foreign tax/business address, and the place of use
  • GST tax invoice issued by the supplier quoting the supplier IRD/GST number and stating that the supply is zero-rated for export under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985
  • Foreign exchange payment/remittance evidence such as a SWIFT MT103, foreign currency bank account statement, or bank credit advice; New Zealand does not issue an Inward Remittance Certificate, and the latter is not a New Zealand statutory requirement

None. New Zealand has no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory e-invoicing API validation, no anti-fraud digital stamping, and no government pre-clearance of invoices. Invoices must contain the standard GST tax invoice particulars prescribed by the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and be retained for audit. Electronic invoicing is permitted, and the voluntary PEPPOL-based New Zealand e-Invoicing network may be used; GST returns and payments are filed through the Inland Revenue myIR portal, without an API clearance mandate.

Where a zero-rated export supply is not supported by the required documentation, Inland Revenue may redetermine the supply as standard-rated, assess GST at 15%, disallow associated input tax credits, and impose use-of-money interest from the due date of the GST return. Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, shortfall penalties may apply at 20% for unacceptable tax positions, 40% for abusive tax positions, and 150% for evasion or fraudulent intent, with separate penalties for failing to take reasonable care or failing to keep sufficient records. Knowingly filing a false GST return or knowingly evading GST can lead to criminal liability under the Tax Administration Act 1994, including fines and imprisonment, and false Customs declarations may be prosecuted under the Customs and Excise Act 2018. Records supporting export zero-rating must be retained for seven years. The general reassessment time bar is four years from the date the return was filed, but no time limit applies where the shortfall arises from evasion, fraud, or insufficient disclosure, meaning retrospective clawback can occur many years after the original zero-rating position.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates under a fully liberalised and free-floating exchange rate regime. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not administer exchange controls or restrict the convertibility of the New Zealand dollar for current account or capital account transactions. The country has accepted Article VIII obligations of the International Monetary Fund, meaning there are no restrictions on payments and transfers for current international transactions, and no discriminatory currency practices. Capital inflows and outflows are freely permitted, subject only to general anti-money laundering obligations and, in limited circumstances, overseas investment screening for significant acquisitions of sensitive land or fishing quota, none of which applies to a software-as-a-service real estate technology entity. Profit remittance by foreign shareholders is not subject to FX approval or mandatory conversion requirements, provided corporate tax obligations are satisfied and standard bank compliance requests are met. Dividend distributions may be remitted in NZD or any freely accepted foreign currency through normal banking channels. There are no surrender requirements on export proceeds, and no requirement to repatriate overseas earnings. The practical compliance burden is limited to accurate purpose codes for balance of payments statistics and robust documentary trails for bank due diligence. Overall, New Zealand presents no structural obstacle for cross-border treasury operations, and the currency is fully convertible on a spot and forward basis within the domestic interbank market.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender requirements and no mandatory conversion timelines for export foreign currency receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency earnings indefinitely in external or domestic accounts and are free to convert to NZD at commercial exchange rates. The only reporting obligations are statistical data collection by the Reserve Bank and suspicious transaction reporting under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating in New Zealand must be enrolled as reporting entities under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. They are legally permitted to offer payment and e-money services, but they cannot accept deposits or conduct banking business without a registered bank licence under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. For a PropTech real estate SaaS platform, offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Stripe, or Wise can be viable for payment routing, client refunds, and low-value disbursements, but they should not be used as the primary treasury clearing house for statutory capital injections or high-value trade settlements. Domestic Tier-1 banks provide more reliable access to the New Zealand payment system, the Real-Time Gross Settlement infrastructure, and SWIFT connectivity for both NZD and foreign currency. EMIs are further constrained by their licensing jurisdictions and by New Zealand bank concentration arrangements, so treasury strategy should hold Tier-1 bank accounts as the principal operational backbone and use EMIs selectively for inbound SaaS subscription collections and outbound recurring refunds.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident shareholder injecting capital from multiple jurisdictions with opaque source-of-wealth evidence

Root cause: Non-resident shareholder injecting capital from multiple jurisdictions with opaque source-of-wealth evidence

Defense evidence: Provide a certified corporate registry extract of the ultimate parent, a 12-month original bank statement chain in the remitting entity's name, formal source-of-wealth statement signed by the beneficial owner, income tax assessment or certificate of tax residence for the funding entities, a board resolution approving the capital injection, a legal opinion confirming no adverse regulatory finding, and a sworn declaration of no third-party financing or hidden principal.
Complex beneficial ownership chain involving trusts or nominee shareholders obscuring true controllers

Root cause: Complex beneficial ownership chain involving trusts or nominee shareholders obscuring true controllers

Defense evidence: Submit an illustrated ownership and control chart, full trust deed or equivalent constitutional documents, a registered agent confirmation of the settlor, trustee, and protector identities, certificate of incorporation and good standing for every intermediate entity, results of a desktop due diligence search on each control level, and a professional legal opinion that the economic substance of the structure is not designed to evade transparency obligations.
PropTech platform receiving rental and property management funds, creating third-party payment and client-money exposure

Root cause: PropTech platform receiving rental and property management funds, creating third-party payment and client-money exposure

Defense evidence: Provide audited financial statements or a client-funds trust account certificate, a payment flow diagram distinguishing platform operating revenues from client rental monies, signed service agreements with property owners and tenants, AML/CFT compliance programme documentation, annual independent assurance report, data protection policies, and contractual clauses confirming that client funds are safeguarded and not available to creditors of the company.

Root cause: Director or ultimate beneficial owner flagged by sanctions or PEP screening with insufficient corroborating identity evidence

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating in New Zealand must be enrolled as reporting entities under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. They are legally permitted to offer payment and e-money services, but they cannot accept deposits or conduct banking business without a registered bank licence under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. For a PropTech real estate SaaS platform, offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Stripe, or Wise can be viable for payment routing, client refunds, and low-value disbursements, but they should not be used as the primary treasury clearing house for statutory capital injections or high-value trade settlements. Domestic Tier-1 banks provide more reliable access to the New Zealand payment system, the Real-Time Gross Settlement infrastructure, and SWIFT connectivity for both NZD and foreign currency. EMIs are further constrained by their licensing jurisdictions and by New Zealand bank concentration arrangements, so treasury strategy should hold Tier-1 bank accounts as the principal operational backbone and use EMIs selectively for inbound SaaS subscription collections and outbound recurring refunds.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) rate on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders is 30%. A reduced rate may be available under an applicable double tax agreement (DTA); a typical DTA rate is 15%, and for certain shareholdings in a New Zealand company the rate may be reduced to 5%. Fully imputed dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder from a New Zealand imputation credit account are exempt from NRWT if the shareholder's country of residence provides for a full credit under the DTA (or if the dividend is treated as fully imputed under New Zealand internal law).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand's transfer pricing rules follow the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. A New Zealand company that is part of a multinational group with global consolidated revenue of at least NZ$1.1 billion is required to prepare and retain a Master File and a Local File. A Local File alone is required where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions (excluding financing) exceeds NZ$2 million per year, or NZ$500,000 in respect of a related-party financing transaction. Where the annual value of related-party services reaches NZ$1 million, a contemporaneous benchmarking study must be maintained. The IRD may request these documents within 30 days of a formal written request.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a separate statutory Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime similar to captive jurisdictions. However, the Income Tax Act 2007 includes a General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, sections BG 1 and GA 1) that allows the IRD to disregard arrangements lacking commercial substance. Pure holding or shell companies with no employees or physical presence in New Zealand are at a high risk of being recharacterised. Moreover, the capitalisation rules and the New Zealand thin capitalisation rules (interest limitation at 60% debt-to-assets for non-resident owners) impose de facto substance requirements. The IRD will closely scrutinise a cross-border arrangement if the local entity does not maintain board minutes, an identifiable office, banking functions, or administrative personnel that would ordinarily be expected of a substance-based business.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Crimes Act 1961 and the Tax Administration Act 1994 can result in imprisonment for a term not exceeding 5 years, a fine up to NZ$50,000 for an individual, and for a company penalties up to NZ$100,000 plus recovery of unpaid tax with interest. The IRD may also impose civil shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall for abusive tax positions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the preparation of false returns may be personally assessed under the anti-avoidance provisions. Personal liability may extend to the full amount of unpaid taxes through the piercing of the corporate veil where the company is merely an instrument. Directors may face a civil restraint order, a travel ban, and, where the evasion scheme involves dishonest activity, imprisonment for up to 7 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The IRD may disregard the legal form of the entity, aggregate transactions between related parties, and impose additional taxes under the GAAR. Assessment can include penalties of 20% of the shortfall for failing to take reasonable care, increasing to 150% for intentional disregard. The company may also be stripped of its imputation credit balance and be denied deductions for interest or royalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell entity are exposed to personal liability for the company's unpaid tax and penalties where the company is used to perpetrate a sham or a mere facade. The High Court has the power to make a civil property recovery order against the director's assets up to the value of the tax avoided. The director may also be disqualified from managing companies under the Companies Act 1993 for a period of up to 5 years and be subject to travel restrictions.
AML/CFT Breach (Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism)

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) and the supervisor (usually the Department of Internal Affairs for non-bank financial institutions) may impose a civil penalty of up to NZ$4 million for a body corporate for each contravention, and a further penalty of up to NZ$500,000 for each subsequent contravention. A criminal conviction for money laundering can lead to a fine of up to NZ$500,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years for individuals; senior managers can be imprisoned for up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who knew or should have known of the AML/CFT breach may be personally liable for the civil penalty. The FIU may also apply for a director disqualification, freezing orders, and a pecuniary penalty order. A separate offence of knowingly or recklessly providing material assistant to money laundering carries a maximum of 14 years' imprisonment under the Crimes Act 1961.
Unpaid Dividend Withholding Tax / Unreported Distributions

Statutory sanction: The payer is liable for the unpaid NRWT amount plus a penalty of 100% of the tax shortfall (reduced to 20% if the taxpayer makes a voluntary disclosure). If the payer fails to obtain the correct certificate of residence, the full 30% statutory rate applies without reduction, and the payer cannot pass the economic burden to the recipient. Interest accrues daily on the unpaid amount.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the company's obligation to withhold tax if they were knowingly involved in the payment of a dividend without proper withholding. The director's personal assets may be attached, and the director may be prohibited from leaving New Zealand pending settlement. An order for restitution may be made against the director for the gross amount of the distribution plus interest.
