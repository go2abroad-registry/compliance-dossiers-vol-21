Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (also known as Inland Revenue), Wellington, New Zealand. Primary compliance portal: myIR secure online services available at www.ird.govt.nz. Corporate registration is administered by the New Zealand Companies Office under the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment, which issues the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). Physical export declarations are administered by the New Zealand Customs Service. No sub-national tax authorities exist because New Zealand is a unitary national tax jurisdiction.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the NZ_LTD via the New Zealand Companies Office online register to obtain the NZBN. Step 2: Apply for an IRD number as part of the incorporation process; the IRD number is the single statutory tax identification number used for income tax, GST, PAYE, and FBT. Step 3: Register for GST through myIR when taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, or elect voluntary registration; the IRD number itself serves as the GST registration number. The effective date of GST registration may be backdated to the date the threshold was exceeded. Step 4: For an overseas company carrying on business in New Zealand, first register as an overseas company under the Companies Act 1993 via the Companies Office, appoint a New Zealand resident agent for service of process, file certified constitutional documents (certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles), and then obtain an NZBN and IRD number through the Companies Office registration process. Step 5: A non-resident company with no New Zealand physical presence that supplies remote services (including digital gaming platforms, simulated social casino software, and virtual slots content) to New Zealand consumers must separately register under the remote services GST rules through myIR once annual supplies exceed NZD 60,000; no New Zealand incorporation is required for this specific GST registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A New Zealand limited liability company is a separate income tax taxpayer subject to 28% corporate income tax on worldwide income, with foreign tax credits available for foreign-source income. New Zealand operates a full imputation system: corporate tax is a final entity-level charge, and resident shareholders receive imputation credits attached to dividends to relieve double taxation. There is no default pass-through treatment for a limited company, and no US-style check-the-box classification exists in New Zealand law.

Available election forms

(a) Look-Through Company (LTC) election under sections HB 1 to HB 15 of the Income Tax Act 2007, available only to a close company with five or fewer shareholders (all individuals or eligible trustees), which causes profits, losses, deductions, and tax credits to flow through to shareholders on a look-through basis, thereby converting entity-level taxation into a pass-through model. (b) Imputation credit account election under Part OB of the Income Tax Act 2007 to maintain an imputation credit account and attach imputation credits to dividends. (c) Consolidated group election under Part IG of the Income Tax Act 2007 for a wholly owned group of New Zealand companies to file a single consolidated income tax return. (d) GST elections under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, including the payments basis accounting election under section 19, the default invoice basis, the GST grouping election under section 55, and special output tax apportionment methods approved by the Commissioner. (e) Hybrid and branch mismatch rules under sections FH 1 to FH 15 of the Income Tax Act 2007 impose mandatory adjustment regimes rather than elective check-the-box treatment; however, the ordering rules and defensive adjustment provisions contain taxpayer elections that must be documented contemporaneously.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of total taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) in any consecutive 12-month period; registration is mandatory upon exceeding the threshold and voluntary below it. Non-resident suppliers of remote services, including digital software, simulated social casino platforms, and virtual slots content, are subject to the same NZD 60,000 threshold; once registered, 15% GST must be charged on supplies made to New Zealand resident consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under sections 11 and 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. For goods: supplies of goods exported by the supplier within 28 days of supply, or goods entered for overseas export by the recipient, are zero-rated. For services: supplies made to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed are zero-rated, provided the services are not supplied directly in connection with land in New Zealand, goods in New Zealand, or other excluded circumstances. Remote services, defined to include digital content, software, and electronically supplied gaming services, are zero-rated when the customer is a non-resident and is outside New Zealand at the time of supply. For NZ_LTD social casino and virtual slots publishing, revenue collected through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing is zero-rated when the playing consumer is a non-resident located outside New Zealand; contemporaneous geolocation records must substantiate offshore consumption. Supplies to New Zealand resident consumers are taxable at 15% GST, and the tax is due on the gross retail consideration before deduction of the App Store or Google Play commission.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (required only for physical exports; for digital simulation software, substitute with content delivery network logs, hosting platform records, and Apple and Google download records)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (not a New Zealand statutory customs or tax document; for digital services, substitute with bank statements evidencing foreign currency receipts, Apple IAP and Google Play Billing payout reports, and monthly revenue reconciliation schedules)

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or tax authority pre-approval system for invoices. E-invoicing is voluntary and uses the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 specifications through the accredited New Zealand e-Invoicing network; there is no legislation mandating e-invoicing or API-based clearance of individual transactions. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR on a monthly basis for large taxpayers with turnover exceeding NZD 24 million, on a two-monthly basis as the default, or on a six-monthly basis for smaller suppliers. A GST-registered supplier must issue a tax invoice within 28 days of a business-to-business supply; for business-to-consumer remote supplies, no tax invoice is required unless requested by the recipient. Source documents, including electronic invoicing transaction data, must be retained for seven years. For ISIC 5820 simulated social casino and virtual slots publishing, Apple App Store and Google Play Billing settlement statements constitute the primary transactional evidence for GST return preparation, output tax calculation, and audit substantiation.

Inland Revenue may reassess erroneously zero-rated supplies retrospectively within four years from the filing date, with no time limit where evasion or fraud is established. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply at 20% for an unapproved tax position taken without a reasonable basis or for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or a similar offence. Use-of-money interest accrues on unpaid GST from the statutory due date until full payment. Fraudulently claiming zero-rating on New Zealand resident digital gaming consumers, fabricating export documentation, or misstating end-user locations constitutes an offence under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994, carrying imprisonment for up to five years and fines up to NZD 50,000 for individuals, and fines up to NZD 100,000 for a company. The Commissioner has extended audit powers to require App Store and Google Play payout statements, to reconstruct supplies, to disregard sham or artificial arrangements, and to cancel GST registration where export substantiation is defective. New Zealand does not impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is applied as a transfer-pricing and income-recognition standard consistent with OECD guidance and Inland Revenue administrative practice for remote-services revenue attribution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full current and capital account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no exchange controls, no central bank approval requirements for ordinary cross-border transfers, and no restrictions on foreign ownership of a New Zealand limited company. Profit repatriation is permitted freely, but cross-border dividends or interest may be subject to New Zealand withholding tax or approved issuer levy; substantive tax planning documentation is required to minimize treaty leakage.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation period for export proceeds. Exporters may freely retain and use foreign currency receipts in any approved bank. The only compliance layer is the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, which obliges reporting entities to maintain transaction records and report suspicious activity; ordinary export receipts are not subject to exchange control or settlement reporting to the central bank.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low Feasibility for primary treasury operations. Offshore digital EMI providers cannot obtain a New Zealand banking license or direct access to the local clear stream settlement system; they are not substitutes for a registered New Zealand bank. They may be used only as auxiliary payment conduits in narrowly defined corridors, subject to strict AML counterparty diligence and prohibition on becoming the main treasury account.
