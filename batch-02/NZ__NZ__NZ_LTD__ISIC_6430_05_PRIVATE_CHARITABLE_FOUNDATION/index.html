Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

New Zealand law prohibits a mere post-office box as the registered office; however, a virtual office can satisfy the physical address requirement if it includes a real street address and responsible agent. For a charitable endowment entity, the Inland Revenue Department and Commercial Banks generally require more than a virtual mailbox; they expect, or at least strongly prefer, a dedicated physical presence or a fully serviced office agreement showing actual substance. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Companies Act 1993 imposes no minimum paid-up share capital, no minimum share premium, and no escrow or blocked capital account for a limited company. The directors may issue shares for such consideration as they determine under the constitution; any uncalled capital may be called only as authorised by the terms of issue. For a charitable endowment entity, the capital is effectively dedicated to charitable purposes through the constitution and Charities Act 2005, not through a paid-up-capital escrow.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 - mandatory registration threshold for taxable supplies in New Zealand over any 12-month period; charitable entities may register voluntarily below this threshold and use a 9-month GST filing period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11(1) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods within 28 days of the time of supply and retains adequate evidence of export. Services are zero-rated under section 11A where supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand when the services are performed, provided the services are not performed in connection with land or moveable property in New Zealand or supplied to an individual present in New Zealand. For grant distributions by a private charitable foundation, grant payments are generally not a taxable supply and therefore outside the scope of GST.

  • New Zealand Customs export entry (CSD export declaration) listing the tariff classification and destination country
  • Original or electronic bill of lading or air waybill issued by the carrier evidencing physical departure from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list at line-item level
  • Foreign exchange repatriation evidence, such as a bank SWIFT confirmation or telegraphic transfer advice showing receipt of export proceeds in the entity's foreign currency account
  • Forwarder's cargo manifest or port/airport terminal receipt as secondary proof of export

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate and no anti-fraud digital stamping or API-based clearance mechanism. New Zealand operates a self-assessment, return-based GST system. E-invoicing via the Peppol network is voluntary. GST returns must be filed electronically through myIR or approved accounting software using the GST return form.

No statutory foreign currency repatriation deadline exists in New Zealand; however, the 28-day export condition under the GST Act is mandatory. Claims of false or unsupported zero-rating are subject to shortfall penalties under section 141 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, ranging up to 150% of the unpaid GST for evasion or abusive tax positions, plus use-of-money interest under section 120. Inland Revenue may reassess within 4 years, extended to 8 years for fraud or evasion. Customs offences under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 for false export documentation may attract fines up to NZ$50,000, and refunds will be clawed back with penalties.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised and open foreign-exchange regime with no restrictions on currency convertibility for either current account or capital account transactions. The New Zealand dollar is freely floating and freely transferable, and neither resident nor non-resident entities require exchange-control approval to move funds, invest, borrow, remit profits, or receive charitable endowment inflows. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand supervises banks and settlement systems but does not operate exchange surrender requirements or quantitative FX limits. The primary compliance obstacles to cross-border banking arise from bank-driven AML/CFT obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, requiring robust source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership evidence. Profit remittances and dividend/grant payments are not subject to FX licensing or mandatory conversion, though taxation can apply and banks must satisfy correspondent-bank sanctions filters. In practice, the foundation can hold funds in NZD or foreign currency, fund disbursements internationally, and exit positions freely provided the documented entity is registered with Charities Services and the corporate register. This makes New Zealand a highly effective treasury hub for a private charitable endowment vehicle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or conversion ratio applies in New Zealand. Exporters and services providers may retain all export proceeds in any currency, in New Zealand or offshore, without mandatory conversion or repatriation to the central bank. Financial reporting only requires the standard banking purpose declaration and any applicable tax declarations.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ); no separate foreign-exchange administration exists in New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. New Zealand banking law reserves acceptances of deposits for registered banks under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989, so pure fintech EMIs cannot act as a primary deposit-taking treasury partner. However, certain AML/CFT-registered payment utilities and payment facilitator platforms may be used for ancillary grant disbursements or lower-value cross-border clearing, provided the core cash treasury is held at a New Zealand-registered bank. Any EMI must be licensed under New Zealand's AML/CFT regime and be otherwise compliant with RBNZ and FMA boundary requirements.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Certificate of incorporation, registered organisations details from the New Zealand Companies Register, the entity's constitution and charitable trust deed, board minutes approving the ownership and treasury structure, a complete organisational chart identifying each natural person beneficial owner/controller, notarised passport copies of directors and trustees, nil/maximum share capital declarations if applicable, and a written legal opinion confirming the charitable purpose and absence of commercial ownership.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound endowment and capital injection funds should be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 from the donating entity's bank to the foundation's selected New Zealand tier-1 commercial bank account, using the applicable bank purpose/reason for payment code for a capital transfer from non-residents to a registered company. Since no exchange control permission is required, the main validation is bank AML/KYC: the MT103 must be accompanied by a payment fee-bearing narration referencing the endowment deed, the donor's full legal-name details, and the foundation's GST/IRD registration where applicable. On receipt, the foundation must advise the bank to credit the capital escrow account and instruct its external auditor to issue a capital verification report to evidence the injection before funds are swept into the operational account. No RBNZ fee or prior approval applies, but the bank may hold the funds for a due diligence review if the depositor is unknown or if the documentary trail is incomplete.Grant distributions and trade settlements should be executed through the SWIFT MT103 prime credit-transfer rail for regular outward payments, MT202 for high-value interbank movement of funds, commercial letters of credit under UCP600 for trade finance operations, and documentary collections for lower-value or documentary trade transactions. For NZD-denominated high-value payments, use the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's Exchange Settlement Account System; for USD and other foreign currencies, use approved correspondent bank clearing through CHIPS/Fedwire or the relevant foreign real-time gross settlement system. All outbound disbursements to approved grantees should contain a structured purpose code, the agreed beneficiary account/SWIFT BIC, and a remittance advice boxed to the programmatic grant title to avoid sanctions filtering delays and to support tax and audit trail evidence.Onshore deliverable forward contracts with a New Zealand-registered bank for hedging NZD/USD, NZD/EUR, NZD/AUD and NZD/GBP endowment and grant exposureNon-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts for emerging-market currencies where onshore deliverability is not available, capped by treasury policy to grant-supported currencies onlyCross-currency swaps for long-dated endowment liabilities when the investment base is NZD but disbursements are projected in USD or EUR over multi-year grant cyclesPlain-vanilla FX options if, and only if, the charitable deed or investment policy allows limited de-risking and the foundation's treasury committee provides a written authority; speculative uncovered positions are prohibited.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: An independent certified CPA audit is mandatory for a New Zealand company when it meets the Financial Reporting Act 2013 'large entity' test: total assets equal or exceed NZD 60 million or total consolidated revenue equals or exceeds NZD 30 million. For registered charitable entities, a Tier 1 statutory audit is required where annual operating expenses exceed NZD 2 million in each of two consecutive accounting periods, or where total assets exceed NZD 6 million. Tier 2 charities with operating expenses between NZD 125,000 and NZD 2 million require an independent review engagement (not a full audit). Tier 3 charities below NZD 125,000 are exempt from review/audit and may file a compilation-only report. There is no standalone headcount trigger for audit in New Zealand; however a board or regulator can constitutionally impose an audit at any size, and any statutory audit must be performed by a licensed auditor in accordance with New Zealand Auditing Standards (NZ ISA).
