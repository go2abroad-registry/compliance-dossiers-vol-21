Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The Companies Act 1993 requires a registered office to be a physical New Zealand address that is open for public service of documents during ordinary business hours. A virtual office may satisfy the Registrar if the provider maintains a real street location, accepts service, and provides secure mailing and scanning facilities. However, the Inland Revenue Department and New Zealand banks will almost certainly reject an automated dropshipping entity using only a shared commercial mailbox because the absence of operational substance is a red flag for medium-risk, cross-border e-commerce. Stripe and PayPal also routinely require proof of physical business premises and direct control over payment infrastructure before onboarding high-volume merchants. A premium licensed serviced office with a dedicated executive suite, meeting room, and contractually guaranteed access to original corporate records is therefore strongly preferred. A mailbox-only arrangement creates a material risk of refusal of GST registration, bank account denial, and eventual company strike-off.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (Te Tari Tauhake Inland Revenue) is the competent New Zealand tax authority. The electronic compliance portal is myIR at myIR.ird.govt.nz; entity identity is first established through the New Zealand Companies Office and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) register at business.govt.nz. Export/import verification is administered by the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window (TSW).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity that is, or will become, a supplier of taxable goods or services in New Zealand, the following procedure applies: (1) Determine whether the entity is already required to be registered as an overseas company under the Companies Act 1993 because it is carrying on business in New Zealand; if so, apply to the New Zealand Companies Office for overseas company registration and obtain a NZBN. (2) Apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number using the prescribed customer registration channel in myIR; provide the overseas incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, director/controlling person details, New Zealand registered office or place of business details if any, a New Zealand contact person, and the expected date of commencement of taxable activity. (3) If the entity makes supplies of goods or services in New Zealand, or supplies remote services or low-value imported goods to New Zealand consumers, register for GST under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when the NZD 60,000 registration threshold is exceeded or voluntarily. (4) Inland Revenue will assign a single IRD number that serves as the TIN; the same number is used for income tax, GST, and other tax obligations. (5) Maintain all New Zealand tax filings through myIR and retain the IRD number and GST registration details for all tax invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election. A New Zealand limited company is taxed as a separate entity on taxable income at the New Zealand corporate income tax rate of 28%. An eligible closely held company may elect look-through company (LTC) treatment under the look-through company rules in the Income Tax Act 2007, but the election is not the default and is generally only available where shareholders are New Zealand tax residents. Hybrid mismatch rules operate automatically under the Income Tax Act 2007; there is no elective hybrid relief available for this structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in New Zealand in any 12-month period; the same NZD 60,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of remote services and low-value imported goods supplied to New Zealand recipients.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the goods are physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier in connection with the supply. In an automated dropshipping model, each sale must be traced to a final recipient outside New Zealand, the goods must leave New Zealand in the fulfilment process, and the supplier must retain evidence of shipment and payment. Qualifying offshore digital or electronically supplied services may also be zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient outside New Zealand who is not physically present in New Zealand and the supply is not made to a New Zealand fixed establishment. If the zero-rating criteria are met, the supplier charges GST at 0% and may still recover input GST on related New Zealand costs through its GST return in myIR; excess input tax is refunded by Inland Revenue in accordance with the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

  • New Zealand Customs Service export declaration (Export Entry) lodged via the Trade Single Window
  • Bill of lading or air waybill showing departure from New Zealand to the offshore destination
  • Commercial invoice and automated dropshipping order report identifying the non-resident buyer, Incoterms, value, and item description
  • Packing list and carrier transport documentation matching the export declaration
  • Bank settlement evidence (SWIFT MT103, foreign currency account credit, or New Zealand bank account receipt) showing overseas buyer payment and inward FX repatriation

New Zealand has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance regime and does not require digital tax stamping or government API invoice clearance. Suppliers must issue statutory GST tax invoices under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and retain records under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Electronic invoicing through the Peppol framework is an optional accepted channel in myIR; no cryptographic anti-fraud stamping is mandated.

Inland Revenue will challenge the zero rate if the export evidence bundle does not establish physical export to a non-resident recipient and repatriation of payment within the compliance deadline. If zero-rating is not substantiated, the supply becomes taxable at 15% and the supplier is liable for output GST plus tax shortfall penalties (20% to 150% depending on fault) and use-of-money interest under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Knowingly fraudulent export zero-rating or false documentation may result in criminal prosecution and the responsible natural persons may be subject to civil and criminal sanctions; records must be retained for a minimum of seven years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand is not a strict foreign-exchange-control jurisdiction. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand runs a freely floating currency framework and does not issue import licences, ration foreign exchange, or require prior approval for ordinary trade payments. Current account convertibility is fully available; dropshipping supply-chain payments, freight charges, marketplace fees, and customer refunds can be remitted freely in NZD, USD, EUR, GBP, and other convertible currencies. Capital account convertibility is equally liberal for a standard New Zealand Limited company: equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, and dividend distributions may be transferred to non-residents without central-bank authorisation. The practical friction is not currency control but bank-level AML/CFT due diligence under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, which requires robust evidence of beneficial ownership, commercial substance, and source of funds. No export proceeds surrender rule exists, and there is no mandatory foreign-exchange conversion ratio. Profit remittance is achieved by a board-declared dividend followed by ordinary SWIFT transfer; the residual further hurdle is taxation, chiefly Non-Resident Withholding Tax on outbound dividends. The Overseas Investment Act 2005 may impose consent requirements only where the transaction involves sensitive New Zealand land or strategic or significant business assets, which a typical automated dropshipping entity does not acquire.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. New Zealand does not compel exporters to surrender foreign-currency export proceeds to the central bank, nor does it impose a statutory conversion timeline or minimum conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency, held in New Zealand foreign-currency accounts, or freely converted to NZD at the exporter's discretion.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Qualitative rating: Conditional Medium for ancillary settlement and Low as the primary treasury bank. New Zealand does not give foreign digital EMIs a de facto banking licence; only licensed registered banks may accept repayable demand deposits and provide the full clearing, capital-management, and depositor-protection architecture expected by a corporate treasurer. An offshore EMI can support rapid cross-border payouts or multi-currency settlement through its locally partnered bank only if it is registered on the Financial Service Providers Register and is an AML/CFT reporting entity in New Zealand. It is not a lawful replacement for a domestic Tier 1 operational or capital-escrow account. Therefore, the EMI should be used peripherally for Stripe and PayPal payouts and small-value supplier facilitation, while capital-escrow and tax-payment cashflows must be held with a registered New Zealand bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity capital and shareholder loans should be remitted by SWIFT MT103 directly from the shareholder's regulated bank to the company's NZD capital-escrow account at a Tier 1 New Zealand bank. The remittance narrative must state the shareholder legal name, the words 'Subscription for ordinary shares in [company name]', and the transaction's Balance-of-Payments classification as direct investment or equity capital. The receiving bank will undertake currency conversion if the amount arrives in USD, EUR, or GBP, but no foreign-exchange approval is needed. After receipt, the company must pass an allotment resolution, file the updated Companies Office share register, and retain the bank's credit advice for audit purposes. Because New Zealand has no minimum paid-up capital and no statutory capital-verification certificate, the bank will instead require source-of-funds evidence and a statutory declaration from the shareholder before accepting the deposit as clean cleared funds.For automated dropshipping disbursements, the primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for supplier invoices above the bank's reporting threshold, commercial letters of credit when a supplier demands documentary security, and documentary collections for consignment or proof-of-dispatch flows. Customer collections from Stripe and PayPal are processed through commercial acquiring rails and then swept into the NZD operational account before being deployed to suppliers. Every outbound MT103 must contain the underlying invoice number and supplier order reference to preserve audit ability and avoid co-mingling. 28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% statutory Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) on dividends paid to non-residents. Under most New Zealand Double Tax Agreements (DTAs), the rate is reduced to 15% for portfolio dividends (less than 10% voting interest) and 5% for substantial shareholdings (10% or more voting power). No domestic withholding applies on fully imputed dividends paid to NZ residents; non-residents cannot use imputation credits.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A taxpayer with cross-border related-party transactions exceeding NZ$1,000,000 in aggregate during an income year is expected to maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File) in New Zealand. Although no formal Local File/Master File thresholds exist like the OECD's €50 million Master File trigger, the IRD applies a risk-based approach; companies with an intercompany turnover above NZ$1 million, or with a gross annual income above NZ$10 million, are routinely compelled to produce full documentation on audit. The documentation must include: the transactional lifecycle, benchmarking studies, entity functions/assets/risks, contractual agreements, and the arm's length price determination.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no self-standing 'Economic Substance Regulations' comparable to Caribbean offshore jurisdictions; however, it fully adopts the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and the concept of Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in attributing profit to New Zealand operations. A New Zealand-registered limited company engaged in automated dropshipping must demonstrate that it exercises real decision-making over inventory selection, supplier contracts, pricing, marketing, and cash flows. If the company merely executes orders under a foreign parent's instruction while all commercial substance is located offshore, the IRD can re-characterise the arrangement as a sham or agency under the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) in sections BG1 and GA1 of the Income Tax Act 2007. The IRD may apply the CFC (Controlled Foreign Company) and permanent establishment rules to attribute profits to the foreign principal or to treat the NZ company as the PE of the foreign entity, subjecting it to NZ income tax on 28% of the repatriated profit and NRWT on related dividends. Directors must ensure that local statutory records (board minutes, employment contracts, IP ownership, supplier agreements) are genuine and reflect actual functions performed in New Zealand.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Omitted Dropshipping Sales / Hidden Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Sections 143A and 141I of the Tax Administration Act 1994: evasion or similar offence is triable summarily; on conviction, imprisonment for up to 5 years and/or a fine up to NZ$50,000 (for individuals) or NZ$250,000 (for companies). Civil shortfall penalty for evasion is 150% of the tax shortfall. Use-of-money interest also accrues from the due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate, authorise, or fail to prevent the evasion are personally liable to equal the company's tax liability as tax officers (under sections 143A(3) and 149A). They may be criminally prosecuted individually, suffer asset forfeiture orders under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009, receive a travel ban or asset freeze enforcement through the courts, and be disqualified from managing companies for up to 10 years under the Companies Act 1993. Even outside criminality, directors who allow the company to trade while tax debts accrue can be personally liable for debts under the reckless trading provision of section 135.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Profit Stripping with Related Overseas Suppliers

Statutory sanction: The IRD may impose a transfer pricing adjustment and assess a shortfall penalty under section 141C (unacceptable tax position) of 20% if the taxpayer did not have a reasonably arguable position; a 40% penalty applies for gross carelessness; a 100% penalty for an abusive tax position; and a 150% penalty for evasion. Interest accrues on the underpayment. Failure to produce transfer pricing documentation on request triggers an additional penalty under section 141E of 50% of the assessable adjustment if documentation was not prepared in a timely manner.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or authorise tax returns relying on unsubstantiated intercompany prices may be held personally liable for the civil penalties if the company is unable to pay (in cases of 'arrangement with a dominant purpose of tax avoidance' where the director has personally benefited). Under the Companies Act 1993, directors who know the company is insolvent and continue to make unnecessary transfer payments can be personally liable for the amount taken. Criminal liability may extend to imprisonment of up to 5 years if the pricing arrangement is found to be a deliberate fraud against the revenue.
Sham / Shell Dropshipping Company with No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under sections BG1 and GA1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 authorises the IRD to void the tax advantage achieved. The company will be reassessed at 28% on the full worldwide profit attributable to activities conducted in New Zealand, plus NRWT on the deemed distribution to shareholders. Penalties under section 141 (abusive tax position) of 100% of the tax shortfall apply. Where the structure is used for money laundering or predicate offences, the AML/CFT Act 2009 and Crimes Act 1961 provide for fines up to NZ$2 million and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: A nominee director who signs the tax return knowing that the company has no real substance can be personally liable for the resulting shortfall penalty, and may be disqualified from office. Under the Crimes Act 1961, a director who facilitates a company being used to launder the proceeds of crime faces a maximum of 14 years' imprisonment. The NZ courts can pierce the corporate veil where the company is a mere fraud façade, making directors personally liable for the entire tax debt and entitled to asset recovery orders, a foreign travel ban, and extradition for tax fraud if the director is overseas. Directors must also be aware of the 'insolvent trading' provisions: if the company becomes unable to pay tax due to the sham structure, each director is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties.
