Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox-only arrangement is not acceptable as the registered office of a New Zealand company. Under section 189 of the Companies Act 1993, the registered office must be a physical address in New Zealand that is not a postal address and at which legal process can be served during ordinary business hours. The Inland Revenue Department and New Zealand banks will reject a virtual office address for GST registration, payroll, and corporate account opening because they require a physical place of business and operational substance. For an internet retailing business, Stripe and PayPal also require a verifiable physical business address. A serviced office with a dedicated physical suite, nameplate, and lease agreement may satisfy the physical-presence test, but a commercial mailbox or mail-forwarding service alone will be rejected by the Companies Office, IRD, and banking AML/CFT checks.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / IRD) of New Zealand; online portal: myIR (https://myir.ird.govt.nz); corporate registration: New Zealand Companies Office (https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz).

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand limited company, first incorporate under the Companies Act 1993 through the New Zealand Companies Office online register; the company automatically obtains a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). The Companies Office electronically shares incorporation data with Inland Revenue for the automatic issue of an IRD number, or, if not issued, the company must complete the online IRD number registration through the myIR portal. The IRD number is the single tax identifier used for income tax, GST, PAYE, FBT, and other taxes; no separate GST ID is issued. For a foreign non-resident company that is not incorporated in New Zealand but will carry on business here, the company must first register on the Overseas Register of the New Zealand Companies Office, appoint a local agent for service of process and a registered place of business, and then apply to Inland Revenue for a New Zealand IRD number through myIR. In the IRD registration application for the NZ_LTD, the entity must state the business classification (ISIC 4791 / NAICS 459110), the intended online payment rails (Stripe and PayPal), the ultimate beneficial ownership details, the registered office, the directors' residence status, and anticipated New Zealand and export turnover. GST registration must be activated in the same myIR profile once taxable supplies in New Zealand exceed, or are expected to exceed, NZD 60,000 in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary GST registration is permitted for non-residents under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. For non-resident online sellers of low-value goods or remote services to New Zealand consumers, the special offshore registration regime in the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11(1)(a) Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a supply of goods is zero-rated when the supplier exports the goods in the course of or as a condition of the supply and they are physically removed from New Zealand within 28 days of the supply date. Under section 11(1)(ba), goods supplied to a non-resident person may also be zero-rated when the goods are removed from New Zealand by the non-resident purchaser or their agent within 28 days. Qualifying services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under section 11(1)(b) where they are not supplied directly in connection with New Zealand land or goods. For an internet retailing exporter of outdoor/camping equipment, each export sale must be supported by an export contract on trade terms showing overseas destination and risk transfer, and the exporter must retain proof that the goods left New Zealand. The export file should also demonstrate foreign currency receipt from the overseas buyer within 180 days, although New Zealand has no exchange-control repatriation statute; the 180-day benchmark is used as the IRD audit-compliant documentary window.

  • New Zealand Customs Service export entry filed through the Trade Single Window (TSW) or an approved customs software provider, showing the export entry number, exporter IRD number, cargo description, tariff classification, quantity, value, and overseas destination.
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international courier manifest evidencing physical departure from New Zealand, naming the New Zealand consignor and the foreign consignee, and confirming delivery to the stated overseas destination.
  • Commercial invoice, export packing list, purchase order from the overseas buyer, and bank-issued foreign exchange receipt or SWIFT/inward remittance credit advice matching the invoice value and confirming receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer within 180 days.

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, API invoice clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping is mandated in New Zealand. GST tax invoices must be issued in accordance with the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and retained under the Tax Administration Act 1994; a valid GST invoice includes supplier name and GST registration number, invoice date, supplier and recipient details, description, quantity, price, GST amount, and total. E-invoicing may be used voluntarily, but Inland Revenue does not require pre-clearance of invoices.

If Inland Revenue determines that export zero-rating is unsupported because the goods did not leave New Zealand within 28 days, the supply is recharacterized as a taxable supply and GST output tax is assessed at 15% on the consideration, together with use-of-money interest under the Tax Administration Act 1994. The exporter may also be subject to shortfall penalties: 20% for lack of reasonable care or unacceptable tax interpretation, 50% for an abusive tax position, and 100% for evasion. Knowingly producing false or misleading export documents can trigger criminal liability under the Crimes Act 1961 and the Tax Administration Act 1994. Any GST input credits previously claimed on export-related costs remain subject to verification, and overpaid refunds can be clawed back with interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Reserve Bank of New Zealand administers a monetary policy framework that permits full convertibility of the New Zealand dollar on both current and capital account. There are no exchange control restrictions, no surrender obligations for export proceeds, no central bank approval for dividend remittances, and no requirement to route foreign exchange transactions through a state-owned bank. New Zealand has accepted Article VIII obligations under the IMF Articles of Agreement, meaning it may not impose restrictive payments for current international transactions without Fund approval. Capital transactions are equally open for an entity such as NZ_LTD except for limited screening of non-resident ownership in sensitive land, fishing quota, and strategically significant businesses under the Overseas Investment Act 2005; an online outdoor gear retailer does not require such consent. Profit repatriation is taxed through normal withholding mechanics rather than exchange control. Non-resident dividend withholding tax at 30% may be reduced by an applicable double tax agreement. There is no requirement to convert receipts into New Zealand dollars; exporters may maintain foreign currency accounts at registered banks. Cash and non-cash transactions are subject only to AML/CFT reporting. Therefore the practical treasury hurdle is documentary KYC, not currency inconvertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender of export proceeds exists in New Zealand. Exporters may leave foreign currency receipts in foreign currency accounts indefinitely, convert at any time, or use them for business purposes. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand imposes no mandatory conversion ratio and no time limit for repatriating receipts. Banks must apply normal AML/CFT checks and may require commercial evidence such as invoices and shipping documents for receipts from low-tax or high-risk jurisdictions.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

New Zealand does not license dedicated electronic money institutions under a separate legal framework. Non-bank payment providers and digital wallet operators must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register and comply with the Financial Markets Conduct Act and Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act. A fintech EMI can legally assist with merchant settlement and cross-border payments, but it does not provide deposit-taking or lending and generally cannot offer the same domestic clearing infrastructure, capital escrow, or FX hedging capabilities as a Tier 1 commercial bank. For treasury purposes, the viability is Low for core banking and Medium for merchant settlement. Recommend using an EMI only for the Stripe/PayPal collection layer and a Tier 1 bank for all FX, capital, and liquidity clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital should be transferred via SWIFT MT103 for priority, with explicit remittance information indicating 'Share Subscription - NZ_LTD - Companies Office Number'. The ISO purpose code should be EQUI, or PCAP if the sending bank requests a personal capital payment category. The sending bank must provide the ultimate beneficial owner and source of funds in the cover or advice. The receiving bank will verify the share allotment resolution and register share issue in the Companies Office. A bank confirmation or capital certificate is issued after the funds are cleared. If the sender is in a jurisdiction subject to FATF enhanced monitoring, the paying bank may require pre-approval of the remittance and a legalised corporate resolution. New Zealand's Overseas Investment Office does not require consent for a retail e-commerce holding company unless the investor is a foreign government or the transaction involves sensitive assets.Outbound settlement for inventory purchases and logistics should be routed through the Tier 1 bank using SWIFT MT103 for open account trade and prepayments, commercial letters of credit issued via MT700 for large or staged vendor shipments, and documentary collections for transactions requiring document presentation without full letter-of-credit security. Each instruction must include the underlying invoice number, purchase order, and ISO transaction purpose code, such as TRAD, to support the bank's AML monitoring and golden source record keeping. The bank will screen all counterparties against sanctions and politically exposed person databases. The entity should maintain a complete trade file containing supplier contracts, commercial invoices, bills of lading or airway bills, customs entries, and payment settlement confirmations. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: New Zealand does not license dedicated electronic money institutions under a separate legal framework. Non-bank payment providers and digital wallet operators must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register and comply with the Financial Markets Conduct Act and Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act. A fintech EMI can legally assist with merchant settlement and cross-border payments, but it does not provide deposit-taking or lending and generally cannot offer the same domestic clearing infrastructure, capital escrow, or FX hedging capabilities as a Tier 1 commercial bank. For treasury purposes, the viability is Low for core banking and Medium for merchant settlement. Recommend using an EMI only for the Stripe/PayPal collection layer and a Tier 1 bank for all FX, capital, and liquidity clearing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Formal OECD-aligned Local File and Master File documentation is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least NZD 50 million and cross-border related-party transactions exceeding NZD 10 million. Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory where consolidated annual group revenue is NZD 1.15 billion or more. Below these thresholds, all taxpayers must still satisfy the arm's length principle and maintain sufficient records to substantiate cross-border pricing.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate foreign-style Economic Substance Regulations comparable to those in some offshore financial centres. However, New Zealand's general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 disregards arrangements lacking genuine commercial substance. Residential status, place of effective management, local management control, business premises, employees, and substantive inventory management are critical in confirming that a New Zealand company is not a mere shell used to divert New Zealand taxable income or treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: A tax shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax under the Tax Administration Act 1994, plus late payment penalties and use-of-money interest. Criminal liability includes prosecution for tax evasion, with fines of up to NZD 50,000 for a first offence and NZD 100,000 for a subsequent offence, and imprisonment of up to 5 years. Restraint and forfeiture orders under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009 may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or authorise evasive filing may be prosecuted personally. The corporate veil may be pierced under sections 135 and 136 of the Companies Act 1993 for reckless or dishonest trading. Directors may face civil compensation orders, disqualification from managing companies, travel restrictions while criminal proceedings are pending, and imprisonment upon conviction.
Shell Company or Artificial Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The arrangement may be voided under section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007. Inland Revenue may reassess taxable income on a realistic commercial basis, deny treaty or imputation benefits, and impose a 100% shortfall penalty for an abusive tax position, plus late payment penalties and use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who permit the company to operate as an empty corporate facade can be personally liable for the company's tax debts if assets are disposed of or the company is left insolvent. Personal civil liability for restitution, costs, and pecuniary penalties is possible. Directors may also face an order prohibiting them from leaving New Zealand while tax investigations or debt recovery proceedings continue.
AML/CFT and Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: AML/CFT Act contraventions may result in civil pecuniary penalties of up to NZD 1 million for a natural person and NZD 5 million for a body corporate. Money laundering offences under the Crimes Act 1961 carry imprisonment of up to 7 years, and serious fraud offences carry penalties including imprisonment of up to 10 years. Inland Revenue may also issue substantial shortfall penalties where undisclosed offshore receipts are found to be the proceeds of offending.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate or direct the unlawful flow of funds may be prosecuted as parties to money laundering. They may face personal criminal conviction, confiscation of personal assets, travel bans, and statutory disqualification from acting as directors of any New Zealand company.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may make a transfer pricing adjustment increasing New Zealand taxable income and deny deductions. Shortfall penalties apply on a tiered basis: 40% for an unacceptable tax position, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion, plus use-of-money interest and compound late payment penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a knowingly false IR4 return or direct the adoption of a contrived TP policy may be personally prosecuted. Civil actions may recover underpaid tax from directors where the company is liquidated and the failure was due to reckless or dishonest management. Director disqualification and border control orders are available in serious cases.
