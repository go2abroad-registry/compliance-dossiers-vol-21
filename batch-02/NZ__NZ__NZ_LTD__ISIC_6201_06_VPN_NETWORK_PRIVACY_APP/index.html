Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

New Zealand law permits a registered office to be located at the address of a professional corporate services provider and does not require the company to own premises. However, for a VPN/network-security SaaS entity, tax and banking substance expectations are higher: Inland Revenue requires a valid business address and may query arrangements that rely solely on a commercial mailbox, while NZ trading banks and third-party payment processors generally reject a virtual office for AML/CFT customer due diligence unless accompanied by a physical place of business, local directorship, or an operational settlement presence. Therefore a virtual office is permissible for incorporation/statutory correspondence but is commercially insufficient for opening bank accounts and establishing merchant rails, and should be paired with a physical service address or co-working space.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in New Zealand. A closely held New Zealand limited company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, making the company fiscally transparent for income tax purposes, provided the ownership and eligibility criteria are satisfied. GST group registration is also available under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 to treat related companies as a single registrant. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; a non-resident supplier of remote services to New Zealand consumers must register if its taxable supplies supplied to New Zealand recipients exceed NZD 60,000 in a rolling 12-month period. Voluntary registration is permitted where the entity carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand or makes a taxable supply in the course of that activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated at 0% GST under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when the supplier exports the goods and obtains written evidence, before or at the time of supply, proving that the goods have left or will leave New Zealand within the statutory export timeframe, generally within 28 days of the time of supply. Services are zero-rated as exported services when the recipient is a non-resident outside New Zealand at the time of supply, the services are not physically performed in New Zealand, the services are not supplied to a person present in New Zealand, and the services are not connected with New Zealand land or goods. For the VPN network privacy SaaS business model, qualifying offshore digital subscription services supplied to overseas end users may be zero-rated where the supplier retains records establishing the non-resident status of the customer and the offshore place of use or enjoyment, including contract jurisdiction, billing address, IP address, payment gateway settlement, and service delivery logs.

  • New Zealand Customs export declaration lodged electronically through the Trade Single Window (TSW) as the statutory Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the export consignment
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract or purchase order matching the Customs declaration value and product description
  • Packing list and cargo or freight movement documentation
  • Customs-approved departure records or port and terminal shipping movement evidence
  • Foreign exchange settlement certificate or bank payment advice, together with Stripe or Paymentwall payout statements, evidencing receipt and repatriation of overseas consideration as the New Zealand counterpart to an Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Service delivery and usage records, subscriber IP and country records, and customer due-diligence file evidencing non-resident recipient and offshore use for exported digital services

No real-time fiscal clearance regime exists in New Zealand; there are no continuous transaction controls, no mandatory government API clearance, and no anti-fraud digital stamping obligation for tax invoices. Invoices must contain the prescribed particulars under section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, including supplier name and GST number, invoice date, description, quantity, GST-exclusive consideration, GST amount, and time of supply. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR. E-invoicing is voluntary and may be exchanged via the PEPPOL network or any standard electronic format agreed with the recipient; no Inland Revenue pre-authorisation or cryptographic clearance is required.

Inland Revenue may audit the export file and assess GST at 15% on the full supply if the zero-rating evidence is insufficient, together with use-of-money interest. Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, shortfall penalties apply in tiers: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or intent to defeat tax. False or fraudulent export zero-rating claims can lead to criminal prosecution under the offence provisions of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, director and shareholder liability risks under New Zealand company and tax law, a four-year reassessment period extended indefinitely where fraud or evasion is involved, and clawback of any previously refunded GST with interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised and freely floating exchange rate regime with no exchange control restrictions imposed under the Foreign Exchange Control Act or subsequent regulations. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both current account and capital account transactions. There are no requirements to surrender export proceeds to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand or any authorised dealer, and there is no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign currency receipts. Residents and non-residents may freely open foreign currency accounts domestically, hold overseas accounts, and remit dividends, loan repayments, royalties, and trade payments without prior approval. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand's role is confined to prudential oversight, anti-money laundering supervision, and liquidity management, not transaction-level authorisation. In practice, businesses must simply comply with standard NZSX and Companies Office filing obligations, proof of source of funds for banking relationship purposes, and ordinary tax compliance under Inland Revenue. As a jurisdiction with no meaningful convertibility hurdle, the VPN Network Privacy App company can treat New Zealand domiciliation as a treasury-neutral platform for global invoicing and capital movement. Profit remittance is unimpeded, subject to corporate tax clearance and documented underlying transactions. The only practical constraints are those imposed by counterparty banks in high-risk jurisdictions or international correspondent banks that may require enhanced due diligence for digital service providers with privacy and encryption attributes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender rules in New Zealand. Exporters retain 100% of foreign currency proceeds in unrestricted foreign currency accounts or may convert to NZD at market rates. No timeline or ratio mandatory conversion applies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech and electronic money institutions (EMIs) are permissible in New Zealand but operate under a restricted regulatory ceiling. A non-bank EMI can be registered as a financial service provider under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Disputes Resolution) Act 2008 and obtain a money transmission licence if it provides wallet or payment services. However, such entities are not banking institutions and cannot accept deposits as a core business, cannot issue credit instruments, and remain barred from participating directly in domestic clearing via the Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system or the Automated Payment System unless they obtain a specialist exchange or clearing membership, which is rarely granted to offshore digital brands. For a VPN / privacy-focused SaaS entity, using a foreign EMI (e.g., digital-first platforms) introduces serious practical risks: no direct access to the NZD payment rails, weaker protection under the Banking Ombudsman scheme, and higher friction for settling Inland Revenue obligations. Therefore, the recommended route is to open a statutory corporate account with a Tier-1 domestic bank and use EMIs only for merchant acquiring (e.g., Stripe or Paymentwall) in a segregated collections manner. The viability of a fully offshore EMI as the treasury spine is low due to restricted bank-account interoperability, limited trade finance capabilities, and prospective AML perception difficulties.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech and electronic money institutions (EMIs) are permissible in New Zealand but operate under a restricted regulatory ceiling. A non-bank EMI can be registered as a financial service provider under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Disputes Resolution) Act 2008 and obtain a money transmission licence if it provides wallet or payment services. However, such entities are not banking institutions and cannot accept deposits as a core business, cannot issue credit instruments, and remain barred from participating directly in domestic clearing via the Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system or the Automated Payment System unless they obtain a specialist exchange or clearing membership, which is rarely granted to offshore digital brands. For a VPN / privacy-focused SaaS entity, using a foreign EMI (e.g., digital-first platforms) introduces serious practical risks: no direct access to the NZD payment rails, weaker protection under the Banking Ombudsman scheme, and higher friction for settling Inland Revenue obligations. Therefore, the recommended route is to open a statutory corporate account with a Tier-1 domestic bank and use EMIs only for merchant acquiring (e.g., Stripe or Paymentwall) in a segregated collections manner. The viability of a fully offshore EMI as the treasury spine is low due to restricted bank-account interoperability, limited trade finance capabilities, and prospective AML perception difficulties.
