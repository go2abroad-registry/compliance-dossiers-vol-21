Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is permissible under the Companies Act 1993 only if it provides a physical New Zealand street address where documents can be served during ordinary business hours and a person is present to accept service. A registered office cannot be a post office box or solely an electronic mail forwarding service. However, for this low-risk management consultancy, a virtual-only arrangement will often be rejected by major New Zealand registered banks, Wise B2B, and payment rails because the AML/CFT customer due diligence process requires a credible operational address. The Inland Revenue Department will also scrutinize a virtual mailing address for GST and income tax records. Therefore, a physical or hybrid office with a local lease, staffed work area, and clearly documented operational substance is strongly recommended to avoid bank and tax onboarding delays.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (Te Tari Taake) and its online secure portal myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident company must first determine whether it derives New Zealand-source income or carries on a GST taxable activity in New Zealand. If a tax presence exists, the non-resident company applies for an IRD number using the Inland Revenue non-resident company application (form IR742). The application must be accompanied by certified copies of the overseas certificate of incorporation, the constitution or equivalent charter, a certified director/shareholder register, and a completed non-resident company supplementary details schedule. The application may be submitted by post or through a New Zealand registered tax agent, who can also file electronically via myIR. Upon verification, Inland Revenue issues an IRD number, typically within 8-10 working days. If GST registration is required or voluntarily chosen, the same IRD number is used to register for GST through myIR by selecting the appropriate filing frequency (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly). If the company will have employees or pay New Zealand-sourced investment income, it must also register for PAYE and/or resident withholding tax / non-resident withholding tax under the same IRD number. All registrations are governed by the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand has no U.S.-style check-the-box election. However, an eligible close company may elect look-through company (LTC) status under sections HA 4 to HA 18 of the Income Tax Act 2007 using form IR861, which makes the company fiscally transparent. This election is available only for companies with five or fewer shareholders that satisfy the qualifying tests in section HA 5. In the absence of an LTC election, a NZ Limited company is subject to entity-level taxation at the corporate income tax rate of 28%. Hybrid mismatch rules in sections FH 1 to FH 11 of the Income Tax Act 2007 are mandatory anti-avoidance provisions, not elective mechanisms. A consolidated group election under the Income Tax Act 2007 is also available for qualifying groups of NZ-resident companies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of gross taxable supplies in any 12-month period, measured on both a historical and prospective basis under section 51 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded or is likely to be exceeded, and voluntary registration is available to any person carrying on a taxable activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the GST Act 1985 if the supplier exports them before the supply is treated as made, or within 28 days after the supply is made. Goods may also be zero-rated when supplied for export under section 11(1)(c) if they are exported in a continuous journey. Services, including overseas market entry consultancy services under ISIC 7020, are zero-rated under section 11A(1)(a) where the services are supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, and the services are not supplied directly in connection with land or goods situated in New Zealand at the time of supply. For cross-border advisory services, the key statutory test is the non-resident status of the recipient and the overseas location of performance; if the consultancy services are performed partly or wholly in New Zealand for a non-resident who is overseas, the supply may still qualify for zero-rating provided it is not connected to NZ land or goods. The supplier must hold sufficient documentary evidence to substantiate the non-resident status and the export nature of the supply.

  • For goods zero-rating: New Zealand Customs Service electronic export declaration (ClearCore) or written export entry confirming the date and mode of export
  • For goods zero-rating: Bill of lading, air waybill, or other transport document evidencing physical export from New Zealand within 28 days of supply
  • For services zero-rating: Signed engagement contract or consultancy agreement identifying the non-resident client and confirming the overseas place of performance and benefit
  • For services zero-rating: GST tax invoice reflecting the supply as zero-rated, with a clear description of the cross-border consultancy scope
  • For both goods and services: Bank remittance advice, SWIFT payment confirmation, or inward remittance notification evidencing receipt of payment in foreign currency from the non-resident counterparty

None. New Zealand operates no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no government-mandated digital stamping, and no API-based e-invoicing clearance regime. GST returns are filed electronically via myIR or through approved gateway software on a periodic pay-and-file basis. Peppol-based e-invoicing is voluntary and is used for commercial exchange only; it carries no tax clearance or anti-fraud stamping effect. Inland Revenue may require digital record access during audits, but there is no upfront state clearance of tax invoices.

Inland Revenue scrutinizes zero-rating claims through desk audits and field investigations. A taxpayer who zero-rates an export without the required evidence is treated as having made a taxable supply at 15% GST, and Inland Revenue may reassess the output tax, impose use-of-money interest under the Tax Administration Act 1994, and levy shortfall penalties ranging from 20% for lack of reasonable care to 100% for an abusive tax position and 150% for evasion. Where a false statement or deliberate misstatement is made to substantiate zero-rating, Inland Revenue may also prosecute under the Tax Administration Act 1994, with fines up to NZD 50,000 for individuals and NZD 100,000 for companies, and the possibility of imprisonment for serious fraudulent conduct. GST records, including export evidence and FX remittance documentation, must be retained for at least seven years. There is no statutory New Zealand foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for GST zero-rating; however, for audit substantiation the exporter should be able to produce contemporaneous inward remittance evidence. Any export having no documented export movement or no demonstrable supply to a non-resident outside New Zealand will be recharacterised as a domestic supply, resulting in full GST plus penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised and open foreign exchange regime. There are no exchange controls, no mandatory central bank approvals for cross-border transfers, and no restrictions on converting New Zealand dollars into foreign currency. Both current account items such as consultancy fees, dividends, interest, and royalties, and capital account items such as direct equity investment, shareholder loans, and repatriation of capital, are freely convertible. The RBNZ does not operate a system of FX surrender requirements or regulated buy/sell rates; commercial banks price trades freely, with settlement occurring through the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) operated by the RBNZ. Profit remittance from a New Zealand limited company is lawful without central bank approval, provided the company complies with the Companies Act 1993 solvency test, tax clearance obligations, and AML/CFT due diligence requests made by the ordering bank. A foreign shareholder may receive dividends, distributor fees, or management consulting payments in NZD or any freely convertible currency. The practical compliance burden is concentrated in customer due diligence, tax residency declarations, and source-of-funds verification rather than government authorisation for the underlying transfer. New Zealand therefore ranks as a full-convertibility, hard-currency treasury jurisdiction with minimal FX friction for the structure described.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rules apply. New Zealand does not impose any mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation deadline, or foreign currency surrender obligation on exporters or service providers. The consultancy may hold export and cross-border fee income in foreign currency accounts with licensed New Zealand banks, or convert proceeds to NZD at a time of its choosing. However, the reporting bank must still satisfy AML/CFT verification of the source and route of all foreign currency receipts.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua / RBNZ)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech EMI viability is High for operational disbursements and Low-Medium for initial capital escrow and capital verification. Wise, Airwallex, and OFX can be used for recurring cross-border consultancy expense payments, contractor disbursements, and small-value overseas settlements. To operate lawfully in New Zealand, a fintech must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and be a reporting entity under the AML/CFT Act 2009. However, major New Zealand banks will not treat an EMI account as equivalent to a licensed bank deposit account for Companies Act capital verification or solvency evidence. Equity injections from non-resident shareholders should first land in a Tier-1 bank account to preserve the audit trail; the EMI may be introduced later as a settlement and liquidity optimisation rail only, not as the primary fiduciary custody layer.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% flat corporate income tax rate for all companies under the Income Tax Act 2007; no local or provincial corporate income tax surcharge in New Zealand.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) is imposed on gross dividends paid by an NZ company to a non-resident shareholder at the statutory rate of 30%. The rate is reduced to 15% where the dividend is fully imputed under the New Zealand imputation regime. Where the foreign shareholder holds a 10% or greater voting interest, the applicable NRWT rate on fully imputed dividends may be further reduced under an applicable double tax agreement (e.g., 15% under most New Zealand DTAs, 5% for certain substantial-holding corporate shareholders under selected treaties). For an un-imputed or insufficiently imputed dividend, the full 30% NRWT applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is legally required in New Zealand where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions of the NZ taxpayer exceeds NZ$10 million in an income year. The NZ$10 million threshold applies to the aggregate of related-party transactions including purchases, sales, services, royalties, management fees, and finance costs. Documentation must be prepared before the IR4 return is filed and be capable of production to Inland Revenue within 30 days of request. There is no statutory exemption for consultancy services; a NZ market-entry consultancy transacting with offshore affiliates (e.g., parent company service charges, licences of proprietary databases, client-referral fees) above NZ$10 million must hold a Local File. A Master File is required where the multinational group's annual consolidated turnover exceeds NZ$1.125 billion (aligned with OECD BEPS Action 13) or where Inland Revenue specifically requests one. Even below the NZ$10 million threshold, Inland Revenue expects arm's-length pricing supported by written agreements, budgets, and allocation methodologies; failure to prepare documentation eliminates the taxpayer's 'reasonably arguable position' defence and exposes the taxpayer to shortfall penalties.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a standalone 'Economic Substance Regulation' (ESR) in the style of the UAE or Caribbean jurisdictions. However, economic substance is embedded across New Zealand's income tax architecture: (a) Tax residence - a company incorporated in New Zealand under the Companies Act 1993 is automatically an NZ tax resident under section YD 2 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and is taxed on worldwide income regardless of where directors meet; transfer of offshore headquarters does not divest residence without an approved Deed of Residency. (b) Treaty availability - the DTA relief protocol requires the non-resident recipient to be a 'person' with fiscal residence in the treaty state; a shell company with no substance in the treaty jurisdiction will be denied treaty benefits under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, section BG 1) and the MLI principal-purpose test. (c) Transfer pricing - related-party transactions lacking commercial rationale may be disregarded or recharacterised by Inland Revenue under sections GC 1-GC 14; a consultancy with no staff, no office, or no revenue-generating functions is a 'risk distribution' target for IRD re-attribution of income to the offshore parent. (d) Thin capitalisation - a foreign-owned NZ company's aggregate related-party debt cannot exceed 60% of its net assets unless an arm's-length debt-capacity exception applies; excess interest deductions are denied. (e) For a New Zealand-registered company used purely as a conduit to invoice overseas market-entry services with no local staff or management, IRD will treat economic substance as absent and may assess on a look-through basis or apply the 100% abusive-tax-position shortfall penalty. Practical substance requirements for a credible NZ market-entry consultancy: a physical office in New Zealand, at least one NZ-resident director with genuine management powers, employment agreements with local staff/contractors, board minutes reflected in actual strategic decisions, a New Zealand bank account, and invoices/cashflow managed from New Zealand.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty under section 141E of the Tax Administration Act 1994 of up to 150% of the evaded tax; criminal conviction under section 143B exposes the entity to fines up to NZ$100,000 and directors/officers to imprisonment of up to 10 years; use-of-money interest accrues on all unpaid tax; Inland Revenue may also issue a default assessment eliminating all expense deductions and apply a 100% penalty for abusive tax positions.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or authorise false tax returns are personally prosecutable as principals; on conviction they face imprisonment, forfeiture of personal assets under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009, and civil orders preventing departure from New Zealand (Departure Prohibition Order under section 157 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 where unpaid tax exceeds NZ$10,000). Inland Revenue may pierce the corporate veil where the company is a mere facade; directors become jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax debts and may be disqualified from managing companies under the Companies Act 1993.
