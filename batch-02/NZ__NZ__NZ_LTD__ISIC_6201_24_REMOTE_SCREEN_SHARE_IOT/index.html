Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake), New Zealand; online portal: myIR secure business services at https://www.ird.govt.nz.

        
E-government filing portal
For a New Zealand-incorporated limited company, tax registration begins with incorporation under the Companies Act 1993 through the New Zealand Companies Office, which issues a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN); the company must then complete Inland Revenue tax registration in myIR to be issued with its IRD number, which is the statutory tax identification number for both income tax and GST. For a foreign non-resident entity not incorporated in New Zealand that makes or intends to make taxable supplies in New Zealand, the entity must apply to the Inland Revenue Department for a non-resident IRD number by submitting the non-resident company registration application through myIR, supported by the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director and ultimate shareholder register, registered office address in the home jurisdiction, New Zealand agent details if any, payment-rail and banking identification, actual business premises evidence for the ISIC 6201 remote screen sharing and IoT telemetry software activities, and a completed residence/tie-break position under the applicable double tax agreement. The IRD number issued for income tax is also used as the GST registration identifier. GST registration under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 is compulsory once taxable supplies in any 12-month period exceed NZD 60,000 or are likely to exceed that threshold in the next 12 months; voluntary GST registration for the remote-services business may take effect immediately. The completed registration in myIR creates the GST account attached to the same IRD number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
None comparable to a United States check-the-box election. A New Zealand limited company is by default an entity-level taxpayer at the corporate rate of 28% under the Income Tax Act 2007. A qualifying closely held company may elect Look-Through Company status under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, subject to the ownership and eligibility tests contained therein, allowing the company to be treated as fiscally transparent. Hybrid mismatch rules introduced by the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 are mandatory and cannot be displaced by election. GST grouping under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 is available by application for related companies. No territorial, tax-exempt-zone, or pass-through election applies to the standard New Zealand limited company except the LTC regime noted above.

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
For goods, zero-rating is available under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand in the course of a taxable activity and retains contemporaneous evidence of the export. For the remote screen sharing and unattended machine telemetry software service in scope (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 511210), zero-rating is available under section 11A as a supply of remote services where the recipient is a non-resident who is outside New Zealand while the services are performed, the services are not supplied to a person in New Zealand, and the services are not supplied in connection with land or movable property located in New Zealand. The supplier must maintain evidence of foreign jurisdiction including customer registration or residential address, payment-rail billing address, IP geolocation, actual use and session logs from the remote support telemetry platform, and a GST invoice marked zero-rated. Supplies to New Zealand-resident customers are subject to 15% GST.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
New Zealand maintains an open, freely convertible currency regime under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act and the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act. There are no general foreign exchange controls, no foreign currency surrender requirements, and no central bank approval gates for day-to-day current account or capital account transactions. Current account payments such as software subscription fees, royalties, interest, and dividend remittances can be executed without RBNZ prior approval; capital account movements including equity injections, shareholder loans, and liquidation distributions are equally lawful, provided the underlying corporate act is valid, source-of-funds evidence is clear, and the receiving bank's AML/CFT and sanctions screening is satisfied. The operational friction is therefore bank due diligence rather than statutory exchange control. Non-resident investors may encounter longer settlement times when supporting documents are inadequate, but New Zealand does not restrict repatriation of profits or invested capital. Profit remittance under the entity-level taxation model requires normal board approvals and, for larger dividends, solvency certification; there is no surrender or conversion quota. The NZD is freely traded, so hedging depth against USD, AUD, EUR, and GBP is strong, while the RBNZ retains prudential oversight of banks rather than exchange authorisation.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts indefinitely in external or domestic foreign currency accounts, convert at any time, and use the proceeds freely. The only residual obligations are AML/CFT record-keeping, balance-of-payments statistical reporting, and the usual foreign-owned entity reporting requirements to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand.

            Stripe
            Fit: High
          

          
Rating: Medium-Low as a primary treasury rail, but acceptable as a secondary collection rail. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, and Airwallex are not licensed banks in New Zealand and cannot provide RBNZ-supervised settlement accounts or true escrow services. New Zealand does not have a dedicated EMI licensing category; any provider holding customer funds must be registered under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and become an AML/CFT reporting entity under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Compliant routing for non-resident shareholders is a SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the investor's own bank account to NZ_LTD's local account at ANZ, ASB, BNZ, Westpac, or Kiwibank, using the payment narrative 'Share capital subscription - NZ_LTD' or 'Shareholder loan - NZ_LTD' to identify the economic purpose. New Zealand has no central foreign exchange purpose code register, but banks use balance-of-payments classifications; the payment message should state 'Direct investment - equity' in the appropriate narrative block. The bank will require the certificate of incorporation, shareholder register, board allotment resolution, and Companies Office annual return record to verify the capital. There is no minimum capital and no statutory capital verification; an external auditor can confirm the injection from the bank credit advice, SWIFT confirmation, share register entry, and director's statutory declaration.For trade settlements from NZ_LTD, the preferred clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for direct wire disbursements to suppliers, commercial letters of credit for high-value hardware or white-label licensing procurement, and documentary collections (D/P and D/A) where the underlying documentation must be matched with invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, and quality certificates. For the core remote screen-share and IoT subscription business, settlement occurs through Stripe and Paddle card rails, with net proceeds automatically converted to NZD and swept to the local bank account. Any manual FX conversion out of the local account can be executed through the bank's FX desk with same-day settlement for major currencies.Deliverable FX forward contracts in NZD/USD, NZD/EUR, and NZD/AUD for genuine commercial exposures with tenors up to 24 months, subject to the bank's master agreement and board-approved treasury policy.Non-deliverable forwards are not generally necessary for the NZD because the currency is freely convertible; if a counterparty insists on an NDF, the trade must be treated as a non-standard derivative requiring specific RBNZ margin and banking approval.Cross-currency swaps are permissible for managing long-term NZD/USD shareholder loan exposures when matched to the underlying debt service timetable, with mandated collateral and margin rules under the RBNZ's derivatives policy.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 in annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers the expectation that the NZ taxpayer will prepare and retain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation, including a Local File and, where applicable, a Master File. Multinational groups with consolidated group revenue above NZD 750 million must file Country-by-Country reports to IRD.

      
Economic substance scope: New Zealand has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to UAE ESR, but the general anti-avoidance rule under ITA 2007 s BG 1 and the OECD multilateral instrument principal purpose test restrict treaty and loss benefits for shell arrangements. For an ISIC 6201 remote desktop and unattended telemetry software business, Core Income-Generating Activities include strategic software design, engineering development, server infrastructure management, IoT data analytics, compliance oversight and customer support. If those activities are not performed by the NZ Ltd, the company will be viewed as a letter-box entity and profits may be reattributed under ss GB 1-GB 4, with treaty benefits denied.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion Through Offshore Revenue Routing
        
Statutory sanction: TAA 1994 s 143A (evasion offence) creates liability for a fine of up to NZD 250,000 for an individual and NZD 500,000 for a body corporate, plus imprisonment for up to 5 years. Inland Revenue may also impose a 150% shortfall penalty under TAA 1994 s 141EB for evasion, plus use-of-money interest. A departure prohibition order under TAA 1994 s 157A may be issued to prevent the director from leaving New Zealand while tax debt is outstanding.

        
Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the company is used as a mere facade for the director's personal profit. Directors can be personally assessed for misapplied company money under Companies Act 1993 s 300, made liable for fraudulent trading under s 320, ordered to pay restitution in civil proceedings, disqualified under s 382, and criminally prosecuted as parties to tax evasion. Civil asset recovery under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2009 can reach personal assets and travel restrictions may be imposed.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Profit Stripping
        
Statutory sanction: ITA 2007 s GC 6 permits IRD to reconstruct the transaction at an arm's-length amount. The tax avoidance arrangement is void under s BG 1. Shortfall penalties escalate from 40% for an unacceptable tax position to 100% for an abusive tax position and 150% for evasion under TAA 1994 ss 141B-141EB. Late payment interest accrues daily, and non-compliance with CbC filing obligations attracts separate penalties under TAA 1994 s 78GB.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing declarations or knowingly approve sham arrangements are personally exposed to civil liability for losses caused by reckless trading under Companies Act 1993 s 135 and for breach of director duties under ss 131-136. Courts may lift the veil and order restitution of diverted profit. Directors may also face criminal liability under TAA 1994 s 247 for false statements and under Crimes Act 1961 s 228 for dishonest use of documents, with imprisonment exposure of up to 7 years.

      

      
      

        Shell Company and DTA Benefit Denial for Lack of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: ITA 2007 s BG 1 and s GB 1 treat the arrangement as void and reattribute the income to where the economic substance is located. The OECD Multilateral Instrument principal purpose test and DTA limitation-of-benefit provisions will deny reduced NRWT rates on dividends, interest and royalties. The taxpayer faces a 100% abusive-tax-position shortfall penalty and payment of the 30% NRWT shortfall on dividend distributions, with interest. Companies Act 1993 s 220 may also be used by the Registrar to strike off or place conditions on a dormant shell.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for reckless trading under Companies Act 1993 s 135 and for breach of duty under s 131. Where the shell is used to evade tax, the corporate veil is pierced and the director must restore diverted assets. Directors may be disqualified for a period of up to 5 years under s 382, made subject to a TAA 1994 s 157A departure prohibition order, and prosecuted for criminal tax evasion. Any personal benefit obtained through the sham structure can be recovered under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2009 civil forfeiture regime.
