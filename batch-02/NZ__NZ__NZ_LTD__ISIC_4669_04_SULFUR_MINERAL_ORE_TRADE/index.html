Then apply to the Inland Revenue Department for an IRD number using the non-resident company registration application accessible through myIR or the IRD application services; the application must attach the Companies Office certificate of incorporation, the company constitution, the registered office address, and particulars of all directors and ultimate beneficial shareholders. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from New Zealand by the GST-registered supplier are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, including granular elemental sulfur mineral ore delivered for dry bulk marine transport to a non-resident purchaser. The supplier must prove that the goods have permanently left New Zealand in the ordinary course of the supply; goods supplied to a non-resident for subsequent export may qualify under the export-delivery provisions of section 11(1)-(2) if the supplier has custody, control, and documentary evidence of exportation. Services related to the export trade are zero-rated only where they fall within either section 11 (supplied to non-residents and performed outside New Zealand) or one of the statutory zero-rating categories in section 11A; to avoid an output-GST clawback, every zero-rated export must be supported by contemporaneous transport, customs, and settlement documentation. New Zealand does not impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for GST zero-rating; the 180-day parameter below is adopted as the entity's internal documentary-contractual standard for retaining proof that export proceeds have been settled.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Export Declaration (electronic export entry lodged via the Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill evidencing physical shipment of the granular elemental sulfur cargo on a dry bulk vessel
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of analysis or weight (sulfur mineral ore quality/quantity certification)
  • Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) and related negotiation documents (drafted bill of exchange, presentation schedule, and transport document) evidencing the cross-border trade receivable
  • Bank SWIFT MT103 or MT202 credit confirmation, or an inward remittance advice from a New Zealand registered bank, confirming settlement of the export sale proceeds in foreign currency

New Zealand operates no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or pre-clearance digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is voluntary and is facilitated through the PEPPOL network under the New Zealand e-invoicing framework. Registered GST filers must file GST returns electronically through myIR on a monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly taxable-period frequency; annual GST returns are not available. Tax agents and intermediaries using bulk-integrated software may use Inland Revenue's approved API/web-service channels for lodgement, but no public fiscal-stamping or clearance approval is required for invoice validity.

If an export zero-rating position is not substantiated, Inland Revenue may issue a default assessment under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 requiring the supplier to account for output GST at 15% on the originally zero-rated supply, effectively clawing back the zero rate retrospectively. Inland Revenue may also impose shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: up to 20% for failure to take reasonable care or an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, and up to 100% for fraudulent evasion or an arrangement intended to defeat the application of the GST rules; use-of-money interest runs from the original due date of the affected GST return. Criminal prosecution under section 148 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 is possible where documents are altered, forged, or knowingly provided to obstruct or deceive Inland Revenue. The entity must retain the full customs and settlement evidence bundle for at least seven years and make it available for audit review by Inland Revenue or the New Zealand Customs Service.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates an open, freely convertible currency regime under a floating exchange rate. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not maintain exchange controls, restrictions on current or capital account transactions, or mandatory foreign currency surrender requirements. The New Zealand dollar is fully convertible for trade settlement, dividend remittance, capital repatriation, and inter-company payments. However, all transactions are channelled through banks subject to the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and banks must ensure the underlying trade or investment is genuine. Practical convertibility constraints arise mainly in documentation: a New Zealand incorporated trading company must provide tax clearance, financial statements, and source-of-funds evidence to support large cross-border flows. Profits may be remitted as dividends without exchange control approval, subject to a 15% non-resident withholding tax on dividends or a lower applicable treaty rate, and to resident withholding tax on interest payments. There is no central bank approval for profit remittance; the banking relationship and tax residency compliance are the main gates. Thus, currency convertibility is high, but robust anti-money-laundering controls may delay anomalous or poorly documented transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender requirements in New Zealand. Exporters of sulfur mineral ore may retain 100% of foreign currency proceeds in the original currency, convert them at any time, or hold them in external accounts. No conversion timeline applies. For balance-of-payments statistical purposes, transaction-level export data must be reported to Statistics New Zealand through the reporting bank under the Statistics Act 1975. Supporting documentation such as the bill of lading, commercial invoice, export declaration, and surveyor reports must be retained under AML/CFT record-keeping obligations.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore fintech electronic money institutions are not absolutely prohibited from providing limited payment services to a New Zealand entity, but they are legally and commercially incapable of satisfying the full treasury requirements for ISIC 4669 sulfur mineral ore trade. A genuine documentary letter of credit, trust-account capital verification, trade-checked disbursement, and underlying commodity trade finance infrastructure require a licensed bank in New Zealand. The Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the AML/CFT Act 2009 impose licensing, disclosure, and compliance conduct standards that offshore EMIs without an New Zealand financial service provider licence cannot fully meet. Therefore, an EMI may support only incidental operational fiat payments, while the primary cross-border clearing and liquidity rail must be anchored at a Tier-1 New Zealand incorporated commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions. Where the aggregate value of related-party sales, purchases, services, finance, or royalties exceeds this threshold, the taxpayer must maintain contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation, supported by a benchmarking study.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Nevertheless, the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, together with the OECD-aligned transfer pricing rules and the business continuity test, requires the NZ Ltd to demonstrate actual operational substance. For a sulfur trading company, this means having real employees or contracted management in New Zealand, genuine decision-making authority, physical or documented commercial infrastructure, and a substantive risk-bearing and risk-control profile. A letterbox company that merely books invoices in New Zealand will be disregarded or reconstructed for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may impose a shortfall penalty of 150% of the evaded tax under the Tax Administration Act 1994, together with use-of-money interest. Criminal prosecution under section 143B may result in imprisonment of up to 7 years and substantial monetary fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns or procure tax evasion face personal criminal liability, civil restitution orders, reparation orders, and disqualification from managing companies. Departure prohibition orders may be used to prevent overseas travel while tax liabilities remain outstanding.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The arrangement may be disregarded under the general anti-avoidance rule, with income reconstructed under the substantial economic substance doctrine. Shortfall penalties for an abusive tax position can reach 40%, and the Commissioner may issue a formal reconstruction assessment plus interest. Directors may also be liable under company law for fraudulent trading.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where a company is used as a mere façade. Directors may be personally liable for civil compensation to creditors and the Crown, barred from acting as directors for up to ten years, subjected to pecuniary penalties, and in egregious cases exposed to criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may adjust taxable income under section GC 13 of the Income Tax Act 2007, impose shortfall penalties of up to 150% in cases of evasion, and apply use-of-money interest. Inadequate contemporaneous documentation also shifts the burden of proof in disputes and may attract separate documentation penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's-length trading arrangements without commercial substance may be held personally liable for tax shortfalls and penalties where they were knowingly involved in structuring the arrangement to avoid New Zealand tax.
AML/CFT Regulatory Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a body corporate may face civil penalties of up to NZD 2 million for each contravention. Senior management and directors involved in egregious AML failures may face separate fines and custodial penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors with direct responsibility for AML/CFT compliance may be personally fined, removed from management, and in cases involving money laundering or sanctions evasion, exposed to imprisonment. The corporate veil will not protect a director who knowingly allows the entity to be used as a conduit for tainted funds.
