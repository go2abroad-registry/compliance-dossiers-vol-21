Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Oman Tax Authority (OTA) — الهيئة العامة للضرائب; established by Royal Decree No. 40/2020 through the integration of the former General Secretariat of Taxation and the Customs Administration. Official digital portal for TIN issuance, VAT registration, corporate income tax filing, VAT returns, certificates, and authorizations: https://taxoman.gov.om. Customs export clearance and Bayan single-window processing are operated under OTA customs jurisdiction.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the Single Person Company (SPC) as a one-shareholder limited-liability company via the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion (MoCIIP) investor portal and obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate; the SPC may be 100% foreign-owned. OTA automatically issues the corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon CR generation, so no separate income-tax registration filing is required for the resident SPC. 2) Maintain the corporate income tax file with OTA and file the annual corporate income tax return (Form CIT); the standard tax year-end for an SPC is 31 December unless OTA approves a different year-end. 3) For VAT: submit the VAT registration e-application through the Taxoman portal within 30 days of crossing the mandatory registration threshold (OMR 38,500 of taxable supplies/imports in any rolling 12-month window) or earlier if the threshold will be crossed within the first 12 months of operations; attach the CR, lease or ownership title of the production facility, shareholder and beneficial owner identification documents, opening or provisional financial statements, bank confirmation letter, and power of attorney for authorized signatories; OTA issues the VAT Registration Certificate with a 15-digit VAT ID. 4) Register the SPC in the Bayan customs system to obtain the customs computerized code required for export declarations of finished instant-noodle consignments.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Omani SPC is a separate juridical person and a tax-resident company subject to corporate income tax at entity level under the Income Tax Law (Royal Decree No. 28/2009, as amended); there is no pass-through, transparent LLC, or tax-transparent treatment available for an Omani-incorporated SPC. Statutory CIT: the first OMR 30,000 of taxable income is exempt and the remaining taxable income is taxed at 12%; eligible small enterprises with gross annual revenue at or below OMR 100,000 may apply for the SME income-tax exemption. No sub-sovereign or municipal corporate income tax exists; VAT, customs duties, and municipal license fees apply separately.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch or hybrid-instrument elections exist under Omani tax law; entity classification follows statutory form exclusively. Elective mechanisms available to the SPC: (i) VAT group registration election — two or more related Omani-resident persons may form one VAT group with a single VAT registration, aggregated threshold, and one periodic VAT return under the group-registration provisions of the VAT Executive Regulations; (ii) small-business income tax exemption election — the SPC may elect the SME regime where first-year gross revenue will not exceed OMR 100,000, reverting to standard 12% CIT when the cap is exceeded; (iii) partial exemption and special pro-rata input-VAT recovery method elections for mixed zero-rated export, standard-rated domestic, and exempt supply portfolios, subject to OTA approval; and (iv) transfer-pricing documentation threshold options for related-party export transactions under Oman's BEPS-aligned framework.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration applies where taxable supplies and taxable imports exceed OMR 38,500 in any rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration is available from OMR 19,250. A newly formed instant-noodle manufacturing SPC must register if it reasonably expects to exceed OMR 38,500 within its first 12 months; the registration application is due within 30 days of entering the mandatory threshold. Zero-rated export supplies are counted for threshold purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies to goods physically exported from Oman to a destination outside Oman, including GCC states subject to destination-state VAT treatment, where: (i) the goods leave Oman within 90 days of the time of supply; (ii) the SPC holds the electronic Customs Export Declaration from Bayan, transport documents (bill of lading or air waybill), the 0%-rated commercial invoice, and evidence of receipt of consideration; and (iii) the SPC demonstrates that title and risk passed to the non-resident buyer. Instant rice-noodle consignments exported through Sohar Port, Salalah Port, or Muscat/Sultan Qaboos Port on letter of credit or telegraphic transfer payment terms qualify for zero-rating, provided the SPC is the declared consignor and the export-proceeds evidence matches the invoice value. Domestically sold processed noodles are standard-rated at 5% unless the specific product appears on the OTA/competent authority basic-food zero-rating list.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan electronic declaration accepted by Oman Customs/OTA)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the physical departure of goods from Oman within 90 days of supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit evidence showing receipt of export consideration
  • Commercial VAT invoice issued at 0% with the SPC's VAT ID, invoice number, and export reference
  • Packing list and export certificate of origin (Oman Chamber of Commerce) where required by the buyer or letter of credit
  • Purchase order, contract, or LC/ TT instruction evidencing the underlying export sale to the non-resident buyer

Oman has not yet implemented mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transaction-level API reporting. Current compliance: every VAT-registered person must issue a VAT invoice containing the statutory mandatory data fields (bilingual Arabic/Latin content, sequential numbering, VAT ID, description, 0%/5% tax line, total), issue credit/debit notes for adjustments, and retain original invoices, records, and export evidence for 10 years. VAT returns must be filed electronically via the Taxoman portal. OTA has published a phased e-invoicing roadmap; once operative, taxpayers will need ERP/API integration with OTA systems. Until that mandate enters into force, PDF and paper invoices without fiscal stamps remain valid, and the Bayan customs declaration operates as the export clearance event in the OTA data environment.

OTA audit procedures cross-match Bayan customs departure data with VAT returns and bank remittance records. Any export invoice lacking complete statutory evidence within the 90-day removal window is re-characterized as a standard-rated domestic supply, triggering assessment of the 5% VAT difference, monthly late-payment fines of 1% of the unpaid tax per month, and administrative penalties under the VAT Law and Tax Procedures Law. Input VAT previously claimed in respect of failed export sales may be clawed back in the same assessment, and VAT refunds are suspended pending audit closure. Deliberate or fraudulent zero-rating — including fabricated export declarations, overstated export values, or goods returned covertly into Oman — constitutes tax evasion punishable by imprisonment of up to 2 years and a fine of between one and two times the evaded tax, plus retrospective assessments within the OTA audit limitation window (5 years standard, extended in evasion cases). Oman tax law does not codify an independent exchange-control repatriation deadline; the 180-day convention in this dossier reflects the Central Bank of Oman's banking-sector monitoring benchmark for export proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Omani Rial (OMR) is fully convertible for current-account transactions and pegged to the USD at 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD, giving foreign-owned SPCs de facto elimination of USD conversion risk on trade corridors. Capital-account operations, including 100% foreign equity injection and dividend/profit repatriation, are allowed without material restrictions under the Foreign Capital Investment Law (Royal Decree 50/2019); however, the executing bank must evidence: (i) valid Commercial Registration (CR) and Tax Identification Number (TIN); (ii) a board resolution authorising distribution; (iii) Oman Tax Authority tax-clearance confirmation or audited financials proving distributable reserves; and (iv) purpose-code statements. Remittance risk is primarily documentary rather than regulatory: lack of audited local accounts or mismatched purpose codes can suspend outbound MT103 transfers for enhanced due diligence. No CBO pre-approval is required for trade-related payments within ordinary banking thresholds, but the CBO requires banks to capture the underlying flow for statistical reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Oman imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may retain 100% of export receipts in original foreign currency in designated export proceeds accounts. Under CBO-sanctioned trade-settlement practice and standard bank facility conditions, export proceeds (e.g., sale of retort-packed rice noodles to GCC, African, and Indian Ocean buyers) must be repatriated into the Oman-based account within 180 days of the bill-of-lading date; any extension requires a written commercial justification filed with the onboarding bank and, if beyond 12 months, CBO notification. Interest earned on foreign-currency export balances is freely usable intra-Oman, and onward transfer outside Oman follows standard foreign-transfer procedures.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Oman (CBO), established under the Banking Law (Royal Decree 114/2000, as amended). The CBO is the sole monetary authority and foreign-exchange supervisor, administering the Omani Rial pegged exchange-rate regime, authorising all commercial banking licenses, issuing balance-of-payments reporting mandates, and enforcing the AML/CFT rules applicable to cross-border treasury flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for any treasury-critical function. Foreign fintech and digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) domiciled outside Oman are not recognised as banks by the CBO, cannot generate local OMR IBANs, cannot access the CBO RTGS or the Oman Clearing House, and cannot hold statutory capital escrow balances for an Omani SPC. An offshore EMI may serve only as an ancillary collection tool for small receivables, and its use beyond that triggers automatic enhanced AML review under the CBO AML/CFT regulations issued pursuant to Royal Decree 30/2016. The permissible fintech route is engagement with a licensed domestic payment service provider using the CBO sandbox framework, but even this cannot displace a bank account for LC negotiation, inward capital verification, or dividend remittance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's bank directly to the SPC's capital account at an Oman bank, with narrative fields carrying: the SPC legal name, CR number, IBAN (OM...), purpose code classified as Direct Investment / Equity Capital under the CBO balance-of-payments statistics framework, and the remitter's full legal name. The Oman receiving bank will pre-clear the wire for source-of-funds integrity before crediting, convert USD to OMR at the onshore spot rate if so mandated by the account mandate, and issue a capital-import memo to the entity. The entity must retain the SWIFT confirmation and the capital-import memo as the forensic record to support the SPC's paid-up capital ledger, for Omani statutory audit, and for future dividend repatriation proportionality.Outbound trade settlements run over two principal rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account raw-material payables, machinery installments, and supplier advances, cleared via the correspondent USD network (New York CHIPS/Fedwire) for USD, or via the CBO RTGS for OMR-denominated domestic legs; and (2) Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (MT700, sight or 90-180 day usance) for bulk rice, seasoning, retort pouches, and packaging machinery purchases, with negotiation against conforming shipping documents. Foreign fintech and digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) domiciled outside Oman are not recognised as banks by the CBO, cannot generate local OMR IBANs, cannot access the CBO RTGS or the Oman Clearing House, and cannot hold statutory capital escrow balances for an Omani SPC. An offshore EMI may serve only as an ancillary collection tool for small receivables, and its use beyond that triggers automatic enhanced AML review under the CBO AML/CFT regulations issued pursuant to Royal Decree 30/2016. The permissible fintech route is engagement with a licensed domestic payment service provider using the CBO sandbox framework, but even this cannot displace a bank account for LC negotiation, inward capital verification, or dividend remittance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (with TP master data and benchmark): required where aggregate related-party transactions in a tax year exceed OMR 300,000; Master File: required where the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds OMR 30,000,000 and the Omani SPC is a group member. Thresholds apply per the Executive Regulations of Royal Decree 9/2023.

Economic substance scope: Oman applies Economic Substance Regulations principles through the new Income Tax Law and Tax Authority practice. The SPC must demonstrate real commercial activity in Oman: physical production facility, adequate staff, genuine board decision-making, and active management of procurement, manufacturing, storage, and sales risks. Letter-box companies claiming Omani residency or treaty benefits without substance are disregarded, their related-party expenses are disallowed, and their license may be revoked.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine of 50% to 100% of evaded tax plus 1% monthly late-payment surcharge; publication of the taxpayer's name in the Official Gazette; criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false returns are personally liable for the evaded tax and civil restitution; courts may impose a travel ban, asset freeze, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax residence certificate; re-characterization of revenue; disallowance of intercompany expenses; fine up to OMR 10,000; commercial registry strike-off and license revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors and the SPC's sole shareholder may be held liable for unpaid taxes after corporate dissolution; travel bans and blacklisting by the Ministry of Commerce are imposed in substance-shop cases.
Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties of OMR 50,000 to OMR 100,000 for the corporate entity; licence cancellation; freezing of bank accounts; potential publication of sanction notices.

Director personal liability: Personal fines up to OMR 20,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years under Oman AML Law (RD 30/2016 as amended) for directors who knowingly facilitate laundering; travel bans apply throughout investigation.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment increasing taxable income; penalty of 1% per month on the additional tax; separate fixed documentation penalty of OMR 5,000 for failure to file the TP disclosure.

Director personal liability: The director signing the TP declaration is personally liable for the TP adjustment where misstatements are wilful; civil restitution of the additional tax and travel bans may be ordered.
Non-Filing and Advance Payment Default

Statutory sanction: Cumulative penalties: OMR 100 to OMR 1,000 fixed fine, OMR 50 per day of delay, 1% monthly surcharge capped at 30%, plus additional fine of up to OMR 5,000 after formal notice.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly liable for taxes and fines if the SPC is struck off without settlement; the Tax Authority may issue a travel ban against board members and seize personal assets.
