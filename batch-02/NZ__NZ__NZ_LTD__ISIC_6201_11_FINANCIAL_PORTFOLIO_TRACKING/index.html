Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office services are permissible for registered office purposes under the Companies Act 1993, provided the address is a physical location in New Zealand where documents can be served. However, for a fintech company engaged in financial portfolio tracking, banks and the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) may require a physical presence or a dedicated office for AML verification and tax correspondence. For this low-risk fintech, a virtual office is likely acceptable, but the company should consider a serviced office or co-working space to demonstrate commercial substance.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand has no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Limited company under the Companies Act 1993. The board determines the amount of issued capital, shares may be issued for a nominal or deferred consideration, and there is no blocked escrow capital account requirement. The incorporation application must state the issued shares, the nominal value, the number of fully paid or partially paid shares, and any unpaid share capital as at the date of registration.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no physical premises requiring Civil Defence fire safety certification, structural load inspections, or industrial workplace safety approval. General occupational health and safety obligations under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 apply only to normal office/remote working conditions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue / Te Tari Taake), electronic filing and account portal: myIR (myIR.ird.govt.nz); company incorporation and non-resident registration are administered by the New Zealand Companies Office

E-government filing portal

The statutory tax identifier for all New Zealand tax purposes is the IRD number; there is no separate VAT ID. For NZ_LTD: (1) incorporate the limited company with the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993 through the online Companies Register; (2) apply for an IRD number in the incorporation application, or later through myIR, and receive the IRD number from Inland Revenue; (3) once the IRD number is allocated, register for GST through myIR if taxable supplies made in or connected with New Zealand are expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, or if registration is otherwise required because the threshold has already been crossed; (4) maintain a New Zealand registered office and at least one director, and keep the Companies Office and myIR profiles updated. For a foreign non-resident entity carrying on business in New Zealand, the entity must first register as an overseas company with the New Zealand Companies Office, then apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number; a foreign entity that only supplies remote services to New Zealand-based consumers may register for GST directly as a non-resident GST registrant through myIR. A financial portfolio tracking software business under ISIC 6201 does not require a statutory tax license, and Inland Revenue uses the IRD number for both income tax and GST obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand does not have a US-style check-the-box election. Eligible close-held New Zealand companies with no more than five owners may elect under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007 to become a look-through company, which passes taxable profits, losses and imputation credits to shareholders. Hybrid mismatch rules introduced by the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 operate as statutory overrides, not as elective regimes; no hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies made in or connected with New Zealand in any 12-month period, using either actual past supplies or expected future supplies; an offshore supplier of remote services to New Zealand consumers has the same NZD 60,000 GST registration threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, goods physically exported from New Zealand are zero-rated if exported by the supplier or by the purchaser within the statutory removal period, ordinarily within 28 days, and transport evidence is retained. Under section 11A, services, including SaaS and financial portfolio tracking software, are zero-rated where the services are supplied to a non-resident customer who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, the services are not supplied to that non-resident for carrying on a New Zealand taxable activity, and the services are not directly connected with New Zealand land or goods. The New Zealand supplier must obtain and retain contemporaneous evidence of customer non-residence, the offshore receipt or use of the service, and the commercial terms of the cross-border supply.

  • For physical exports: New Zealand Customs export declaration lodged electronically through the Trade Single Window, including the Customs-assigned export transaction or journey number
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight), sea waybill, or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list, plus certificate of origin when required by the destination market
  • For zero-rated digital services and SaaS: signed contract or service agreement, customer non-residence declaration or business registration extract, login or IP usage logs showing offshore recipient access, and a GST tax invoice marked as zero-rated
  • Foreign exchange repatriation evidence: New Zealand does not issue or use an Inward Remittance Certificate; retain SWIFT MT103 or remittance credits, bank foreign currency conversion records, and credited export proceeds evidence in the New Zealand bank account as auditable substantiation

New Zealand has no real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no continuous transaction control API, and no anti-fraud digital stamp requirement. Electronic invoicing is voluntary under the PEPPOL-based New Zealand e-Invoicing Network. A GST-registered supplier must issue a GST tax invoice under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, retain supporting documentation for Inland Revenue audit, and lodge periodic GST returns through myIR or approved gateway software. Inland Revenue does not clear individual invoices in real time but uses risk assessment, data matching and audit review.

New Zealand imposes no statutory foreign exchange repatriation deadline, so the deadline value is set to 0; however, the exporter must be able to substantiate zero-rating with contemporaneous documentation when the GST return is filed and when Inland Revenue requests it. Inland Revenue may reassess under section 108 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 generally within the four-year time bar, subject to extended reassessment for fraud, evasion or gross carelessness. Incorrect zero-rating creates a GST shortfall with use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties under Part 7A of the Tax Administration Act 1994, with tiers including 20 percent for not taking reasonable care or unacceptable tax positions, 40 percent for gross carelessness, 100 percent for abusive tax positions, and up to 150 percent for evasion. Retrospective GST, penalties and interest may be recovered from the New Zealand supplier if the export documentation bundle is false, incomplete or unsupported by actual cross-border supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand Dollar is freely convertible with no capital or current account exchange controls. New Zealand maintains a fully liberalized foreign exchange market and a floating exchange rate regime. Direct investment in an ISIC 6201 cloud software holding company is permitted without Reserve Bank approval, provided the Overseas Investment Act 2005 sensitive-land and significant-business-asset thresholds are not triggered; a software special-purpose vehicle with no New Zealand land and low asset value is categorically outside the consent regime. Non-resident shareholders may subscribe for equity and receive dividends, redemption proceeds, or liquidation distributions in New Zealand Dollars without repatriation approval. Profit remittance is unrestricted as a matter of exchange control law; the only relevant frictions are tax-based, namely non-resident dividend withholding tax at 15% for imputed dividends or 30% otherwise, subject to treaty relief under applicable double tax agreements. Current-account software export receipts may be held in foreign currency or converted freely; no central bank approval, registration, or certification is required for any cross-border payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation deadline, and no export receipt certification regime. Software subscription revenue derived from Stripe and Apple IAP settlement may be retained offshore in a foreign currency account, swapped on the spot market, or repatriated to the New Zealand operating bank account at the company's sole discretion. The only residual administrative duty is statistical: banks may ask for a balance-of-payments nature-of-transaction code on an incoming wire, such as BOP code 5B2 for inward equity investment or the appropriate services code for software export revenue, and the Reserve Bank may collect aggregated balance-of-payments survey data. Failure to provide a statistical code may delay clearing but does not constitute an exchange control violation.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (the central bank and prudential regulator for registered banks); the Financial Markets Authority supervises financial market conduct under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013; the Department of Internal Affairs supervises AML/CFT reporting entities under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009; the Overseas Investment Office administers foreign investment consent under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 but does not operate exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low as a primary treasury rail. New Zealand does not operate an electronic-money-institution licensing regime in the European sense, and no foreign EMI or digital bank holds a New Zealand registered-bank licence. A foreign EMI such as a UK or EU payment institution, or a United States non-bank money transmitter, may lawfully provide auxiliary payment services to a New Zealand resident company only if it is registered on the Financial Service Providers Register under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and, if its activity amounts to money remittance or a financial service, becomes an AML/CFT reporting entity under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. Funds held at a foreign EMI are not protected by the New Zealand under the Deposit Takers Act 2023 deposit compensation scheme, create non-bank counterparty risk, and are unlikely to satisfy New Zealand company-law treasury custody expectations. A foreign EMI is therefore viable only as an auxiliary pay-in or pay-out collection rail, never as the primary liquidity and clearing account; the core operating account must sit with a Reserve-Bank-registered Tier 1 New Zealand bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident ultimate beneficial owners and a layered holding or family-trust ownership chain obscuring the natural-person beneficiaries of the interest in the entity.

Root cause: Non-resident ultimate beneficial owners and a layered holding or family-trust ownership chain obscuring the natural-person beneficiaries of the interest in the entity.

Defense evidence: (1) Historical Companies Office registry extract and certificate of incorporation; (2) group organizational chart tracing each intermediate entity to every natural-person beneficial owner; (3) signed ultimate beneficial owner declaration identifying all persons holding 25% or more; (4) notarized passport and proof-of-address for each ultimate beneficial owner; (5) redacted trust deed or trustee and settlor identification where a trust is a shareholder, limited to information legally disclosable; (6) board resolution ratifying the ownership structure; and (7) professional legal opinion from New Zealand counsel confirming that the structure does not exist for tax evasion or concealment.
Unexplained source of inbound share premium or initial capitalization from private angel or venture investors, raising suspicion of undisclosed third-party funding.

Root cause: Unexplained source of inbound share premium or initial capitalization from private angel or venture investors, raising suspicion of undisclosed third-party funding.

Defense evidence: (1) Executed share subscription agreement and board resolution approving the issue of shares and the premium; (2) remitting investor's bank statements covering at least six months demonstrating the source of funds; (3) investor identity documents and passport verification; (4) source-of-wealth evidence such as employment contracts, sale of business deeds, or dividend statements; (5) Companies Office share register entry evidencing allotment; and (6) confirmation that the funds will be used solely for working capital, cloud hosting, and software development.
Misclassification of the platform as a money-service business, payment institution, or custodian because the software application collects customer credit-card payments or interfaces with external brokerage accounts.

Root cause: Misclassification of the platform as a money-service business, payment institution, or custodian because the software application collects customer credit-card payments or interfaces with external brokerage accounts.

Defense evidence: (1) Formal legal opinion from New Zealand counsel confirming that the entity provides only data analytics and investment-tracking software and does not provide financial advice, discretionary investment management, custody, foreign exchange services, or payment processing; (2) executed Stripe Merchant of Record agreement and Apple Developer Program Paid Applications Agreement demonstrating that Stripe and Apple are the merchants of record and that settlement flows arise from software subscription sales, not customer funds; (3) product and user-interface narrative or demo showing no money movement by the company; (4) business risk assessment demonstrating low money-laundering and counter-financing-of-terrorism risk; and (5) the company's analysis of why the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 do not apply to the business model.
Failure to maintain a New Zealand-resident director as required by section 151 of the Companies Act 1993, resulting in automatic rejection at the bank's pre-screen stage.

Root cause: Silent-company cash-flow profile with a projection of first-year revenue only from anticipated Stripe and Apple IAP settlements, which banks view as insufficient evidence of genuine ongoing business operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must effect subscription capital by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the company's Tier 1 New Zealand bank account using the following discipline: beneficiary name NZ_LTD, beneficiary BIC of the New Zealand clearing bank, beneficiary account in NZD (or a designated USD sub-ledger), and a payment narrative stating SUBSCRIPTION FOR ORDINARY SHARES IN NZ_LTD / BOP CODE 5B2 / SHARE CAPITAL INJECTION. No Reserve Bank approval, no Overseas Investment Office consent, and no export surrender certificate are required for an ISIC 6201 software holding company because the transaction does not concern sensitive land or significant business assets. The receiving bank will nevertheless run a balance-of-payments statistical questionnaire and apply standard AML/CFT verification; the remitting shareholder must furnish the source-of-funds package described in the corporate banking blueprint before or at the time of the transfer so that the funds are not entrapped in a hold. If the shareholder remits in USD, AUD, or EUR, the receiving bank will execute a spot conversion into NZD at the corporate dealing desk, or the funds may remain in the multi-currency ledger at the company's instruction. Upon any future redemption of capital, return of share premium, or winding-up distribution, the only applicable clearance requirements are tax clearance in respect of non-resident withholding tax and compliance with the Companies Act 1993 capital-maintenance rules; exchange control imposes no obstacle.For an ISIC 6201 software subscription business, outbound treasury flows are operational rather than trade-related. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Low as a primary treasury rail. New Zealand does not operate an electronic-money-institution licensing regime in the European sense, and no foreign EMI or digital bank holds a New Zealand registered-bank licence. A foreign EMI such as a UK or EU payment institution, or a United States non-bank money transmitter, may lawfully provide auxiliary payment services to a New Zealand resident company only if it is registered on the Financial Service Providers Register under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and, if its activity amounts to money remittance or a financial service, becomes an AML/CFT reporting entity under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. Funds held at a foreign EMI are not protected by the New Zealand under the Deposit Takers Act 2023 deposit compensation scheme, create non-bank counterparty risk, and are unlikely to satisfy New Zealand company-law treasury custody expectations. A foreign EMI is therefore viable only as an auxiliary pay-in or pay-out collection rail, never as the primary liquidity and clearing account; the core operating account must sit with a Reserve-Bank-registered Tier 1 New Zealand bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 1 million aggregate cross-border related-party transactions per income year (mandatory contemporaneous documentation above this de minimis threshold; below this, basic records are still required).

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations code exists in New Zealand. Substance is evaluated through the general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1), CFC attribution rules, and transfer pricing analyses. A company managing a financial portfolio tracking platform must demonstrate active business functions in New Zealand, including substantive staff, premises, and risk-bearing decision-making, to defend its profit allocation and tax residency.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994 s 143A; imprisonment up to 5 years; civil shortfall penalty up to 150% of the evaded tax; recovery of unpaid tax and use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in evasion may be prosecuted personally and held jointly and severally liable for civil restitution; asset restraints and travel bans may be imposed under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue re-determination of the arm's length profit; shortfall penalties of 20%-40% (up to 150% for an abusive avoidance scheme); use-of-money interest on the underpaid corporate tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or execute non-arm's length pricing schemes may be caught as promoters; personal liability extends to civil penalties, disqualification from managing companies, and travel restrictions.
Failing to Withhold Non-Resident Tax

Statutory sanction: NRWT of 30% on the gross dividend is recoverable from the company; shortfall penalty of 20%-150%; use-of-money interest; IRD may treat the distribution as an unallowable deduction to the payor.

Director personal liability: Directors approving distributions without withholding are responsible officers for the unpaid NRWT and may be personally liable for the amount; in liquidation, personal civil recovery and criminal prosecution can follow.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rule (s BG 1) cancels the tax benefit; CFC rules attribute undistributed foreign income; the company may be reclassified as a non-resident, losing treaty benefits and any domestic loss deductions.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the shell is a mere sham; directors can be personally liable for the company's tax debts and may face disqualification, travel bans, and in severe cases imprisonment.
AML/CFT or Unlicensed Financial Services

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to NZD 2 million for a body corporate and NZD 300,000 for an individual; for serious offences, imprisonment up to 2 years; confiscation of criminal proceeds.

Director personal liability: Senior officers may face personal liability for AML/CFT failures if they consented or were knowingly involved; money laundering offences can trigger asset freezes, travel bans, and extradition.
