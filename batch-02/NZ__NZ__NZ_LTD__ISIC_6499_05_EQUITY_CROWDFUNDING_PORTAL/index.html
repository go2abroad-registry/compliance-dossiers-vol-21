Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand law imposes no minimum paid-up share capital, no initial capital deposit, and no blocked statutory escrow account for a limited company. Shares may be issued for cash, property, or services under section 56 of the Companies Act 1993. The company must state its share classes and number of shares in the application. For equity crowdfunding, the Financial Markets Authority licensing regime does not require a minimum paid-in capital under the Companies Act, although the licensed portal must maintain adequate operational resources and a separate client-money escrow account for investor funds.

Funding & unlock path: No Civil Defence industrial fire permit is required for a pure digital intermediary. The entity must nevertheless comply with the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, maintain a workplace fire and evacuation plan accepted by Fire and Emergency New Zealand, obtain a building warrant of fitness for its leased office under the Building Act 2004, and meet MBIE health and safety due diligence. These are ordinary occupational clearances, not industrial licensing.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Apply to the Inland Revenue for the statutory IRD number, either through the Companies Office incorporation process or through the Inland Revenue's non-resident company registration channel. 3. Submit certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitution, director names and addresses, ultimate shareholder ownership details, and, where applicable, home-jurisdiction registration certificates of non-resident shareholders. 4. Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 if the supplier exports them, or they are exported on the supplier's behalf, within the statutory period (generally 28 days of supply) and the export is evidenced by New Zealand Customs Service records. Services supplied to non-resident recipients who are outside New Zealand at the time of performance can be zero-rated under section 11A unless the supply is directly connected with NZ land, goods in NZ, or physically performed in NZ. For a crowdfunding portal, platform facilitation and marketing services supplied to overseas issuers may qualify as zero-rated remote services if the place of consumption is offshore, but exempt financial services such as arranging or brokering equity securities are exempt, not zero-rated, and carry no input-tax credit entitlement.

  • New Zealand Customs Service export declaration lodged via the Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Foreign exchange receipt evidence: SWIFT credit advice or bank statement showing overseas funds credited to the New Zealand exporter

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government approval API, or anti-fraud digital stamping exists in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates on the Peppol network. Tax invoices must contain the prescribed particulars under section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; GST returns and payments are lodged through myIR.

Inland Revenue may reassess a zero-rated supply as taxable at 15% if the export or non-resident service requirement is not substantiated, and may claw back GST previously refunded or not paid. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply for lack of reasonable care or unacceptable tax positions, escalating significantly for abusive tax positions and evasion, plus use-of-money interest. Fraudulent or knowingly false GST and export zero-rating declarations are criminal offences under the Tax Administration Act 1994, with fines and imprisonment available; for fraud or evasion there is no statutory time limit on reassessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates under a fully liberalized foreign exchange regime with no statutory exchange controls, capital account restrictions, or currency convertibility barriers. The New Zealand dollar is freely convertible for both residents and non-residents, and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not impose any surrender requirements on export proceeds or foreign currency receipts. Current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, are unrestricted, and capital account movements such as foreign direct investment, equity injections, and portfolio flows are permitted without prior central bank approval. Profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriations may be executed freely through any authorized dealer bank, subject only to standard bank KYC/AML documentation. For an equity crowdfunding portal, the main regulatory constraints arise not from FX controls but from the Financial Markets Authority licensing requirements under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, particularly regarding the custody and segregation of client funds. Therefore, treasury planning should focus on compliant clearing architecture, not on convertibility permissioning.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign currency receipts. Exporters may retain proceeds in any foreign currency, convert them at commercial banks at market rates, or use them for offshore working capital without any mandatory surrender to the central bank or government authority.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI entities are not a recognized bank class in New Zealand banking law. While the country has a permissive payments regulatory environment, non-bank financial institutions must be registered as a financial service provider under the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) with the appropriate licensing, and those handling client money must also comply with the Financial Markets Conduct Act client money provisions. Practical viability of a fintech EMI as the primary operational treasury rail is rated low-to-medium because registered New Zealand banks remain the only institutions able to provide direct NZD clearing via the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS). A philanthropically capitalized foreign EMI may support ancillary currency conversion or low-value retail disbursements, but the core capital escrow and investor refunds must be held with a New Zealand registered bank to satisfy licensing and trust accounting obligations.

Defense evidence: An ownership chart tracing all natural persons who own 25% or more, certified corporate registry extracts, passport identity verification, source of wealth documentation for each ultimate beneficial owner, and independent credit or corporate background checks from a recognized due diligence provider such as Dun & Bradstreet or a FATF-jurisdiction registered agency.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from overseas investors and shareholders should be routed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers into the segregated client funds trust account at an approved New Zealand bank. All remittances must reference the FX purpose code designated for 'Equity Investment' or 'Capital Injection' as per the bank's FX classification. For crowdfunding investor contributions, the platform must ensure that funds are received into the segregated escrow account and not commingled with operational funds. The bank may require a statutory declaration or certificate from the company's auditor confirming the nature of the foreign investment for New Zealand Government statistics reporting under the Overseas Investment Act, although no exchange control approval is required. Capital verification for statutory solvency purposes should be supported by a Chartered Accountant's certificate confirming the paid-up share capital has been received in cleared funds.Disbursements for trade settlements and operating expenses should be executed via the New Zealand Clearing System for NZD transactions and SWIFT MT103 for cross-border currency payments. For trade settlements involving entity operations or payments to suppliers, commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are permitted, but the more efficient rail is SWIFT MT103 direct transfers from the bank's online corporate banking platform. The portal must manage investor refunds and successful funding distributions through the same segregated trust account, using batch ACH processing for high-volume low-value payments. The Registrar may issue reminders and, if the default continues, give notice of intention to remove the company from the New Zealand Register. The entity may be fined up to NZ$250,000 and each director up to NZ$50,000. The FMA may issue a violation notice, seek a compliance order, or apply for civil pecuniary penalties of up to NZ$1,000,000. Continued default can undermine the licensing good-standing requirement for the crowdfunding service and may trigger FMA licence suspension or cancellation.
Company Income Tax Return and terminal tax payment For a standard 31 March balance date, the IR4 company income tax return must be filed by 7 July in the income year following the balance date, or by the later extension date granted to a registered New Zealand tax agent. Terminal tax must be paid by the applicable IRD terminal tax date, normally 7 April for a 31 March balance date unless an extension applies. $0 USD Inland Revenue imposes late filing penalties, late payment penalties and use-of-money interest. The late payment penalty regime begins with an initial 1% of unpaid tax, followed by an additional 4% after 7 days and a further 1% each subsequent month. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand does not prescribe a statutory Local File / Master File dollar threshold; however, the IR1061 International Questionnaire must be completed when the total value of cross-border related-party transactions, including interest, royalties, management fees, and the transfer of goods or services, equals or exceeds NZD 10,000,000 in the income year. In all cases, the taxpayer must retain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation sufficient to demonstrate that its intercompany pricing is arm's length, as required by section 93 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, even below the NZD 10 million questionnaire threshold.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the corporate substance doctrine is enforced through the concepts of resident management and control, permanent establishment, and the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007. An equity crowdfunding portal must demonstrate genuine economic substance in New Zealand by maintaining its head office, board decision-making, senior management, compliance officers, escrow controls, AML/CFT review, and core income-generating activities in New Zealand. A shell arrangement that shifts platform technology ownership, investor selection, and risk-bearing to a zero-tax offshore entity while claiming treaty income in New Zealand will be recharacterised under the anti-avoidance and transfer pricing rules, and the transaction may be void for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Return

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 sections 141, 143A and 143B; a taxpayer who evades tax by omission or false declaration is liable to a shortfall penalty up to 150% of the tax shortfall, a criminal fine up to NZD 50,000 for the first offence and NZD 100,000 for a subsequent offence, and imprisonment for up to 5 years; Inland Revenue may also issue assessments based on reconstructed income and cancel any DTA benefits.

Director personal liability: Under Companies Act 1993 sections 300 and 301, directors may be personally liable to contribute to the company's assets if they knowingly signed a false tax return or allowed the company to trade recklessly while tax liabilities were unpaid. Directors face civil asset recovery and seizure orders, director disqualification under sections 382 and 383, a direction requiring restitution of diverted corporate funds, court-imposed travel bans and border alert listings, and personal criminal liability leading to imprisonment for up to 5 years where the director is proven to have directed the evasion.
AML/CFT Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 sections 59 to 81 imposes civil penalties of up to NZD 2,000,000 for a body corporate and up to NZD 200,000 for an individual, plus criminal penalties for intentional non-compliance of up to NZD 300,000 and imprisonment for up to 2 years; the FMA, Reserve Bank, or DIA may issue compliance orders, appoint a manager, or suspend the special licence required under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to civil liability where they knowingly permit fraud, sign false annual AML/CFT compliance declaration, or fail to terminate a suspicious wire transaction; the corporate veil is pierced under Companies Act 1993 and the financial sector regulators may require restitution of the funds, issue a director banning order, record the director on public registers, impose an international travel-booking restriction, and refer the director to the New Zealand Police for criminal proceedings that can result in a term of imprisonment and forfeiture of personal assets.
Misleading Investor Disclosure and Crowdfunding Fraud

Statutory sanction: Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 sections 480, 481 and 489 impose criminal liability of up to NZD 5,000,000 for a body corporate and up to NZD 1,000,000 and 5 years imprisonment for an individual for dishonest conduct relating to financial products; the FMA may also impose civil pecuniary penalties of up to NZD 5,000,000 and issue stop-orders or compensation orders requiring the portal and its directors to pay restitution to defrauded investors.

Director personal liability: The court may hold directors personally liable to pay compensation to investors under the FMC Act, and under Companies Act 1993 sections 297, 298, 300, 301 and 383, liquidators and receivers can set aside payments, recover preference transactions, claw back director bonuses and salaries, and disqualify directors for up to 10 years. Beyond New Zealand, a director who knowingly participates in investor fraud involving cross-border bank wires may face extradition and asset-freeze orders, travel bans, and criminal imprisonment in the investor's home jurisdiction, thereby eliminating any practical protection from the limited liability shield.
