For refrigerated logistics warehousing under ISIC 5210 and NAICS 493120, a pure virtual office or commercial mailbox is not acceptable to Inland Revenue, the Ministry for Primary Industries, or trading banks as the principal place of business. Companies Act 1993 permits the registered office to be a physical New Zealand address maintained by a licensed registered office service provider, but that address cannot substitute for an occupied, temperature-controlled cold storage facility. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (Te Tari Tāke) of New Zealand; secure online portal: myIR (myIR Secure Online Services). Corporate registry interface: New Zealand Companies Office / New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) Register.

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand incorporated limited company, the Registrar of Companies supplies company registration data to Inland Revenue and the company is issued an IRD number; the IRD number is confirmed and administered through myIR. If a separate non-resident company must obtain its own tax identifier, the non-resident entity must apply through Inland Revenue's myIR portal for an IRD number as a non-resident company, submitting: (i) certified certificate of incorporation or equivalent registration document from the home jurisdiction; (ii) constitution or governing documents; (iii) complete register of directors and ultimate beneficial owners; (iv) New Zealand address for service or registered agent; (v) declaration of non-residence and tax residency status; and (vi) intended New Zealand business and GST activities. Any application to register for GST may be made simultaneously in myIR, selecting the appropriate GST registration effective date and filing frequency. The IRD number is the statutory taxpayer identification used for all New Zealand GST, income tax, and PAYE obligations; the NZBN is a separate business identifier and does not replace the IRD number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A New Zealand limited company is a separate legal and taxpaying entity under the Companies Act 1993 and the Income Tax Act 2007. The company is subject to entity-level taxation at the prevailing New Zealand corporate income tax rate; the shareholder is not taxed directly on the company's income.

Available election forms

None for this entity configuration. New Zealand has no check-the-box election. A New Zealand close company may elect look-through company (LTC) status under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, but a standard limited company (NZ_LTD) is not eligible for LTC treatment unless it meets ownership and resident requirements and files an election. No hybrid mismatch election is available; the hybrid mismatch rules under sections FH 1-FH 11 of the Income Tax Act 2007 apply automatically.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000: a person must register for GST if the value of taxable supplies made in New Zealand exceeded or is expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period (Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, section 51). Registration is voluntary below this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when they are physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier in the same or substantially the same condition as at the time of supply, and export occurs within the statutory time frame (generally within 28 days of the time of supply; certain non-resident purchaser scenarios allow removal within 60 days). Services may be zero-rated under section 11(1)(b) where the recipient is a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the supply is not connected with New Zealand land or moveable personal property physically situated in New Zealand. For this cold-chain logistics operation, warehousing and controlled-temperature storage of pharmaceuticals physically located in New Zealand is generally a taxable supply at 15% GST; zero-rating may apply only to the separately identifiable international transport or qualifying offshore services, and contemporaneous export evidence must be retained.

  • New Zealand Customs Service export entry lodged through the Trade Single Window (approved EDI/declaration manifest for departure clearance)
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) naming the exporter and consignee
  • Commercial invoice and packing list evidencing the exported pharmaceuticals
  • Warehouse release / cold-chain temperature logs and pharmaceutical handling records substantiating physical removal from New Zealand
  • Bank foreign exchange credit advice (SWIFT MT103 or corresponding bank confirmation) showing the export proceeds credited to the New Zealand company's account
  • GST working papers and zero-rating reconciliation schedules supporting the 0% treatment on the GST return

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime applies in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and interoperable through the New Zealand e-Invoicing Network (Peppol-based PEPPOL network run with MBIE). GST returns are filed electronically through myIR, and invoice-level reporting is not required. Invoices must satisfy the content requirements of section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, and business records must be preserved under the Tax Administration Act 1994.

Inland Revenue may deny zero-rating and assess GST at 15% if the exported goods or offshore service criteria are not supported by adequate records. The Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes use-of-money interest from the original due date and shortfall penalties of 20% for lack of reasonable care or unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% for abusive tax positions; fraudulent or intentionally misleading export/zero-rating claims can also lead to criminal prosecution and assessments outside the normal four-year time bar. Although New Zealand does not impose a specific statutory 180-day export proceeds repatriation limit, the banking/foreign-exchange confirmation should evidence receipt within that benchmark to preserve a contemporaneous audit trail.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a structurally open financial system. The New Zealand dollar is independently floated since 1985 and trades freely against all major currencies in spot, forward, swap and option markets. There are no exchange control licenses, no capped conversion thresholds, and no pre-approval requirement from the Reserve Bank of New Zealand for current account or capital account transactions. The current and capital accounts are effectively fully convertible for residents and non-residents, subject only to overseas investment screening in sensitive land and significant business assets under the Overseas Investment Act 2005. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal; non-resident shareholders may receive dividends with applicable withholding taxes, and double taxation agreements can reduce these rates upon submission of a certificate of residence. Exporters are not required to convert earnings into NZD; proceeds may be retained in any permitted currency. The practical frictions in New Zealand are therefore banking counterparty due-diligence, AML/CFT verification and corporate documentation, rather than state-imposed foreign exchange restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rules exist under New Zealand law. Exporters are permitted to hold foreign currency proceeds in external accounts indefinitely, may use them directly for import settlements, and there is no mandatory conversion ratio or timeframe imposed by the Reserve Bank. The only compliance obligation is the standard tax treatment of foreign currency gains or losses, requiring conversion to NZD for financial statements and tax reporting, with any repatriation made at the commercial wholesale rate quoted by the registered bank.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs are not licensed as banks in New Zealand and cannot provide direct participation in the Exchange Settlement Account System operated through the Reserve Bank of New Zealand. Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, any provider of money changing or remittance services must be registered and supervised by the New Zealand Department of Internal Affairs or the Financial Markets Authority. Most offshore EMIs hold no New Zealand registration and therefore cannot issue local account numbers, accept local electronic transfers, or support statutory corporate banking requirements. Use of an offshore EMI for primary treasury clearing is legally exposed to unregistered financial service provider objections from external auditors and local banks. The viability is rated Low for capital escrow, trade settlement and clearing rails; at most it can act as a supplementary pass-through for non-core receivables, and even then only where the EMI is a registered reporting entity under the AML/CFT regime.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital should be directed to the NZ registered bank receiving account using a SWIFT MT103 from the investor's regulated bank, with the beneficiary account named precisely as the New Zealand limited company. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 aggregate annual value of cross-border related-party transactions generally triggers enhanced transfer pricing documentation expectations, including a Local File; New Zealand groups with consolidated revenue above NZD 1.1 billion are expected to prepare Master File and Country-by-Country Report documentation. No formal statutory de minimis exists, and Inland Revenue may request a full transfer pricing analysis for any related-party transaction regardless of size.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations filing regime, but substance is decisive under the general anti-avoidance rule in Income Tax Act 2007 section BG 1 and under DTA permanent establishment analysis. For a temperature-controlled cold storage company, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are the physical receipt, storage, temperature monitoring, inventory control, release, and hygiene management of perishable pharmaceuticals inside New Zealand. The entity must have real premises, employees, equipment, risk-bearing, and commercial control in New Zealand; a letterbox office or offshore invoicing shell cannot support profit allocation, treaty residence, or the arm's-length attribution of income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through mischaracterised fees and sham offshore invoices

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may invoke the general anti-avoidance rule under Income Tax Act 2007 section BG 1, reconstruct the arrangement under sections GC 13 and GC 14, impose a 100% to 150% shortfall penalty for evasion or similar conduct under the Tax Administration Act 1994, and prosecute criminally under section 143A for knowingly evading assessment, carrying a fine of up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced through the Companies Act 1993: directors who allow reckless or insolvent trading face personal liability under sections 135 and 136, and a court may order the director to compensate the company or the Commissioner under section 383 and order disqualification as a director. Inland Revenue may also apply to place a departure prohibition order (travel ban) under Part 11 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 until the tax debt and penalties are settled, and persistent serious breach can result in criminal imprisonment of the directing officers.
Failure to withhold and pay NRWT on outbound dividends, interest, and royalties

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Tax Administration Act 1994, Inland Revenue may assess the company for the full unpaid NRWT, add late payment penalties (1% initial, 4% after seven days, then 1% per month), and apply shortfall penalties for failing to take reasonable care (20%) or for an abusive tax position (40%). Criminal prosecution under section 143A may follow where the withholding omission was deliberate, with fines up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be made personally and jointly and severally liable for tax withheld or required to be withheld but not remitted to Inland Revenue under section 39A of the Tax Administration Act 1994. This personal liability attaches to the full unpaid NRWT plus penalties and interest. Directors may also face disqualification under the Companies Act 1993, civil restitution claims, departure prohibition orders, and criminal imprisonment where the failure was knowing and fraudulent.
False permanent establishment declarations, sham foreign entity structure, and fraudulent record keeping

Statutory sanction: The Income Tax Act 2007 and Tax Administration Act 1994 authorise reassessment of the full New Zealand-source profit, cancellation of improper DTA relief, and criminal prosecution under section 143B for knowingly providing false or misleading information. The false-documentation offence carries fines up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment up to five years, while civil shortfall penalties can reach 150% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the company to make false declarations are exposed to direct criminal liability as principals under the Crimes Act 1961, personal pecuniary penalties, contribution and compensation orders, disqualification under Companies Act 1993, and travel bans preventing departure from New Zealand. The court may order the directors personally to restore any tax shortfall and pay punitive damages where the sham structure was used to defeat public revenue.
