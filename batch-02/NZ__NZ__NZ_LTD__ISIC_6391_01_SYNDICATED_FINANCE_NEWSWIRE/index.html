Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office, Business Registries, Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), 15 Stout Street, Wellington, New Zealand; online portal: https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), sections 15 (Companies to have full capacity), 16 (Constitution not required, allowed), 18 (Application for registration of company), 20 (Effect of registration), 24 (Minutes of meetings), 122 (Board of directors) and 131 (Board duties). Also the Financial Reporting Act 2013 for financial statements and the New Zealand Business Number Act 2016 for mandatory NZBN issuance.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form COMP01 - Application for Registration of a Limited Company, together with a copy of the Constitutive Instrument (if adopted), Consent to Act as Director forms, and Notice of Registered Office and Address for Service. Submission is made through the New Zealand Companies Office online registration system.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

New Zealand law imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement for a limited liability company. There is no escrow account rule, no capital deposit, and no requirement that shares be paid upfront; consideration may be in cash, property, or services unless otherwise prescribed in the company's constitution. The company must, however, maintain adequate records of share capital and reserves in accordance with the Companies Act 1993.

Funding & unlock path: As a digital professional service, the company must comply with general health and safety obligations under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, including providing a safe electrical and ergonomic environment for its employees. If premises are physically occupied, the territorial authority's building consent and fire evacuation plan must be in place, and a 'Produced Supply and Conformity' certificate from the building owner or a fire evacuation scheme is recommended. No additional civil defense clearance is required beyond standard office compliance, as the activity is not hazardous in nature.

Paid-in capital rule: New Zealand law imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement for a limited liability company. There is no escrow account rule, no capital deposit, and no requirement that shares be paid upfront; consideration may be in cash, property, or services unless otherwise prescribed in the company's constitution. The company must, however, maintain adequate records of share capital and reserves in accordance with the Companies Act 1993.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand. Primary digital portal: myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz. Company registration is a prerequisite via the New Zealand Companies Office (https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a New Zealand limited company must apply for an IRD number immediately after incorporation. The company application is submitted to Inland Revenue using Form IR596 (Company IRD Number Application) or through the online myIR service. The company must provide its New Zealand Companies Register number, registered office address, principal business activity (ISIC code), and details of directors and shareholders. A non-resident company not incorporated in New Zealand but carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand must register for an IRD number under section 24B of the Tax Administration Act 1994. For GST purposes, registration is mandatory if the entity makes taxable supplies in New Zealand exceeding NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration is available. The same IRD number serves as the GST registration number, with GST registration applied via myIR or Form GST 2A. The IRD number must be used for all corporate income tax, PAYE, GST, and customs interactions. The standard processing time is 5-10 business days for local entities, with non-resident applications possibly taking longer due to additional verification.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A New Zealand limited company is a separate tax resident under section YD 2 of the Income Tax Act 2007 if it is incorporated in New Zealand, has its head office in New Zealand, or has its centre of management in New Zealand. Resident companies are taxed on worldwide income at the corporate rate of 28%. Non-resident companies (including foreign-owned NZ-incorporated companies that elect to become non-resident under the tie-breaker rules of a double tax agreement) are taxed only on New Zealand-source income, with branch profits taxed at the same 28%. New Zealand has no exempt-zone regimes, no territorial reduction for international business headquarters, and no tax-exempt entity classification for ordinary trading companies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A New Zealand limited company is a separate tax resident under section YD 2 of the Income Tax Act 2007 if it is incorporated in New Zealand, has its head office in New Zealand, or has its centre of management in New Zealand. Resident companies are taxed on worldwide income at the corporate rate of 28%. Non-resident companies (including foreign-owned NZ-incorporated companies that elect to become non-resident under the tie-breaker rules of a double tax agreement) are taxed only on New Zealand-source income, with branch profits taxed at the same 28%. New Zealand has no exempt-zone regimes, no territorial reduction for international business headquarters, and no tax-exempt entity classification for ordinary trading companies. The default classification is a fully taxable resident company unless a Double Tax Agreement tie-breaker determines residence elsewhere.

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a 'check-the-box' classification system, but offers several elective regimes: (1) Look-Through Company (LTC) election, available only to close companies (five or fewer shareholders) with all shareholders individuals or eligible trusts, which causes the company's income, expenses, and tax credits to flow through to shareholders; (2) Consolidated Group election, allowing a group of NZ-resident companies (100% common ownership) to be treated as a single entity for tax purposes; (3) GST Group election, allowing related companies to register as a single GST group; (4) Imputation election, mandatory for most resident companies, allowing the company to credit tax paid to shareholders via imputation credits; (5) Hybrid mismatch elections under the OECD BEPS Action 2 rules, including elective ordering rules for adjustment of imported mismatches, though such elections are limited and highly fact-specific. For international tax, New Zealand applies the Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules with a default attribution to NZ shareholders unless the CFC is in a grey list jurisdiction; no election is available to change the CFC status, but entity-level exemptions apply for active business income under the Australian/New Zealand functional currency rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (mandatory registration threshold for taxable supplies made in New Zealand in any 12-month period; applies to both goods and services, with no separate registration threshold for digital or cross-border services. Non-resident suppliers of remote services to NZ consumers are subject to a separate NZD 60,000 threshold under the 'remote services' GST rules effective 1 October 2016).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to: (1) Exported goods - goods physically removed from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply, provided the supplier retains evidence of export (customs export declaration, bill of lading, or airline waybill). (2) Services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of performance, where the services are not supplied to a private consumer physically present in New Zealand, and the services are not directly in connection with land or moveable property situated in New Zealand. For the 'financial newswire and syndicated finance news' business (ISIC 6391), the supply of information services (real-time financial news, market data feeds, analytics) to overseas financial institutions, media organisations, and other non-resident subscribers qualifies for zero rating if the recipient is a non-resident and the service is effectively performed outside New Zealand. However, care is required: if the same service is supplied to a person within New Zealand, even if a non-resident, the supply is taxable at 15% GST. Additionally, the 'remote services' reverse charge rules may apply to services supplied by an offshore supplier to a New Zealand GST-registered business, but this does not affect the NZ resident supplier's zero-rating claim. Zero-rating is also available for certain supplies of a going concern, supplies of fine metals, and supplies of land to a GST-registered person, but these are not directly relevant to the newswire activity.

  • For exported goods: valid export entry (Customs Export Declaration) lodged via the New Zealand Trade Single Window (TSW), Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing the goods departed New Zealand within 28 days, and the supplier's sales invoice
  • For exported services (including financial newswire subscription): a service contract or purchase order identifying the non-resident customer, the supplier's tax invoice, evidence of payment (e.g., SWIFT wire, credit card settlement, or a signed statement from the customer), and a declaration from the non-resident recipient acknowledging the supply is for business consumption outside New Zealand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an NZ registered bank or a statement of foreign currency inward payment, providing verification of foreign exchange repatriation; the IRC is not exclusively mandated by IRD but is retained as best practice evidencing that consideration was received from outside New Zealand and the transaction was genuinely offshore

New Zealand has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no government-issued e-invoicing API clearance requirement. E-invoicing is optional and operates on a voluntary PEPPOL-based framework administered by the Ministry of Business, Innovation, and Employement (MBIE). Businesses may transmit e-invoices through certified service providers using the Australian/New Zealand PEPPOL network. GST returns must be filed electronically via myIR (electronic filing mandatory for registered persons with annual taxable turnover above NZD 10 million or those using a tax agent), but the submission of GST returns is not an invoice clearance mechanism. Invoicing must comply with section 24 of the GST Act 1985, requiring the invoice to contain the supplier's name and GST number, the date, description, consideration, and GST amount, but there is no pre-clearance or stamping. The IRD may require taxpayers to use electronic filing and specific record-keeping formats under section 44T of the Tax Administration Act 1994 for large enterprises, but this is not a real-time clearance system. There are no API integration mandates for tax authority communication beyond the myIR portal and optional EDI for customs via the New Zealand Customs Service.

The Inland Revenue Department actively audits zero-rated export supplies, particularly for service businesses with significant export turnover. Under section 11 of the GST Act 1985, the supplier bears the burden of proving zero rating. If the IRD determines that zero rating was incorrectly claimed, it will assess the GST output tax at 15% from the original time of supply, with use-of-money interest running from the date the GST was originally due. Penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 include: (1) a 'not taking reasonable care' penalty of 20% of the tax shortfall; (2) an 'unacceptable tax position' penalty of 20%; (3) an 'abusive tax position' penalty of 100% of the shortfall; (4) an 'evasion' penalty of 150% of the shortfall, with criminal prosecution possible (imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to NZD 50,000 for individuals, with higher fines for companies). For missing documentation, the IRD may reverse the zero rating even if the exporter genuinely supplied the services offshore. Retrospective clawbacks can extend up to seven years from the end of the tax year under the time bar rules for non-disclosure; if the taxpayer knowingly failed to disclose, no time bar limitation applies and clawback may reach back to any year of the original shortfall. In addition, New Zealand Customs may impose penalties under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 for false or misleading export declarations, including fines up to NZD 10,000 per declaration. The IRD also has the power to make a default assessment if sufficient records are not kept, and to require a tax security or bond from the exporter in high-risk cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised exchange control regime. Both current account and capital account transactions are freely convertible into and out of New Zealand dollars without foreign exchange control approval. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds may be remitted offshore through registered banks subject only to standard AML/KYC verification and applicable New Zealand tax withholding. There are no material convertibility hurdles, no exchange rate band, and no central bank permit requirement for trade or investment flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rules. There is no mandatory repatriation timeline and no required conversion ratio for export receipts. A New Zealand exporter may retain export earnings offshore, use them directly for offshore disbursements, or convert them to New Zealand dollars at commercial market rates through an authorised dealer. The sole obligations are record-keeping, tax reporting, and AML/CFT transaction monitoring where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Legal constraints: a foreign fintech or electronic money institution is not a New Zealand registered bank and cannot offer domestic banking services unless separately licensed or registered under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and the Financial Service Providers Act 2008. An offshore EMI can provide virtual IBANs and multi-currency settlement, but it is generally unsuitable as the primary treasury rail for a syndicated finance facility because such accounts lack bank-grade credit backing, may not meet the undertaking requirements of lenders and counterparties, and may trigger enhanced due diligence by clearing banks. EMIs may be used only as a supplementary collection or payment rail after independent legal review of the EMI's licence status, segregation of funds, and compliance with sanctions screening.

Legal constraints: a foreign fintech or electronic money institution is not a New Zealand registered bank and cannot offer domestic banking services unless separately licensed or registered under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and the Financial Service Providers Act 2008. An offshore EMI can provide virtual IBANs and multi-currency settlement, but it is generally unsuitable as the primary treasury rail for a syndicated finance facility because such accounts lack bank-grade credit backing, may not meet the undertaking requirements of lenders and counterparties, and may trigger enhanced due diligence by clearing banks. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% flat corporate income tax rate; no state or municipal income tax

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) is imposed at 30% on gross dividends paid by a New Zealand company to a non-resident shareholder; the rate is reduced to 15% where the dividend is fully imputed. An applicable double tax agreement may further reduce the rate to 5% or 15% depending on the shareholder's level of ownership.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Transaction value threshold triggering robust contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation: related-party cross-border transactions with an aggregate annual value above NZ$10,000,000. Multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding EUR 750,000,000 are required to file a Country-by-Country Report and are expected to maintain Master File and Local File documentation. Even below the NZ$10,000,000 threshold, all related-party transactions must be arm's length and supported by adequate records.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime. Nevertheless, economic substance is decisive: a New Zealand-incorporated company is tax-resident in New Zealand by incorporation, and the location of its management, staff, physical infrastructure, content licensing functions, customer contracts, and risk-bearing capital must align with its profit allocation. Inland Revenue may apply the general anti-avoidance rule and the Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test to deny treaty benefits to a conduit company with no real commercial substance. For an ISIC 6391 financial newswire business, substantive activities include editorial/news aggregation, API feed infrastructure, client support, licensing negotiations, and contract risk management in New Zealand.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Tax Administration Act 1994: imprisonment up to 5 years and/or criminal fines as prescribed, plus civil shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the evaded tax, use-of-money interest, and late payment penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise or participate in evasion are personally exposed to prosecution, criminal fines, and imprisonment. Civil recovery may be pursued against personal assets, and Inland Revenue may invoke director penalty liability for unpaid withheld taxes; restraint orders may include asset freezes and court-imposed travel bans.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may recharacterise or disregard the shell structure, reassess taxable income in New Zealand, deny treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test, impose shortfall penalties of 20% to 150%, and the company may be deregistered under the Companies Act 1993 for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Nominee and inactive directors may be liable for reckless trading under the Companies Act 1993, personally liable for company debts where the company continued trading while insolvent, and disqualified from managing companies. Failure to maintain accurate beneficial ownership records also exposes directors to AML/CFT civil penalties.
Abusive Transfer Pricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustments under the Income Tax Act 2007 are made to reflect arm's length outcomes, with disallowed deductions, grossing-up adjustments, NRWT consequences, shortfall penalties of 20% to 150%, and use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false tax returns or cause the company to take a non-arm's-length position may face civil shortfall penalties personally if the company is unable to pay, and in egregious cases criminal liability for tax evasion.
Unaudited Profit Repatriation / NRWT Evasion

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may impose NRWT at the full statutory rate, disallow deductions for the disguised payment, charge shortfall penalties of 20% to 150%, and assess use-of-money interest on the unpaid withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be made personally liable for unpaid NRWT and other withholding taxes under section 149 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 if the company defaults; personal recovery action may include attachment of assets and director disqualification.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: AML/CFT civil penalties may reach NZ$2,000,000 for companies and NZ$300,000 for individuals; money laundering offences under the Crimes Act carry imprisonment up to 7 years and substantial fines; asset forfeiture may be ordered under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2009.

Director personal liability: Directors, senior managers, and AML compliance officers are personally exposed to AML/CFT enforcement action where failures are deliberate or reckless. Court-issued restraint and forfeiture orders can freeze personal assets, and bail or sentencing conditions in criminal proceedings may impose travel bans and surrender of passports.
