A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not acceptable as the sole registered office for this trade-finance factoring entity. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement exists under the Companies Act 1993. The board may determine the issue price and consideration for shares, subject to solvency under s 4 and s 39 of the Act. For trade-supply-chain factoring, no prescribed capital or escrow deposit is imposed by the Companies Office; however, the company must comply with Financial Markets Authority conduct expectations and, if the factoring activity constitutes a regulated financial service, the applicable Financial Service Providers Register and AML/CFT source-of-funds obligations.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for an industrial Civil Defense fire permit because the business is an office-based financial services entity. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand - online portal: myIR (myIR.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity with New Zealand-sourced income or GST taxable activities must obtain an IRD number by applying to Inland Revenue through myIR or the official IRD paper application, providing certified entity formation documents, registered office details, director and ultimate beneficial ownership information, and the specific New Zealand tax nexus rationale. If the entity will make GST taxable supplies in New Zealand, it must separately register for GST in the same myIR platform; IRD validates the application and issues a GST registration number. For a New Zealand-incorporated limited company, the IRD number is issued following Companies Office incorporation and an application for an IRD number; a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) may also be used to identify the entity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident entity with New Zealand-sourced income or GST taxable activities must obtain an IRD number by applying to Inland Revenue through myIR or the official IRD paper application, providing certified entity formation documents, registered office details, director and ultimate beneficial ownership information, and the specific New Zealand tax nexus rationale. If the entity will make GST taxable supplies in New Zealand, it must separately register for GST in the same myIR platform; IRD validates the application and issues a GST registration number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists; however, a closely held eligible company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under the Income Tax Act 2007 if all shareholders are qualifying individuals, trustees, or partnerships and the eligibility criteria are met. A trade supply chain factoring company owned by non-resident corporate shareholders will generally fail eligibility and remain subject to entity-level corporate taxation. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in total GST taxable supplies in any 12-month period; voluntary registration is permitted where the entity is carrying on a taxable activity, and non-resident suppliers may be required to register if making taxable supplies in New Zealand with no deemed deduction threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods physically exported from New Zealand where the exporter retains proper documentary evidence of export, and to services supplied to non-residents, including qualifying financial services such as cross-border factoring and reverse factoring fees, where the effective use and enjoyment of the supply is outside New Zealand and the supply is not connected with New Zealand land or goods. The supplier must hold contemporaneous commercial and payment evidence demonstrating that the recipient is a non-resident and that the exported goods or cross-border services were genuinely delivered outside New Zealand.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Export Entry (customs export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or comparable shipping evidence showing physical departure from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Foreign exchange repatriation evidence such as the Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) where applicable, or bank / SWIFT MT103 records showing payment in New Zealand or foreign currency from the offshore purchaser
  • Documentation evidencing the factoring agreement and beneficial ownership of the underlying trade receivables for cross-border supply of financial services

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government API, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is voluntary in New Zealand via the Peppol-based New Zealand e-Invoicing Network; tax invoices must comply with the content and timing requirements of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985.

If a zero-rated export cannot be substantiated by the required customs, shipping, and foreign exchange repatriation documentation, IRD and New Zealand Customs can recharacterize the supply as standard-rated and assess GST at 15%, together with use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Deliberately false or fraudulent zero-rating may attract administrative penalties up to 100% of the tax avoided, criminal prosecution, and potential imprisonment for responsible officers; directors and shareholders may also be pursued under careless or fraudulent tax shortfall provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully open financial account with no exchange control legislation. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely floated and fully convertible on both the current and capital account categories. Accordingly, direct investors may freely convert NZD to any foreign currency for profit remittances, dividend distributions, or liquidation proceeds without central bank approval or pre-clearance. There are no enforced surrender obligations on export receipts, no retention quotas, and no mandated conversion channels. However, all cross-border transfers are processed through registered banks and financial institutions subject to the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. The sole formal reporting is statistical in nature and processed by the Reserve Bank through the Balance of Payments survey. Additionally, non-resident acquisitions of sensitive land or significant business assets above NZD thresholds may trigger Overseas Investment Act screening, but this is not a balance of payments control. For factoring and trade finance invoices, cross-border receivables can be collateralised with minimal FX friction provided the commercial terms are at arms length and the underlying trade documents substantiate the business purpose. Ratings agencies and global peers regard New Zealand as a free-capital-jurisdiction, making it comparatively efficient for treasury migrants. The practical challenges are not regulatory convertibility barriers but rather bank de-risking policies, credit-line availability, and documentary compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export surrender timelines, no mandatory conversion percentages, and no requirement to liquidate foreign currency receipts into NZD. Exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in original currency, maintain foreign currency accounts domestically or offshore, and remit profits freely subject only to AML/CTF screening.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms can legally service a New Zealand-registered entity, but they are required to register as financial service providers and obtain an FSP number from the Financial Markets Authority. They are generally classified as money service businesses, not licensed banks, and cannot offer UCP600 letters of credit directly. For this Stage 4 trade factoring pipeline, the viability is Medium: viable for inbound rerouting of funds, utility payments, and optional FX treasury, but low-suitability for large trade settlements due to the absence of a New Zealand banking licence and stricter counterparty acceptance. At least one domestic Tier-1 commercial bank should remain the principal clearing bank to ensure credible sanction screening and trade finance rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound equity funding should be wired through a Tier-1 New Zealand bank using SWIFT MT103 with precise remittance instructions. The wire must be labelled for capital injection with the Companies Office share register updated within 10 working days and an accountant's capital verification certificate. No prior central bank authorisation is required, but the bank will require a director's certificate stating that the funds are not criminal proceeds. Distinguish clearly between paid-up capital and shareholder loans, and ensure relevant FX purpose code is used (e.g., 'G200/6210' style codes per bank policy). Wire settlement must be in NZD or foreign currency according to the bank's currency account structure, and any conversion will occur at spot market rate without surrender or quota.Trade disbursements are settled over SWIFT rails using MT103/MT202 for direct bank transfers, commercial letters of credit under UCP600 for controlled buyer payments, and documentary collections under URC522 for invoice deferred settlements. The bank will debit the operating NZD account or multi-currency account, apply FX conversion if the beneficiary operates in USD/EUR, and the underlying trade documents must be annexed to the trade finance application. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) rate on gross dividends is 30%. Under most double tax agreements (DTAs), the rate is reduced to 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for substantial shareholdings (generally at least 10% voting power).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand's transfer pricing documentation rules require a Local File for controlled transactions where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZD 2 million in a 12-month period. A Master File is required when the group has cross-border related-party transactions exceeding NZD 50 million and a worldwide group revenue exceeding NZD 500 million. In practice, Inland Revenue expects robust contemporaneous documentation for related-party transactions above NZD 5 million. The net related-party debt threshold for close scrutiny is NZD 10 million.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime, but tax avoidance rules under section BG 1 and section GA 1 apply to arrangements lacking economic substance. Factoring and trade finance companies must maintain real commercial substance in New Zealand: physical office, employees with decision-making authority, local bank accounts, and substance-based transfer pricing of risks and capital. A mere booking entity with no commercial substance will have its transactions recharacterised and could be subject to anti-avoidance adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under section 143B Tax Administration Act 1994: imprisonment up to 5 years and/or a fine of up to NZD 50,000 for an individual (triple that for a company). In addition, evasion attracts a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall, plus interest, and the imposition of the 1000% penalty for an abusive tax position (non-tax evasion). The IRD may also issue a 'tax secrecy' flag, which severely restricts banking and credit relationships.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly participating in tax evasion can be held personally liable for the company's tax debts via director penalty notices (notices may be issued for unpaid PAYE, RWT, NRWT and GST). Additionally, courts may award pecuniary penalties, disqualify directors, impose travel bans, and order imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The arrangement is voidable under the general anti-avoidance rule (section BG 1 Income Tax Act 2007) and may be recharacterised: deductions disallowed, income deemed derived in New Zealand, and shortfall penalties of 100% for taking an unacceptable tax position. Inland Revenue may impose NRWT on deemed repatriation. Criminal charges may follow if false documents are filed.

Director personal liability: If the company trades recklessly while insolvent, directors may be personally liable to creditors (Companies Act 1993, s135 and s136). The IRD will seek director penalty notices, which permit recovery from directors personally. Persistent non-compliance may trigger forfeiture of assets, travel bans and an order to report to the Official Assignee.
AML/CFT Breach (Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009)

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under the AMLCFT Act: up to NZD 2 million for each breach by a company; NZD 100,000 per breach for individuals (or NZD 200,000 for a willful breach). Criminal liability for willful non-reporting or knowingly dealing with proceeds of crime can result in fines up to NZD 500,000 and imprisonment for up to 2 years. The FMA or Reserve Bank may also suspend or revoke any registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers can be personally liable for AML compliance failures. Penalties include personal pecuniary orders in civil cases, director bans, and criminal prosecution (including up to 2 years imprisonment). The AML/CFT supervisor may also publicly name non-compliant directors.
Transfer Pricing / Thin Capitalisation Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under sections GC 6–GC 14 Income Tax Act 2007. The IRD may adjust income, disallow interest deductions, and apply a 50% shortfall penalty (or 150% if fraudulent). Thin capitalisation excess debt is denied deductibility, and NRWT may be payable on any deemed interest or dividend. Fines for non-contemporaneous documentation: up to NZD 20,000 per document (default assessment may be issued).

Director personal liability: Directors who approve a transfer pricing policy that deliberately shifts profits can be personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall if they are knowingly a party to the arrangement. Director disqualification and travel bans may be imposed under the Companies Act for insolvent trading; criminal penalties apply if false documents are certified.
Fraud / False Representation to Third Parties

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the Crimes Act 1961 (obtaining by deception, s240): imprisonment up to 7 years for individuals. The Financial Markets Authority may bring civil proceedings for market manipulation if instruments are listed; false statements to banks and creditors expose the company to compensatory damages and exemplary penalties. The IRD will treat profits from the fraud as assessable income.

Director personal liability: Directors risk personal liability for fraudulent trading (Companies Act 1993 s136), including civil liability to the company's creditors for the full amount of advances. Courts may award punitive damages, issue disqualification orders of up to 15 years, and impose travel bans where assets are moved offshore. Imprisonment up to 7 years applies.
