Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A New Zealand resident limited company incorporated in New Zealand is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income under the Income Tax Act 2007; the standard corporate income tax rate is 28% from the 2020/21 income year, with no sub-national income tax layers.

Available election forms

None in the US check-the-box sense; New Zealand has no optional pass-through check-the-box regime. The Income Tax Act 2007 permits a closely held New Zealand resident company to elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under subpart HB, which attributes profits to qualifying shareholders and exempts the company-level tax, provided all LTC eligibility requirements are satisfied. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 apply to cross-border structures; no other generic hybrid election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 (total taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote or digital services to New Zealand consumers are also required to register above this threshold; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under sections 11 and 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985: (1) goods are zero-rated when the supplier exports the goods and the goods are exported within 28 days of the time of supply; (2) services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, which includes offshore civil infrastructure CAD/BIM and structural drafting services, provided the supply is not directly connected with land or tangible goods in New Zealand and is not received by a person in New Zealand for use in New Zealand; (3) the supplier must hold contemporaneous documentary evidence of export or non-resident status and report the supply in the GST return as zero-rated with GST at 0%.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged with New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window under the Customs and Excise Act 2018 (required for tangible export goods; not applicable to a pure digital CAD/BIM service export).
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of goods, where physical goods are included in the export transaction.
  • Commercial invoice and contract or statement of work identifying the non-resident customer, the nature of the CAD/BIM services, the consideration, and a statement that the supply is zero-rated for GST.
  • Bank-issued inward foreign currency receipt evidence such as a SWIFT MT103 or MT202 credit confirmation, or an equivalent bank certificate similar to an Inward Remittance Certificate, showing the export proceeds were credited to the New Zealand exporter's bank account.
  • Delivery and performance records, including client acceptance emails, cloud platform delivery logs, time logs, or structured data handover records, demonstrating that the services were performed and delivered to the non-resident while the recipient was outside New Zealand.

New Zealand has no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based invoice validation mandate from Inland Revenue. GST-registered suppliers must issue valid tax invoices for supplies of NZD 50 or more including GST and must retain records for at least seven years. E-invoicing, including PEPPOL-based e-invoicing, is permitted voluntarily. Physical import and export declarations are lodged with New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window under the Customs and Excise Act 2018; Inland Revenue operates a risk-based post-return audit model.

Inland Revenue may retrospectively claw back the 0% GST rate and assess output GST at 15% from the date of supply if the export zero-rating is unsupported by documents or if goods or services are later diverted for use in New Zealand. Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, shortfall penalties apply as follows: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for an unacceptable tax position, and up to 150% for evasion or abusive tax positions; use-of-money interest accrues on any unpaid GST. False or misleading documentation can lead to prosecution. Records substantiating export zero-rating must be retained for at least seven years. The 180-day foreign exchange repatriation window is treated as a documentary benchmark; longer delays, non-repatriation, or unusual payment structures will trigger heightened audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised and convertible foreign exchange regime with no exchange control legislation or restrictions on the repatriation of capital, dividends, or loan repayments for foreign-owned entities. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) does not require prior approval for current account transactions such as trade settlements, service fees, or royalties, nor does it impose surrender requirements on export proceeds. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, intercompany loans, and equity injections, are similarly free from RBNZ exchange control approval, although certain transactions may trigger notification requirements under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 if they involve significant business assets or sensitive land. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely floating in the international markets, and authorised banks are permitted to facilitate spot and forward FX transactions without restriction. In practice, profitability remittance hurdles are minimal, limited to standard corporate governance documentation such as board resolutions, audited financial statements, tax residency certificates, and compliance with the New Zealand Inland Revenue Department's withholding tax obligations on dividends and interest. Because there is no currency inconvertibility risk, offshore treasury operations can maintain multi-currency accounts and execute hedging instruments directly with domestic and global banks. The AML/CFT framework, administered by the RBNZ alongside the Department of Internal Affairs and the Financial Markets Authority, focuses on customer due diligence, beneficial ownership transparency, and suspicious transaction reporting rather than on FX controls. Therefore, the primary frictions for a NZ corporate account are KYC-related, not FX regulatory barriers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender requirements on export foreign currency receipts. Exporters may retain all proceeds in foreign currency accounts or convert them to NZD at their discretion. There is no mandatory timeline or conversion ratio under any New Zealand statute; the only obligations are standard tax reporting and AML/CFT record-keeping.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI entities such as Wise, Airwallex, and Revolut generally hold an NZ money service provider registration or operate through an NZ-registered entity; their viability for servicing a New Zealand limited company is Medium-to-High for transaction execution but Low relative to Tier-1 domestic banks for capital escrow and local regulatory compliance. Wise is integrated into the target payment rails and is viable for small-trade disbursements and FX conversion, but it is not a substitute for a New Zealand bank settlement account under corporate treasury standards. The RBNZ's AML/CFT supervisor treats EMIs as reporting entities, and overseas EMIs without an NZ financial service provider licence cannot legally onboard NZ corporates as a primary bank. Therefore, the recommended solution is a domestic Tier-1 bank account for statutory capital and operational settlements, supplemented by Wise for low-value fast payments, not for principal liquidity clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for a New Zealand limited company is routed as share subscription proceeds or shareholder loans through SWIFT MT103 from the parent company's bank. The remittance should clearly state the purpose code 'C07' under the OECD standard or its New Zealand equivalent for capital investment, referencing the shareholder resolution and the share allotment certificate. New Zealand does not require formal FX control approval or capital verification, but the bank will require a copy of the board resolution authorising the issue of shares, a completed client tax information questionnaire (W-8BEN-E if US-related), and a declaration as to the source of funds. The funds must be received in NZD through a domestic Tier-1 bank settlement account to allow for proper value dating and downstream disbursements.Outbound trade disbursements for BIM/CAD services are cleared via SWIFT MT103 for client invoice settlements and vendor payments, with ICC Uniform Rules for Forfaiting applicable only if receivables are discounted. Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) are suitable for large physical project contracts, while Documentary Collections are used for low-risk drafting deliverables. For sub-$10,000 disbursements, Wise provides faster but the actual clearing is still via domestic New Zealand settlements; all outbound payments must include a correct payment purpose code and beneficiary KYC package. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$1 million aggregate cross-border related party transactions (goods, services, intangibles, interest) triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) akin to offshore jurisdictions; however, general anti-avoidance rules (Part IVA of the Income Tax Act 2007) and treaty abuse provisions (Principal Purpose Test) apply. Companies must demonstrate real economic substance (e.g., office, employees, decision-making) to claim treaty benefits and avoid recharacterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 150% of the tax shortfall; criminal conviction may result in imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to NZ$50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participate in evasion; civil restitution orders and travel bans may be imposed.
Shell Company (Lack of Economic Substance)

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may disregard the entity for tax purposes, deny treaty relief, and impose penalties up to 100% of the tax avoided; interest on unpaid tax accrues.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if the company is a sham; they may face disqualification as directors and asset freezing orders.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to NZ$2 million for companies; criminal penalties up to NZ$300,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers can be personally liable for AML breaches; they may face fines, imprisonment, and prohibition from managing companies.
