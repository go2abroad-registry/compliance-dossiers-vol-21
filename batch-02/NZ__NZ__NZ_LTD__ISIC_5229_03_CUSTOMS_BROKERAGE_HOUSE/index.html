Virtual office and commercial-mailbox providers are acceptable to the New Zealand Companies Office for registered-office purposes only where the arrangement includes a genuine street address in New Zealand, a named person available during ordinary business hours to accept service of process, and a real lease for mail handling; a bare PO Box or shared mailbox is not allowed. For the licensed customs brokerage industry, virtual offices are rejected as the primary place of business by the New Zealand Customs Service and Inland Revenue: the entity must maintain a physical office with secure controlled-access storage for client documents, customs bond and drawback records, and the licensed customs officer must be physically locatable. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a New Zealand Limited Company under the Companies Act 1993. A single ordinary share with a par value of NZD 1.00 fully paid is sufficient. No escrow or capital-blocking account is required, but the allotted shares must be paid in accordance with the terms of the allotment and the consideration must be recorded in the share register under section 88 of the Act.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: a Building Warrant of Fitness (BWoF) with an annual compliance schedule must be held for premises comprising specified systems under sections 100-108 of the Building Act 2004; a Fire Evacuation Scheme approved by Fire and Emergency New Zealand is required for premises with 10 or more occupants. Structural: a Chartered Professional Engineer (CPEng) floor-load and seismic adequacy certificate for the office and record-archive areas. Occupational: a general workplace clearance under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 addressing PCBU duties, electrical and mechanical certification, emergency and first-aid plans, and worker consultation; the civil defence readiness plan and business continuity plan are maintained pursuant to the Civil Defence Emergency Management Act 2002.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a New Zealand Limited Company under the Companies Act 1993. A single ordinary share with a par value of NZD 1.00 fully paid is sufficient. No escrow or capital-blocking account is required, but the allotted shares must be paid in accordance with the terms of the allotment and the consideration must be recorded in the share register under section 88 of the Act.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department, New Zealand Government; online portal: myIR (https://www.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited company must first determine whether it is carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand. If so, registration for income tax and GST is required. The foreign entity must obtain an IRD number by completing the relevant IRD registration form (typically IR596 for non-resident companies) and submitting supporting documents including certificate of incorporation, registered office details, director particulars, and proof of New Zealand business activity. Registration can be completed online via myIR or by postal submission. The same IRD number serves as the GST number; separate VAT identification is not issued. If the entity is not otherwise required to be registered for NZ GST because turnover does not exceed NZD 60,000 per 12-month period, voluntary GST registration is permitted subject to IRD approval.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand law does not provide a check-the-box election. Entities carrying on a taxable activity may elect to register for GST voluntarily if annual taxable turnover is below NZD 60,000. New Zealand's hybrid mismatch rules apply under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 and do not offer elective carve-outs; compliance is mandatory for cross-border arrangements falling within the rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods or services may be zero-rated for New Zealand GST where the supplier receives evidence of export or supply to a non-resident recipient and the statutory conditions in sections 11 or 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 are met. For goods, zero-rating requires the exporter to export the goods within 28 days of supply and maintain proof of export. For services supplied to a non-resident recipient, zero-rating requires that the services are not supplied to an NZ resident and are not directly in connection with land or goods situated in New Zealand, subject to specific exclusions. Customs brokerage services supplied in connection with export cargo and to a non-resident principal may qualify for zero-rating where supporting documentation clearly evidences the export.

  • Customs export entry and clearance approval from New Zealand Customs Service
  • Bill of lading or airway bill evidencing physical export
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank statement showing foreign exchange receipt

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime applies in New Zealand. GST returns must be filed electronically through myIR on a monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly frequency depending on turnover and IRD allocation. Peppol e-invoicing is supported on a voluntary basis but no statutory electronic invoicing or clearance mandate exists.

If zero-rating is incorrectly claimed or substantive documentation is incomplete, the full 15% GST becomes payable and is retrospectively clawed back. Inland Revenue may impose use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994, ranging from 20% for unacceptable tax positions to 150% of the tax shortfall for evasion or abusive tax positions. Fraudulent GST returns may additionally lead to criminal prosecution, fines of up to NZD 50,000, and imprisonment for up to five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a freely floating, fully convertible currency with no exchange control legislation currently in force. Since the abolition of exchange controls in 1984, both current account transactions (trade settlement, dividends, interest, royalties, freight, and brokerage fees) and capital account transactions (equity injection, intercompany loans, loan repayments, and portfolio investment) are unrestricted and may be executed at market-determined rates. No prior Reserve Bank consent, transaction licensing, or balance-of-payment certification is required for either resident or non-resident counterparties. Profit remittance and capital repatriation by non-resident shareholders face no statutory hurdle or withholding on equity returns except the standard non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at the applicable domestic or treaty rate, which is withheld by the paying entity and not by the banking channel. The practical constraints are limited to bank-level AML/CFT verification and the absence of a domestic foreign-investment screening filter for a customs brokerage entity unless the underlying activity triggers the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which is inapplicable to a purely service-based customs clearance business without freehold or leasehold farmland or sensitive land acquisition.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion regime exists in New Zealand. Exporters and service providers, including customs brokerage houses receiving cross-border fees and reimbursement of duties, are under no legal obligation to convert foreign currency receipts into NZD, no timing requirement applies, and no percentage threshold of proceeds must be repatriated. Receipts may be held in foreign currency settlement accounts or converted at the entity's commercial discretion. The only compliance obligations are those inherent to the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act), namely record-keeping of the source of funds and reportable transaction thresholds, and to the Income Tax Act 2007 for revenue recognition and FX gain or loss accounting.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua), operating under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 as the sole central bank, prudential supervisor of registered banks, and operator of the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) high-value payment and settlement infrastructure. Foreign exchange policy and payments system oversight reside with the Reserve Bank, while registered banks execute FX transactions without requiring prior central bank approval.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with significant legal constraints. Globally licensed fintech and electronic money institutions (EMIs), such as Airwallex, Wise, and OFX, operate in New Zealand as registered Financial Service Providers (FSPs) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and must appoint an approved dispute-resolution scheme. They may legally hold client funds and offer multi-currency settlement accounts, FX conversion, and SWIFT outbound payments, provided they comply with the AML/CFT Act 2009 and the Companies Act 1993. However, they are not registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and therefore cannot provide a domestic NZD direct-debit arrangement with the New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS) for the payment of customs duty and GST through the Customs Online Services (COS) system, nor can they provide an ESCA-backed settlement capability. A customs brokerage house requires a resident bank account with one of the registered banks to execute the mandatory electronic direct-debit payment rails to NZCS. The prudent architecture is to use a Tier-1 registered bank as the primary duty settlement and liquidity account and to retain an EMI only for high-speed cross-border commission receipts and disbursement of overseas importer reimbursements, with clear contractual segregation of client funds and daily reconciliation.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident individual or corporate shareholders domiciled in FATF high-risk jurisdictions (per the FATF grey list) holding more than 25% of the share capital.

Root cause: Non-resident individual or corporate shareholders domiciled in FATF high-risk jurisdictions (per the FATF grey list) holding more than 25% of the share capital.

Defense evidence: Certified copy of the Companies Office share register and certificate of incorporation; full chain-of-title through to all ultimate beneficial owners; notarised passports and recent residential proof for each beneficial owner; one independent reference letter from a foreign-regime licensed financial institution; a written source-of-wealth declaration covering the capital injection; and a corporate organisational chart with percentage holdings. If a corporate shareholder sits in a grey-listed jurisdiction, the foreign-licensed bank reference must be the sole and sufficient remedy.
Unexplained high transaction velocity or round-dollar daily wire volume consistent with trade-based money laundering, customs-duty pass-through without corresponding importer contracts, or use of the brokerage account as a conversion point for third-party cargo payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder loan capital flow directly by SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or beneficial owner's bank to the NZ_LTD's Tier-1 operational or escrow account. The SWIFT field 23B should encode 'CRED' as the transaction type, field 33B the currency (NZD or foreign currency) and amount, field 50K/50F the remitter's account and full legal name, field 59/59F the NZ_LTD beneficiary account with its BBAN (12-digit account number), field 70 the purpose narrative: 'SHARE CAPITAL INJECTION' or 'INTERCOMPANY LOAN AGREEMENT DATED YYYY-MM-DD', and field 71A 'OUR' or 'SHA' as agreed for charges. There is no statutory New Zealand purpose code regime, so the narrative automatically populates the bank's balance-of-payments and AML/CFT monitoring fields. For equity above incidental thresholds, the bank will request a board resolution from NZ_LTD, a capital increase registry filing with the New Zealand Companies Office (AR01 or share register update), and for intercompany loans, an executed loan agreement with fixed maturity and arm's length interest rate. The funds are credited on the same value day via ESAS, and no prior Reserve Bank consent is required. For any participation by a non-resident person, the entity must also confirm that the Overseas Investment Act 2005 does not apply to its service-only asset base, which it can evidence through a solicitor's certificate stating the entity holds no purchase of sensitive New Zealand land.Outbound treasury disbursements from NZ_LTD comprise four distinct rails: (1) duty and GST payments to the New Zealand Customs Service via the Customs Online Services (COS) portal, executed exclusively through the bank's nominated direct-debit authority (DDA) from the NZD operational account, with settlement on a value date and an automated broker interface (ABI/ACH-equivalent) that references the customs entry and importer client code; (2) settlement of importer reimbursements and customs brokerage fees via local BECS (Bulk Electronic Clearing System, NZCLEAR) in NZD, using the account number and reference fields for reconciliation; (3) cross-border supplier and commission payments via SWIFT MT103 in the trade currency, particularly USD, AUD, and EUR, for settlement of customs brokerage sub-contracts, logistics partner invoices, and warranty deposits, with field 70 narrative 'BROKERAGE FEE CONTRACT' or 'CUSTOMS DUTY REIMBURSEMENT TRADE']; and (4) documentary collection and commercial letters of credit (UCP600) for the payment of imports under INCOTERMS such as DDP, DAP, and FOB, issued or advised by the Tier-1 bank, with the LC covering duty-inclusive cargo value and the broker financing any duty-funded portion through a short-term trade facility. The recommended treasury structure establishes a dedicated duty pass-through account with a minimum 30-day average balance equal to at least seven days of duty disbursements to absorb timing mismatches between Customs direct debits and importer reimbursements, supported by a contingent working capital line.Outright FX Forward (NZD vs USD, AUD, EUR, GBP) on a deliverable basis, maturing up to 12 months, with a bank credit facility and ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or bank-format FX termsFX Swap (Upi-to-forward or forwards-against-spot) combining two legs to manage matched duty-payment cash-flow timingCross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (CCIRS) on intercompany loan and capital instruments where non-resident shareholders fund in foreign currencyEuropean-style FX Option (call or put) and zero-cost collars for protection against adverse NZDUSD moves on customs fee receivablesNon-Deliverable Forward (NDF) on exotic trade-pair currencies such as CNY, INR, or IDR where the offshore NDF market offers liquidity and physical settlement is infeasible due to local capital controls, permitted under New Zealand law and RBNZ supervision but requiring bank counterparty approvalBasis swap between NZD and USD floating rates to hedge any working capital facility funding mismatch

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with significant legal constraints. Globally licensed fintech and electronic money institutions (EMIs), such as Airwallex, Wise, and OFX, operate in New Zealand as registered Financial Service Providers (FSPs) under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and must appoint an approved dispute-resolution scheme. They may legally hold client funds and offer multi-currency settlement accounts, FX conversion, and SWIFT outbound payments, provided they comply with the AML/CFT Act 2009 and the Companies Act 1993. However, they are not registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 and therefore cannot provide a domestic NZD direct-debit arrangement with the New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS) for the payment of customs duty and GST through the Customs Online Services (COS) system, nor can they provide an ESCA-backed settlement capability. A customs brokerage house requires a resident bank account with one of the registered banks to execute the mandatory electronic direct-debit payment rails to NZCS. The prudent architecture is to use a Tier-1 registered bank as the primary duty settlement and liquidity account and to retain an EMI only for high-speed cross-border commission receipts and disbursement of overseas importer reimbursements, with clear contractual segregation of client funds and daily reconciliation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A complete Local File is expected where the aggregate annual value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZ$10 million; a Master File (and Country-by-Country Report) is required for multinational enterprise groups with global consolidated revenue exceeding NZ$1.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not issue Economic Substance Regulations in the BVI/Cayman model, but the general anti-avoidance rule in Income Tax Act 2007 s BG 1, reinforced by the BEPS-strengthening rules introduced in 2018, denies treaty and domestic tax benefits where a corporate structure lacks real commercial activity, local staff, office premises, and decision-making substance. For a licensed customs brokerage house, substantive functions include handling cargo documentation, securing clearance licences, and communicating with New Zealand Customs; a 'bare shelf' company acting solely as an invoice conduit faces cancellation of tax benefits and licence revocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act 1994 ss 143 and 143A, criminal penalties include a fine of up to NZ$50,000 (or NZ$100,000 where the tax shortfall exceeds NZ$100,000) and imprisonment of up to 5 years (or 10 years for large-scale evasion); a civil shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the evaded tax is also imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate, direct, or approve the offending conduct face personal criminal liability, a penalty of up to three times the unpaid tax, civil asset-recovery orders, and an overseas travel ban pending final settlement of Inland Revenue assessments; the corporate veil is pierced under the Taxes Act's director-penalty provisions.
Shell Company Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of all tax treaty benefits, reassessment of profit at normal rates, and a 40% to 150% shortfall penalty under the Tax Administration Act 1994; the Registrar may deregister the company under the Companies Act 1993 for failure to maintain a resident director and a registered office in New Zealand.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid company tax where they knowingly allow the company to trade without reasonable prospect of payment (Companies Act 1993 s 135), and may be ordered to repay diverted assets; a pecuniary penalty order can cover the full amount of tax avoided and travel restrictions may be imposed by the High Court.
Money Laundering and AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, civil penalties for reporting entities can be up to NZ$5 million per contravention; criminal money laundering under the Crimes Act 1961 s 242(2) attracts imprisonment of up to 7 years and a substantial fine; the customs broker licence may also be suspended or revoked under the Customs and Excise Act 2018.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or recklessly allow the brokerage to be used as a laundering conduit are treated in law as principals, with surrender of personal assets, disqualification from managing companies for up to 5 years, and preventive travel bans under the High Court; imprisonment applies for the term of the money-laundering offence.
Unlicensed Customs Brokerage and Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: An offence under the Customs and Excise Act 2018; a person convicted is liable to a fine not exceeding NZ$50,000 per offence and, for a misdeclaration causing a loss of duty, to a further amount equal to the lost duty and forfeiture of the goods or a penalty in lieu.

Director personal liability: Where the offending act was carried out with the consent or connivance of a director, the director is personally liable under the officer-liability provision of the Act; civil restitution, confiscation of illegally obtained benefits, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to 7 years may be ordered, effectively piercing the corporate veil.
