Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mail-forwarding address can satisfy the initial registered office requirement only if it is a physical street address in New Zealand and not a PO Box, and the company's records are available for inspection at that address during business hours. However, for a maritime crew recruitment and crewing management agency, the Inland Revenue Department, the AML/CFT supervisor, and New Zealand banks will generally reject a pure virtual office for the operational and payroll account because the sector requires demonstrable substance, employment contracts, and seafarer welfare records. Therefore a virtual office is permissible only for the statutory registered-office mailbox, not for the revenue and banking substance of the entity, and the risk of refusal is high unless a lease or managed serviced-office suite with physical records is maintained.

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a New Zealand limited company under the Companies Act 1993 via the New Zealand Companies Office. Step 2: During incorporation, the Companies Office will coordinate with Inland Revenue so that the company is issued an IRD number; the same IRD number is used for income tax and GST, with a GST suffix for GST purposes. Step 3: For a foreign non-resident company not incorporated in New Zealand but carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand, complete the Non-resident Company IRD Number Application (IR596) through myIR, attaching the overseas certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director and shareholder details, a New Zealand agent or contact address, and evidence of New Zealand-sourced income or taxable activity. Step 4: Register for GST through myIR when the NZD 60,000 rolling 12-month taxable supply threshold is exceeded, or voluntarily if carrying on a taxable activity; the IRD number with a GST suffix is the GST registration number. Step 5: Register as an employer for PAYE through myIR because the crew recruitment agency may pay New Zealand employees. Step 6: Maintain statutory records under section 16 of the Tax Administration Act 1994, the Companies Act 1993 and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for at least 7 years.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. A standard New Zealand limited company is a separate taxable entity subject to company income tax at 28%. It cannot make a US-style check-the-box election. The Look-Through Company (LTC) election is not available here because all shareholders are not New Zealand-resident individuals or qualifying trusts; the target entity expressly permits non-resident ownership. Hybrid mismatch rules under New Zealand BEPS legislation apply to cross-border intragroup arrangements but are not an elective regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period for supplies connected with New Zealand; a non-resident supplier of remote services or low-value goods to New Zealand consumers is also required to register when those consumer supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 if they are exported by the supplier or supplied to a non-resident and removed from New Zealand, in each case within 28 days of the supply or entered for export within that period. Services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed are zero-rated to the extent the supply is not an excepted supply, such as services in connection with New Zealand land or certain movable personal property. For a maritime crew recruitment and crew management agency, supplies of crew sourcing, vetting, payroll administration, and manning coordination to a non-resident shipowner or international vessel operator can qualify for zero-rating when the contractual recipient is genuinely non-resident, the services are supplied to that non-resident outside New Zealand, the vessel's employment is international, and the payment is settled from overseas. Offshore digital services supplied to New Zealand consumers are not export supplies and remain subject to 15% GST under the remote services rules.

  • New Zealand Customs export declaration (export entry) lodged in the Trade Single Window for any goods physically exported
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill or Airway Bill evidencing departure from New Zealand within the statutory period
  • Commercial invoice describing the zero-rated supply and referencing the relevant Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 section
  • Signed crew management or crew recruitment contract showing the non-resident shipowner or foreign vessel operator as the principal and the New Zealand entity as supplier
  • Certificate of non-residence or equivalent foreign tax or company registry evidence for the recipient shipowner
  • Bank and foreign exchange settlement records showing cross-border receipt in a foreign currency or via SWIFT wire from the overseas counterparty

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no pre-approval API obligation, and no anti-fraud digital stamping regime in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Peppol network, where accredited New Zealand service providers transmit Peppol-compliant invoices to Inland Revenue and trading partners. GST returns are filed through myIR, not through invoice clearance.

New Zealand has no fixed statutory deadline for foreign-exchange repatriation of export earnings; the substantiation requirement is to demonstrate that payment came from the non-resident recipient. Inland Revenue may request the supporting export and cross-border payment documents during audit. If zero-rating is claimed without valid export evidence, or goods are not exported within 28 days, the supply is recharacterised as taxable at 15% and the GST refund is clawed back with use-of-money interest from the due date. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. Knowingly filing a false GST return can also lead to criminal liability. Records must be retained for at least 7 years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised, freely convertible current and capital account. There are no general exchange controls under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989, no centralised foreign exchange approval for ordinary trade transactions, and no mandatory surrender of export proceeds or service fees in foreign currency. The NZD floats independently and may be bought or sold at market-clearing rates through licensed registered banks. For a maritime crew recruitment agency invoicing international shipowners in USD, GBP, EUR or NZD, convertibility risk is negligible; funds may remain in foreign-currency accounts at a registered bank or be converted to NZD at the bank's spot rate. Statutory constraints remain confined to customer due diligence, sanctions and AML/CFT obligations. Profit remittance and dividend distribution to non-resident shareholders do not require exchange-control approval, but must comply with Companies Act 1994 distribution solvency tests and applicable non-resident withholding tax procedures. However, for counterparty banks located in OFAC/EU-sanctioned jurisdictions, correspondent banking due diligence may block inward wires; treasury policy should require remitters to route via non-sanctioned intermediary banks. Overall, New Zealand raises no capital or current account convertibility obstacle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules and no mandatory conversion ratio or timeline for foreign currency receipts in New Zealand. Crew-manning service fees in USD, GBP, EUR or other currencies may be retained in an offshore or onshore foreign-currency account, converted to NZD voluntarily, or applied directly to business disbursements. Balance of payments statistics may require transaction-level reporting to Stats NZ/RBNZ if the entity is selected for survey, but this is statistical, not surrender-based.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Financial Markets Supervisory Department; New Zealand operates without a separate Foreign Exchange Administration)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary settlement rail; moderate only as an ancillary sub-limit facility. New Zealand law restricts deposit-taking to registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989. A fintech EMI such as a non-bank payment provider may hold a Financial Service Provider registration but cannot operate for the purpose of holding statutory capital escrow or settling crew payroll from its own balance sheet. The New Zealand AML/CFT Act 2009 requires the reporting entity to be supervised by RBNZ/FMA; a foreign digital EMI ordinarily cannot meet the capital and reserve requirements for deposit-taking. Therefore, the NZ Ltd must open its treasury gateway directly with a Tier 1 domestic bank; EMI rails may only support travel expense or treasury card spending after the underlying bank account has been approved, and must not be named beneficiary of inbound capital injections.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions per income year triggers the mandatory Transfer Pricing Disclosure Return (IR833); contemporaneous Master File and Local File documentation must be maintained to the OECD standard for any transaction above this threshold, and IRD may also request documentation for smaller intercompany contracts under its general information powers.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not have an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute of the type used in offshore financial centres, but the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1, the specific anti-avoidance rules in sections GC 6-GC 14, and the limitation-on-benefits/principal-purpose test embedded in New Zealand's tax treaties require the crew agency to demonstrate real activity and core income-generating activities in New Zealand. A so-called contractor that only invoices commissions while the actual recruitment, payroll, and vessel-crew supervision are performed by non-resident group companies will be treated as having no economic substance; IRD may reattribute the income to the entity that performs the value-creating functions, require arm's length service fees, and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Maritime payroll and withholding tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 sections 141B and 141C impose a 20% shortfall penalty for an unacceptable tax position and a 40% shortfall penalty for an abusive tax avoidance arrangement, with use-of-money interest. Section 143A criminalises evasion with a company fine of up to NZD 250,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years for the natural-person officers involved. The employer-level PAYE obligations are imposed under the PAYE rules in Schedule 4 of the Income Tax Act 2007, and a failure to account for withheld PAYE triggers director penalties under section 149A of the TAA.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced by statute: directors can be held jointly and severally liable for withheld PAYE and NRWT that the company deducted but failed to pay to the Commissioner. Civil asset-recovery proceedings may attach personal assets under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2009; the Commissioner may apply for a departure prohibition order under TAA sections 157A-157G, barring the director from leaving New Zealand until the tax debt is secured. Reckless trading liability under sections 135-136 of the Companies Act 1993 permits the Court to order a director to repay company losses, and fraudulent trading under section 380 of the Companies Act can lead to personal liability and criminal disqualification as a director.
Transfer pricing documentation failure and base eroding intercompany fees

Statutory sanction: Sections GC 6-GC 14 of the Income Tax Act 2007 empower the IRD to calculate the taxpayer's income at arm's length, reduce the amount of the deduction, and recharacterise the payment as a dividend or capital contribution. A failure to hold contemporaneous documentation reverses the burden of proof and exposes the company to a 40% abusive-tax-position shortfall penalty under TAA section 141C, plus shortfall interest. A false IR833 declaration is a tax refund/false statement offence under TAA section 143A, with fines of up to NZD 250,000 for companies.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused or permitted the false documentation may be personally assessed for the shortfall penalty under TAA section 141D when they materially contributed to the offending tax position without taking reasonable care. In insolvency settings, directors who continue the company while the tax debt is mounting are liable under the reckless trading provisions of the Companies Act 1993, and the Court can make a personal contribution order against directors' assets. Criminal conviction for dishonest transfer-pricing documents attracts imprisonment of up to 5 years, in addition to travel restrictions and director disqualification under company law.
Treaty shopping and sham DTA relief on profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner cancels the treaty benefit under the relevant DTA's beneficial-ownership, limitation-of-benefits, or principal-purpose test, and applies the general anti-avoidance rule in section BG 1. The under-withheld NRWT is collected at the statutory rate (30% dividends, 15% interest/royalties) with interest; a shortfall penalty of 40% applies for an abusive tax arrangement under TAA section 141C. Knowingly submitting false declarations to support the treaty claim is a criminal offence under TAA sections 143A-143B, with fines up to NZD 250,000 and 5 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who arranged or participated in the conduit structure may be jointly liable for the company's withholding-tax shortfall; a court may pierce the corporate veil where the NZ Ltd and the conduit are a mere sham or façade. Civil liability includes asset restitution in favour of the Commissioner, personal accountability for company debts under the Companies Act 1993, a departure prohibition order for unpaid tax, and in serious cases conspiracy or dishonesty charges under the Crimes Act 1961 carrying imprisonment of up to 7 years.
