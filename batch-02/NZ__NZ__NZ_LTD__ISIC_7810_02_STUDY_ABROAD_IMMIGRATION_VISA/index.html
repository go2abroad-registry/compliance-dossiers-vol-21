Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox may satisfy the minimum registered-office address condition of the Companies Act 1993 only if it is a physical street address in New Zealand at which service can be effected. However, a virtual office is not acceptable as the principal place of business for an immigration-visa advisory company required to be licensed under the Immigration Advisers Licensing Act 2007, and New Zealand banks will ordinarily reject a pure commercial-mailbox address for corporate account opening because AML/CFT customer due diligence requires verifiable physical business premises, client-meeting capacity, and operational substance. Inland Revenue may also reject a mailbox only address for GST registration absent evidence of genuine economic activity. Therefore, for this industry, a virtual office is not recommended and is treated as high-risk if used.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up share capital is required under New Zealand law; there is no blocked escrow capital account and no capital deposit threshold. The board may authorise shares to be issued for cash, property or services, and shares must either be fully paid or lawfully designated as unpaid with a call requirement recorded in the share register.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable for manufacturing or chemical operations because there is no physical industrial plant. As a low-risk advisory office, the business must nevertheless hold a valid Building Warrant of Fitness where the Building Act 2004 compliance schedule applies to means of escape from fire, maintain a Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 worker safety system with fire extinguishers, first aid kit, emergency evacuation plan, and a traffic control plan only if a dedicated waiting area of more than ten persons is used. No Civil Defense fire permit is required for a professional office, and an emergency evacuation procedure must be registered with the local fire and emergency authority if the landlord requires it.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up share capital is required under New Zealand law; there is no blocked escrow capital account and no capital deposit threshold. The board may authorise shares to be issued for cash, property or services, and shares must either be fully paid or lawfully designated as unpaid with a call requirement recorded in the share register.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD / Te Tari Taake), the national revenue authority of New Zealand, operating the myIR online portal at https://www.ird.govt.nz/myir. New Zealand tax filings, GST registrations, and IRD number administration are all processed through this statutory body.

E-government filing portal

A foreign limited company must obtain an IRD number (the statutory tax identification number for income tax, GST, PAYE, and FBT) by: (1) undertaking the Companies Office New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) registration if the entity is incorporated in New Zealand or, for offshore entities, registering the foreign company on the national companies register under the Companies Act 1993 if it carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand; (2) filing the Inland Revenue non-resident company tax registration application either online through myIR or through an approved tax agent, supplying certified certificate of incorporation, registered address in New Zealand, directors' names, foreign tax residence details, and the date New Zealand taxable activity commences; (3) upon IRD acceptance, receiving a unique IRD number for filing corporate income tax returns, GST returns, and employer obligations; (4) separately activating GST registration in myIR once the NZD 60,000 taxable-supply threshold is exceeded or where the entity voluntarily elects registration; and (5) maintaining the IRD number as the primary TIN while procuring a customs client code from the New Zealand Customs Service for any physical export transactions.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign limited company must obtain an IRD number (the statutory tax identification number for income tax, GST, PAYE, and FBT) by: (1) undertaking the Companies Office New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) registration if the entity is incorporated in New Zealand or, for offshore entities, registering the foreign company on the national companies register under the Companies Act 1993 if it carries on a taxable activity in New Zealand; (2) filing the Inland Revenue non-resident company tax registration application either online through myIR or through an approved tax agent, supplying certified certificate of incorporation, registered address in New Zealand, directors' names, foreign tax residence details, and the date New Zealand taxable activity commences; (3) upon IRD acceptance, receiving a unique IRD number for filing corporate income tax returns, GST returns, and employer obligations; (4) separately activating GST registration in myIR once the NZD 60,000 taxable-supply threshold is exceeded or where the entity voluntarily elects registration; and (5) maintaining the IRD number as the primary TIN while procuring a customs client code from the New Zealand Customs Service for any physical export transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand has no US-style check-the-box election. A limited company is taxed as a separate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act 2007. Eligible closely held companies with no more than five qualifying shareholders may elect look-through company (LTC) treatment to be fiscally transparent. Additional statutory elections include group tax consolidation under subpart IG, GST grouping under section 55 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, and available hybrid entity mismatch adjustments under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018, which are not elective waivers but mandatory override rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; once this economic turnover threshold is exceeded, GST registration is compulsory. Voluntary registration below the threshold is available for businesses carrying on a taxable activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for zero-rating under section 11 of the GST Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand by contractual arrangement and ownership passes to a non-resident before departure. Services, including study-abroad placement and immigration visa advisory, qualify for zero-rating under section 11A when the services are supplied to a non-resident person who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided they are not supplied directly in connection with New Zealand land, New Zealand moveable personal property, or supplied to an associated person physically present in New Zealand. The supplier must hold written evidence of the non-resident status, the offshore location of the recipient, and the source of the payment to satisfy Revenue audit.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or House Air Waybill (if any physical shipment accompanies the service)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or supply-chain e-invoicing approval. Inland Revenue supports voluntary Peppol BIS 3.0-based e-invoicing through the A-NZ network; traditional paper and PDF tax invoices remain legally valid. GST-registered suppliers must issue a tax invoice for every taxable supply exceeding NZD 50 (excluding GST), and retain full records for seven years. GST returns are filed electronically in myIR with no API-based pre-clearance requirement.

Inland Revenue may reopen historical GST returns and assess unrecovered GST where zero-rating is not substantiated; the statutory non-evasion time bar is four years and evasion has no time bar. Clawback includes the previously zero-rated output tax re-grossed at 15%, use-of-money interest from the original due date, and TAA shortfall penalties: 20% for lack of reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 100% for fraudulent or wilful evasion. Fraudulent claims can also attract criminal conviction penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994, with separate exposure for misleading Customs documentation under the Customs and Excise Act 2018.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised convertible currency regime. Both current account and capital account transactions are freely permitted with no general exchange control approvals, pre-notification, or mandatory foreign exchange surrender requirements. Profit remittances and repatriation of investment proceeds are unrestricted; the sole practical hurdles are standard corporate tax compliance, director verification, and bank-level AML/CFT source-of-funds due diligence. Non-resident investment is facilitated through standard share capital or shareholder loan structures; the only regulatory overlay is the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which applies to specific sensitive land and significant business assets, not to ordinary treasury flows of a study-abroad and immigration advisory company.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or obligatory surrender ratio for export receipts in New Zealand. The RBNZ does not require conversion of foreign currency earnings into NZD, and no minimum export proceeds retention threshold applies. An NZ LTD may invoice, receive, hold, and disburse foreign currency freely. However, all foreign-sourced income and foreign currency gains must be declared for New Zealand income tax purposes, and bank transfers referencing consultancy services must be supported by properly executed service agreements and tax invoices to satisfy AML/CFT record-keeping.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ), acting jointly with the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) as the AML/CFT supervisor for registered financial service providers

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium viability as primary treasury infrastructure. Offshore digital EMI providers are not licensed banks in New Zealand and cannot directly access local clearing systems such as Bulk Electronic Clearing System (BECS) or acquire RBNZ settlement accounts. Their use should be limited to ancillary receivables collection or pre-payment facilitation for overseas students, and only if the EMI is registered on the FMA AML/CFT supervised entity register and meets New Zealand data-residency and consumer-protection standards. The principal operating entity in New Zealand must maintain at least one physical corporate account with a locally incorporated licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections from non-resident shareholders should be remitted by SWIFT MT103 to the NZ corporate account using the principal bank's BIC and the corporate account IBAN. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory non-resident withholding tax on dividends is 30% for non-resident companies; the approved issuer levy (AIL) of 2% applies only in respect of interest paid on public securities issued by approved issuers, not dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand's transfer pricing documentation thresholds: Local File is not mandatory for all taxpayers but the IRD requires contemporaneous documentation if the transaction value exceeds NZD 4 million for cross-border related-party transactions in a single income year; the Master File is expected where consolidated group revenue exceeds NZD 100 million and cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZD 10 million. Administrative penalty provisions apply if documentation is not prepared and a tax shortfall arises from the transfer pricing adjustment.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand applies a robust economic substance doctrine through section GB 41 and GA 1 (general anti-avoidance). For immigration consulting companies, the IRD examines whether the New Zealand entity performs substantive functions: employment of local licensed advisers, physical office, local client contracts, and decision-making. A foreign-owned agency that merely routes study-abroad application fees or escrow funds to an offshore parent while claiming commission deductions without local substance will be recharacterised. New Zealand has adopted the OECD BEPS package; the transfer pricing rules in subpart GC apply to cross-border related-party arrangements, and the Arbitration Panel may allocate income based on economic substance. Dividends distributed from an enterprise lacking economic substance do not qualify for imputation credit refunds.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, evasion penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall (s 141E); absolute discharge penalty of 200% for evasion with intent to conceal. Interest accrues. Criminal conviction for tax evasion under s 143A can result in a fine up to NZD 50,000 for an individual and NZD 250,000 for a company, and up to 5 years imprisonment for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for tax shortfalls if they have knowingly assisted in evasion or if they operated the company otherwise than in good faith (Companies Act 1993, s 300 and s 301). Courts may issue civil recovery orders, assets freezing orders, travel bans under the Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters Act, and extradition proceedings for serious tax evasion. Director disqualification under the Companies Act can last up to 5 years.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: IRD may disregard the structure and attribute all New Zealand-sourced income to the foreign principal under the reconstruction provisions (s GA 1 and s GC 6). Penalties: shortfall penalty for unacceptable tax position (20%) or gross carelessness (40%). The Registrar of Companies may strike off the company under the Companies Act for not carrying on business; directors risk criminal liability for filing false statements under s 377.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell company can be personally liable for the company's debts if they allowed the business to operate while insolvent (s 135 reckless trading). The Financial Markets Authority or Immigration New Zealand may refer directors for fraudulent conduct in immigration services, causing cancellation of the company's immigration consulting licence and adverse immigration consequences for director visas.
Immigration and Licensing Infraction

Statutory sanction: Fines up to NZD 100,000 for unlicensed immigration advice (s 64 of the Immigration Advisers Licensing Act); a company that employs unlicensed advisers is subject to fines up to NZD 75,000 for each offence. Under the Immigration Act 2009, criminal liability for fraudulent documents carries imprisonment up to 7 years and a fine of up to NZD 100,000. Revenue derived from unlicensed activity is taxable as assessable income and cannot claim tax deductions for fines or penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors, as persons concerned in the management, are jointly and severally liable for the fine if the offence is attributable to their consent, connivance or neglect. The Immigration Advisers Authority may impose a permanent ban on acting as an immigration adviser; personal travel bans and refusal of visas under section 157 Immigration Act may also apply.
Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AMLCFT Act), a company is subject to civil liability of up to NZD 2 million for each violation, and NZD 200,000 per violation for individuals. Criminal penalties for wilful or reckless breaches can reach imprisonment for up to 2 years. The FMA, Reserve Bank of New Zealand, and Department of Internal Affairs may suspend or cancel the reporting entity's AML registration, which functionally terminates the business.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable if they were a party to the contravention or knowingly assisted in the breach. Individuals face imprisonment up to 2 years per offence in addition to fines. Directors of reporting entities must ensure an effective compliance programme and undergo AML training; AML enforcement action can result in statutory management, director banning orders, and extraterritorial travel bans under criminal mutual assistance mechanisms.
Cross-Border Bribery and Corruption

Statutory sanction: The Secret Commissions Act 1910 imposes a fine of up to NZD 20,000 for companies and imprisonment up to 2 years for individuals. The Crimes Act 1961 bribery provisions carry imprisonment up to 7 years and confiscation of proceeds. New Zealand ratified the OECD Anti-Bribery Convention; the Serious Fraud Office (SFO) may prosecute corporate offenders; any bribe-related payment is non-deductible and a criminal confiscation order under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1991 can recover assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised or knowingly permitted bribery face imprisonment up to 7 years. The director's conduct constitutes management breach under companies law, triggering personal liability for losses; travel bans and extradition are available to foreign anti-bribery authorities. A criminal conviction automatically disqualifies the director from managing a company for up to 10 years unless relief is granted by the Court.
