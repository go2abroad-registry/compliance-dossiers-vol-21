Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under sections 16-18 of the Companies Act 1993, a New Zealand company must maintain a registered office in New Zealand at a physical street address; a PO Box and an entirely electronic address are not sufficient. A virtual-office provider that supplies a unique physical street address, mail scanning, and same-day forwarding can satisfy the registered-office requirement for Companies Office purposes. However, for a programmatic AdTech DSP/SSP entity targeting Swift Wire and ACH Direct payment rails, IRD and the major New Zealand trading banks will generally require demonstrable commercial substance. Banks frequently reject commercial-mailbox-only addresses for current accounts and treasury services because the address cannot be verified as actual occupancy, utility and business rates are absent, and AML/CFT customer due diligence cannot be satisfied. A virtual office is therefore permissible only as an initial accommodation address where the directors are New Zealand residents, GST and company tax obligations are current, and the DSP/SSP business maintains genuine contracts, invoices, and systems in New Zealand.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand, administered through the 'myIR' secure online portal at www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity obtains an IRD number (the single statutory tax identification number) by applying to Inland Revenue and submitting certified corporate documents; once the entity intends to carry on a taxable activity or makes taxable supplies in New Zealand, it registers for GST through myIR if the NZD 60,000 threshold is met or exceeded. Under the Tax Administration Act 1994, the IRD number is used for income tax, GST, PAYE, and withholding tax; no separate VAT identification number is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Eligible close-held companies may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under the Income Tax Act 2007; GST registration may be elected voluntarily; GST group members may elect to file as a single registered person under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. New Zealand does not have a US-style check-the-box election, and hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically rather than by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in a rolling 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services (including programmatic advertising/SSP services) also register when taxable remote supplies to New Zealand consumers exceed NZD 60,000 in 12 months

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under section 11(1) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 for goods physically exported from New Zealand and for services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, provided the services are not connected to New Zealand land or goods and are not supplied to a New Zealand fixed establishment of the recipient. For goods, physical export must occur within the 28-day statutory export window. For the programme advertising/SSP model, the supplier must retain current documentary evidence of the recipient's non-resident status and the offshore receipt of consideration; for services, no physical customs cycle applies.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Electronic Export Declaration lodged through the Trade Single Window for physical exports
  • Ocean bill of lading or airway bill evidencing actual export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the goods and overseas destination
  • SWIFT/MT103 foreign exchange receipt confirmation or bank inward remittance advice evidencing payment from the non-resident buyer
  • For zero-rated services: executed customer contract, nature of services statement, and a certificate of tax residence or foreign business registration extract for the non-resident recipient

No real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping is required in New Zealand. E-invoicing is optional and operates through the PEPPOL network; New Zealand government agencies are required to accept PEPPOL-compatible e-invoices. GST returns are filed electronically through myIR, and registration details are maintained by Inland Revenue.

Inland Revenue may reassess a supply at the standard 15% GST rate if the documentary export file is incomplete, if goods do not leave New Zealand within the 28-day statutory export period, or if the non-resident status of the recipient is not established. Clawback assessments may include use-of-money interest; shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 range from 20% for an unacceptable tax position up to 150% for evasion, with criminal prosecution available for fraudulent export zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a free-floating exchange rate regime with full and unrestricted convertibility of the New Zealand dollar (NZD) across both current account and capital account transactions. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not impose exchange control regulations, mandatory surrender requirements, or prior approval mechanisms for foreign currency transactions. There are no restrictions on the repatriation of dividends, capital, or loan repayments for non-resident investors, provided corporate governance formalities such as director resolutions and Inland Revenue compliance are observed. The Overseas Investment Act 2005 does impose screening on certain sensitive land and commercial fishing assets, but standard operational activities in the programmatic advertising technology sector are not notifiable. Trade settlement, service payments for real-time media procurement, and intercompany funding can flow freely through the banking system without FX licensing. Profit remittance is subject only to standard taxation obligations, including Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) on dividends and interest, which can be reduced under New Zealand's double tax agreements. Overall, New Zealand is a fully liberalized capital account jurisdiction with no surrender, conversion quota, or waiting-period impediments, making it a highly efficient liquidity hub for cross-border treasury operations in the AdTech and digital advertising value chain.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign currency receipts. Exporters of services, including programmatic advertising and SSP platform fees, may retain all foreign currency earnings in multi-currency accounts or convert them to NZD at their discretion without any surrender obligation to the Reserve Bank or any authorized dealer.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) acting as the central bank and prudential supervisor, with external exchange control functions managed under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, and similar payment service providers have moderate-to-high feasibility only as ancillary settlement rails for low-value programmatic payouts within New Zealand. However, they are not licensed as registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act and are therefore legally barred from taking deposits or issuing NZD account numbers that participate in the domestic Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) and BECS clearing. Offshore EMIs also lack the statutory capacity to hold capital escrow funds, issue official NZD bank confirmation letters for capital verification, or support the International Investment Position reporting obligations to Statistics New Zealand. Consequently, for the NZ_LTD treasury blueprint, offshore digital EMIs should be used exclusively for merchant payouts and small-volume publisher disbursements, while all capital injection, tax settlement, and major trade settlement flows must be routed through a Tier-1 domestic registered commercial bank to ensure full prudential compliance and auditability.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Within New Zealand, the statutory Non-Resident Withholding Tax rate on gross dividends paid by a NZ company to a non-resident shareholder is 30%. The tax is final, is withheld at source by the paying company, and attaches to the full amount of the dividend before the application of any Double Tax Agreement rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million aggregate cross-border related-party transactions is the generally accepted threshold recognized by the IRD in its transfer pricing operational statement for contemporaneous Local File / Master File preparation; separate Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for New Zealand-resident MNE groups with consolidated group revenue above EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation regime, but the IRD applies OECD BEPS principles and tax avoidance doctrines to strip benefits from shell structures. For a NZ Ltd operating a programmatic AdTech platform, Core Income-Generating Activities include real-time bid- and ad-inventory decisioning, contract negotiation with DSP/SSP partners, platform risk management, data stewardship, and treasury management. If some or all of these functions are actually performed outside New Zealand through a paper offshore entity, the NZ entity may have a permanent establishment in that offshore jurisdiction; conversely, a foreign interposed company with no substance will not be recognized as beneficial owner for treaty purposes and profits will be re-attributed to New Zealand.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Revenue Suppression

Statutory sanction: This constitutes evasion under the Tax Administration Act 1994, attracting a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the evaded tax, use-of-money interest, late payment penalties and criminal sanctions. Under s 144 TAA 1994 and s 228 Crimes Act 1961, a person who intentionally obtains a tax advantage by deceit is liable to imprisonment and fines.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced because the evasion is a deliberate wrongdoing: directors are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax plus penalties, may face charges of dishonest use of corporate documents, may be disqualified as directors, barred from leaving New Zealand under a Departure Prohibition Order, and sentenced to terms of imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The arrangement is void against the Commissioner under Income Tax Act 2007 ss BG 1 and GB 1 as a tax avoidance arrangement; transfer pricing adjustments are imposed under s GC 13; the tax shortfall penalty is increased to 40-50% for the abusive tax position, and treaty benefits are denied under the DTA Limitation on Benefits / Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement a sham may be personally liable for amounts unpaid by the company under the intentional wrongdoing exceptions; they face civil restitution, insolvent trading liability under Companies Act 1995 s 135, disqualification under s 383, travel restrictions and criminal imprisonment when the sham is used to defraud revenue authorities.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) Evasion and False DTA Certification

Statutory sanction: Under s 149 Tax Administration Act 1994, unpaid RWT/NRWT can be recovered jointly and severally from directors; the failure to withhold and the knowingly false treaty document constitute an abusive tax position attracting up to 50% shortfall penalty, further late payment penalties, use-of-money interest, and a criminal evasion fine of up to NZD 50,000 plus imprisonment.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for non-payment of withholding taxes: directors and shadow directors are personally liable for the unpaid NRWT, civil asset restitution may be ordered, the director may be blacklisted and prohibited from departing New Zealand, and imprisonment may follow where the false certification was made knowingly.
