Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

New Zealand's Companies Act 1993 requires a registered office within New Zealand where legal notices may be served. A virtual office is permissible only if it is a physical street address accepted by the Companies Office and not a bare postal box. However, Inland Revenue and commercial banks reject a mail-forwarding-only address for the digital webtoon publishing industry, because royalty flows, platform agreements, and GST registration require actual management presence and business records. A serviced office or co-working lease in Auckland or Christchurch, with named local directors or an authorized agent, is the market-accepted minimum to avoid adverse tax and banking treatment.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is imposed under the Companies Act 1993 for a New Zealand limited company. Shares may be issued at any subscription price including nil par value, and no escrow or bank account blocking requirement is mandated by law. Fully paid shares can be issued to the founding subscriber immediately upon incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: No fire safety certificate, structural load inspection, or workplace industrial clearance is mandatory for a purely white-collar office-based digital webtoon operation. However, the Company must ensure its leased premises comply with the Building Act 2004 and the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017, and that the landlord holds a current Building Warrant of Fitness if the building is classified for commercial use. Under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, the Company must provide a safe working environment, including adequate egress, fire extinguishers, and electrical safety inspection for all workstation equipment. A simple workplace fire compliance statement should be retained with the property manager.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department of New Zealand, administered via myIR secure online portal at www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity deriving New Zealand-source income or making taxable supplies in New Zealand must apply for an IRD number using the Non-Resident Individual/Company registration form (IR742 or IR596 for companies) submitted to Inland Revenue, with supporting documents including certificate of incorporation, registered office address, director details, and evidence of New Zealand tax nexus. For GST purposes, the same IRD number is used; registration must be completed via myIR before the date on which the entity expects to exceed the GST registration threshold, and retroactive registration may be required from the effective date of taxable activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity deriving New Zealand-source income or making taxable supplies in New Zealand must apply for an IRD number using the Non-Resident Individual/Company registration form (IR742 or IR596 for companies) submitted to Inland Revenue, with supporting documents including certificate of incorporation, registered office address, director details, and evidence of New Zealand tax nexus. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand has no check-the-box entity classification regime. Companies are taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act 2007 as companies. Certain eligible closely-held companies may elect to become qualifying companies with imputation credit accounting, but no hybrid mismatch check-the-box election or look-through taxation election is available for standard foreign-owned limited companies. New Zealand applies hybrid mismatch rules under subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 which may recharacterise certain payments, but these are mandatory anti-avoidance provisions, not elective classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period for domestic suppliers; non-resident suppliers of remote services and digital goods must register from their first dollar of supplies to New Zealand consumers unless the supplier is already GST-registered and the supplies are B2B in certain circumstances

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of exported goods (GST Act 1985 section 11), supplies of services to non-residents who are outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed (section 11A), and supplies deemed to be supplied outside New Zealand. For digital webtoon syndication services, zero-rating is available where the recipient is a non-resident and the services are not performed directly in New Zealand, including the supply of IP licensing, digital content distribution, and online advertising services to foreign platforms or end consumers. Zero-rating also requires that the supply would be a taxable supply but for the zero-rating provision and, for services, the non-resident recipient must not be in New Zealand when the services are received, and the services must not be supplied to a New Zealand resident or performed partly in New Zealand in a way that would make them taxable. Export documentation must demonstrate that the service was supplied to an offshore recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUSDEC or Trade Single Window lodgement)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export of goods (applies only to goods; not applicable to pure digital services)
  • Commercial invoice
  • Sales contract or service agreement with non-resident counterparty
  • Evidence of non-resident status of recipient (e.g., foreign tax identification number, registered business address, or passport evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer records evidencing foreign exchange repatriation
  • GST return worksheet and zero-rating substantiation schedule
  • IP licensing or syndication agreement showing worldwide or offshore distribution rights

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance regime. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and based on the Peppol framework. GST returns are filed monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly via myIR. No API-based digital stamping or pre-clearance of invoices is required. Tax invoices must contain specified data fields (supplier details, recipient details, date, description, GST-exclusive amount, GST amount, and total) and can be issued electronically without fiscal device approval. Buyers and sellers may voluntarily use continuous transaction controls under commercial agreements, but this is not a statutory mandate.

Inland Revenue may conduct GST audits with a four-year assessment period (extended to seven years for evasion). If export zero-rating is claimed without adequate substantiation, the supplier may be reassessed for unpaid GST at 15%, plus use-of-money interest on the unpaid amount. Knowingly preparing or providing false documentation supporting zero-rating may result in shortfall penalties of 40% for gross carelessness, 75% for fraudulent intent (or 150% for evasion), and criminal liability under section 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994. For digital services fraudulently zero-rated, the penalties are assessed on the full consideration, and personal liability may attach to directors and the person charged with GST obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully open capital account under a free-floating exchange rate regime. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions; there is no exchange control legislation, no requirement for central bank approval on inward or outward remittances, and no restriction on the purchase, holding, or transfer of foreign currency. The RBNZ conducts monetary policy through the Official Cash Rate (OCR) and does not defend any parity band or administer capital-flow management measures. Profit remittances and dividend distributions by a New Zealand-resident webtoon publisher are unrestricted at the banking level; the only frictions are (i) non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) on dividends at 30%, generally reduced to 15% under a comprehensive double tax agreement, (ii) the Approved Issuer Levy regime at 2% for certain interest flows, and (iii) the solvency test under section 52 of the Companies Act 1993. Sterilised intervention is not a present policy tool for the RBNZ, so treasury hedging is essential for USD-denominated Apple IAP and Google Play Billing receipts. Inward direct investment is screened only under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 for sensitive land and significant business assets, neither of which is engaged by this digital publishing entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand abolished all export-proceeds surrender and repatriation-timing controls in 1984. There is no statutory deadline for repatriation of digital export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio to NZD. A webtoon publishing company may lawfully retain USD, EUR, JPY, KRW, or any other foreign currency in New Zealand bank accounts or offshore accounts indefinitely. The only compliance obligations are indirect: (i) bank AML/CFT record-keeping requires each foreign-currency receipt to be tagged to a commercial source such as an Apple or Google settlement report, (ii) accounting records must book foreign exchange gains and losses on an accrual basis, and (iii) GST legislation classifies business-to-consumer supplies of digital content to non-residents as zero-rated (0% GST) rather than standard-rated (15% GST), which must be reflected in the reconciliation between treasury inflows and the GST return.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ), Wellington, New Zealand

Airwallex Fit: High

Wise New Zealand and Airwallex operate under New Zealand financial-service-provider registration (Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008), but they are not registered banks under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act and do not provide RBNZ-regulated deposit-taking protection. Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlement proceeds can be routed into a multi-currency virtual account (USD, EUR, SGD) at a fintech EMI and converted to NZD before sweeping to the master bank account; however, the company still requires a resident registered bank account for Inland Revenue GST payment, investor capital injection, and formal trade settlement. The Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 treats EMIs as reporting entities, so the KYC burden is not reduced, and EMIs commonly impose enhanced scrutiny on high-frequency micropayment business models. Recommended architecture: a tier-1 registered bank account as the master treasury account and a single fintech EMI account as an ancillary collections sub-rail for KRW and USD receipts requiring cheaper conversion.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions per income year; contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared and retained.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate standalone Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to those in certain other jurisdictions. However, the general anti-avoidance rule in sections BG 1 and GA 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, together with transfer pricing rules and treaty beneficial-ownership requirements, compels genuine commercial substance, local decision-making, and risk control. A shell or conduit company claiming treaty benefits or artificially shifting digital content income may be recharacterised, denied treaty relief, and reassessed at the full 28% corporate rate plus penalties.
