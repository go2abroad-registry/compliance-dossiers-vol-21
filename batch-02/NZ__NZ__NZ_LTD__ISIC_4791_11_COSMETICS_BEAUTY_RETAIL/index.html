Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered office in New Zealand must be a physical street address where documents may be legally served during business hours; a post office box or a pure commercial mailbox is not permissible. A virtual office may be accepted by the Companies Office only if it provides a genuine physical street address, a dedicated office suite, and a local service agent physically present to accept service. However, Inland Revenue and banking partners for a cosmetics DTC retail business will generally reject a pure virtual office or commercial mailbox because they require a verifiable trading location, accessible records, and operational substance for GST registration, Shopify Payments, and PayPal merchant accounts. A serviced office with a signed lease, reception services, and demonstrable business presence is acceptable.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand company law does not prescribe any minimum issued or paid-up capital for a company limited by shares. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department, New Zealand Government; online portal: myIR (secure.ird.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity, the statutory procedure is to apply to Inland Revenue for a New Zealand tax file number using the non-resident company IRD number application form available from the Inland Revenue website; the application must provide certified copies of the foreign certificate of incorporation, the registered office address, director/beneficial ownership details, and confirmation of any permanent establishment in New Zealand. Once the IRD number is issued, the same number is used as the GST registration number. GST registration must be completed concurrently through myIR if the entity makes or intends to make taxable supplies in New Zealand exceeding NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period, or if registering voluntarily to claim input tax credits. An overseas company that is required to register under the Companies Act 1993 on the New Zealand Companies Register must first obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) and then complete IRD registration through the Companies Office direct data transfer or myIR.

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in any 12-month rolling period for taxable supplies; non-resident suppliers of remote services or low-value goods to New Zealand consumers must register under the offshore supplier registration rules when annual New Zealand consumer supplies exceed NZD 60,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under sections 11 and 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1986, a supply of goods is zero-rated for export where the goods are physically removed from New Zealand and the supplier retains evidence of export within the statutory timeframe; a supply to a non-resident can also be zero-rated where the goods are subsequently exported by the non-resident under the statutory conditions. Qualifying cross-border services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated if the services are performed outside New Zealand or are otherwise covered by the statutory export provisions. For cosmetics online retail, the zero-rating applies only to goods shipped by or on behalf of the supplier to an overseas destination, not to goods delivered in New Zealand.

  • New Zealand Customs Service Export Entry/Export Declaration submitted electronically by the exporter or licensed customs broker
  • Signed commercial invoice and packing list with product description and HS classification for cosmetic products
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing goods departed New Zealand and reached the declared overseas destination
  • Inward foreign exchange credit/remittance advice from a New Zealand bank evidencing repatriation of export proceeds to a New Zealand bank account
  • Non-resident customer evidence and overseas delivery address documentation, including purchase order, customer registration, and tracked shipping confirmation

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, tax authority API clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping is required. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and operates on the PEPPOL e-invoicing network. GST returns and payments are filed electronically via myIR, and the supplier must issue valid GST tax invoices for taxable supplies above the statutory invoice threshold.

Inland Revenue may audit export zero-rating by requiring the full documentation bundle. If the export or foreign exchange repatriation is not substantiated, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 15% GST and the exporter must account for GST with use-of-money interest. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply, including penalties for lack of reasonable care, unacceptable tax position, gross carelessness, abusive tax position, and evasion, with the maximum penalty for evasion up to 150% of the tax shortfall; willful or reckless false documentation can also give rise to criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand is an open economy with no exchange controls. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully convertible on both current account and capital account transactions, and there are no gatekeeper exchange-control approvals, currency surrender requirements, or repatriation restrictions for ordinary trade and investment flows. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not ration foreign currency or impose mandatory buy/sell spreads. A New Zealand limited company may receive shareholder equity, director loans, intercompany advances, and trade receivables in NZD or any foreign currency without central bank permission. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is constrained only by corporate law and tax law: a director-approved solvency test is required for dividend distributions under the Companies Act 1993, and a non-resident dividend withholding tax (NRWT) may apply at the treaty rate, generally 15% unless a Double Tax Agreement reduces it; these are fiscal formalities, not exchange-control restrictions. External debt repayments are likewise free from exchange-control consent. Urgent or unusually high-value capital flows may attract bank enhanced due diligence, anti-money-laundering, and Inland Revenue/CRS reporting, but no economic or administrative convertibility hurdle exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. New Zealand abolished exchange controls in March 1985. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to surrender foreign-currency receipts to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand or to a local dealer. Exporters may retain balances abroad or in NZ registered bank foreign-currency accounts and may convert at freely negotiated commercial rates.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ); no separate foreign-exchange-control administration exists because New Zealand maintains a fully floating and freely convertible currency regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Conditional, medium feasibility for non-custodial payment-clearing flows; low feasibility as a primary company bank or capital-escrow depositary. New Zealand has no separate electronic-money-institution licence; a non-bank fintech cannot accept demand deposits or conduct a deposit-taking business unless it obtains registered-bank status from the Reserve Bank of New Zealand. Offshore digital EMIs such as global payment platforms may lawfully provide payment facilitation, merchant settlement, and remittance services while acting as FSP-registered reporting entities under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, but they are not acceptable sub-custodians of regulatory capital or statutory corporate treasury liquidity. Their solvency, ring-fencing, and oversight structures differ from registered banks, and banks that process their settlements may aggregate consumer and DTC funds in ways that intensify CDD complexity. Therefore, use a Tier-1 local bank for the corporate account, capital escrow, FX hedging, and outbound wire facilities; treat EMIs only as supplemental collection/positivity rails for Shopify/PayPal payouts, subject to a documented risk assessment.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% NRWT on gross dividends paid to non-resident shareholders. Under an applicable double tax agreement, the rate is commonly reduced to 15% for portfolio shareholdings and 5% for substantial shareholdings. Fully imputed dividends do not give non-resident shareholders access to imputation credits.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Country-by-Country reporting and the associated Master File obligation apply where the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds NZD 1.1 billion. A Local File and international dealings disclosure are expected where the New Zealand entity has material cross-border related-party transactions, generally exceeding NZD 10 million in aggregate per transaction category, or where related-party transaction values exceed NZD 5 million and the New Zealand entity has gross income above NZD 100 million.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations code comparable to certain offshore financial centres. Instead, New Zealand applies OECD BEPS-aligned principles: corporate residence is determined by incorporation and management/control, loss continuity requires real shareholder identity, treaty access requires beneficial ownership and economic substance, and the general anti-avoidance rule under section BG1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 can disallow artificial arrangements lacking commercial purpose. An online cosmetics retailer must demonstrate genuine New Zealand operational substance, including local management, staff, inventory decision-making, marketing control, and risk management, to sustain treaty benefits and tax residence outcomes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under sections 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994: imprisonment for up to 5 years for individuals and a fine of up to NZD 50,000; a company is liable to a fine of up to NZD 250,000. Civil shortfall penalties can reach 150% of the evaded tax, plus late payment penalties and use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct the filing of false returns may be prosecuted as principals. A Departure Prohibition Order can be issued to prevent travel outside New Zealand. Directors may also be disqualified under the Companies Act 1993 and held personally liable for tax diverted through fraud or sham arrangements.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The arrangement may be voided as a tax avoidance structure under section BG1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, allowing IRD to reconstruct profits and deny treaty benefits. Penalties of 20% to 100% of the tax shortfall apply for unacceptable tax positions and abusive tax positions. The Registrar of Companies may also strike off a company that operates no genuine commercial activity.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit the entity to operate as a shell can be personally assessed for company tax shortfalls where the corporate form is used as a mere facade. Civil recovery proceedings may be brought under the Criminal Proceeds Recovery Act 2009 to freeze and forfeit assets traceable to contrived arrangements.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue may adjust pricing under sections GC 6 to GC 14 of the Income Tax Act 2007. Shortfall penalties of 40% apply for gross carelessness, increasing to 100% for abusive tax positions, with use-of-money interest on all reassessments. Intentional documentation fraud may trigger criminal tax evasion sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign off non-arm's-length pricing with knowledge of the tax advantage may become jointly and severally liable for the resulting shortfall. Serious conduct can lead to criminal prosecution, asset preservation orders, and travel restrictions while investigations are ongoing.
AML/CFT and Payment-Rail Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, non-compliance attracts fines of up to NZD 2 million for a company and NZD 1 million for an individual. Breach of the Sanctions Act 2020 can lead to imprisonment for up to 7 years and unlimited fines. Payment processors may also terminate merchant facilities without notice.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers of AML/CFT reporting entities can be held personally liable where the breach is attributable to their consent, participation, or neglect. Conviction can result in disqualification from corporate management, financial restitution orders, and restrictions on overseas travel during criminal proceedings.
