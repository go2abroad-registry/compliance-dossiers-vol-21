Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The registered office must be a physical street address in New Zealand; a post office box is statutorily prohibited. A virtual-office service that supplies a real street address and mail forwarding is accepted by the Companies Office for the registered-office slot. However, for the B2B MRO electronic-catalog industry the operational assessment is adverse: New Zealand resident banks operating under Reserve Bank of New Zealand customer due diligence rules will require a verifiable physical place of business (lease, utility bill, or IRD record) and will decline a SWIFT wire or corporate credit-card merchant facility if only a virtual mailbox is offered. Inland Revenue will also challenge the address unless a physical presence is substantiated by tax records.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Taake), operating the myIR secure online portal; company incorporation is administered by the New Zealand Companies Office, and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) is assigned at incorporation.

E-government filing portal

A New Zealand limited company is incorporated with the New Zealand Companies Office and receives an NZBN automatically. The company must then register with the Inland Revenue Department for income tax and Goods and Services Tax (GST) using the myIR portal, obtaining a single IRD number that serves as the national tax identification number. For a foreign non-resident entity without New Zealand incorporation, registration for an IRD number is completed through myIR by submitting an application as a non-resident, including full legal name, country of incorporation, foreign registered address, director and shareholder details for any New Zealand presence, principal business activity, estimated New Zealand taxable supplies, contact details, and bank account information for refunds. A non-resident entity that only supplies remote services to New Zealand consumers and has no other New Zealand taxable activity may be registered for GST only if its taxable supplies exceed the registration threshold. The IRD number is the sole tax identification identifier; there is no separate corporate income tax registration document or entity number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in New Zealand. A qualifying close-held company with five or fewer owners may elect look-through company (LTC) status under the Income Tax Act 2007, causing certain income tax items to be attributed to owners, but the default classification for a New Zealand limited company is entity-level taxation. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act 2007 may apply to cross-border structures and do not provide elective check-the-box treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000; a person must register for GST if the total value of taxable supplies exceeds NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period. Non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand resident consumers are subject to the same NZD 60,000 registration threshold. Registration also may be required for persons intending to carry on a taxable activity and making taxable supplies that exceed the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when the supplier exports the goods, or arranges for their export, under a valid New Zealand Customs export entry, and the goods physically leave New Zealand within 28 days of the supply. The supply must be made to a place outside New Zealand, and contemporaneous evidence of export must be retained. Remote services and other cross-border services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed may also be zero-rated if the services are not directly connected with land or movable property within New Zealand.

  • Customs export entry submitted electronically to the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window/Joint Border Management System
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing the physical movement of goods and the overseas destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the goods and the export sales terms
  • Supplier's GST tax invoice record showing zero-rated treatment
  • Bank SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice showing settlement from the non-resident buyer, together with foreign exchange or currency conversion records where applicable

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime, tax authority pre-clearance of individual invoices, or anti-fraud digital stamping exists under New Zealand GST. E-invoicing may be used voluntarily through the Peppol network via New Zealand's Interoperable e-Invoicing framework, but the Inland Revenue Department does not require invoice-by-invoice clearance. Tax invoices must nevertheless contain the substantive details required by the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and must be retained in accordance with the Tax Administration Act 1994 to substantiate GST treatment during audit.

New Zealand GST law does not impose a fixed foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export zero-rating; however, payment and currency conversion evidence should be retained for at least seven years under the Tax Administration Act 1994 to demonstrate commercial substance. Inland Revenue audits export zero-rating claims against New Zealand Customs Service movement records and the supplier’s contemporaneous documentation. If the export requirement is not substantiated, the supply becomes taxable at 15% GST, and the full GST amount may be assessed and clawed back. Additional consequences include late payment interest, escalating shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 for unreasonable tax positions or evasion, and potential criminal liability for directors and persons involved in knowingly providing false or misleading information to the Inland Revenue Department.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalized and floating currency framework under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989. The New Zealand Dollar is freely convertible for both current account and capital account transactions; there are no surrender requirements on export earnings, no exchange control licensing approvals, and no central bank permission required for dividend repatriation, capital returns, or intercompany loan servicing. Profit remittance is subject only to standard corporate governance solvency tests under the Companies Act 1993 and withholding tax obligations under the Income Tax Act 2007, not to FX rationing or mandatory conversion ratios. The RBNZ does not administratively impose convertibility restrictions on NZD for residents or non-residents. It collects balance of payments statistics through the International Transactions Reporting System and may impose macro-prudential loan-to-value restrictions on residential lending, but such measures do not affect trade or treasury flows. Non-resident-owned entities are fully permitted to hold foreign currency accounts (USD, EUR, AUD, GBP) alongside NZD accounts at registered banks, execute FX forwards with a genuine underlying trade exposure, and re-export funds without a cooling-off period. This convertibility profile places New Zealand firmly in the OECD free-capital-movement category, presenting zero onshore FX convertibility blockers for an MRO e-commerce treasury structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline for surrendering export foreign currency receipts and no mandatory conversion ratio whatsoever. Exporting entities may retain proceeds indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts held with a NZ registered bank and convert at commercial discretion. The only obligation is statistical: banks must report to the RBNZ International Transactions Reporting System any individual overseas transaction equal to or above NZD 200,000 per month, but this reporting is purely data-driven and does not constitute an exchange control or a repatriation timeline.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua); foreign exchange market oversight and prudential regulation sit exclusively with the RBNZ, while the Minister of Finance holds only extraordinary emergency powers under the Public Finance Act 1989 that have never been exercised as currency controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-to-Moderate for operational treasury and Low for statutory capital escrow. New Zealand does not statutorily bar fintech electronic money institutions; the payments sector is regulated under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and the AML/CFT Act 2009, and firms such as Wise (NZ-founded), Airwallex, and Stripe can legally service NZ entities. However, licensed EMIs in New Zealand are generally non-deposit-taking payment facilitators and cannot issue Commercial Letters of Credit, provide overdraft or treasury lines, or be accepted by auditors as the 'bank' for capital verification under the Companies Act. Tier-1 domestic banks also condition trade finance approval on the primary operating account being held onshore with them. Therefore, the blueprint demands a Tier-1 domestic bank as the treasury spine, with EMIs deployed only as secondary disbursement conduits for small-value B2B MRO orders below NZD 5,000 per transaction, and never as the custodian of statutory capital,

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity or shareholder loan funding must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the company's NZD-denominated corporate account at a Tier-1 NZ bank. The sending bank must quote a clear FX purpose code, typically '6F Capital Transfer' or 'share capital injection', and reference the company's NZBN number and the allotment resolution date in field 70. Monthly aggregate inflows above NZD 10,000 are screened against the AML/CFT transaction monitoring profile, and the RBNZ International Transactions Reporting System requires banks to report any single foreign transaction of NZD 200,000 or more. The bank will hold the compliance pack comprising the Companies Office Return of Allotment (filed within 20 working days of share issue), a directors' solvency certificate, and the inward remittance advice, which the external auditor later maps to the share register. No central bank approval is required for the capital inflow itself, but funds must clear from a bank account in the genuine name of the shareholder to avoid third-party-payment rejection.Clearing rails in descending order of use for the MRO catalog business: first, SWIFT MT103 for standard supplier payments and inventory purchases below NZD 50,000 per transaction, quoting purchase order and invoice numbers in field 70; second, Irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600 for overseas supply contracts above NZD 50,000 or where the supplier demands a bank payment undertaking, issued with 60-120 day sight tenors aligned to inventory turnover; third, Documentary Collections on Documents against Payment or Documents against Acceptance terms for established repeat suppliers in APAC and Oceania, lowering cost while preserving shipping document inspection rights. E-commerce card acquiring settlements arrive via acquiring rails (Visa/Mastercard batch settlement) routed through the BNZ or ANZ payment gateway and are consolidated daily into the NZD operating pool; all outbound USD disbursements are sequenced against manual FX conversion only when the aggregate order pipeline justifies a forward hedge, avoiding spot-conversion drift on thin MRO margins.Onshore NZD/USD outright FX forwards with maturities up to 12 months executed under a master dealing agreement, requiring documented underlying import invoices or signed supplier purchase ordersNZD/USD cross-currency swaps for translating large USD-denominated trade payables or hedging the balance sheet exposure of the company's foreign currency accountsZero-cost participating forwards and range-forward collars for converting MRO export and domestic online revenue while preserving favorable NZD movement, subject to the bank's approved unsecured FX credit limitOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only where the offshore counterparty can evidence a genuine underlying trade exposure, but because the NZD is fully deliverable onshore, NDF usage is structurally rare and discouraged in favor of onshore deliverable forwards

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 5,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions (section 78C Tax Administration Act 1994) triggers mandatory transfer pricing documentation, including Master File and Local File content where applicable under OECD BEPS Action 13.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation, but section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 voids arrangements lacking commercial reality. For DTA relief, the NZ company must demonstrate substantive New Zealand operations: physically managed board functions, active commercial decision-making, full-time qualified staff, real premises, and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in respect of MRO catalogue e-commerce. An offshore shell claiming NZ residence or treaty benefits without substance will be denied treaty relief and can be recharacterised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty up to 150% of additional tax under section 141A of the Tax Administration Act 1994; criminal liability under section 143A for tax evasion with fines up to NZD 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the fraudulent return are personally and jointly liable under section 28C of the Tax Administration Act 1994 for the tax shortfall, penalties and interest. Inland Revenue may apply for a Departure Prohibition Order, banning the director from leaving New Zealand while tax remains outstanding; the Court may order civil asset restitution, and directors face criminal imprisonment for fraud.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment under section GC 13 of the Income Tax Act 2007, plus shortfall penalties under section 141A for taking an unacceptable tax position: 20% of the underpaid tax for a standard position, up to 40% if tax avoidance was the dominant purpose, and 100% for abusive positions. The IRD may also impose use-of-money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the transfer pricing scheme without arm's-length evidence face personal recourse as 'involved persons' under section 28C TAA, making them jointly liable for the company's unpaid tax and penalties. If the pricing arrangement renders the company insolvent or deliberately strips NZ assets, directors may be liable for reckless trading under section 135 Companies Act 1993, with civil restitution orders, travel restriction via Departmental Prohibition Order, and disqualification from managing companies.
Shell Company / Deep Economic Substance and AML Conduit Risk

Statutory sanction: The arrangement is void for tax avoidance under section BG 1 Income Tax Act 2007; DTA relief is denied; the Registrar of Companies may strike the company off under section 318 Companies Act 1993; non-compliance with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 may result in civil penalty orders and criminal liability for registered financial institutions processing the company's credit-card and SWIFT flows.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the company is a façade and directors ignore commercial substance; directors become personally liable for unpaid tax, penalties and restored assets, may be disqualified under the Companies Act, placed on a travel-ban/blacklist pending tax recovery, and prosecuted criminally for intentionally using the shell to defeat tax creditors or conceal beneficial ownership.
