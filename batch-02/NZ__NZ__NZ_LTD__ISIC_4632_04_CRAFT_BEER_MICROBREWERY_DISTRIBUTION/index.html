Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The Companies Act 1993 requires a registered office in New Zealand with a physical street address; a post office box is not sufficient. A virtual office may satisfy the Companies Office only if it is a genuine street-address service that accepts legal documents in person. However, for ISIC 4632 craft beer cold-chain keg wholesale and distribution, the Inland Revenue Department, New Zealand Customs, the district council licensing authority, and most commercial banks will reject a mailbox-only arrangement as the principal place of business. The industry requires bonded cold storage, a food control plan, alcohol licensing, excise records, and on-site stock access. A virtual office is therefore treated as impermissible for operational licensing and banking substance unless supported by a separate physical cold-chain warehouse and clearly disclosed registered premises.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue New Zealand, IRD) - Te Tari Taake; online portal myIR (myIR.ird.govt.nz); company and NZBN registration through the New Zealand Companies Office and the New Zealand Business Number Register.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the NZ Limited company with the New Zealand Companies Office through the online company register, obtaining a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). Step 2: Complete the joint tax registration application in the Companies Office transaction (or file IRD entity registration via myIR for an already-registered foreign entity), which creates the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) number as the unique taxpayer identifier. Step 3: Secure the IRD number for the company, the equivalent of a TIN, and activate a myIR account. Step 4: Apply for Goods and Services Tax (GST) registration in myIR using the 'Register for GST' service, indicating whether registration is compulsory or voluntary; GST registration is recorded under the same IRD number, and no separate GST tax identification number is issued. Step 5: Nominate reporting frequency (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly) and filing method; no additional domestic tax registration is required for a New Zealand-registered company. Non-resident directors and shareholders are permitted, but the entity must maintain a registered office and an agent address in New Zealand.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. New Zealand has no check-the-box or entity classification election. A New Zealand limited company is automatically taxed at the entity level at the corporate income tax rate; distributions to shareholders are non-deductible but carry imputation credits under the New Zealand dividend imputation system. Hybrid mismatch rules in subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 apply to cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 - compulsory registration once taxable supplies made in New Zealand exceed or are expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period; voluntary registration is available

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) and related provisions of the GST Act 1985 where the supplier exports the goods from New Zealand under the contract of sale and physical export occurs. For craft beer distribution, the NZ vendor must export the kegs or cans directly to the foreign buyer or cause the goods to be delivered to a carrier for export; title and risk must pass to the non-resident and the goods must actually leave New Zealand. Services may be zero-rated under section 11A where the services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, and the supply is not materially connected to New Zealand land or goods. Export documentation must prove the goods left New Zealand; otherwise GST must be returned at 15%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Export Entry in the New Zealand Trade Single Window, accepted by the New Zealand Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and signed purchase order or contract of sale with the foreign customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange settlement/credit advice evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in foreign currency or NZD

No real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory prior digital stamping and no government pre-clearance API. New Zealand operates a voluntary e-invoicing framework based on the Peppol network. GST-registered suppliers must issue and retain valid tax invoices containing the statutory particulars in section 24 of the GST Act 1985. GST returns and payments are filed electronically through myIR; no additional anti-fraud electronic authorisation is required before issuing invoices.

Inland Revenue requires exporters to retain the export evidence bundle for at least seven years. If export zero-rating is claimed without sufficient documentation or is later found to be non-qualifying, the supply is recharacterised as standard-rated, GST output tax of 15% is assessed, any previously refunded GST is clawed back, and shortfall penalties and use-of-money interest are imposed under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Penalties range from 20% of the tax shortfall for an unacceptable tax position or gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion. Late payment penalties (1% on day 1, further 4% after 7 days) and daily interest also apply. Fraudulent export claims may be prosecuted criminally and company directors may be pursued as persons concerned in the management of a company under the company and garnishee enforcement powers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar is fully convertible under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. No exchange controls restrict current account or capital account transactions. Profit remittance by dividend is permitted, subject to non-resident withholding tax and applicable tax declaration requirements. Banks apply standard internal due diligence for large or unusual inward and outward fund flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirement exists for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency earnings in offshore or domestic accounts, convert to New Zealand dollars at commercial rates, or use the funds directly for trade settlement. No mandatory timeline or conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (central bank and monetary authority; responsible for prudential supervision and registered bank authorization)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Offshore digital EMIs cannot independently provide local New Zealand dollar clearing or direct access to the local payment system unless they are registered on the New Zealand AML/CFT regime and hold the required financial service provider registration or operate through a licensed bank-as-a-service partner. They remain viable mainly for multi-currency collections, virtual IBAN-style routing, and ancillary payment cards, but not as a primary treasury settlement rail.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory dividend withholding tax on non-resident dividend payments is 30% (NRWT), unless an exemption applies. Fully imputed dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder may be subject to NRWT at 15% where a lower treaty rate applies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The IR4 is due 12 months after the balance date for non-standard balance dates; for a standard 31 March balance date, the return is due no later than 7 July of the following tax year if filed via a tax agent with an extension of time, or 31 March (following year) in certain agent extension regimes. The underlying tax payment is due at the terminal tax date, typically 7 February after the balance date (standard balance date), or, with a tax agent extension, 7 April of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No New Zealand-dollar-specific statutory threshold requiring automatic Local File or Master File preparation; however, Inland Revenue may request transfer pricing documentation (contemporaneous records) where the aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed NZ$1,000,000 in a tax year. A taxpayer must also prepare a transfer pricing disclosure (TPD) form in myIR as part of the annual return when these transactions exceed specified thresholds (generally NZ$1 million for loans, NZ$1 million for goods/services, and NZ$1 million for intangibles).

Economic substance scope: New Zealand applies a general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and Inland Revenue's Economic Substance guidelines. For manufacturing/distribution structures, the company must demonstrate real local business activity including procurement, logistics, inventory risk, contractual negotiation, and decision-making performed by local employees. Distributors earning thin margins without operational substance are vulnerable to re-characterisation and adjustment under the GAAR. The OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) rules in New Zealand apply to large multinational groups, and the OECD's Multilateral Instrument (MLI) is in effect for treaty abuse provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under section 143A of the Tax Administration Act 1994: a fine not exceeding NZ$150,000 for the company; civil penalties under section 141 (tax shortfall) of up to 150% of the tax shortfall; additional evasion penalties and repayment of all primary tax, plus UOMI.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the company's tax shortfall under the Tax Administration Act 1994 if they knowingly participate in evasion; a court may issue a travel ban under insolvency or tax enforcement proceedings, and imprisonment for up to 5 years is available for evasion offences.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation of the structure under the GAAR (ITA 2007 subpart GB); disallowance of payments; imposition of a 100% shortfall penalty; Inland Revenue may strike off the company under the Companies Act; deregistration under the Financial Markets Authority for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell company remain liable for unpaid tax where the company is insolvent and they have breached director duties under the Companies Act 1993; travel restrictions can be imposed for failure to satisfy tax judgment debts; persistent breach of the GAAR may result in disqualification as a director.
Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009: a first breach can attract a civil penalty of up to NZ$2 million for a company and NZ$300,000 for an individual; serious or repeat failure can result in a fine of up to NZ$5 million; an additional fine may be imposed by the High Court, plus injunctions and industry sector prohibitions.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines up to NZ$350,000 per contravention; a director who knowingly participates in money laundering faces a maximum term of imprisonment of 7 years under section 311 of the Crimes Act 1961; travel bans may be granted by court order where a director is a flight risk.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: If a transfer pricing adjustment is made, Inland Revenue may impose a tax shortfall penalty of 20% of the shortfall plus UOMI where no reasonable attempt to comply was made; in egregious cases a 50% penalty is possible. There is also a separate failure to furnish disclosure penalty of up to NZ$20,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documents or knowingly cause an incorrect return may be subject to director liability for tax under the Tax Administration Act; the Companies Act allows recovery of the loss directly from the director; a criminal prosecution for assisting with tax evasion can lead to imprisonment.
Dividend Repatriation Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: The full amount of NRWT is assessed against the company as the payer under the Income Tax Act 2007; non-payment attracts a 100% evasion shortfall penalty, UOMI, and a late remittance penalty of 5% of the unpaid amount; failure to remit can lead to criminal prosecution with a fine of up to NZ$50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid NRWT and penalties where the company has knowingly failed to withhold; the Inland Revenue can issue a certificate of debt recovery and pursue director assets; travel bans can be imposed for significant outstanding tax debt while a Court conviction for evasion may include a term of imprisonment.
