Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. There is no minimum paid-up capital requirement for a New Zealand company. Subscribers may validly pay share consideration in cash or in kind as the board determines; there is no statutory blocked escrow account requirement. The issued capital must be accurately described in the share register and must be paid or contracted for under sections 37-41 of the Companies Act 1993, and no share may be issued at a discount except as law permits.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and occupancy approval are mandatory. The premises must comply with the Building Act 2004, obtain a Code Compliance Certificate or equivalent occupancy permission for public retail use, and satisfy the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017 requirements for means of escape, fire alarms, extinguishers, and evacuation procedures where applicable. In addition, the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 requires a documented workplace safety policy, hazard register, staff induction, electrical certificates of compliance, and food hygiene training for employees involved in handling unpackaged or imported food products.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand; online electronic portal: myIR (myir.ird.govt.nz); customs-related tax clearance is separately administered by the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate the entity with the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993; the Companies Register automatically issues a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). (2) Apply through myIR to the Inland Revenue Department for an IRD number, which is the single statutory tax identification number for company income tax, GST, PAYE, RWT, and other taxes. (3) For a foreign non-resident entity that is not locally incorporated, register as an overseas company under Part 18 of the Companies Act 1993 or establish a taxable presence, then complete the IRD number application via myIR, furnishing director/shareholder identity documents, overseas registered address, and appointing a New Zealand tax agent where required. (4) Register for GST in myIR if the entity makes, or intends to make, taxable supplies exceeding NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period; non-residents may voluntarily register for GST even without a local fixed establishment and must provide overseas business details. (5) Obtain a New Zealand Customs client code through the Trade Single Window for import and export activities. (6) Register through myIR for PAYE and any applicable employer obligations if the supermarket hires NZ staff. The IRD number remains linked to the company’s permanent tax account; the GST registration number must be quoted on tax invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in New Zealand. A close-held NZ company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) status under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, moving current taxation from the company to eligible shareholders. A wholly owned group may elect consolidated group treatment under the Income Tax Act 2007, and GST registration can be combined in a GST group under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Hybrid mismatch rules in Subpart FH of the Income Tax Act 2007 operate as mandatory anti-avoidance overrides rather than taxpayer elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies) in any rolling 12-month period; a non-resident supplier of remote/digital services or low-value imported goods to New Zealand consumers must register once those supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in 12 months; voluntary registration is available below threshold; GST on imported goods is collected at the border by the New Zealand Customs Service regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under sections 11 and 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, goods supplied for export are zero-rated when the supplier physically exports the goods from New Zealand within 28 days after the time of supply and retains contemporaneous evidence of that export. Services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed may also be zero-rated, subject to exceptions for services relating to land in New Zealand or goods physically in New Zealand; remote/digital services supplied to unregistered New Zealand consumers are standard-rated at 15%. For the supermarket grocery activity, zero-rating is available only on identifiable lots of condiments and vegetables that are consigned abroad and physically leave New Zealand; domestic sales in the NZ store remain 15% GST taxable.

  • NZ Customs export declaration (Export Entry) submitted through the Trade Single Window
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the NZ exporter and the overseas consignee
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with HS codes, quantities, and country-of-origin/food-safety details
  • Transport and departure evidence (container tracking, port/airport departure reports, or NZ Customs movement records) proving goods left NZ within the 28-day zero-rating window
  • Bank payment instruction, SWIFT MT103, or foreign-exchange settlement advice supporting the commercial reality of the export sale

New Zealand does not mandate electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API approval, or anti-fraud digital stamping. GST returns are filed electronically in myIR using the GST101 return; tax invoices must satisfy the content requirements in section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. Use of the New Zealand Peppol e-invoicing network is voluntary; statutory acceptance of an invoice is determined by its GST content and the taxpayer’s records, not by a government clearance platform.

New Zealand has no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds; the value 0 is used deliberately to mean not applicable. Failure to substantiate zero-rating causes the supply to be reassessed at 15% GST, with use-of-money interest from the original GST return due date. The Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes shortfall penalties of 20% for lack of reasonable care, 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion; criminal liability may also arise for knowingly false GST or export statements. False export declarations may attract penalties under the Customs and Excise Act 2018. The IRD may amend assessments for up to four years after the return, with no time limit for fraudulent or deceptive conduct, and requires GST records to be kept for seven years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates under a freely floating exchange rate regime with no statutory foreign exchange controls. Both current account and capital account transactions are fully convertible, reflecting the country's commitment to open capital markets and its adoption of IMF Article VIII obligations. The New Zealand dollar is actively traded globally and is considered a hard currency for commercial purposes. There are no requirements to seek central bank approval for inward or outward remittances, nor are there any surrender requirements on export proceeds. Profit remittances, including dividends and branch repatriation, can be made freely provided that the underlying tax obligations (such as non-resident withholding tax) are satisfied and the bank's anti-money laundering (AML) due diligence files support the transaction. In practice, banks may impose internal documentation thresholds for large or unusual flows, and the Overseas Investment Act 2005 may require regulatory consent if an inbound investment involves certain sensitive land, fishing quota, or significant business assets exceeding statutory thresholds. However, a standard grocery retail entity with modest capitalization faces no convertibility hurdle. The primary regulatory friction lies not in FX approval but in banking onboarding, statutory director verification, and the need to evidence the economic substance and source of funds to the bank's compliance committee.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender rules or mandatory conversion timelines for export proceeds in New Zealand. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in offshore or onshore accounts indefinitely, or convert them to New Zealand dollars at their own discretion. Banks record transaction purpose codes for statistical reporting to the Reserve Bank of New Zealand, but there is no requirement to convert any percentage of export earnings within a specified period. The only practical obligation is that the underlying trade documentation, such as invoices and shipping records, is retained to satisfy Goods and Services Tax (GST) and income tax requirements.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) offering offshore digital banking services are of Low-to-Medium viability for this entity. New Zealand does not have a dedicated EMI licensing regime analogous to the UK's FCA or EU's PSD2 regime. Payment service providers must register under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and comply with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, but only registered banks are authorized to accept deposits and provide full clearing and transactional banking services. Offshore EMIs that rely on partner bank rails may service the company for card payments or receipt collection, but they are legally barred from providing local account-to-account clearing in NZD and are not recognized as authorized deposit-taking institutions for the purposes of the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's prudential framework. Furthermore, local counterparties such as landlords, suppliers, and tax authorities generally expect settlement via the domestic payments system, which requires a bank account with a New Zealand registered bank. Therefore, an offshore fintech EMI cannot serve as the primary treasury rail.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Under Companies Act 1993 section 195 and the Financial Reporting Act 2013, a New Zealand limited company must appoint an independent certified auditor for its financial statements unless all shareholders have unanimously resolved to waive the audit and the company is not an FMC reporting entity. A statutory independent audit is mandatorily required for: (1) any FMC reporting entity under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, whether because it issues securities to the public, is listed, or is otherwise classified as an FMC reporting entity; (2) any large company or group exceeding the statutory size test of total assets of NZ$30 million or more at balance date, or total annual revenue of NZ$60 million or more; (3) any company whose constitution, court order, or a shareholder requisition holding at least 5% of the voting rights requires an audit; and (4) any subsidiary whose parent is subject to an audit or group financial statement filing requirement.
