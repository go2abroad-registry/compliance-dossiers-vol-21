Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under the Companies Act 1993, the registered office must be a physical New Zealand address and cannot be a post office box. A virtual office with a genuine serviced street address may be sufficient to satisfy the registry at incorporation, but the Inland Revenue Department and New Zealand banks performing AML/CFT enhanced due diligence for copper cathode trading will likely reject a mere commercial mailbox because there is no verifiable management presence, operational control, board minutes, books and records, or physical trading infrastructure. For a wholesale metals trader using LME warrant transfers and documentary letters of credit, the minimum acceptable substance is a serviced office in New Zealand with locally engaged management or a registered agent, together with a New Zealand bank account, local records, trade contracts, warehouse receipts, and accounting systems. A pure virtual office is therefore not permissible for this industry for banking and tax substance purposes.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Wellington, New Zealand; electronic portal: myIR (www.ird.govt.nz/myir); company registration portal: New Zealand Companies Office (www.companiesoffice.govt.nz)

E-government filing portal

New Zealand uses the IRD number as the single taxpayer identification number and, for GST-registered persons, the GST registration number. A New Zealand incorporated limited company automatically receives an IRD number and New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) upon incorporation through the Companies Office. A foreign non-resident entity without a New Zealand incorporated vehicle must apply to the Inland Revenue Department for an IRD number using the prescribed non-individual registration process, submitting proof of legal existence, certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, confirmation of foreign tax residence, and any required New Zealand tax agent appointment. Once the IRD number is issued, the non-resident entity must register separately for GST if its annual taxable supplies are NZD 60,000 or more, if it intends to claim input tax credits, or if it is required to register because of taxable activity carried on in New Zealand. All registrations and subsequent returns are administered through myIR.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, supplies of goods physically exported from New Zealand may be zero-rated if the supplier exports the goods within 28 days of the time of supply and retains appropriate documentary evidence. For qualifying services supplied to non-residents outside New Zealand, including qualifying offshore digital services, the service may be zero-rated if the recipient is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are not directly connected with New Zealand land or tangible movable property. For copper cathode wholesale arbitrage, physical export from New Zealand must be substantiated by customs and shipping records; where the transaction is performed through London Metal Exchange warrant transfers without goods entering or leaving New Zealand, the supply is generally outside New Zealand GST rather than a zero-rated New Zealand supply.

  • New Zealand Customs Service approved export entry (submitted through the Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bank SWIFT MT103 or foreign exchange remittance confirmation evidencing settlement and repatriation of export proceeds

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping regime applies in New Zealand. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates on the PEPPOL network through approved NZ e-invoicing providers. GST returns are filed electronically via the IRD myIR portal on the GST101 return; no government pre-clearance API stamping is required.

If export zero-rating is claimed without sufficient documentation or with inaccurate customs/financial evidence, the Inland Revenue Department may reverse the zero-rating and assess the supplier for 15% GST on the full consideration, together with use-of-money interest from the original due date. Tax Administration Act 1994 shortfall penalties also apply, ranging from 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and up to 100% for fraudulent or abusive tax positions. False export declarations may additionally attract penalties under the Customs and Excise Act 2018.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a floating exchange rate regime with no general foreign exchange controls. The New Zealand Dollar (NZD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions, and there are no restrictions on holding or transacting in foreign currencies within or outside New Zealand. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not impose surrender requirements on export proceeds, nor does it mandate any conversion ratio. Profit remittance for non-resident shareholders is freely permitted, subject only to standard tax withholding obligations under New Zealand domestic tax law and any applicable double tax agreement. Non-resident companies may open bank accounts and inject capital in any major currency, and funds can be repatriated without central bank approval. However, all banks must comply with the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, which requires robust customer due diligence and source-of-funds verification. For a copper cathode trading entity, banks will scrutinize the underlying commodity trade documentation to ensure legitimate economic substance. There are no restrictions on import settlement, export invoicing, or the use of hedging products such as forwards, options, and swaps. Overall, New Zealand ranks as one of the most open and convertible jurisdictions for cross-border treasury activities, with the central bank focusing on monetary policy stability rather than ex-ante transactional approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts in offshore or onshore foreign currency accounts and may convert to NZD at any time. Reporting obligations are limited to standard tax and AML record-keeping requirements.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not considered viable as primary banking partners for a corporate entity engaged in high-value commodity trading in New Zealand. To provide New Zealand domestic banking services, an entity must hold a registered bank license under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989 or operate under the Non-Bank Deposit Taker regime, which most international fintech EMIs do not obtain. Offshore EMIs such as Wise or specialist FX platforms may offer settlement in certain currencies through licensed partners, but they are not recognized as deposit-taking institutions in New Zealand and cannot provide locally denominated escrow accounts, trade finance facilities, or documentary letter of credit services. Their utility is limited to auxiliary payment execution for low-value transactions; the primary treasury liquidity must be held with a tier-1 domestic bank or a registered New Zealand branch of an internationally systemic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the New Zealand copper trading entity should be routed exclusively through the principal tier-1 bank account using SWIFT MT103 for wire transfers, with the remitting party declaring the purpose code appropriate to the underlying commercial transaction (e.g., import payment for goods, trade finance settlement, or equity injection). For capital injection as equity, the remittances must reference the Companies Office issued share allotment resolution, and the local bank will likely require copies of the share allotment and a statement of capital contribution from the directors to satisfy evidence of capital. For trade-related advances, banks will verify the LME warrant reference, the supplier pro-forma invoice, and the documentary letter of credit terms. All inbound funds are subject to standard New Zealand AML verification, and amounts exceeding NZD 10,000 require full beneficial ownership disclosure. Banks may also require a New Zealand tax number (IRD) and a completed tax residency declaration for the entity and its shareholders.Outbound trade settlements for purchases of copper cathodes should be executed via documentary letter of credit (LC) issued by the local New Zealand bank through a SWIFT MT700 to the supplier's advising bank, especially for high-value shipments. Alternatively, direct settlement via SWIFT MT103 is permissible if the supplier is an established premium-grade counterparty with a strong track record and full shipping documents are provided before settlement. For lower-risk intermediate payment milestones, documentary collections (CAD/DP/DA) may be used, with the necessary customs and inspection documents consigned through the presenting bank. Copper cathode purchases under LME warrant terms should require the delivery of complete warrant chain and title transfer documents to the negotiating bank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million aggregate cross-border related-party transactions is the threshold for IRD expectation of contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation; lower thresholds apply to related-party debt and cross-border services, and country-by-country reporting applies where the multinational group has consolidated revenue above NZD 750 million.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations filing regime, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in section BG 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and the statutory purpose test will invalidate arrangements lacking commercial substance. For DTA and CFC/FIF purposes, the core income-generating activities, including LME contract negotiation, risk management, storage decision-making, delivery logistics and treasury functions, must be performed in New Zealand by the NZ entity's own personnel and offices. A bare-brass-plate entity holding no real function in the copper cathode trade will not be recognised and may be pierced.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / false tax declaration

Statutory sanction: Section 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994 (criminal evasion) imposes a fine of up to NZD 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; civil shortfall penalties up to 150% (evasion) or 100% (gross carelessness) apply under section 141, plus use of money interest.

Director personal liability: A director who directs or participates in the evasion can be personally liable for the unpaid tax and shortfall penalties; the corporate veil may be pierced; assets may be restrained; a departure prevention order (travel ban) can be issued; and the director may face imprisonment up to 5 years and disqualification from managing companies.
Transfer pricing manipulation / intercompany sham

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment is made under Subpart GC of the Income Tax Act 2007; shortfall penalties apply under section 141EB; thin capitalisation disallows interest on excess non-resident debt; ancillary and tertiary adjustments may also be made to the related party.

Director personal liability: If the transaction is a sham or is orchestrated solely to defeat tax, the corporate veil can be pierced and directors are jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax shortfall; directors may face Crown bankruptcy proceedings, registration of the IRD certificate as a judgment debt, a ban on leaving New Zealand, and criminal prosecution leading to imprisonment up to 5 years.
NRWT / DTA abuse and lack of economic substance (shell company)

Statutory sanction: IRD will assess NRWT at 30% on dividends and 15% on interest/royalties on the gross amount, deny DTA relief under the beneficial ownership test, and impose shortfall penalties of 100%-150% plus use of money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to withhold and account for NRWT are treated as statutory trustees of the withheld amount; they face personal civil recovery of the gross WHT, personal liability as a judgment debt, Departure Prohibition Orders/blacklisting, and criminal liability under section 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994 with imprisonment up to 5 years.
