Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Wellington, New Zealand; online portal myIR at myir.ird.govt.nz. Export declarations are filed with the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing operations in New Zealand must first obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) via the New Zealand Companies Office, filing as an overseas company under the Companies Act 1993 if it is not locally incorporated. Thereafter, the entity must apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number, the single statutory taxpayer identifier, using Form IR596 for non-resident company registration, or through myIR using a registered New Zealand tax agent. If taxable supplies are expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in 12 months, the entity must also submit Form IR356 to register for GST. Required documentation includes the foreign Certificate of Incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, foreign tax resident certificate, a New Zealand registered office or agent address, and the identities of directors and ultimate beneficial owners. IRD numbers are normally issued within 5 to 15 working days; GST registration takes effect from the date of application or the date the threshold is first exceeded.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand does not provide a general purpose check-the-box election. Statutory elections include: (i) Look-Through Company (LTC) status under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, available on Form IR879 to close companies satisfying the five-owner and shareholder residency tests; (ii) consolidated group treatment for qualifying wholly owned New Zealand-resident companies; and (iii) tax loss grouping and subvention payment elections. Hybrid and branch mismatch rules in Subpart FM of the Income Tax Act 2007 are mandatory and not subject to taxpayer election; affected entities must file disclosure schedules and compute conforming adjustments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any trailing 12-month period; voluntary registration is available below the threshold. Non-resident suppliers of remote services or low-value goods to New Zealand consumers must register under the offshore GST rules once the NZD 60,000 threshold is met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from New Zealand qualify for the 0% GST rate when the supplier exports them within 28 days after the time of supply (or any extended period permitted by the Commissioner), retains conclusive evidence of physical export, and the goods are removed from New Zealand. Services are zero-rated under Section 11A of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supplier supplies them to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the services are not supplied directly in connection with New Zealand land or moveable property and are not received by a New Zealand GST-registered person. For bulk petrochemical polymers sold to overseas resellers, the zero-rating claim rests on the shipment terms, the carrier-issued bill of lading, the Customs export entry, and payment in foreign currency from the non-resident buyer.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government server pre-approval of invoices. E-invoicing is voluntary and follows the Peppol interoperability framework. GST-registered persons are required to file GST returns electronically through myIR or IRD-certified tax software; Inland Revenue publishes APIs and a machine-to-machine gateway for accredited software providers, but no transaction-level continuous control or prior clearance regime applies.

Inland Revenue may reassess any incorrectly zero-rated supply, recover the resulting GST shortfall, and impose use-of-money interest from the original return due date. Penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 escalate from 20% for lack of reasonable care to 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion; fraudulent refund arrangements may also trigger criminal prosecution with imprisonment. The standard assessment amendment window is four years, extended to eight years for non-disclosure or fraud, with no time limit where returns are fraudulent or evasive. Export records must be retained for seven years, and retrospective clawback remains available whenever documentary substantiation is falsified or lacking.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully floating and fully convertible currency regime. Both current account transactions (goods, services, dividends, royalties, interest) and capital account transactions (equity injections, shareholder loans, intercompany advances, and liquidation proceeds) are freely convertible into and out of NZD for both residents and non-residents. There are no statutory exchange control permits, no capital outflow restrictions, no imposition of mandatory conversion on offshore receipts, and no prior RBNZ approval for profit remittance or dividend repatriation. The practical hurdles are limited to: (i) bank-level screening under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 and the associated AML/CFT Codes; (ii) tax compliance requirements, including non-resident withholding tax on dividends and interest at 15% (or 2% approved issuer levy on interest where the lender is eligible); and (iii) New Zealand sanctions and counter-terrorism financing screening administered by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade. None of these hurdles operate as a substantive convertibility or remittance barrier. A New Zealand company may freely retain, convert, and pay abroad any NZD or foreign currency balances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. New Zealand does not impose any requirement on exporters to surrender, repatriate, or convert export proceeds. There is no statutory timeline for receivables collection, no mandated percentage to be converted into NZD, and no obligation to maintain balances in New Zealand. An exporter of bulk petrochemical polymers may lawfully hold export receipts offshore in any currency, operate foreign currency accounts at licensed New Zealand banks, or direct payments to third-party suppliers through correspondent banks. The only compliance obligations are of a documentary and tax nature, namely accurate record-keeping for income tax and GST purposes, including accrual recognition upon invoices and bills of lading, and bank-level verification of the underlying trade transaction under the AML/CFT framework.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua). RBNZ is the prudential regulator and licensing authority for all New Zealand registered banks and non-bank deposit takers. There is no separate foreign exchange control agency; foreign exchange policy is liberalised and administered through the general banking and payments system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Limited ancillary role only. Offshore digital EMI and neobank providers such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not regulated as registered banks by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. They cannot lawfully accept escrow deposits from the public, issue documentary letters of credit, or provide a full corporate clearing relationship in New Zealand. Offshore EMI platforms may be used as an auxiliary collection or payment conduit in certain low-risk jurisdictions, provided they are registered as financial service providers under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and comply with the AML/CFT Act 2009. However, the primary treasury architecture for a bulk petrochemical trading entity must rely on a Tier-1 New Zealand bank for capital escrow, SWIFT clearing, trade finance, and treasury risk management. Feasibility rate: Medium as a settlement overlay; Not Acceptable as sole banking rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection is executed by SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or shareholder bank to the NZD or USD account of the New Zealand entity held at the Tier-1 New Zealand bank. The payment narrative must align with the letter of undertaking and contain references such as 'Equity Contribution under Share Subscription Agreement' or 'Intercompany Loan Agreement dated ...'. Prior to crediting, the bank will verify that the New Zealand company has an Inland Revenue number and a valid GST registration. For an equity injection, the company must pass a board resolution, allot shares in accordance with the Companies Act 1993, and file the notice of allotment with the Companies Office. For an intercompany loan, a written loan agreement with arm's-length commercial terms, including at least a market-rate interest component to satisfy transfer-pricing rules, must be executed. No RBNZ approval or exchange control permit is required for the inward transfer. If the ultimate investor is a non-resident holding more than 25% of the shares, the Overseas Investment Act 2005 must be assessed: ordinary trading companies with business value below NZ$100 million (or NZ$200 million for Australian non-government investors) do not require consent from the Overseas Investment Office. The funds are fully usable immediately upon settlement, and the bank will convert USD to NZD at the prevailing wholesale rate if an NZD account is used.Two principal clearing rails will be used. First, SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfers for open-account settlements, intercompany payments, freight, customs, and payment of insurance and inspection fees. Second, an Irrevocable Documentary Letter of Credit (LC) issued by the New Zealand Tier-1 bank as the issuing bank in favour of the overseas polymer producer or first-tier supplier. The LC will be issued via SWIFT MT700 and will be subject to the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600), with the settlement terms typically sight or up to 90 days after presentation of compliant documents. The required document set under the LC will include the signed commercial invoice, full set of clean on-board bills of lading marked freight prepaid, packing list, certificate of quality and quantity, certificate of origin, and an insurance policy where the sale is CIF. In addition, the company may utilise bank-to-bank reimbursement under URR 725 for the drawing of proceeds. All outbound messages must include the relevant HS code for petrochemical polymers (HS 3901 for polyethylene, HS 3902 for polypropylene) and a purpose code covering the trade in goods.
