Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The Companies Act 1993 requires a physical registered office in New Zealand and a separate address for service. A virtual office with a real street address, staffed reception, and a signed occupancy agreement will likely be accepted by the Companies Office and is provisionally permissible. However, Inland Revenue requires a physical business address and will reject a bare commercial mailbox or unmanned servo-office for a virtual-concert company because digital-ticket tokenization creates a measurable tax presence. New Zealand trading banks and high-risk payment processors will reject an entity with only a virtual office unless the business also maintains an independent bookkeeper, a formal service-of-process agent, and evidence of substance such as lease of workspace or contractual regular occupancy. Accordingly, a serviced office arrangement with a staffing agreement is recommended, while a pure virtual mailbox is not permissible for this industry.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue), New Zealand; online portal is myIR at https://www.ird.govt.nz, with New Zealand company incorporation and NZBN issuance via the New Zealand Companies Office at https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand Limited Company, the IRD number (which functions as the combined income tax/GST/VAT identification number) is obtained at incorporation through the Companies Office for locally incorporated companies. For a foreign non-resident company establishing a tax presence in New Zealand, the non-resident entity must apply for an IRD number using Inland Revenue's non-resident company tax registration form (IR596), providing the overseas certificate of incorporation, registered office details, director and shareholder particulars, NZ agent or care-of address, business activity details including ISIC 7110 virtual performance hosting, and expected New Zealand GST supply figures. The same IRD number is then used for GST registration, which is completed via myIR or Inland Revenue form IR356; if the entity has employees, PAYE and employer obligations are activated under the same IRD number. A foreign currency-denominated business may also need to document its NZ permanent establishment / fixed establishment analysis and any offshore parent company tax reference numbers for the CRS/FATCA and tax-residency profile.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in New Zealand. A New Zealand Limited Company may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) treatment if all shareholders are New Zealand-resident individuals or qualifying trustees, causing tax to be imposed at shareholder level, but the default classification for an NZ Ltd is entity-level taxation at the corporate rate. New Zealand's hybrid mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 do not offer elective hybrid treatment; no GST grouping election is applicable for cross-border zero-rating except the standard registration and filing rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of annual taxable supplies in New Zealand; non-resident remote-services suppliers making supplies to New Zealand consumers are also required to register if those supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for goods is available under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when goods are physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier or are deemed exported and the supplier retains contemporaneous export evidence. Zero-rating for services is available under section 11A when services are supplied to a person who is not resident in New Zealand and who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not exceptions such as services directly in connection with New Zealand land or goods in New Zealand. For the virtual concert and metaverse ticketing business, the supply is a remote/digital service: it is zero-rated to non-resident consumers whose usual place of residence is outside New Zealand, and the supplier must maintain two non-conflicting pieces of evidence of non-resident status and overseas location, such as billing address, IP geolocation, foreign payment card country code, digital wallet residency data, or bank account jurisdiction. The zero-rating is denied if the recipient is a New Zealand resident or is in New Zealand when receiving the virtual concert supply.

  • Non-applicable to pure digital services: no physical customs clearance is required; the substantiation file for the metaverse virtual concert must contain a signed digital services agreement or ticket purchase terms identifying the offshore recipient, a zero-rated GST tax invoice, payment evidence from an overseas bank account, foreign payment card, or USDC/cryptocurrency custody record, IP address and geolocation logs, and a non-resident status declaration or foreign tax residency record.
  • If the scope later includes physical goods, merchandise, or collectible ticket hardware: New Zealand Customs Services export entry/declaration and clearance record, Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, commercial invoice, packing list, proof of foreign purchase order, and an inward remittance or foreign exchange settlement record showing payment from outside New Zealand.
  • In all cases retain the accountant/organisation's foreign exchange repatriation evidence, banking SWIFT/MT103 records or stablecoin transaction records for audit verification.

New Zealand does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, API tax-validation, or anti-fraud digital stamping. E-invoicing is voluntary and operates through the Peppol-based New Zealand e-Invoicing Network. GST tax invoices must satisfy the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 section 24 content requirements, be issued within 28 days of the time of supply, be preserved in original unmodified form for at least 7 years, and be filed digitally via myIR when required.

Inland Revenue may assess the GST shortfall plus use-of-money interest and retrospectively claw back zero-rated treatment if documentation fails. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 apply on a tiered basis: 20% for failing to take reasonable care, 20% for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or an arrangement intended to defeat the intent of the GST Act. Fraudulent or wilfully misleading export zero-rating claims may also trigger criminal prosecution, penalties, and a 4-year reassessment period extended to 8 years for evasion or wilful misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised current and capital account convertibility regime. There are no exchange control regulations, no licensing requirements for FX transactions, no surrender rules, and no requirement to route foreign exchange through the Reserve Bank. The New Zealand dollar is a freely floating, deliverable currency. Current account payments for goods, services, royalties, event streaming fees and virtual concert ticketing can be settled without approval. Capital account transactions, including equity injections, intra-group loans, dividends, and liquidation proceeds, may also be moved freely, provided the origin and destination are not subject to sanctions or an AML/CFT offence. The role of the Reserve Bank is limited to monetary policy, prudential supervision, and settlement system oversight; it does not approve individual commercial FX contracts. Non-resident shareholders may receive dividends after statutory solvency tests and applicable non-resident withholding tax, and no exchange-control profit remittance barrier exists. The Overseas Investment Office restrictions apply only to certain sensitive land, farming, fishing quota, and significant business assets; a virtual concert metaverse company holding no such real property or high-value strategic assets will not require consent. Strict documentation of beneficial ownership, source of wealth, and economic substance will still be required by the relationship bank to prevent the open regime from becoming a laundering conduit. Overall conversion and repatriation risk for this virtual ticketing treasury is minimal.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligations, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline. Exporters and providers of cross-border virtual event services may retain foreign currency receipts offshore, convert them into New Zealand dollars through any registered bank, or use hedging instruments as needed. The only requirements are standard tax declarations for assessable income, arm's length transfer pricing for related-party transactions, and AML/CFT reporting of unusual or large cash-related activities to the New Zealand Police Financial Intelligence Unit.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a primary banking infrastructure and Medium only as a technology distribution bridge. New Zealand does not operate a distinct fintech electronic money institution license. The taking of deposits is reserved for banks registered under the Banking Prudential Supervision Act 1989, so an offshore EMI cannot lawfully serve as the entity's principal deposit-taking treasury counterparty. Payment service providers and digital currency exchange providers must comply with the Financial Service Providers Registration and Dispute Resolution Act 2008 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and must register as reporting entities with the New Zealand Financial Markets Authority if they are offering regulated financial services. In practice, an offshore EMI can only support the company as an auxiliary collections gateway if it partners with a New Zealand-licensed bank for segregated fiat settlement. Because the target payment rails include Stripe and USDC, the treasury blueprint should use a local registered bank as the core clearing and custody institution, a licensed New Zealand-based virtual asset exchange for USDC to fiat conversion, and Stripe only as a card-acquiring front end. Direct wallet-to-wallet USDC treasury retention should be limited and deprecated in favour of same-day fiat conversion into a tier-1 bank account.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Companies Act 1993 and Financial Reporting Act 2013, a New Zealand limited company is required to appoint an independent licensed auditor unless it is exempt under section 195 of the Companies Act. Mandatory audit is triggered where the company satisfies two or more of the following 'large company' tests at balance date: (i) total assets exceed NZD 60,000,000; (ii) total revenue exceeds NZD 30,000,000 in the accounting period; (iii) 50 or more full-time equivalent employees are engaged. Audit is also mandatory if the company is an FMC reporting entity under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, has public accountability, is an overseas company, or is otherwise required by its constitution or a shareholder resolution to produce audited financial statements. A company that does not meet these thresholds may still be required to appoint an auditor unless all shareholders holding voting rights pass a unanimous annual exemption resolution for that accounting period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under New Zealand's transfer pricing rules (Income Tax Act 2007, Subpart GC and Inland Revenue's Transfer Pricing Guidelines), contemporaneous documentation is required where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZ$1 million in an income year. Where intercompany loans or other financial arrangements exceed NZ$1 million, full documentation including a Local File and Master File per OECD guidance is typically expected. Failure to prepare documentation may lead to documentation penalties and adjustments to an arm's length position.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not apply dedicated ESR (Economic Substance Regulations) regimes akin to UAE or Cayman, but the Tax Administration Act and Income Tax Act 2007 contain a strong general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR, section BG 1) and transfer pricing rules that require the actual economic substance of the non-resident entity to be tested. For a metaverse/virtual concert business, Inland Revenue will scrutinise the location of key decision-makers, servers, marketing functions, contract execution, and risk management. A mere booking vehicle or shell that books New Zealand revenue but delegates substantive activity to related entities without an arm's length reward will be re-characterised. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis based on OECD Guidelines and New Zealand case law (e.g., Ben Nevis) will attribute profit to the entity that exercises the real authority, performs the functions, and assumes the risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misstatement of Digital Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under section 143A and 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994, knowingly providing false or misleading information carries a shortfall penalty of 100% of the tax gap (150% if evasive). Criminal conviction can lead to a fine up to NZ$50,000 (first offence) or NZ$100,000 (subsequent offence) and imprisonment up to 5 years. Failure to maintain adequate records under section 22 also attracts a penalty of NZ$1,000 plus daily penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who aided, abetted, or directed the fraud can be jointly and severally liable under section 79 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 because they have personally contributed to a company tax shortfall. The Companies Act 1993 permits piercing of the veil where the company is used for dishonesty: directors may face civil liability, disqualification from management (up to 5 years under section 383), asset freezing orders, director travel bans through the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009, and imprisonment up to 5 years for tax evasion or up to 7 years for false accounting under the Crimes Act 1961.
Shell Company / Substance Abuse and Transfer Pricing Distortion

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may reconstruct the arrangement under the transfer pricing rules (subpart GC, Income Tax Act 2007) and the GAAR in section BG 1. The shortfall penalty for transfer pricing mispricing can be 40% of the adjustment (unacceptable tax position) or 100% for evasion. Failure to provide transfer pricing documentation on request triggers a penalty of NZ$20,000 per month, up to NZ$200,000 (section 139B, Tax Administration Act 1994).

Director personal liability: Directors who implemented a scheme to deliberately strip profits from New Zealand may be personally liable under section 79 of the Tax Administration Act 1994. If the company is insolvent or acts recklessly, directors may face civil liability under sections 135, 136, and 383 of the Companies Act 1993 (reckless trading, disqualification). Where the transfer pricing abuse amounts to deceptive conduct, directors could be prosecuted under section 228 of the Crimes Act 1961 (fraud by deception, 7 years' imprisonment) and the insolvency court may award compensation out of their personal assets.
AML / AML-CFT & Sanctions / Unregistered Financial Service using Cryptocurrency

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a reporting entity that fails to register or comply with CDD obligations can be fined up to NZ$2,000,000 per violation for corporate entities (and NZ$300,000 for individuals). Under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008, unregistered financial service providers face fines and injunctions. Inland Revenue can also impose shortfall penalties for misclassification or non-disclosure of crypto income (up to 150% under section 141A-141E).

Director personal liability: Directors or senior managers who knowingly participate in unlicensed crypto activity can be personally prosecuted under the AML/CFT Act: imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine up to NZ$300,000 for individuals. Directors who knowingly allow the company to be used for money laundering face up to 7 years' imprisonment under section 243 of the Crimes Act 1961. Civil liability includes contribution to AML fines, restitution of funds, and disqualification. Overseas travel bans and asset freezes are possible under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009 and the Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters Act 1992.
