UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT Act), Companies Act 1993, and the Companies (Disclosure of Beneficial Ownership Information) Amendment Act 2023 (commenced 1 May 2024) collectively govern UBO identification, verification, record-keeping and filing. The AML/CFT Act obliges every reporting entity, including banks and payment service providers, to identify and verify beneficial owners where the voting or ownership interest threshold is 25% or greater. The Companies Office maintains a beneficial ownership register accessible to government agencies, regulated supervisors and the Police Financial Intelligence Unit, but not to the public.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A New Zealand registered office must be a physical address, open and accessible during normal business hours and not a post office box. A virtual office that provides a genuine physical street address, meeting rooms and a local agent may satisfy the Companies Office, but the Inland Revenue and registered banks will reject a commercial mailbox-only arrangement for this App Growth Agency. For an ISIC 7310/NAICS 541810 digital advertising and localization business, the absence of real premises, active New Zealand staff, payroll returns, client files and management activity will trigger GST registration refusals, anti-money-laundering bank-account refusals, and economic substance checks by first-party payment rails such as Wise B2B and Payoneer. A pure virtual office with no local operational substance is therefore highly likely to be rejected by tax authorities and banks.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand - online portal: myIR via https://www.ird.govt.nz

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity with a New Zealand tax presence, apply to the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) for an IRD number. If the entity is registered on the New Zealand Companies Register, register through the Companies Office first; the IRD number will then be applied for via myIR. For a purely non-resident entity outside the Companies Register, the process is: (1) access myIR and select the non-resident/overseas entity registration service; (2) provide the entity's full legal name, country of incorporation, incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, registered office, director details, ultimate beneficial ownership, and an NZ-appointed representative or tax agent where applicable; (3) complete the statutory questionnaire on taxable activity, NZ-source income, expected turnover, and intended GST registration; (4) submit certified supporting documents showing identity and lawful establishment; (5) IRD validates the application and issues a single IRD number that also functions as the GST registration number for New Zealand GST purposes. Separate GST registration is required once annual taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 or on a voluntary basis; this is also done via myIR using the same IRD number. No separate VAT identification number exists in New Zealand.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand has no US-style check-the-box election. A standard New Zealand limited company is taxed at the entity level. Eligible closely-held companies may elect Look-Through Company (LTC) treatment under subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, causing tax transparency for shareholders; hybrid mismatch elections are not available because New Zealand applies the OECD anti-hybrid rules under the Income Tax Act 2007.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand consumers must register once their total taxable supplies to NZ residents exceed NZD 60,000 per 12-month period, with voluntary registration permitted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when the goods are physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier or delivered to a carrier for export. Qualifying digital and remote services, including ASO/SEO localization services, are zero-rated under section 11B when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the supply is not made to a New Zealand resident in connection with a New Zealand taxable activity. The supplier must hold credible evidence of the recipient's non-resident status, including foreign business registration, overseas billing address, IP/bank location, and contractual documentation, plus evidence that payment flowed from outside New Zealand.

  • Customs Export Entry lodged with the New Zealand Customs Service via the Trade Single Window
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract or purchase order
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from New Zealand
  • Cargo manifest or carrier transport document showing the goods left New Zealand
  • Bank or foreign exchange remittance evidence such as SWIFT MT103 or overseas bank statement proving payment from the non-resident customer
  • Non-resident business status evidence for remote services, such as foreign tax registration certificate or equivalent declaration

None. New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal invoice clearance, government API stamping, or digital anti-fraud stamping regime. E-invoicing is voluntary and may be transmitted through the Peppol network using accredited service providers. Tax invoices must still contain the statutory particulars required under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, including supplier name, address, IRD number, invoice date, description, GST-exclusive amount, and GST charged, and must be retained electronically for audit.

IRD may retrospectively disallow zero-rating and assess 15% GST, use-of-money interest, and shortfall penalties if export documentation is insufficient, false, or not retained. Penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994 range from 20% for lack of reasonable care to 40% for an unacceptable tax position, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or fraud. Criminal prosecution is possible for knowingly providing false export or foreign exchange evidence. For physical goods, GST is clawed back if the goods are not exported within the statutory timeframe or are diverted into New Zealand before leaving; for remote services, zero-rating is reversed if the recipient is found to be in New Zealand or the service is connected to a New Zealand taxable activity.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully open capital account and a freely floating exchange-rate regime. No government entity has the power to deny conversion of NZD or to require receipt of export earnings. Residents may establish foreign-currency accounts with registered banks, and non-residents may inject equity or repatriate dividends without exchange-control approval. The practical friction is not regulatory convertibility but bank-level documentation: a dividend payment must satisfy the Companies Act 1993 solvency test, the Income Tax Act 2007 withholding obligations applicable to non-resident shareholders, and the bank's foreign-remittance AML/CFT validation. The current account is equally open; invoicing for cross-border ASO/SEO services requires only the usual commercial contract and invoice trail. The country's Financial Transactions Reporting Act has been superseded by the AML/CFT Act 2009, so conversion of NZD into USD is available on market terms via any tier-1 bank. Unsurprisingly, no surrender rules exist for export or service receipts; businesses are free to hold and retain balances offshore.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or repatriation timeline under New Zealand foreign exchange regulations. Export and cross-border service proceeds may remain in an offshore USD account or convert at market; only statistical balance of payments reporting is required above thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand, New Zealand's central bank and prudential banking regulator

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise and Payoneer are viable as payment-processing rails for low-friction disbursements and should be integrated by API only for settlement of app-store optimization and localization supplier invoices. However, they are not registered banks under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989 and are legally prohibited from taking deposits as core banking business without a New Zealand banking registration; therefore they cannot host capital escrow accounts or act as the primary treasury relationship. New Zealand AML/CFT obligations apply to the NZ LTD itself, so any EMI must be a reporting entity or agent with a demonstrably compliant KYB process. Fintech EMIs are a complementary liquidity channel, not a substitute for tier-1 domestic banking.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder equity must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own bank account to the company's NZD account at a tier-1 bank, using the product code EQUI for equity participation or the equivalent purpose code accepted by the receiving bank. Remittance must name the purchasing shareholder and reference the NZD account or capitalization board resolution. New Zealand does not operate a separate capital verification process; the bank will require the capital injection to match the audited accounting records and the Companies Act solvency declaration if it is recorded as share premium or contributed equity. Cryptocurrency rails are not acceptable for corporate capital injection.SWIFT MT103 is the standard clearing rail for recurring cross-border SAAS, contractor, and localization vendor payments. Commercial letters of credit are permissible for conditional settlement of high-value media placement contracts involving foreign suppliers; documentary collections may be used only where transport documents or performance certificates trigger payment. All outbound USD and multi-currency settlement is cleared using the New Zealand Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) and the corresponding global banking network of the tier-1 relationship bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

NRWT of 30% on dividends paid to any non-resident shareholder. If the dividend is fully imputed, the imputation credit (at 28/72) is attached to the dividend, reducing the effective cash withholding to 28% (for a resident shareholder). For non-residents, the NRWT still reduces the cash amount; however, many DTIAs reduce the 30% rate to 15% (for portfolio shareholders) and 5% (for a person holding at least 10% of the voting power in a company, provided the 80% asset/income test is satisfied).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a standard balance date of 31 March, the return and terminal tax are due by 7 July of that income year. A tax agent with an extension may extend the due date for filing to 31 March of the following year; payment of terminal tax remains due on 7 July unless an instalment arrangement applies. If the entity has a non-standard balance date, the due date is the 7th day of the second month after the end of the income year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 1,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions. A Local File must be prepared to substantiate the arm's-length nature of intercompany transactions (including integrated aggregation of SEO, ASO, and localization services); a Master File is required in New Zealand when the multinational group's consolidated annual turnover exceeds NZD 1,000 million. Maintaining contemporaneous documentation demonstrates reasonable care and mitigates shortfall penalties.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like the UAE, but it rigorously applies the OECD BEPS principles through the Income Tax Act 2007 s GC 1–14 and s BG 1 (general anti-avoidance) to require that profits are aligned with actual economic activity. For a NZ Ltd acting as an app-growth agency, the company must demonstrate substantive board and employee functions, real management decisions, and physical presence—typically a registered office with staff, service contracts, accounting records, and operational bank accounts. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must reside in New Zealand: strategy, campaign management, localized keyword research, performance analytics, and client communication. If the local company simply invoices global customers while the relevant staff are in a related foreign entity, the IRD may reallocate income to the foreign jurisdiction and assess the NZ company on a deemed commission at arm's-length. DTAs also require that the profits are attributable to a permanent establishment if the services are rendered offshore; otherwise, DTA protection is denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fradulent Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 s 143A and s 143B; civil evasion penalty up to 150% of the shortfall; criminal evasion punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine of up to NZD 50,000 in addition to the tax. Under s 245H of the same Act, the IRD can issue an assessment for a fictitious return. Non-filing of a return after three years triggers automatic 5-year imprisonment eligibility.
