Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue (Te Tari Taake) of New Zealand, acting through the electronic portal 'myIR' (myIR Secure Online Services); customs and border functions are administered by the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity intending to operate as a New Zealand limited company must first incorporate the NZ_LTD entity with the New Zealand Companies Office, which issues the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). The statutory tax identifier for Inland Revenue purposes is the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) number, which functions as both the TIN and the GST registration identifier. The procurement procedure is as follows: (1) incorporate the company under the Companies Act 1993 through the New Zealand Companies Office and obtain the NZBN; (2) submit the IRD number application to Inland Revenue through myIR or via the approved non-resident company application route, providing the NZBN, certificate of incorporation, constitution, director and shareholder particulars, and a registered office address in New Zealand; (3) if taxable supplies are expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, register for Goods and Services Tax (GST) at the same time, using the same IRD number; (4) once Inland Revenue issues the IRD number and confirms GST registration, the entity is fully tax-registered. No requirement exists for a New Zealand resident shareholder or director to be a natural person; non-resident ownership is expressly permitted. A foreign non-resident entity that is not incorporated in New Zealand but supplies remote services to New Zealand consumers may separately obtain an IRD number and register for GST without establishing a local subsidiary.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None - New Zealand has no check-the-box election comparable to the United States system. A New Zealand-resident limited company is automatically assessed at entity level on worldwide income; a non-resident company is assessable only on New Zealand-sourced income. New Zealand applies hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act 2007, but these are mandatory compliance rules, not elective classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period, or a reasonable expectation that NZD 60,000 will be exceeded within 12 months; compulsory registration applies from the date the threshold is crossed or the supply is first made in the course of an existing taxable activity, with separate remote-services registration rules for certain non-resident suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating at 0% GST on exports is claimed under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. For goods, the goods must be exported from New Zealand by the supplier within 28 days of supply and supplied to a non-resident purchaser who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, or otherwise removed from New Zealand under a customs-approved export movement. For services, and specifically for clinical trial CRO management services, zero rating applies when the services are supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, the benefit of the services is received offshore, and the services are not directly related to land or goods situated in New Zealand. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied by a New Zealand supplier to a non-resident recipient outside New Zealand are also zero-rated. The supplier must retain contemporaneous commercial evidence demonstrating the export movement or the offshore status of the recipient; the prescribing clinical trial protocol and master service agreement naming the offshore sponsor support zero rating for contract research organization services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry) lodged through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window, identifying the goods description, quantity, value, tariff classification and destination country
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Airway Bill for air freight, issued by the independent carrier, evidencing physical removal of the goods from New Zealand
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the non-resident buyer and the exporting New Zealand supplier
  • Bank foreign exchange inward remittance confirmation, functionally equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate, such as a SWIFT MT103 or bank statement evidencing foreign currency settlement from the offshore buyer
  • GST tax invoice and the underlying clinical trial services agreement or CRO master services agreement evidencing the non-resident offshore recipient and the offshore place of performance or benefit

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or central digital stamping regime exists in New Zealand. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and may be exchanged via the Peppol network, but Inland Revenue does not pre-clear or validate individual tax invoices through an API. The supplier must issue a tax invoice containing the statutory particulars required under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, retain appropriate records, and file periodic GST returns electronically through myIR.

Inland Revenue may audit zero-rated export supplies under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. If the mandatory customs documentation, evidence of physical export, offshore recipient status, or foreign exchange repatriation is missing or unsatisfactory, the zero rating is reversed and GST is reassessed at 15% on the full consideration, with use-of-money interest running from the original due date. Shortfall penalties for incorrect or fraudulent positions are 20% for lack of reasonable care, 20% for unacceptable tax interpretation, 100% for an abusive tax position, and up to 150% for evasion or equivalent conduct, with criminal prosecution available in egregious cases. Retrospective clawback extends to prior GST periods when an initial zero rating is found to be unsubstantiated, goods are not exported within the statutory period, or the arrangement is determined to be an artificial tax avoidance structure without genuine commercial substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised and freely convertible currency regime under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989. There are no exchange control regulations, no approval requirements for capital account transactions, and no restrictions on remittances of dividends, royalties, interest, or capital outflows. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is classified as a freely floating major currency, and both current account and capital account transactions are unfettered by surrender or repatriation rules. Corporate treasury operations can move funds into and out of the country with no central bank pre-approval; however, transactions remain subject to standard banking prudential requirements, anti-money laundering obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, and the Overseas Investment Act 2005 where the counterparty is acquiring sensitive New Zealand assets. For a contract research organisation (CRO), clinical trial sponsor payments and milestone payments may be received and held in NZD or foreign currency without the need for exchange control consent. Profit remittance is hurdle-free from an FX-control perspective, but the remitting company must comply with Inland Revenue's non-resident withholding tax rules on cross-border payments and maintain proper transfer pricing documentation where connected-party services are involved. This regime materially lowers treasury friction and permits banks to offer a full range of FX products without requiring a central bank approval condition.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriating or converting foreign currency receipts. Exporters and service providers, including CROs earning clinical trial fees offshore, may retain export receipts in any designated currency, convert them through licensed dealers, or remit them at a time of their choosing. The only applicable obligations are tax reporting and AML/CFT record-keeping for the underlying transaction.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High for operational disbursement only; Low for capital escrow and statutory treasury purposes. Offshore fintech EMIs are not legally prohibited from servicing New Zealand entities, but they must be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR) and comply with the AML/CFT Act 2009; operational supervision sits with the Financial Markets Authority and the Department of Internal Affairs. A pure offshore EMI without these registrations cannot lawfully maintain an account for a New Zealand company. For a CRO in the low-risk standard tier, a registered digital bank or EMI such as a licensed neo-bank can provide nimble multi-currency receivables and vendor payments, but Tier-1 local banks remain the mandatory backbone for capital verification, overseas investment monitoring, and SWIFT-based clearing. The EMI channel should therefore be structured as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail, not as the primary treasury custody relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder equity is routed by SWIFT MT103 from the overseas parent or beneficial owner directly into the NZ company's Tier-1 local currency account. New Zealand has no FX approval requirement, so the remittance should be endorsed only with a clear corporate narrative such as Paid-up share capital for NZ Ltd - unequivocal equity subscription. The company must issue share certificates and update its Companies Office shareholder register within the statutory period; a capital verification statement or auditor's confirmation should be retained. Where the investment involves a more than 25% significant interest in a business carrying on, or intending to carry on, a significant business asset in New Zealand, the transaction must be screened against the Overseas Investment Act 2005; a CRO in normal office-based consulting normally requires no consent. Banks will expect the corresponding credit received in NZD or in the invoicing currency, with no division into sequential fragmented credits, to avoid the appearance of smurfing.Operational disbursements to clinical trial sites, principal investigators, logistics providers, and research laboratories are executed via SWIFT MT103 for direct payments, documentary collections for freight-backed service settlements, and sight or revolving commercial letters of credit where investigational products and medical devices are imported. Payments to local New Zealand subcontractors are settled through the Electronic Clearing House System (ESAS) and bulk electronic clearing. Outbound cross-border wires must include a bona-fide business rationale in the remittance information, be tested against sanctions and PEP screening, and comply with Inland Revenue's non-resident withholding tax filing obligations when the recipient is a non-resident independent contractor.Outright spot FX contracts with a registered New Zealand bank for immediate conversion of trial receipts from USD, EUR, or GBP to NZD.Plain-vanilla OTC FX forward contracts up to 12 months, extendable with documented underlying clinical trial payment exposure, for fixing future sponsor milestone conversions.Cross-currency swaps for large multi-country trial cash-flow balancing, permitted only where the bank can evidence the commercial hedge rationale and underlying receivables.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permitted only for hedging cross-border exposures in currencies not freely deliverable locally; they are not required for NZD-USD or NZD-EUR corridors due to the free convertibility of NZD.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

NRWT on fully imputed dividends is 15%; if the dividend is not fully imputed, the NRWT rate is 30% on the gross dividend. The Approved Issuer Levy (AIL) can be applied to fully imputed dividends at 5% instead of NRWT (electable by approved issuers on a dividend-by-dividend basis; the 5% AIL satisfies the withholding requirement).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZ$3 million in aggregate annual related-party cross-border transactions (income or expenditure) triggers the mandatory contemporaneous documentation requirement (Local File / Master File). Related-party cross-border transactions above NZ$15 million per year for the NZ group may be required to file Country-by-Country Report if the ultimate group has consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million (or NZD equivalent).

Economic substance scope: While New Zealand does not enforce UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, the Income Tax Act 2007 GAAR, transfer pricing recharacterisation rules, and New Zealand DTA comprehensive limitation-on-benefits / PPT clauses require a NZ CRO to demonstrate real commercial substance: clinical trial project management functions, risk assumption, decision-making, full-time qualified staff, office premises, contracts, and financial autonomy. A 'shell' CRO without substantive NZ operations may be disregarded or recharacterised; treaty benefits may be denied under the Principal Purpose Test; and the entity may be treated as an agent of the foreign parent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misstatement of CRO Income

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 1994: fine up to NZD 50,000, civil shortfall penalty up to 150% of the evaded tax, interest on unpaid tax, asset freezing orders and compensatory orders.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in tax evasion may be jointly and severally liable for the company's tax shortfall, face imprisonment of up to 5 years, be ordered to make restitution, and be subjected to travel bans and departure prohibitions.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: IRD may recharacterise transactions under transfer pricing rules and the GAAR, increasing taxable income and assessing shortfall penalties of 20% (unacceptable tax position) or 40% (gross carelessness), plus UOMI; criminal sanctions for fraudulent transfer pricing documents.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or approve artificial transfer pricing arrangements may be personally liable for resulting tax if the arrangement is void under the anti-avoidance rules; court-imposed disqualification as a director may follow; travel bans may be issued to secure recovery of tax debts.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the OECD Principal Purpose Test; recharacterisation or disregard of the legal entity; reassessment of all income as belonging to the parent/beneficial owner; deregistration under the Companies Act 1993; penalty for carrying on business without meeting NZ Companies Office obligations up to NZD 10,000 and possible strike-off.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for company debts, including tax liabilities, under the Companies Act and tax legislation if they allowed the company to trade while insolvent or as a sham; directors may face orders prohibiting management, civil restitution, and travel restrictions.
AML / Sanctions Red Flags Linked to Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: For non-AML-reporting ordinary companies, failing to report suspicious transactions under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (if conducting relevant financial business) can lead to civil penalties up to NZD 5 million per violation and criminal penalties with imprisonment up to 2 years; for sanctions breaches under the Sanctions Act 2020, imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally prosecuted for money laundering or sanctions evasion, with imprisonment up to 7–14 years depending on the predicate offence; personal asset freezing orders, travel bans, and director disqualification may be imposed.
Failure to File / False Non-Resident Withholding Claims

Statutory sanction: Automatic late filing penalties of NZD 250 per month (max NZD 1,000); shortfall penalty of 20%–40%; for a knowingly false declaration, penalty of 150% of the tax and prosecution with fine up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or withholding exemption declarations are personally liable for the shortfall and subject to criminal penalties including imprisonment of up to 5 years and confiscation of assets; the High Court may issue travel bans to enforce white-collar crime sanctions.
