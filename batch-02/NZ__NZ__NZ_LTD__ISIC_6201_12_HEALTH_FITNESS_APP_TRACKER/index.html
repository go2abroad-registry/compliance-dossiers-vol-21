Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

New Zealand allows a serviced office or a registered commercial address to serve as the registered office under the Companies Act 1993, provided it is a physical street address (not a PO Box), with a person authorised to accept service of legal documents and sufficient record-keeping capacity. A pure commercial mailbox without staffing or document storage is not acceptable. For a digital health fitness app tracker operating through Apple and Google payment rails, the Inland Revenue Department, the New Zealand Business Number registrar, and major New Zealand trading banks will require demonstrable physical presence and genuine commercial substance before granting GST registration, a bank account, or a merchant facility. An isolated virtual office without a local director, a lease, staff, or an active New Zealand bank relationship carries high risk of being treated as a tax shell, being de-banked, or having payment payouts from Apple/Google refused. We recommend a serviced office with a reception area, meeting room, and dedicated file storage for at least the first two tax years.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand, with the secure online tax portal myIR (myIR.ird.govt.nz); customs-related export clearance is administered by the New Zealand Customs Service through the Trade Single Window (TSW).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident company seeking to operate or derive New Zealand-source income must obtain a New Zealand IRD number from the Inland Revenue Department. The application is submitted through myIR using the IRD's prescribed non-resident company IRD number application; the applicant must provide the foreign certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered office or address for service in New Zealand, director and ultimate beneficial owner details, nature of the proposed New Zealand taxable activity, and projected turnover. Once the IRD number is issued, the entity must separately assess compulsory GST registration under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. If taxable supplies in New Zealand exceed or are expected to exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, GST registration must be completed through myIR using the IRD's GST registration process. A non-resident offshore supplier of remote services or digital goods to New Zealand consumers must also use the offshore supplier registration procedure, which requires an IRD number first. For a limited company incorporated in New Zealand, the Companies Office will issue a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) upon incorporation, and the IRD number is assigned through the same Companies Office registration process; for foreign non-resident companies not incorporated in New Zealand, the IRD non-resident company application route is used directly.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for a non-resident company. New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box regime. Domestic limited companies may elect look-through company status under the Income Tax Act 2007, but that election is available only to New Zealand tax-resident individual shareholders and cannot be used by a non-resident corporate-owned entity. A non-resident company is therefore taxed at entity level on New Zealand-source income, subject to the Interest Limitation Rule and the OECD hybrid mismatch rules, both of which are compulsory and not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; for non-resident offshore suppliers of remote services and digital goods, the threshold is NZD 60,000 of supplies to New Zealand consumers in a 12-month period. Voluntary registration is available to non-resident suppliers carrying on a taxable activity in New Zealand even if below the threshold, allowing recovery of GST incurred on local costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under section 11(1) of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 where the supplier exports goods from New Zealand, the goods are exported within the statutory time limit, and the supplier retains evidence of physical export. Services, including digital health and fitness app platform services, are zero-rated under section 11A where the services are supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed, and the services are not directly connected with New Zealand land or goods. For sales through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, the supply by the New Zealand app company to the non-resident platform operator may be zero-rated as an exported service if the platform operator is the disclosed recipient and the applicable conditions are met. Direct B2C supplies of remote digital services to private consumers present in New Zealand are subject to 15% GST under the offshore supplier rules and cannot be zero-rated unless the recipient is a non-resident business customer outside New Zealand with adequate documentary evidence of non-residence.

  • New Zealand Customs Service electronic Export Entry (customs export declaration) lodged through the Trade Single Window (TSW) for goods exports above the prescribed value or for controlled goods
  • Commercial invoice and sale contract stating the overseas buyer, consideration in NZD or foreign currency, and Incoterms
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight, or Master or House Air Waybill for air freight, evidencing physical departure of goods from New Zealand
  • Packing list and, where applicable, certificate of origin
  • Bank records showing cross-border receipt of funds from the non-resident buyer, including SWIFT MT103 or equivalent inward payment instruction from the receiving New Zealand bank
  • For services, a signed declaration or certificate from the non-resident recipient confirming offshore status and that the services are not connected with New Zealand land or goods
  • For Apple IAP and Google Play Billing transactions, the platform settlement statement and app marketplace reporting showing the non-resident platform operator as the contracting counterparty
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank foreign exchange repatriation certificate where the buyer's jurisdiction requires such documentation; New Zealand IRD accepts underlying bank records as supporting evidence

New Zealand has no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction control regime, no mandatory government API clearance, and no anti-fraud digital stamping. GST returns and payments are filed electronically through myIR. E-invoicing may be exchanged through the New Zealand Peppol network on a voluntary basis, but it does not replace the statutory GST return. Tax invoices must comply with sections 24 and 24B of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; a tax invoice must be issued for taxable supplies exceeding NZD 1,000 within 28 days, and buyer-created tax invoices are permitted in prescribed circumstances.

If an export zero-rating claim is not supported by the required customs and foreign exchange documentation, the supplier must account for GST at 15% on the full consideration, plus use-of-money interest under the Tax Administration Act 1994 from the original due date. Shortfall penalties apply on a scale: 20% for failure to take reasonable care or for an unacceptable tax position, 40% for gross carelessness, 100% for an abusive tax position, and 150% for evasion or similar wilful default. Late filing penalties of NZD 250 to NZD 1,000 per GST return period may also apply. The Commissioner may reassess a GST position within 4 years of the filing date, extended to 7 years in cases of fraud or evasion, and with no time limit where a return has not been filed or where evasion is established. All export zero-rating records, including customs export entries, transport documents, and bank foreign exchange receipts, must be retained for at least 7 years; failure to maintain records attracts a strict liability penalty of NZD 500. Deliberately providing false information to obtain a GST refund is a criminal offence under the Tax Administration Act 1994 and may result in imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines up to NZD 50,000 for an individual or NZD 250,000 for a company.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised current and capital account. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand does not impose exchange controls, nor does it regulate the convertibility of the New Zealand dollar (NZD) for trade, investment, or profit remittance. Residents and non-residents may freely buy and sell foreign currency through authorised banks, and no surrender requirement applies to export proceeds or service receipts. Inward foreign direct investment in a digital health application company is not screened unless the business involves sensitive land, fishing quota, or significant business assets under the Overseas Investment Act. Dividend and royalty outflows are subject to non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at 15% under the NZ-Australia double tax treaty, or 30% where no treaty applies, but this is a tax obligation, not a currency control. Consequently, treasury operations face no exchange rationing, no import approval, and no retained FX amounts. The practical hurdle is the bank's own KYC/AML underwriting, not state FX regulation. Profit remittance is effected through ordinary SWIFT payments in NZD or any convertible currency, and hedging instruments are freely available from licensed providers without derivative trade exchange approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender or conversion timeline. Exporters and service providers may retain foreign currency proceeds in any authorised bank account and disburse freely, subject only to standard bank recording requirements for anti-money laundering purposes.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

New Zealand does not recognise the European-style EMI licence class. Non-bank financial service providers may offer payment and wallet services, but must register as Financial Service Providers and as Reporting Entities under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. An offshore digital EMI without a local subsidiary, local AML/CFT compliance program, and a partnership with a domestic tier-1 bank will not be able to service an NZ entity directly. Viability is rated Low to Medium; direct multi-currency account holding via an offshore EMI in NZD is legally constrained and operationally risky. A licensed NZ-registered non-bank entity or an EMI with an omnibus account held at ANZ/ASB/BNZ may serve as a supplementary collection channel, but the primary treasury account must remain with a local tier-1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound investor remittances should be sent via SWIFT MT103 from the investor home account to the NZ entity tier-1 bank account, with field 70 referencing Equity subscription NZ_LTD and the company registration number. For amounts above NZD 50,000, banks may request a foreign exchange purpose code, typically 1020 for foreign direct investment or 1110 for intercompany loans. Capital verification is not filed with the Companies Office, but for statutory records the company must retain the share allotment resolution, director declaration of solvency, bank advice proving full settlement, and shareholder ledger entries. No central bank permission is required for capital injection or debt finance, but the bank may require a source-of-funds breakdown for funds originating from related parties.Operational disbursements use SWIFT MT103 with purpose codes corresponding to royalties, licensing, service fees, or intercompany loan repayments. Merchant payouts from Apple and Google are received through automated clearing house credits from the platforms in NZD, USD, or EUR; treasury should maintain dual-currency NZD/USD accounts or a daily sweep conversion to reduce exposure. For high-value trade settlements, the company may issue Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600 through ANZ, BNZ, ASB, or Westpac trade desks, or use Documentary Collections under URC522 for slower trust-based transactions. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand does not impose a simple statutory bright-line threshold for all Local File preparation, but Inland Revenue's transfer pricing guidelines and the OECD transfer pricing framework require contemporaneous documentation where cross-border related-party transactions are material. In practice, IRD expects full documentation for aggregate related-party cross-border goods transactions exceeding NZD 1,000,000 and service or intangible/royalty transactions exceeding NZD 500,000, with lower thresholds for high-risk financing, IP, and digital-business transfers. Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for NZ-resident ultimate parents of multinational groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more, and Master File/Local File expectations apply to those significant groups.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand is not a Caribbean Economic Substance Regulations jurisdiction, but economic substance is enforced through NZ tax residence, treaty PE thresholds, and anti-avoidance doctrine. A NZ company is tax resident if incorporated in New Zealand or if its centre of day-to-day management is in New Zealand. For an app company monetising fitness biometric data, the core income-generating activities (CIGA) - algorithm development, server-side data processing, data-security compliance, contract negotiation, and strategic product decisions - must be managed and performed by personnel in New Zealand. A circular arrangement where the NZ company pays a large deductible licence fee to an offshore trust or zero-substance IP shell will be attacked under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in section BG1 and reconstructed under section GB1 of the Income Tax Act 2007, and may create a taxable permanent establishment in New Zealand for the offshore entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent IR4 Filing

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 section 141E imposes a civil shortfall penalty of 150% of the tax evasion shortfall, plus use-of-money interest. Criminal prosecution under section 143B of the same Act can lead to a fine of up to NZD 50,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years. The company may also be disqualified from government or health-sector procurement and lose any eligibility for the R&D tax incentive.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly caused or signed the false return are treated as active participants in the evasion; the corporate veil is pierced. Directors may be ordered to make civil asset restitution under section 301 of the Companies Act 1993 and may be personally liable for the company's tax debt if assets were diverted to defeat creditors. Inland Revenue may obtain a departure prohibition order preventing the director from leaving New Zealand, and a criminal conviction triggers automatic director-disqualification review and potential travel blacklisting.
Transfer Pricing Abuse and Base-Erosion Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 sections GC 13 and GC 14 allow the Commissioner to adjust the taxpayer's income to reflect arm's-length terms between associated parties. Failure to maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation may be treated as an unreasonable tax position under section 141EA, attracting a 20-40% shortfall penalty and, where the arrangement is designed to defeat tax, a 100-150% penalty. The general anti-avoidance provisions in sections BG1 and GB1 allow reconstruction of the arrangement. Failing to file a mandatory CbCR or Master File can also attract separate penalty actions.

Director personal liability: A director who approves a sham intercompany royalty arrangement can be sued for breach of the duty of care and recklessness under sections 135-137 of the Companies Act 1993. If profit stripping renders the company unable to pay Inland Revenue, the court may order the director to pay compensation to the company and restore diverted assets. The director may be prohibited from leaving New Zealand while the tax debt is unsatisfied and may be personally pursued as a principal or an aider of the corporate evasion.
Unlawful Dividend and Disguised Profit Repatriation with NRWT Evasion

Statutory sanction: The failure to deduct NRWT makes the NZ company liable for the unpaid NRWT, plus late payment penalties, use-of-money interest, and a shortfall penalty under the Tax Administration Act 1994. A dividend paid in breach of the solvency test is invalid and the shareholders may be required to repay it. Directors who vote for an unlawful distribution are jointly and severally liable to compensate the company under sections 52-58 of the Companies Act 1993.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise an unlawful distribution must restore the full amount of the distribution or contribute to the company's loss. The court may make a recovery or compensation order under section 301 of the Companies Act 1993, and if the director acts dishonestly, personal criminal liability may follow. The director may be served with a departure prohibition order, treated as a tax-evasion participant for the unpaid NRWT, and disqualified from managing companies in New Zealand. This death-spiral red flag allows Inland Revenue and a liquidator to pierce the corporate veil and pursue the director's personal assets.
