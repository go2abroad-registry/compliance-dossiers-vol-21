Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand law imposes no minimum paid-up share capital for a standard limited company, and there is no blocked escrow account requirement at incorporation. The share capital and consideration are fixed in the shareholders subscription agreement and, where adopted, the constitution. Shares may be issued as fully paid or partly paid; any unpaid capital constitutes a debt recoverable by the company.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a fire safety and evacuation scheme approval from Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) where required by the premises category; secure a building warrant of fitness from the territorial authority covering structural load and fire egress; obtain WorkSafe New Zealand workplace health and safety clearance for the office and any laboratory, and implement site-specific safety inductions, hazard registers, personal protective equipment protocols and first aid arrangements for staff conducting factory pre-shipment inspections.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) - electronic portal: myIR Secure Online Services at ird.govt.nz; export-side customs clearance: New Zealand Customs Service (NZCS) Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the entity is a non-resident company for New Zealand tax purposes under the Income Tax Act 2007 (not incorporated in New Zealand, no head office or centre of management in New Zealand, and no directors controlling the company in New Zealand). Step 2: If the non-resident company will carry on business in New Zealand, including a taxable activity such as on-site factory inspection, testing, pre-shipment QC or audit services contracted from New Zealand, first complete overseas-company registration with the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993 to obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN); an overseas company must be registered before carrying on business in New Zealand unless an exemption applies. Step 3: Use the NZBN and New Zealand address for service to apply for an Inland Revenue (IRD) number through myIR Secure Online Services. The IRD application for a non-resident company requires the overseas certificate of incorporation, NZ overseas-company registration certificate, constitutional documents, director and ultimate beneficial owner details, New Zealand registered office/address for service, and a description of the business activities (ISIC 7120 / NAICS 541380 commercial inspection, testing and quality control). Step 4: After the IRD number is issued, activate GST registration in myIR by selecting GST as the tax type; the entity must declare the effective registration date, normally the later of the first taxable supply date or the date the NZD 60,000 rolling 12-month threshold is exceeded, or an earlier voluntary registration date accepted by IRD. Step 5: IRD confirms the GST registration, filing frequency (monthly, two-monthly or six-monthly) and GST return periods; no registration fee is charged and the IRD number functions as the GST number. Step 6: Retain all formation, ownership, contract and cross-border payment records for at least 7 years; non-resident companies without a New Zealand bank account should preserve SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advices to evidence foreign consideration if GST refunds are later claimed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in New Zealand, and no elective hybrid-mismatch regime is available; hybrid and branch mismatch rules in Part FM of the Income Tax Act 2007 apply mandatorily. A qualifying close-held New Zealand company may elect look-through company (LTC) status under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, but this is generally not available or commercially appropriate for a standard Ltd subsidiary owned by a foreign corporate shareholder in the commercial inspection/testing sector.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies (excluding exempt supplies) in any rolling 12-month period; the same NZD 60,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of remote/digital services supplied to New Zealand consumers. Voluntary registration is available if a taxable activity exists and the threshold may not yet be reached. There is no state-level sales tax; New Zealand applies a unitary national GST system.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when supplied by a GST-registered supplier for export from New Zealand, provided the supplier retains sufficient and credible evidence that the goods were physically removed from New Zealand in accordance with the contract of sale and the statutory export period. Services, including factory inspection, testing, pre-shipment quality control, audit reports and related verification services supplied to an offshore principal or overseas buyer, are zero-rated under section 11A when the recipient is a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and the supply is not made to a New Zealand GST-registered person for use or consumption in New Zealand. The supplier must retain the executed service agreement, purchase order, completion/certificate reports and payment evidence. New Zealand has no formal Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) because it has no exchange-control certificate regime; the accepted substitute is a bank-issued inward credit advice, foreign currency settlement statement or SWIFT MT103.

  • New Zealand Customs Export Declaration lodged through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window, showing the export entry number, exporter/customs client code and commodity/statistical code.
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical movement from a New Zealand port or airport to a foreign destination; container status or cargo movement records are also accepted by IRD as supporting export evidence.
  • Commercial invoice or sales contract showing supplier, buyer, Incoterms, quantity, unit and total value, goods description and, where relevant, the service scope under ISIC 7120 / NAICS 541380; for pure exported services, an executed service agreement and inspection/QC completion certificate replace the customs export declaration.
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt or SWIFT MT103 payment advice from the overseas buyer; where a formal repatriation certificate is required by the group treasury policy, an inward remittance credit advice issued by a New Zealand or foreign bank serves as the local FIRC equivalent because no statutory FIRC exists in New Zealand.

New Zealand does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing pre-authorisation, or anti-fraud digital stamping. GST invoice content must meet section 24 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, and invoices may be issued in paper or electronic form. E-invoicing is voluntary and operated through the PEPPOL-based New Zealand e-Invoicing network; central government agencies are required to accept e-invoices through that network. IRD does not pre-clear individual invoices; compliance is enforced via periodic GST returns in myIR, data matching and risk-based audit.

IRD will disallow export zero-rating and claw back output GST at 15% if the substantiation bundle is incomplete, internally inconsistent, or fails to prove the goods left New Zealand or the services were supplied to a qualifying non-resident. The assessment is made for the original supply period and attracts use-of-money interest from the original due date plus late payment penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Shortfall penalties apply where the zero-rating claim results from lack of reasonable care, gross carelessness, an unacceptable or abusive tax position, or evasion, with the most serious fraud/evasion penalty reaching 100% of the tax shortfall; the standard IRD reassessment window is four years, and there is no time limit for fraudulent evasion. If goods zero-rated as exports are later returned to New Zealand, diverted for local consumption, or if the export payment is not received within the commercial timeframe required by the transaction, the zero-rating must be reversed in a subsequent GST adjustment return. All export and service-export records should be retained for at least 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand operates a fully liberalised and open capital account with no exchange control legislation. The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is freely convertible in both current and capital account transactions. There are no restrictions on the purchasing or selling of foreign currency, nor any requirement for Central Bank approval for trade-related payments, dividends, or capital repatriation. Foreign direct investment into an NZ Limited company, capital injections, intercompany loans, and profit remittances are all processed without exchange control surrender requirements. The Financial Markets Conduct Act and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 (AML/CFT) govern the conduct of financial institutions, but they do not impose currency convertibility constraints. For a commercial inspection and quality control agency, fees for services rendered overseas can be received in foreign currency and retained in NZD or converted to NZD without mandatory conversion timelines. The only practical hurdle is the standard banking requirement that all cross-border receipts be accompanied by accurate purpose codes and supporting documentation for tax and AML purposes. Profit repatriation to foreign shareholders is subject to non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) but is otherwise freely permitted. There is no requirement to surrender export proceeds to the RBNZ or any other state agency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender requirement exists. All export proceeds may be retained in any freely convertible currency. There is no statutory timeline or conversion ratio imposed under New Zealand law; the RBNZ exercises no compulsory conversion authority.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms and fintech providers such as Wise are legally permitted to service New Zealand entities in a limited capacity, primarily for inbound/outbound payment routing as payment service providers registered under the Financial Service Providers Act 2008. However, full corporate banking services, such as escrow accounts, commercial letters of credit, trade finance facilities, and in-country NDIS-compliant clearing, are not available through these EMIs. For a New Zealand limited company engaged in on-site factory pre-shipment inspection, EMIs are viable only as supplementary wires for low-value operational payments. The primary treasury relationship must be with a Tier-1 NZ-registered bank to establish a legal trust account for client funds, issue documentary collections, and access RBNZ settlement systems such as ESAS and Bulk Electronic Clearing System (BECS).

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Unverified ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO) chain through a nominee shareholder or trust structure

Root cause: Unverified ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO) chain through a nominee shareholder or trust structure

Defense evidence: Full UBO declaration form (RBNZ-prescribed), certified trust deed, register of directors and shareholders, apostilled certificate of incorporation, passport copies of all controlling individuals, and a signed statutory declaration identifying all natural persons with 25% or more ownership. A third-party corporate services provider letter confirming the commercial rationale for the inspection agency and its client list.
Cross-border invoice and payment flow mismatch, particularly receipt of funds from a third-party factoring or intermediary entity instead of the end-client factory

Root cause: Cross-border invoice and payment flow mismatch, particularly receipt of funds from a third-party factoring or intermediary entity instead of the end-client factory

Defense evidence: Executed master service agreements with each client, itemised inspection reports referencing factory names and dates, bank statements showing consistent client payment names, and a sworn affidavit from the financial controller confirming payment is for verification services only. If using Wise or an EMI, provide a payment reconciliation ledger mapping each inbound transfer to the corresponding inspection report.
Sanctions and adverse media exposure of the factory inspection counterparties in target export markets

Root cause: Sanctions and adverse media exposure of the factory inspection counterparties in target export markets

Defense evidence: A digital compliance screening report from a reputable provider (Dow Jones, Refinitiv, or Sanction Scanner) covering all counterparties, the company's own sanctions policy, an independent OFAC/EU/NZ sanctions list check, and a detailed narrative explaining the purpose of pre-shipment inspection in non-sanctioned goods markets. Any contracts with entities in high-risk jurisdictions must include a Benford's law anomaly check on transaction volumes.
Suspected shell-company indication due to minimal local operating footprint and remote director residency

Root cause: Suspected shell-company indication due to minimal local operating footprint and remote director residency

Defense evidence: Lease agreement for registered office or co-working space, utility bills in the company name, evidence of an in-country professional accountant or tax agent appointment, New Zealand IRD registration certificate, and a corporate bank reference letter from the overseas parent (if any). A video walkthrough of the company's quality control equipment, calibration certificates, and inspection methodology template may also be required by the bank's AML committee.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the NZ Limited company should be routed via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'C.MT1' (capital contribution to a resident enterprise). The remitter must use the exact legal name of the NZ entity and reference the shareholder loan or share allotment resolution. Bank of New Zealand or ANZ will open a capital escrow account that holds the funds pending verification by the NZ Companies Office and a registered accountant. For foreign direct investment under NZ's overseas investment rules, the transaction is generally exempt if it is a small equity investment, but the bank may request proof of exemption or a no-consent notice. All wire instructions must include the 11-digit NZD account number and the bank's BIC code. Capital verification requires a statutory declaration from the entity's auditor confirming the source of funds is legitimate and the amount matches the share allotment minutes.For outbound trade disbursements, primarily settlement of third-party factory inspection fees and operational costs, the recommended clearing rails are (i) SWIFT MT103 for standard supplier payments, with purpose code 'B.124' for business services; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) only when a client requires pre-shipment inspection to be tied to a bank guarantee; and (iii) Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) for high-value inspection contracts where the bank holds the original inspection report as a collection instrument. For local New Zealand operating expenses, use BECS direct credit and the ESAS high-value settlement network for amounts over NZD 1 million. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) is imposed at 15% on dividends that are fully imputed. Unimputed dividends attract a statutory rate of 30%. An additional resident withholding tax (RWT) obligation applies to resident shareholders but is supplanted by NRWT for non-residents to the extent of the dividend.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is mandatorily required where the total value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds NZ$5,000,000 in an income year. This includes transactions for goods, services, intangibles, interest, royalties, and insurance premiums. In addition, an IR4 return must disclose related-party financial arrangements where the aggregate loan balance exceeds NZ$5,000,000.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not enact Gulf-Cooperation-Council-style Economic Substance Regulations. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Part G of the Income Tax Act 2007 (specifically sections BG 1 and GA 1) invalidates tax arrangements that lack commercial substance or are entered into for a purpose of defeating the intended application of tax law. Inland Revenue may disregard artificial transactions, re-characterise legal form, and allocate profits further to the economic substance. For an international factory-inspection and quality-control agency, the New Zealand Ltd is expected to maintain a real headquarters office, a substantive presence of employees who are involved in managerial decisions, contemporaneous board minutes, contracts, invoices, accounting records, and control over payment rails (bank accounts, Wise infrastructure) inside New Zealand. A pure intermediary or a pass-through booking company that routes profits to a low-tax affiliate without any staff, office or operational decision-making in New Zealand will be treated as a shell for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction for tax evasion under section 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994: a fine of up to NZ$50,000 for the first offence (or NZ$100,000 for subsequent offences) and imprisonment for up to five years. The taxpayer must pay the full tax shortfall plus a shortfall penalty of 150% for a tax evasion arrangement (s 141D) plus 1% monthly late payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or participate in a fraudulent tax return are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall under the Companies Act 1993, and may be disqualified from managing a company under section 383 of the Act. Inland Revenue may also apply a travel ban notice under section 157 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 prohibiting the director from leaving New Zealand while unpaid tax liabilities exceed a threshold. Criminal prosecution of individual directors is available.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue invokes the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s BG 1 and s GA 1) to nullify the tax benefit of the arrangement, reconstructing income to the New Zealand entity. The tax shortfall is subject to a 50% shortfall penalty for taking an abusive tax position (s 141AB) or a 100% penalty if the arrangement is also fraudulent. Business registration may be cancelled by the Companies Office for contravention of the Companies Act 1993 (e.g., insolvent trading).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the company's tax debts under section 135 (reckless trading) and section 136 (duty to prohibit insolvent trading) of the Companies Act 1993. Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the company is a mere facade. Inland Revenue may issue a travel ban on any director who is a non-resident; the director may be sued for civil compensation for the tax loss caused to the New Zealand state.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may reassess at the arm's length amount under section GC 13 of the Income Tax Act 2007 and apply a 20% shortfall penalty under section 141FB for failing to retain adequate TP documentation, a 40% penalty for an unacceptable tax position, or a 100% penalty where evasion is proven. Additional late interest applies. Inland Revenue also may impose a formal TP determination and require restatement from the date of the first relevant transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors who are responsible for setting, approving, or implementing the controlled transaction without independent expert evidence may be liable for the tax shortfall and any penalty in equity. In New Zealand, directorial liability for avoidance arrangements has been enforced by piercing the veil under the Companies Act 1993. Inland Revenue may ban a director from leaving New Zealand under section 157 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 and may seek disqualification under the Companies Act 1993 for up to 10 years.
AML / Counter-Terrorism Financing Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, a reporting entity (which includes a New Zealand company providing international payment services or acting as a similar financial conduit for clients) is liable for civil penalties up to NZ$2 million for each systemic failure, a criminal fine up to NZ$5 million for a body corporate upon conviction, and a public warning or suspension of business registration from the Department of Internal Affairs. In addition, the Financial Markets Authority may impose a remediation order.
