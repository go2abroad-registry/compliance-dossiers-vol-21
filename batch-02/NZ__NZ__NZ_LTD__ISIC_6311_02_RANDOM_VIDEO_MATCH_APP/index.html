Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Inland Revenue) of New Zealand, operating through the myIR secure online portal and administered in coordination with the New Zealand Companies Office and the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) system.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a New Zealand limited company: (i) Incorporate the company online through the New Zealand Companies Office under the Companies Act 1993; the system automatically issues the New Zealand Business Number (NZBN). (ii) Apply to Inland Revenue for the company's IRD number using the prescribed non-resident company registration procedure lodged through myIR or the applicable Inland Revenue paper application; furnish the certificate of incorporation, NZBN, registered office address, director and ultimate beneficial owner details, and a valid street address for service both in New Zealand if a place of business exists and abroad if not. (iii) Concurrently or subsequently complete Goods and Services Tax (GST) registration in the same myIR account if the entity will carry on a taxable activity and annual taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold, electing a GST filing frequency and an accounting basis that fits the cross-border digital services model. (iv) Maintain a New Zealand bank account or documented foreign currency settlement arrangement for GST refund and export substantiation purposes, and notify Inland Revenue within the statutory timeframe of any changes to directors, address, contact information, or cessation of the New Zealand taxable activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a New Zealand limited company is a separate legal entity taxed at entity level on its worldwide income if incorporated in New Zealand, with branch equivalent rules applying to non-resident companies carrying on business in New Zealand; there is no pass-through classification for a standard NZ Ltd).

Available election forms

None. New Zealand has no check-the-box or elective pass-through regime for limited companies; hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018, and no statutory entity classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 of taxable supplies made in New Zealand over any 12-month period; the same NZD 60,000 threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of remote services to New Zealand resident customers, with voluntary registration permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated when the goods are physically exported from New Zealand by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf, and the supplier retains statutory evidence of export. A supply of cross-border services, including digital video matching and social telephony services, is zero-rated when the services are supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside New Zealand at the time the services are performed and the services are not connected directly with New Zealand real property or with goods in New Zealand that are subsequently supplied to the recipient in New Zealand; services supplied remotely to New Zealand resident consumers are outside zero-rating and instead attract the standard 15% GST under the offshore remote services rules. The zero-rating claim must be supported by commercial, customs, and foreign exchange records sufficient to satisfy section 11 and related provisions of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 and Inland Revenue practice.

  • Customs export declaration lodged electronically through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window in the approved export entry format
  • New Zealand Customs Service clearance/export certificate confirming the goods have left New Zealand
  • Bill of lading (sea freight) or airway bill (air freight) evidencing movement to the overseas destination
  • Original commercial invoice and packing list matching the export declaration
  • Foreign exchange repatriation evidence in the form of an inbound remittance advice, inward foreign currency transfer record, or bank statement identifying settlement of the export invoice from the overseas buyer's currency
  • Executed cross-border service agreement or user terms establishing the video matching services are delivered to non-resident users outside New Zealand
  • IP geolocation and server access logs substantiating the overseas use of the digital platform where zero-rated services are supplied
  • Certificate or statutory declaration where physical export documentation cannot be obtained, in accordance with Inland Revenue's acceptable evidence guidelines

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or government pre-clearance requirement exists in New Zealand. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a fully liberalised and open financial system with no foreign exchange controls or restrictions on current or capital account transactions. The New Zealand dollar is freely convertible and floats independently under a managed official cash rate framework operated by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand. There are no surrender requirements, no central bank approval for trade payments, no restrictions on the establishment of foreign currency bank accounts, and no dividend or profit remittance approval hurdles. New Zealand has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and does not apply multiple currency practices. In practice, the only constraints arise from the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009, which requires banks to conduct customer due diligence, beneficial ownership identification, and source-of-funds verification for cross-border payments. The Overseas Investment Act 2005 also imposes consent thresholds for acquisitions of sensitive land or significant business assets, but a standard non-resident equity subscription into an app developer company below those thresholds is unrestricted. Profit remittance to a non-resident shareholder is generally subject to non-resident withholding tax at treaty rates, but no foreign exchange conversion or Reserve Bank approval is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion obligation exists under New Zealand law. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts indefinitely in offshore or domestic foreign currency accounts. There is no mandated conversion timeline or minimum conversion ratio. Banks require accurate transaction details for Balance of Payments statistical reporting and AML/CFT record-keeping, and taxable income must be translated into New Zealand dollars for IRD filing using an acceptable exchange rate methodology.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) - no separate foreign exchange administration; overseas investment screening is conducted by the Overseas Investment Office under the Overseas Investment Act 2005

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers are not statutorily prohibited in New Zealand and may be registered on the Financial Service Providers Register, but they are not licensed banks and cannot offer a fully regulated New Zealand dollar settlement account with a local branch number. For a medium-risk video-chat app with Apple IAP and Paymentwall payment rails, relying solely on a fintech EMI is assessed as medium-to-low viability. Mainstream banks require local domiciliation for GST/IRD reconciliation and Companies Office disclosure, and major clearing banks are better positioned to handle app-store merchant settlement and high-velocity cross-border receipts. The recommended structure is to use a tier-1 New Zealand bank as the treasury anchor and use EMI capabilities only as a supplementary collection gateway after the bank has approved the merchant-of-record model.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from the overseas shareholder's regulated bank account to the New Zealand company's NZD clearing account at an RBNZ-registered bank. There is no Reserve Bank foreign exchange approval for ordinary share subscription, but the bank will require the beneficiary account name to match the registered company name, and the remittance instruction should state 'share subscription - [allottee name] - [share allotment reference]'. After receipt, the company must file a share allotment return with the New Zealand Companies Office. Treasury capital verification is supported by the bank credit entry, a director board resolution allotting shares, and a solicitor's confirmation. Source-of-funds documentation must be maintained for AML/CFT purposes. If the business is a sensitive industry or exceeds the applicable Overseas Investment Act threshold, an OIO consent application must be completed before settlement. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

28% flat New Zealand corporate income tax rate, payable on the net taxable income of the New Zealand-resident company

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic statutory NRWT on outbound dividends is 30% of the gross dividend, payable before the dividend is remitted. The dividend-paying NZ company is the withholding agent and must deduct and return NRWT regardless of whether the shareholder is a direct foreign holder or an intermediary in the Apple IAP or Paymentwall payment collection chain. Imputed dividends, excessive shareholder loans, and artificial management fees can be recharacterised as dividends subject to this 30% rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Zealand has no bright-line statutory NZD threshold requiring automatic Local File or Master File preparation, but any material cross-border related-party transaction must be supported by contemporaneous OECD transfer pricing documentation. Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for NZ constituent entities of multinational groups with consolidated group revenue above NZ$680 million; failure to file or produce supporting local files allows the IRD to draw adverse inferences and apply shortfall penalties at the top statutory range.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations law of the Cayman or BVI type, but the general anti-avoidance rule in ITA 2007 s BG 1, the commerciality rules, Part 6 thin-capitalisation legislation, and the OECD-aligned transfer pricing provisions require real economic substance. Core Income-Generating Activities for the video-matching app, including algorithm maintenance, content moderation, user revenue collection, and board control over risk and capital, must be performed by actual staff in New Zealand. If those CIGA functions are relocated to a low-tax offshore shell while the New Zealand entity retains only the risk of high-cost operations, the IRD will recast the arrangement, deny deductions, impose NRWT, and impose director liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing and Profit-Shifting Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may reconstruct the transaction at the arm's length amount under ITA 2007 ss GC 13-GC 15, impose mandatory use-of-money interest under TAA 1994 s 120, and apply a shortfall penalty under s 141 of up to 150% of the resulting tax shortfall. If the failure is deliberate, criminal prosecution under TAA 1994 s 143B may follow, with fines up to NZ$250,000 and imprisonment for up to five years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced under the Companies Act 1993 ss 300 and 301 where directors cause the company to trade recklessly or fraudulently by draining profits through non-arm's length intercompany charges. Directors may be personally ordered to repay dissipated company assets, disqualified from managing companies for up to five years, served with Departure Prohibition Orders that blacklist them from leaving New Zealand, and subjected to criminal imprisonment for intentionally falsified TP records.
NRWT Evasion through Disguised Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: NRWT is imposed under ITA 2007 Part 8, and the payer company is personally liable for any amount it fails to withhold under TAA 1994 s 31B and s 159. The unpaid tax is subject to UOMI, shortfall penalties up to 150%, and criminal evasion under s 143B. Similarly, false classification of revenue as debt repayment rather than dividend is an offence that carries fines up to NZ$250,000 and imprisonment for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Under TAA 1994 s 159, the duty to withhold is imposed on directors and officers in their personal capacity, so directors can be made personally liable for unrecovered NRWT, penalties, and interest without the need to lift the corporate veil. Courts may order asset restitution, issue departure prohibition orders preventing travel, and convict directors for knowingly filing false declarations with the Companies Office, resulting in imprisonment under the Companies Act 1993 s 382.
AML-Backed Shell Company and Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 ss 43-47 requires identification of beneficial owners and suspicious-transaction reporting; serious contraventions expose a body corporate to civil penalties up to NZ$2 million and individuals to fines up to NZ$200,000 and imprisonment. False UBO and director information filed with the Companies Office is an offence under the Companies Act 1993 s 382, and the IRD may invoke ITA 2007 s BG 1 to void the corporate structure and reassess all income as that of the true economic owner.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot rely on the corporate veil where the company is a mere facade for tax fraud: they can be ordered to compensate creditor losses for reckless or fraudulent trading, disqualified as directors, blacklisted on Departure Prohibition Orders preventing departure from New Zealand, personally fined under AML/CFT legislation, and sentenced to imprisonment for deliberately using shell structures, nominee shareholders, and undisclosed video-matching revenue flows to evade tax and hide the ultimate beneficiaries.
