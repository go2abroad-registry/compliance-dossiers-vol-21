Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered office must be a physical location within New Zealand that is open and accessible to the public for no fewer than 20 hours per week during ordinary business hours, as required by section 202 of the Companies Act 1993. A pure virtual office or mail-forwarding address without physically accessible premises will be rejected by the Companies Office. For an offshore supply vessel chartering entity, the registered office may be maintained at the premises of a licensed corporate service provider, but the entity must also satisfy the operational substance expectations of Maritime New Zealand for vessel registration and any coastal trading license, which may require a place of business in New Zealand.

Companies Act 1993 (Public Act 1993 No 105), in particular sections 10 (incorporation), 14 (notice of incorporation), 16 (constitution), 23 (name restrictions), 91 (written resolutions), 94 (shares with no par value), 151 (director disqualification), 189 (registered office) and 212 (address for service).

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement apply under New Zealand law. At least one ordinary share must be issued on incorporation (section 94 of the Companies Act 1993), and shares may be of no par value with consideration being cash, property, services or other lawful consideration. Non-resident ownership of the issued shares is permitted.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for any shore-based office, workshop or storage facility under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Act 2017; structural engineering inspection and Building Code compliance under the Building Act 2004; workplace safety clearance under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015; Maritime New Zealand Certificate of Survey for each vessel; and crew competency certification under Maritime Rules Part 32.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Te Tari Taake; online portal: MyIR (Secure Online Services)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) from the Companies Office, then apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number and GST registration using the appropriate registration forms (IR596 for non-resident companies or IRD entity registration via MyIR). Non-resident companies may also be required to register under NRWT, FBT, or PAYE if applicable. The GST registration becomes effective from the date of application or the date taxable supplies begin, subject to the NZD 60,000 compulsory registration threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must first obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) from the Companies Office, then apply to Inland Revenue for an IRD number and GST registration using the appropriate registration forms (IR596 for non-resident companies or IRD entity registration via MyIR). Non-resident companies may also be required to register under NRWT, FBT, or PAYE if applicable. The GST registration becomes effective from the date of application or the date taxable supplies begin, subject to the NZD 60,000 compulsory registration threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in New Zealand; entity classification is determined by the Income Tax Act 2007. Available statutory elections include GST grouping elections, provisional tax payment pooling elections, and certain hybrid mismatch ordering elections under subpart FH. There is no free election to reclassify a limited company as pass-through; NZ Limited Companies are taxable entities at the corporate rate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 annual taxable supplies; voluntary registration available below the threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 when the supply is exported or directly connected with exports. For offshore vessel chartering and offshore platform support ship operations, zero-rating applies if the services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply, or the supply is physically performed outside New Zealand, or the supply directly relates to goods that have been exported or to a vessel used in international transport. Adequate documentary evidence must establish the export/offshore nature of the supply, including contracts, invoices, customs entries, vessel movement records, and evidence of payment from non-resident counterparties.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. Electronic invoicing in New Zealand is voluntary and operates on the PEPPOL network, facilitated by the NZBN. No API-based fiscal clearance obligation applies; business records may be maintained electronically for GST compliance.

Inland Revenue audits zero-rated export supplies strictly. If documentary evidence is insufficient or zero-rating has been incorrectly claimed, the full GST amount becomes payable as a shortfall. For fraudulent or fraudulent-intent GST claims, the Tax Administration Act 1994 imposes civil penalties up to 150% of the tax shortfall, late payment penalties and interest, and criminal liability on conviction with fines up to NZD 150,000 for individuals and up to NZD 250,000 for companies, plus potential imprisonment. Retrospective reassessment may claw back incorrectly refunded GST for prior periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains full current and capital account convertibility. The New Zealand dollar is freely floating and fully convertible through all registered financial institutions. There are no general exchange controls, no prior approval requirements for profit repatriation, and no prescribed conversion rights or restrictions on capital outflows by non-resident shareholders of a New Zealand limited company. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to tax compliance, principally non-resident withholding tax on dividends or the approved issuer levy, and corporate formalities such as a valid dividend declaration, solvency certification, and submission of the appropriate tax returns. For an offshore supply vessel leasing company, no sector-specific FX conversion or repatriation restriction applies. The Overseas Investment Act 2005 may require consent only if the transaction involves sensitive land or significant business assets; ordinary vessel chartering and leasing do not trigger exchange control screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory surrender rules, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio for export proceeds or foreign currency charter hire receipts. Export proceeds and vessel charter revenues may be retained in any freely convertible currency and settled through any registered New Zealand bank. Banking records must be maintained for anti-money laundering and countering financing of terrorism purposes under the AML/CFT Act 2009, but no repatriation or conversion obligation exists.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury rail and Medium as an ancillary payment channel. Offshore digital electronic money institutions are not registered New Zealand banks, are not prudentially supervised by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand, and cannot be direct participants in the New Zealand high-value or retail clearing systems. They cannot legally hold or settle NZD corporate transactional accounts in the same manner as a licensed New Zealand bank. Legal constraints include the absence of depositor protection, the requirement that all NZD clearing settle through a locally incorporated registered bank, and the need to maintain a qualifying sponsor bank relationship for any inbound or outbound wire activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed through the New Zealand beneficiary bank using the SWIFT payment network, typically an MT103 customer credit or an MT202 for interbank transfers. The instruction must include the BIC of the receiving New Zealand bank, the company's account number for the capital escrow or operational account, and a purpose code that accurately describes the transaction as an equity injection, shareholder loan, or intercompany loan. The receiving bank will perform full source of funds verification, sanctions screening, AML/CFT customer due diligence, and may require the Companies Office share allotment resolution, an executed loan agreement, and the final beneficial ownership schedule before releasing funds. If the capital injection is linked to vessel acquisition or another transaction requiring consent under the Overseas Investment Act 2005, legal clearance should be obtained before remittance.Outward trade disbursements for vessel operations, bunker fuel, crew wages, insurance premiums, port fees, repair services, and charter-related settlements should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for customer payments or MT202 for bank-to-bank reimbursements. Commercial letters of credit and standby letters of credit opened or confirmed by a tier-one New Zealand bank are the standard clearing rails for major vessel acquisition and leasing disbursements. All outbound instructions must include a compliant purpose code, match supporting invoices and contracts, and pass enhanced due diligence.
