Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or registered agent address is accepted for Companies Act 1993 section 199 compliance only if it is a physical New Zealand street address, is not a post office box, and is authorized to receive service of legal documents. However, for an offshore marine fuel bunkering and lubricant replenishment company rated Medium_Risk_Enhanced, New Zealand registered banks, Inland Revenue, Maritime New Zealand, and the AML/CFT supervisor will reject a bare virtual office as the sole business address because the industry requires identifiable operational premises, local personnel, bunker supply records, port and berth access arrangements, and physical presence for inspection. A virtual office may be permitted only as the statutory registered office address if the company actually maintains a separate operational waterfront office, a local accountable person, and a valid bunkering or port facility agreement.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department, Te Tari Taake (New Zealand); primary taxpayer portal myIR; border and export controls administered jointly with New Zealand Customs Service

E-government filing portal

A New Zealand-registered limited company automatically obtains an Inland Revenue (IRD) number through the integrated Companies Office business registration upon incorporation. A foreign company carrying on taxable activity in New Zealand must first register as a non-resident company for an IRD number via myIR or the Business Registration portal, providing the offshore address, director details, New Zealand agent details, and expected turnover. GST registration is filed through the same portal; compulsory registration arises when taxable supplies made in New Zealand over a 12-month period exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed the statutory threshold. A separate GST number is not issued; IRD uses one taxpayer identifier for income tax and GST obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand does not operate a check-the-box regime. A close-held company meeting the statutory ownership and shareholder residence conditions may elect look-through company status under the Income Tax Act 2007, causing income and losses to be attributed to shareholders on a pass-through basis; however, a non-resident-owned standard limited company remains subject to entity-level taxation. Hybrid mismatch and branch mismatch rules apply under New Zealand tax law to deny deductions and maintain symmetry where an entity or instrument is characterised differently in another jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in taxable supplies made in New Zealand over any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services and digital goods must register if those supplies exceed NZD 60,000 per annum

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply may be zero-rated at 0% GST where goods are exported from New Zealand or where services are supplied to a non-resident outside New Zealand. For ship bunkering and refueling, marine fuel supplied as vessel stores to a ship engaged in international voyages is eligible for zero-rating under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, provided the supplier retains documentary evidence of physical export or foreign consumption, the vessel is departing New Zealand on an international leg, and the fuel is not used for domestic or coastal services. Bunkers supplied for domestic voyages or coastal operations are standard-rated at 15%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (NZ electronic export entry)
  • Bill of Lading showing the bunker vessel and international voyage
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire confirmation establishing foreign exchange receipt for the export sale

New Zealand imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no API-based pre-clearance clearance, and no digital stamping for GST invoices. E-invoicing is supported via the PEPPOL network, and IRD accepts GST returns filed electronically through myIR or certified accounting software using IRD APIs. GST invoices must meet the substantive content requirements of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985; electronic transmission is permitted without advance government authorisation.

IRD audits export zero-rating claims through transactional matching and customs data. Where zero-rating is incorrectly claimed or documentary evidence is inadequate, the supplier is liable for the uncollected GST, use-of-money interest, and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994. Deliberate or abusive tax positions attract shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall; fraudulent GST refund claims may lead to criminal prosecution with fines and terms of imprisonment. Directors and controlling shareholders may also face personal liability for unpaid GST in specified circumstances.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is fully and freely convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. No prior central bank approval, registration, or licensing is required for inbound or outbound cross-border wire transfers, dividend remittances, loan repayments, trade settlement, or profit repatriation to non-resident shareholders. The absence of exchange controls is statutory and not a discretionary administrative grace. However, the Overseas Investment Act 2005 may impose consent obligations for foreign persons acquiring certain sensitive land, fishing quota, or significant business assets (typically a business asset value above NZD 100 million or direct acquisition of farmland or foreshore/seabed interests). A marine bunkering entity engaged purely in the supply of ship fuel and lubricants through port concession agreements or vessel-to-vessel transfers does not typically require OIO consent. Profit remittances are free of any exchange approval, but are subject to standard withholding tax mechanics: non-resident withholding tax (NRWT) at the treaty rate (commonly 15% under a double tax treaty; reduced to 5% for fully imputed dividends if the approved issuer levy is selected by the paying company), with no delay or penalty beyond the ordinary tax return filing cycle. All banks are permitted to contract freely in any major international currency, and multiple-currency settlement capabilities are standard.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand law imposes no statutory surrender, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no required conversion ratio for export receipts. A bunkering company may retain its export-derived revenue in USD, EUR, SGD, or any eligible account currency in either onshore or offshore accounts without a Reserve Bank directive. The company may operate a multicurrency account structure with its Tier 1 banks and convert at its commercial discretion. The only regulatory constraints are indirect: (a) IRD requires that taxable income be reported in NZD using the applicable exchange rate in accordance with the Income Tax Act 2007 and the Financial Reporting Act 2013; (b) banks may include soft covenants in working-capital facilities requiring concentration limits or reimbursement in the currency of the underlying trade invoice; and (c) the AML/CFT regime requires threshold transaction reports for cash transactions of NZD 10,000 or more and does not differentiate between foreign and domestic currency movements. There are no surrender ratios, no time-bound conversion windows, and no export proceeds escrow requirements under New Zealand law.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Putea Matua) is the central bank and prudential regulator for registered banks. There is no standalone foreign exchange administration; exchange controls were abolished in 1984. Supervision of the AML/CFT regime is shared between the Reserve Bank, the Financial Markets Authority (FMA), and the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009. The Overseas Investment Office (OIO) exercises non-banking approval powers for inward foreign investment under the Overseas Investment Act 2005.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: Conditional and Limited. Fully-licensed digital banks or virtual banks do not exist in New Zealand as a distinct license category. Offshore fintech EMI platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Stripe, Wise, Currencycloud) are legally able to provide payment and remittance services in New Zealand only if they are registered on the Financial Service Providers Register under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and become reporting entities under the AMLCFT Act 2009. They may hold a custody or authorized money remitter status in their home jurisdiction, but they cannot issue standby letters of credit or documentary letters of credit, and they do not carry deposits insured under the New Zealand Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 or any Crown deposit compensation regime. In practice, an offshore EMI is viable only for high-volume, low-value current-account payments (e.g., port fees, crew petty cash) and cannot serve as the primary treasury or clearing rail for marine fuel settlement, where the industry norm requires a regulated bank-to-bank MT103 or an SBLC issued by a reputable bank. Legal constraints include the Deposit Takers Act 2022, which will require any entity taking deposits from the New Zealand public to be a licensed deposit taker, and the RBNZ's operational risk expectations for commercial banks refusing to clear payments to unregulated EMIs. The recommended architecture is: Tier 1 bank as the sole clearing rail for SBLC, LC, and high-value settlement; a licensed EMI used in a ring-fenced manner for sub-NZD 50,000 payments, subject to prior approval by the bank's relationship manager and full audit trail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan capital flows into the New Zealand entity must be routed through a licensed bank or a fully overseen money remitter. The preferred instrument is a SWIFT MT103 for direct transfers and an MT202 for interbank cover. There is no RBNZ approval requirement for the repatriation of funds into New Zealand, but standard bank customer due diligence will require the receiving file to include: (a) a copy of the executed Board Resolution authorizing the issuance of shares or the incurrence of debt, (b) the share subscription agreement or loan agreement reflecting the exact value date and currency, (c) the Companies Office share registry extract identifying the new shareholder, and (d) the UBO source-of-funds disclosure form. The correct SWIFT purpose codes should be selected: for share capital, use 'F01 - Investment in a commercial enterprise'; for an intercompany loan, use 'G107 - Loans to non-residents' or 'G108 - Loans to residents' (depending on which side classifies the inflow); if the capital is to fund working capital for trade, 'P010 - Deposits of a financial institution' is not appropriate and should be avoided. The receiving bank will verify the credit against the registration details and may place a short post-value compliance hold if any name on the transfer matches a sanctions list. For liquidity optimization, the entity should open a foreign-currency account (FCA) alongside the NZD account so USD/EUR capital can be held in the same bank without immediate conversion, and an internal FX-forward can be layered over the position to lock in a predetermined NZD cash-flow yield. All inbound transfers must carry the full originator name, address, and account number to satisfy the AML/CFT Travel Rule; an incomplete originator indicates a high-likelihood automated rejection. The bank will not require OIO approval if the capital is less than the sensitive asset thresholds and purely for operating purposes in marine fuel distribution.Outbound trade disbursements for a ship bunkering and refueling business are settled using two primary clearing rails: (1) direct SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for payment on delivery or net-30 trade credit to international fuel suppliers and lubricant providers; and (2) documentary letters of credit and standby letters of credit for milestone-based purchasing or the extension of supplier credit lines. The documentary LC rail must be governed by the ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP600) for commercial LCs and by the International Standby Practices (ISP98) for standby LCs. For an SBLC used as a parent guarantee to secure a Singapore or Fujairah supplier's credit line, the New Zealand bank will issue the SBLC with an expiry not exceeding 12 months, a maximum liability capped at the underlying off-take or chartering contract value, and a presentation period standard to the industry. The issuer will require 100% collateral coverage or a counter-indemnity executed by the New Zealand company and, where applicable, a guarantee fee of 100-150 basis points per annum. MT103 disbursements must reference the invoice number, the bunker delivery note (BDN) identifier, and the vessel IMO number in the remittance information field; a mismatch between the beneficiary name and the bank account holder name may delay the payment and generate a sanctions-compliance review. Settlement for a vessel bunker purchase is commonly due within 24 to 72 hours after the completion of the delivery note, and the New Zealand clearing bank will process the wire on the same business day if the instruction is sent before the cut-off (typically 16:00 NZST). For multi-currency payments, the bank will auto-debit the FCA if available or execute an internal conversion at the daily corporate rate. The second-tier requirement is that any outbound wire exceeding NZD 10,000 must include a statement of purpose and be preserved in the audit trail. The recommended treasury practice is to pre-load the beneficiary bank template for each approved supplier, enforce dual authorization for any wire above NZD 100,000, and reconcile each BDN against the MT103 to ensure no unknowing payment to a sanctions-hit counterparty.Outright deliverable FX forward contracts (plain vanilla) between NZD and USD, EUR, SGD, AUD, and JPY, with maturities of 1, 3, 6, and 12 months, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Credit Support Annex; fully permitted without central bank approval.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally not available for NZD because the New Zealand dollar is a free-currency delivery market; however, a few global trading desks may quote an NDF under a master netting agreement for a corporate that cannot open a settlement account in New Zealand, which is not the case for a locally incorporated NZ Ltd, therefore this instrument is treated as impermissible and unnecessary.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) between NZD and USD or EUR, used to hedge both the principal and periodic interest flows on any external borrowing or capital lease structure, subject to the bank's credit approval and the entity demonstrating an underlying exposure in accordance with the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013.European-style currency options (call and put) with 100% upfront premium, offering discretionary downside protection without a requirement to settle on an adverse rate; typically only available to corporates that have executed an options-eligible master agreement with the dealing bank.FX swaps and spot-forward swaps for treasury liquidity management, including tom/next and spot-next swaps, to manage the value dating of multicurrency cash pools while maintaining a closed risk position.Overnight indexed swaps (OIS) in NZD for the management of the floating-rate exposure on any working capital line, coupled with a sell-buy foreign exchange swap to fund the NZD exposure from the USD revenue stream; all instruments are lawful and observable under the RBNZ prudential governance.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Conditional and Limited. Fully-licensed digital banks or virtual banks do not exist in New Zealand as a distinct license category. Offshore fintech EMI platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Stripe, Wise, Currencycloud) are legally able to provide payment and remittance services in New Zealand only if they are registered on the Financial Service Providers Register under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 and become reporting entities under the AMLCFT Act 2009. They may hold a custody or authorized money remitter status in their home jurisdiction, but they cannot issue standby letters of credit or documentary letters of credit, and they do not carry deposits insured under the New Zealand Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 or any Crown deposit compensation regime. In practice, an offshore EMI is viable only for high-volume, low-value current-account payments (e.g., port fees, crew petty cash) and cannot serve as the primary treasury or clearing rail for marine fuel settlement, where the industry norm requires a regulated bank-to-bank MT103 or an SBLC issued by a reputable bank. Legal constraints include the Deposit Takers Act 2022, which will require any entity taking deposits from the New Zealand public to be a licensed deposit taker, and the RBNZ's operational risk expectations for commercial banks refusing to clear payments to unregulated EMIs. The recommended architecture is: Tier 1 bank as the sole clearing rail for SBLC, LC, and high-value settlement; a licensed EMI used in a ring-fenced manner for sub-NZD 50,000 payments, subject to prior approval by the bank's relationship manager and full audit trail.
