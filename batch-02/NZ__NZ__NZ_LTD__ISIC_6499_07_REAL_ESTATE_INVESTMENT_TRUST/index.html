Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

New Zealand Companies Office (NZCO), a business unit of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), operating through the Companies Register online portal.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993 (New Zealand), sections 12 (Incorporation), 37 (Share Capital), 122 (Registered Office), 128 (Appointment of Directors), 131 (Duties of Directors), and 153 (Financial Reporting); Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 (if offering securities to the public); Real Estate Agents Act 2008 (if engaging in real estate agency activities, but not applicable for passive REIT holding).

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NZCN/01 – Application for Registration of a Company (Incorporation), filed electronically via the Companies Register.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 17 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. New Zealand law imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement for a limited liability company. However, the company must have at least one share issued and one shareholder. The board may resolve to issue shares for consideration in cash or non-cash assets, and any unpaid capital must be recorded in the company's financial statements. No escrow or blocked account is mandated by statute.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required under the Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) Act 2017. Each building must have a current Fire Evacuation Scheme approved by FENZ. Structural load inspections are mandated by the Building Act 2004, requiring a Building Warrant of Fitness (BWoF) for buildings with specified systems (e.g., fire alarms, sprinklers). Occupational safety clearance is issued by WorkSafe New Zealand under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, requiring a documented health and safety plan and regular audits. For a REIT, the property manager must ensure all tenants comply with these requirements.

Paid-in capital rule: None. New Zealand law imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement for a limited liability company. However, the company must have at least one share issued and one shareholder. The board may resolve to issue shares for consideration in cash or non-cash assets, and any unpaid capital must be recorded in the company's financial statements. No escrow or blocked account is mandated by statute.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (Te Tari Tāke), Wellington; online portal myIR at www.ird.govt.nz, supplemented by New Zealand Companies Office for corporate registration and New Zealand Customs Service for export declarations

E-government filing portal

For a New Zealand incorporated limited company, the New Zealand Companies Office issues the NZBN at incorporation and Inland Revenue automatically allocates the IRD number used for income tax and GST. If automatic allocation has not occurred, or a foreign non-resident company needs to register for New Zealand tax, the entity must: (1) obtain a NZBN or provide an overseas company registration certificate equivalent; (2) lodge a non-resident company IRD number application through myIR, supplying legal name, country of incorporation, registered office and residential agent details, director information, principal place or place of business, expected first taxable period, and the scope of New Zealand activities; (3) use the same IRD number as the GST number, as New Zealand does not issue a separate VAT identification number; (4) register for GST under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985 through myIR when taxable supplies exceed NZD 60,000 in any 12-month period, or voluntarily where allowed, selecting the filing frequency and accounting basis; (5) appoint a New Zealand agent or contact address for tax notices if the company has no physical New Zealand office.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a New Zealand limited company is a separate taxpaying person resident in New Zealand if incorporated in New Zealand; it is taxed on worldwide income at the standard corporate rate of 28%; there is no automatic pass-through classification for a limited company and no tax-exempt zone treatment; a qualifying close company may elect Look-Through Company status under the Income Tax Act 2007, and a qualifying portfolio investment vehicle may elect Portfolio Investment Entity rules)

Available election forms

New Zealand has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: Look-Through Company election for close companies; Portfolio Investment Entity election for qualifying investment entities; voluntary GST registration; GST accounting basis elections (payments basis, invoice basis, or hybrid basis); GST return frequency election; and non-resident supplier remote-services GST registration. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Taxation (Neutralising Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Act 2018 are self-executing and do not require an elective filing mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000; compulsory when taxable supplies made in New Zealand exceed this threshold in any rolling 12-month period, subject to limited exceptions for non-resident suppliers of remote services who may need simplified registration irrespective of the threshold; voluntary registration is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A GST-registered person may claim 0% GST on exported goods where the goods are physically removed from New Zealand and contemporaneous export evidence is retained. Services may be zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient outside New Zealand and not directly connected with New Zealand land. A real estate investment trust earning rental income from New Zealand real property cannot treat domestic property income as export zero-rated; the zero-rating regime applies only to genuine offshore supplies of goods or qualifying services, not to New Zealand land-based income.

  • New Zealand Customs export declaration (Export Entry) lodged electronically through the New Zealand Customs Service Trade Single Window system with customs reference number
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing goods are physically consigned from New Zealand to the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and supply contract supporting the zero-rated transaction
  • Bank foreign exchange inward remittance/SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds (FIRC is an Indian banking instrument and is not a New Zealand statutory document, but bank-generated foreign exchange credit evidence is accepted)
  • Freight, port, vessel/voyage records and insurance certificate proving departure from New Zealand territory
  • GST tax invoice for the zero-rated supply and any related agency or logistics agreements

New Zealand does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance mandate, mandatory continuous transaction control, or anti-fraud digital stamping system. E-invoicing through the Peppol network is voluntary. GST-registered persons must issue tax invoices containing the prescribed particulars under the GST Act 1985, retain complete records for at least seven years, and file GST returns electronically through myIR on a monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly basis depending on turnover and election.

Inland Revenue may audit export zero-rating claims and withdraw zero-rating where the goods are not exported, the services do not meet statutory recipient tests, tax invoices are absent, or export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day foreign exchange deadline. The supply is recharacterized as taxable at 15% GST with use-of-money interest and shortfall penalties imposed under the Tax Administration Act 1994, ranging from 20% for lack of reasonable care up to 150% for evasion or abusive tax positions. Directors and controlling shareholders can incur personal liability for outstanding GST clawbacks in cases of deliberate or reckless non-compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Zealand maintains a lawfully unrestricted foreign exchange regime. The New Zealand dollar is freely floating under the Reserve Bank Act 1989, without curbs on current account transactions, trade settlement, capital transfers, dividends, or profit remittance. Non-resident shareholders may hold local bank accounts and repatriate distributions once the board declares a dividend and withholding tax formalities are observed. There is no surrender ratio and no central bank licence required for payment execution. The only possible conditional barrier is the Overseas Investment Act 2005, which requires consent for non-residents acquiring sensitive land; a real estate investment trust must obtain Overseas Investment Office clearance or a statutory exemption and demonstrate that the trust portfolio is not strategically sensitive. New Zealand's Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 imposes bank-level verification, but that is a documentary control, not an exchange restriction. In practice, Tier-1 banks can complete conversion to NZD at market rate and execute cross-border wires in major currencies without administrative bottlenecks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion rules apply; the mandatory conversion ratio is 0. Exporters may retain 100% of export receipts in NZD or foreign currency at any licensed bank, use them directly for onward trade payments, or convert at market rate at a time of their choosing. There is no forced timeline.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low, 2 out of 10, and legally constrained for the primary banking rail. In New Zealand, an offshore digital EMI is not a registered bank and generally cannot accept public deposits or provide unrestricted payment services under the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989. Most offshore EMIs operate under a foreign e-money licence and are not participants in Payments NZ clearing, meaning they cannot receive or issue funds through New Zealand bank account numbers, cannot provide settlement finality, and cannot support the required capital verification trail for a REIT. They also create avoidable ML/TF friction and audit risk when used as the sole liquidity vehicle. Offshore EMIs should be restricted to marginal auxiliary collections only and must never be used as the capital escrow, dividend distribution engine, or treasury clearing rail.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership obscured by a multi-layered trust or offshore corporate holding structure.

Root cause: Beneficial ownership obscured by a multi-layered trust or offshore corporate holding structure.

Defense evidence: Certified company registration document for every entity in the chain; authenticated trust deed listing settlor, trustees, protector and discretionary beneficiaries; graphical ownership structure; passport copies and proof of address for all natural persons with 25% or more economic interest; independent tax and accounting office confirmation of the ultimate senior beneficial ownership.

Root cause: Source-of-wealth and source-of-funds opacity on capital contribution intended to purchase real estate.

Defense evidence: Detailed wealth acquisition narrative; personal or corporate bank statements for 6 to 12 months; purchase or sale agreements, inheritance certificate, gift deed, dividend voucher, or private equity exit statement; independent accountant letter tracing the capital in NZD or foreign currency to the shareholder; no unexplained cash deposits.

Defense evidence: Original tax residency certificates from each relevant jurisdiction; signed CRS self-certification; FATCA classification for any US affiliate; IRD tax file number if applicable; registered office letter; independent legal opinion confirming that the trust is not an opaque passive investment vehicle and that distribution withholding taxes are correctly documented.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must arrive by SWIFT wire (MT103) from the shareholder own bank account, with the ordering customer name matching the beneficial shareholder and the narrative stating capital contribution to NZ Ltd REIT and the BOP/international transactions purpose code for equity capital. The receiving bank holds funds in a capital escrow account until board allotment; the company files the share return with the New Zealand Companies Office. Capital verification is completed by reconciling the deposit ticket, the company share register, and the board resolution. If the REIT acquires sensitive land, a supporting Overseas Investment Office clearance must be attached before the injection is authorised.Cross-border trade disbursements ride on SWIFT MT103 for clean payments; commercial letters of credit are opened through MT700 with the advising bank, and documentary collections D/P and D/A are processed via the same correspondent network. New Zealand domestic vendor payments use Payments NZ local clearing rails including bulk electronic clearing, while high-value domestic settlement runs through the Reserve Bank settlement account system. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NZD 10 million aggregate cross-border related-party transactions (IRD contemporaneous documentation benchmark); Country-by-Country reporting applies if the worldwide group has consolidated revenue of EUR 750m or more, requiring a New Zealand master/local file equivalent.

Economic substance scope: New Zealand applies economic substance principles through the general anti-avoidance rule under Income Tax Act 2007 section BG 1 and through CFC attribution and place of effective management tests. For a REIT SPV, Core Income-Generating Activities must actually be performed in New Zealand: asset management, leasing negotiations, property maintenance, financing decisions, and board governance. A tax-haven shell with no real staff, premises, or decision-making power will not satisfy New Zealand tax residence or treaty entitlement and may be recharacterised with penal adjustment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 1994 sections 143A and 143B: criminal fine of up to NZD 100,000 for a body corporate and up to NZD 50,000 for an individual; civil shortfall penalties up to 150% of the avoided tax under the shortfall penalty regime of the Tax Administration Act 1994; use of money interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or procure a false return may be convicted personally, imprisoned for up to 5 years, and barred from managing companies; the court may pierce the corporate veil, order restitution of diverted funds, and Inland Revenue may issue a departure prohibition order preventing overseas travel until tax debts are settled.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2007 section BG 1 general anti-avoidance and section GB 1 reconstruction of income; shortfall penalties for abusive tax position up to 100% of the tax avoided; Companies Act 1993 sections 135 and 136 for reckless trading and failure to perform director duties; Financial Reporting Act 2013 penalties for false financial statements.

Director personal liability: Under Companies Act 1993 section 301, directors can be personally ordered to repay money or restore property to the company or creditors; directors may be disqualified for up to 5 years or more; if the shell is used to evade tax, directors face criminal imprisonment under the Tax Administration Act, and blacklisting or travel restrictions may be imposed.
AML/CFT Non-Compliance (Reporting Entity / High-Value Dealer)

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009: civil penalties of up to NZD 2 million for a body corporate and up to NZD 200,000 per individual; separate criminal liability for money laundering under the Crimes Act 1961 sections 243 to 243A; suspension or cancellation of the statutory licence of the REIT.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the body corporate's AML/CFT failure if the contravention occurred with their knowledge, consent, or neglect; courts can impose custodial sentences of up to 2 years in serious cases, prohibit the director from acting as a manager of a financial institution, and place the director on the AML/CFT blacklist.
