A virtual office or commercial mailbox is acceptable as a New Zealand registered office only if it is a physical street address in New Zealand, open for service of process, and not a PO Box. Competent corporate registry

Companies Office of the New Zealand Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment, acting through the New Zealand Companies Register.

Governing statute

Companies Act 1993, including sections 10 to 16 concerning company incorporation and registration, sections 27 to 36 concerning company constitutions, sections 45 to 50 concerning shares and shareholder liability, sections 128 to 138 concerning directors, sections 145 to 149 concerning directors duties and disclosure, and sections 189 to 193 concerning company records and accounting records. The Companies Act 1993 must be read with the Companies Regulations 1994 and the Companies Act 1993 fee regulations in force on the filing date.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for registration of a company under section 12 of the Companies Act 1993, submitted through the Companies Office online incorporation service, together with the required director, shareholder, registered office, address for service, and consent information.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

New Zealand has no statutory minimum issued or paid-up share capital for a limited company and no general statutory requirement to deposit subscription funds into an escrow account before incorporation. The company may be incorporated with one or more shares, provided that the application states the proposed share structure and the persons acquiring the shares. Shares should be issued for legally valid consideration and the company must maintain an accurate share register. Any unpaid amount on issued shares remains a liability of the relevant shareholder to the company. The Companies Office incorporation fee is prescribed in New Zealand dollars and the USD amount stated above is an indicative conversion only; the filing system charges the prescribed New Zealand dollar amount and may apply card or payment-provider fees.

Certify racking, mezzanines, loading equipment, refrigeration systems and structural loads through competent persons. Obtain any required hazardous substance location, stationary container, certified handler, compliance certification, dangerous goods, or transport documentation before storing or moving relevant substances.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), New Zealand; online portal myIR

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities deriving New Zealand-source income must first obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) via the Companies Office registration process or apply directly to the Inland Revenue using the applicable IRD number application form (e.g., IR 742 for non-individuals or the composite company registration process through the Companies Office). Upon approval, the entity is issued a unique IRD number or is linked to the existing NZBN-based registration, after which it must register for GST, employer obligations, and any industry-specific excise or levy regimes as applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities deriving New Zealand-source income must first obtain a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN) via the Companies Office registration process or apply directly to the Inland Revenue using the applicable IRD number application form (e.g., IR 742 for non-individuals or the composite company registration process through the Companies Office). Upon approval, the entity is issued a unique IRD number or is linked to the existing NZBN-based registration, after which it must register for GST, employer obligations, and any industry-specific excise or levy regimes as applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

New Zealand permits a look-through company (LTC) election for qualifying close-held companies under Subpart HB of the Income Tax Act 2007, but does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Hybrid mismatch rules under Subpart FH may apply to cross-border arrangements, and an LTC election is irrevocable for the first five years.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NZD 60,000 in taxable supplies over a 12-month rolling period; voluntary registration permitted below threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from New Zealand within 28 days of the time of supply are zero-rated; services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time of supply and not directly connected with New Zealand land or goods, or remote services consumed outside New Zealand, may also be zero-rated. The supplier must hold sufficient documentary evidence proving export and offshore consumption to support the zero-rating treatment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (NZ Export Entry / Trade Single Window electronic declaration)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Customs-approved proof of export / port and voyage details
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign exchange receipt evidence
  • Packing list and any applicable GDP / conformity certificates

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based pre-approval, or digital stamping mandate exists in New Zealand. Electronic invoicing is voluntary and primarily facilitated through the New Zealand Peppol e-Invoicing network. GST returns are filed electronically via myIR or approved software.

The Inland Revenue and New Zealand Customs Service may audit export zero-rating claims. Incorrect zero-rating exposes the supplier to reassessment at the standard 15% GST rate, use-of-money interest from the original due date, and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 1994: 20% for failing to take reasonable care, up to 150% for evasion or gross carelessness. Fraudulent export refunds can result in criminal prosecution and substantial financial penalties, with mandatory repayment of any GST refund plus penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Zealand dollar (NZD) is a freely floating and fully convertible fiat currency with a liberalised capital and current account. There are no restrictions on the purchase or sale of foreign exchange, no surrender requirements, and no central bank licensing for FX transactions. Profit remittances from a non-resident-owned NZ Ltd are unrestricted, subject only to normal Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT) on dividends and ordinary banking due diligence for source-of-funds verification. Capital repatriation via share buyback, capital reduction, or intercompany loan repayment may proceed without exchange control approval, although the Overseas Investment Act 2005 may apply only when the transaction crosses the 'significant business assets' threshold (generally NZD $100 million for non-Australian/non-US investors) or involves sensitive land.

Export proceeds surrender rules

New Zealand imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or retention period for export proceeds. Export receipts from GDP pharma freight services may be retained in foreign currency, converted freely into NZD, or offset against offshore trade payables. The RBNZ collects balance-of-payments statistical data from registered banks, but this reporting is strictly statistical and does not limit currency choice, timing, onshore repatriation, or offshore retention of funds.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Te Pūtea Matua) — the sole central bank and prudential regulator of registered banks. New Zealand maintains no exchange control regime administered by the RBNZ; foreign exchange transactions are unrestricted. The Overseas Investment Office (OIO) administers the Overseas Investment Act 2005 for non-resident investment screening, while the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) supervises AML/CFT compliance for conventional banks and the Financial Markets Authority (FMA) regulates non-bank financial services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rated: High-Feasible with Constraints. Offshore fintech EMIs and digital payment institutions (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) can provide low-cost multi-currency collection, SWIFT disbursement, and FX conversion for a low-risk GDP freight entity after remote onboarding with standard AML/KYC. However, they cannot fulfil statutory corporate banking requirements where a New Zealand-registered bank account is necessary for: (i) Inland Revenue tax payment obligations, (ii) NZD domestic ACH/BECS clearing through the Bulk Electronic Clearing System, (iii) documentary Letter of Credit operations under UCP600, or (iv) trust account / capital escrow functions. Legal constraints under the AML/CFT Act 2009 and the FMA e-money licensing framework require any EMI providing New Zealand-sourced services to residents to maintain a registered presence in NZ and to register as a reporting entity; offshore digital EMIs lacking a NZ-registered financial services provider licence are restricted to non-resident receivable collection and ancillary payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) execute a board capital resolution and update the Companies Office share register; (2) remit via SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the investor's bank using purpose code 'CAPT' (Capital Contribution) or 'GFOA' (Goods/Freight Operating Advance) and include the NZ Ltd bank account and BIC (e.g., BNZENZ2L for BNZ); (3) tag the transfer as RBNZ balance-of-payments code 'Share Capital' or 'Intercompany Loan' — no exchange control approval is required; (4) confirm Overseas Investment Act 2005 nil-consent position because the capital injection funds an operating pharma freight entity below the significant business assets threshold and does not involve sensitive land; (5) deliver to the receiving bank the source-of-funds declaration, company resolution, and certified beneficial ownership register. The bank will tag the credit to the entity's 'Capital Suspense' for verification prior to release to the operating account.Primary clearing rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments and freight settlement to USD/EUR/CAD payees; (b) Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP600 issued by the NZ commercial bank for offshore consignment purchases of pharmaceutical goods, including usance or 'red/blue clause' advances where contractually agreed; (c) Domestic NZD ACH/BECS disbursements through the Bulk Electronic Clearing System for local cold-chain logistics, storage, and customs clearance fees; and (d) on-behalf-of (OBO) structured SWIFT payments where the NZ Ltd funds a counterparty's account at a third-party bank for combined freight settlements. Every outbound transfer must include a correct RBNZ BOP transaction code (e.g., 'Freight Services' or 'Trade Credit'), pass sanctions and PEP screening, and be supported by the underlying invoice, packing list, bill of lading or air waybill, and GDP temperature record.
